'''Pétion-Ville''' ({{IPA|fr|petjɔ̃ vil}}; {{langx|ht|Petyonvil}}) is a [[Communes of Haiti|commune]] and a suburb of [[Port-au-Prince]], [[Haiti]], in the hills east and separate from the city itself on the northern hills of the Massif de la Selle. Founded in 1831 by president [[Jean-Pierre Boyer]], it was named after [[Alexandre Pétion|Alexandre Sabès Pétion]] (1770–1818), the Haitian general and president later recognized as one of the country's four founding fathers.

The district is primarily a residential and touristic area. It had a population of 283,052 at the 2003 Census, which was officially estimated to have reached 376,834 in 2015.<ref name="IHSI" /> Many diplomats, foreign merchants and wealthy people who engage in business reside in Pétion-Ville.<ref>{{cite news|last=Katz |first=Jonathan |title=Major quake hits Haiti; many casualties expected |publisher=AP |url=https://news.yahoo.com/s/ap/20100113/ap_on_re_la_am_ca/cb_haiti_earthquake |date=2010-01-13 |access-date=2010-01-13 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100114182637/http://news.yahoo.com/s/ap/20100113/ap_on_re_la_am_ca/cb_haiti_earthquake |archive-date=January 14, 2010 }}</ref>

Despite the distance from the capital and the general affluence of the district, the lack of administrative enforcement has led to the formation of shantytowns on the outer edges of the district, as poor locals migrate upward and have settled there in search of job opportunities. On 28 or 29 August 2020, Haitian Lawyer [[Monferrier Dorval]] was assassinated in front of his home. On 7 July 2021, Haitian president [[Jovenel Moïse]] was assassinated at his private residence.

==2010 earthquake==
{{main|2010 Haiti earthquake}}
On 12 January 2010, around 4:53 pm, [[2010 Haiti earthquake|a 7.0 earthquake struck]] the Pétion-Ville area. The earthquake caused the collapse of a hospital in the city.<ref name=NYT-2010-01-12>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2010/01/12/world/AP-CB-Haiti-Earthquake.html|title=Haitian Earthquake Causes Hospital Collapse|date=12 January 2010|work=The New York Times|access-date=12 January 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=http://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/20100113203633/https://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2010/01/12/world/AP-CB-Haiti-Earthquake.html|archive-date=13 January 2010}}</ref>
The 7.0-magnitude earthquake destroyed many buildings in Port-au-Prince, and a lot of homes in the Montana area, including the [[Hôtel Montana]].

The ''Club de Pétion-Ville'' golf course was converted into a tent city by the US Army and housed 50,000 to 80,000 Haitians in 2010.<ref>Ireland On-Line, [https://archive.today/20120707004750/http://breakingnews.iol.ie/news/world/aid-agencies-overhaul-food-distribution-in-haiti-444186.html "Aid agencies overhaul food distribution in Haiti"], 30 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref> Its tennis courts hosted elements of the US [[82nd Airborne]] division.<ref>''Boston Globe'', The field hospital at the club was operated by NDMS teams from the US Department of Health and Human Services. [http://archive.boston.com/news/world/latinamerica/articles/2010/01/31/much_rests_on_haiti_elite/ "Much rests on Haiti elite"], Maria Sacchetti, 31 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref> The club was built in the 1930s and had around 300 members at the time.<ref name=Golf-2010-01-20>Golf.com, [http://www.golf.com/golf/tours_news/article/0,28136,1955178,00.html "Once a club with 300 members, Haiti's lone golf course is now a refugee camp"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100123024949/http://www.golf.com/golf/tours_news/article/0,28136,1955178,00.html |date=January 23, 2010 }}, Farrell Evans, 20 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref> The golf clubhouse was turned into a field hospital.<ref name=NP-2010-01-23>National Post, [https://nationalpost.com/news/world/story.html?id=2475317 "This won't do any good for most people"]{{dead link|date=October 2025|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}, Sheldon Alberts, 23 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref>

In early February 2010, the Israeli-based humanitarian organization, [[IsraAid]], opened a child education center in the Pétion-Ville tent city, in conjunction with other agencies, such as [[Operation Blessing International|Operation Blessing]]. The center was set up initially in the tents from the [[Israel Defense Forces]]' field hospital.<ref name=Israschools>Heyman, Jeffrey [http://www.israel21c.org/the-resilience-of-the-people-is-stronger-than-we-expected/ "The resilience of the people is stronger than we expected"], (10 February 2010), in [http://www.israel21c.org/ ''Israel 21c Innovation News Service''], Retrieved 2010-02-11</ref>
 
The "Muncheez" pizza restaurant was turned into a community [[soup kitchen]]. It served approximately 1,000 free meals a day. Before the quake, the restaurant chain was a place where few even in Pétion-Ville could afford to eat. After the quake, owners realized that the food stored at the three restaurants would spoil before it would get back into business and decided to give it all away. Although still living in the streets, the cooks still came to cook for the masses. Owners distributed blue bracelets throughout Pétion-Ville, one bracelet for one meal. They selected one of the outlets to become the soup kitchen and moved all 105 employees to that site to cook. When food ran out after two days, the [[Hôtel Montana]] donated what could be salvaged from their freezers. When they began running out of fuel, cooking oil and food, a convoy from relatives of the owners in the Dominican Republic arrived, funded by donations with more food and fuel. [[USAID]] supplied fuel, cooking oil and food to cook, and [[World Vision]] supplied  [[bulgur]] and [[lentil]]s.<ref>NPR, [https://www.npr.org/templates/story/story.php?storyId=122989207 "Haitian Eatery Serves Up Taste Of Hope Amid Despair"], Tamara Keith, 27 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref><ref>IOL, [http://www.iol.co.za/index.php?set_id=1&click_id=122&art_id=nw20100127212402186C717440 "Quake survivors score meals from pizza place"], Ruth Morris, 28 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref><ref>''Christian Science Monitor'', [https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Americas/2010/0124/Haiti-earthquake-Restaurateur-turns-Port-au-Prince-eatery-into-soup-kitchen "Haiti earthquake: Restaurateur turns Port-au-Prince eatery into soup kitchen"], Howard LaFranchi, 24 January 2010 (accessed 31 January 2010)</ref>

Electricity was restored to some sectors at the beginning of February<ref>Haiti Press Network, {{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20100209193325/http://www.haitipressnetwork.com/news.cfm?articleID=13365 "Haïti – Séisme : L’électricité se rétablit peu à peu"]}}, MJB, 4 February 2010 (accessed 5 February 2010)</ref> and to most of the rest of the city later.

== 2021 assassination of Jovenel Moïse ==
{{Main|Assassination of Jovenel Moïse}}
On 7 July 2021, then-President of Haiti [[Jovenel Moïse]] was [[Assassination of Jovenel Moïse|assassinated]] by a group of 28 gunmen while sleeping in his private residence located in Pétion-Ville.<ref>{{Cite web|author=Etant Dupain, Gerardo Lemos, Ivana Kottasová and Caitlin Hu|title=Haiti President Jovenel Moise assassinated in attack on his residence|url=https://www.cnn.com/2021/07/07/americas/haiti-president-jovenel-moise-attack-intl/index.html|access-date=2021-07-10|website=CNN|date=7 July 2021 }}</ref>

==Facilities==
The ''Club de Pétion-Ville'' country club was built in the 1930s and has Haiti's only golf course, a nine-hole course.<ref name=Golf-2010-01-20/> It also contains tennis courts and a swimming pool.<ref name=NP-2010-01-23/> The Club de Pétion-Ville is not in the suburb of Pétion-Ville, but is actually in Bourdon.{{citation needed|date=July 2021}}

The El Rancho Hotel & Casino is in Pétion-Ville. It was built from the private estate of Albert Silvera, a sports and luxury car collector who was one of the pioneers of Haiti's hotel industry.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.sun-sentinel.com/news/fl-xpm-1985-10-20-8502160008-story.html | title=Haiti's Accommodations Include Panoramic Views, Non-Tourist Creole Cuisine | date=20 October 1985 }}</ref>

In the neighborhood of Juvenat, the Hotel Karibe is a 174-room hotel with a spa, restaurant and conference center. In 2014, it hosted the [[Miss Haiti]] pageant.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Haiti - Social : Miss Haiti 2014 is... Carolyn Desert ! - HaitiLibre.com : Haiti news 7/7 |url=https://www.haitilibre.com/en/news-11996-haiti-social-miss-haiti-2014-is-carolyn-desert.html |access-date=2025-04-19 |website=HaitiLibre.com}}</ref>
