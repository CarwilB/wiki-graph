'''Guapi''' is a town and municipality in the [[Cauca Department]], Colombia.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://guapi-cauca.gov.co/index.shtml |title=Sitio web del municipio Guapi en Cauca |website=guapi-cauca.gov.co|access-date=2016-06-06|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070727230039/http://guapi-cauca.gov.co/index.shtml|archive-date=2007-07-27|url-status=dead}}</ref> The municipality of Guapi is located on the [[Guapi River]], {{convert|4|mi}} from the [[Pacific Ocean]], and also covers the island of [[Gorgona, Colombia|Gorgona]].

== Culture ==
[[File:Actividades culturales.jpg|thumb|left|Traditional music being performed in Guapi]]
Guapi is a predominantly [[Afro-Colombians|Afro-Colombian]] and [[Catholic Church in Colombia|Catholic]] area,<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |date=2022-12-14 |title=Row over ancestral festivity divides Afro-Colombian town |language=en-GB |work=BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-63895510 |access-date=2022-12-16}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Fides |first=Agenzia |title=VATICAN - Cardinal Filoni in Guapi: "It is necessary to develop a strong missionary conscience" - Agenzia Fides |url=https://fides.org/en/news/60089-VATICAN_Cardinal_Filoni_in_Guapi_It_is_necessary_to_develop_a_strong_missionary_conscience |access-date=2022-12-16 |website=fides.org}}</ref> with a rich local history. Combining both aspects, and most prominent in local tradition, Guapireños have been building ''balsadas'' – handcrafted two-tiered boats decorated with native materials – every December, as part of two ceremonies related to [[Christmas in Colombia|Christmas]], for many generations. A Guapireño described the tradition as "a cultural manifestation that has been preserved for centuries and that is linked with the black community's history of resistance".<ref name=":0" />

The ''balsadas'' traditionally honor the [[Virgin Mary]], the town's [[patron saint]], and are floated down the [[Guapi River]] on the nights of both 7 December ([[Day of the Little Candles]]) and [[Christmas Eve]]. Over three days at the start of December, families in the community build the ''balsadas'', which consist of large canoes, then wooden rafts with two levels constructed on top of the canoes. Local yellow [[Elaeis oleifera|corozo]] leaves are used for the roof and for decoration, carefully looped into patterns. Other decorations are less natural, including balloons and lights; these are used to make the rafts appear blue and white, the colors of the town's flag. The principal decoration is an image of the Virgin Mary.<ref name=":0" /> Afro-Pacific music is performed on the ''balsadas'' when they are floated, and occupants often dance; the festivities have expanded to include pyrotechnics as the ''balsadas'' approach the town square. Thousands of [[coconut]]s set alight meet the ''balsadas'' in the river at the square, where the image of the Virgin Mary is carried from the ''balsadas'' to the church ahead of the next day's [[Mass (liturgy)|Mass]] of the [[Feast of the Immaculate Conception]].<ref name=":0" />

Residents identify strongly with the ''balsadas'', which unites them both as a community activity and as a unique traditional activity pertaining to Guapi. In the 21st century, a large tourist ''balsada'' was added to the procession, with visitors able to pay to ride on it. This, and the protected heritage status of the ''balsadas'', have been points of contention among the resident population. Some feel that being named cultural heritage and the resulting extra funding would help to protect the tradition, while others worry that the increased awareness will further commercialise it.<ref name=":0" /> The associated music was inscribed as [[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists|UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage]] in 2015, with the ''balsadas'' highlighted in the listing.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Balsadas en Guapi - lullabies and songs of the South Pacific Colombian, Colombia |url=https://ich.unesco.org/en/photo-pop-up-00973?photoID=09660|access-date=2022-12-16 |website=ich.unesco.org}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2015-12-10 |title=Marimba music, traditional chants and dances from the Colombia South Pacific region and Esmeraldas Province of Ecuador - intangible heritage - Culture Sector - UNESCO |url=http://www.unesco.org/culture/ich/en/RL/marimba-music-traditional-chants-and-dances-from-the-colombia-south-pacific-region-and-esmeraldas-province-of-ecuador-01099 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151210233106/http://www.unesco.org/culture/ich/en/RL/marimba-music-traditional-chants-and-dances-from-the-colombia-south-pacific-region-and-esmeraldas-province-of-ecuador-01099 |archive-date=10 December 2015 |access-date=2022-12-16 |website=UNESCO ICH}}</ref>

== Religion ==
Guapi is largely Catholic. The [[Apostolic Vicariate of Guapi]] is located in Guapi. Cardinal [[Fernando Filoni]] has visited at the Vicariate, where in 2016 he called on Colombians to embrace peace among the community and the wider country. At the time, the church (as organised religion) in the town was identified as in development, seeking clergy, with the visit also forming an appeal to local people to become more materially involved with the church.<ref name=":1" />

==Climate==
Like all of the Pacific Coast of Colombia, Guapi has a hot, oppressively humid, cloudy and very wet [[tropical rainforest climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] ''Af''). Though it is primarily served by the Guapi River, the Nape River and San Francisco River also run through the area. In November 2015, the Nape and San Francisco rivers significantly flooded Guapi, affecting 1,662 families. People were evacuated to Chuare, three hours upstream, despite this town having suffered a landslide due to the floods.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dennis |first=Claire |date=2015-11-09 |title=Floods in west Colombia leave 6000 homes damaged |url=https://colombiareports.com/floods-in-west-colombia-leave-6000-homes-damaged/ |access-date=2022-12-16 |website=Colombia News {{!}} Colombia Reports |language=en-US}}</ref>
