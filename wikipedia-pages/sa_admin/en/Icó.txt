'''Icó''' ({{IPA|pt|iˈkɔ}}) is a town in [[Ceará]], [[Brazil]], located in the Microrregião of Salgado in the central part of the state. Icó borders the municipalities of [[Umari, Ceará|Umari]], [[Cedro, Ceará|Cedro]], [[Iguatu]], [[Orós]], [[Jaguaribe]], [[Pereiro, Ceará|Pereiro]], and the states of [[Rio Grande do Norte]] and [[Paraíba]]. The seat of the town, also called Icó, is located {{convert|375|km|mi}} from the state capital, [[Fortaleza]]. The municipality covers {{convert|1,872|km2|mi2|abbr=on}} and has a population of 68,162 with a population density of 36 inhabitants per square kilometer. It is located {{convert|1,872|km|mi|abbr=on}} from the state capital of Sergipe, [[Aracaju]].<ref name="c"/><ref name="i"/>

Icó sits on a flat plain on the right bank of the [[Salgado River (Ceará)|Salgado River]], a short distance from its confluence with the [[Jaguaribe River|Jaguaribe]].<ref name="c"/>

==History==

The greater area of central Ceará was explored in the 17th century with the town being founded in 1738. Icó reached its greatest point of  development in the 18th century as a commercial center connecting the hinterlands of the sertão and the coast. Its development declined due to successive droughts and a domestic cattle market centered on the south and southeastern provinces of Brazil. This process was accentuated by the construction of the [[Baturité Railroad]] in 1870, which left Icó on the fringe of regional trade.<ref name="c"/>

In the mid-19th century the "Teatro da Ribeira dos Icós" was built. The town also has a historic church.<ref name="c"/>
