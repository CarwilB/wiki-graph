'''Guánica''' ({{IPA|es|ˈɡwanika}}, {{IPA|es|ˈwanika|local}}) is a [[Guánica barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] in southern [[Puerto Rico]], bordering the Caribbean Sea, south of [[Sabana Grande, Puerto Rico|Sabana Grande]], east of [[Lajas, Puerto Rico|Lajas]], and west of [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]]. It is part of the [[Yauco metropolitan area|Yauco metropolitan statistical area]].

[[Guánica barrio-pueblo]], also known as [[Pueblos in Puerto Rico|Pueblo de Guánica]], is the downtown of the municipality. Guánica's population in 2020 was 13,787 people. Guánica is located on a deeply indented [[Ensenada de Guánica|harbor]] of the same name. The harbor resembles a tropical [[fjord]], narrow and bordered by rugged hills, barely a quarter-mile wide, but about {{convert|2|mi|spell=in}} from mouth to the town. The town is about {{convert|100|mi|-1}} and over two hours' driving distance from [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], and about {{convert|20|mi|-1}} west of [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]].

Guánica's postal ZIP Code is 00653 and telephone area codes are 787 and 939. The urban settlement of [[Ensenada, Guánica, Puerto Rico|Ensenada]] has a separate postal ZIP Code of 00647.

==History==
===Settlement===
[[Juan Ponce de León]] landed in the [[Ensenada de Guánica|Guánica harbor]] on August 12, 1508, and founded a town called '''[[Guaynia|Guaynía]]''', a word derived from the [[Taíno language|Taíno]] language that is popularly said to mean "''Here is a place with water''". The town, considered the first capital of the island of Puerto Rico (which was at that time named ''Isla de San Juan Bautista''), was destroyed during the indigenous uprising of 1511, and the area was abandoned by Europeans for some years, during which time [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] (itself at first called Puerto Rico) became the capital of the island.

The Spanish overseas province of Puerto Rico was ceded by a decadent imperial Spain in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]]. Thus, Puerto Rico became a colony of the ascendant United States Empire. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Guánica was 2,700.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n256 165]|language=es|access-date=2020-04-18|archive-date=2012-11-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121115044918/http://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|url-status=live}}</ref> The re-founded town of Guánica was at first a barrio of the municipality of [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]] until Guánica was established as a separate municipality on March 13, 1914. Víctor Ángel Sallaberry Safini was Guánica's first mayor.

===Invasion===
{{main|Puerto Rico campaign}}
[[Image:Christy - The Landing at Guanica.jpg|thumb|American troops landing at Guánica in 1898, by [[Howard Chandler Christy]]|left]]
On July 25, 1898, American forces (who included the young poet-writer [[Carl Sandburg]] led by General [[Nelson A. Miles]]) landed in Guánica as part of the course of the [[Puerto Rico campaign]] in the [[Spanish–American War]]. This invasion led to Puerto Rico being acquired by the United States. The invasion, just one small part of the war between Spain and United States, occurred in Guánica due to its sheltered harbor and proximity to [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]], besides being such an unexpected site for such an attack, which had been anticipated at the heavily fortified city of [[Old San Juan|San Juan]]. The ''Gloucester'' was the first ship to set anchor in the [[Ensenada de Guánica|Bay of Guánica]]. Twenty-eight sailors and [[United States Marine Corps|Marines]], under the command of lieutenants H. P. Huse and Wood, departed from the ship on rafts and landed on the beach. The Marines lowered the [[Flag of Spain|Spanish flag]] from the beach flagpole and replaced it with the [[American flag]]. They then proceeded to set up a machine gun nest and placed [[barbed wire]] around their perimeter. The first land skirmish in Puerto Rico between the Puerto Rican militia and the American forces occurred when Lt. Méndez López and his men attacked and opened fire on the Americans. During the small battle which followed, the Americans returned fire with their machine gun and the ''Gloucester'' began to bombard the Spanish position. Lt. Méndez López and three of his men were wounded and the militia unit was forced to retreat to the town of [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]].<ref name="AAMPR">{{cite web|access-date=2008-08-02|url=http://www.spanamwar.com/puertoland2.htm|last=Barnes|first=Mark R.|title=The American Army Moves on Puerto-Rico, Part 2|work=War in Puerto Rico|publisher=Spanish American War Centennial Website|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080724113938/http://spanamwar.com/puertoland2.htm|archive-date=2008-07-24|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="EDG">{{cite web|access-date=2008-08-02|url=http://home.coqui.net/sarrasin/desembarco.guanica.htm|title=El desembarco en Guánica|work=1898 La Guerra Hispano Americana en Puerto Rico|language=es|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100421161340/http://home.coqui.net/sarrasin/desembarco.guanica.htm|archive-date=2010-04-21|url-status=live}}</ref> The invasion is commemorated by a contentious monument on the waterfront: along a broad paseo (''el malecón''), there is a large coral boulder known as the [[Guánica Rock]] (''Piedra de Guánica'') marked by the carved words, ''"3rd Battalion, 1st U.S.V. Engineers, September 16, 1898."'' July 25 was subsequently commemorated in Puerto Rico as Occupation Day, later renamed Constitution Day (see [[Public holidays in Puerto Rico]]).[[Image:Marinos.glaucester.jpg|thumb|Gloucester landing team|center]]

===Today===
[[File:Guanica Rock.jpg|right|200px|thumb|[[Guánica Rock|Rock in Guánica]] which indicates where Major General [[Nelson A. Miles]] and his men landed]]
Guánica is a modern town that maintains roots and connections to a traditional past. Known as ''el pueblo de la amistad'' ('the town of friendship'), it is also occasionally referred to as ''el pueblo de las doce calles'' ('the town of the twelve streets'). The [[Guánica barrio-pueblo|central part of town]] consists of five streets running north–south crossing seven other streets that run east–west, resulting in a compact grid of 24 square blocks, one of which is the main town square. Facing the square are the Catholic church, city hall, a school, and many shops; the plaza itself contains greenery, walks, and a music stand. In recent years this central area of the twelve streets has been extensively supplemented by suburbs in the south and west. Hills surround the town and harbor, including the {{convert|450|ft|adj=on}} hill to the east of town, itself topped by the tiny [[Fort Caprón (Guánica, Puerto Rico)|Fort Caprón]]. Two large factories, one producing fertilizer, partially distract the eye from the pleasant landscape, but both have been important to the economy of the town, at one time dominated by the sugar plantations of [[Central Guánica]]. The resort chain known as [[Club Med]] once attempted to set up a luxury resort on beaches east of the town but withdrew due to local opposition which was apprehensive about both environmental and community degradation. East of the town some {{convert|200|acre|1}} of land, including {{convert|3|mi|spell=in}} of beach, have been intermittently for sale. It is a fishing village; commercial fishermen still ply their traditional trade beyond the harbor entrance. [[Copamarina Beach Resort & Spa]] offers beach access and a short boat ride to [[Gilligan Island]], the westernmost key of the [[Cayos de Caña Gorda]], which is a great spot to go [[snorkeling]]. The water is crystal clear and shallow, in which old pieces of coral and fish can be seen. People can walk or swim from one island to another.

On September 20, 2017, [[Hurricane Maria]] struck the island of Puerto Rico.  In Guánica, 951 homes were damaged or destroyed.<ref>{{cite news| title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. ==Geography==
[[File:Guayacán Centenario, Guánica, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|{{lang|es|Guayacán Centenario}}]]
Guánica has mountains and a dry forest. The [[Guánica State Forest]] (''Bosque Estatal de Guánica'') is also the name of a [[Puerto Rican dry forests|dry forest reserve]] east and west of the town, the largest remaining tract of tropical dry coastal forest in the world and designated an international [[Man and the Biosphere Programme|Biosphere Reserve]] in 1981. The park comprising much of the dry forest is known as ''Bosque Seco de Guánica''.<ref name="Caña Gorda">{{cite web |title=Balneario Caña Gorda |url=http://www.drdpuertorico.com/parquesnacionales/balnearios/balneario-cana-gorda/ |website=drdpuertorico |language=es |publisher=Programa de Parques Nacionales de Puerto Rico |access-date=13 February 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190213135419/http://www.drdpuertorico.com/parquesnacionales/balnearios/balneario-cana-gorda/ |archive-date=13 February 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/guanica-municipality/|title=Guánica Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190829110518/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/guanica-municipality/|archive-date=2019-08-29|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Puerto Rico Highway 116|Highway Route 116]], the nearest principal road, heads west toward [[Lajas, Puerto Rico|Lajas]] and east toward [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]], passing through the island of Puerto Rico's driest area. The largely [[intact forest landscape|intact forest]] of the Guánica Dry Forest reserve hosts the greatest number of bird species found on the island, including several bird species seldom found anywhere else: the [[Puerto Rican lizard cuckoo]], [[Puerto Rican woodpecker]], the [[Puerto Rican nightjar]], and the [[Puerto Rican emerald]] hummingbird. Other animals thought to be extinct in Puerto Rico have turned up in this forest. Many different types of cacti grow here, a stunning contrast to the lush [[El Yunque National Forest|Caribbean National Forest]] in the northeast part of the island, which is a [[tropical rainforest]]. The contrast is due to the mountain ridge ''[[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]]'' which separates Guánica from the northeast part of the island; while the northeast receives over {{convert|100|in}} of precipitation each year, Guánica receives less than 30, and some regions of the forest reserve are said to receive only six inches.

The forest reserve of some {{convert|9500|acre|km2|0}} contains {{convert|36|mi|0}} of trails through four different forest types: [[deciduous]] trees, a coastal region with tree-size [[Asclepias|milkweed]] and {{convert|9|ft|m|adj=mid|-tall|spell=in}} [[Opuntia|prickly pear cactus]], a [[mahogany]] forest, and twisted [[Bursera simaruba|gumbo limbo trees]]. There are about 700 varieties of plants, including ''aroma'' ([[acacia]]) and ''[[Guaiacum officinale|guayacan]]'' (''Lignum vitae''—Latin for 'wood of life'). One ''guayacan'' is about 500–700 years old. The squat melon cactus and other cacti can be found here along with 40 species of birds, including the ''guabairo'' ([[Puerto Rican nightjar]]), found nowhere else. Also found in the area are the Puerto Rico crested toad (''[[Puerto Rican crested toad|Peltophryne lemur]]'') and, sometimes on the beaches, [[Green sea turtle|green]] and [[Leatherback sea turtle|leatherback turtles]], though their eggs suffer severe predation from [[mongoose]]s one time introduced to fight [[rat]]s in [[sugarcane]] fields.

==Culture==
=== Festivals and events ===
Guánica celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in July. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de Santiago Apostol}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /> The festival has featured live performances by well-known artists such as [[Cano Estremera]], [[Tito Rojas]], and [[Arcángel]].<ref name="Fiestas">{{cite web |title=Fiestas Tradicionales Guánica |url=https://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/07/580260_567716893267666_318364391_n.jpg?w=869 |website=sondeaquiprnet |publisher=El Gobierno Municipal de Guánica |access-date=14 August 2023 |archive-date=May 17, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200517165705/https://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/07/580260_567716893267666_318364391_n.jpg?w=869 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Guánica include:
*Fish Festival – April
*July 25 Parade – July

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
Like all municipalities in [[Puerto Rico]], Guánica is administered by a mayor. The current mayor is [[Ismael (Titi) Rodríguez Ramos]], from the [[Popular Democratic Party (Puerto Rico)|Popular Democratic Party (PPD)]]. Rodríguez was first elected in the [[2020 Puerto Rican general election|2020 general election]] after a close race with Santos Seda (Papichy) from the [[New Progressive Party (Puerto Rico)|New Progressive Party (PNP)]]. While both candidates received almost the same share of the vote, 2,000 ballots where write in votes mostly for Edgardo Cruz Vélez an independent candidate.<ref>{{Cite web|title=CEE Event|url=http://elecciones2020.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_93/index.html#es/default/ALCALDES_Guanica.xml|access-date=2021-05-26|website=elecciones2020.ceepur.org|archive-date=November 12, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201112031747/https://elecciones2020.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_93/index.html#es/default/ALCALDES_Guanica.xml|url-status=live}}</ref> This resulted in a vote recount and while the initial results signaled Vélez was the victor, after weeks of counting, Rodríguez was declared the winner.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-01-14|title=Inicia con polémica el recuento de votos de la contienda por la alcaldía de Guánica|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/politica/notas/inicia-con-polemica-el-recuento-de-votos-de-la-contienda-por-la-alcaldia-de-guanica/|access-date=2021-05-26|website=El Nuevo Día|language=spanish|archive-date=May 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526012328/https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/politica/notas/inicia-con-polemica-el-recuento-de-votos-de-la-contienda-por-la-alcaldia-de-guanica/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=2020-12-17|title=La alcaldía de Guánica queda en manos del candidato del PPD, Ismael Rodríguez Ramos|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/politica/notas/la-alcaldia-de-guanica-queda-en-manos-del-candidato-del-ppd-ismael-rodriguez-ramos/|access-date=2021-05-26|website=El Nuevo Día|language=spanish|archive-date=December 18, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201218233631/https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/politica/notas/la-alcaldia-de-guanica-queda-en-manos-del-candidato-del-ppd-ismael-rodriguez-ramos/|url-status=live}}</ref> Vélez initially conceded the race but then petitioned a court to adjudge early voting ballots to his count. This court case and subsequent court appeal where both dismissed.<ref>{{Cite web|last=NotiCel|title=Tribunal vuelve a fallar en contra del candidato por nominación directa en Guánica, Edgardo Cruz|url=https://www.noticel.com/ahora/20210517/tribunal-vuelve-a-fallar-en-contra-del-candidato-por-nominacion-directa-en-guanica-edgardo-cruz/|access-date=2021-05-26|website=www.noticel.com|language=en-US|archive-date=May 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526012327/https://www.noticel.com/ahora/20210517/tribunal-vuelve-a-fallar-en-contra-del-candidato-por-nominacion-directa-en-guanica-edgardo-cruz/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-05-17|title=Desestiman pleito de Edgardo Cruz, candidato por nominación directa a la alcaldía de Guánica|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/tribunales/notas/desestiman-pleito-de-edgardo-cruz-candidato-por-nominacion-directa-a-la-alcaldia-de-guanica/|access-date=2021-05-26|website=El Nuevo Día|language=es|archive-date=May 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526012329/https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/tribunales/notas/desestiman-pleito-de-edgardo-cruz-candidato-por-nominacion-directa-a-la-alcaldia-de-guanica/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Flag===
The five waves, blue and yellow represent the Guánica Bay, a fragment of the Official Shield of Guánica.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=GUANICA | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/GUANICA.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001305/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/GUANICA.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
Shield divided in four quarters. In the superior right side, it has a "bohío" (a [[Taíno|native]] hut) under a crown that represents Cacique [[Agüeybaná II|Agüeybaná]], whose yucayeque (Indian territory) was in this region. In the superior left quarter, a lion representing [[Juan Ponce de León]]. The red and yellow checkered strips over a silver-plated background in the inferior right side represent the shields of Don [[Cristóbal de Sotomayor]], founder of the town of '''Tavara''', the actual location of Guánica. The waved stripes represent the bay of this town. The branches surrounding the shield represent the [[sugarcane]] industry that was very important in this region.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />
