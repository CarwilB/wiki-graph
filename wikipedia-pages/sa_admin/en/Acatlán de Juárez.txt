''' Acatlán de Juárez  ''' is a town and [[municipalities of Jalisco|municipality]], in [[Jalisco]] in central-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of  154&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 22,540.<ref name="Enciclopedia">{{cite web|title=Acatlán de Juárez |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14002a.htm |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |language=es |access-date=April 13, 2009 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070528055604/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14002a.htm |archive-date=May 28, 2007 }}</ref>

==History==
The first settlers in the region were members of a tribe that was established in [[Cocula, Jalisco|Cocula]], They were defeated by the [[Purépecha people|Purepecha]] in 1509.

In the year of 1550 the area for the first time attained the first level of a municipality, under the power of the viceroy of the [[New Spain]], [[Antonio de Mendoza]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> During his rule he encountered discontent with people in [[Zacoalco de Torres]], [[Ahualulco de Mercado|Ahualulco]] and [[Ameca, Jalisco|Ameca]]. In the first half of the 17th century the Augustinians built a temple dedicated to Santa Ana, known today as the "Parroquia de Santa Ana".

In 1825 the area fell under the canton of [[Sayula, Jalisco|Sayula]]. In 1858, [[Benito Juárez]], in his journey through these lands, stayed at the inn in the town of Acatlán de Juárez, owned by Miguel Gomez. During his short stay, he was killed at the hands of the conservatives, and died saving the pastor of the place, Meliton Gutierrez Vargas.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> On March 22, 1906, by decree 1158, the name of the earlier village changed its name from Santa Ana Acatlan to Acaltán de Juarez,  and acquired the title of a town.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

==Geography==
Acatlán de Juárez is located in the centre of [[Jalisco]] state between the coordinates 20° 14'30" north latitude and 103° 32'30" west longitude at an altitude of 1,393 metres above sea level.

The municipality is bordered on the north by the municipalities of [[Tala, Jalisco|Tala]] and [[Tlajomulco de Zuñiga]], to the east by the municipalities of [[Tlajomulco de Zuñiga]], [[Jocotepec]] and [[Zacoalco de Torres]] to the south with the municipalities of Zacoalco de Torres and [[Villa Corona]], and to the west by the municipalities of Villa Corona and Tala.

Approximately 57% of the land area is flat, especially in the middle east, west and south of the municipality with its characteristic valleys. The primary agricultural areas at some 39% are located to the north and east, with altitudes of 1,400 and 1,500 m. There are some hilly areas accounting for just 4% of the land area which lies to the south-east and north-west at altitudes that reach the 2.200 m.

The municipality belongs to the hydrological basin known as Lerma-Chapala-Santiago.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://mapserver.inegi.gob.mx/geografia/espanol/estados/jal/rh.cfm?c=444&e=14|title=Mapa de Regiones Hidrológicas|publisher=INEGI|year=2008|language=es|access-date=May 10, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090612111756/http://mapserver.inegi.gob.mx/geografia/espanol/estados/jal/rh.cfm?c=444&e=14|archive-date=2009-06-12|url-status=dead}}</ref>  Its water resources are provided by the [[Acatlán River]] and the springs El Cajón del Muerto and Charco Verde.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>  In addition, several small streams flow in the rainy season,  the Hurtado, Presa Chica and Bordo de San Gerardo.

The climate is dry with a dry winter and spring, semi-warm in the winter season. The average annual temperature is 20.5&nbsp;°C, with an average annual rainfall of 714.7&nbsp;mm and can reach up to 1,100, with the heaviest rainfall in July and August. Prevailing winds flow in the easterly direction.

==Economy==
Agriculture is the primary activity in the municipality, with crops such as [[sugarcane]], [[maize]], [[bean]]s, [[peanut]]s, [[sunflower]] and [[tomato]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> Livestock is also a main economic activity with the rearing of cattle, swine, sheep, goats, horses, rabbits and hives. Sugar and alcohol production are of note.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> The town has a wide variety of commercial establishments and provides services to tourists.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

==Government==
The form of government is [[democracy|democratic]] under the [[Jalisco|state]] and [[Mexico|Federal]] elections, which are held every three years. At the municipal level, the mayor and her/his council are elected. The current municipal president is Jaime Enrique Velasco, from the [[Citizens' Movement (Mexico)|Citizens' Movement]] party. He was elected in the election of 6 June 2021.

The municipality has 27 villages, the most important ones are: Acatlán de Juárez (town), Bellavista, El Plan, San José de los Pozos, San Pedro Valencia, and Villa de los Niños.

Much of the architecture in the municipality is stone work dating back to the 1850s. The town contains the "Benito Juárez" arts and crafts museum. A religious festival is celebrated on July 26 n honor of [[St. Anne]], the patron saint of the city.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> It is customary on December 11 each year to also light bonfires at night along the streets, to commemorate the vision of [[Our Lady of Guadalupe]] to [[Juan Diego]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

Local cuisine includes the Birria goat, Carnitas beef, pork and beans and sweets known as Encalada flour fritters and ponteduro.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> [[Sugar cane juice|Guarapo]], a fruit cane juice and fruit punch are common beverages.
