'''Cupey''' is one of the 18 [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrios]] of the municipality of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], [[Puerto Rico]] located in the mountainous area of the [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]]. It is the largest barrio or district in San Juan and the third most populous with 32,833 inhabitants according to the [[2020 United States census|2020 US Census]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Census profile: Cupey barrio, San Juan Municipio, PR |url=http://censusreporter.org/profiles/06000US7212722847-cupey-barrio-san-juan-municipio-pr/ |access-date=2023-09-08 |website=Census Reporter |language=en}}</ref> The territorial land area of Cupey is 7.49 square miles (19.40&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>). It is bound by the municipality of [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]] to the South, by the municipality of [[Trujillo Alto, Puerto Rico|Trujillo Alto]] to the East, by the barrios of [[Caimito, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Caimito]] and [[Monacillo, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Monacillo]] to the West, and by the barrios of [[El Cinco, San Juan, Puerto Rico|El Cinco]] and [[Sabana Llana Sur, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Sabana Llana Sur]] to the North. Between 1990 and 2000, Cupey had a 17.98% increase in population, more than any other barrio in San Juan.

==History==
Established in 1878, this barrio was a former ward of the now defunct town of [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]].<ref name="Puerto Rico Office of Historian 1949">{{cite book | author=Puerto Rico. Office of Historian | title=Tesauro de datos historicos: indice compendioso de la literatura histórica de Puerto Rico, incluyendo algunos datos inéditos, periodísticos y cartográficos | publisher=Impr. del Gobierno de Puerto Rico|issue=v. 2 | year=1949 | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA306 | language=es | access-date=4 January 2020 | page=306}}</ref> It was divided into the "subbarrios" of Cupey Alto and Cupey Bajo. It is named for the Cupey tree, sometimes spelled copey (''[[Clusia rosea]]''), which is indigenous to the [[Caribbean]]. It belongs to the family [[Clusiaceae]]. Linguistically, ''cupey'' or ''copey'' most likely come from the [[Taíno language|Taino language]] (''cubey'') and might or might not share a lexical relation to the name [[Cuba]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Dictionary of the Taino Language|url=https://www.taino-tribe.org/terms1.htm|access-date=2021-10-14|website=www.taino-tribe.org}}</ref>

Cupey was in Spain's [[gazetteer]]s<ref>{{cite web | title=Anuario del comercio, de la industria, de la magistratura y de la administración. 1881 | website=Biblioteca Nacional de España | url=https://hemerotecadigital.bne.es/hd/es/viewer?id=9047075f-3c7e-411c-9e1e-41a2f650e1ba&page=1587 | language=es |page=1614| access-date=4 April 2023}}</ref> until Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became an [[Insular area|unincorporated territory of the United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Cupey barrio was 1,834.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. In 1956, the Puerto Rico Legislature integrated the town of Río Piedras and the town of San Juan. Today, Cupey is the largest [[Barrios of San Juan, Puerto Rico|barrio in San Juan]] in area.

==Landmarks==
The [[Interamerican University]] of Puerto Rico's Metropolitan Campus, and the [[Metropolitan University (Puerto Rico)|Metropolitan University]], a unit of the [[Ana G. Méndez University System]], are located in Cupey.

TV personality and astrologer, [[Walter Mercado]], is buried at Señorial Memorial Park in Cupey.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.etonline.com/walter-mercado-laid-to-rest-in-puerto-rico-135937|title=Walter Mercado Laid to Rest in Puerto Rico|website=Entertainment Tonight}}</ref>

==Geography==
Cupey barrio is 7.49 square miles (19.40&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>). It is bound to the north by the San Juan barrios of [[El Cinco, San Juan, Puerto Rico|El Cinco]] along State Road [[Puerto Rico Highway 176|PR-176]], and by [[Sabana Llana Sur, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Sabana Llana Sur]] in the Venus Gardens neighborhood. From the south it is bound by the municipality of [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]], starting at the intersection of state roads [[Puerto Rico Highway 176|PR-176]] and [[Puerto Rico Highway 175|PR-175]]. From the east it is bound by the town of [[Trujillo Alto, Puerto Rico|Trujillo Alto]] along state road [[Puerto Rico Highway 199|PR-199]]. From the west it is bordered by the San Juan wards of [[Caimito, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Caimito]], starting at the intersection of state road [[Puerto Rico Highway 199|PR-199]] and state highway [[Puerto Rico Highway 52|PR-52]], and by [[Monacillo, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Monacillo]].

San Juan's only lake, [[Las Curias Lake|Lago Las Curias]], is located in Cupey. It was originally dammed in 1946 by the Municipal Government of San Juan to provide potable water to the cities of [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]] and San Juan and was later transferred to the [[Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority]] (PREPA). it is currently owned by the [[Puerto Rico Aqueducts and Sewers Authority|Puerto Rico Aqueducts and Sewer Authority]] (PRASA). The [[Embankment dam|earthen dam]] is approximately 75 feet high and 800 feet long and its design capacity is 1,120 acres-feet.<ref>{{cite web|title=REPRESA LAS CURÍAS - Wikimapia|url=http://wikimapia.org/27026331/es/REPRESA-LAS-CUR%C3%8DAS|publisher=Wikimapia.org|accessdate=16 August 2016}}</ref>
