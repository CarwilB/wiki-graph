'''Amalfi''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Colombia|municipality]] of the [[Colombian Andes]], northern part of the [[Cordillera Central (Colombia)|Central Mountain Range]] in the [[Antioquia Department]] and part of the subregion of [[Northeastern Antioquia]]. The territory of Amalfi is bordered by the municipalities of [[Anorí]] and [[Segovia, Antioquia|Segovia]] at the north; Segovia, [[Remedios, Antioquia|Remedios]] and [[Vegachí]] at the east; Vegachí, [[Yalí]], [[Yolombó]] and [[Gómez Plata]] at the south and Anorí and [[Guadalupe, Antioquia|Guadalupe]] at the west. The town is served by [[Amalfi Airport (Colombia)|Amalfi Airport]].

It has an extension of 91 square miles, making it one of the biggest Antioquean municipalities. As of 2015, it holds a population of 22,088 inhabitants.<ref>DANE 2015</ref> The [[county seat]] has a population of 11,481 inhabitants<ref>DANE 2009</ref> and it is located in La Víbora Valley, a mountainous region that is rich in gold and water reserves. 

These resources attracted settlers during the late 19th century. They came from other Antioquean regions, especially Copacabana, Rionegro, La Ceja, Santa Fe de Antioquia, Yarumal and Santa Rosa de Osos, as well as migrants from countries such as Spain, Syria, Lebanon, Morocco, China, Germany, Sweden, Finland and West African countries like Nigeria. 

The region held the native communities of the Yamesíes, Guamocoes and Tahamíes. None of their original culture remains, but traces can be found in local expressions, geographical names, the people's connection with the jaguar and the [[Petroglyph|petroglyphs]] that can be found nearby.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=PROCOLOMBIA |date=June 23, 2026 |title=Amalfi |url=https://colombia.travel/en/medellin/amalfi}}</ref>

The municipality is identified with the Tiger of Amalfi. This is due to a historical event that took place in 1949 when some locals hunted, what they believed to be a [[Bengal Tiger|Bengal Tiger,]] that was eating thousands of the townspeople's cattle. The tiger was later discovered to be a [[jaguar]], however the legend held the position of tiger, which led the inhabitants to be known as the ''Tigers of Amalfi''.<ref name=":0" />

== Toponymy ==

The name Amalfi in Colombia honors the Italian city of Amalfi of Salerno. There are two competing hypotheses for the name of this city:

# Derivation from the name ''Melfi'', a commune of the [[Vulture, Italy|Vulture]] area in the [[Province of Potenza]], southern [[Italy]], at the foot of the [[Vulture Mountains|Vulture Mountain]]. Today, Melfi is a commercial and touristic area.
# A [[Roman gens]] of the 1st century that was a family consisting of all those individuals who shared the same nomen and claimed descent from a common ancestor in ancient Rome. The Roman gens of Amalfi created this town at the Adriatic coast in 596.<ref>Giuseppe Gargano. Origini e Storia di Amalfi. Comune di Amalfi. == History ==

=== Pre-Columbian time ===

The position of Amalfi between the last edges of the Andes to its north and the slopes to the Caribbean region, made it a human corridor of ancient migrations. [[Native Americans (Americas)|Native Americans]] were present in what is today modern Colombia since about 15 thousand years ago. In the region of the municipality of Amalfi there are archeological traces dated 9 thousand years ago related to the cultures of [[Yamesíes]], [[Guamocoes]] and [[Tahamíes]].<ref name=Álvarez>Álvarez, Manuel José (1993). Amalfi 1987. Ed. Vieco y Cía, Amalfi, 1987. Segunda edición agosto de 1993.</ref> Although those peoples are considered today extinct, their descendants survived in the [[mestizo]] population of the region, as well as ancient traditions, names, believes and myths like the [[Jaguar]] cult, the [[petroglyphs]], words and other elements that are object of current anthropological and archaeological studies.

=== Land of pioneers ===

The territory of the modern municipality of Amalfi was not of much interest for the Spaniards during the three centuries of colonial rule (16th to 18th centuries) as it was in neighbouring regions such as Segovia, Remedios and Yolombó, attracted by their rich gold mines.

In 1580 the Spaniard conqueror of [[Antioquia Department|Antioquia]], [[Gaspar de Rodas|Don Gaspar de Rodas]], made an excursion to the region following the [[Porce River|Porce]] and [[Nechí River|Nechi]] rivers, but he did not make Spanish foundations.<ref name=Álvarez/> He would established the town of [[Zaragoza, Antioquia|Zaragoza]] in 1581, while searching for a connection with the [[Magdalena River]].

During the 18th century groups of traders from [[Cartagena de Indias]], [[Santa Marta]] and [[Santa Cruz de Mompox|Mompox]] settled on the [[Bajo Cauca Antioquia|Lower Cauca of Antioquia]] searching for gold and founded the first town in the today Amalfi territory known as ''Cancán'' that is today known as ''Corregimiento de Portachuelo''. They built a Catholic church and some persons lived there, depending their administration from Zaragoza, but it was abandoned few years later.<ref name=Álvarez/>

After the [[Independence of Antioquia]], General Julián Trujillo, acting governor, tried to recover the old foundation under the name of ''San Martín'' town, but it did not attract enough settlers and was abandoned in 1888.

During the ''[[Antioquia Department#History|Antioquean Colonization]]'', families from [[Rionegro]], [[La Ceja, Antioquia|La Ceja]] and other towns of the [[Eastern Antioquia]], [[Medellín]], [[Copacabana (Colombia)|Copacabana]], [[Yarumal]], [[Santa Rosa de Osos]] and [[Santa Fe de Antioquia]], migrated to the region in search of gold and other opportunities. Among these first Antioquean settlers, came also foreigners, especially [[German Jews]], [[Arabs]] from the [[Middle East]], [[Chinese Colombians|Chinese]] that were working on the Antioquean Rail Constructions and [[Nordic countries|Nordic Europeans]] ([[Norway]]).

=== Official foundation ===

In 1838 Reverend Father José Santamaría y Zola, a Spaniard Catholic priest from [[Málaga]], [[Spain]] that was living in Copacabana, led an expedition of families that were looking for new opportunities around the mine golds. They founded a town at the [[Riachón River]] Valley that was concluded in 1843. The first settlers changed the name many times: Riachón, Santa Bárbara, Cueva Santa and Nueva Población.

Bishop [[Juan de la Cruz Gómez Plata]] made a visit to [[Italy]] and he got in love with the Italian town of [[Amalfi]] of [[Salerno]]. Although it was a town at the side of the [[Tyrrhenian Sea]], very different from the mountain town on the Antioquia's Andes, Bishop Gómez Plata thought that the ''blue skies'' of the Italian town resembled that of the Antioquean one. For this reason he decided to give that Italian name to the new settlement of gold miners to be called ''Amalfi-Antioquia''.

A [[Swedish emigration and emigrant communities|Swedish migrant]] into the region, engineer and geographer, [[Carlos Segismundo de Greiff]] (1793–1870), made his contribution with the design of the streets of the new town with professor Antonio Aguilar, a master plan that is preserved and admired for its symmetric distribution on the Riachón Valley. This Swedish Greiff is the first ancestor in Colombia of the Greiff family that generated remarkable characters such as poet [[León de Greiff]] (1895–1976), musician and journalist Otto de Greiff (1903–1995), politician [[Gustavo de Greiff Restrepo]] (1929–), lawyer [[Mónica de Greiff]] (1956–) and historian [[Jorge Arias de Greiff]] (1922–).
