'''Isla de la Juventud'''<ref name="cia">{{cite web |title=The World Factbook&nbsp;— Cuba |author=Directorate of Intelligence |date=August 16, 2007 |url=https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/cuba/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210110102915/https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/cuba |url-status=dead |archive-date=January 10, 2021 |access-date=September 3, 2007}}</ref> ({{IPA|es|ˈizla ðe la xuβenˈtuð}}; {{lit|Isle of Youth}}) is the second-largest [[Cuba]]n [[island]] (after Cuba's mainland) and the seventh-largest island in the [[West Indies]] (after mainland Cuba, [[Hispaniola]], [[Jamaica]], [[Puerto Rico]], [[Trinidad]], and [[Andros Island]]). The island was called the '''Isle of Pines''' ({{langx|es|Isla de Pinos}}) until 1978. It has an area {{convert|2,200|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} and is {{convert|48.4|km|0|abbr=on}} south of the island of [[Cuba]], across the [[Gulf of Batabanó]]. The island lies almost directly south of [[Havana]] and [[Pinar del Río Province|Pinar del Río]] and is a Special Municipality ({{convert|2,419|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}), not part of any [[Provinces of Cuba|province]] and is therefore administered directly by the central government of Cuba. The island has only one municipality, also named Isla de la Juventud.

The largest of the 350 islands in the [[Canarreos Archipelago]] (''Archipiélago de los Canarreos''), the island had an estimated population of 83,544 in 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Statistical Yearbook of Cuba Population 2020 Edition January-December 2019|url=http://www.onei.gob.cu/sites/default/files/03_poblacion_2019_sitio.pdf|access-date=October 7, 2020|website=oficina nacional de estadística e información}}</ref> The capital and largest city is [[Nueva Gerona]] in the north, and the second largest and oldest city is [[Santa Fe, Cuba|Santa Fe]] in the interior. Other communities include Columbia, [[La Demajagua, Isle of Youth|La Demajagua]] (formerly Santa Bárbara), Mac Kinley, Cuchilla Alta, Punta del Este, Sierra de Caballos and Sierra de Casas.{{citation needed|date=October 2023}}

== History ==
Little is known of the [[pre-Columbian]] history of the island, though a cave complex near the Punta del Este beach preserves 235 [[Cave art|ancient drawings]] made by the native population,<ref>[https://isladelajuventud-cuba.com/museums_monuments7.html Punta del Este Caves], on ''isladelajuventud-cuba.com''</ref> the [[Guanahatabey]]. The first written mention of these drawings comes from French traveller Charles Berchon.<ref>Charles Berchon, [https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k58130642/f95.item.r=grotte ''A travers Cuba. Récit de voyage descriptif et économique''], 1910, p. 91. Story of a trip he made in 1903 or 1904. Contrary to what some say, he was not shipwrecked on Isla de la Juventud (according to his own telling).</ref> The caves were collectively designed as National monument in 1979.

The island was first sighted by Europeans in 1494 during [[Christopher Columbus]]'s [[Voyages of Christopher Columbus|second voyage]] to the [[New World]]. Columbus named the island '''La Evangelista''', claiming it for Spain. The island was also known, at various points in its history, as '''Isla de Cotorras''' (Isle of Parrots) and '''Isla de Tesoros''' (Treasure Island).

[[Pirate]] activity in and around the area left its trace in [[English literature]]; notably, ''[[Treasure Island]]'' by [[Robert Louis Stevenson]], and ''[[Peter and Wendy|Peter Pan]]'' by [[J. M. Barrie]], each drawing on accounts of the island and its native and pirate inhabitants, by reflecting the long dugout [[canoe]]s that both pirates and the indigenous peoples used, and the [[American crocodile]] (''Crocodylus acutus'').

Following its defeat in the [[Spanish–American War]] and the [[Cuban War of Independence]], [[Spain]] dropped all claims to Cuba under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris (1898)|1898 Treaty of Paris]]. The [[Platt Amendment]] of 1901, which defined Cuba's boundaries for the purposes of U.S. authorities, left the U.S. position on sovereignty over the then Isla de Pinos undetermined. This led to competing claims to the island by the United States and Cuba.<ref>{{cite news |title=The Isle of Pines |url=https://query.nytimes.com/gst/abstract.html?res=F0081EFC3F5912738DDDAF0994D9415B858CF1D3 |quote=The 'secession' from Cuba of a few American residents of the Isle of Pines is an incident of which National good faith and self-respect, we think, will compel the Government at Washington to take, not approving, but adverse notice. |newspaper=[[New York Times]] |date=November 16, 1905 |access-date=November 9, 2013}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Scott |first=James Brown |date=1923 |title=The Isle of Pines |url=https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-journal-of-international-law/article/abs/isle-of-pines/759F7F87654B7D15F5F7D1D064BEC267 |journal=American Journal of International Law |language=en |volume=17 |issue=1 |pages=100–104 |doi=10.2307/2187756 |jstor=2187756 |issn=0002-9300|url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Wright |first=Quincy |date=1925 |title=The Isle of Pines Treaty |url=https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-journal-of-international-law/article/abs/isle-of-pines-treaty/48935CB8DC2495DDA571A50FE1A2F8FA |journal=American Journal of International Law |language=en |volume=19 |issue=2 |pages=340–344 |doi=10.2307/2189260 |jstor=2189260 |issn=0002-9300|url-access=subscription }}</ref> In 1907, the [[Supreme Court of the United States|U.S. Supreme Court]] decided, in ''[[Pearcy v. Stranahan]]'', that control of the island was a political decision, not a judicial one.<ref>{{cite book |first=Spencer C. |last=Tucker |title=The Encyclopedia of the Spanish-American and Philippine-American Wars |url=https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaspan13tuck_153 |url-access=limited |location=[[Santa Barbara, California]] |publisher=[[ABC-CLIO]] |year=2009 |isbn=978-1-85109-951-1 |page=[https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaspan13tuck_153/page/n563 525]}}</ref> In 1916, a pamphlet titled ''Isle of Pines: American or What?'' called for the U.S. to annex or purchase the island to settle the issue.<ref>{{cite news |title=Would Annex The Isle of Pines |url=https://query.nytimes.com/gst/abstract.html?res=FA0F14F8355B17738DDDA10994D0405B868DF1D3 |quote=The proposed purchase by the United States of the Danish West Indies for $25,000,000 has started a renewal of agitation by American residents of the Isle of Pines for its purchase or acquisition from Cuba by the United States. The Isle of Pines Chamber of Commerce has issued a pamphlet entitled "Isle of Pines: American or What?" |newspaper=New York Times |date=August 18, 1916 |access-date=November 9, 2013}}</ref>

In 1903, Cuba and the United States negotiated and signed the [[Hay-Quesada Treaty]], which recognized Cuba's sovereignty over the island. The [[U.S. Senate]] ratified this agreement on March 13, 1925, over the objections of some four hundred United States citizens and companies, who owned or controlled about 95% of the island's land.<ref name="Ireland">{{cite book |title=Boundaries, possessions, and conflicts in Central and North America and the Caribbean |page=333 |year=1941 |last=Ireland |first=Gordon |publisher=Octagon Books |location=New York}}</ref><ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=O97bWQ6N8PwC&pg=PA39& ''Cuba's Island of Dreams: Voices from the Isle of Pines and Youth'']</ref><ref>Neagle, Michael E. (2016), America's Forgotten Colony: Cuba's Isle of Pines (Cambridge University Press) - http://www.cambridge.org/us/academic/subjects/history/twentieth-century-american-history/americas-forgotten-colony-cubas-isle-pines?format=PB#tle1iZbI2eqyfs2G.97</ref>

Prior to 1976, the island was part of [[La Habana Province (1976–2010)|La Habana Province]]. With the political and administrative reorganization of Cuban provinces in 1976, the island was given the status of "special municipality"; it is administratively equivalent to a province.<ref>{{cite book |title=Encyclopedia of Cuba: People, History, Culture |date=2003 |publisher=Greenwood Press |location=Westport, Conn. |isbn=157356334X |pages=[https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaofcu0000unse/page/33 33, 41] |url-access=registration |url=https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaofcu0000unse/page/33 }}</ref>

[[Fidel Castro]] presided at a ceremony changing the name of the island from '''Isla de Pinos''' to '''Isla de la Juventud''' on 3 August 1978.<ref>{{cite news |title=Cuba has New Name for 'Treasure Island' |url=https://timesmachine.nytimes.com/timesmachine/1978/08/04/112793029.pdf |access-date=11 April 2016 |work=New York Times |date=4 August 1978}}</ref> This was the realization of a promise that he had made in 1967, when he said "Let’s call it the Isle of Youth when the youth have done something grand with their work here, when they have revolutionized the natural environment, when they see the fruits of their labor and have revolutionized society here."<ref>{{cite web |last1=Gonzalez Suarez |first1=Lisanka |title=History and present state of Isla de la Juventud Cuba |url=http://havanajournal.com/politics/entry/history-and-present-state-of-isla-de-la-juventud-cuba/ |publisher=Havana Journal Inc. |access-date=22 September 2016 |archive-date=September 23, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160923095718/http://havanajournal.com/politics/entry/history-and-present-state-of-isla-de-la-juventud-cuba/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

== Geography and economy ==
Much of the island is covered in [[Cuban pine forests|pine forests]], which is the source of the island's large [[lumber industry|wood industry]] (as well as its old name). The northern region of the island has low ridges from which [[marble]] is [[Quarry|quarried]], while the southern region is an elevated plain. [[Agriculture]] and [[fishing]] are the island's main industries, with [[citrus fruit]] and [[vegetable]]s being grown. A black sand beach was formed by volcanic activity.

The island has a mild climate but is known for frequent [[Tropical cyclone|hurricanes]]. It is a popular [[Tourism|tourist]] destination, with many [[beach]]es and [[resort]]s, including Bibijagua Beach. Until the Cuban government [[Eminent domain|expropriated]] all foreign-owned property in the early 1960s, much land was owned by Americans, and the island contained a branch of the [[Hilton Hotels]] chain.

== Demographics ==
In the 2012 official census, the Special Municipality of Isla de la Juventud had a population of 84,751. The racial makeup of the population was [[white people|white]] 50,732 (59.9%), [[mestizo]] or [[mulatto]] 26,013 (30.7%) and [[Afro-Cubans|black]] 8,006 (9.4%).
<ref name="autogenerated2" /> With a total area of {{convert|2419.27|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}},<ref name="census2002">{{cite web |url=http://www.cubagob.cu/otras_info/censo/tablas_html/ii_4.htm |title=Population by Special Municipality |author=Government of Cuba |year=2002 |access-date=October 2, 2007 |language=es |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928013913/http://www.cubagob.cu/otras_info/censo/tablas_html/ii_4.htm |archive-date=September 28, 2007 }}</ref> the Municipality had a population density of {{convert|35.8|/km2|/sqmi|abbr=on}}.

"Caimaneros", the inhabitants of the south coast of the island, are descended from settlers from the [[Cayman Islands]] and [[Jamaica]], and are [[English language|English]]-speaking Cubans.

== Transportation ==
{{see also|Autopista de la Isla de la Juventud}}
The main transportation to the island is by boat or aircraft. [[Hydrofoil]]s (kometas) and motorized [[catamaran]]s will make the journey from [[Batabanó, Cuba|Batabanó]] to [[Nueva Gerona]] in between two and three hours. A much slower and larger cargo ferry takes around six hours to make the crossing.

== Presidio Modelo ==
[[File:Presidio Modelo.JPG|thumb|Presidio Modelo in 2005]]
The [[Presidio Modelo]] was erected between 1926 and 1931. The model [[panopticon]] penitentiary is on the outskirts of Nueva Gerona.<ref name="Ireland"/> It was the place where, after leading a failed attack on the [[Moncada Barracks]] in July&nbsp;1953, [[Fidel Castro]], along with his brother [[Raúl Castro]], was imprisoned by the regime of [[Fulgencio Batista]] from 1953 to 1955.<ref>{{cite book | author = Alex von Tunzelmann |title=Red Heat: Conspiracy, Murder and the Cold War in the Caribbean |date= 2011 |publisher= Simon & Schuster}}</ref> 

Following the [[Cuban Revolution]], the same facility was used to imprison the new regime's enemies and political dissidents. They included [[Huber Matos]], an officer in the revolutionary army who attempted to resign and who said he was tortured there.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/castro-huber-matos-moderate-cuban-revolution/ |title=Huber Matos, a Moderate in the Cuban Revolution |date=December 21, 2004 |work=American Experience |publisher=PBS |access-date=January 26, 2011}}</ref> [[Armando Valladares]] wrote a memoir describing the prison's harsh conditions and cruel treatment of [[political prisoners]].<ref>Armando Valladares, Against All Hope (New York: Knopf, 1986)</ref> Presidio Modelo ceased functioning as a prison in 1967. It has been declared a national monument and its hospital rooms converted into a museum.
