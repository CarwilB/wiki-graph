'''San Juan Cotzocon''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-western [[Mexico]]. 
It is part of the [[Sierra Mixe]] district within the [[Sierra Norte de Oaxaca]] Region.

==Name==
The name "Cotzocón" or "Cozogón" means "Dark Mountain".<ref name=INF>{{cite web|title=San Juan Cotzocon|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20190a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=2010-07-22|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070528044908/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20190a.htm|archivedate=2007-05-28}}</ref>

==Geography==
The municipality covers an area of 945.4&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. The territory is rugged, with grazing and cultivation of coffee and corn practiced only the lower irregular plains. The Chiquito River runs through the northern part, a tributary of the Rio Grande. The climate is warm and humid, with rain almost all year round. The forested areas contain pine, cedar, and ceiba.<ref name=INF/>

==Population==
As of 2005, the municipality had 5,030 households with a total population of 22,478 of whom 10,712 spoke an indigenous language.
The main town is now María Lombardo de Caso, located at a height of 140 meters above sea level.
Although in a [[Mixe people|Mixe]] area, many of the people in this town are [[Mazatec people|Mazatec]] or [[Chinantec]] who moved here after being displaced by the [[Miguel Alemán Dam]] in the 1960s.<ref name=INF/>
In the 1950s the remote municipality, accessible only via dirt track, drew visitors from the USA investigating use of hallucinogenic [[psilocybin mushroom]]s in the traditional Mixe ceremonies.<ref>{{cite book

==Economy==

The main economic activity is coffee cultivation, followed by livestock raising.<ref name=INF/>
Some of the Mixe women of the village of San Juan Cotzocon use back strap looms to weave traditional [[huipil]], rebosos, napkins, table cloths and other textile crafts.<ref>{{cite web
 |url=http://www.mexicantextiles.com/grouppages/groups/mixe/cotzocon_mixe/index.htm
 |title=Cotzocon Mixe
 |work=Mexican Indigenous Textile Project
 |accessdate=2010-07-22}}</ref>
The [[Union of Indigenous Communities of the Isthmus Region]], a cooperative founded in 1982, assists in production and distribution of the local products, notably coffee, under a [[fair trade]] label.<ref>{{cite web
