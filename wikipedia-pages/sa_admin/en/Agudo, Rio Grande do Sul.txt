'''Agudo''' ({{IPA|pt|aˈɡu.du|lang|Br-Agudo.ogg}}, {{lit|acute}}) is a [[municipality]] in [[Rio Grande do Sul]], the southernmost state of [[Brazil]].

==Geography==

Agudo is 83 meters above sea level. The total area is 553,1&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> and the population was estimated at 16,041 in 2022.

The municipality contains part of the {{convert|1848|ha}} [[Quarta Colônia State Park]], created in 2005.<ref>{{citation|language=pt|ref={{harvid|Parque Estadual Quarta Colônia – SEMA}}
 |title=Parque Estadual Quarta Colônia|publisher=SEMA: Secretaria do Ambiente e Desenvolvimento Sustentável
 |url=http://www.sema.rs.gov.br/parque-estadual-quarta-colonia|access-date=2017-01-20}}</ref>
The municipality contains part of the hydroelectric [[Dona Francisca Dam]] on the upper [[Jacuí River]].<ref>{{citation|language=pt|ref={{harvid|Dona Francisca – OSAB}}|title=Dona Francisca|publisher=OSAB: Observatório Sócio-Ambiental da Baragens|url=http://www.observabarragem.ippur.ufrj.br/barragens/9/dona-francisca|access-date=2017-01-20|archive-date=2019-05-23|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190523184533/http://www.observabarragem.ippur.ufrj.br/barragens/9/dona-francisca|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:Indian Soldiers from the Coritiba Province Escorting Native Prisoners.jpg|thumb|left|A [[Guarani people|Guarani]] family captured by Indian slave hunters. A drawing by the French travelling artist Jean Baptiste Debret]]

Archeological evidence indicates that this area was settled by humankind as far back as 8,000 years ago. The first Europeans to come into the area were Jesuit priests who in the 16th century began establishing the so-called ''Reductions'' or ''[[Mission (Christian)|Missions]]'' as they also were named in the wider region (i.e. [[Brazil]], [[Argentina]] and [[Paraguay]]). At a later date with the expulsion of the [[Jesuit Order|Jesuit]] order by both the [[Spain|Spanish]] and [[Portugal|Portuguese]] crowns from [[South America]] left the area inactive as far as European activities were concerned.

The local indigenous population suffered attacks by Paulistas from the north who, amongst other things, made it their business to capture [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indians]] to be put up for sale in the [[Slavery|slave]] markets of [[São Paulo]], etc. In 1857, a new wave of [[immigration]] started to affect the region, this time attracting Germanic settlers and subsequently peoples of other European origins. The [[German language]] is still spoken by some residents of the Municipality of Agudo and in areas around it. In 2001, a [[fossil]] of a [[dinosaur]] was found in Agudos and after the analysis of its skeleton, it was reported to be a new species of [[ornithischian]] dinosaur, named [[sacisaur]] (''Sacisaurus agudoensis'') after the evidence that the skeleton missed the bones of one of its leg.
