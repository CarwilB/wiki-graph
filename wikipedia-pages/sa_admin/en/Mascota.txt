'''Mascota '''is a town and [[municipalities of Jalisco|municipality]] in [[Jalisco]], in central-western [[Mexico]], part of the  [[Región Costa Norte-Sierra Occidental, Jalisco|Costa-Sierra Occidental Region]]. The municipality covers an area of 1,843&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. The name Mascota comes from the root "Amaxacotlán", which means "place of deer and [[Colubridae|colubrids]]".<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=Jalisco - Mascota|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM14jalisco/municipios/14058a.html|access-date=2021-05-25|website=www.inafed.gob.mx|archive-date=2021-05-18|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210518080524/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM14jalisco/municipios/14058a.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The municipality has smaller villages such as Tecuani and La Plata, both a couple miles away. Mascota is surrounded by roads and pine-covered mountains and is a usual stop on the road to Talpa De Allende or Puerto Vallarta.

As of 2020, the municipality had a total population of 14,451.<ref>{{cite web|title=Mascota|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14058a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|access-date=April 16, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120403005957/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14058a.htm|archive-date=April 3, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== History ==
In prehispanic times, Mascota was the head of a ''[[cacicazgo]],'' which ruled over the populations of [[Talpa de Allende|Talpa]], El Tuito and [[Chacala]].<ref name=":0" /> In 1525, the Spanish explorer [[Francisco Cortés de San Buenaventura]] arrived to Mascota, who was commanded by [[Hernán Cortés]] to conquer the provinces north of [[Colima]], which was subsequently renamed to "Valle de Banderas".<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1">{{Cite web|last=Turismo|first=Secretaría de|title=Mascota, Jalisco|url=http://www.gob.mx/sectur/articulos/mascota-jalisco|access-date=2021-05-25|website=gob.mx|language=es}}</ref> 10 years after, in 1535, a rebellion of indigenous people was held in Mascota, along with other villages nearby.

After the time of the [[Mexican independence|Mexican Independence]], in 1824, the Department of Mascota was created and the town of Mascota was declared ''Villa de Mascota''. On 10 April 1885, ''Villa de Mascota'' received the title of city.<ref name=":0" />

In 2015, the city of Mascota was declared a "[[Pueblos Mágicos|Pueblo Mágico]]".<ref name=":1" />
