'''Taubaté''' is a medium-sized city in the state of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]], in [[Paraíba Valley]] southeastern [[Brazil]]. The city that was established in the early 17th century, by [[Jacques Felix|Jacques Félixis]], is home for an estimated population of 250,000 people, living between São Paulo to [[Rio de Janeiro]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=2026 |title=Taubaté |url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Taubate}}</ref>

==History==
{{Unreferenced section|date=November 2024}}
[[File:Great view -Taubaté, São Paulo, Brazil.jpg|thumb|left|Hills and fields surround Taubate.]]
Taubaté was part of the ancient [[Tupinambá people|Tupinambá]] Territory, along the [[Paraíba do Sul|Paraíba do Sul River]]. The Tupinambá Territory in the 16th century, stretched from the [[Juqueriquerê River]] on the shores of [[Caraguatatuba]] to the Cape of Saint Thomas (Cabo de São Tomé) in the [[State of Rio de Janeiro]]. The first village was created in 1640 being proclaimed as an autonomous locality on December 5, 1645, by a pioneer named [[Jacques Felix]]. It was the first location in the [[Paraíba Valley|Vale do Paraíba]] region to obtain autonomy. The locality got its current city status in 1842, by which time it was a coffee production center. It hosted [[Taubaté Agreement|Taubaté's Agreement]] in 1906. In 1908 the city was made the seat of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Taubaté]]. In 1900 it was the largest city in the interior of [[São Paulo]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=CONVENTINHO DE TAUBATÉ. OS DEHONIANOS NO VALE DO PARAÍBA |url=https://www.dehonianos.org.br/conventinho-de-taubate-os-dehonianos-no-vale-do-paraiba/ |access-date=2026-04-28 |language=pt-BR}}</ref>

The county name comes from the [[Guarani language]] and means high village (taba ybaté).<ref>{{Cite web |title=Centro de Atendimento ao Turista - Prefeitura Municipal de Taubaté - SP |url=https://www.prefeituradetaubate.org/centro-de-atendimento-ao-turista-em-taubate/ |access-date=2026-04-28 |website=Prefeitura de Taubaté |language=pt-BR}}</ref>

==Geography==
===Location===

Its strategic location between the two most important Brazilian cities ([[São Paulo]] {{convert|123|km|mi|abbr=off}} away, and [[Rio de Janeiro]] {{convert|280|km|mi|abbr=off}} away), connected to both by the [[Presidente Dutra Highway]], between high, cold mountains and the [[Atlantic Ocean]] has helped the development of the city. It is part of the [[Metropolitan Region of Vale do Paraíba e Litoral Norte]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.al.sp.gov.br/leis/legislacao-do-estado/|title=Lei Complementar nº 1.166, de 09 de janeiro de 2012|website=www.al.sp.gov.br}}</ref> The population is 317,915 (2020 est.) in an area of {{convert|625.00|km2|2|abbr=off}}.<ref name=ibge>[http://cod.ibge.gov.br/236AH Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]</ref> The city has become an [[Industrial sector|industrial]] center, seating branches of several companies, including [[Volkswagen]], [[Alstom]], [[LG]], [[Embraer]], among many others.

A traditional city in São Paulo state, it played an important role in the historical and economic development of the country. In the gold cycle was radiating center of bandeirismo discovering gold in Minas Gerais, founding several cities. In the Second Empire, during the coffee boom of the Paraíba Valley, has emerged as the largest municipality in the state production area, hosting the Taubaté Convention in 1906.<ref>ABREU, Maria Morgado de. Taubaté: de núcleo irradiador de bandeirismo a centro industrial e universitário do Vale do Paraíba.,2.ª edição. Aparecida: Santuário, 1991.</ref>

The municipality contains part of the {{convert|292000|ha|adj=on}} [[Mananciais do Rio Paraíba do Sul Environmental Protection Area]], created in 1982 to protect the sources of the [[Paraíba do Sul]] river.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|Unidade de Conservação ... MMA}}|language=pt

The average humidity is 60% and the annual rainfall is {{convert|55|in|mm|0|abbr=out|order=flip}}. The most extreme temperatures recorded in the city are {{convert|33|F|C|order=flip}} and {{convert|40.1|C|F|0}}.

==Education and literature==

[[File:Anfiteatro fmt.jpg|thumb|left|Inside a class room of the University of Taubaté]]
[[File:Btl hangar.jpg|thumb|Taubaté is home to a large Brazilian Army Aviation base.]]

Taubaté is also a university city. The [[Universidade de Taubaté|University of Taubaté]], UNITAU, is a municipal institution of higher learning run by the state, but also by the private initiative. UNITAU is not free. It has schools of [[medical school|medicine]], [[law]], [[dentistry]], [[Engineering College|engineering]], and [[Business school|Business Management]], and a Department of Architecture.

[[Monteiro Lobato]], an important [[Portuguese language|Portuguese-language]] writer of [[children's literature]], was born in Taubaté. On 6 January 2010, the Chamber of Deputies granted Taubaté was granted the title of National Capital of Children's literature.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www2.camara.leg.br/camaranoticias/noticias/EDUCACAO-E-CULTURA/148516-CAMARA-APROVA-TAUBATE-COMO-CAPITAL-NACIONAL-DA-LITERATURA-INFANTIL.html | title=Câmara aprova Taubaté como capital nacional da literatura infantil - Notícias }}</ref>

[[Amácio Mazzaropi]], a pioneer in the movie industry, was born in the city to [[Italian Brazilians|Italian immigrants]].

==Sports==
[[Esporte Clube Taubaté]] is a [[association football|football]] club based in the city. The main competition played by the club is the [[Campeonato Paulista Série A2]], the second level of the [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]] state professional football championship.

==Quality of life==

Taubaté has been ranked by the UNDP ([[United Nations Development Programme]]) the 21st out of 645 cities in the state in terms of quality of life (such as safety, public schools, medical and dental care, public transport, low air pollution, sewers and piped water reaching all houses).
