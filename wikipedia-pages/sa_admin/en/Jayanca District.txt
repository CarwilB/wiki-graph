'''Jayanca District''' is one of twelve [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of the [[Lambayeque Province]] in the [[Lambayeque Region|Lambayeque]] region, [[Peru]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} Municipalidad Distrital de Jayanca. == Toponymy ==
In a well-documented 17th-century source, the name appears as '''''Iaianca''''' in the local [[mochica language]] of the time. This toponym was recorded by the [[catholic priest]] and [[parish]] [[vicar]] of ''[[Reque District|San Martín de Reque]]'', [[Fernando de la Carrera Daza]], in his book ''Arte de la lengua yunga'' (1644). The work stands as a notable [[descriptive grammar]] of the [[mochica language]] during the colonial era, authored by Carrera, who was born in [[Trujillo, Peru|Trujillo]] and had learned the region’s native tongue in his childhood in the nearby city of [[Lambayeque, Peru|Lambayeque]].<ref>{{Cite book |last=de la Carrera Daza |first=Fernando |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=1VRpAAAAcAAJ |title=Arte de la Lengua Yunga de los valles del Obispado de Truxillo del Peru, con un Confessonario, y todas las Oraciones Christianas, traducida en la lengua, y otras cosas |publisher=I. de Contreras |year=1644 |location=Lima |language=es, mochica}}</ref>
== History ==
The district's origin is unclear, in his ''Miscelánea Antártica'', the 16th-century chronicler [[Miguel Cabello de Balboa]] recounts the legend that the valley was first settled by ''Llapchillully'', the favored tailor of ''[[Naylamp]]'', the mythical founder of the [[lambayeque culture]] (of mochica ethnicity). Cabello’s account was later studied by [[Hans Heinrich Brüning|Enrique Brünning]] in relation to this tradition.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Brüning|first1=Hans Heinrich|title=Estudios monográficos del Departamento de Lambayeque|date=1989|publisher=Sociedad de Investigación de la Ciencia, Cultura y Arte Norteo|location=Chiclayo, Perú}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Cabello de Balboa |first=Miguel |url=http://archive.org/details/miscelanea-antartica |title=Miscelánea Antártica |year=1586 |language=es}}</ref> American anthropologist [[Izumi Shimada]] has claimed that the district's first inhabitants, the ''Sarayoq'', belonged to the Andean civilization known as Chavín de Huántar, who formed their first rural settlements in the fertile valleys of Jayanca, Salas, and Motupe.{{CN|date=August 2025}}

During the [[Inca Empire|Incan period]] Jayanca was subdued by the Inca [[Huayna Capac]] who, commanding 40,000 men, left Cusco and headed for Quito. During his voyage, he conquered several valleys of the north coast, among them Jayanca.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Aranibar Zerpa|first1=Carlos|title=Pachacutec|date=1964|publisher=Editorial Universitaria|location=Lima, Perú}}</ref>

In the late 14th and early 15th centuries, the valley of the Jayanca lordship, ethnically [[Mochicas|mochica]], experienced significant depopulation as a result of the [[forced resettlement]] of local populations under the Inca system of [[Mitma|''mitmas'']]. This policy aimed to politically weaken the local lordship, prevent uprisings, and promote the ''quechuanization'' of the [[Mochica language|mochica-speaking]] population. One such case was that of the jayanca potter ''mitmas'' relocated to the lordship of ''Guambos'' or ''Guampu'', in present-day [[Huambos District|Huambos]] in [[Department of Cajamarca|Cajamarca]], and to the arid Pabur region in [[Department of Piura|Piura]]. This process ceased after the fall of the inca empire. During the colonial period, the local indigenous elite made attempts to bring them back to Jayanca, but without success. Such events were recorded by chroniclers like [[Sarmiento de Gamboa]] and later studied by the peruvian historian [[Waldemar Espinoza Soriano]] in ''El'' ''valle de Jayanca y el reino de los Mochica, siglos XV y XVI'' (1975).<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Espinoza Soriano |first=Waldemar |date=1975 |title=El valle de Jayanca y el reino de los Mochica, siglos XV y XVI |url=https://www.persee.fr/doc/bifea_0303-7495_1975_num_4_3_921 |journal=Bulletin de l'Institut Français d'Études Andines |language=es, fr |volume=4 |issue=3 |pages=243–274 |doi=10.3406/bifea.1975.921}}</ref>
