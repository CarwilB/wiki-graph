'''Lerdo de Tejada''' is a [[Municipalities of Veracruz|municipality]] in the [[Mexican state]] of [[Veracruz]], located {{convert|207|km}} southeast of the state capital [[Xalapa]]. It is named after [[Sebastián Lerdo de Tejada]], the 27th [[President of Mexico]].<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Geography==
The municipality of Lerdo de Tejada is located in southern Veracruz on the coastal plain of the [[Gulf of Mexico]]. It borders the municipalities of [[Ángel R. Cabada (municipality)|Ángel R. Cabada]] to the east, [[Saltabarranca]] to the south, [[Tlacotalpan (municipality)|Tlacotalpan]] to the southwest and [[Alvarado (municipality)|Alvarado]] to the west. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|84.245|km2}} and comprises 0.1% of the state's area.<ref name=survey/> 

The wetland La Popotera is located in Lerdo de Tejada and the adjacent municipality of Alvarado. It is protected as a [[Ramsar site]].<ref name=ramsar>{{cite web |url=https://rsis.ramsar.org/ris/1462 |title=Humedales de la Laguna La Popotera |publisher=Ramsar Sites Information Service |date=5 June 2005 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref> La Popotera is drained by the San Agustín River, which is a tributary of the [[Papaloapan River]] and also serves as the municipality's southwestern border. There is also a small lake known as the Laguna del Márquez located on the municipality's eastern border with Ángel R. Cabada.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Lerdo de Tejada, Veracruz de Ignacio de la Llave |language=es |publisher=INEGI |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref>

Lerdo de Tejada's climate is warm and humid with abundant rain in the summer. Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|24|and(-)|28|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|2000|and(-)|2500|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
The [[Codex Mendoza]] mentions a ''[[calpulli]]'' of [[Tlacotalpan]] called Tlazintla located in what is now Lerdo de Tejada.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://ciudadespatrimonio.mx/descargables/biblioteca/PLAN_DE_MANEJO_TLACOTALPAN_2014_CONTEXTO.pdf |title=Plan de manejo: Centro Histórico de Tlacotalpan Veracruz |language=es |publisher=[[Secretariat of Agrarian, Land, and Urban Development (Mexico)|SEDATU]] |date=April 2014 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref>

After the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire]], Tlazintla served as a port for one of [[New Spain]]'s first [[sugarcane mill]]s built by [[Hernán Cortés]] near [[San Andrés Tuxtla]], which remained in operation for over fifty years.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref>{{cite book |last=Barrett |first=Ward J. |date=1970 |title=The Sugar Hacienda of the Marqueses Del Valle |page=11 |publisher=[[University of Minnesota Press]] |isbn=9780816605651}}</ref> In the 1800s, the San Pedro and San Francisco sugarcane mills were built in the area.<ref name=sp>{{cite news |last=Fernández Cención |first=Calixto |url=http://imagendelgolfo.mx/columna.php?id=41540 |title=Genios del Ingenio |language=es |publisher=Imagen del Golfo |date=23 April 2016 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref><ref name=sf>{{cite news |last=Fernández Cención |first=Calixto |url=http://imagendelgolfo.mx/movil/columna/41605/genios-de-ingenio.html |title=Ingenio San Francisco Naranjal |language=es |publisher=Imagen del Golfo |date=1 May 2016 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref> Around 1876, the ''[[ranchería]]'' of San Francisco Naranjal was founded by settlers from San Andrés and [[Santiago Tuxtla]]. On 31 December 1901, San Francisco Naranjal became the seat of the municipality of Saltabarranca. On 23 January 1923, San Francisco Naranjal was separated from the rest of Saltabarranca and became its own municipality, which on 11 August 1923 was renamed Lerdo de Tejada.<ref>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Veracruz-Llave. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |pages=111, 222 |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222574/702825222574_1.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |isbn=970-13-1517-0}}</ref>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and two trustees (''regidores''), one elected by [[relative majority]] and one by [[proportional representation]].<ref name=encyclopedia/> The current president of the municipality is Hermas Cortes García.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.lerdodetejada.gob.mx |title=Portal de Transparencia Municipal |language=es |publisher=Órgano de Fiscalización Superior del Estado de Veracruz |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Census, the municipality of Lerdo de Tejada recorded a population of 20,141 inhabitants living in 5987 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=30&mun=097 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Lerdo de Tejada |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref> It recorded a population of 19,606 inhabitants in the 2015 Intercensal Survey.<ref name=survey/> 

There are 30 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> of which only the municipal seat is classified as urban. Also called Lerdo de Tejada, it recorded a population of 18,715 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=catalog/>

==Economy==
Sugarcane is the main crop grown in Lerdo de Tejada and sugarcane processing is the main industrial activity.<ref>{{cite report |url=http://www.legisver.gob.mx/fiscalizacion/Planes%20Municipales/PlanesMunicipales2014pdf/LERDO%20DE%20TEJADA.pdf |title=PLAN MUNICIPAL DE DESARROLLO DE LERDO DE TEJADA 2014-2017 |pages=244, 258 |language=es |publisher=Government of Lerdo de Tejada |date=2014 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref> Declining sugar prices since 2011 have caused a crisis in the local economy and forced the closure of the San Francisco Naranjal mill.<ref name=sp/><ref name=sf/> The remaining San Pedro mill, located southwest of the municipal seat at {{coord|18|36|39.7|N|95|31|47.8|W|region:MX|display=inline}}, produced 119,150 tonnes of sugar in the 2014–2015 season.<ref>{{cite report |url=http://www.conadesuca.gob.mx/atlas/Atlas_HD.pdf |title=Atlas de la Agroindustria de la caña de azúcar 2015 |pages=186–187 |language=es |publisher=Comité Nacional para el Desarrollo Sustentable de la Caña de Azúcar |date=13 December 2017 |access-date=8 February 2018}}</ref>
