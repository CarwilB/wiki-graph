'''Marquetalia''' is a town and municipality in the [[Colombia]]n [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[Caldas Department|Caldas]]. It is situated on the eastern side of the [[Cordillera Central (Colombia)|Cordillera Central]] and is part of the highland region known as the ''Oriente Caldense'' (eastern Caldas) or ''Alto Oriente'' (high east). Inhabited originally by [[Native Colombian|native american tribes]] called ''Amaníes, Pantágoras'' or ''Palenques'' by [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]] colonizers,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Generaciones, Movimientos y Grupos Literarios en Caldas |url=https://academiacaldensedehistoria.blogspot.com/2015/03/los-indigenas-del-oriente-de-caldas-y.html |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=Academia Caldense de Historia|language=Spanish|trans-title=Generations, Movements and Literary Groups in Caldas}}</ref> Marquetalia became permanently settled at the end of the XIX century by a wave of creole colonizers from [[Antioquia Department|Antioquia]], a phenomenon known as the ''colonización antioqueña'' (Antioquian colonization).<ref>{{Cite web |last=Christie |first=Keith H. |date=May 1, 1978 |title=Antioqueño Colonization in Western Colombia: A Reappraisal |url=https://read.dukeupress.edu/hahr/article/58/2/260/150614/Antioqueno-Colonization-in-Western-Colombia-A}}</ref> With an economy that revolves mainly around the production of [[coffee]], [[avocado]], [[Cooking banana|plantain]], [[Theobroma cacao|cacao]] and [[Sugarcane|sugar cane]], among other agricultural practices such as cattle ranching, Marquetalia ranked 22nd out of the 27 municipalities that contribute to Caldas' gross domestic product in 2022,<ref>{{Cite web |title= |url=https://caldata.caldas.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2.-Paper_Analisis-subrregional_2022_.pdf |archive-url=http://web.archive.org/web/20250625075229/https://caldata.caldas.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2.-Paper_Analisis-subrregional_2022_.pdf |archive-date=2025-06-25 |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=caldata.caldas.gov.co}}</ref> with an average annual per capita income of approximately $9.2 million pesos (about [[United States dollar|USD]] $2500).<ref>{{Cite web |title= |url=https://caldata.caldas.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/3.-Fichas-municipales_constantes_.pdf |archive-url=http://web.archive.org/web/20250625075704/https://caldata.caldas.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/3.-Fichas-municipales_constantes_.pdf |archive-date=2025-06-25 |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=caldata.caldas.gov.co}}</ref> It is the third smallest municipality by area of Caldas behind [[Marmato, Caldas|Marmato]] and [[La Merced, Caldas|La Merced]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web == Etymology ==
Originally established as the [[Corregimiento]] de Risaralda in 1903, then officially incorporated as the town of ''Núñez in 1924'' and finally named Marquetalia in 1930, it is believed<ref name=":0" /> that its current name stems from the [[metaplasm]] of the title given to native tribal leaders, either ''mareketá, malchita'' or ''marikita.'' It shares a notable similarity with the name of the nearby, but much older town of [[Mariquita, Tolima|Mariquita]], which is immediately adjacent to its south-east in the department of [[Tolima Department|Tolima]].

=== Misconception ===
Marquetalia is often mistaken with a rural area with the same name that is part of the town of [[Planadas]] in southern [[Tolima Department|Tolima]], which was the birthplace of the extinct [[Guerrilla warfare|guerrilla]] group [[Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia|FARC-EP]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Radio |first=Caracol |date=2014-06-09 |title=En Marquetalia, Caldas, piden no confundirlos con Marquetalia, Tolima |url=https://caracol.com.co/radio/2014/06/09/regional/1402305720_265058.html |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=Caracol Radio |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-11-16 |title=Peace reigns in Colombia's former guerrilla fiefdom |url=https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20211116-peace-reigns-in-colombia-s-former-guerrilla-fiefdom |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=France 24 |language=en}}</ref> [[FARC dissidents|Dissidents]] of this group that have remained in [[Colombian conflict|open conflict]] with the Colombian government have called for the establishment of a ''Segunda Marquetalia'',<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-12-01 |title=EXPLAINER: What are Colombia's ex-FARC splinter groups? |url=https://apnews.com/article/colombia-united-states-south-america-armed-forces-revolutionary-armed-forces-of-colombia-492c423824351ff8dc1ed4bba761d200 |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref> alluding to a re-creation of the self-proclaimed independent republic founded there in the 1960's, but this makes no reference to the Caldas town of Marquetalia, which is located 386 [[Kilometre|kms]] (240 [[Mile|miles]]) north of Marquetalia, Tolima.[[File:Pictogramas caracoli.jpg|left|thumb|212x212px|Native art of the Rocks of Caracolí.]]

== History ==

=== Indigenous Past and Spanish Conquest ===
Little is known about the native peoples that occuppied the area where Marquetalia currently stands before Spanish and creole colonization, except that they are referred to as ''Amaníes'', ''Marquetones, Pantágoras, Palenques'' and ''Panches,'' sometimes interchangeably. The name ''palenque'', in particular, seems to make reference to the type of fortifications used by indigenous tribes in defense of the territories they claimed, which consisted of sharp wooden stakes made of black palm trees, according to the second-hand account made by the chronicler [[Pedro Cieza de León]] in 1536''<ref name=":3">{{Cite book |last=Ocampo Cardona |first=Ángel María |title=De Nuñez a Marquetalia, La Villa del Sol. |date=2014 |isbn=978-958-46-4748 |location=Manizales |publication-date=2014 |language=Spanish |trans-title=From Nuñez to Marquetalia, The Village of the Sun.}}</ref>''. On the other hand, historians have also discussed that the name ''pantágora'' is a misspelling from the name ''patángora,'' which was given by the Spaniards to a tribe or group of tribes that often used the prefix "patan" in their words''<ref name=":3" />''.

It has been argued that the territory began to see Spanish [[Conquistador|conquistadors]] in 1549 under captain [[Baltasar Maldonado]] and later on by [[Hernán Venegas Carrillo]] and Francisco Núñez Pedroso. It is said that these explorers identified some areas with a potential for [[mining]], but desisted of their conquest efforts after encountering highly aggressive native American tribes''<ref name=":3" />''.  

The tribes' customs and social structures were not well understood by Spanish colonizers, although the figure of ''cacique,'' or tribal chief, was present in some of these and was recognizable to the europeans''<ref name=":3" />''. Their main crop is believed to have been [[maize]]. 11 [[Petroglyph|petroglyphs]] containing what is accepted to be [[Pre-Columbian era|prehispanic]] art can be found in different ''veredas'' or rural localities near the town center, and some of these have acquired a legally protected status.  

Regarding the iconic ''Cacica Marquetona,'' or Marquetalian Chief, historian [[Soledad Acosta|Soledad Acosta de Samper]] argues that she could have been an indigenous woman who, in spite of holding legend status for her leadership role during Spanish conquest and being a symbol of identity for Marquetalians, was, in fact, a mistress or slave by the name of Mariquita taken by the conquistador [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] to the founding of the city of San Sebastián, today known as [[Mariquita, Tolima|Mariquita]], in 1552.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Acosta |first=Soledad |title=Biografías de Hombres Ilustres o Notables, Relativos a la época del Descubrimiento, Conquista y Colonización de la parte de América denominada actualmente EE.UU. de Colombia |date=1883 |publisher=Imprenta de la Luz |location=Bogotá |publication-date=1883 |language=Spanish}}</ref> Nevertheless, there is no consensus either on the identity of the ''cacica'' or the origin of the name ''Marquetalia.''

=== Between the XVI and the XIX centuries ===
The region was not actively colonized by europeans or european descendants until the Antioquian expansion reached these territories in the second half of the XIX century; hence, these in-between centuries are seen as an obscure period for historians of Marquetalia''<ref name=":3" />''. However, it has been argued that the trascendental [[Royal Botanical Expedition to New Granada]], which had its original base in nearby [[Mariquita, Tolima]] and was led by Spanish priest [[José Celestino Mutis]], may have passed through the path that is now Marquetalia´s main road, which has been in use since prehispanic times and has had the important role of connecting southern Antioquia to the [[Magdalena River]], Colombia's most important fluvial body''<ref name=":3" />''.

=== First half of the XX century ===
Although Manzanares and Victoria were founded in 1863 and 1879 respectively, Marquetalia, who is in the middle of both towns, was not officially founded until 1903, with records indicating first signs of colonizing activity in 1885.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sánchez |first=Ing Cristian Giovanny Toro |title=Marquetalia, 95 Años de Vida Administrativa |url=https://www.samanacaldas.net.co/notiver.php?idnoticia=2447 |access-date=2026-05-28 |website=samanacaldas.net.co |language=es-ES}}</ref> Its founding was an effect of the migration wave of Antioquians and Tolimenses (people belonging to the departments of Antioquia and Tolima) who arrived to the unexploited territories of today's departments of Quindio, Risaralda and Caldas in search for economic growth, particularly for arable land, silver and gold. This event is known as the ''Colonización Antioqueña'' and it attracted both colombian-born colonizers, as well as Spaniards, many of them of [[Basques|Basque]] origin. 

Marquetalia was officially declared a town under the name of ''Núñez'' on April 15th, 1924 after being part of Manzanares under the name of ''Corregimiento de Risaralda'' since its foundation''.'' Its main catholic church, the ''Templo de Nuestra Señora de los Dolores'' (Our Lady of Sorrows Temple), was built between 1936 and 1954 featuring gothic, romanesque and rennaissance elements,<ref name=":3" /> three naves and an apse.

=== Second half of the XX century ===
A celebrated figure in the history of Marquetalia is that of father Antonio Hincapié Soto,<ref name=":3" /> a catholic priest who arrived in Marquetalia in 1957 at the end of the period known as [[La Violencia]] and who initiated a social transformation in the municipality. 

Father Hincapié was crucial in the establishment and building of the ''Instituto Juan XXIII,'' the ''Escuela Normal Superior Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria'' and the ''Institución Educativa Antonio María Hincapié Soto'' (located in Santa Elena) public schools. He also created the Society for Mutual Aid that offered funeral services to poor families for a small fee, established a program for widows and orphans of scarce means, donated parish lands for the creation of the ''El Paraíso'' gereatric home and sold other church terrains in 1964 for the erection of the ''Hospital San Cayetano<ref name=":3" />''. 

Marquetalia's main square is named after the revered priest and the ''Fiestas de la Cordillera,'' a folk festival that occurs every two years and which celebrates Marquetalia´s culture, began in 1965 after the first cohort of students graduated from the ''Instituto Juan XXIII'' founded by Hincapié.<ref name=":3" />

== Geography and Climate ==
[[File:Soil_map_-_Marquetalia.jpg|left|thumb|307x307px|Soil map of Marquetalia.]]
Marquetalia is located 146 kms (about 90 miles) from the city of [[Manizales]], capital of the department of Caldas, and 216 kms (134 miles) from Bogotá, Colombia's capital city. It limits to the north with [[Pensilvania, Caldas|Pensilvania]] and [[Samaná, Caldas|Samaná]], to the east with [[Victoria, Colombia|Victoria]], to the southeast with [[Mariquita, Tolima|Mariquita]], to the south with [[Fresno, Tolima|Fresno]] and to the west with [[Manzanares, Caldas|Manzanares]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Geoportal DANE- Geovisor de Consulta de Codificación de la Divipola |url=https://geoportal.dane.gov.co/geovisores/territorio/consulta-divipola-division-politico-administrativa-de-colombia/ |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=geoportal.dane.gov.co}}</ref>

The municipality of Marquetalia has a cool [[tropical rainforest climate]] (Af) due to its altitude, with areas ranging from around 800 [[Height above mean sea level|mts]] (2,624 [[Foot (unit)|ft]]) on the east to 1900 mts (6,233 ft) on the west; the town itself is 1550 mts (5,200 ft) above sea level.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Mapa topográfico Marquetalia, altitud, relieve |url=https://es-ni.topographic-map.com/map-fhfvzs/Marquetalia/?center=5.30435,-75.09956&zoom=15&popup=5.31082,-75.10622 |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=Mapas topográficos |language=es}}</ref> Although it is nicknamed ''Village of the Sun'', the area can experience both intense rains as well as hot and dry seasons; April and October being the rainiest months, while January and July being the driest. Temperatures can reach highs below 29 [[Celsius|C°]] (84 [[Fahrenheit|F°]]) and fall to 17 C° (62 F°) depending on the time of day, altitude and season, with an average temperature of 23 C°. Average monthly rainfall is approximately 228 [[Millimetre|mm]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Marquetalia, Caldas, CO Climate Zone, Monthly Averages, Historical Weather Data |url=https://weatherandclimate.com/colombia/caldas/marquetalia |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=weatherandclimate.com}}</ref>{{Weather box

It is estimated that only 2% of the total area of Marquetalia is flat, the rest being undulated or severely fragmented.<ref name=":0" /> Notable peaks and hilltops are the ''Alto de Penagos'' (1780 m), ''Cerro de la Laguna'' (2330&nbsp;m), ''Alto de la Cruz'' and ''Cerro de Morrogacho.'' The ''Unión Esperanza'' lagoon is another notable natural attraction located on the west up the San Juan river.

The three main rivers are the ''La Miel'' to the north'', Guarinó'' to the south and the San Juan to the west, potable water being obtained from the last. While these are fed by 18 microbasins and feature many brookes and cascades, the most prominent waterfalls include ''La Paila, El Tigre, La Parda'' and ''Dantas'' on the ''La Miel'' river; ''El Hospital, Minitas, La Zona and Danticas'' on the ''Guarinó'' river; and the ''Penagos'' on the San Juan.<ref name=":2" />

== Flora and fauna ==
The San Juan microbasin in the west is considered to be the most biodiverse region in Marquetalia as an effect of its steepness and difficulty of access to people.<ref name=":2" />

=== Flora ===
The colonization of the region has transformed the quality, composition and extension of the diverse native forests, nonetheless, native tree species can still be found. Some of these include: [[Cecropia|Yarumo]], [[Ochroma|Balso]], Comino, [[Cedar tree|Cedar]], Cedrillo, Chingalé or [[Jacaranda]], [[Walnut]], [[Guadua]], Arboloco (''[[Smallanthus]]Pyramidalis)'', [[Trichanthera gigantea|Quiebrabarrigo]], Chilca (''[[Baccharis]]'' Latifolia), [[Fern|Ferns]], [[Juncus]], [[Terminalia oblongata|Guayabo de Monte]], Gallinazo (''[[Schizolobium parahyba]]''), [[Laurus nobilis|Laurel]], [[Melicoccus bijugatus|Limoncillo]] and [[Bursera simaruba|Carate]].<ref name=":2" />

[[Inga edulis|Notably, Guamo]], Nogal, Cedar, [[Tabebuia chrysantha|Guayacán]] and Carbonero (''[[Calliandra]] Pettieri'') trees are often used to provide shade to coffee trees. [[Hevea brasiliensis|Rubber]] and [[Theobroma cacao|Cacao]] trees have also been introduced, with about 133 Ha planted by the year 2000. Scattered [[Guadua]] forests amounted up to 45 Ha in the same year.

=== Fauna ===
Due to Colombia's topographic and climatic variety, it is recognized for being one of the most biodiverse countries in the world, and it's well known for being home to the most number of species of birds on the planet.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-06-02 |title=What country has the most bird species in the world? - Birdful |url=https://www.birdful.org/what-country-has-the-most-bird-species-in-the-world/ |access-date=2026-05-27 |language=en-US}}</ref> Over 250 of these have been registered in Marquetalia, belonging to the following families and subfamilies:<ref>{{Cite web |website=Avescaldas |date=October 2021 |title=Listado de Aves del Municipio de Marquetalia – Global Big Day 2022 |url=https://www.avescaldas.com/wp-content/uploads/Listado-de-aves-Marquetalia.pdf}}</ref>

== Culture ==
[[File:Main Square (Plaza Principal) of Marquetalia, Caldas.jpg|thumb|"Yipaos" stationed in front of the ''Parroquia de Nuestra Señora de los Dolores'' (Our Lady of Sorrows Parish) in Marquetalia's main square.|300x300px|left]]
Historically, the department of Caldas has been one of the most prolific growers of coffee in Colombia, with 57,880 [[Hectare|Ha]] (143,024 [[Acre|Acres]]) of coffee being cultivated across the small department in 2026.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=Café de Caldas |url=https://caldas.federaciondecafeteros.org/cafe-de-caldas/ |access-date=2026-05-28 |website=Federación de cafeteros Caldas |language=es-ES}}</ref> Due to the oscillation of its climate, its altitude, as well as its rich soil, Marquetalia is likewise heavily dependent on the production and commercialization of products related to the cultivation of coffee,<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=García L |first1=Juan Carlos |last2=Posada-Suárez |first2=Húver |last3=Läderach |first3=Peter |date=2014 |title=Recommendations for the regionalizing of coffee cultivation in Colombia: a methodological proposal based on agro-climatic indices |journal=PLOS ONE |volume=9 |issue=12 |article-number=e113510 |doi=10.1371/journal.pone.0113510 |doi-access=free |issn=1932-6203 |pmc=4250036 |pmid=25436456 |bibcode=2014PLoSO...9k3510G }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Café de Caldas |url=https://caldas.federaciondecafeteros.org/cafe-de-caldas/ |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=Federación de cafeteros Caldas |language=es-ES}}</ref> including farm equipment, coffee plants and green and dried coffee beans. According to data provided by the National Coffee Federation of Colombia, most coffee farms in the department are run by local families and are usually below 5 Ha (12,36 Acres) in size.<ref name=":1" />  [[File:"Helda"_for_drying_coffee_in_Colombia.jpg|thumb|360x360px|Traditional house with a movable roof called ''casa helda'' where coffee is laid to dry in the open air.]]Marquetalia celebrated its first ever roasted coffee contest during its centennial celebration in October 2024, where 3 out of 15 participating brands were chosen for their quality, whose scores ranged from 84,00 to 85,00 points according to the judges from the National Coffee Federation.<ref>{{Cite web |first=Juan |last=Sánchez Romero |date=2024-10-30 |title=Este es el pequeño pueblo de la cordillera en Caldas cuyo café brilla en el mundo |url=https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/10/30/el-cafe-de-un-pequeno-pueblo-de-la-cordillera-en-caldas-brilla-en-las-exportaciones-sus-granos-no-bajan-de-selectos/ |access-date=2026-05-28 |website=infobae |language=es-CO}}</ref> This event marked a turning point for Marquetalia, as its focus until recent years had been primarily on the production of raw coffee. 

Marquetalia's difficult terrain has also given way to both the ''[[arriero]]'' and the ''[[yipao]]'' subcultures, the first one being the name of the mover of goods atop [[Mule|mules]] and [[Horse|horses]] between commercial centers before the arrival of motor vehicles, and the second being the name of the widespread use of [[Jeep|Jeeps]] and other high-torque car brands that made transportation of people and goods possible along the unpaved roads of the region of the XX century. ''Yipaos'' are still widely used for local and intermunicipal transportation and both are cultural markers of the [[Paisa people]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Procolombia |title=El Yipao: el Jeep Willys que se volvió ícono colombiano |url=https://colombia.co/cultura-colombiana/el-yipao-la-historia-de-como-un-jeep-willys-se-convirtio-en-simbolo-de-la-cultura-colombiana |archive-url=http://web.archive.org/web/20260316212513/https://colombia.co/cultura-colombiana/el-yipao-la-historia-de-como-un-jeep-willys-se-convirtio-en-simbolo-de-la-cultura-colombiana |archive-date=2026-03-16 |access-date=2026-05-25 |website=Marca país |language=es}}</ref>

== Subdivisions ==
The municipality of Marquetalia is divided in two administrative regions: Marquetalia and the ''Corregimiento'' of Santa Elena. This ''Corregimiento'' was established by municipal decree on December 12, 1990.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2016-02-19 |title=Santa Helena, un corregimiento joven |url=https://archivo.lapatria.com/caldas/santa-helena-un-corregimiento-joven-245244 |access-date=2026-06-04 |website=archivo.lapatria.com |language=es}}</ref> Apart from the town of Marquetalia, there are 48 subdivisions, or ''veredas,'' in total:<ref name=":2" />

'''Marquetalia (33 ''veredas''):'''

Alegrías Alto, Alegrías Bajo, Buenos Aires, Campo Alegre, Costa Rica, Cúcuta, El Aguacate, El Chocó, El Placer, El Porvenir, Encimadas, La Bamba,  La Esmeralda, La Estrella, La Florida, La Maporita, La Miel,  La Palma, La Parda, La Playa, La Quiebra, El Rosal, La Tebaida, Las Gaviotas, Lituania, Los Zainos, Patiobonito, San Gregorio, San Juan, San Luis, Unión Alta, Unión Baja and Unión Esperanza.

'''Santa Elena (15 ''veredas''):'''

Altobonito, El Palmar, El Retiro, El Rosario, El Vergel,  Gancho, Guacas, Guarinó-Guamo, Guarinó-San Roque, La Bella, La Mirla, La Moscovita, San Pablo, San Roque and Santa Elena.

== Celebrations ==

* ''Exposición Equina''-''Grado B'' - B-grade equine expo. Yearly in September.
* ''Fiestas de la Cordillera'' - Folk festival. Biennal. Celebrated in October on even years.
* ''Fiestas del Árbol'' - Folk festival. Celebrated in the ''Corregimiento'' of Santa Helena every two years in October.
* ''Semana Santa'' (Holy Week or easter) - Catholic celebration. Celebrated at the begininng of April.
