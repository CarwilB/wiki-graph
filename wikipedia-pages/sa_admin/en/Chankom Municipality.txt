'''Chankom Municipality''' (in the [[Yucatec Maya language|Yucatec Maya]] language: “little ravine”) is a [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Yucatán]] containing 137.95&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> of land and located roughly 135&nbsp;km southeast of the city of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]].<ref name="municipios"/>

==History==
Before the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán|arrival of the Spanish]], the area was populated as evidenced by archeological sites but no specific information is known of the inhabitants nor the [[encomienda]] system. The area was depopulated by the [[Caste War of Yucatán]] and the inhabitants did not return to the area until the 1890s.<ref name="municipios"/>

Chankom was established as a ranchería assigned to the Valladolid region in 1928. Seven years later, it was designated as its own municipality.<ref name=municipios />

In 1931-1948 the anthropologists [[Robert Redfield|Robert]] and [[Margaret Park Redfield]] and {{interlanguage link|Alfonso Villa Rojas|es}} stayed in the county seat of Chankom for extended periods (Villas taught there) and published ''Chan Kom, a Maya Village'',<ref name="ck1">{{Cite book |last=Redfield |first=Robert |title=Chan Kom: a Maya village |last2=Villa Rojas |first2=Alfonso |date=1971 |publisher=Univ. of Chicago Pr |isbn=978-0-226-70661-0 |edition=4. impr |series=Phoenix Books |location=Chicago |language=en}}</ref> ''A Village that Chose Progress, Chan Kom Revisited'',<ref name="ck2">{{Cite book |last=Redfield |first=Robert |title=A Village that Chose Progress, Chan Kom Revisited |publisher=U of Chicago Press |year=1950}}</ref> and ''The Folk Culture of Yucatan''.<ref name="ck3">{{Cite book |last=Redfield |first=Robert |title=The Folk Culture of Yucatan |publisher=U of Chicago Press |year=1941}}</ref>

In 1986-2006 the anthropologist [[Alicia Re Cruz]] stayed in Chankom and published ''The two Milpas of Chan Kom: scenarios of a Maya village life''<ref name="cruz">{{Cite book |last=Re Cruz |first=Alicia |title=The two Milpas of Chan Kom: scenarios of a Maya village life |date=1996 |publisher=State University of New York Press |isbn=978-0-7914-2829-0 |series=Suny series in anthropology of work |location=New York}}</ref> and a documentary, ''The Mayan dreams of Chan Kom: tourism, migration, and changing identities in the Yucatan''.<ref name="cruz2">{{Cite AV media |title= The Mayan dreams of Chan Kom: tourism, migration, and changing identities in the Yucatan |date=2006 |last=Re Cruz |first=Alicia |type=DVD}}</ref>

==Governance==
The municipal president is elected for a three-year term. The town council has four councilpersons, who serve as Secretary and councilors of policing, public services and ecology.<ref name=governance>{{cite web|title=Chankom|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|website=inafed|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=8 July 2015|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es|archive-date=4 July 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220704100544/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Communities==
The head of the municipality is [[Chankom]], Yucatán.  There are 17 populated places in the municipality including Chuntabil, Maykab, [[Muchucux|Muchucuxca]], Nictehá, Sacpasil, San Isidro, San Juan, Santa María Koochilá, Santa Rosa, Ticimul, Tomku, Tzukmuc, Xanla, Xbohon, Xcalakdzonot, X-Cocail, Xhuaymil, Xkatún, Xkopeteil, X-Pamba, Xtamech, Xtohil, Yacbchem, and  Yochotún.<ref name="municipios"/><ref name=governance /> The significant populations are shown below:
