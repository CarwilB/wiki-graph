'''Simacota''' is a town and municipality in the [[Santander Department]] in northeastern [[Colombia]].  In 1965, the city had briefly been invaded by more than 100 members of the anti-government [[National Liberation Army (Colombia)|''Ejército de Liberación Nacional'']] (ELN), the "National Liberation Army") <ref>Jorge Pablo Osterling, ''Democracy in Colombia: Clientelist Politics and Guerrilla Warfare'' (Transaction Publishers, 1988) p246</ref> and "captured the public imagination" in its first act as a new guerrilla organization.<ref>René De La Pedraja, ''Wars of Latin America, 1948-1982: The Rise of the Guerrillas'' (McFarland, 2013) p193</ref>  The invaders murdered three of Simacota's four policemen, robbed the local bank, harassed the townspeople and looted the local pharmacy of its medicines, before being driven out by the Colombian Army.  Only three of the 100 ELN men were captured.<ref>"Guerrillas Active in Several Latin American Nations; Problem Called Serious in Five of Them", by Barnard L. Collier,  Herald-Tribune Wire Service, in the ''Janesville (WI) Daily Gazette'', January 30, 1965.</ref>
