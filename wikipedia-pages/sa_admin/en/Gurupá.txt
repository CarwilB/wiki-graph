'''Gurupá''' or '''Santo Antonio de Gurupá''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] on the [[Amazon River]] in state of [[Pará]], [[northern Brazil]] located near the world's largest river island, [[Marajó]], 300&nbsp;km upstream from the upper mouth of the river on the Atlantic coast.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/brasil_em_sintese/download/demografia.pdf |title=Job |format=PDF |access-date=2018-05-12}}</ref>

The city is a center for palm heart extraction and commerce. It is a municipal seat and major river boat port.

==History==
Gurupá is derived from the [[tupi language]] words ''guru'' (mouth) and ''pa'' (wide), and is associated with regions where water channels become wider. The word appears in the name of many localities in Brazil.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Mendonça de Souza |first1=João |title=O grande Amazonas: mítologia, história e sociologia |publisher=Sérgio Cardoso & Cia |pages=404 |url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/biblioteca-catalogo.html?id=264422&view=detalhes |access-date=26 August 2019}}</ref>

Gurupá was founded in 1609 as a Dutch trading post that they called Mariocai, after the indigenous peoples living there. It was the third of three trading posts established by the Dutch along the lower reaches of the Amazon and [[Xingu River]]s. The Dutch traded for dye, timber and mother-of-pearl. They also cultivated sugarcane along the Xingu river, to the south of Gurupa. It was subsequently conquered and occupied by the Portuguese in 1623, where they built the [[Fort of Santo Antônio do Gurupá]] (St. Anthony of Gurupá). It became the royal [[captaincy of Gurupá]] in 1633 in recognition of its strategic military and trading position.  It was absorbed into Grao-Pará in 1756.
