'''Puente Nacional''' ({{IPA|es-419|ˈpwente nasjoˈnal|pron}}; {{langnf|es||National Bridge}}) is an agricultural town and municipality in the Suárez River Valley, part of the [[Santander Department]] of northeastern [[Colombia]]. It is colloquially referred to as "Puente" by its inhabitants.
[[File:Puente nacional ferias y fiestas 2088.JPG|thumb|Annual parades celebrating local culture and music]]

The area was originally inhabited by four Muisca tribes, three of which were the Semisos, Irobaes, and Popobas.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pita Pico |first=Roger |date=6 November 2012 |title=Vestigios de a Lengua Guane: Una Aproximación al Fenómeno del Mestizaje Idiomático en Santander |url=http://www.scielo.org.co/pdf/linli/n63/n63a17.pdf |website=Academia Colombiana de Historia}}</ref><ref name=":0">{{Cite web |first=Carlos Andrés |last=Gamba Matesus |title=Puentenal.com Todo sobre Puente Nacional en internet |url=http://www.puentenacional.com/historia.html |access-date=2018-04-12 |website=www.puentenacional.com}}</ref> Their heritage now only survives in the names of three surrounding [[vereda]]s. 

The area between Puente and neighbouring [[Santa Sofía, Boyacá|Santa Sofía]] (formerly called Guatoque) was inhabited by a major tribe called the '''Sorocotá'''<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://searchworks.stanford.edu/view/58407|title=Valle de Sorocota; estudio monográfico de Guatoque-Santa Sofía y remembranza histórica de la antigua provincia de Ricuarte, memorias, etc. in SearchWorks catalog|website=searchworks.stanford.edu|series=Bibliotecade autores boyacenses |year=1965 |publisher=Imprenta del Departamento |language=en|access-date=2018-04-12}}</ref> who governed a major [[Muisca economy|commercial centre]] (possibly home to the region's largest agricultural market)<ref name=":1">{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=L_IpAQAAMAAJ|title=Santander y sus municipios|last1=Fulgencio Gutiérrez|first1=José |date=1990|publisher=Impr. Departamental de Santander|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5QhsAAAAMAAJ|title=Libro de oro de Santander|date=1996|language=es|last1=Boada|first1=Boada Ordóñez O.}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IBFsAAAAMAAJ|title=Puente Nacional ayer y hoy|date=1994|publisher=IDESAN|language=es}}</ref> which is why Puente's local radio station is called ''[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EULLrnYr4is La Voz de Sorocotá] (The Voice of Sorocotá)''. The town still has a market every Monday which sees locally sourced produce brought to the town from its many surrounding farms.

[[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] travelled south through the area in [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|1537]]<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=LljuZjm7pIsC|title=Los inconquistables panches del Magdalena: epopeya de un exótico reino caribe y su infortunado tropiezo con el Imperio Español|last=T|first=Martínez Trujillo Martínez|date=2005|publisher=Ángel Martínez T|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=qyllAAAAMAAJ|title=Geografía e historia de Santander|date=1947|publisher=Librería "Stella"|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5qk-AAAAYAAJ|title=The history of nations|last=Lodge|first=Henry Cabot|date=1928|publisher=P.F.Collier|language=en}}</ref> in the search for [[El Dorado#Quesada brothers' expeditions|El Dorado.]]<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=b3hLAAAAMAAJ|title=Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada, capitán de Eldorado|last=Padrón|first=Francisco Morales|date=1974|publisher=Publicaciones Españolas|isbn=9788450064155 |language=es}}</ref> Having followed the course of the [[Magdalena River]],<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://archive.org/details/conquestofnewgra00cunnrich|title=The Conquest of New Granada: Being the Life of Gonzalo Jimenez de Quesada|last=Graham|first=Robert Bontine Cunninghame|date=1922|publisher=W. Heinemann|language=en}}</ref> his expedition then travelled down the [[Suárez River|Saravita]], which formed the main trajectory of the subsequent [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|conquests]]. It was in this area that some sources report that Quesada's men made the first ever encounter with a "truffle" crop later identified as the [[History of the potato|potato]].<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=EV4YE_0RsywC&q=history+potato+sorocota&pg=PA36|title=The History and Social Influence of the Potato|last1=Salaman|first1=Redcliffe N.|last2=Burton|first2=William Glynn|date=1985-11-21|publisher=Cambridge University Press|isbn=9780521316231|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=KYZwAgAAQBAJ&q=history+potato+sorocota&pg=PA1|title=Potato Production, Processing and Technology|last=Gould|first=W. A.|date=2013-11-28|publisher=Elsevier|isbn=9781845696122|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OA8lAQAAMAAJ|title=Contributions from Texas Research Foundation|date=1962|publisher=Texas Research Foundation|language=en}}</ref>

Following the [[Spanish conquest of the Chibchan Nations|Spanish conquests]] a new town was baptised '''Puente Real de [[Vélez, Santander|Vélez]],''' existing as a subsidiary to the town founded by [[Martín Galeano|Martin Galeano]] in 1539,<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=7whsAAAAMAAJ|title=Pueblos de Santander: procesos de desarrollo urbano|date=1996-01-01|publisher=Terpel Bucaramanga S.A.|isbn=9789589589816|language=es}}</ref> before eventually acquiring its current name during the period following the [[Revolt of the Comuneros (New Granada)|Comunero Rebellions of 1781.]]<ref>{{Cite book|title=The people and the King : the Comunero Revolution in Colombia, 1781|author=Phelan, John Leddy|date=June 2011 |isbn=9780299072940|location=Madison|oclc=680040669}}</ref> These local uprisings set in motion the first wave of [[Spanish American wars of independence|Spanish American]] victories against the [[Spanish Empire]], although liberation only arrived after [[Bolívar's campaign to liberate New Granada|1819]]. Every year on 8 May week, the town transforms with homage to the C''omuneros''. Townsfolk dress in traditional late-18th century attire and parades are held through the town.

In 1960 the town's Cantarrana Street was the setting of a now-regularly commemorated massacre. It happened two years after the end of [[La Violencia]] (The Violence); the name given to the ten year sectarian civil-political conflict which pitted conservatives against liberals.<ref>{{Cite journal|first1=Fajardo|last1=Sánchez|first2=Jhon|last2=Armando|date=2017-07-27|title=La masacre de La Cantarrana: tensiones políticas y bandolerismo|url=http://repository.usta.edu.co/handle/11634/4328|language=en}}</ref> On September 29, tensions still present from the decennial conflict culminated with a shooting involving local [[wiktionary:bandolero#Spanish|brigand]] [[:es:Efraín González Téllez|Efrain Gonzalez]] which left 11 civilians dead and injured 19.
