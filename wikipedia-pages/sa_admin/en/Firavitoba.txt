'''Firavitoba''' is a town and municipality in [[Sugamuxi Province]], a subregion of the [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]] in [[Colombia]].

Before Spanish colonization, Firavitoba was part of the [[Muisca Confederation]] of the Chibcha people in the highlands of the [[Cordillera Oriental (Colombia)|Eastern Cordillera]] of the [[Andean natural region|Colombian Andes]]. Firavitoba belonged to the [[Iraca]] or Suamox state which, uniquely, did not observe a hereditary leadership system but elected its ruler alternately from Firavitoba and Tobasá, two of its many tribes.

Firavitoba is distinguished by its [[Gothic Revival architecture|neo-Gothic]] church, the third biggest in Colombia. Nuestra Señora de las Nieves (Our Lady of the Snows) was built between 1873 and 1976, entirely of stone sourced from nearby [[Sogamoso]]'s Pedregal district. 

== Etymology ==
The name Firavitoba derives from the Muysccubun language of the [[Muisca]] people. One etymology gives the root words as ''fiba'' ("air"),<ref>[[#CITEREFUricoechea |Uricoechea 1871]], p.106</ref> and ''faoa'' ("clouds").<ref>[[#CITEREFUricoechea |Uricoechea 1871]], pp.173-174</ref>

== Geography ==
Firavitoba is located on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], at an altitude of {{convert|2490|m|ft}} [[metres above sea level]] (m a.s.l.). It borders the municipalities of [[Paipa]] and [[Tibasosa]] in the north, [[Iza, Boyacá|Iza]] and [[Pesca]] in the south, [[Sogamoso]] in the east, and [[Tuta, Boyacá|Tuta]] and [[Toca, Boyacá|Toca]] to the west.<ref name=website>{{in lang|es}} [https://archive.today/20140310085149/http://www.firavitoba-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml General information Firavitoba] - accessed 28-04-2016</ref> It covers an area of {{convert|109.9|km2}}, in part on the plains and in part mountainous. Its highest point is the hill of Guática, at {{convert|3,228|m|ft}} a.s.l. It is bounded by the Chiquito River, one of the tributaries of the [[Chicamocha River]].

Firavitoba is {{convert|77|km|mi|spell=in|0}} from the departmental capital of [[Tunja]], and {{convert|10|km|mi|spell=in|0}} from the provincial capital, [[Sogamoso]].<ref name='Alcaldía'>{{cite web |url=http://www.firavitoba-boyaca.gov.co/municipio/nuestro-municipio |title=Nuestro Municipio |last=Alcaldía Municipal de Firavitoba |date=8 June 2018 |access-date=October 19, 2018 |language=Spanish |trans-title=Our Municipality}}</ref>

== History ==

=== Pre-Columbian period ===
The original, lakeside-dwelling inhabitants of the territory may have been the [[Chibchan]]-speaking [[Arhuaco]] people, according to Spanish archaeologist José Pérez de Barradas. Tribal migrations had slowed and settlement had been stable in the area for some time prior to the arrival of the Spaniards. The local community had organized its social structure around a hilltop site subdivided into a number districts that provided multiple vantage points. This settlement, Guática, "song of the fenced the mountain", later became the town of Firavitoba.<ref>{{Cite journal|last=URICOECHEA|first=Ezequiel|date=December 2012|title=Grammar vocabulary, catechism and confessional of the Chibcha language according to ancient anonymous and unpublished manuscripts, augmented and corrected.|journal=The Modern Language Journal|volume=96|issue=4|pages=558–575|doi=10.1111/j.1540-4781.2012.01398.x|issn=0026-7902}}</ref>

The [[Muisca]], also a Chibcha people, arrived in the central highlands of the Colombian Andes, the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], in the ninth or tenth century. They came up into the mountains from the ''Llanos Orientales'', or Eastern Plains of the [[Orinoquía natural region|Orinoquía region]].<ref>[[#CITEREFSuescún|Suescún 1998]], p.28.</ref> The early Spanish chronicler, the bishop [[Lucas Fernández de Piedrahita]], believed that the [[Muisca Confederation]] of the highlands originally founded its state in the eleventh or twelfth centuries, centred on Hunza, capital of the ruler [[Hunzahúa]], now the city of [[Tunja]].<ref>[[#CITEREFSuescún|Suescún 1998]], pp.207, 208.</ref>  

Some seventy years before the arrival of the Spaniards, the Altiplano region had separated into four independent Muisca states: Hunza, Bacatá, [[Tundama]], and [[Iraca]] or Suamox (present day [[Sogamoso]]).  Each state comprised a number of tribes, which formed its primary administrative and territorial units. These tribes, brought together by cultural affinity, geographic proximity or shared military and defensive interests, organized under a common legal and political framework and sovereign leadership.<ref>[[#CITEREFSuescún|Suescún 1998]], p.219.</ref> Of the two principal states in the Muisca's Altiplano territory at the time of the Spanish conquest one, in its southern area with its ceremonial capital in [[Bacatá]] (now [[Bogotá]]), was ruled by a [[Muisca rulers|high priest]] called the ''zipa'',  and the other was in its northern area, and ruled by the ''zaque'' from Hunza.<ref>[[#CITEREFBushnell|Bushnell 1993]], p.6.</ref>

Firavitoba was a tribe and settlement of Iraca or Suamox, the smallest of the four states, whose high priest and leader was the ''iraca'' or ''sogamuxi''. Uniquely among the Chibcha states, it did not observe the usual hereditary leadership structure of succession by the ruler's nephew, oldest son of his oldest sister.<ref>[[#CITEREFBushnell|Bushnell 1993]], p.7.</ref> Instead the ''iraca'' was elected by a council comprising the ''[[cacique]]s'' of four of its twenty main tribes, the [[Pesca]], [[Toca, Boyacá|Toca]], [[Gámeza]] and [[Busbanzá]]. If the election process failed to achieve a result, the cacique (or ''[[tundama]]'') of the [[Duitama|Tundama]] tribe also voted.<ref>[[#CITEREFSuescún|Suescún 1998]], pp.214–217.</ref> Tradition held that the sovereign ruler was to be elected from the settlements of Firavitoba and Tobasá alone, in strict alternation and to the exclusion of all other population centres.<ref>[[#CITEREFSuescún|Suescún 1998]], p.225.</ref> This custom continued until the Spanish conquest.

=== Spanish colonization ===
{{main article|Spanish conquest of the Muisca}}

Six expeditions by Spanish [[conquistador]]s led to the founding of the [[New Kingdom of Granada]] over much of the territory of modern Colombia, [[Venezuela]] and [[Panama]]. It was the exploratory expedition led by [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] which rapidly overcame the Muisca states of the Altiplano and colonized the region.<ref>[[#CITEREF Avellaneda|Avellaneda 1990]], p.33.</ref><ref>[[#CITEREF Avellaneda|Avellaneda 1990]], pp.49–50.</ref> Jiménez de Quesada departed on April 5, 1536, from [[Santa Marta]], on behalf of its governor [[Pedro Fernández de Lugo]], leading a mainly military force of some 600 land troops and several hundred sailors manning brigantines for river navigation. The third stage of the land route took them up the [[Cordillera Oriental (Colombia)|Cordillera Oriental]] of the Andes into Muisca territory. The expedition suffered heavy losses from disease, and several times stopped for periods of weeks or months to rest and regain strength along the way.<ref>[[#CITEREF Avellaneda|Avellaneda 1990]], pp.44–48.</ref> 

By 1537 they had explored as far as the site of the future city of [[Bogotá]] and the beginning of the eastern plains (''[[Orinoquía natural region|Llanos Orientales]]'') of the [[Orinoco]] watershed, covering much of the Muisca's Altiplano territory, including its salt and emerald mining areas. By August 1537 they had seized the treasure of the Tunja ''zaque'', a month later they conquered Suamox, and shortly after that killed the Bacatá ''zipa'' and his successor.<ref>[[#CITEREF Avellaneda|Avellaneda 1990]], pp.49–50.</ref> While the chroniclers of the rapid conquest and colonization of the region did not record the wars against its inhabitants, or the mistreatment of their rulers, in great detail, archival documents held in [[Seville]] and Bogotá trace the course of events. It is known that many caciques were tortured, the leader of Firavitoba among them.<ref>[[#CITEREF Avellaneda|Avellaneda 1990]], p.167.</ref>

=== New Kingdom of Granada ===
{{main article|New Kingdom of Granada}}

The 1536-1538 expedition led by Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada founded the city of Santa Fé de Bogotá (Bogotá) and gave the name ''El Nuevo Reino de Granada'', the [[New Kingdom of Granada]], to the region. The [[New Kingdom of Granada#Royal Audiencia|Royal Audiencia]] of the New Kingdom of Granada was established in Santa Fé de Bogotá in 1549. Its governor was dependent on the [[Viceroyalty of Peru]] in [[Lima]].

In 1547, one of the soldiers of the German adventurer [[Nikolaus Federmann]], Luis de Sanabria, had been granted administrative responsibility over the territories of Firavitoba, Cormechoque and Sichacá in the form of an [[Encomienda#History|''encomienda'']], so becoming the first ''encomendero'' over the communities there.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.archivogeneral.gov.co|title=Archivo General de la Nación|website=www.archivogeneral.gov.co|language=es|access-date=2010-05-20}}</ref><ref name='Tutiempo'>{{cite web |url=https://tierra.tutiempo.net/Colombia/Firavitoba-CO023127.html |title=Firavitoba |website=www.tierra.tutiempo.net |last=Patarroyo Gama |first=Emiro |access-date=October 18, 2018 |language=Spanish }}</ref>       

His successor was Martín de Rojas, who assumed the role on marrying his daughter, Catalina de Sanabria y Macías, in 1577. The office passed to their son, Gerónimo Donato de Rojas y Sanabria. It was inherited by his son in turn. This son, Gerónimo de Rojas y Niño, married Josefa de Fonseca y  Alarcón in 1629, but conflict quickly developed with her maternal relatives, among them [[Tota, Boyacá|Tota]] ''encomendero'' Pedro de Alarcón. This came to a head at a gathering in Tota of local ''encomenderos'' on the occasion of the visit of Royal Audiencia judge (''[[oidor]]'') Juan de Valcárcel to the region in 1636; Gerónimo de Rojas was ambushed and murdered in nearby [[Iza, Boyacá|Iza]] by several brothers of de Alarcón. His son, Martín de Rojas y Fonseca, took over the ''encomienda'' of Firavitoba.<ref name='Tutiempo'/>

Tunja was one of the main centres of a first cycle of gold production in the region, from 1550 to around 1620. The gold-mining workforce came essentially from the indigenous population.<ref name='Jaramillo, Meisel, Urrutia'>{{cite conference |url=https://www.banrep.gov.co/sites/default/files/publicaciones/archivos/borra074.pdf |title=Continuities and Discontinuities in the Fiscal and Monetary Institutions of New Granada 1783-1850 |first1=Jaime |last1=Jaramillo U. |first2=Adolfo |last2=Meisel R. |first3=Miguel |last3=Urrutia M. |date=July 1997 |series=Borradores Semanales de Economía |number=N° 74 |publisher=Subgerencia de Estudios Económicos, Banco de la República, Colombia |location= Santa Fé de Bogotá, Colombia |pages=1, 2 |access-date=October 19, 2018  }}</ref>

The municipality of Firavitoba had the status of indigenous village (village of Indians) or religious district (head of doctrine), with a chaplaincy founded in 1633 by Catalina de Rojas and Sanabria.

The last ''encomendero'' of Firavitoba, Lorenzo de Rojas, gifted several large buildings on the main square of Tunja, and his own large estate (''hacienda''), to the [[Society of Jesus]] in 1661.<ref>[[#CITEREF Gonzalez Mora| Gonzalez Mora 2004]], p.125, Note 38.</ref> Situated between Firavitoba and Iza, this Jesuit estate of over 300 ''fanegadas'' (1 ''fanegada'' = 6,400 m<sup>2</sup>, 0.64 ha, or 1.6 acres) was known as the Hacienda La Compañía, Firavitoba or de Sogamoso. <ref name='Tutiempo'/><ref>[[#CITEREF Gonzalez Mora| Gonzalez Mora 2004]], p.125, Note 38.</ref>

=== Viceroyalty of New Granada ===
{{main article|Viceroyalty of New Granada}}

In May 1717, the Spanish Crown established the independent [[Viceroyalty of New Granada]], with jurisdiction over the territories of modern Colombia, [[Ecuador]], [[Panama]] and [[Venezuela]]. Its administrative centre and ''audiencia'' were in Bogotá, which joined Lima and [[Mexico City]] as a major seat of Spanish authority in the [[New World]]. A second cycle of gold production in the region, from 1680 to 1820, was focused principally on departments of [[Chocó Department| Chocó]] and [[Antioquia Department|Antioquia]], rather than on earlier centres such as Tunja.<ref name='Jaramillo, Meisel, Urrutia'/>

The original church of Firavitoba was destroyed in a disaster the details which are no longer known. It may have burnt down. It was refounded in 1718 with the name ''Nuestra Señora de las Nieves''.<ref name='Alcaldía'/>

Until 1729, only baptismal registers exist for Firavitoba. The first recorded entry was signed by the priest Francisco Antonio Velez Ladrón de Guevara. The first pastoral visit recorded in the books was made by [[Antonio Claudio Álvarez de Quiñones]], [[Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Bogotá|Archbishop of Santa Fé de Bogotá]], in 1730.

The Jesuits occupied the Hacienda La Compañia estate in Firavitoba until their expulsion from the possessions of the Spanish crown in the Americas and the Philippines in 1767. It was then subdivided and sold, but sections of walls of the old estate remain.<ref name='Tutiempo'/><ref>[[#CITEREF Gonzalez Mora| Gonzalez Mora 2004]], p.125, Note 38.</ref>

In 1781, the ''encomienda'' came to an end when Firavitoba was granted the higher status of parish (''parroquia''), with a mayor and a priest in residence.<ref name='Tutiempo'/> The indigenous inhabitants were transferred to [[Nobsa]] to serve out the remainder of their ''encomienda'' obligations, but were returned before the popular uprising against the Spanish authorities in the [[Viceroyalty of New Granada]] known as the ''[[Revolt of the Comuneros (New Granada)|Revolt of the Comuneros]]'', from May through October of that year, although they lost some of their land.

=== Independence ===

Several years after the revolt and [[Colombian Declaration of Independence]] on July 20, 1810, in Santa Fé de Bogotá came the [[Spanish reconquest of New Granada]] in 1815–1816, under General [[Pablo Morillo]]. In 1816, the priest in Firavitoba, Carlos Suarez, was imprisoned for preaching freedom. The liberation campaign of 1819 peaked with the battles of [[Battle of Vargas Swamp|Vargas Swamp]], at Paipa, very close to Firavitoba, and of [[Puente de Boyacá]] (The Bridge of Boyaca), at which Firavitoba freedom fighter Cayetano Avella was killed. These two battles led to the full independence of New Granada and the creation of [[Gran Colombia]] in August, 1819. Gran Colombia was dissolved in 1830, with the region becoming part of the [[Republic of New Granada]], formed in November 1831.

Although several public primary schools were established in Firavitoba before 1832, during the Republican era the importance of the municipality diminished and it passed largely unremarked in national affairs. Earthquakes in 1826 and 1827 caused serious damage to the church, but the local governmental authorities did little to make repairs. 

In 1869, a new parish priest arrived in Firavitoba, and the situation began to improve. Father Ignacio Ramón Avella proposed the building of a new and imposing church in the town, Nuestra Señora de las Nieves (Our Lady of the Snows). It was to be based on the model he had brought back from Paris of a European church, and built of stone carted from the Pedregal district of Sogamoso.<ref name='RCN'>{{cite news |last=Osorio Muñoz |first=Mauricio |date=October 5, 2011 |title=Nuestra Señora de las Nieves un patrimonio de los firavitobenses|trans-title=Our Lady of the Snows, Heritage of Firavitoba |url=https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/CMS-7809226 |language=Spanish |work=Crónicas RCN, Radio Cadena Nacional |location=Colombia |access-date=October 19, 2018 }}</ref>

Construction began four years later in 1873, and was carried out under his personal direction. Deep foundations were dug and laid. When the time came for the raising of the walls, most of Firatoba's residents participated in a day-long expedition to fetch material for the scaffolding, crossing [[Lake Tota]] to the village of Bogüita for the timbers.

=== 20th century ===
[[File:Majestuosa bajo el cielo.jpg|thumb|Nuestra Señora de las Nieves, Firavitoba]]
==== Nuestra Señora de las Nieves ====

Father Ignacio Ramón Avella died in 1901, and was much mourned in Firavitoba. 

The church was still under construction but the original model had been lost, complicating progress. Work languished until 1916, when the priest Luis Abdénago Zambrano arrived in Firavitoba. He commissioned a new design, from Father Hugo Orjuela, and work resumed under the direction of Gregorio Gómez. 

1937 saw completion of the church roof, providing protection for the finished back and side walls. Construction of the facade had been postponed by order of the [[Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Tunja|bishop of Tunja]], [[Crisanto Luque Sánchez]], until the roof was in place. Work on the facade then progressed without further interruption. The north tower of the church was finished in 1946, and the south tower in 1949, completing the exterior of the church. Work then focused on the interior of the church, Luis Alberto Molano supervising installation of the central altar.

Firavitoba's church was officially completed in 1976, just over one hundred years after work began.<ref name='Tutiempo'/> The consecration of Nuestra Señora de las Nieves was performed by the [[Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Tunja|Archbishop of Tunja]], Augusto Trujillo Arango. It is the third largest church in Colombia, built entirely of stone in the [[Gothic Revival architecture|neo-Gothic]] style, and is a main feature of the municipality.<ref name='El Espectador reliquias'>{{cite news |last=El Espectador editorial staff |date=April 24, 2015 |title=Las reliquias robadas de la iglesia de Firavitoba |trans-title=Relics Stolen From Firavitoba’s Church |url=https://www.elespectador.com/noticias/nacional/reliquias-robadas-de-iglesia-de-firavitoba-articulo-556977 |language=Spanish |work=El Espectador |location=Colombia |access-date=October 19, 2018 }}</ref>     

==== Other key events ====

The arrival of the telegraph in Firavitoba in 1919, and of the public telephone in 1926, facilitated communication with other regions. Running water was connected to homes in 1929, and the road to Sogamoso was completed in 1933.

After many interruptions, the [[Paipa]] road running through the Vargas Swamp was completed in 1951, just over twenty years after roadworks began.

The Pro-Luz Company of Firavitoba was established at the beginning of 1953 to install electric lighting in the municipality, successfully implementing a project that dated back to 1936.

In 1962, the national Minister of Health and other government figures came to Firavitoba for the laying of the foundation stone of the clinic, on the southwestern side of the School Concentration. It was built with the support of the American government. 

On December 2, 1962, the Municipal Council adopted the official flag and coat of arms of Firavitoba. The flag is green with a diagonal stripe from bottom left to top right in the yellow, blue and red of the [[Flag of Colombia|Colombian flag]]. The coat of arms is a green shield with a golden cathedral representing the town through the church of Our Lady of the Snows. Two gold and green interlaced ears of wheat top the shield, the same motif repeating around its blue border to symbolize fertility of the land and agriculture in the municipality. The shield is flanked by four flags of Firavitoba, two on each side, symbolizing its patriotic citizens.<ref name='Alcaldía'/>

In 1963, the secondary school curriculum leading to the qualification of ''[[Education in Colombia|bachiller]]'' was introduced in Firavitoba, with the first students graduating in 1973.

In 1965, the sewer system was built through the streets of the town.

In 1985, a proposal to pipe water from [[Lake Tota]] to the municipality was negotiated. The inhabitants of Firavitoba participated in community action to contribute 1,000 pesos towards compensation costs for the water transfer.

June 1988 marked the first mayoral election in Firavitoba. Initially every two years, the election is now held every four years. The mayor's term of office begins on January 1.

At the beginning of 1989, land was granted to national telecommunications company [[Telefónica Telecom|Telecom]] on the site of a demolished children's school north of the main square, to build local headquarters. This was intended to facilitate rollout of the municipal telephone network, a project planned since 1978.

On January 19, 1995, a powerful earthquake struck the department of Boyacá. In Firavitoba, both towers of the church of Nuestra Señora de las Nieves were badly damaged. Reconstruction work began that same year and continued until 2000. The local community organized fundraising events, which included two festivals, two bazaars, and a beauty contest, and several companies and individuals also made donations. These efforts raised some 30 million [[Colombian peso|peso]]s towards the cost of reconstruction. The total cost of work was anticipated in 1999 to be 180 million pesos.<ref name='El Tiempo unidos'>{{cite news |last=El Tiempo editorial staff |date=March 11, 1997 |title=Unidos en torno a su templo |trans-title=United Around Their Church |url=https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/MAM-699756 |language=Spanish |work=El Tiempo |location=Colombia |access-date=October 19, 2018 }}</ref><ref name='El Tiempo obra'>{{cite news |last=El Tiempo editorial staff |date=May 28, 1999 |title=Obra, fruto del esfuerzo ciudadano |trans-title=Works Stem From Citizens’ Efforts |url=https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/CMS-7809226 |language=Spanish |work=El Tiempo |location=Colombia |access-date=October 19, 2018 }}</ref>

=== 21st Century ===

In 2005, work began under government contract to surface the Firavitoba-[[Vargas Swamp Lancers|Vargas Swamp]] road which, if completed, would link to [[Paipa]] and reduce travel distance to [[Tunja]]. In the same year, the mayors of [[Sogamoso]], Iza and Firavitoba signed a contract with the [[Sugamuxi Province|Sugamuxi]] Consortium to repair and improve the road connecting the three municipalities, replacing an earlier contract with another consortium that had run into difficulty. The project was for the paving of the road to a width of {{convert|7|m| ft}}, at a cost of over 3.5 billion pesos.

In 2012, a new municipal library was purpose-built in the town. It was inaugurated by the national Ministry of Culture on December 11 of that year.<ref name='Mincultura'>{{cite web |url=http://www.mincultura.gov.co/prensa/noticias/Paginas/2012-12-10_50866.aspx |title= Firavitoba, una iglesia con pueblo y biblioteca |date=December 10, 2012 |trans-title=Firavitoba, a Church with a Town and Library |website=www.mincultura.gov.co |access-date=October 18, 2018 |language=Spanish }}</ref> Its {{convert|300|m2}} provide a general area with bookshelves, reading tables for adults, computers, and a special zone for children, as well as bathrooms for adults and children and facilities compliant with regulatory requirements for people with disabilities.

In 2015, after renovation works, a park on the site of Firavitoba's first Dominican church was inaugurated in honor of Sister Gabriela de San Martín, founder of the [[Dominican Order|Dominican Community]] of [[Catherine of Siena|Saint Catherine of Siena]], who was born at the Jesuit Hacienda de la Compañía on May 22, 1848.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://firavitobamipueblito.blogspot.com/2015/05/inauguracion-del-parque-sor-gabriela.html |title=Inauguración del Parque Sor Gabriela |last=Rojas |first=Adriana |date=May 28, 2015 |access-date=October 20, 2018 |language=Spanish |trans-title=Inauguration of Sister Gabriela Park |website=www.firavitobamipueblito.blogspot.com }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.municipios.com.co/pyr/__que_personaje_famoso_nacio_en_firavitoba__boyaca__colombia__/ |title=¿Qué personaje famoso nació en Firavitoba, Boyacá (Colombia)? |date=January 7, 2014 |trans-title=What famous personality was born in Firavitoba, Boyacá, Colombia? |website=www.municipios.com.co |access-date=October 20, 2018 |language=Spanish }}</ref>

In 2017, repair work began some of Firatoba's streets, and four-lane road-widening work was completed. New rainwater drains were built, repairing damage that had been done to the old sewage system during works in 2015.

== Economy ==
The main economic activity of Firavitoba is [[livestock]] farming for beef and dairy production. Crop growing is the second most important sector of the local economy, mainly producing potato, wheat, corn, beans, peas, and barley, with other vegetables produced on a smaller scale. The principal activity in the mining sector is [[limestone]] [[quarry]]ing for [[cement]] production.<ref>{{cite book |last=Pedraza Puerto |first=Luis Edilberto |title=Plan de Desarrollo Firavitoba 2012-2015 |trans-title=Firavitoba Development Plan 2012-2015 |date=2012 |url=http://cdim.esap.edu.co/bancomedios/Documentos%20PDF/firavitobaboyacápd20122015.pdf#page=43|language=Spanish |publisher=Alcaldía de Firavitoba|access-date=October 20, 2018}}</ref>
