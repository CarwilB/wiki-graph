'''Maricao''' ({{IPA|es|maɾiˈkao}}) is a [[Maricao barrio-pueblo|town]] and the second-least populous [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]]; it is located at the western edge of the [[Cordillera Central, Puerto Rico|Cordillera Central]]. It is a small town set around a small square in hilly terrain, north of [[San Germán, Puerto Rico|San Germán]], [[Sabana Grande, Puerto Rico|Sabana Grande]] and [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]]; south of [[Las Marias, Puerto Rico|Las Marías]] and [[Lares, Puerto Rico|Lares]], southeast of [[Mayagüez, Puerto Rico|Mayagüez]], and west of [[Adjuntas, Puerto Rico|Adjuntas]]. Maricao has 7 barrios including [[Maricao barrio-pueblo|Maricao Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city).

The town borders the [[Maricao Fish Hatchery]], a [[fish hatchery]] made up of tanks and pools in a garden setting, where some 25,000 fish are raised yearly to stock farm fishponds and island lakes.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2015-05-20|title=Agua dulce|url=https://www.drna.pr.gov/programas-y-proyectos/agua-dulce/|access-date=2021-08-26|website=DRNA|language=en-US}}</ref> The hatchery is part of the [[Maricao State Forest]], also known as [[Monte del Estado]]. Though of dryer vegetation than the other mountain forest, Maricao is home to large number of [[List of Puerto Rican birds|bird species]]. Its stone observation tower provides far-flung views to the coast and the [[Mona Passage]].

Maricao has a [[2020 United States census|2020 census]] population of 4,455.<ref>{{Cite journal|date=2004-04-28|title=Census of Population and Housing, 2000 [United States]: Summary File 4, Puerto Rico|url=http://dx.doi.org/10.3886/icpsr13563.v1|access-date=2021-08-26|website=ICPSR Data Holdings|doi=10.3886/icpsr13563.v1 |url-access=subscription}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:Haciendajuanitamaricao.jpg|thumb|right|Hacienda Juanita in Maricao]]
Maricao was founded on April 1, 1874, when Bernardo Collado, Julián Ayala, Francisco M. Sojo, Napoleón Pietri and Leoncio S. Martínez requested the Provincial Deputation authorization to establish an official town. It originally belonged to the district of [[San Germán, Puerto Rico|San Germán]], but they alleged the roads were too long and almost impassable. Their request made references to a [[parish]] already established in 1866.

During the 19th century [[coffee]] boom, Maricao and other regions surged economically. When the spurt of coffee in the island ended by the start of the 20th century, the economic conditions deteriorated again. Many of the old plantation houses have been turned into museums to stimulate the tourism industry. While Puerto Rico still has a niche in the gourmet coffee market, the large scale coffee growing which built Maricao is no longer economically feasible.

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a colony of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Maricao was 8,312.<ref>{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Report on the census of Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office| url=https://archive.org/details/reportoncensusof00unitiala/page/156/mode/2up |year=1900|publisher=Washington : Govt. print. off.|page=156}}</ref>

On September 20, 2017 [[Hurricane Maria]] struck Puerto Rico. Several major landslides covered roads into Maricao. Highways and bridges were washed away by flooding rivers. Residents were left incommunicado.<ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. Deslizamientos dejan incomunicados a residentes de Maricao| trans-title=Maria, a name we won't forget. ==Geography==
Maricao is mountainous and rugged and belongs to the Western portion of the [[Cordillera Central, Puerto Rico|Cordillera Central]].<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/maricao-municicpality/|title=Maricao Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190621041650/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/maricao-municicpality/|archive-date=2019-06-21|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Special Communities===

==Culture==
===Tourism===
[[File:Coffee tree atop the Maricao mountains in Puerto Rico (5661610485).jpg|thumb|left| Coffee tree atop the Maricao mountains]]
Due to its importance as a coffee producer in the past, some of Maricao's coffee [[hacienda]]s were turned into museums or guest houses: Hacienda Delicias and [[Hacienda Juanita]], which has since closed. Other places for tourists to visit are the Bambúa Recreational Center and the [[Maricao Fish Hatchery|Maricao Fish Nursery]]. Some natural spots to visit are the [[Monte del Estado]] forest reserve, the [[Maricao River]] Natural Protected Area, the Prieto Lake and the Salto de Curet waterfall.

===Festivals and events===
[[File:Fiesta del Acabe del Café en Maricao, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|{{lang|es|Fiesta del Acabe del Café}} in Maricao in 2014]]
Maricao celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in June. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Juan Bautista}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref name="J.D. 2006">{{cite web | author=J.D. | title=Maricao | website=Link To Puerto Rico.com | date=2006-05-02 | url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/maricao.html# | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18 | archive-date=2014-04-01 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140401113729/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/maricao.html | url-status=live }}</ref>

Known locally as the {{lang|es|Festival del Acabe de Café}}, the End of the Coffee Harvest Festival is celebrated on [[Presidents' Day|President's Day]] weekend in February.<ref>{{cite web | title=Festival del Acabe del Café - Maricao, Puerto Rico Events | website=Lonely Planet | date=31 March 2020 | url=https://www.lonelyplanet.com/puerto-rico/maricao/events/festival-del-acabe-del-cafe/a/poi-fes/1560061/358129 | access-date=29 August 2020}}</ref> It celebrates the tradition wherein the Hacienda owners would provide a feast for their workers at the end of the coffee harvest. The festival was founded in 1977 by then mayor, Vicente Byron.

Other festivals and events celebrated in Maricao include:
* Indigenous Day Festival - April - celebrated in the Indiera barrios of Maricao
* [[Mother's Day (United States)|Homage to Mothers]] – May
* Youth Festival – July
* [[New Year's Eve]] celebrations – December

==Economy==
[[File:Coffee tree and orange trees, multicroping in Maricao, Puerto Rico. (5662136142).jpg|thumb|Multi-crop farm in Maricao]]
For decades, Maricao's economy has relied on [[agriculture]], specifically coffee plantations. Fruits and vegetables are also grown in the town. Currently only one factory is established in Maricao, ''Fenwal Blood Technologies''. Devices and materials that are used in blood donation, transfusion, transportation and storage are produced there. Tourism has also played an important role in the town's economy.

For two decades, this municipality has had the highest level of [[Child poverty#Developed countries|children living in poverty]] in Puerto Rico.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/nocedelapobrezainfantilenpuertorico-2483916/|title=No cede la pobreza infantil en Puerto Rico|date=24 March 2019|website=El Nuevo Dia|language=es|access-date=24 March 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190324192449/https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/nocedelapobrezainfantilenpuertorico-2483916/|archive-date=24 March 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

According to the 2000 census, Maricao is the second least populous [[municipality]] in [[Puerto Rico]], above the island municipality of [[Culebra, Puerto Rico|Culebra]], with 6,449 inhabitants. The 2020 census shows the municipality remains the second least populated with a population of 4,455. The population of the town has decreased during the past decades in part due to the decline in coffee production and the deteriorating economy. In 1899, the population was 8,312. Making the [[population density]] is 174.2 people per square mile (67.0/km<sup>2</sup>).

As a whole, Puerto Rico is mainly made up of people from a [[Criollo people|Criollo]] (born on the Island of European descent) or [[Spanish people|Spanish]] and [[Ethnic groups in Europe|European]] descent, with small groups of [[Ethnic groups of Africa|African]] and [[Asian people]]. Statistics taken from the 2000 census shows that 90.3% of Maricaeños have [[Spanish people|Spanish]] or [[white people|White]] origin, 3.0% are [[black people|black]], 0.2% are [[Amerindian]] etc. The majority of the local population are partly descended from pre Columbian indigenous inhabitants.

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
Maricao's first mayor was Juan Ferrer y Arnijas, whose term ran from 1874 to 1876. [[Gilberto Pérez Valentín]], alias "El Enano" or the Dwarf, was mayor for seven consecutive terms until he lost his seat in 2020 to [[Wilfredo "Juny" Ruiz]], who was re-elected in 2024.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Caraballo|first=Harry Rodríguez|title=Juramenta hoy el primer alcalde popular de Maricao en 27 años|url=https://www.metro.pr/pr/noticias/2021/01/12/juramenta-hoy-el-primer-alcalde-popular-de-maricao-en-27-anos.html|access-date=2021-06-18|website=Metro|language=es}}</ref>

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district V]], which is represented by two Senators. In [[Puerto Rican general election, 2024|2024]], [[Marially González Huertas]] and [[Jamie Barlucea]], from the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] and [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]], respectively, were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241115225812/https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml |date=November 15, 2024 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag of Maricao derives its colors, design and symbolism from the municipal shield. It consists of a green cloth, with the three usual dimensions of the municipal flags of [[Puerto Rico]], crossing from an end to another a yellow band with three points. The color green symbolizes the vegetation of the municipality and the yellow band symbolizes the mountains of the region.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=MARICAO | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/MARICAO.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001507/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/MARICAO.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
In a silver field, resides an inverted V-shaped green band. Contained in the band are five golden huts. To either side and below the band are a total of three Maricao (''[[Byrsonima spicata]]'') tree branches with flowers. Surrounding the shield below and to either side are two coffee tree branches. Above it is placed a gold mural crown with three towers outlined in black with green doors and windows.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

===Name===
[[File:Byrsonima spicata illustration.jpg|thumb|Illustration of Byrsonima spicata or the Maricao tree]]
The origin of its name has two versions. It is said to come from the name of the Maricao tree (''Byrsonima spicata'') which has yellow flowers and grows in the region. The second version is a legend about a [[Taíno people|Taíno]] woman called María that, during the [[Spanish colonization]], fell in love with a Spanish soldier. She informed him of an attack planned by her tribe and the Spanish took the offensive. María was then taken prisoner by her people and tried for [[treason]]. As was the tradition, she was tied to a tree and sacrificed. Allegedly "cao" means "[[sacrifice]]" so Maricao means "María's sacrifice"; this is considered [[folk etymology]].

==Education==
[[File:Endangered Species Posters (7217683840).jpg|thumb|Kids gathering information on display about endangered species at a school in Maricao on May 4, 2012]]
Maricao has several public and private schools distributed through several regions. Public education is handled by the [[Puerto Rico Department of Education]].

==Transportation==
There is no direct [[highway]] connection to Maricao. [[Puerto Rico Highway 119]] and [[Puerto Rico Highway 120]] lead from the north, while [[Puerto Rico Highway 121]] and [[Puerto Rico Highway 105]] lead from the south. Maricao lies about three hours from [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]].

There are 13 bridges in Maricao.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Maricao Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/maricao/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=19 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190220181326/http://bridgereports.com/pr/maricao/|archive-date=20 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>
