'''Callalli District''' is one of twenty [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of the [[Caylloma Province]] in Peru and is located within the geographical area of the [[Colca Canyon]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=April 23, 2008 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref>

Its capital, Callalli, lies on an ancient trade route for [[Obsidian]] that ran from [[Chivay]] to [[Lake Titicaca]] around 1800-200BC. The earliest findings in the region, carvings of camelids in the Mollupunko Cave, date around 8,000 years back.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|title=Monografia y Fuentes Documentales sobre la historia del distrito de Callalli|last=Ramos Yuera|first=Walter Billy|publisher=Publicont S.A.C.|year=2018|pages=20}}</ref> The town of Callalli was founded as a Spanish [[Reductions|Reduccion]] in 1573 with the initial name of ''Alcantara de Callalli''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.yumpu.com/es/document/read/14190471/fragmentos-de-la-historia-del-distrito-de-callalli-colca-life/37|title=este lad|last=Yumpu.com|website=yumpu.com|language=es|access-date=2019-07-16}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.facebook.com/Autocolca/posts/1845351705543297|title=Autoridad Autónoma del Colca y Anexos|website=www.facebook.com|language=en|access-date=2019-07-16}}</ref>

Callalli is the main seat of a catholic pastoral team that takes care of a 7,000&nbsp;km² area including the towns of Callalli, [[Sibayo District|Sibayo]], [[Tisco District|Tisco]], Imata and [[Caylloma District|Caylloma]]. The parish is led by the Austrian priest and missionary Franz Windischhofer since 1991, whose work was acknowledged by the Peruvian government for "building a community of peace" in Peru since 1980.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.pe.undp.org/content/peru/es/home/presscenter/articles/2012/09/21/se-entregaron-los-premios-por-la-paz-2012-.html|title=Se entregaron los “Premios por la Paz 2012”|website=El PNUD en Perú|language=es|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://diariocorreo.pe/edicion/arequipa/conozca-al-sacerdote-que-aprendio-quechua-para-llevar-la-palabra-de-dios-fotos-748125/|title=Conozca al sacerdote que aprendió quechua para llevar la palabra de Dios (FOTOS) {{!}} Foto 1 de 4|date=2017-05-07|website=Diario Correo|language=es|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref>

== Tourism ==
*'''Alpaca Fair''', a [[Caylloma Province]] regional fair of [[Alpaca]] breeders convening in Callalli each June to present their alpacas.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://infoalpacas.com.pe/esperan-comercializar-300-alpacas-por-dia-en-feria-de-callalli/|title=Esperan comercializar 300 alpacas por día en feria de Callalli|website=Infoalpacas|language=es-ES|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref> 
*'''Mollepunko''' '''Caves''', a rocky shelter and shallow cave system dating from about 6000 years BC,<ref name=":0" /> featuring more than 20 carved [[cave painting]]s.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://viestramagazine.com/colca-canyon-perus-lesser-known-natural-wonder-p421-103.htm|title=COLCA CANYON: PERU'S LESSER KNOWN NATURAL WONDER|website=viestramagazine.com|language=en|access-date=2019-07-13}}</ref> Said paintings, made by pre-Inca inhabitants, show the early domestication of [[Camelid]]s among other figures. The caves were probably used for rituals in the pre-Inca era, and as a grave site after the Incas.  Local legends point to an [[Inca Empire]] treasure being buried close to a large black boulder, as one of the abandoned deliveries of gold to buy free [[Atahualpa]] after his execution.{{Citation needed|date=July 2019}} Multiple excavation attempts – including an intact, 3m long tunnel in front of the caves and a 2.5m deep man-made ravine under the large boulder, point to [[treasure hunting]] having taken place.
*'''Enchanted Castles of Callalli''', a rock formation featuring jagged boulders directly above the city of Callalli.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://rpp.pe/peru/actualidad/callalli-tierra-de-los-castillos-encantados-noticia-732627|title=Callalli: tierra de los castillos encantados|website=RPP|language=es|access-date=2019-07-13}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.colcaperu.gob.pe/callalli/|title=Callalli – Autocolca|language=es|access-date=2019-07-16}}</ref>
*'''Banos Termales de Inca''', a thermal bath complex between Callalli and [[Sibayo District|Sibayo]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.google.com/maps/place/Ba%C3%B1os+Termales+de+Inca/@-15.4996837,-71.4546719,15.13z/|title=Google Maps|website=Google Maps|language=en|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref>
*'''Choqueshisha''', a ritual site inside of Callalli where local women thank [[Pachamama]] for the gift of water with a sacrifice of [[Llama]] fat, corn and other local products once a year.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.google.com/maps/place/Choqueshisha/@-15.5052838,-71.4412289,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m5!3m4!1s0x9169c5a973f4f50f:0xf73f421dea63c76!8m2!3d-15.5052838!4d-71.4390402?shorturl=1|title=Google Maps|website=Google Maps|language=en|access-date=2019-07-16}}</ref>
* '''Puente de Pachachaca''', colonial bridge built with ashlars made of volcanic material and boulder. On the road from [[Inca road system|Cápac Ñan]] that runs from [[Cusco]] to [[Arequipa]] and on the [[Colca River]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.google.com/maps/place/PUENTE+PACHACHACA/@-15.478891,-71.2354689,7082m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x6480082f86ad038e!8m2!3d-15.4985551!4d-71.2024355|title=Google Maps|website=Google Maps|language=en|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref> The bridge is featured on the upper right part of the coat of arms of Callalli.
* '''Iglesia Callalli de San Antonio de Padua''', a Catholic parish church under the patronage of [[Anthony of Padua|San Antonio de Padua]], built in an [[Andean Baroque]] style, located in the town center of Callalli. It features a cross-shaped blueprint and a baptistry, two symmetrical towers on the sides and a vaulted roof. Inside are artworks depicting the miracles of San Antonio.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.colcavalley.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=54&Itemid=60 |title=Callalli |access-date=6 June 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090314234058/http://www.colcavalley.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=54&Itemid=60 |archive-date=14 March 2009 }}</ref>
*'''Chulpas Tahuaya''', an above-ground, Pre-Inca grave site featuring four small stone buildings that contain well-preserved bones and skulls.<ref name="photos.google.com">{{Cite web|url=https://photos.google.com/share/AF1QipOaIxWrJTGXfINQpeY3MJ40-EIQWunlvziJiAltw-CpTwHwOhHj5kurYZa86nDoUg?key=SUh3Nks0VU82aVJSOE5OUGlnM21udzNuaElwVEhn|title=Arequipa: Chullpas Tahuaya e iglesia Llapa Yanahuara|website=Google Photos|language=en|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref> 
*'''Llapa Yanahuara''', an Collagua-Inca-Colonial town, with a church built on top of a pre-Hispanic [[Huaca]].<ref name="photos.google.com"/>
*'''Ventana del Colca''', a rock formation in the form of a window as a natural entrance to the valley and [[Colca Canyon]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.deaventura.pe/destino/ventana-del-colca|title=Ventana del Colca: Destino De Aventura|website=De Aventura|language=es|access-date=2019-07-13}}</ref>
*'''Choqolaqa''', a large rock formation about 2 hours outside Callalli, belonging to the district of [[Tisco District|Tisco]], featuring white towers of eroded rocks. A road leads up to an altitude of 4700m; the rock formation itself can be reached after a short hike at an altitude of 4950m.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.google.com/maps/place/Choqolaqa+bosque+de+piedras/@-15.157618,-71.2986446,1082m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x9169af5f66ae48f9:0xc95b925fa9e382f8!8m2!3d-15.1545828!4d-71.2976056?shorturl=1|title=Google Maps|website=Google Maps|language=en|access-date=2019-07-15}}</ref>

== Ethnic groups ==
The people in the district are mainly [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Quechua language|Quechua]] is the language which the majority of the population (62.06%) learnt to speak in childhood, 37.48% of the residents started speaking using the Spanish language ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe]  {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=January 27, 2013 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007, Frequencias: Preguntas de Población: Idioma o lengua con el que aprendió hablar (in Spanish)</ref>
