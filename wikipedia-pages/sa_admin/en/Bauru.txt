'''Bauru''' ({{IPA|pt|bawˈɾu}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the [[Interior of São Paulo|interior of São Paulo state]], recognized as the most populous city in the Central-West region of São Paulo. It is one of the 19 municipalities comprising the Bauru Immediate Geographic Region, which is one of four [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|immediate regions]] within the Bauru Intermediate Geographic Region, encompassing a total of 48 municipalities.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv100600.pdf |title=Divisão Regional do Brasil em Regiões Geográficas Imediatas e Regiões Geográficas Intermediárias |year=2017 |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE)}}</ref>

Situated northwest of the [[São Paulo (city)|state capital]], Bauru is approximately 326 km away and covers an area of 667.684 km².<ref name="IBGE_Bauru"/> According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]]'s [[2022 Brazilian Census|Census of 2022]], the city had an estimated population of {{formatnum:379146}} inhabitants,<ref name="IBGE_Bauru"/> making it the [[List of municipalities in the state of São Paulo by population|18th most populous municipality in São Paulo]].

Founded in 1896, Bauru experienced significant population growth due to the [[March to the West]], a government initiative under [[Getúlio Vargas]] to promote development and settlement in Brazil's central region. In the early 20th century, the city's infrastructure expanded with the arrival of the railway and, later, highways. [[Coffee production in Brazil|Coffee cultivation]] became prominent in the early 1900s, but Bauru transitioned into an industrialized city, aligning with Brazil's [[Industry in Brazil|national industrialization]] starting in the 1930s. Industry, alongside the [[Tertiary sector of the economy|service sector]], is the primary source of municipal revenue, contributing to Bauru's 68th highest GDP in Brazil. Since the 1950s, sugarcane production has gained prominence in agriculture.

Several highways, including Marechal Rondon, Comandante João Ribeiro de Barros, Cesário José de Carvalho, and Engenheiro João Batista Cabral Renno, connect Bauru to various municipalities in São Paulo. The city is a critical hub for air, road, and rail transport. Beyond its economic significance, Bauru is a key cultural center in its region. The Bauru Municipal Botanical Garden and the Bauru Forest Garden are vital environmental preservation areas, while the Celina Lourdes Alves Neves Municipal Theater, the Bauru Cultural Center, and the Bauru Automobile Club are notable urban landmarks. The Municipal Culture Secretariat oversees cultural projects and events, enriching Bauru's cultural life.

== Etymology ==
One widely accepted theory regarding the origin of Bauru's name suggests it derives from ''mbai-yurú'', meaning "waterfall" or "steep river" in the [[Tupi language]]. Alternatively, it may come from ''ybá-uru'', translating to "basket of fruits," or "bauruz," the name given to the indigenous people living along the Batalha River. [[Teodoro Fernandes Sampaio|Teodoro Sampaio]] proposed that Bauru is a corruption of "upaú-r-u" or "upaú-r-y," referring to a "river of the lagoon," from Tupi terms "Upá" or "Upaú" (lagoon or dammed water) and "U" or "I" (flowing water or river).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.todafruta.com.br/portal/icNoticiaAberta.asp?idNoticia=6165 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IA7YbH1?url=http://www.todafruta.com.br/portal/icNoticiaAberta.asp?idNoticia=6165 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Bauru - SP |author=Toda Fruta |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref>

== History ==
=== Early history ===
[[File:Slaved.guarani.debret.jpg|thumb|left|Painting depicting the [[Guaraní people]], one of the indigenous groups vying for control of the Bauru region in the 18th century.]]
[[File:Primeira Locomotiva Chefada em Bauru - 24 - 9 - 910 - 1, Acervo do Museu Paulista da USP (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|First locomotive to arrive in Bauru, September 24, 1910.]]
The area now occupied by Bauru was historically contested by two indigenous groups: the [[Kaingang]] and the [[Guaraní people|Guarani]]. In the 18th century, [[Bandeirantes|''bandeirantes'']] attempted to settle in the region, a key crossing point for the fluvial expeditions heading to [[Mato Grosso]] and [[Goiás]], but were repelled by attacks from local indigenous groups. Non-Indians only managed to settle in the region in the 19th century, with settlers arriving from São Paulo’s coast, [[Minas Gerais]], and [[Rio de Janeiro (state)|Rio de Janeiro]].<ref name="Câmara_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.camarabauru.sp.gov.br/Municipio/Page.aspx?tipo=historico |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IAAJSTm?url=http://www.camarabauru.sp.gov.br/Municipio/Page.aspx?tipo=historico |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=O município e a Câmara Municipal |author=Municipal Chamber |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref> After 1850, pioneers from São Paulo and Minas Gerais began exploring the vast region between the Botucatu Ridge, the [[Tietê River]], the [[Paranapanema River]], and the [[Paraná River]], previously inhabited by Kaingang groups.<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

In 1856, Felicíssimo Antônio Pereira, originally from Minas Gerais, acquired land and established the Fazenda das Flores near present-day central Bauru. In 1884, part of this estate (also known as Campos Novos de Bauru) was subdivided to form the São Sebastião do Bauru settlement. Despite Kaingang attacks and relative isolation, the district progressed and became a district of [[Agudos]] in 1888. The influx of migrants from eastern São Paulo and Minas Gerais led to Bauru's emancipation as a municipality on August 1, 1896.<ref name="Prefeitura_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/arquivos/arquivos_site/publicacoes/Primeiros%20Tempos%20da%20Nossa%20Bauru.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IABEptQ?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/arquivos/arquivos_site/publicacoes/Primeiros%20Tempos%20da%20Nossa%20Bauru.pdf |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Primeiros Tempos da Nossa Bauru |author=Municipality |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref>

Bauru was officially established as a municipality in 1896. The exploration of this region of [[Federative units of Brazil|São Paulo state]] occurred extensively in the last decade of the 19th century and the first decade of the 20th century.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The lands west of the Botucatu Ridge, beyond the Agudos Ridge, never supported the [[Slavery in Brazil|slavery system]] that prevailed in much of Brazil until 1888. The municipality of [[Lençóis Paulista]] marked the geographical limit of slavery in that region of São Paulo. This factor influenced the demographic and ethnic composition of the region. Consequently, the proportion of Black and mixed-race individuals in Bauru is relatively lower than in other parts of São Paulo, while the population of Asian descent, particularly Japanese, is higher than the national average.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

=== Post-Emancipation period ===
[[File:Bauru (SP).tif|thumb|Bauru in the 1970s. [[National Archives of Brazil|National Archives]].]]
[[File:BauruTrem.jpg|thumb|View of Bauru with the railway in the foreground. For decades, the city was a major rail hub.]]
Initially, the new municipality relied on coffee cultivation, despite having less fertile soil compared to other parts of the state. In 1906, Bauru was selected as the starting point for the Northwest Brazil Railway, connecting Bauru to [[Corumbá]] in [[Mato Grosso do Sul]], near the [[Bolivia]] border. During the first half of the 20th century, Bauru emerged as the primary economic hub of a vast region encompassing Western São Paulo, Northern [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]], and Mato Grosso do Sul.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The lack of a robust industrial sector prevented significant internal migration, such as the Northeastern migration that began in the 1930s to [[Greater São Paulo]] and Eastern São Paulo. The extermination of indigenous groups, particularly the Kaingang, was a tragic episode in the region’s incorporation into São Paulo’s territory. These factors underscored the importance of foreign immigration in shaping Bauru’s current ethnic and demographic profile.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

The [[March to the West]], initiated by [[Getúlio Vargas]]’s government to encourage development and settlement in Brazil’s [[Central-West Region, Brazil|Central-West]], attracted many settlers to this part of São Paulo.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Bauru’s population growth necessitated infrastructure investments, particularly as the industrial sector developed in the 1940s and 1950s.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Decree No. 5349, issued on October 18, 1904, formalized the creation of the Northwest Brazil Railway Company, with its route starting from Bauru. In July 1905, the tracks reached the municipality, extending the Sorocabana Railway. In 1906, the first newspaper, "O Bauru," was established, and in 1908, telephone services were introduced. On March 9, 1911, the Bauru Judicial District was created, and on March 16 of the same year, public lighting was installed. In 1913, the first school was established, and in 1928, the Portuguese Beneficence Society Hospital, the region’s first major hospital, was founded. On March 8, 1934, the first radio station, PRC-8 (later PRG-8) Bauru Radio Club, was launched, and on April 19, 1942, a new water supply service was inaugurated.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The cultural sector flourished in the 1940s and 1950s, exemplified by the opening of the Bauru Cultural Center on March 15, 1942, and the establishment of the Official Fine Arts Salon on July 16, 1950.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

Bauru is renowned for the [[Bauru (sandwich)|Bauru sandwich]], created by Bauru lawyer Casimiro Pinto Neto at the Ponto Chic bar in [[Largo do Paiçandu]], São Paulo, in 1934, while he was a student at the [[Law School, University of São Paulo|Law School of the University of São Paulo]]. The sandwich later gained fame through the "Zé do Esquinão" bar, located in downtown Bauru for decades. The original Bauru sandwich recipe, as prepared in the city, consists of [[Pão francês|French bread]], roast beef, tomato slices, thin cucumber pickle slices, and melted white cheese.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.portaldebauru.com.br/template.php?pagina=guia_comercial_ultima2.php&id_item=782&id_grupo=1&id_node=738 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IFVxWxm?url=http://www.portaldebauru.com.br/template.php?pagina=guia_comercial_ultima2.php&id_item=782&id_grupo=1&id_node=738 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=A História |author=Portal de Bauru |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref>

In the latter half of the 20th century, rail transport was gradually replaced by highway construction.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The city has since recorded strong development indicators, revitalizing degraded areas and now boasts a diversified industrial park with a skilled workforce. Its strategic location offers extensive transport options, including the largest road, rail, air, and waterway hub in São Paulo, along with reliable energy and telecommunications networks.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria">{{cite web |url=http://www.vivendobauru.com.br/economia/segmentos/industria |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IACd0u3?url=http://www.vivendobauru.com.br/economia/segmentos/industria |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=A indústria em Bauru |author=Vivendo Bauru |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

== Geography ==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], Bauru’s municipal area spans {{formatnum:667.684}} km².<ref name="IBGE_Bauru"/> Located at 22°18′54″ [[Latitude|south latitude]] and 49°03′39″ [[Longitude|west longitude]], it is 326 kilometers northwest of the [[São Paulo (city)|state capital]]. Bauru borders [[Reginópolis]] to the north, [[Arealva]] to the northeast, [[Pederneiras]] to the east, [[Piratininga]] to the south, [[Agudos]] to the southeast, and [[Avaí]] to the west.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia">{{cite web |url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/cidade/dados_geograficos.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IJf1V2U?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/cidade/dados_geograficos.aspx |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Dados Geográficos |author=Municipality |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref><ref name="Seade_Bauru">{{cite web |url=http://www.seade.gov.br/produtos/perfil/perfil.php?loc=60 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IJgAxE6?url=http://www.seade.gov.br/produtos/perfil/perfil.php?loc=60 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Perfil Municipal de Bauru |author=State System for Data Analysis (SEADE) |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Geomorphology and hydrography ===
[[File:Rio Batalha, Bauru.jpg|thumb|upright|left|[[Batalha River]] in Bauru.]]

Bauru’s terrain is predominantly undulating, with 64.71% of the territory characterized by rolling hills, while flat areas account for 23.85%.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/> The landscape is low-lying and dissected at its edges, a remnant of post-Cretaceous tropical denudation processes, with an average elevation of 526 meters.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ugb.org.br/home/artigos/SEPARATAS_RBG_Ano_7_n_1_2006/RBG_Ano_7_n_1_2006_43_64.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IJhciGh?url=http://www.ugb.org.br/home/artigos/SEPARATAS_RBG_Ano_7_n_1_2006/RBG_Ano_7_n_1_2006_43_64.pdf |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Lamelas (bandas onduladas) em argissolo vermelho-amarelo como indicadores da evolução do relevo: o caso das colinas médias do Platô de Bauru (SP) |author=Leonardo José Cordeiro Santos and Selma Simões de Castro |date=August 2006 |publisher=Brazilian Geomorphology Union (UGB) |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

The city’s soils are primarily sandy, with low drainage density, a characteristic of the Western São Paulo Plateau, influenced by the region’s warm climate for much of the year. Predominant soil types include red-yellow [[latosol]], found extensively, and red-yellow [[acrisol]], common on steeper slopes, both with medium to sandy textures. Latosol areas are prone to large [[gully|gullies]]. These soils are developed, stable, and well-drained but lose micro-aggregates under intense irregular occupation, leading to significant erosion.<ref name="UNESP_Relevo">{{cite web |url=http://prope.unesp.br/xxi_cic/27_31082848867.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IOEUPow?url=http://prope.unesp.br/xxi_cic/27_31082848867.pdf |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Análise morfométrica do relevo em área de expansão urbana: o caso do Córrego do Castelo - Bauru (SP) |author=Leonardo da Silva Thomazini and Cenira Maria Luppinaci da Cunha |publisher=[[São Paulo State University]] (UNESP) |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

The terrain’s density directly affects drainage networks, which, in turn, can alter the surface configuration. Bauru’s main rivers are the [[Bauru River]] and the [[Batalha River]]. The Bauru River originates near the urban perimeter in the former Fazenda Fortaleza (now a subdivision in the Lagoa Sul neighborhood), flowing 42 km to join the [[Tietê River]] between [[Boraceia]], [[Pederneiras]], and [[Itapuí]]. The Batalha River, originating in [[Agudos]], is a significant Tietê tributary, stretching 167 km. It supplies Bauru with water.<ref name="Channel_Bauru">{{cite web |url=http://www.brasilchannel.com.br/municipios//mostrar_municipio.asp?nome=Bauru&uf=SP&tipo=lazer |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IJkM3rv?url=http://www.brasilchannel.com.br/municipios//mostrar_municipio.asp?nome=Bauru&uf=SP&tipo=lazer |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Bauru - SP |author=Brasil Channel |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Climate ===
[[File:Bauru Céu.jpg|thumb|Sky over Bauru on a partly cloudy day.]]
[[File:Cores da Chuva.jpg|thumb|Rainbow near Bauru.]]
According to the Center for Meteorological and Climatic Research Applied to Agriculture (CEPAGRI) at the [[State University of Campinas]] (UNICAMP), Bauru’s climate is classified as a [[Subhumid temperate climate|tropical highland climate]] (''Aw'' per the [[Köppen climate classification]]), prevalent in central-western São Paulo. It features dry, mild winters and rainy, warm summers, with the warmest month’s average temperature exceeding {{formatnum:22}}°C.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cpa.unicamp.br/outras-informacoes/clima-dos-municipios-paulistas.html |title=Clima dos Municípios Paulistas |author=Center for Meteorological and Climatic Research Applied to Agriculture (CEPAGRI) |publisher=[[State University of Campinas]] (UNICAMP) |access-date=9 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120118093518/http://www.cpa.unicamp.br/outras-informacoes/clima-dos-municipios-paulistas.html |archive-date=18 January 2012}}</ref> [[Autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] serve as transitional seasons. The annual [[Precipitation|rainfall]] averages approximately {{formatnum:1330}} millimeters (mm), concentrated in spring and summer, with a significant decrease in winter.<ref name="Climatologia">{{cite web |url=http://www.cpa.unicamp.br/outras-informacoes/clima_muni_068.html |title=Bauru |author=CEPAGRI |publisher=UNICAMP |access-date=9 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120118162841/http://www.cpa.unicamp.br/outras-informacoes/clima_muni_068.html |archive-date=18 January 2012}}</ref>

During the rainy season, [[Precipitation|precipitation]] primarily falls as [[rain]], occasionally accompanied by [[hail]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://leianoticias.com.br/regiao/chuva-forte-com-granizo-causa-estragos-em-bauru/ |title=Chuva forte com granizo causa estragos em Bauru |date=31 January 2019 |access-date=19 July 2020 |archive-date=19 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200719231214/https://leianoticias.com.br/regiao/chuva-forte-com-granizo-causa-estragos-em-bauru/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> These events are sometimes marked by electrical discharges, such as [[Lightning|lightning]] and [[Thunderstorm|thunder]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.climatempo.com.br/noticia/2018/05/16/chuva-raios-e-trovoadas-em-bauru-sp--0462 |title=Chuva, raios e trovoadas em Bauru (SP) |author=Pinheiro, Marcelo |date=16 May 2018 |publisher=[[Climatempo]] |access-date=19 July 2020}}</ref> In winter, during the [[Dry season|dry season]], [[Relative humidity|relative humidity]] levels often drop below 30%.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://94fm.com.br/bauru-registra-maior-temperatura-desde-o-inicio-do-inverno/ |title=Baixa umidade do ar deixa Bauru em estado de atenção no final de semana |date=2 August 2013 |access-date=19 July 2020 |archive-date=19 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200719231216/https://94fm.com.br/bauru-registra-maior-temperatura-desde-o-inicio-do-inverno/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> This period also sees frequent [[Wildfire|wildfires]] in brushlands, contributing to [[Deforestation|deforestation]] and atmospheric pollution, which degrades [[Air quality index|air quality]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.recordpaulista.com.br/portal/noticias/4534 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68ILy5BP8?url=http://www.recordpaulista.com.br/portal/noticias/4534 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Queimadas em Bauru |date=9 March 2010 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref> Winter also brings polar [[Air mass|air masses]], some strong enough to lower temperatures to {{formatnum:10}}°C or below, occasionally causing rare [[Frost|frosts]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias/geral/2011/06/329819-bauru-registra-dia-mais-frio-em-11-anos.html |title=Bauru registra dia mais frio em 11 anos |author=Oshiro, Vitor |date=29 June 2011 |access-date=19 July 2020}}</ref><ref name="Frio-Bauru-SP">{{cite web |url=https://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias/geral/2019/07/558356-bauru-registra-a-menor-temperatura-dos-ultimos-19-anos--3-1-graus.html |title=Bauru registra a menor temperatura dos últimos 19 anos: 3,1 graus |date=6 July 2019 |access-date=19 July 2020}}</ref>

Bauru hosts the Institute of Meteorological Research (IPMet), a complementary unit of the [[São Paulo State University]] (UNESP), established in 1969 to conduct meteorological research for weather forecasting across São Paulo, as well as to quantify and monitor storms and rainfall using a [[weather radar]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ipmet.unesp.br/index2.php?menu_esq1=&abre=ipmet_html/institucional/IPMet_Historia.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68UW5LX06?url=http://www.ipmet.unesp.br/index2.php?menu_esq1=&abre=ipmet_html/institucional/IPMet_Historia.htm |archive-date=17 June 2012 |title=Meteorologia com radar desde 1974 |author=Institute of Meteorological Research (IPMet) |access-date=17 June 2012}}</ref>

In partnership with IPMet, the [[National Institute of Meteorology]] (INMET) installed an automatic [[weather station]] at the institute, operational since August 30, 2001. The station recorded a record low of {{formatnum:2.1}}°C on August 4, 2011.<ref name="INMET-A705-Bauru-SP"/><ref name="Gráficos-INMET-A705-Bauru-SP"/> The highest temperature reached {{formatnum:41.6}}°C on October 7, 2020. The highest 24-hour [[Precipitation|precipitation]] accumulation was {{formatnum:135.4}} mm on January 19, 2017. On March 1, 2018, a peak [[Wind gust|wind gust]] of {{formatnum:23.8}} m/s ({{formatnum:85.7}} km/h) was recorded. The lowest [[Relative humidity|relative humidity]], at 9%, occurred on August 23, 2006, triggering an emergency state.<ref name="INMET-A705-Bauru-SP"/><ref name="Gráficos-INMET-A705-Bauru-SP"/>

=== Parks and environment ===
{{Multiple image
| align             = left
| direction         = vertical
| width             = 210
| image1            = Parque Vitória Régia Bauru.jpg
| caption1          = Vitória Régia Park
| image2            = Zoo Bauru 310710 REFON 4.JPG
| caption2          = Bauru Municipal Zoo
}}

The original and predominant vegetation in Bauru is the [[Atlantic Forest]], but due to climatic factors and deforestation, the [[Cerrado]] biome is increasingly prevalent.<ref name="Channel_Bauru"/>

In the early 20th century, deforestation for the construction of the Northwest Brazil Railway and urban expansion led to numerous cases of [[Leishmaniasis|leishmaniasis]], as the disease-carrying mosquito migrated from forests to homes, causing an epidemic that earned the nickname "Bauru ulcer."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www2.uol.com.br/debate/1217/regiao/regiao06.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68INhmeNh?url=http://www2.uol.com.br/debate/1217/regiao/regiao06.htm |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Secretaria registra casos de leishmaniose |author=Uol |date=2000 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

To curb deforestation, several conservation areas have been established. As of 2011, Bauru had nine such areas: the Community Grove ({{formatnum:16200}} m²); the Pederneiras State Forest ({{formatnum:1941}} hectares, established in 2002); the Bauru Ecological Station (278.7 ha, established in 1983); the Bauru Experimental Station (43.09 ha, established in 1939); the Batalha River Environmental Protection Area (APA) (established in 1998 to protect the [[Riparian forest|riparian forest]] along the [[Batalha River]]); the Bauru Municipal Botanical Garden (established in 1994); the Vargem Alegre Municipal APA (established in 1996); the Água Parada APA (established in 1996); and the Bauru Municipal Zoo (established in 1992, covering 30 ha and housing endangered species).<ref name="Channel_Bauru"/>

Bauru also features parks, medium and large squares, sports courts, and recreational areas such as the Vitória Régia Park and Castelo Park, among others.<ref name="Channel_Bauru"/>

The Bauru Municipal Botanical Garden began construction in the 1910s at a site used for water extraction, rich in springs protected by forests. The park’s Visitor Center, opened on July 7, 2003, displays information about the park, with key attractions including ecological trails, a herbarium, and a nursery.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.jardimbotanicobauru.com.br/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiSEnOH?url=http://www.jardimbotanicobauru.com.br/ |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Histórico |author=Bauru Botanical Garden |access-date=15 July 2012}}</ref> The Bauru Forest Garden spans 50 hectares, hosting an experimental station for research on pine, eucalyptus, and other exotic and native plant species.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.olharverde.com.br/agenda/29 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiTKnsI?url=http://www.olharverde.com.br/agenda/29 |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Horto Florestal de Bauru |author=Olhar Verde |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref> The Bauru Municipal Zoo, inaugurated on August 24, 1980, houses various animal species and attracts approximately 150,000 visitors annually.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://zoobauru.com.br/portal/?page_id=1017 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiXJBig?url=http://zoobauru.com.br/portal/?page_id=1017 |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Zoo em Números |author=Bauru Municipal Zoo |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref>

== Demographics ==
{{Historical populations|1970|131936|1980|186659|1991|261112|2000|316064|2010|344039|2022|379146|type=Brazil|footnote=Source: Brazilian Institute of Geography and<br /> Statistics (IBGE)<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010"/><ref name="IBGE_Bauru"/> and National<br /> Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)<ref name="População_Sexo">{{cite web|access-date=21 May 2011 |archive-date=21 May 2011 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yqoVxITh?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/demografia/mu_dem_pop_total.asp?iIdMun=100135068 |author=Confederacão Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |title=Demografia - População Total |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/demografia/mu_dem_pop_total.asp?iIdMun=100135068}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>}}

According to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]], conducted by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), the population of Bauru was {{formatnum:379146}} inhabitants, with a population density of {{formatnum:567.85|hab./km²}}.<ref name="IBGE_Bauru"/> It ranks as the [[List of municipalities in the state of Sao Paulo by population|18th most populous municipality in the state]] and the most populous in the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Mesoregion of Bauru]], with a [[population density]] of 510.83 inhabitants per km².<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/tabelas_pdf/total_populacao_sao_paulo.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yqkmObyt?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/tabelas_pdf/total_populacao_sao_paulo.pdf |archive-date=21 May 2011 |title=Censo Populacional 2010 |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=29 November 2010 |access-date=11 December 2010}}</ref>

The [[2010 Brazilian Census]] reported that {{formatnum:166692}} inhabitants were male and {{formatnum:177347}} were female. According to the same census, {{formatnum:338891}} residents lived in the [[urban area]] and {{formatnum:5148}} in the [[rural area]].<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010" />

Bauru's Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) is considered very high by the United Nations Development Programme ([[United Nations Development Programme|UNDP]]). Its HDI-M score is 0.801, making it the [[List of municipalities in São Paulo by HDI|20th highest in São Paulo state]] (out of 645 municipalities), the 24th in the Southeast Region of Brazil (out of 1666), and the 37th in Brazil (out of 5565). Most of Bauru's indicators are very high and exceed the national average, according to the UNDP.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010">{{cite web|access-date=3 August 2013 |date=2010 |publisher=Programa das Nações Unidas para o Desenvolvimento (PNUD) |title=Ranking decrescente do IDH-M dos municípios do Brasil |url=http://www.pnud.org.br/arquivos/ranking-idhm-2010.pdf |work=Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

In 2003, the IBGE reported Bauru's [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[Economic inequality|social inequality]], as 0.43, where 1.00 represents the highest inequality and 0.00 the lowest.<ref name="GINI" /> The incidence of poverty, as measured by the IBGE, was 14.01%, with a lower limit of 9.68%, an upper limit of 18.34%, and a subjective poverty incidence of 9.37%.<ref name="GINI">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Mapa de Pobreza e Desigualdade - Municípios Brasileiros 2003 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2003 |access-date=29 April 2011}}</ref> From 1991 to 2010, the proportion of individuals with a per capita household income of up to half the minimum wage decreased by 16.0%. In 2010, 85.6% of the population lived above the poverty line, 9.6% were at the poverty line, and 4.7% were below it.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/PDF/gera_PDF.php?cidade=48720 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IY2rUKm?url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/PDF/gera_PDF.php?cidade=48720 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Perfil municipal — Bauru - SP |author=Portal ODM |date=2010 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref> In 2000, the richest 20% of Bauru's population accounted for 62.8% of the municipality's total income, 23 times higher than the 2.7% share of the poorest 20%. In 1991, the poorest 20% held 3.9% of the income, indicating an increase in social inequality from the early 1990s to 2000.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

=== Housing shortage ===
In 2008, according to the municipal government, there were records of [[slum]]s, [[stilt house]]s, and irregular settlements. In 2000, the IBGE reported that {{formatnum:5888}} inhabitants lived in subnormal agglomerations. However, data from November 2008, released by the Planning Secretariat, estimated that nearly 15,000 people were living in shacks.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> In 2010, the IBGE identified 23 slums in Bauru (Jd. Ivone, Barreirinho, Ferradura, V. Aimorés, Sta. Teresinha, Jd. Olímpico, Jd. Nicéia, Jd. Yolanda, J. Europa, Vila Zilo, Parque das Nações, Comendador/Santista, Jd. Vitória, Cutuba, Parque Real, Jd. Andorfato, Parque Jaraguá, São Manoel, Vila Sta. Filomena, J. Gerson França, Jd. Marise, Jd. Maria Célia, and Pousada da Esperança). The first irregular housing clusters began forming in the mid-1980s, as many people migrated to Bauru seeking better living conditions and settled in subnormal agglomerations. These grew due to the absence of a housing policy or housing secretariat in Bauru. Many of these occupied areas were public lands designated for green spaces.<ref name="Livre_Diretor">{{cite web|url=http://www.livrevista.com/article.php?id=403 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IY3xLC5?url=http://www.livrevista.com/article.php?id=403 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Plano Diretor Participativo e o problema das favelas |author=Karen Ferraz |date=November 2008 |publisher=Livre Vista |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

To address this situation and improve living conditions in the slums, the Bauru Participatory Master Plan was approved in August 2008. It provides for the regularization of slums not located in high-risk areas prone to flooding or erosion, or in environmental preservation zones, with those in such areas to be relocated. Other municipal projects aim to curb the expansion of slums.<ref name="Livre_Diretor"/> Additionally, irregular settlements—areas lacking legal land ownership—pose challenges, though many are in the process of regularization.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://institutosoma.org.br/noticias/69 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IY52oOg?url=http://institutosoma.org.br/noticias/69 |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=83% das favelas de Bauru podem ser legalizadas |author=Instituto Soma |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
Reflecting Bauru's cultural diversity, the city is home to a wide range of religious manifestations. Although it developed within a predominantly Catholic social framework, influenced by both colonization and immigration—and the majority of Bauru residents still identify as Catholic—the city is now home to numerous Protestant denominations, as well as practices of [[Buddhism]], [[Islam]], [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]], and others. In recent decades, Buddhism and Eastern religions have seen significant growth in the city.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/>
[[File:Bauru 280209 REFON 23.JPG|thumb|upright|[[Independent Presbyterian Church of Brazil|Independent Presbyterian Church]]]]

The Jewish, Mormon, and Afro-Brazilian religious communities are also significant. According to the 2000 IBGE census, Bauru's population is composed of: [[Roman Catholicism|Catholics]] (62.37%), [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]] (23.59%), [[Irreligion|non-religious individuals]] (7.31%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (3.12%), with the remainder distributed among other religions.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=11&i=P |title=População residente por cor ou raça e religião |author=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |date=2000 |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref>

==== Protestant churches ====
Bauru is home to a variety of Protestant or Reformed denominations, including the [[Lutheran Church]], [[Presbyterian Church]], [[Methodist Church]], [[Anglican Episcopal Church]], and various Evangelical groups such as the Sara Nossa Terra Evangelical Community, Maranata Christian Church, [[Baptist Church|Baptist churches]], [[Assembleias de Deus|Assemblies of God]], [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], [[World Church of God's Power]], [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], and [[Christian Congregation in Brazil]], among others.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/>

As noted, the 2000 IBGE census indicated that 23.59% of the population was Protestant. Of this group, 16.52% belonged to Pentecostal Evangelical churches, 3.61% to mission-based Evangelical churches, 2.65% to institutionally unaffiliated Evangelical churches, and 0.81% to other Evangelical denominations.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/>

Other Christian denominations are also present, including [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (1.19% of the population) and members of [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (0.29%).<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/> The first Protestant church established in Bauru was the Presbyterian Church, with its local congregation founded on 15 October 1933.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

==== Roman Catholic Apostolic Church ====
[[File:CatedralBauru.jpg|thumb|left|Façade of the Divine Holy Spirit Cathedral, located in Rui Barbosa Square in the city center.]]
According to the [[Catholic Church]]'s organizational structure, Bauru is part of the [[Archdiocese of Botucatu]], established as a diocese on 7 June 1908 and elevated on 19 April 1958. It also serves as the seat of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Bauru|Diocese of Bauru]], created on 15 February 1964, encompassing Bauru and twelve other municipalities. For pastoral purposes, it is divided into seven pastoral regions and 41 parishes.<ref name="Diocese_Paróquias">{{cite web|url=http://www.bispadobauru.org.br/nova/paroquias.php?acao=listar |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IdYQREO?url=http://www.bispadobauru.org.br/nova/paroquias.php?acao=listar |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Paróquias |author=Diocese de Bauru |access-date=8 June 2012}}</ref> The seat of the Diocese of Bauru is the Divine Holy Spirit Cathedral, the city's main religious monument, inaugurated on 21 July 1897.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bispadobauru.org.br/nova/paroquias.php?paroquia_id=5&acao=ler |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IdWu4D4?url=http://www.bispadobauru.org.br/nova/paroquias.php?paroquia_id=5&acao=ler |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Catedral do Divino Espírito Santo |author=Diocese de Bauru |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

The establishment of the Diocese was driven by rapid population growth in the region during the early 20th century, which fostered the development of Catholicism. The "Divine Holy Spirit Chapel" was built in the late 1880s by Faustino Ribeiro da Silva, with financial support from the Municipal Council, but it was demolished in 1913. Prior to this, the first sign of religious expression in Bauru appeared around 1886, with the placement of a cross in front of the current cathedral site, in what was then the Municipal Square, renamed Rui Barbosa Square in 1923.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

=== Ethnic composition ===
[[File:Pessoas em Bauru SP.jpg|thumb|right|Sample of Bauru's population during a cultural event in 2010.]]
In 2010, according to IBGE census data, Bauru's population was composed of {{formatnum:243028}} Whites (70.66%), {{formatnum:17041}} Blacks (4.95%), {{formatnum:78039}} [[Pardo|Pardos]] (22.69%), {{formatnum:5394}} Asians (1.57%), and 435 Indigenous individuals (0.13%).<ref name="SIDRA_Raça">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3175&z=cd&o=5&i=P |title=Tabela 3175 - População residente, por cor ou raça, segundo a situação do domicílio, o sexo e a idade |author=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |date=2010 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref> In 2010, there were {{formatnum:1070}} immigrants from other parts of São Paulo state and Brazil, according to the IBGE.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3172&z=cd&o=5&i=P |title=Tabela 3172 - Emigrantes internacionais, por sexo, segundo as Grandes Regiões e Unidades da Federação das pessoas com quem residiram antes de emigrarem |author=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |date=2010 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref> The city received a significant influx of people from other parts of the country during the [[March to the West]], many of whom settled in the region hoping for better living conditions.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Conversely, {{formatnum:1070}} individuals left Bauru for other countries, with 345 (32.45%) going to [[Japan]], 217 (20.28%) to the [[United States]], and 63 (5.89%) to the [[United Kingdom]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3173&z=cd&o=5&i=P |title=Tabela 3173 - Emigrantes internacionais, por sexo, segundo os continentes e países estrangeiros de destino |author=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |date=2010 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

Immigration was more common in the early 20th century, contributing significantly to agriculture. Many immigrants sought employment, particularly in [[coffee]] plantations, and helped strengthen commerce. Bauru welcomed waves of immigrants from various parts of the world, notably Italians, Spaniards, Portuguese, and Japanese. Its strategic location at a major road and rail junction, connecting the municipality to much of the country and other [[South America]]n nations, also attracted Syrian, Lebanese, German, French, Chinese, and Jewish immigrants of various nationalities. More recently, the city has received Bolivians, Argentines, Chileans, Palestinians, and North Americans, making it one of the most cosmopolitan municipalities in the [[Interior of São Paulo]].<ref name="Ache_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.achetudoeregiao.com.br/sp/Bauru/historia.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68IdTOtR4?url=http://www.achetudoeregiao.com.br/sp/Bauru/historia.htm |archive-date=9 June 2012 |title=Historia de Bauru |author=Ache Tudo e Região |date=15 March 2011 |access-date=9 June 2012}}</ref>

== Politics and administration ==
[[File:Prefeitura de Bauru.jpg|thumb|left|upright=1.4|Cherry Blossom Palace, the seat of the Bauru City Hall]]

Municipal administration in Bauru is carried out through the [[executive branch]] and the [[legislative branch]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67UGgjcdE?url=http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074 |archive-date=7 May 2012 |title=The Municipal Public Authority and the Constitutional Obligation to Establish an Internal Control System |author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima |date=8 February 2006 |publisher=JusVi |access-date=9 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> The first person to govern the municipality was José Alves de Lima, who served as intendant from January to May 1896.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The current mayor is Suéllen Silva Rosim of the [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|Social Democratic Party]] (PSD). She won the 2020 mayoral election in the second round with 89,725 votes (at the time representing the [[Patriota]] party), securing 55.98% of the valid votes. In the [[2024 Brazilian municipal elections|2024 mayoral election]], Suéllen Rosim was re-elected as mayor. She became the first woman to be elected mayor of Bauru.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/sp/bauru-marilia/eleicoes/2020/noticia/2020/11/29/suellen-rosim-chora-e-agradece-pelos-votos-orgulho-de-ser-a-primeira-mulher-a-governar-bauru.ghtml|title=Suéllen Rosim chora e agradece pelos mais de 89 mil votos: 'Orgulho de ser a primeira mulher a governar Bauru'|date=2020-11-29|access-date=2025-01-19|website=G1|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/sp/bauru-marilia/eleicoes/2024/noticia/2024/12/31/prefeita-de-bauru-sp-toma-posse-nesta-quarta-1o-veja-lista-de-vereadores-eleitos.ghtml|title=Prefeita de Bauru (SP) toma posse nesta quarta (1º); veja lista de vereadores eleitos {{!}} Eleições 2024 em Bauru e Marília - SP|date=2024-12-31|access-date=2025-01-19|website=G1|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/cidades-e-estados/sp/bauru.html|title=Bauru (SP) {{!}} Cidades e Estados {{!}} IBGE|access-date=2025-01-19|website=www.ibge.gov.br|language=pt}}</ref>

== Economy ==
[[File:Bauru calcadao1.jpg|right|thumb|Sidewalk on Batista de Carvalho Street in the commercial center.]]

Bauru’s [[Gross domestic product|Gross Domestic Product]] (GDP) is the largest in the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Bauru Microregion]], the 18th largest in the state of São Paulo, and the 68th largest in Brazil.<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{cite web|access-date=23 March 2016 |author=[[Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]] (IBGE) |date=2016 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/sp/bauru/pesquisa/38/46996}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> According to 2009 IBGE data, the municipality’s GDP was R$6,795,517,000.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Of this, R$747,297,000 consisted of taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices.<ref name="IBGE_PIB2">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pibmunic&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios |author21=City Hall |date=2008 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> The [[per capita GDP]] was R$18,906.42.<ref name="IBGE_PIB" />

According to the IBGE, in 2010, the city had 14,233 local units and 13,613 active commercial enterprises and establishments. A total of 131,698 workers were employed, with 114,667 classified as salaried employees. Salaries and other remunerations amounted to R$2,082,034,000, and the average monthly salary in the municipality was 2.9 [[Minimum wage|minimum wages]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Estatísticas do Cadastro Central de Empresas |publisher=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Until the 1940s, Bauru’s economy was heavily dependent on agriculture. However, its strategic location at a major road, air, water, and rail junction in São Paulo, combined with access to electricity and telephone networks, enabled the growth of industry and commerce throughout the 20th century, particularly in the second half.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/>

=== Primary sector ===
[[File:Plantação de abacaxi em Bauru SP.jpg|thumb|left|Pineapple plantation in Bauru.]]

Agriculture is the least significant sector of Bauru’s economy. Of the city’s total GDP, R$18,069,000 is the gross added value of agriculture.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> According to the IBGE, in 2010, the municipality had approximately 52,740 cattle, 1,912 horses, 244 buffalo, 20 donkeys, 45 mules, 11,058 pigs, 180 goats, and 2,893 sheep. There were 249,180 poultry, including 245,500 roosters, pullets, chickens, and chicks, and 3,680 hens, producing 102,000 dozen chicken eggs. A total of 915 cows were milked, yielding 1,263,000 liters of milk. Additionally, 26,500 kg of honey was extracted.<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2010&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Pecuária 2010 |publisher=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> In temporary [[Agriculture|crops]], the main products are [[sugarcane]] (430 hectares cultivated and 37,883 tons harvested in 2010), pineapple (220 hectares cultivated and 3,520,000 fruits harvested), and sweet potato (75 hectares and 1,200 tons harvested).<ref name="agricultura">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2010&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Lavoura Temporária 2010 |publisher=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Agriculture was a dominant economic activity in Bauru at the beginning of the 20th century, with significant contributions from immigrants. At that time, [[Coffee production in Brazil|coffee cultivation]] was the primary economic driver, largely due to the labor of European immigrants. However, following the [[Great Depression|1929 economic crisis]], coffee production declined and was replaced by [[cotton]] cultivation. Over time, agriculture’s importance in Bauru and western São Paulo diminished, exacerbated by rural exodus as people sought better living conditions in urban areas. In rural areas, cotton was gradually replaced by sugarcane.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/><ref name="Ache_História"/>

=== Secondary sector ===
[[File:Walmart em Bauru SP.jpg|thumb|right|[[Walmart]] in Bauru.]]
[[File:Shopping Bauru.jpg|thumb|Bauru Shopping Mall]]

The industry is currently the second most significant sector of Bauru’s economy, contributing R$1,208,787,000 to the municipal GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/>

Key industries in the municipality include metalworking, publishing and printing, food processing, electronics, and plastics, employing over 20,000 workers directly in factories and industries.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/>

Bauru produces automotive batteries, plastics, continuous forms, packaging, food products (such as candies and chewing gum), and clothing. It is also a major exporter of meat derivatives and a national leader in the production of school notebooks. The city has three industrial districts, hosting over 130 companies in the industrial, service, and wholesale trade sectors.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/>

Industry played a pivotal role in Bauru’s urbanization, attracting significant populations from rural areas seeking better living conditions and income opportunities. Migrants came not only from Bauru’s rural zones but also from various small municipalities in the [[Interior of São Paulo]].<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/><ref name="Ache_História"/>

Effective planning also supported the industrial sector’s growth. Strict environmental controls ensured that factory expansion had minimal adverse environmental impacts. The São Paulo State Industries Center (Ciesp/Regional Bauru) coordinates the productive and service sectors, addressing institutional and macroeconomic issues.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/>

=== Tertiary sector ===
The [[service sector]] generates R$4,821,365,000 of the municipal GDP, making it the largest contributor to Bauru’s economy.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In addition to commerce, the higher education sector is notable, with several [[higher education]] institutions, both public and private, establishing campuses in the city.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/>

The development of transportation infrastructure, starting in the 1910s with the formation of a road-rail junction, made services and commerce the dominant economic activities in Bauru. Immigrants played a significant role in the growth of this sector.<ref name="Vivendo_Industria"/><ref name="Ache_História"/> On 2 April 1931, the Bauru Commercial and Industrial Association was established to coordinate the commercial sector.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

Commercial activity is concentrated in Bauru’s central region and its [[Shopping mall|shopping malls]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias.php?codigo=225632 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68JmWRyNt?url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias.php?codigo=225632 |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Comércio central de Bauru abre à noite a partir desta sexta |author=Neto del Hoyo |date=2 December 2011 |publisher=JC Net |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

== Infrastructure ==
=== Health ===
[[File:Centrino Bauru.jpg|thumb|left|Hospital for Rehabilitation of Craniofacial Anomalies at the University of São Paulo in Bauru, known as "Centrinho"]]

In 2009, Bauru had 149 healthcare facilities, including hospitals, [[emergency department|emergency rooms]], [[health center|health posts]], and [[dentistry|dental services]], with 49 public and 100 private establishments. These facilities provided 1,046 hospitalization beds, with 465 in public facilities and 581 in private ones.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Serviços de Saúde 2009 |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> In 2011, 98.5% of children under one year old were up to date with their vaccinations.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> In 2010, there were 4,429 births, with an infant mortality rate of 11.3 per 1,000 children under one year old. Additionally, 99.7% of live births were attended by qualified health professionals.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> In the same year, 14.1% of pregnant women were under 20 years old.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> A total of 11,817 children were weighed by the [[Programa Saúde da Família|Family Health Program]], with 0.1% classified as malnourished.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

The Bauru Municipal Health Department, directly linked to the City Hall, is responsible for maintaining and operating the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|Unified Health System]] (SUS) and developing policies, programs, and projects to promote municipal health.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/sec_saude.aspx?sec=73 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68JrkQgW4?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/sec_saude.aspx?sec=73 |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Secretaria da Saúde |author=City Hall |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> For emergencies, the city has four Urgent Care Units (in the Bela Vista, Mary Dota, Ipiranga, and Geisel/Redentor neighborhoods), the Mobile Emergency Care Service (SAMU), the Central Emergency Department, and the Pediatric Emergency Service. Support services include the Municipal Mental Health Outpatient Clinic, the Human Milk Bank, Psychosocial Care Centers, the Zoonosis Control Center, and the Municipal Elderly Care Program (PROMAI).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/unidades.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68JrlZTtL?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/unidades.aspx |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Unidades e Departamentos da Saúde |author=City Hall |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> Primary care services include the Family Health Program (PSF) with six care units and 17 Basic Health Units (UBS).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/nucleos.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68JrmgNUE?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/nucleos.aspx |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Núcleos de Saúde |author=City Hall |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Dental clinics are also set up in municipal and state schools, conducting hygiene campaigns and consultations for children.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/outros.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68JrnprUz?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_saude/outros.aspx |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Outros Serviços |author=City Hall |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> The Lauro de Souza Lima Institute (ILSL), established in 1933, is located in Bauru and is a national and international reference center for general [[dermatology]] and, in particular, [[leprosy]]. In addition to dermatology services, the institute conducts research, teaching, physical rehabilitation, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, and corrective plastic surgeries.<ref>{{cite web|title=ILSL - Instituto Lauro de Souza Lima|url=http://www.ilsl.br/perfil.htm|website=www.ilsl.br|access-date=2019-03-16|language=pt|archive-date=2019-01-29|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190129073503/http://www.ilsl.br/perfil.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 2009, the average Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) for Bauru’s public schools was 5.1 (on a scale from 1 to 10). Fifth-year students (formerly 4th grade) scored 5.5, while ninth-year students (formerly 8th grade) scored 4.7. The national average for municipal and state schools was 4.0. For private institutions, the municipal index rose to 6.1 (6.4 for fifth-year students and 5.9 for ninth-year students).<ref name="ODM_Perfil" />

In 2009, Bauru had approximately 66,237 enrollments in public and private schools.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> According to the IBGE, of the 97 primary schools, 48 were state-run, 48 were municipal, and 33 were private. Of the 51 secondary schools, 32 were state-run, and 19 were private.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> In 2000, 10.5% of children aged 7 to 14 were not enrolled in primary education. The completion rate for youths aged 15 to 17 was 66.5%. In 2010, the literacy rate for the population aged 15 and older was 99.2%. In 2006, for every 100 girls in primary education (aged 7 to 14), there were 105 boys.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

The Bauru Municipal Education Department (SME) coordinates and provides administrative and pedagogical support to the city’s school system. Programs include Adult and Youth Education (EJA), a free education network for adults who have not completed primary education, and Special Education, where students with physical disabilities are guided by specialized teachers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://portais.bauru.sp.gov.br/AreaPortalInstitucional/PaginaSimples/PaginaSimples.aspx?pgn=1296&portal=21 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8oMhE8?url=http://portais.bauru.sp.gov.br/AreaPortalInstitucional/PaginaSimples/PaginaSimples.aspx?pgn=1296&portal=21 |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Secretaria |author=Bauru Municipal Education Department |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Bauru also has technical and vocational schools, such as the [[SENAI|National Industrial Apprenticeship Service]] (SENAI), [[Industrial Social Services]] (SESI), Bauru Industrial Technical College (CTi), and Rodrigues de Abreu State Technical School (ETEC). The city is home to three public universities: the [[University of São Paulo]] (USP), the [[São Paulo State University]] (UNESP), which has its largest campus in Bauru, and the Bauru Faculty of Technology (FATEC). Private universities include the Sacred Heart University (USC), Bauru Integrated Faculties (FIB), Paulista University (UNIP), Toledo Educational Institution (ITE), and the medicine course at UNINOVE.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tudobauru.com.br/educacao/escolas-tecnicas-e-profissionalizantes |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8ppCXd?url=http://www.tudobauru.com.br/educacao/escolas-tecnicas-e-profissionalizantes |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Escolas técnicas e profissionalizantes em Bauru |author=Tudo Bauru |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> Additionally, Bauru hosts a unit of the [[Agência Paulista de Tecnologia dos Agronegócios|São Paulo Agribusiness Technology Agency]] for agricultural research.<ref>{{cite web |last=São Paulo |first=State Government |title=Regional Research Units |url=https://webserver-saaagrospgedave-prd.lfr.cloud/apta-regional/apta-regional-de-bauru |access-date=11 April 2024 |language=pt}}</ref>

=== Housing, services, and communication ===
Bauru’s first water supply system was installed in 1912 by then-intendant José Carlos de Freire Figueiredo, operated by the Bauru Water and Sewage Company. The system was reinaugurated on 19 April 1942, drawing water from the [[Batalha River]]. By Law No. 1,006 of 24 December 1962, the Bauru Water and Sewage Department (DAE) was established, which has since managed public water and sewage services. In early 2012, there were 29 deep wells (underground springs) and a Batalha River intake station, which supplies 40% of the population.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.daebauru.sp.gov.br/7dc/empresa/historia/index.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8sNRxh?url=http://www.daebauru.sp.gov.br/7dc/empresa/historia/index.php |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=História do abastecimento de água de Bauru |author=Bauru Water and Sewage Department (DAE) |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> The city has two sewage treatment plants (ETEs), Tibiriçá and Candeia, with plans for a third, Vargem Limpa, expected to begin operations in 2020.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.daebauru.sp.gov.br/7dc/esgoto/tratamento/index.php#../etes/ete |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8tslD1?url=http://www.daebauru.sp.gov.br/7dc/esgoto/tratamento/index.php |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Estações de tratamento de esgoto |author=Bauru Water and Sewage Department (DAE) |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

[[File:Vista aérea de Bauru.jpg|thumb|left|City panorama with numerous residential buildings. Bauru has over 13,000 apartments.]]
[[File:Bauru - under construction (2532676608).jpg|thumb|left|Partial nighttime view of Bauru. According to the [[CPFL Energia|CPFL]], the city has 39,218 public streetlights installed (March 2011).]]

In 2010, according to the IBGE, Bauru had 109,875 permanent private [[household]]s, including 94,653 [[house]]s, 13,363 [[apartment]]s, 1,549 village houses or condominiums, and 310 rooms or shanties. Of these, 78,830 were owned properties (63,365 fully paid, 15,465 under acquisition), 24,841 were rented, 5,780 were provided (1,040 by employers, 4,740 otherwise), and 424 were occupied in other ways. Most of the municipality has access to treated water, [[electricity]], [[sewage]], [[solid waste|urban cleaning]], [[fixed-line telephone]], and [[mobile phone]] services. In 2010, 98.42% of households were served by the [[drinking water|general water supply network]], 99.4% had garbage collection, and 97.03% had sewage systems.<ref name="IBGE_DomicípiosPop">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=result_univer_censo2010&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Censo Demográfico 2010: Características da População e dos Domicílios: Resultados do Universo |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

On 16 March 1911, the city hall inaugurated the first electricity supply service. In 1927, the [[CPFL Energia|São Paulo Power and Light Company]] (CPFL Paulista) took over, currently serving 234 other municipalities in the São Paulo interior.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> According to CPFL, in March 2011, there were 39,218 public streetlights installed in the city’s streets and avenues.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias.php?codigo=220304 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8vWxdC?url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/noticias.php?codigo=220304 |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Energia elétrica faz 100 anos em Bauru |author=JC Net |date=16 March 2011 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> In 2010, 99.85% of the municipality’s households had electricity.<ref name="IBGE_DomicípiosPop" />

The city offers [[internet]] services, including dial-up, [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]), and fiber optic (FTTH and FTTX), provided by various free and paid [[internet service provider]]s. Fixed-line telephony was initially provided by the [[Companhia Telefônica Brasileira|Brazilian Telephone Company]] (CTB),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imprensaoficial.com.br/DO/BuscaDO2001Documento_11_4.aspx?link=/1973/ineditoriais/junho/20/pag_0009_2TIM6BGCRNUOSe8LEFA7U4SO2TJ.pdf&pagina=9&data=20/06/1973&caderno=Ineditoriais&paginaordenacao=100009|title=Relação do patrimônio da CTB incorporado pela Telesp|date=|access-date=|publisher=Official Gazette of the State of São Paulo|last=|first=|language=pt}}</ref> which built the city’s first telephone exchange, still in use today. In 1973, [[Telesp|São Paulo Telecommunications]] (TELESP) took over,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.telesp.com.br:80/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19980114175120/http://www.telesp.com.br:80/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm|url-status=dead|archive-date=1998-01-14|title=Área de atuação da Telesp em São Paulo|date=|access-date=|publisher=Official TELESP Page (archived)|last=|first=|author=|language=pt}}</ref> implementing the [[direct distance dialing]] (DDD) system and building additional exchanges. In 1998, TELESP was privatized and sold to [[Telefônica Brasil|Telefônica]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://telefonica.mediagroup.com.br/pt/Empresa/Nossa_Historia.aspx|title=Nossa História|date=|access-date=|publisher=Telefônica / VIVO|last=|first=|author=|language=pt}}</ref> which adopted the [[Vivo (telecommunications company)|Vivo]] brand in 2012.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/economia/negocios/noticia/2012/04/telefonica-conclui-troca-da-marca-por-vivo.html|title=Telefônica conclui troca da marca por Vivo|author=Gabriela Gasparin|date=12 April 2012|publisher=G1|access-date=|language=pt}}</ref> Mobile phone services are offered by various operators, with some areas featuring [[wireless communication|wireless]] networks.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.abusar.org.br/prov_wireless.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67WHxgZNQ?url=http://www.abusar.org.br/prov_wireless.html |archive-date=9 May 2012 |title=Provedores de Todo o Brasil |author=Brazilian Association of High-Speed Internet Users |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> Bauru’s area code ([[direct distance dialing|DDD]]) is 014,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://dddcidade.com.br/bauru/ |title=DDD |author=DDD Cidade |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> and its [[postal code]] ranges from 17000-001 to 17109-999.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correios.com.br/ |title=CEP de cidades brasileiras |author=Correios |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Several [[Ultra High Frequency]] (UHF) channels are available in digital technology, with major broadcasters having affiliates in Bauru or nearby cities, including RecordTV Paulista (owned by [[RecordTV]]),<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.recordpaulista.com.br/portal/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8xOliI?url=http://www.recordpaulista.com.br/portal/ |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Mais de 55 anos de história |author=TV Record Paulista |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> [[TV TEM Bauru]] ([[Rede Globo]]),<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sucom.redeglobo.com.br/atlas2004/mapas/php/con_emissora.php?pexib=BAU&puf=SP |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8zfU7f?url=http://sucom.redeglobo.com.br/atlas2004/mapas/php/con_emissora.php?pexib=BAU&puf=SP |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=TV TEM Bauru |author=Rede Globo |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> and SBT Central (owned by [[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.arquivosbt.com/2008/05/listas-de-emissoras-di.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K90pWS8?url=http://www.arquivosbt.com/2008/05/listas-de-emissoras-di.html |archive-date=10 June 2012 |title=Lista de emissoras SBT |author=Brazilian Television System |date=6 May 2008 |access-date=10 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> On 28 March 2018, analog television signals were discontinued in Bauru. The city also has several circulating newspapers. The first, ''O Bauru'', was established in 1906.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Current publications include ''Bom Dia Bauru'', ''Jornal da Cidade'', ''Vivendo Bauru'', and ''Tudo Bauru''.<ref name="GuiaM_Bauru">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiademidia.com.br/sao-paulo/jornais-bauru.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68K8j3Vvc?url=http://www.guiademidia.com.br/sao-paulo/jornais-bauru.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=2012-06-10 |title=Jornais de Bauru - São Paulo |author=Guia de Mídia |access-date=21 April 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> The first radio station, Bauru Rádio Clube, began broadcasting on 8 March 1934.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Major stations today include Rádio Auri-Verde, Rádio 94 FM Bauru, Rádio 96 FM Bauru, and Rádio Unesp FM.<ref name="GuiaM_Bauru"/>

=== Crime and public safety ===
[[File:Copom Bauru.jpg|thumb|Military Police Operations Center (COPOM)]]

As in most medium and large Brazilian municipalities, crime remains a challenge in Bauru. In 2008, the [[homicide]] rate was 10.0 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 62nd in the state and 694th nationally.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/HTN.xls |title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de homicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=6 May 2011 |format=xls |language=pt |archive-date=1 March 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110301222935/http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/HTN.xls |url-status=dead }}</ref> The [[suicide]] rate in 2008 was 7.0 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 66th in the state and 735th nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls |title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de suicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=6 May 2011 |format=xls |language=pt}}</ref>

In the 2018 Violence Atlas, produced by the Institute for Applied Economic Research (IPEA) in partnership with the Brazilian Public Safety Forum, Bauru was identified as having the lowest homicide and violent crime rates among Brazilian cities with over 300,000 inhabitants. Among the 309 Brazilian municipalities with over 100,000 inhabitants, Bauru ranked 16th.<ref>{{cite web|last=Gregorio|first=Claudia Di|url=https://revistaatencao.com.br/bauru-e-a-menos-violenta-entre-as-cidades-com-mais-de-300-mil-habitantes-no-brasil/|title=Bauru é a menos violenta entre as cidades com mais de 300 mil habitantes no Brasil|date=2019-03-19|access-date=2022-12-03|website=Revista Atenção|language=pt-BR}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===

=== Air ===
[[File:Aeroporto_de_Bauru-Arealva.jpg|link=https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficheiro:Aeroporto_de_Bauru-Arealva.jpg|thumb|[[Bauru-Arealva Airport]]]]
Bauru has two medium-sized airports, both managed by the [[DAESP|São Paulo State Aviation Department]] (DAESP). The [[Bauru Airport]], inaugurated on 8 April 1939, features a 1,500-meter asphalt runway and a passenger terminal. It hosts an aeroclub and an aircraft and glider workshop and is located less than 3 km from the city center.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.daesp.sp.gov.br/aeroportos/bauru.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q86npMr?url=http://www.daesp.sp.gov.br/aeroportos/bauru.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru |author=São Paulo State Aviation Department (DAESP) |access-date=12 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

The [[Bauru-Arealva Airport]], opened in 2006, has a 2,500 m² passenger terminal, a 2,100-meter runway, and an aircraft maintenance yard. It offers flights to São Paulo and [[Campinas]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.daesp.sp.gov.br/aeroportos/bauru_arealva.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q87qZ3n?url=http://www.daesp.sp.gov.br/aeroportos/bauru_arealva.html |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru Arealva |author=São Paulo State Aviation Department (DAESP) |access-date=12 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

===Rail ===
{{Main|Bauru station (NOB)}}

The first railway to reach Bauru was the Sorocabana Railway, with the [[Bauru station (NOB)|first railway station]] inaugurated on 22 April 1905. This small station operated until the late 1990s, when the railway, then managed by [[Ferrovia Paulista S/A]] (FEPASA), closed.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru-efs.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q88yV8c?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru-efs.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru-EFS |author=Brazilian Railway Stations |date=22 July 2010 |access-date=12 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>
[[File:Maquinado (2893837347).jpg|thumb|left|Interior of the former railway station.]]

The main station, established on 27 September 1906, was one of Brazil’s largest railway junctions between the 1940s and 1950s. It served as a busy passenger terminal and a key hub for cargo loading and unloading. However, in 1997, the railway was sold to Novoeste Railway S.A., and the last passenger train ran on 15 March 2001, leaving the station unused since then.<ref name="Estações_Bauru">{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8A385b?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru |author=Brazilian Railway Stations |date=10 March 2012 |access-date=12 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> Another medium-sized station, Bauru Paulista, opened on 8 September 1910 and was abandoned by FEPASA in 1997.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru-cp.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8B5em7?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/b/bauru-cp.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru Paulista |author=Brazilian Railway Stations |date=22 July 2010 |access-date=12 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Rail transport in Bauru, as in much of São Paulo, declined significantly due to the rise of road and air transport, particularly in the early 1990s.<ref name="Estações_Bauru"/> Current efforts focus on preserving the main railway station’s heritage, with its administration transferred to the city hall in 2011.<ref name="Estações_Bauru"/>

Bauru has an extensive road network connecting it to various cities in the [[Interior of São Paulo|São Paulo interior]] and the state capital, with access to major state and national highways via paved, dual-lane secondary roads. As part of a major road junction, it offers easy connections to various parts of Brazil. The following highways pass through the municipality: João Ribeiro de Barros and Engineer João Batista Cabral Renno Highways (sections of [[SP-225 (São Paulo highway)|SP-225]]); João Ribeiro de Barros Highway (section of [[SP-294 (São Paulo highway)|SP-294]]); [[Rodovia Marechal Cândido Rondon|Marechal Rondon Highway]] (SP-300); and Cesário José de Carvalho Highway (section of [[SP-321 (São Paulo highway)|SP-321]]).<ref name="Câmara_Dados">{{cite web |author=Municipal Chamber |title=Aspectos Gerais |url=http://www.camarabauru.sp.gov.br/Camara/Page.aspx?tipo=aspectosgerais |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110719205802/http://www.camarabauru.sp.gov.br/Camara/Page.aspx?tipo=aspectosgerais |archive-date=2011-07-19 |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

The Bauru Bus Terminal is one of the main terminals in its region, handling an average of 25,000 passengers weekly.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=6 May 2012 |title=Vereador pede mais segurança e conforto no Terminal Rodoviário |url=http://94fm.com.br/noticias/bauru/vereador-pede-mais-seguranca-e-conforto-no-terminal-rodoviario/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8DWJMK?url=http://94fm.com.br/noticias/bauru/vereador-pede-mais-seguranca-e-conforto-no-terminal-rodoviario/ |archive-date=14 June 2012 |access-date=12 June 2012 |website=Rádio 94 FM |language=pt}}</ref> The most popular destinations from the terminal are [[São Paulo]], Rio de Janeiro, [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Curitiba]], [[Campo Grande]], [[Londrina]], and [[Maringá]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://emdurb.blogspot.com.br/2010/10/emdurb-informa-sobre-movimento-no.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8FIMkA?url=http://emdurb.blogspot.com.br/2010/10/emdurb-informa-sobre-movimento-no.html |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=EMDURB informa sobre Movimento no Terminal Rodoviário para o feriado de 12 de outubro |author=Bauru Municipal Urban and Rural Development Company (EMDURB) |date=12 October 2010 |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

===Urban ===
The Bauru Municipal Urban and Rural Development Company (EMDURB), established by Municipal Law No. 2166 of 25 September 1979, oversees and maintains the city’s traffic, including monitoring public roads, driver and pedestrian behavior, developing traffic engineering projects, paving, building road infrastructure, and managing services such as taxis, alternative transport, chartered and school buses.<ref name="Câmara_Dados"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.emdurb.com.br/site2010/historia_emdurb.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8GbW1V?url=http://www.emdurb.com.br/site2010/historia_emdurb.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=História |author=Bauru Municipal Urban and Rural Development Company (EMDURB) |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

In 2010, the municipal vehicle fleet totaled 203,651 vehicles, including 129,388 cars, 4,863 trucks, 608 tractor-trucks, 12,430 pickups, 5,650 vans, 426 minibuses, 37,689 motorcycles, 6,555 scooters, 1,045 buses, 14 wheeled tractors, 549 utility vehicles, and 4,434 other vehicle types.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=frota&codmun=350600&nomemun=Bauru |title=Frota 2010 |publisher=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Dual-lane, paved avenues and numerous traffic lights facilitate city traffic. However, the rapid increase in vehicle numbers over the past decade has led to slower traffic, particularly in the Seat District. Finding parking spaces in the commercial center has also become challenging, impacting local commerce.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://tn.temmais.com/noticia/8/19249/rodovias_sao_utilizadas_por_motoristas_de_bauru_para_encurtar_distancia.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8I8CIm?url=http://tn.temmais.com/noticia/8/19249/rodovias_sao_utilizadas_por_motoristas_de_bauru_para_encurtar_distancia.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Rodovias são utilizadas por motoristas de Bauru para encurtar distância |author=TV Tem |date=10 August 2010 |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Public transportation in Bauru is provided through urban and interurban buses and taxis, considered essential services. Urban bus transport is managed by the Bauru Urban Public Transport Companies Association (Transurb), established in 2002, which in 2010 operated 70 lines covering nearly the entire city.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.emdurb.com.br/site2010/coletivo/itinerarios.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8J4Jyn?url=http://www.emdurb.com.br/site2010/coletivo/itinerarios.htm |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Itinerários - linhas ônibus |author=Bauru Municipal Urban and Rural Development Company (EMBURB) |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> Transurb represents three public transport concessionaires: Grande Bauru Public Transport, Baurutrans CN General Transport, and Cidade Sem Limites.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.transurbbauru.com.br/do/Transurb |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Q8KAubg?url=http://www.transurbbauru.com.br/do/Transurb |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Transurb |author=Bauru Urban Public Transport Companies Association (Transurb) |access-date=14 June 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

=== Performing arts ===
Bauru is home to several venues dedicated to cultural events in theater and music. The Bauru Municipal Theater is a significant cultural hub both locally and statewide, offering a diverse range of renowned performances and attractions throughout the year. It occasionally hosts public musical performances, fairs, and exhibitions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/busca.aspx?q=Teatro+Municipal+de+Bauru |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiMjxaB?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/busca.aspx?q=Teatro+Municipal+de+Bauru |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Secretaria Municipal de Cultura divulga agenda de 13 a 20 de setembro e retoma rotina do Teatro Municipal de Bauru |author=Prefeitura |date=12 September 2011 |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref> Another notable venue is the Bauru Cultural Center, which was inaugurated on 15 March 1942.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

The Secretariat of Culture also provides art classes to the community. The Arts Education Division serves over 500 students per semester in courses such as [[guitar]], [[flute]], [[classical ballet]], [[jazz dance]], [[street dance]], [[modern dance]], theater, [[drawing]], [[ceramics]], and [[capoeira]]. It also forms dance groups composed of students who represent the city at festivals across the state and throughout Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_cultura/ensino_artes.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiNy9IX?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_cultura/ensino_artes.aspx |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Ensino às Artes |author=Prefeitura |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref>
[[File:Teatro Bauru.jpg|thumb|left|Celina Lourdes Alves Neves Theater.]]

The Municipal Library System comprises the Rodrigues de Abreu Municipal Library, the Ivan Engler de Almeida Children's Library, the Aucione Torres Agostinho Comic Library, eight smaller libraries, and mobile "Bibliobus" units that serve events and schools by appointment. The Bauru Central Library has been computerized since August 2002, offering over 44,000 works, as well as periodicals, videos, and a newspaper archive. The eight smaller libraries collectively hold a collection of 16,000 items. The city is also home to three museums: the Image and Sound Museum, the Bauru Regional Railway Museum, and the Bauru Municipal Historical Museum.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_cultura/bibliotecas_museus.aspx |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiPa8Rz?url=http://www.bauru.sp.gov.br/secretarias/sec_cultura/bibliotecas_museus.aspx |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Bibliotecas e Museus |author=Prefeitura |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref>

Bauru has 15 [[movie theater]]s, considered among the most modern in the region, attracting audiences from neighboring cities. These theaters are located at Bauru Shopping-Multiplex Cinema (five screens, including one with THX sound and one 3D screen), Boulevard Shopping Bauru-Cinépolis (six screens, including one Macro XE), and Alameda Quality Center-Cine ‘n Fun (four screens, all with THX sound). The cineclub movement, which was highly active in Bauru during the 1960s and 1970s (with the city hosting up to four cineclubs simultaneously, one dedicated to screening full operas), no longer has a permanent cineclub.<ref name="Prefeitura_Educação">{{cite web|url=http://hotsite.bauru.sp.gov.br/pme/arquivos/arquivos/11.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68RiQdKDl?url=http://hotsite.bauru.sp.gov.br/pme/arquivos/arquivos/11.pdf |archive-date=15 June 2012 |title=Plano Municipal de Educação |author=Prefeitura |date=19 August 2011 |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Events ===
[[File:Show de rock na Virada Cultural de Bauru SP.jpg|thumb|Rock concert at the 2010 Virada Cultural in Bauru.]]
Bauru is one of the host municipalities of the [[Virada Cultural#Virada Cultural Paulista|Virada Cultural Paulista]], an annual event held in various cities across São Paulo state, modeled after the [[Virada Cultural]] in the state capital. Organized in Bauru since 2007, the event aims to provide 24 hours of continuous cultural activities, including musical performances across various genres, theater productions, and art and history exhibitions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/Cultura/2012/05/virada-cultural-oferece-24-horas-de-arte-gratuita.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QJNNeMb?url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/Cultura/2012/05/virada-cultural-oferece-24-horas-de-arte-gratuita.html |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Virada Cultural oferece 24 horas de arte gratuita |author=JC Net |date=18 May 2012 |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref>

The Bauru Carnival was regarded in the 1980s as the most famous in the interior of São Paulo. The Bauru Municipal Sambadrome was the second to be inaugurated in Brazil, surpassed only by the [[Sambadrome Marquês de Sapucaí|Marquês de Sapucaí Sambadrome]]. In the early 2000s, the samba school parades were broadcast live online, a novelty at the time, having previously been transmitted via radio and, less frequently, local television channels. However, official parades were suspended starting in 2002, with no formal competition.<ref name="zcastel">{{cite web |url=http://www.zcastel.com.br/2011/03/08/o-carnaval-de-bauru/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61BC9j6KK?url=http://www.zcastel.com.br/2011/03/08/o-carnaval-de-bauru/ |archive-date=24 August 2011 |title=O carnaval de rua de Bauru |author=Equipe ZCastel |date=8 March 2011 |access-date=24 August 2011}}</ref> The parades resumed in 2010, and organizers are gradually working to restore the event’s prominence from past decades.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariosp.com.br/noticia/detalhe/13710/Anonimos+garantem+festa+do+Carnaval+em+Bauru |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QJJGUt4?url=http://www.diariosp.com.br/noticia/detalhe/13710/Anonimos+garantem+festa+do+Carnaval+em+Bauru |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Anônimos garantem festa do Carnaval em Bauru |author=Cristina Camargo |date=19 February 2012 |publisher=Diário SP |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Sports ===
[[File:Planador no Aeroclube de Bauru SP.jpg|thumb|left|Glider at the [[Bauru Airport]].]]
Bauru is home to several sports clubs, including [[Esporte Clube Noroeste]], the city’s largest and one of the most traditional football teams in São Paulo, founded on 1 September 1910.<ref name="capu">{{cite web|url=http://www.noroestebauru.com.br/noroestebauru/pt/index.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QFhXeuJ?url=http://www.noroestebauru.com.br/noroestebauru/pt/index.php |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=História do Clube |author=Esporte Clube Noroeste |access-date=16 December 2009}}</ref> Its stadium, [[Estádio Alfredo de Castilho]], is the largest in Bauru, with a capacity of over 17,000 spectators. The city also hosts an amateur football championship with two divisions and numerous clubs, with Parquinho Futebol Clube standing out as the most successful in the amateur scene.<ref name="Parquinho">{{cite web|url=http://clubesamadoresdofutebolpaulista.blogspot.com.br/2012/06/162-parquinho-futebol-clube.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QFmbF3p?url=http://clubesamadoresdofutebolpaulista.blogspot.com.br/2012/06/162-parquinho-futebol-clube.html |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Parquinho Futebol Clube |author=Lincoln Scorsoni |date=14 June 2012 |publisher=Clubes Amadores e Extintos do Futebol Paulista |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref> Additionally, Bauru is notable for being the city where [[Pelé]] began his career, playing in the youth categories of [[Bauru Atlético Clube]] before transferring to [[Santos FC]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://bauruac.com.br/fundacao.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QFnXwN1?url=http://bauruac.com.br/fundacao.php |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Fundação |author=Bauru Atlético Clube |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref>

Beyond football, Bauru offers facilities for various sports. [[American football]] is represented by the Bauru Hunters, active since 2009.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.blogger.com/profile/02696733564347007238 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QFoe2ZG?url=http://www.blogger.com/profile/02696733564347007238 |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru Hunters |author=Bauru Hunters |date=1 February 2009 |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref> The Bauru Aeroclub, located at the Bauru Airport, is the largest gliding center in Brazil, boasting the country’s largest and most diverse fleet of gliders and currently leading the national gliding rankings.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aeroclubebauru.com.br/o-aeroclube/bauru-a-capital-nacional-do-voo-a-vela/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QFpSbFQ?url=http://www.aeroclubebauru.com.br/o-aeroclube/bauru-a-capital-nacional-do-voo-a-vela/ |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Bauru: A Capital Nacional do Voo a Vela |author=Aeroclube de Bauru |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref> In motorsports, the “Toca da Coruja” kart track hosts regional, state, and national karting and motorcycle competitions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.livrevista.com/article.php?id=688 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QGLNIOt?url=http://www.livrevista.com/article.php?id=688 |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=A "Toca da Coruja" |author=Livre Vista |date=7 February 2012 |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref> In [[basketball]], the [[Bauru Basket]] has won the [[São Paulo State Basketball Championship]] in 1999, 2013, and 2014; the [[Brazilian Basketball Championship]] in 2002 and 2016-17; the [[South American Basketball League]] in 2014; the [[FIBA Americas League]] in 2015; and was runner-up in the [[FIBA Intercontinental Cup]] in 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/Esportes/2015/03/titulo-inedito-bauru-e-campeao-invicto-da-liga-das-americas.html|title=Título inédito: Bauru é campeão invicto da Liga das Américas - JCNET|access-date=2016-09-13}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=É CAMPEÃO! Gocil/Bauru fatura título do NBB 2016/17|work=jcnet.com.br|url=http://www.jcnet.com.br/Esportes/2017/06/campeao-gocilbauru-fatura-o-titulo-do-nbb-201617.html}}</ref> In [[volleyball]], the city is represented by [[Associação Vôlei Bauru|SESI Vôlei Bauru]], which won the São Paulo State Championship in 2018 and competes in the Women’s Superliga.<ref>{{cite web|title=Em momento histórico, Vôlei Bauru estreia na Superliga feminina contra Osasco|url=http://94fm.com.br/noticias/esportes/em-momento-historico-volei-bauru-estreia-na-superliga-feminina-contra-osasco/|website=94fm.com.br|access-date=2015-11-16|archive-date=2015-11-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151117024644/http://94fm.com.br/noticias/esportes/em-momento-historico-volei-bauru-estreia-na-superliga-feminina-contra-osasco/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gazetaesportiva.com/mais-esportes/volei/sesi-bauru-volta-bater-osasco-e-conquista-paulista-de-volei/|title=Sesi Bauru volta a bater Osasco e conquista Paulista de Vôlei - Gazeta Esportiva|access-date=2018-11-06|website=www.gazetaesportiva.com|date=5 November 2018 |language=pt-BR}}</ref>

=== Holidays ===
Bauru observes two municipal [[public holiday|holidays]], eight national holidays, and six optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the city’s anniversary, celebrated on 1 August, and [[All Souls' Day|All Souls’ Day]], observed on 2 November.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://antigo.bauru.sp.gov.br/prefeitura/site/conteudo/administracao/feriados_decreto.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68QEM9opv?url=http://antigo.bauru.sp.gov.br/prefeitura/site/conteudo/administracao/feriados_decreto.pdf |archive-date=14 June 2012 |title=Decreto nº 10846, de 9 de janeiro de 2009 |author=Prefeitura |date=9 January 2009 |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref> According to federal law No. 9,093, enacted on 12 September 1995, municipalities may designate up to four religious municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tscUhIs?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref><ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaLei">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tsfGoVI?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Lei Nº 9.093, de 12 de setembro de 1995 |author=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=14 June 2012}}</ref>
