'''Lauricocha''' is one of 11 [[Provinces of Peru|provinces]] of the [[Huánuco Region]] in [[Peru]]. The capital of the province is [[Jesús, Peru|Jesús]]. The province is found at high elevations in the [[Andes]]. The highest elevation in the province is snow-capped [[Yerupaja]] peak with an elevation of {{cvt|6617|m|feet}}<ref name="Andean Mountains - All above 5000m">{{Cite web|url=http://www.andes-specialists.com/andean-mountains-5000/|title=Andean Mountains - All above 5000m|website=Andes Specialists|language=en|access-date=2020-04-12}}</ref> and the lowest elevation is approximately {{cvt|3200|m|feet}} along the [[Marañon River]] north of the town of Rondos.<ref>Google Earth</ref> The population of the province was 19,956 in 2017 and had been declining for many years before that.

Lauricocha province is the source of the Marañon. [[Lauricocha Lake]] is the largest of many glacial lakes in the province.  The lake and the headwaters of the [[Lauricocha River|Lauricocha]] and [[Nupe River|Nupe]] Rivers have been identified as among the [[Source of the Amazon River|sources of the Amazon River]].<ref>Dasgupta, Shreya (2016), "Why the Source of the Amazon river remains a Mystery," BBC, [http://www.bbc.com/earth/story/20160516-why-it-is-hard-to-find-the-source-of-rivers-like-the-amazon], accessed 6 Nov 2018</ref> The high, cool climate of the province limits economic activity. Agriculture is mostly pastoral and [[potato]]es are the main crop.<ref>{{cite web |title=Peru is the leading potato producer in Latin America |url=https://peru.info/en-us/foreign-trade/news/7/29/on-top--peru-is-the-leading-potato-producer-in-latin-america |website=Peru Info |access-date=10 August 2021}}</ref> The Raura mine, producing [[copper]], [[lead]], [[silver]], and [[zinc]], is one of the highest mines in the world at an elevation of {{cvt|4791|m|feet}}.<ref name="Raura">{{cite web |title=The World's 10 Highest Mines |url=https://www.mining.com/the-worlds-10-highest-mines/ |website=Mining |access-date=10 August 2021}}</ref> The province is popular for hikers and mountain climbers, especially to hike and climb in the [[Cordillera Huayhuash]] on the western border of Lauricocha.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Rivera |first1=Agnes |title=The Huayhuash Trek: A High Elevation Eden |url=https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/huayhuash-trek-high-elevation-eden |website=Lonely Planet |access-date=10 Aug 2021}}</ref> The major north-south [[Inca road system|Inca road]], the Qhapaq Ňan, which runs from [[Cuzco]] to [[Quito]], [[Ecuador]], traverses Lauricocha province and is well-preserved along much of its route in this province.<ref name="Hyslop">{{cite book |last1=Hyslop |first1=John |title=The Inka Road System |date=1984 |publisher=Academic Press |location=Orlando |isbn=0-12-363460-1 |pages=68–69}}</ref>

==Location==
The province is bordered by the provinces of [[Dos de Mayo Province|Dos de Mayo]] and the [[Yarowilca Province|Yarowilca]] in the north, the provinces of [[Huánuco Province|Huánuco]] and [[Ambo Province|Ambo]] in the east, the [[Pasco Region]] in the south, and the [[Lima Region]] and the [[Ancash Region]] in the west.

===Climate===
Climatic information about the province of Lauricocha is available for Rondos and San Miguel de Cauri. The climate of Lauricocha is cool and cloudy. In the [[Köppen Classification]] system, elevations below about {{convert|3500|m|feet}} are usually [[Oceanic climate|Cfb]] climates (temperate, cool summers) or Cwb (temperate, cool summers, dry winters). At higher elevations, the climate is ET ([[Alpine tundra]]).<ref name="Weatherspark">{{cite web |title=Average Weather in Rondos |url=https://weatherspark.com/y/21372/Average-Weather-in-Rondos-Peru-Year-Round |website=Weatherspark |access-date=7 August 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=San Miguel de Cauri, Peru |url=https://www.weatherbase.com/weather/weather.php3?s=604546&cityname=San-Miguel-de-Cauri-Peru |website=Weatherbase |access-date=10 August 2021}}</ref> At elevations of {{cvt|3500|m|ft}}, average monthly temperatures hover around {{convert|10|C|F}} for every month of the year. Annual precipitation is about {{convert|850|mm|inch}}. The permanent snowline in the Huayhuash and Raura mountains is about {{convert|5000|m|ft}}.<ref name="McFadden">{{cite journal |last1=McFadden |first1=E. M. |last2=Ramage |first2=J. |last3=Rodbell |first3=D. T. |title=Landsat TM and ETM+ derived snowline altitudes in the Huayhuash and Cordillera Raura, Peru, 1986-2005 |journal=The Cryosphere |date=2011 |volume=5 |pages=419–430 |url=https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/5/419/2011/}}</ref>

== Ethnic groups ==
The province is inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Spanish language|Spanish]], however, is the language which the majority of the population (89.48%) learnt to speak in childhood, 10.36% of the residents started speaking using the [[Quechua language]] ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=2013-01-27 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007</ref>
