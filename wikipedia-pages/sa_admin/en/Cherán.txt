'''Cherán''' ({{IPA|es|tʃe'ɾan}}) is a municipality located in the [[Mexican state]] of [[Michoacán]], which is situated in the central western portion of Mexico, extending west to the Pacific Shore. Cherán itself lies in the northwestern portion of Michoacán, about {{convert|360|km|abbr=on}} west of Mexico City and approximately {{convert|123|km|mi|abbr=on}} west of the state capital of [[Morelia]]; its average elevation is {{convert|2251|m}} above sea level.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} The ''Municipio'' (municipality) of Cherán is reported to have a population of 16,243, while the ''Localidad'' (town) of Cherán is officially accounted to have a population of 12,616, including 5,827 men and 6,787 women.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}}

Cherán is one of a contiguous group of eleven ''Municipios'' that are demographically denoted as [[Purépecha]]. Inhabitants speak the [[Purépecha language]], as well as the local variety of Spanish.<ref>Prince, Alan. [http://www.bookpage.com/0110bp/nonfiction/crossing_over.html Review of Crossing Over] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061202123739/http://www.bookpage.com/0110bp/nonfiction/crossing_over.html |date=December 2, 2006 }}</ref> Cherán is noted for its unique style of government that followed a 2011 civil uprising over local concerns about corruption and crime. The area is now treated as a self-ruling indigenous community, largely free of federal intervention.

== Etymology ==
In his book ''Crossing Over'', [[Rubén Martínez (writer)|Rubén Martínez]] explains that ''Cherán''  means "a place of fear" in the [[Purépecha language]], alluding to its unfriendly landscape of "abrupt, irregular peaks and chasms" which bodes disaster to anyone taking a careless step.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}}

== 2011 uprising and the transition away from government control ==
Like many other Michoacán communities, Cherán suffered from organized crime, bribed politicians and corrupt police. Kidnappings, extortion, murders, and illegal logging of the local forest—the lifeblood of the community—were part of daily life.<ref name=":1">{{Cite news|url=https://truthout.org/articles/life-without-politicians-a-mexican-indigenous-community-finds-its-own-way/|title=Life Without Politicians: A Mexican Indigenous Community Finds Its Own Way|last=Cárdenas|first=Lourdes|date=2016}}</ref><ref>{{Citation|last=Cortés Calderón|first=Sofía Valeria|chapter=Security, Justice, and Forest Protection! An Indigenous Community's Fight Against Illegal Deforestation and Organised Crime in Cherán, Mexico|date=2018|title=Green Crime in Mexico: A Collection of Case Studies|pages=63–73|editor-last=Arroyo-Quiroz|editor-first=Ines|series=Palgrave Studies in Green Criminology|publisher=Springer International Publishing|language=en|doi=10.1007/978-3-319-75286-0_5|isbn=978-3-319-75286-0|editor2-last=Wyatt|editor2-first=Tanya}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://elenemigocomun.net/2012/06/autonomous-paths-converge-cheran/|title=Autonomous Paths Converge in Cherán|date=2012-06-03|website=El Enemigo Común|language=en-US|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref> ''[[Los Angeles Times|The Los Angeles Times]]'' provides the background of the 2011 uprising:<ref name=":0">{{Cite news|url=http://www.latimes.com/world/mexico-americas/la-fg-mexico-cheran-20170710-htmlstory.html#|title=One Mexican town revolts against violence and corruption. Six years in, its experiment is working|last=McDonnell|first=Patrick J.|date=July 10, 2017|work=Los Angeles Times}}</ref> <blockquote>That was the year that residents, most of them indigenous and poor, waged an insurrection and declared self-rule in hopes of ridding themselves of the ills that plague so much of Mexico: raging violence, corrupt politicians, a toothless justice system and gangs that have expanded from drug smuggling to extortion, kidnapping and illegal logging.</blockquote>

In the three years leading up to the uprising, cartels had clear cut about half of the community's almost 60,000 acres; afterwards the community banned logging healthy wood and began a project to regrow native trees.<ref name=":0"/>

"To defend ourselves," explained a community leader, "we had to change the whole system — out with the political parties, out with City Hall, out with the police and everything. We had to organize our own way of living to survive".<ref name=":0" /> Thus, on April 15, 2011 a group of women and men using rocks and fireworks attacked a busload of illegal loggers who were armed with machine guns and associated with the [[Mexican drug cartel]] [[La Familia Michoacana]]. The [[vigilante]]s assumed control over the town, expelled the police force and politicians and blocked roads leading to the forest that had been subject to [[illegal logging]] by armed gangs supported by corrupt officials.<ref name="NYT8212">{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/08/03/world/americas/in-mexico-reclaiming-the-forests-and-the-right-to-feel-safe.html|title=Reclaiming the Forests and the Right to Feel Safe|author=Karla Zabludovsky|date=August 2, 2012|newspaper=The New York Times|access-date=August 3, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.cbc.ca/thecurrent/episode/2013/09/16/cheran-citizens-stand-up-to-cartels-for-illegal-logging/|title=Cheran citizens stand up to drug cartels|author=Anna Maria Tremonti|date=2013-09-16|work=[[CBC.ca]]|author-link=Anna Maria Tremonti}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Cite web|url=http://upsidedownworld.org/archives/mexico/self-defense-groups-and-community-police-forces-in-mexico-differences-as-seen-from-the-villages/|title=Self-defense Groups and Community Police Forces in Mexico: Differences as Seen from the Villages|last=Hewitt|first=Christina|date=2014-02-09|website=Upside Down World|language=en-US|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref>

Community policing has been extended to twenty-thousand inhabitants, and over 27,000 hectares of communal land.<ref name=":3" />  Neighborhood watch members patrol both the town and the surrounding forests.<ref name=":6">{{Cite web|url=https://ourworld.unu.edu/en/the-cheran-indigenous-communitys-remarkable-road-to-self-rule-in-mexico|title=The Cherán Indigenous Community's Remarkable Road to Self-rule in Mexico|last=Salazar|first=Giovanna|date=2015-04-24|website=Our World, brought to you by United Nations University|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref>

The [[Constitution of Mexico]] permits self government and self-policing by indigenous communities.<ref name=":7">{{Cite web|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/after-long-fight-self-government-indigenous-town-cher-n-mexico-n906171|title=After long fight for self-government, indigenous town of Cherán, Mexico ushers in new council|website=NBC News|language=en|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref> Following lengthy legal battles, the Mexican government is treating autonomous Cherán as a legal self-governing indigenous community.<ref name="NYT8212" /><ref name=":6" /> <blockquote>"In Cherán’s unique form of government, the real power lies wholly with the people. There is not a single decision taken without consensus, from who will get a local job in construction, to the allocation of public services and overseeing the spending of the budget. The authority of the community’s assembly is above any other local governmental body."<ref name=":1" /></blockquote>The community elects a 12-person council, "K'eri Jánaskakua," and has about 180 ''fogatas'' (campfires / community fires) in its four neighborhoods.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://longreads.tni.org/state-of-power-2018/people-defence-life-territory/|title=Counter-power and self-defence in Latin America - State of power 2018|last=Zibechi|first=Raúl|date=2018|website=Longreads|language=en-US|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://subversiones.org/archivos/115140|title=Cheran K'eri: cuatro años construyendo autonomía|date=2015-04-21|website=SubVersiones|language=es|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref><ref name=":7" />

The Third Council (Council of Elders, High Council, Consejo Mayor, Consejo de Keris) was named in 2018.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://elenemigocomun.net/2018/08/cheran-names-government/|title=Cherán Names its Third Council of Elders to Their Communal Government|date=2018-08-15|website=El Enemigo Común|language=en-US|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref><ref name=":7" /> Other representative bodies include a youth council, a women's council, neighborhood councils, and a communal territory council focused on business development.<ref name=":7" />

The town has prohibited political parties and political campaigns.<ref name=":7" /><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://elenemigocomun.net/2015/02/cheran-political-parties-dead/|title=Cherán K'eri: Political parties are dead to us in this town » El Enemigo Común|last=Petersays|date=2015-02-10|website=El Enemigo Común|language=en-US|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref>

According to ''The Guardian'',<ref name=":2">{{Cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/apr/03/mexico-indigenous-town-banned-politicians-cheran|title=The Mexican indigenous community that ran politicians out of town|last=Agren|first=David|date=April 3, 2018|work=The Guardian}}</ref> Cherán's version of [[direct democracy]] provided "a simple solution to the vote-buying and patronage which plague Mexican democracy." Direct democracy, according to one community activist, not only saved the forest, but brought peace: In 2017, Cherán had the lowest homicide rate in the entire state of Michoacán and perhaps even in the entire country of Mexico.<ref name=":2" />

==Geography==
Rubén Martínez describes the length of Cherán from north to south as about three-quarters of a mile.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}}

===Climate===
Cherán is in a tropical area but, because of its altitude, is cooler than the lower lying jungles and coastlands.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} ''Instituto Nacional de Estadística Geografía e Informática'' ([[INEGI]]) indicates that the average temperatures for nearby [[Zamora, Michoacán|Zamora]], 48 kilometers away, between the years of 1971 and 1999 ranged between 17&nbsp;°C and 24&nbsp;°C.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} INEGI also reported average annual precipitation in Zamora over the same period of time as being {{convert|820.3|mm|abbr=on}}.<ref>[http://mapserver.inegi.gob.mx/geografia/espanol/estados/mich/precip_tot_an.cfm?c=452&e=16&CFID=1213064&CFTOKEN=99461171 Total Annual Precipitation] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110722225633/http://mapserver.inegi.gob.mx/geografia/espanol/estados/mich/precip_tot_an.cfm?c=452&e=16&CFID=1213064&CFTOKEN=99461171 |date=July 22, 2011 }}</ref>

== Business and government statistics ==
The three prominent buildings in Cherán include the church, the ''presidencia municipal'', and the ''casa de cambio'', or currency exchange center.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} In 2000, the government website reported the town was home to 2,589 dwellings.<ref name=":8">{{Cite web|date=2007-09-27|title=PORTAL DE LOS MUNICIPIOS DEL ESTADO DE MICHOACAN - Cherán - Nuestro Municipio|url=http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/cheran/municipio/estadistica/infraestructura.php|access-date=2020-06-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927162508/http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/cheran/municipio/estadistica/infraestructura.php|archive-date=2007-09-27}}</ref>

==Infrastructure==
In 2000, the municipal website of Cherán reported that 80% of water is potable, 60% of the roads are paved, 90% of the streets have public lighting, and 35% of garbage is collected.<ref name=":8" /> The municipality also reported having pre-school, primary, and secondary educational facilities.<ref name=":8" /> Adults have access to the services of the ''Instituto Nacional de Educación''.<ref name=":8" />

A [[Rainwater harvesting|rainwater capture]] system located at the extinct volcano Cerro de Kukundikata covers 1.5 hectares. It is capable of providing 20,000 liters of drinking water, sufficient for two months of drought.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.milenio.com/estados/cheran-5-anos-crimen-partidos-politicos|title=Cherán: 5 años sin crimen ni partidos políticos|date=2016-09-13|website=www.milenio.com|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref><ref name=":4">{{Cite web|url=https://matadornetwork.com/es/cheran-pueblo-indigena-que-consiguio-el-autogobierno-en-mexico/|title=Conoce al pueblo indígena que consiguió el autogobierno en México y que va camino al primer mundo|website=Matador Español|language=es|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref>

==Economy==
The average annual wage is estimated at about US$3,000. {{Citation needed|date=June 2020}}

In 2007, agriculture and raising livestock accounted for 49% of Cherán's economic activity: corn, wheat, potatoes, beans and oats make up the bulk of the community's harvest while its local farmers raise cows, horses, pigs, sheep and goats. Wooden products, including furniture and furniture repair, and cork account for less than 19% of the economy. Commercial production of apples, peaches, apricots, pears and plums comprised 10% of Cherán's economic resources. There were essentially no services besides ''fondas'', or small restaurants, that serve local fare.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/cheran/municipio/estadistica/economia.php |title=PORTAL DE LOS MUNICIPIOS DEL ESTADO DE MICHOACAN - Cherán - Nuestro Municipio |access-date=2006-12-09 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927162442/http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/cheran/municipio/estadistica/economia.php |archive-date=2007-09-27 }}</ref>

In 2015, the town created its own [[Worker cooperative|collectively owned]] sawmill, greenhouse and concrete factory.<ref name=":4" /> The town engages in the production of resin and concrete blocks for construction.<ref>{{Cite web|title=After long fight for self-government, indigenous town of Cherán, Mexico ushers in new council|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/after-long-fight-self-government-indigenous-town-cher-n-mexico-n906171|access-date=2020-07-07|website=NBC News|language=en}}</ref>

=== Forestry ===
One estimate states that about half of the area's 17,000 hectares of forest have been lost to illegal logging.<ref name=":5">{{Cite news|last=Pressly|first=Linda|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-37612083|title=The town that threw out police, politicians and gangsters|date=2016-10-13|work=BBC News|access-date=2020-02-08|language=en-GB}}</ref> According to other sources, "about fifty thousand acres of forest were illegally cut between 2008 and 2011," and 200–250 [[logging truck]]s a day passed through the town.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Americas/2013/0313/One-Mexican-town-finds-more-security-by-throwing-out-the-police|title=One Mexican town finds more security by throwing out the police|date=2013-03-13|work=Christian Science Monitor|access-date=2020-02-08|issn=0882-7729}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://indyreader.org/content/cher%C3%A1n-community-self-defense-mexico%E2%80%99s-drug-war-photo-essay|title=Cherán: Community Self Defense in Mexico's Drug War (Photo Essay)|date=4 July 2011|website=Indypendent Reader|access-date=2020-02-08}}</ref>

During the initial confrontation that provoked the 2011 uprising, women were concerned that logging threatened a [[Spring (hydrology)|spring]] important for local water supply.<ref name=":5" /><ref name=":4" />

In the five years since the uprising, the town's tree nursery provided seedlings to replant 3,000 hectares of trees.<ref name=":5" />

In 2022, AP News published an update about the community which patrols the forest looking for illegal logging and avocado planting.<ref>{{cite news|title=Mexican town protects forest from avocado growers, cartels|url=https://apnews.com/article/business-world-news-mountains-mexico-caribbean-32a4508534bffb00cf341119de733d5d}}</ref>

==Culture==
The town's [[Group cohesiveness|social cohesion]] stems in part from its many large [[Extended family|extended families]]. Most residents are native to the area, and it is customary to marry within the town.<ref name=":5" />

==Media==
[[XEPUR-AM]], a government-run [[Indigenous peoples of Mexico|indigenous]] [[community radio]] [[radio station|station]] that broadcasts in [[Purépecha language|Purépecha]], is based in Cherán.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} The community also has a YouTube channel and blog.{{Citation needed|date=June 2020}} [[Community television]] station TV Cherán was launched in Nov 29, 2014.<ref name=":6" />
