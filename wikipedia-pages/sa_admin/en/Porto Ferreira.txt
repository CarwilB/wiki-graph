'''Porto Ferreira''' is a municipality in the state of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]] in [[Brazil]]. It is situated on the [[Mojiguaçu River]] at an altitude of 559 meters. The population is 56,504 (2020 est.) in an area of 244.9&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref>[http://cod.ibge.gov.br/236V3 Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]</ref>

==History==
The valley where Porto Ferreira now lies used to be inhabited by [[Payaguá people]]. The settlement Porto Ferreira on the [[Mojiguaçu River]] was founded in the 1860s, at the place of a ferry. It was named Ferreira after the ferryman João Inácio Ferreira. The settlement grew because of its strategic location on the way to the coffee plantations in the region of [[Ribeirão Preto]]. Porto Ferreira was officially established on 9 February 1888, as a part of the municipality [[Descalvado]]. It was detached from Descalvado and attached to [[Pirassununga]] on 1 October 1892. It became an independent municipality on 29 July 1896.<ref>[https://cod.ibge.gov.br/1KXT IBGE], history</ref>

== Geography ==
The highest point of the city is the High Mount, with 798 meters. The lowest point is the Island of the Ducks, with 545 meters. In the Railroad station the altitude is of 549.7 meters above sea level.

The municipality holds the {{convert|611|ha}} [[Porto Ferreira State Park]], created in 1987.<ref>{{citation |date=21 November 2012 |language=pt|ref={{harvid|Parque Estadual Porto Ferreira completa 50 anos}}

=== Topography ===
The general city landscape is flat, with small hills, slightly sloping towards the basins of the Moji Guaçu river and its tributaries. To the north, is possible to see some mountains.

=== Transportation ===
The city is served by two state highways, [[Rodovia Anhangüera]] (SP 330), SP 215.  There are also the disused rails from the former state railway company, FEPASA.

=== Soil ===
Four types of soil are found, thus distributed for the area of the city: Yellow Red [[Latosol]] (62&nbsp;km ²), Purple Latosol (74&nbsp;km ²), Hidromórfico (27&nbsp;km ²) and Dark Red Latosol (77&nbsp;km ²).
