'''Villarrica''' is a [[List of cities in Chile|city]] and [[Communes of Chile|commune]] in southern [[Chile]] located on the western shore of [[Villarrica Lake]] in the [[Cautín Province|Province of Cautín]], [[Araucanía Region]] {{convert|746|km|0|abbr=on}} south of [[Santiago]] and close to the [[Villarrica (volcano)|Villarrica Volcano]] ski center to the south east. Residents of Villarrica are known as ''Villarricences''.

Tourism, grain and dairy farming, and forestry are the main economic activities of the community. The climate is mild and humid with the greatest rainfall experienced between May and July (autumn and winter). The dry season is between December and February (Summer).

Other lakes found nearby include [[Calafquén Lake|Calafquén]], [[Caburgua Lake|Caburgua]], Huilipilún and [[Colico Lake|Colico]]. In the summer water sports and sunbathing are popular activities in the warm temperatures experienced at the low altitudes ranging from 200 to 500m above sea level.

Fishing and rafting are popular activities in the various rivers in the area, which include the [[Toltén River|Toltén]], Voipir, [[Trancura River|Trancura]], Llancahue and Lincura. Popular waterfalls include León, La China, Palguín, [[Ojos del Caburga]]. Thanks to the numerous volcanos, hot springs with temperatures ranging from 48&nbsp;°C to 90&nbsp;°C, are found nearby. Included are [[Coñaripe]] (48&nbsp;°C), [[San Luis (Chile)|San Luis]] (43&nbsp;°C), [http://www.termashuife.com Huife] (49&nbsp;°C), [[Palguín]] (57&nbsp;°C) and [[Liquiñe]] (87&nbsp;°C).

[[Villarrica National Park]] is {{convert|36|km|0|abbr=on}} away, and was created to preserve the natural beauty of the volcano. The area is noted for using wood as its principal building material.

== History ==
Villarrica was founded as ''Santa María Magdalena de la Villa Rica'' (''Saint Mary Magdalene of the Rich Town'') in 1552 by [[Jerónimo de Alderete]]. The site of foundation was likely near that of present-day [[Pucón]].<ref name=Petit2004>{{cite book |last=Petit-Breuilh Sepúlveda|first=María Eugenia |date=2004 |title=La historia eruptiva de los volcanes hispanoamericanos (Siglos XVI al XX): El modelo chileno|chapter=Los volcanes y la evolución del poblamiento |location=Huelva, Spain |publisher=Casa de los volcanes|language=es |pages=48–50 |isbn=978-84-95938-32-9 }}</ref> During its early years Villarrica was an important [[history of mining in Chile|gold and silver mining]] centre.<ref name=Petit2004/> However mining activity declined likely as consequence of burial of [[placer deposit]]s beneath [[lahar]]s of [[Villarrica (volcano)|Villarrica Volcano]]. This burial would have prompted settlers to move the city westward to its modern site, a place less prone to [[volcanic hazard]]s.<ref name=Petit2004/> Gold mined in Villarrica was known, together with gold from [[Osorno, Chile|Osorno]] and other localities, as "gold from [[Valdivia]]" ({{langx|es|oro de Valdivia}}) as the [[Mint (facility)|mint]] where gold was sent was in that city.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Camus |first1=Pablo |last2=Solari |first2=María Eugenia |date=2008 |title=La invención de la selva austral. Bosques y tierras despejadas en la cuenca del río Valdivia (siglos XVI-XIX) |journal=[[Norte Grande Geography Journal|Revista de Geografía Norte Grande]] |language=es |volume=40 |issue= 40|pages=5–22 |doi=10.4067/S0718-34022008000200001   |doi-access=free }}</ref>

Abandoned after his death in 1554, it was refounded five years later by [[García Hurtado de Mendoza, 5th Marquis of Cañete|García Hurtado de Mendoza]].  It grew into a small city, despite suffering destruction by a [[1575 Valdivia earthquake|great earthquake on December 16, 1575]]. It had opened communications with [[Buenos Aires]] in its last years.

Following the [[Battle of Curalaba]] in the [[Arauco War]], the last Spanish city to the south of the [[Bío Bío River]] was destroyed after a three-year-long siege, surrendering on February 7, 1603.  Before its destruction it had more than 600 Spanish inhabitants, not counting its Indian servants. At its surrender only 11 men and 13 women were taken captive.

The city was refounded on the site of the old city in the 1880s during the [[occupation of Araucanía]]. The population in 2002 was 45,531. Population growth and economic development has increased, due to a global reputation as a tourist destination for nature lovers, summer recreation and winter sports enthusiasts. {{Citation needed|date=July 2010}}

After the city's refounding, the Chilean government established a land sale program to encourage settlement and invited European, esp. [[Swiss people|Swiss]], [[Germans|German]] and [[Austrians|Austrian]] immigrants in the last half of the 19th century to populate an area.

According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Villarrica spans an area of {{convert|1291.1|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 45,531 inhabitants (22,694 men and 22,837 women). Of these, 30,859 (67.8%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 14,672 (32.2%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 26.9% (9,664 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Villarrica is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2021 alcalde is Germán Vergara Lagos (independent).<ref name="Munitel"/><ref name="Official"/>

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Villarrica is part of the 23rd District in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] (Araucanía South). The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] as part of the Araucanía Senate Constituency.
