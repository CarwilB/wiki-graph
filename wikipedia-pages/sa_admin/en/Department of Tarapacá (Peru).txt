'''Tarapacá''' ([[Aymara language|Aymara]]: ''Tarapaka'')<ref>{{Cite book |title=Diccionario Ilustrado de la Lengua Aymara |last=Castro Mamani |first=Edith |publisher=[[Ministry of Education (Chile)|Ministerio de Educación de Chile]] |year=2019 |edition=2nd |pages=47 |language=es |url=https://peib.mineduc.cl/recursos/diccionario-ilustrado-de-la-lengua-aymara/}}</ref> was a [[Regions of Peru|department]] of [[Peru]]. Located in the country's then southernmost coast, it existed as a [[Provinces of Peru|province]] from 1837 to 1878, when it was elevated to a department, after which it was occupied by [[Chile]] in 1879 and unconditionally ceded in 1883. Its capital was [[Iquique]].

Tarapacá was created in 1837 as a province of the [[Department of the Littoral (Peru)|Littoral]], a department of [[South Peru]]. The short-lived country became one of three constituent countries of the [[Peru–Bolivian Confederation]], which was [[Dissolution of the Peru–Bolivian Confederation|dissolved]] in 1839 following the [[War of the Confederation]]. It was later given autonomy as a [[Provinces of Peru|littoral province]] in 1868, after which it was elevated to a [[Regions of Peru|department]] in 1878. Tarapacá was essential to the [[Peruvian nitrate monopoly]], created by the Peruvian government in 1875 to capitalize on the high demand for nitrates at the time.

The political disputes that emerged over the territory of the [[Atacama Desert]] and its respective exploitation rights eventually led to war in 1879. The conflict, later known as the [[War of the Pacific]], concluded with a Chilean military victory and the occupation of several areas of Peru and the Bolivian coast. Tarapacá had been [[Tarapacá campaign|occupied]] since 1879, starting with a [[Battle of Pisagua|landing operation]] on November 2, but was only formally ceded in 1883, with the signing of the [[Treaty of Ancón]].

== Etymology ==
The department's name is of pre-Columbian origin, originally used to describe [[Viracocha]], a local deity adored both by the [[Inca Empire|Incans]] and their predecessors.<ref>{{Cite journal |title=Tarapacá: el, dios cautivo. Reflexiones en torno al regionalismo de los tarapaqueños del Callao |journal=[[Pontifical Catholic University of Peru|Debates En Sociología]] |last=González Miranda |first=Sergio |date=1997-12-01 |url=https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/debatesensociologia/article/view/6966 |volume=22 |issue=22 |pages=143–151 |language=es |doi=10.18800/debatesensociologia.199701.006}}</ref> It was first used by [[Diego de Almagro]] as a geographical term in 1536.<ref name=UrbinaUribe>{{Cite journal |title=Tarapacá: aportes a la historia andina desde una perspectiva regional (s. XV-XVI) |last1=Urbina Araya |first1=Simón |last2=Uribe Rodríguez |first2=Mauricio |journal=[[Pontifical Catholic University of Peru|Boletín De Arqueología PUCP]] |date=2016-10-04 |url=https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/boletindearqueologia/article/view/18671 |volume=20 |pages=167–185 |language=es |doi=10.18800/boletindearqueologiapucp.201601.008}}</ref>

== History ==
{{See also|Tarapacá Region#History}}
[[File:Plano general del Reyno del Perú en la America Meridional de Andrés Baleato (1796).jpg|thumb|left|Tarapacá within Arequipa in 1796.]]
Prior to the [[Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire]], the area was part of the [[Qullasuyu]], one of the empire's four regions or ''suyu''. It was also part of the ''Colesuyu'' (or ''Colesuyo''), an administrative division that differed from the aforementioned four subdivisions whose exact boundaries are disputed.<ref name=UrbinaUribe/> According to historian [[María Rostworowski]], it possibly comprised an area that reached from [[Camaná]] to [[Tarapacá]], with a width of 20 to 30 [[League (unit)|leagues]] to the east of the [[Pacific Ocean]].<ref>{{Cite journal |title=La región del Colesuyu |journal=[[University of Tarapacá|Chungará: Revista de Antropología Chilena]] |last=Rostworowski |first=María |date=1986-10-01 |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/27801862 |issue=16–17 |pages=127–135 |via=[[JSTOR]] |author-link=María Rostworowski |jstor=27801862 |language=es}}</ref>

The [[Kuraka|curaca]] (tribal chief) of the coastal region in Tarapacá of the Kingdom of [[Chucuito]] prior to the Spanish conquest was Felipe Lucaya.

The area was first explored and named by [[Diego de Almagro]] in late 1536 as part of [[Diego de Almagro's expedition to Chile|his expedition]] that concluded the following year. In 1558, fellow conquistadors [[Jerónimo de Vivar]] and [[Pedro de Valdivia]] described the area as being located 37 leagues south of a path that began in the [[Tacna|province of Tacna]].<ref name=UrbinaUribe/> In 1565, the [[Arica Province (Peru)|corregimiento of San Marcos de Arica]] was created which, at the time, included Tarapacá.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Fundación de Antofagasta y su primera década |last=Cruz Larenas |first=Jorge |publisher=[[Antofagasta|Ilustre Municipalidad de Antofagasta]] |year=1966 |pages=18 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Ryp5AAAAMAAJ}}</ref>

In 1600, the parcels of Lluta, Arica, Azapa, Tarapacá were handed over to Pedro Mesia Cordova, who then handed over the valleys of Tácana and Sama. In 1612 [[Pope Paul V]] authorised the establishment of the [[Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Arequipa|Diocese of Arequipa]] in which were seven jurisdictions including the corregimiento of Arica, comprising the regions of Tacna, Tarata, Sama, Ilabaya, Locumba, Putina and Tarapacá.

The '''corregimiento of Tarapacá''' ({{langx|es|Corregimiento de Tarapacá}}) was established by Viceroy [[Manuel de Amat y Junyent]] in May 1768,<ref>{{Cite journal |title=Prosperidad y decadencia del mineral de Huantajaya: Una aproximación |journal=[[University of Tarapacá|Diálogo Andino - Revista de Historia, Geografía y Cultura Andina]] |last=Donoso Rojas |first=Carlos |url=http://www.redalyc.org/articulo.oa?id=371336242005 |issue=32 |via=[[Redalyc]] |year=2008 |pages=59–70 }}</ref> separate from Arica and composed of four ''repartimientos'': Camiña, Sibaya, Tarapacá and Pica.

=== History as a province ===
[[File:Departamento moquegua 1865.JPG|thumb|right|Tarapacá within Moquegua in 1865.]]
The '''Province of Tarapacá''' ({{langx|es|Provincia de Tarapacá}}) was created in 1837 as part of the [[Department of the Littoral (Peru)|Department of the Littoral]], a subdivision of the [[Peru–Bolivian Confederation|Confederate state]] of [[South Peru]]. The newly formed department was created from territory originally belonging to the [[department of Arequipa]], and also included the [[Tacna province|province of Tacna]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.leyes.congreso.gob.pe/Documentos/LeyesXIX/1837027.pdf |title=Peru–Bolivian Law establishing the Littoral Department |date=1837-04-25 |website=[[Congress of Peru]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221103205128/https://www.leyes.congreso.gob.pe/Documentos/LeyesXIX/1837027.pdf |archive-date=2022-11-03 |quote=Las provincias de Tacna y Tarapacá quedan separadas del departamento de la Ley [Arequipa], en lo civil y político, y forman otro departamento independiente de él, con el nombre de Departamento Litoral.}}</ref>

Following the [[War of the Confederation]] and the separation of Peruvian and Bolivian states, Peruvian president [[Agustín Gamarra]] invaded Bolivia seeking to annex to [[Lower Peru]] what once was [[Upper Peru]]. The invasion led to [[Peruvian-Bolivian War of 1841-1842|a full-scale war]] between both countries, which ended following Gamarra's defeat at the [[Battle of Ingavi]] in 1841 by General [[José Ballivián]]. Ballivián's troops occupied Puno, Moquegua, Tacna and Tarapacá, until Peruvian José María Lavaysen's troops from Sama defeated the invading army. In Locumba, Colonel [[Manuel de Mendiburu]] also organised forces, as well as Justo Arias Aragüez in 1842. In 1853, the [[Department of Moquegua]] was created. Its provinces included Tarapacá, Moquegua, [[Tacna Province|Tacna]], and [[Arica Province|Arica]].

The '''Littoral Province of Tarapacá''' ({{langx|es|Provincia Litoral de Tarapacá}}) was established on December 1, 1868, through a law that separated it from the [[department of Moquegua]] and gave it equal status to the country's [[Regions of Peru|departments]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.leyes.congreso.gob.pe/Documentos/LeyesXIX/1868041.pdf |title=L. 1 de Diciembre de 1868. Separando del departamento de Moquegua la provincia de Tarapacá con la denominación de provincia litoral. |date=1868-12-01 |website=[[Congress of Peru]] |last=Balta |first=José |author-link=José Balta}}</ref> This autonomous status lasted until 1878.

On August 9, 1873, the government of Peru passed the [[Municipalities of Peru|Organic Law of Municipalities]] ({{langx|es|Ley Orgánica de Municipalidades}}). On October 28 of the same year, a Municipal Council ({{langx|es|Consejo Municipal}}) was installed in the city of Iquique, composed of a mayor, a deputy mayor, and three ''regidores''. This governing body functioned until December 31, 1875. Iquique was elevated to capital of the littoral province on February 23 of the same year, which meant that both the Departmental Council ({{langx|es|consejo departamental}}) and the Provincial Council ({{langx|es|consejo provincial}}) had to move to the city. The former was installed on January 3, 1876, and headed by [[Antonio Gutiérrez de la Fuente]]. The latter, headed by [[Alfonso Ugarte]], followed suit the day after.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Trabajos y antecedentes presentados al supremo gobierno de Chile por la Comisión consultiva del norte |last=Salas Lavaqui |first=Manuel |publisher=[[Ministry of the Interior (Chile)|Ministerio del Interior]] |year=1908 |pages=360 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=jg7cVKdhZyMC |chapter=IV. Importancia Municipal de Iquique}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |title=El Dios cautivo |last=González Miranda |first=Sergio |publisher=LOM Ediciones |year=2004 |isbn=9789560010933 |pages=132 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=qcCEDwAAQBAJ}}</ref> {{ill|Modesto Molina Paniagua|es|Modesto Molina}} served as prefect of Tarapacá until 1876.

=== History as a department ===
On August 17, 1878, the littoral province was elevated to a [[Regions of Peru|department]]. This newly formed subdivision was established with two provinces: Tarapacá and Iquique. Both provinces' capital cities were their respective settlements of the same name.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.leyes.congreso.gob.pe/Documentos/LeyesXIX/1878041.pdf |title=Law establishing the Department of Tarapacá |date=1878-08-17 |website=[[Congress of Peru]] |last=Ignacio Prado |first=Mariano |author-link=Mariano Ignacio Prado}}</ref> The province of Iquique was composed of the department's flat terrain, while Tarapacá was composed of the more rough and mountainous part.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Arbitraje de Tacna y Arica: Documentos de la Comision Especial de Límites |publisher=Casa Editora "La Opinion Nacional" ([[Jirón Callao|Mantas]] 152) / [[Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Peru)|Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores]] |year=1926 |location=Lima |pages=34 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JigL_LCyJ20C}}</ref>
[[File:La ville et le port d'Iquique- Le Monde illustré - 1879.jpg|thumb|right|The [[Blockade of Iquique]] in 1879.]]
The department was the first Peruvian territory to be affected by the [[War of the Pacific]], as the [[Chilean Navy]] began a blockade of the port of [[Iquique]] soon after war was declared between both countries. The blockade was ultimately unsuccessful, however, and served as a prelude to a ground action that would eventually lead to the [[Chilean occupation of Peru|military occupation of key areas in the country]].

An [[Battle of Pisagua|amphibious assault]] on the port city of [[Pisagua, Chile|Pisagua]] that took place on November 2, 1879, marked the beginning of the [[Chilean Army]]'s occupation of the department. The military action that would later become known as the [[Tarapacá campaign]] concluded with the [[battle of Tarapacá]]. Despite a military victory by the [[Peruvian Army]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=Noviembre de 1879: Victoria y Heroísmo en Tarapaca |url=http://www.ccffaa.mil.pe/NotaActual.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100528012541/http://www.ccffaa.mil.pe/NotaActual.php |archive-date=2010-05-28 |website=[[Joint Command of the Armed Forces of Peru|Comando Conjunto de las Fuerzas Armadas]]}}</ref> a strategic withdrawal was deemed necessary, after which the department was occupied by the [[Chilean Army]] by the end of the year.

Following the Chilean victory in the conflict, the department was formally ceded to Chile on October 20, 1883, through the [[Treaty of Ancón]]. The territory was then formally integrated into Chile on October 31, 1884, through the creation of [[Tarapacá Department]].<ref>{{Cite book |title=Migraciones. Una mirada interdisciplinaria |last1=López Pérez |first1=Felipe |publisher=Universidad Católica de la Santísima Concepción |year=2013 |isbn=978-9563533170 |pages=17 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=pr42AwAAQBAJ |last2=Jara Román |first2=Marcelo}}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Territorio ocupado en Tarapacá por el ejército chileno (1879).jpg|thumb|right|1879 map of Chile's [[Tarapacá campaign|land campaign]].]]
The department had an area of about 69,000 km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name=Porras>{{Cite book|title=Historia de los Límites del Perú|last=Porras Barrenechea|first=Raúl|publisher=Librería Francesa y Casa Editorial E. Rosay|year=1926|location=Lima |page=86}}</ref>

=== Boundaries ===
The department was limited to the north by the [[Arica Province (Peru)|province of Arica]] (within the [[department of Tacna]]), by [[Bolivia]] to the east and south, and by the [[Pacific Ocean]] to the west. It was located in its entirety in the [[Atacama Desert]], originally between the [[Lluta River|Lluta]] and [[Loa River|Loa]] rivers. Most of the territory belonged to a plain and drainage basin known as the [[Pampa del Tamarugal]].<ref name=UrbinaUribe/>

=== Climate ===
The department was located in the [[Atacama Desert]], a region with a [[Desert climate#Cold desert climates|cold desert climate]] and almost no precipitation.

== Politics ==
The [[Regions of Peru|department]] was administered by a departmental council ({{langx|es|consejo departamental}}). It was administered by the [[Catholic Church in Peru]] as part of the [[Archdiocese of Arequipa|diocese of Arequipa]] until 1880, when it was replaced ''de facto'' by the Chilean [[Diocese of Iquique|Apostolic vicariate of Tarapacá]].

== Demographics ==
Peruvian people constituted a minority in the region as both [[Chilean people|Chileans]] and [[Bolivians]] were more numerous.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Vergara |first1=Jorge Iván |last2=Gundermann |first2=Hans |year=2012 |title=Constitution and internal dynamics of the regional identitary in Tarapacá and Los Lagos, Chile |journal=[[Chungara (journal)|Chungara]] |language=Spanish |publisher=[[University of Tarapacá]] |volume=44 |issue=1 |pages=115–134 }}</ref> According to Peruvian historian [[Raúl Porras Barrenechea]], its population included 17,013 Peruvians and 9,664 Chileans.<ref name=Porras/>

== Economy ==
The department was central to the [[Peruvian nitrate monopoly]], established in 1875 by the Peruvian government to capitalize on the world market's high demand for nitrates. It was operated by the ''Peruvian Nitrate Company''.<ref>David P. Werlich, ''Peru: A Short History'' (Southern Illinois University Press, 1978), p. 101.</ref>

== Culture ==
From 1880 to 1883, the provinces of Iquique and Tarapacá were subject to a process of [[Chilenization of Tacna, Arica and Tarapacá|forced acculturation]], which was concurrent with an [[Anti-Chilean sentiment|anti-Chilean]] campaign carried out by its Peruvian colony.

== Transport ==
The department was serviced by {{ill|Patillos railway|es|Ferrocarril de Patillos|lt=a railway line}} established in 1872, which operated six steam locomotives and connected the port of Patillos, a port town 60 km to the south of Iquique, and today part of its commune. Danish engineer {{ill|Holger Birkedal|es}}, who would later cooperate with Chile during the war, participated in the railway's construction.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Servicio secreto Chileno: En la guerra del Pacífico |last=Parvex |first=Guillermo |publisher=Ediciones B |year=2017 |isbn=978-9569977114 |pages=135 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=dTA_DwAAQBAJ}}</ref>
