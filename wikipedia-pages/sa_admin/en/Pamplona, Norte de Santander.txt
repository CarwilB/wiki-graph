'''Pamplona''' (pronounced {{IPA|es|pamˈplona|}}) is a [[municipality]] and city in [[Norte de Santander]], [[Colombia]]. It is the fifth most populated city and the sixth most populated municipality in the [[Departments of Colombia|department]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021 |title=La información del DANE en la toma de decisiones regionales - Cúcuta, Norte de Santander |url=https://www.dane.gov.co/files/investigaciones/planes-departamentos-ciudades/210319-InfoDane-Cucuta-Norte-de-Santander.pdf |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=[[National Administrative Department of Statistics (Colombia)|Colombian National Administrative Department of Statistics]] |page=91 |language=es}}</ref>

==History==
===Colonization===
The town was founded on 1 November 1549 as '''Nueva Pamplona del Valle del Espíritu Santo''', named after [[Pamplona|the capital of the]] [[Kingdom of Navarre]], [[Crown of Castile]], by [[Pedro de Ursúa]] and Ortún Velasco de Velázquez. From there, the expeditions departed which founded the towns of Mérida, San Cristóbal and La Grita, in the Republic of Venezuela, and Ocaña, Salazar de las Palmas, Chinácota, [[San Faustino]], [[Bucaramanga]] and [[Cúcuta]] in Colombia, among others.

The natives, called {{Lang|es|Chitareros}} by the Spanish, were the first inhabitants of the old [[Province of Pamplona]]. They received the name because of the men had a custom of carrying a {{Lang|es|calabazo}} or {{Lang|es|totumo}} (gourd) hanging from the waist, with ''[[chicha]]'' or maize wine as the Spaniards called it. Asked for the name of what they were carrying, the natives responded that it was a {{Lang|es|chitarero}}.

When the area was occupied by Pedro de Ursúa and Ortún Velasco in 1549, they reduced the primitive settlers to the regime of {{Lang|es|encomiendas}}.{{Clarify|date=August 2022}} Around 100 groups or {{Lang|es|capitanejos}} were distributed among 53 ''{{Lang|es|encomiendas}}'' throughout the territory, according to researcher Jaramillo Uribe.

The town's location allowed it to become an important commercial route between the Viceroyalty of [[Viceroyalty of New Granada|New Granada]] and the Captaincy of [[Venezuela]]; with highly fertile regions and [[auriferous]] deposits in the mountains, it became one of the richest territories of the colony, rivaled only by the province of Socorro, which contributed to it being considered a political and administrative axis of the Spanish crown from the time of the conquest.

===Independence===
Pamplona earned the nickname of "Patriotic City" as described by [[Simón Bolivar]] for having pioneered the New Granadan revolution by proclaiming its independence on 4 July 1810, led by Doña {{Ill|Águeda Gallardo|lt=Agueda Gallardo de Villamizar|es|Águeda Gallardo}} (freedom that was finally declared 31 July of the same year with a provisional assembly), and later, between 1819 and 1821, for having contributed notably with human and economic resources for the liberation of Colombia and Venezuela. Pamplona was as important as Bogotá.

In 1910, with the creation of the [[Department of Norte de Santander]], it was included within its political jurisdiction, becoming part of the province of Pamplona, which is in turn made up of the municipalities of [[Cácota|Cácota de Velasco]], [[Cucutilla]], [[Chitagá]], [[Labateca]], [[Mutiscua]], [[Pamplonita]], [[Toledo, Norte de Santander|Toledo]] and [[Silos, Norte de Santander|Silos]]. Culturally there are a number of national and international activities making the city a tourist epicenter, including Holy Week, along with the celebrations held in [[Santa Cruz de Mompox|Mompox]] and [[Popayán]], which are the most important in the country.

The city of Pamplona is in the southwest of the department, along with the municipalities of Pamplonita, Chitagá, Silos, Cácota and Mutiscua. Its historic center stands out in the urban area (declared as a Monument of National Interest according to Decree 264 of 1963), which has long been the main educational center of eastern Colombia and [[Táchira]] and [[Mérida, Mérida|Mérida]] in Venezuela. The solemnity of religious celebrations is also notable – events that attract a large number of people from other parts of the country and Táchira, Venezuela.

==Economy==
One of the main agricultural products of Pamplona is the potato,<ref>{{Cite book |last=B |first=Mariano Olazábal |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=k_IqAAAAYAAJ&dq=Economia+Pamplona+Norte+de+Santander&pg=PT32 |title=Organización de un centro de acopio para la comercialización de la papa en la ciudad de Pamplona |date=1980 |publisher=IICA Biblioteca Venezuela |language=es}}</ref> followed by the production of other vegetables and fruits such as strawberries, garlic, carrots, wheat, {{Lang|es|morón}},{{Clarify|reason=what is this in English?|date=August 2022}} beans and corn.<ref name=":1" /> During 2014, Pamplona became the municipality that produced the most peas in the department.<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Guerrero |first1=Carlos Andrés Gualdrón |last2=Gómez |first2=Bryan Arley Maldonado |last3=Velandia |first3=Darvis René Espitia |last4=Serrano |first4=Johan Nicolás García |date=2017 |title=APROXIMACIÓN AL CASO DE DESARROLLO LOCAL DE LA ZONA RURAL DEL MUNICIPIO DE PAMPLONA |url=https://ojs.unipamplona.edu.co/ojsviceinves/index.php/face/article/view/543 |journal=FACE: Revista de la Facultad de Ciencias Económicas y Empresariales |volume=17 |issue=2 |pages=142–156 |doi=10.24054/01204211.v2.n2.2017.543 |doi-broken-date=12 July 2025 |issn=2500-9338 |accessdate=2022-08-06}}</ref> The main sources of livestock production in the municipality are poultry, cattle and swine.<ref name=":1" /> The province of Pamplona is one of the largest food production areas in the department, it also produces dairy products, especially cheese, the main cheeses marketed in the area are [[Cuajada]], in addition to Queso Campesino ({{Lit|Farmer's Cheese|i=yes|lk=yes}}) and Queso de Hoja ({{Lit|Leaf Cheese|i=yes|lk=yes}}).<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Contreras |first1=Y. Albarracín |last2=Sarmiento |first2=P. |last3=Camacho |first3=A. K. Carrascal |last4=Mercado |first4=M. |date=2006 |title=Estimación de la proporción de Listeria monocytogenes y Salmonella spp en quesos frescos (queso de hoja, cuajada) y queso Doble Crema producidos y comercializados en el Municipio de Pamplona, Norte de Santander |url=https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=2483184 |journal=Bistua: Revista de la Facultad de Ciencias Básicas |volume=4 |issue=2 |pages=30–41 |issn=0120-4211 |quote=La provincia de Pamplona, es una de las mayores productoras de productos alimenticios, siendo el área de lácteos una de las más posicionadas, específicamente la producción de quesos.}}</ref> Eighty-five percent of the city's establishments are engaged in commerce.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=Diagnóstico Económico - Pamplona |url=https://www.unipamplona.edu.co/unipamplona/portalIG/home_174/recursos/pamplona/26042015/diagnostico_economico.jsp |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=[[University of Pamplona]]}}</ref>

Pamplona is a [[college town|student city]], hosting the [[University of Pamplona]]. That and tourism are probably the main present economic activities, where thousands of students are lodged and fed and are thus the main consumers in nightclubs and cafés.

== Heritage sites ==

=== Churches ===
According to historian Silvano Pabón Villamizar, the construction of religious buildings in Pamplona began at the end of the 16th century and the beginning of the 17th century.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sandoval |first=Héctor |date=2020-04-09 |title=Las historias de la 'ciudad de la neblina': Pamplona |url=https://www.elespectador.com/el-magazin-cultural/las-historias-de-la-ciudad-de-la-neblina-pamplona-article-309887/ |access-date=2022-08-05 |website=[[El Espectador]] |language=spanish}}</ref> The {{Ill|Santa Clara Cathedral|lt=|es|Catedral de Pamplona (Colombia)}} was built in 1584 by Magdalena Velazco, the daughter of the founder of Pamplona.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date= |title=Catedral de Santa Ana |url=https://servicios.unipamplona.edu.co/ictp/hermesoft/portalIG/home_1/recursos/2019/06052019/pag_catedralsantaclara.jsp |access-date=2022-08-05 |website=[[University of Pamplona]] |language=es}}</ref> The cathedral contains one of the most important musical instruments of Eastern Colombia, a pipe organ created by Celestino Balbiani from Milan, Italy,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Piano de la catedral de Pamplona, joya italiana |url=https://www.laopinion.com.co/piano-de-la-catedral-de-pamplona-joya-italiana |access-date=2022-08-05 |website=La Opinión |language=es-co}}</ref> in addition to a [[Baroque]]-style altarpiece.<ref name=":0" /> In 1990, architect Jaime Salcedo was awarded the Carlos Arbelaéz Camacho National Restoration Prize for the restoration of the Caterdal at the 12th Biennial of Architecture in Colombia.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Laverde |first=Camilo Mendoza |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=UvpGx_JO0JcC&dq=XII+Bienal+de+Arquitectura+Catedral+de+Pamplona+Norte&pg=PA31 |title=50 años de arquitectura: apuntes para la historia de la Facultad de Arquitectura y Diseño, 1951-2000 |date=2001 |publisher=Pontificia Universidad Javeriana |isbn=978-958-683-387-5 |language=es}}</ref>

One of the most visited places in Pamplona during Holy Week is the Humilladero Sanctuary.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-04-08 |title=Semana Santa: Turistas disfrutarán de gastronomía y paisajes de Norte de Santander |url=https://www.rcnradio.com/colombia/santanderes/semana-santa-turistas-disfrutaran-de-gastronomia-y-paisajes-de-norte-de |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=RCN Radio |language=es}}</ref> It is an example of colonial architecture, featuring a preserved wood carving of Jesus Christ along with [[Impenitent thief|Gestas]] and [[Dismas]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-04-15 |title=Semana Santa en Colombia: Cuatro emblemáticas celebraciones |url=https://es.aleteia.org/2019/04/15/semana-santa-en-colombia-cuatro-emblematicas-celebraciones/ |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=[[Aleteia]] |language=es}}</ref> The sanctuary has existed since the foundation of Pamplona.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Guía departamental del Norte de Santander (CITUR) |url=https://www.citur.gov.co/upload/publications/documentos/51.Guias_departamentales_Norte_de_Santander.pdf |access-date=2022-08-05 |website=Centro de Información Turística}}</ref>

=== Museums ===
Pamplona houses several museums of the department such as the Águeda Gallardo de Villamizar House, built around 1644 after an earthquake. In the house lived {{Ill|Águeda Gallardo|lt=Águeda Gallardo|es|Águeda Gallardo}}, one of the women who played a role in the independence of Colombia.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Museo Casa Águeda Gallardo de Villamizar |url=http://simco.museoscolombianos.gov.co/Home/Museo?personaJuridicaId=422 |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=Sistema de Información de Museos Colombianos (SIMCO) |language=es}}</ref> According to the historians Luis Eduardo Paez Courvel and Luis Febres Cordero, the house was the site for gatherings (Spanish: {{Lang|es|tertulias}}) on the process of independence for the country.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Reseña Historica de la Casa Águeda |url=https://www.unipamplona.edu.co/unipamplona/portalIG/home_37/recursos/01_general/18012012/pag_historia.jsp |access-date=2022-08-06 |website=[[University of Pamplona]] |language=es}}</ref>
