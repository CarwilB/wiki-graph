'''Ourinhos''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[Interior of São Paulo|interior]] of the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]]. Located west of the [[São Paulo|state capital]], it is approximately 370&nbsp;km away. Covering an [[area]] of {{cvt|296|km2}}, its population according to the [[2022 Brazilian census]] by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) was {{formatnum:103970}}, making it the [[List of municipalities in São Paulo|76th most populous]] in São Paulo. It is 994&nbsp;km from [[Brasília]], the federal capital.

The municipality was emancipated from [[Salto Grande, São Paulo|Salto Grande]] in the 1910s, and its name refers to the former municipality of Ourinho, now [[Jacarezinho, Paraná|Jacarezinho]], in the state of [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]]. Currently, it consists of the city of Ourinhos, with its seat being its only [[Administrative divisions of Brazil|district]], further subdivided into approximately 120 neighborhoods.<ref name="IBGE_História"/> It is also one of the main cities in the region, with [[commerce]] as its primary economic activity.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In [[agriculture]], the cultivation of [[sugarcane]], [[soybean]], and [[maize]] stands out.<ref name="agricultura"/>

The municipality also boasts a rich cultural tradition, encompassing [[handicrafts]], [[theater]], [[music]], and [[sports]]. The city is notable for events often organized by the Ourinhos city hall, sometimes in partnership with local businesses. One of the main events is the Ourinhos Agricultural and Industrial Fair, held annually in June and considered one of the largest events of its kind in the country.<ref name="Prefeitura_FAPI"/>

== History ==
=== Before emancipation ===
Until the late 19th century, the area that is now the municipality of Ourinhos was a pristine forest, inhabited by the [[Kaingang|Kaingang Indians]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.portalkaingang.org/index_povo_1.htm |title=Portal Kaingang |publisher=www.portalkaingang.org }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://pib.socioambiental.org/pt/povo/kaingang/287 |title=Kaingang - Povos Indígenas no Brasil |publisher=pib.socioambiental.org }}</ref> During this period, monocultures of [[coffee]] and [[cotton]] reached the hinterlands along the [[Paranapanema River]], accompanied by the onset of [[Italian immigration to Brazil|Italian immigration]], which rapidly populated the area. This led Jacinto Ferreira de Sá, originally from [[Santa Cruz do Rio Pardo]], to acquire a vast tract of land from Escolástica Melchert da Fonseca. He subdivided the central portion and donated land for the construction of a school, an administrative headquarters, and a Methodist church.<ref name="CityBrazil_História">{{cite web|url=http://citybrazil.uol.com.br/sp/ourinhos/historia-da-cidade |title=City History |author=CityBrazil |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

In 1906, a small settlement with a limited number of houses began to form. In 1908, a railway post was established, which, four years later, was transformed into a [[railway station]] belonging to the Sorocaba Railway. The station served as a transfer point for passengers heading to the neighboring settlement of Ourinhos (now [[Jacarezinho, Paraná|Jacarezinho]], [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]]). From this time onward, the future city’s development was driven by the fertility of its lands and its advantageous geographical position, making it a strategic economic location due to its connection with northern Paraná and its position between [[Assis]] and [[Avaré, São Paulo|Avaré]], key cities in the Paranapanema Valley.<ref name="Câmara_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.camara-ourinhos.com.br/historia.asp |title=History of Ourinhos |author=Municipal Council |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref> The small settlement became a district under Salto Grande de Paranapanema in 1915. Three years later, it was elevated to the status of [[municipality]] on 13 December 1918, with its official establishment on 20 March 1919.<ref name="Prefeitura_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/conteudo/mostrar/id/756/titulo/Hist%C3%B3ria |title=History |author=City Hall |access-date=25 November 2010 |archive-date=12 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100912094337/http://www.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/conteudo/mostrar/id/756/titulo/Hist%C3%B3ria |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Administrative formation and etymology ===
It was elevated to a [[district]] under the name Ourinhos by State Law No. 1484 on 13 December 1915, belonging to the municipality of [[Salto Grande, São Paulo|Salto Grande]]. It was then elevated to municipality status under the same name by Law No. 1618 on 13 December 1918, separated from Salto Grande. It consisted of the seat district, with its establishment occurring on 20 March 1919. By State Decree-Law No. 9073 of 31 March 1938, the Municipality of Ourinhos was part of the sub-district of Salto Grande, within the Salto Grande judicial district. Under the framework established by State Decree No. 9775 of 30 November of the same year, it became the sole sub-district of the Ourinhos judicial district.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

The name "Ourinhos" was used even before its political emancipation. A 1908 map shows a city named Ourinho (singular) in [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]], where [[Jacarezinho, Paraná|Jacarezinho]] now stands. In fact, the Paraná Ourinho was also called Nova Alcântara, chosen by its founder, Antônio Alcântara da Fonseca, a miner who settled there in 1888. State Law No. 352 of 2 April 1900 established that Nova Alcântara (or Ourinho) and the Jacarezinho police district be incorporated into the Jacarezinho sub-district, with the appointment of a judge and deputy prosecutor. Law No. 525 of 9 March 1904 created the Jacarezinho judicial district, effectively dissolving the Paraná Ourinho, though maps continued to use the old name for some time. The Sorocaba Railway formalized the São Paulo Ourinhos, which inherited the name through oral tradition. Thus, Ourinhos was already referred to by this name regionally, as Ourinho was sometimes called Nova Alcântara, causing confusion among residents who often referred to the current Ourinhos even before its official designation. Like many Brazilian cities, Ourinhos was already a city in practice before its official political emancipation.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

=== After emancipation ===
[[File:Correio de Ourinhos 1917.jpg|thumb|left|210px|Image of one of the first publications of the ''Correio de Ourinhos''.]]

With the resumption of the railway and [[deforestation]], fertile lands facilitated [[coffee]] cultivation for export, which was highly profitable for landowners at the time. Concurrently, merchants and professionals from various regions arrived, contributing to the municipality’s growth and development. During this period, immigrants from Japan, Italy, and other countries were attracted to Ourinhos, primarily to cultivate lands along the railway. Another railway, the São Paulo-Paraná Railway, built in 1922, connected São Paulo and Paraná, making Ourinhos a significant economic hub and junction.<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

The city’s urban development required improvements in urban infrastructure. In the 1910s, the first newspaper was published.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ourinhos.blogspot.com/2009/11/o-correio-de-ourinhos.html |title="O Correio de Ourinhos" |author=Memórias Ourinhenses |date=16 November 2009 |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref> The Ourinhos Water and Sewage Superintendency (SAE) was established by Law No. 808 on 13 April 1967, during the administration of Mayor Domingos Camerlingo Caló.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ourinhos.blogspot.com/2010/11/sae.html |title=The SAE |author=Memórias Ourinhenses |date=7 November 2010 |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

Due to the growth of Ourinhos and neighboring cities, the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Ourinhos Microregion]] was created, encompassing Ourinhos and 18 other municipalities, including [[Santa Cruz do Rio Pardo]], [[Piraju]], [[Fartura (municipality)|Fartura]], [[Ipaussu]], [[Chavantes]], [[Taguaí]], [[Bernardino de Campos]], [[Manduri]], [[Salto Grande, São Paulo|Salto Grande]], [[São Pedro do Turvo]], [[Tejupá]], and [[Ribeirão do Sul]]. In 2006, its population was estimated by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] at approximately {{formatnum:294902}} inhabitants across a total area of 5,568.472&nbsp;km². Its average [[Human Development Index|HDI]] was 0.792, and the average [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]] per capita was R$9,501.74 in 2003. It is located in the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Assis Mesoregion]].<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

The Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Ourinhos is considered high by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP), with a value of 0.778 (the 145th highest in Brazil). The city has most indicators close to the national average according to the UNDP. The education index is 0.727, the longevity index is 0.859, and the income index is 0.753.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

=== Poverty and inequality ===
According to the IBGE, in 2003, the [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.46, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="GINI" /> The incidence of poverty, as measured by the IBGE, was 14.92%, with the lower poverty threshold at 14.56%, the upper at 14.92%, and subjective poverty at 18.12%.<ref name="GINI">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=353470&nomemun=Ourinhos |title=Social indicators of Brazilian municipalities |author=IBGE |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref>

According to IBGE data published by the [[Ministry of Social Development (Brazil)|Ministry of Social Development]], Ourinhos has {{formatnum:1256}} families below the poverty line, or {{formatnum:3768}} people (3.65% of the population). In March 2013, the municipality had {{formatnum:2426}} families enrolled in the [[Bolsa Família]] Program, representing 65.89% of the estimated total families eligible for the program (65.89% coverage). Of the Bolsa Família beneficiaries, {{formatnum:6986}} people lived in families with a per capita income below R$70.00 and would be in extreme poverty without the program’s benefits.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://aplicacoes.mds.gov.br/sagi/RIv3/geral/index.php |title=Relatório de Informações - RI v.4 |publisher=aplicacoes.mds.gov.br }}</ref>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Catedral Senhor Bom Jesus REFON 2.jpg|thumb|150px|Cathedral of Senhor Bom Jesus, seat of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Ourinhos|Diocese of Ourinhos]].]]
Reflecting the cultural diversity in Ourinhos, the city is home to a variety of religious traditions. While it developed on a predominantly [[Catholicism|Catholic]] social foundation, dozens of different [[Protestantism|Protestant]] denominations can now be found. The growth of evangelicals has also been notable, reaching nearly 26.00% of the population.<ref name="Religião"/>

According to the 2010 census conducted by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, the population of Ourinhos consists of: [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (67.57%), [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (25.56%), [[Irreligion|people with no religion]] (3.76%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (1.02%), and 2.16% divided among other religions.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web |url=http://www.mai.org.br/tabelas/municipios/municipioslista.php?a=search&value=1&SearchField=AnyField&SearchOption=Equals&SearchFor=Ourinhos |title=Religion |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref>

Ourinhos is located within the Paraná River hydrographic basin, at an average [[altitude]] of 492 meters, with several sub-basins of small and medium streams playing significant roles in its configuration. Its main rivers are the [[Paranapanema River|Paranapanema]], [[Pardo River (Rio Grande tributary)|Pardo]], and [[Turvo River (Grande River tributary)|Turvo]], all of which flow through the city’s urban perimeter. The topography is slightly rugged but predominantly flat.<ref name="geo_BV">{{cite web|url=http://geo.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/geopro/index.php/perfil-do-municipio |title=Welcome to the maps of Ourinhos |author=Ourinhos Geoprocessing Management |access-date=24 November 2010}}</ref>
[[File:Rio Paranapanema e zona rural de Ourinhos SP.jpg|thumb|Forested areas amidst [[pasture]] in the [[rural area]]]]
The native [[vegetation]] of the municipality belongs to the [[Atlantic Forest]] domain, featuring a variety of [[fauna]] and [[flora]] species. One of the city’s main environmental reserves is the Biologist Tânia Mara Netto Silva Ecological Park, preserving the last stretch of native Atlantic Forest in the municipality, covering about 122,000 m² of wildlife and native plants.

This park also plays a significant role in municipal tourism, hosting activities such as trail walks, meditation, and various sports. To preserve local fauna and flora and improve air quality in various neighborhoods, tree-planting initiatives are carried out across the municipality, using native species such as [[Schinus terebinthifolia|aroeira]], ''myrcia oblongata'', [[Handroanthus|ipê]], and [[Eugenia uniflora|pitanga]].<ref>{{cite web |author=City Hall |date=11 November 2010 |title=Environment and Agriculture Department conducts new tree seedling planting initiative |url=https://www.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/portal/noticias/0/3/3620/secretaria-de-meio-ambiente-realizou-o-plantio-de-mudas-de-arvores-no-corrego-furnas |access-date=24 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Climate ===
[[File:Pôr-do-sol em Ourinhos SP.jpg|thumb|[[Sunset]] in Ourinhos]]
According to a study by the [[State University of Campinas]] (UNICAMP), Ourinhos has a [[tropical savanna climate]] (type ''Am'' in the [[Köppen climate classification]]),<ref name="Unicamp_Am">{{cite web |url=http://www.cpa.unicamp.br/imagens/classkoeppensp2.gif |title=Köppen Climate Classification of São Paulo State |author=[[State University of Campinas]] (Unicamp) |publisher=Center for Meteorological and Climatic Research Applied to Agriculture (CEPAGRI) |access-date=24 November 2010}}</ref> with an average temperature above 18&nbsp;°C in the coldest month and [[precipitation]] below 60 millimeters (mm) in the driest month. During the summer months, [[Convective rainfall|convective rains]] are frequent, helping to mitigate significant thermal and humidity contrasts compared to days without precipitation.<ref name="prope.unesp_clima.urbano">{{cite web |author=SOUZA |first=Débora Moreira de |last2=NERY |first2=Jonas Teixeira |date=2009 |title=Urban Climate in Ourinhos |url=http://prope.unesp.br/xxi_cic/27_22781328812.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731195037/http://prope.unesp.br/xxi_cic/27_22781328812.pdf |archive-date=31 July 2013 |access-date=26 November 2010 |publisher=[[São Paulo State University]] (UNESP)}}</ref> The annual pluviometric index is approximately {{formatnum:1300}} mm,<ref name="CIIAGRO-chuva">{{cite web|url=http://www.ciiagro.sp.gov.br/ciiagroonline/Quadros/QChuvaPeriodo.asp|title=Monthly Rainfall|author=Centro Integrado de Informações Agrometeorológicas (CIIAGRO-SP)|access-date=June 8, 2021}}</ref> with a notable decrease during the [[winter]].

According to data from the Integrated Center for Agrometeorological Information (CIIAGRO-SP), since May 2000, the lowest temperature recorded in Ourinhos was {{formatnum:-2.5}} [[Celsius|°C]] in July 2000, on the 16th and 21st. Sub-zero temperatures were also recorded on 28 June 2011 ({{formatnum:-1.8}} °C) and 17 July 2000 ({{formatnum:-1}} °C). The highest temperature reached {{formatnum:42.3}} °C on 7 October 2020. The highest 24-hour [[rainfall]] accumulation was {{formatnum:171.5}} mm on 20 June 201 Odysseys, followed by {{formatnum:120}} mm on 15 December 2002 and {{formatnum:105.2}} mm on 11 January 2009.<ref name="ciiagro.sp.gov.br">{{cite web|url=http://www.ciiagro.sp.gov.br/fotos/ciiagro/DadosDiarios.txt|title=Daily Data|author=CIIAGRO-SP|access-date=June 8, 2021}}</ref>

=== Subdivisions ===
[[File:Foto-ourinhos-2024.jpg|thumb|Aerial view of Ourinhos]]
Ourinhos is officially subdivided into a single district, the Seat, established on 20 March 1919. This subdivision has existed since the city’s political emancipation. By State Decree-Law No. 9073 of 31 March 1938, the Municipality of Ourinhos was part of the Salto Grande sub-district, within the Salto Grande judicial district. Under the framework established by State Decree No. 9775 of 30 November of the same year, it became the sole sub-district of the Ourinhos judicial district.<ref name="IBGE_História">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/saopaulo/ourinhos.pdf |title=History |author=Cities@ - IBGE |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> The city is also divided into approximately 120 [[neighborhoods]].<ref name="GrandeBrasil_Ourinhos">{{cite web|url=http://www.grandebrasil.com.br/Brasil/Cidades/?Municipio_de_Ourinhos,_estado_de_Sao_Paulo_(SP)+9435&Grupo=4 |title=Ourinhos |author=GrandeBrasil.com |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref>

== Politics and administration ==
Municipal administration is carried out by the [[executive power]] and the [[legislative power]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100211142840/http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074 |archive-date=11 February 2010 |title=The Municipal Public Authority and the Constitutional Obligation to Establish an Internal Control System |author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima |date=9 February 2006 |publisher=JusVi |access-date=21 November 2010 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Currently, Lucas Pocay Alves da Silva, from the [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|Social Democratic Party]] (PSD), is the mayor and representative of the executive, having won the 2016 municipal elections with 62.34% of the vote.

The [[legislative power]] is constituted by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of fifteen councilors elected for four-year terms (in accordance with Article 29 of the [[Constitution of Brazil|Constitution]]<ref name="Constituição">{{cite web |url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101121191055/http://dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm |archive-date=21 November 2010 |author=DJI |title=Federal Constitution - CF - 1988 |access-date=21 November 2010 |url-status=dead }}</ref>) and is composed as follows:<ref name="vereadores">{{cite web |author= |date=7 October 2012 |title=Candidates for Councilor Ourinhos/SP |url=http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-ourinhos-sp/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221183155/http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-ourinhos-sp/ |archive-date=21 February 2014 |access-date=13 February 2014 |work=Eleições 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> two seats for the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB), two seats for the [[Brazilian Labour Party (1981)|Brazilian Labour Party]] (PTB), two seats for the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB), two seats for the [[Workers' Party (Brazil)|Workers' Party]] (PT), one seat for the [[Christian Democracy (Brazil)|Christian Social Democratic Party]] (PSDC), one seat for the [[Republicans (Brazil)|Brazilian Republican Party]] (PRB), and one for the [[Liberal Party (Brazil, 2006)|Party of the Republic]] (PR).<ref name="vereadores"/> The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law).<ref name="Constituição"/>

The municipality is governed by an organic law<ref name="Orgânica">{{cite web|url=http://www.camaraourinhos.sp.gov.br/download.php?a=Q01PdXJpbmhvcyNMZWlfT3JnYW5pY2FfT3VyaW5ob3MucGRmI2E= |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/6NM9b2Zk7 |archive-date=13 February 2014 |title=Organic Law of the Municipality of Ourinhos |author=Municipal Chamber |access-date=13 February 2014}}</ref> and is the seat of the Ourinhos Judicial District.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jusbrasil.com.br/topicos/1278591/comarca-de-ourinhos-2a-vara-criminal |title=Ourinhos Judicial District 2nd Criminal Court |author=JusBrasil |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> It had {{formatnum:76057}} voters in June 2012, representing 0.241% of the total in the state of São Paulo.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/consulta-quantitativo |title=Quantitative Consultation |date=12 April 2013 |author=[[Superior Electoral Court]] (TSE) |access-date=13 February 2014 |archive-date=10 June 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140610023817/http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/consulta-quantitativo |url-status=dead }}</ref>

== Infrastructure ==
In 2000, Ourinhos had {{formatnum:26536}} [[housing units]] including [[apartments]], [[houses]], and rooms. Of these, 18,308 were owned properties, with {{formatnum:14311}} fully paid (53.93%), 3,997 under acquisition (15.06%), 4,989 [[Renting|rented]] (18.80%); 3,158 properties were provided, with 1,202 by employers (4.53%) and 1,956 provided otherwise (7.37%). 81 were occupied in other ways (0.31%).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100135393 |title=Infrastructure - Permanent Private Households and Residents |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> The municipality has treated water, [[electricity]], [[sewage]], urban cleaning, [[fixed-line telephone|fixed telephony]], and [[mobile telephony]]. In 2000, 96.83% of households were served by the [[Water supply network|general water supply network]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp?iIdMun=100135393 |title=Infrastructure - Water Supply |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> 95.84% of homes had waste collection;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp?iIdMun=100135393 |title=Infrastructure - Waste Destination |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> and 94.19% of residences had sanitary drainage.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp?iIdMun=100135393 |title=Infrastructure - Sanitary Drainage |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Healthcare ===
In 2005, the municipality had 63 health facilities, 42 private and 21 public, including hospitals, [[emergency departments]], [[health centers]], and [[dentistry|dental services]]. The city has 357 hospitalization beds, all private.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=353470&nomemun=Ourinhos |title=Health Services 2009 |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=21 November 2010}}</ref> There are three general hospitals, two private and one [[philanthropic]]. Ourinhos also has 311 nursing assistants, 187 dental surgeons, 168 general practitioners, 71 nurses, and 1,012 in other categories, totaling 1,837 health professionals.<ref name="Datasus_SaúdePP">{{cite web |author=DATASUS |title=Health Information Notebook - General Information |url=ftp://ftp.datasus.gov.br/caderno/geral/sp/SP_Ourinhos_Geral.xls |access-date=21 October 2010 |format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]]}}</ref> In 2007, the [[birth rate]] was 13.11%, and 6.76% of babies were born underweight. Cesarean births accounted for 55.74% of all births, and 6.81% of these births were to mothers aged 10 to 18.<ref name="Prefeitura_DadosGerais">{{cite web |url=http://www.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/conteudo/mostrar/id/758/titulo/Dados+Gerais |title=General Data |author=City Hall |access-date=21 November 2010 |archive-date=9 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100909181050/http://www.ourinhos.sp.gov.br/conteudo/mostrar/id/758/titulo/Dados+Gerais |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Services under the Municipal Health Department include: the Central Sterilization Unit; the Central Medicine Dispensary; the Specialty Drug Dispensary (High-Cost Medications); the Mental Health and HIV/AIDS Medicine Dispensary; the Municipal Compounding Pharmacy; and Health Service Waste Management.<ref name="Prefeitura_DadosGerais"/> Hospital services affiliated with the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|Unified Health System]] (SUS) include the Ourinhos Holy House of Mercy and the Ourinhos Mental Health Hospital.<ref name="Prefeitura_DadosGerais"/> The city also has four municipal [[ambulances]], two private [[intensive care unit|mobile ICU]] ambulances, two basic transport ambulances ([[Santa Casa da Misericórdia|Holy House of Mercy]]/Mental Health Hospital), and one rescue unit from the São Paulo Military Police Fire Department.<ref name="Prefeitura_DadosGerais"/>

Ourinhos has schools in all regions of the municipality. Due to extensive urbanization, the few [[rural area]] residents have easy access to schools in nearby urban neighborhoods. Education in state schools is slightly superior to municipal schools, but the city hall is conducting studies to improve public municipal education to achieve better results in the Basic Education Development Index.<ref name="IDEB_Índice">{{cite web|url=http://ideb.inep.gov.br/Site/ |title=Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) - Results and Goals |author=Federal Government |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> In 2008, the municipality had approximately 21,815 enrollments, 971 teachers, and 87 schools in public and private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/>

The city hall, through its Education Department in partnership with various public and private entities, promotes activities and programs to enhance educational quality, such as the Literacy Teacher Training Program (PROFA/CENP); Math Literacy Program ([[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|MEC]]/UNDIME); Elementary Education Reference Center (CREF)/[[São Paulo State University]] (UNESP); Read and Write (CENP); National Fiscal Education Program; Distance Training for School Managers – PROGESTÃO (UNDIME and CONSED, with cooperation from the Roberto Marinho Foundation); Training Course for Managers and Educators, Inclusive Education: Right to Diversity (MEC/Ourinhos SME); Drug Abuse Resistance Education (PROERD) in partnership with the [[São Paulo Military Police]]; Specialized Educational Care Training Course in partnership with MEC/[[Federal University of Ceará]] (UFCe); School Managers Program, Postgraduate Program in School Management, in partnership with MEC/[[Federal University of São Carlos]] (UFSCAR); and Robotics Education Training ([[Lego Education|LEGO Education]]).<ref name="Portal_Educação"/> Ourinhos's education system is considered noteworthy.<ref name="Portal_Educação">{{cite web|url=http://www.ourinhos.net/NoticiaDetalhe.aspx?id=195 |title=Ourinhos Education Highlighted in Specialized Magazine |author=Portal Ourinhos.NET |date=20 May 2009 |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Services and communications ===
The sewage collection and water supply services for the entire city are managed by the Ourinhos Water and Sewage Superintendency (Sae-Ourinhos). Much of the water consumed in the municipality comes from the [[Pardo River (Rio Grande tributary)|Pardo River]] and small underground reservoirs and springs.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sae-ourinhos.com.br/aguaour.htm |title=History |author=Ourinhos Water and Sewage Superintendency |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> 100% of the city is served by the electricity distribution network, supplied by the [[Companhia Paulista de Força e Luz]] (CPFL), headquartered in [[Campinas]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodeourinhos.com.br/noticia.asp?codnot=471 |title=CPFL Atende opens modern call center in Ourinhos |author=Diário de Ourinhos |date=8 July 2009 |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref>

The automatic telephone system was introduced in the city in 1964 by the Ourinhos Telephone Company. The [[Direct distance dialing|direct distance dialing (DDD) system]] was implemented in 1978 by [[Telesp|Telecomunicações de São Paulo]] (TELESP) with the area code (0143).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imprensaoficial.com.br/DO/BuscaDO2001Documento_11_4.aspx?link=/1979/ineditoriais/abril/04/pag_0009_B3S4RNA09GBHIe0IVGF0T0IBUUT.pdf&pagina=9&data=04/04/1979&caderno=Ineditoriais&paginaordenacao=100009|title=Telesp - 1978 Annual Report|publisher=D.O.E. São Paulo}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm|title=Telesp's operational area in São Paulo|date=1998-01-14|access-date=2025-05-23|website=www.telesp.com.br|publisher=Official page of Telecomunicações de São Paulo (archived)|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19980114175120/http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm|archive-date=1998-01-14|url-status=dead}}</ref> In the 1990s, the city’s DDD code was changed to (014) to standardize the telephone system with the mobile telephony being implemented across the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/ddd_pref/ddd_pref.htm|title=Telesp - DDD Code and Prefixes|date=1998-01-14|access-date=2025-05-23|website=www.telesp.com.br|publisher=Official page of Telecomunicações de São Paulo (archived) |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19980114162505/http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/ddd_pref/ddd_pref.htm |archive-date=14 January 1998 }}</ref>

Mobile telephone services are provided by various operators. [[3G]] access has been available in the municipality since 2009.<ref name="Portal_3G">{{cite web|url=http://www.ourinhos.net/NoticiaDetalhe.aspx?id=192 |title=Brazil may receive computer-cell phone in the second half |author=Portal Ourinhos.NET |date=18 May 2009 |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> The area code ([[Direct distance dialing|DDD]]) for Ourinhos is 014,<ref name="Guiatel_DDD">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatel.com.br/info_sp1.htm |title=São Paulo |author=Guiatel |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> and the [[postal code]] (CEP) ranges from 19900-000 to 19919-999.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correios.com.br/|title=Postal codes of Brazilian cities|author=Correios|access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> On 1 September 2008, the municipality began to be served by [[Local number portability|number portability]], along with other cities in [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]] (codes 14 and 17), [[Espírito Santo]] (27), [[Minas Gerais]] (37), [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]] (43), [[Goiás]] (62), [[Mato Grosso do Sul]] (67), and [[Piauí]] (86).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.estadao.com.br/economia/not_eco232943,0.htm |title=Portability will have a fee of R$4 per customer, says Anatel |author=Leonardo Goy |date=29 August 2008 |publisher=[[O Estado de S. Paulo]] |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> There are also [[Dial-up Internet access|dial-up internet]], [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]), [[wireless internet]], and [[fiber optic]] services, offered by various free and paid [[Internet service providers]].

The municipality also has various newspapers. The main ones are Jornal de Ourinhos, Jornal da Divisa, and Diário de Ourinhos. There are also [[radio stations]], including Rádio de Ourinhos, Divisa FM 93.3, Itaipu FM 92.5,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nossosaopaulo.com.br/Reg_04/Reg04_Ourinhos.htm |title=Additional information |author=Nossa São Paulo |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref> Rádio Clube de Ourinhos, and Rádio Sentinela de Ourinhos.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radios.com.br/rela_am4.htm |title=List of AM radios in Brazil |author=Rádios |access-date=22 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===
[[File:Ourinhos 270609 REFON 13.JPG|thumb|Ourinhos railway station, part of the Sorocaba Railway, deactivated since 1999.<ref name="EF_OurinhosSP"/>]]

Ourinhos is served by the Trunk Line of the former Sorocaba Railway, currently concessioned to [[Rumo Logística]], with the Ourinhos Railway Station, inaugurated in late 1908. However, the railway and station ceased passenger services on 16 January 1999, remaining active only for tourist trips on a [[steam locomotive]] between Rubião Júnior (a district of [[Botucatu]]) and [[Presidente Epitácio]].<ref name="EF_OurinhosSP">{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/o/ourinhos.htm |title=Line history |author=Railway Stations of Brazil |date=23 July 2010 |access-date=23 November 2010}}</ref> Ourinhos also has a railway junction between the Sorocaba line and the Cianorte Branch of the former São Paulo-Paraná Railway Company, also under Rumo’s concession. Passenger transport on this latter railway ceased in 1981, with only freight trains remaining.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/o/ourinhos.htm |title=Ourinhos -- Railway Stations of São Paulo State |access-date=2020-10-03 |website=www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br}}</ref> The municipality is crossed by the Raposo Tavares Highway (SP-270), connecting São Paulo to the border with [[Mato Grosso do Sul]]; [[SP-278 (São Paulo highway)|SP-278]] - Mello Peixoto State Highway - linking Ourinhos to nearby cities; [[SP-327 (São Paulo highway)|SP-327]] - Orlando Quagliato Highway - connecting the city to [[Santa Cruz do Rio Pardo]]; [[BR-153 (Brazil highway)|BR-153]] - Transbrasiliana Highway - linking the municipality to various cities across the country (a highway stretching from [[Aceguá, Brazil|Aceguá]], [[Rio Grande do Sul]], to [[Marabá, Pará|Marabá]], [[Pará]]). Additionally, it has access to regionally and nationally significant highways through paved, double-lane secondary roads. The city also has the [[Ourinhos Airport|Ourinhos State Airport]] {{Airport codes|OUS|SDOU}}, with a capacity for about 3,000 passengers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.emsampa.com.br/wardest_ourinhos.htm |title=Ourinhos Airport |author=Emsampa |access-date=23 November 2010}}</ref>

The municipal fleet in 2018 consisted of 75,639 vehicles, including 39,027 cars, 2,250 trucks, 2,357 pickups, 652 buses, 16,762 motorcycles, and 5,351 mopeds.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=frota&codmun=353470&nomemun=Ourinhos |title=Fleet 2009 |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=23 November 2010}}</ref> Between 2001 and 2010, a 78.5% increase in the number of vehicles was recorded, considered concerning by the municipal Traffic and Transport Coordination. [[Dual carriageway|Divided]] and paved [[Avenue (landscape)|avenues]] and numerous [[traffic lights]] facilitate city [[traffic]], but the growth in vehicle numbers over the past decade has led to increasingly slow traffic, especially in the municipal seat. Additionally, finding parking spaces in the commercial center has become challenging, causing some losses to [[commerce]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tanacidade.com/?noticia=10989 |title=In nine years, the vehicle fleet grew 78.5% in Ourinhos SP |author=TaNaCidade.com |date=3 March 2010 |access-date=23 November 2010}}</ref>

Public transportation in Ourinhos is currently operated by Auto Viação Ourinhos Assis (Avoa), which has been responsible for the service since 1978. There are 22 urban lines available, with a fare of R$2.50, and discounts for students and people aged 60 to 65.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/debate/1274/cidade/cidade18.htm |title=In Ourinhos, Avoa admits decline and blames mototaxis |author=Uol |access-date=23 November 2010}}</ref>

== Culture and leisure ==
=== Arts and crafts ===
Within the domain of [[performing arts]], several structures are dedicated to municipal culture, such as the Miguel Cury Municipal Theater, the Historical and Pedagogical Municipal Museum, the Popular Art Center, and the 'Para Ler o Mundo' Cultural Center, part of the Tristão de Athayde Municipal Library.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://curtaourinhos.blogspot.com/p/equipamentos-da-cultura.html |title=Cultural Facilities |author=Curta Ourinhos |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

[[Handicraft]]s are one of the most prominent forms of cultural expression in Ourinhos. Throughout the municipality, unique artisanal production is found, made with regional [[raw materials]] and created according to local culture and lifestyle. The Superintendence of Handicraft Work in Communities (SUTACO) brings together various regional artisans, providing space for production, exhibition, and sale of artisanal products. Items such as crocheted quilts and table runners, flowers made from dried corn husks, and pieces produced on looms are commonly made. These items are typically sold at fairs, exhibitions, or shops specializing in handicrafts.<ref name="SUTACO_Diploma">{{cite web|url=http://www.sutaco.com.br/noticias/06/09_12.html |title=Sutaco certifies artisans in Ourinhos |author=Superintendency of Community Artisanal Work (SUTACO) |date=2006 |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Events ===
To promote local socioeconomic development, the Ourinhos city government, sometimes in collaboration with local companies, invests in the organization of fests and events. These celebrations often attract visitors from other cities, necessitating improved infrastructure in the municipality and fostering the professionalization of the sector, which benefits not only tourists but also the entire local population. These activities take place throughout the year.<ref name="Prefeitura_FAPI"/>

The Ourinhos Agricultural and Industrial Fair is one of the main events held in the municipality. Since its inception in 1967, it has been held annually in May or June, drawing visitors from the surrounding region. It is also recognized as one of the largest fairs in the country, offering free admission. The event is currently hosted at the Olavo Ferreira de Sá Exhibition Grounds. In recent years, each fair has featured an average of 260 exhibitors from the commerce and industry sectors and 350 from the livestock sector, with the latter including over 1,600 animals. During the eleven-day event, free artistic performances draw thousands of people to the fairgrounds.<ref name="Prefeitura_FAPI">{{cite web |author=Prefeitura |date=15 April 2010 |title=44ª FAPI - FEIRA AGROPECUÁRIA E INDUSTRIAL DE OURINHOS - De 03 a 13 de junho |url=https://www.agrolink.com.br/eventos/44--fapi---feira-agropecuaria-e-industrial-de-ourinhos |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

Another significant event is the [[music festival]] held annually in the city during the month of July, which has gained prominence for featuring musicians from across the country, elevating the city's reputation both nationally and internationally. The event includes [[workshop]]s led by renowned musicians, contributing to the development of the city's cultural scene. Notable artists such as [[Toninho Horta]], Nélson Ayres, and others have participated in the event as guest performers or workshop instructors.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ourinhosfestivaldemusica.com.br/2010/principal/ |title=Festival de Música de Ourinhos - 10 anos de Sucesso! |author=Festival de Música de Ourinhos |access-date=25 November 2010 |archive-date=11 October 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101011001435/http://www.ourinhosfestivaldemusica.com.br/2010/principal |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Sports ===
As in most Brazilian cities, the most popular and widely practiced sport in the municipality is [[association football|football]]. The sport has been played in Ourinhos since 1908, during the construction of the small railway station on land owned by Dona Escolástica, when workers, during their free time, played football matches in the area now known as Melo Peixoto Square. On 5 June 1919, a group of residents founded the Clube Atlético Ourinhense. On 27 June 1920, the working-class community established the now-defunct Esporte Clube Operário. With the establishment of the city government, its employees also founded another football club, Municipal A.C. Another club, Aurora F.C., emerged in the 1920s, with its field located among the coffee plantations of the former Sá farm. In 1932, Aurora F.C. ceased to exist, and its field was acquired by Operário, which by then also maintained a recreational center.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ourinhos.blogspot.com/2007/11/1908-1941-o-futebol-em-ourinhos-por.html |title=1908 - 1941 - O futebol em Ourinhos |author=Carlos Lopes Bahia |date=19 November 2007 |publisher=Memórias Ourinhenses |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

Ourinhos also stands out nationally in women's [[basketball]], boasting one of the best teams in the world. The team was established in 1995 with support from the municipal government. Subsequently, it received funding and sponsorship from local private companies and other collaborators. From 1995 to August 2009, the team won the national championship five times (2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, and 2008), the São Paulo state championship six times (2000, 2002, 2004, 2005, 2006, and 2007), and the South American Club Championship (2008), among other titles. The municipality is known as the "''National Capital of Women's Basketball''" and serves as a hub for the national basketball team.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://pbf.blogspot.com/ |title=Painel do Basquete Feminino (PBF) |author=PBF |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>

=== Holidays ===
Ourinhos observes two municipal [[public holiday|holidays]], eight national holidays, and three optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the day of the patron saint [[Good Lord Jesus]], on 6 August, and the city's anniversary, on 13 December.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ourinhos.net/NoticiaDetalhe.aspx?id=12 |title=Excesso de feriados em 2009 preocupa comércio |author=Portal Ourinhos.NET |date=1 December 2009 |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref> According to Federal Law No. 9,093 of 12 September 1995, municipalities may have a maximum of four municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm|title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref><ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaLei">{{cite web|url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/Leis/L9093.htm|title=Lei Nº 9,093, de 12 de setembro de 1995 |author=Presidência da República |access-date=25 November 2010}}</ref>
