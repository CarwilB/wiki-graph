'''Comerío''' ({{IPA|es|komeˈɾi.o}}) is a [[Comerío barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]] in the center-eastern region of island, north of [[Aibonito, Puerto Rico|Aibonito]]; south of [[Naranjito, Puerto Rico|Naranjito]] and [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]]; east of [[Barranquitas, Puerto Rico|Barranquitas]]; and west of [[Cidra, Puerto Rico|Cidra]] and [[Aguas Buenas, Puerto Rico|Aguas Buenas]]. Comerío is spread over 7 barrios and [[Comerío barrio-pueblo|Comerío Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

==History==
[[File:Comerio to Barranquitas Road, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Comerío to Barranquitas Road c. 1900–1917|left]]
Comerío was founded on June 12, 1826. It was first named '''Sabana del Palmar''', but later was renamed ''Comerío''.<ref name="PREncyclopedia">{{cite web |title=Comerío Municipality Founding and History |url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/comerio-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14 |website=enciclopediapr.org |access-date=February 14, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214022820/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/comerio-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14 |archive-date=February 14, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Memoria41">{{cite book |title=Mapa de Municipios y Barrios, Comerio, Memoria Núm. 41 |date=1955 |publisher=Estado Libre Asociado de Puerto Rico, Oficina del Gobernador,  Junta de Planificación |page=6 |url=https://rcm1.rcm.upr.edu/demografia/wp-content/uploads/sites/30/2020/04/Memoria_41_Comerio-1955.pdf |access-date=24 October 2025|archive-date=April 9, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230409084950/https://demografia.rcm.upr.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/35/2020/04/Memoria_41_Comerio-1955.pdf }}</ref>

In 1854, nearly 1000 acres (1000 {{lang|es|[[cuerda]]s}}) of land were being used for agriculture. In 1894, there was one sugar and two coffee [[plantations]] ([[Hacienda|estates]]) in Comerío.<ref name="Puerto Rico. Office of Historian 1949">{{cite book | author=Puerto Rico. Office of Historian | title=Tesauro de datos historicos: indice compendioso de la literatura histórica de Puerto Rico, incluyendo algunos datos inéditos, periodísticos y cartográficos | publisher=Impr. del Gobierno de Puerto Rico | issue=v. 2 | year=1949 | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA191 | language=es | access-date=January 4, 2020 | page=191 | archive-date=August 22, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200822190014/https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA191 | url-status=live }}</ref> In the early 20th century, Comerio residents, had a short but legendary territory war with residents of the city of [[Barranquitas]].

Don [[José Ramón Figueroa]], a coffee planter, merchant and businessman who played a major role in the economic and agricultural development of the region, was from Comerío. 

Puerto Rico was ceded by Spain in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the United States. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a census of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Comerío was 8,249.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=cZ9QAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA161|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=161| language=es}}</ref>

[[Hurricane Maria]] on September 20, 2017, triggered numerous landslides in Comerío.<ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides map">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref> The flooded river went through the center of Comerío. A week later the hospital, trying to run via generator, was out of diesel but due to the landslides, travel and deliveries, into and out of Comerío was next to impossible.<ref>{{cite web | last=Ortiz Rivera | first=Joel | title=Las cosas siguen igual en Comerío | website=El Nuevo Día | date=September 27, 2017 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/notas/las-cosas-siguen-igual-en-comerio/ | language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref> Over 4,000 homes were affected, and of those 1,537 completely destroyed.<ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. María embraveció al río La Plata y Comerío revivió una pesadilla | trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget. María infuriated the La Plata River and Comerío relived a nightmare | newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=June 13, 2019 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/comerio/| language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref> After the hurricane, the [[Ohio National Guard|National Guard from Ohio]] were tasked with restoring water purification systems for the people of Comerío. Two months after the hurricane, the mayor of Comerío said no personnel had made it yet to attempt the restoration of electrical power to Comerío.<ref>{{cite web | last=Delgado | first=José A. | title=Alcalde de Comerío alega que es lenta la recuperación tras María | website=El Nuevo Día | date=November 29, 2017 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/notas/alcalde-de-comerio-alega-que-es-lenta-la-recuperacion-tras-maria/ | language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref> On October 9, another landslide happened blocking transit on PR-167.<ref>{{cite web | title=Reportan deslizamiento de tierra en carretera que conduce a Comerío | website=El Nuevo Día | date=October 9, 2017 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/notas/reportan-deslizamiento-de-tierra-en-carretera-que-conduce-a-comerio/ | language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref> On October 10, a group of doctors from New York announced they would travel to volunteer their services to Comerío and nearby mountain municipalities.<ref>{{cite web | last=Ruiz Kuilan | first=Gloria | title=Organización de médicos de Nueva York ofrece servicios a la montaña | website=El Nuevo Día | date=October 10, 2017 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/notas/organizacion-de-medicos-de-nueva-york-ofrece-servicios-a-la-montana/ | language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref>

===Special Communities===
{{See also|Puerto Rico Office for Socioeconomic and Community Development}}
{{lang|es|Comunidades Especiales de Puerto Rico}} (Special Communities of Puerto Rico) are marginalized communities whose citizens are experiencing a certain amount of [[social exclusion]]. A map shows these communities occur in nearly every municipality of the commonwealth. ==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Comerío celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in August. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales Santo Cristo de la Salud}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Enc_Places" />

Other festivals and events celebrated in Comerío include:
*Spring Carnival – April 
*La Mora Cave Festival – June 1,2,3
*Jíbaro Festival – June 
*El Jobo Festival – July 
*[[Andrew the Apostle|San Andrés Apóstol]] Festivities – November 
*El Seco Marathon – December 
*Christmas Fantasy – December

===Religion===
First [[Methodism|Methodist]] Church of Comerío was founded in 1904.

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a mayor, elected every four years. The current mayor of Comerío is [[Irvin Rivera]], of the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] (PPD). He was elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2024|2024 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VI]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Rafael Santos Ortiz]] and [[Wilmer Reyes Berríos]] were elected as District Senators.<ref name="k172">{{cite web | title=Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General| website=elecciones2024.ceepur.org | url=https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Guayama_VI.xml | access-date=October 24, 2025}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The Comerío coat of arms is made up quarters (4) bearing what is known as an [[Escutcheon (heraldry)|escutcheon]] of displays a shield in the center. There's a green field with a golden cross in the left quarter which represents [[Jesus|Holy Christ the Healer]], the patron of the town. Green stripes on a silver field are on the lower left quarter, symbolizing the Comerío falls and the [[Río de la Plata (Puerto Rico)|La Plata River]]. Three [[Roystonea regia|royal palms]] in the right quarter to remember the original name of the town, and an undulating line symbolizes the mist that often covers the town. A crown with [[Taino]] [[Motif (visual arts)|motifs]] represents the cacique. The tobacco plant is a reminder of what was once an important part of the economic activity of the municipality. Inscribed with "the pearl of La Plata" a reference is made to the location of Comerío, on the banks of the La Plata River and the turreted castle which is a symbol of municipalities of Puerto Rico.<ref name="PREncyclopedia" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

==Transportation==
Public bus from [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]] to Comerío at Centro de Estacionamiento de Bayamón is the main public transportation.

There are 16 bridges in Comerío.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Comerio Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/comerio/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=February 19, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190220181346/http://bridgereports.com/pr/comerio/|archive-date=February 20, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>
