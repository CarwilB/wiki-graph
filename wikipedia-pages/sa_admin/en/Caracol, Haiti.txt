'''Caracol''' ({{IPA|fr|kaʁakɔl}}; {{langx|ht|Karakòl}}) is a [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] in the [[Trou-du-Nord Arrondissement]], in the [[Nord-Est (department)|Nord-Est]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Haiti]]. It has 6,236 inhabitants.

==Caracol Industrial Park==

===Background===
[[File:President Clinton and Secretary Clinton Pose for a Photo With Workers at Caracol Industrial Park (8122239533).jpg|thumb|President Clinton and Secretary Clinton pose for a photo with workers at Caracol Industrial Park.|left]]Prior to the 2010 earthquake, [[Bill Clinton]] was named special envoy to Haiti by UN Secretary-General [[Ban Ki-moon]], whose connections with the [[South Korea|South Korean]] company [[Sae-A Trading Co. Ltd]] were later put to work in the planning of the park.<ref name="Johnston">{{cite web|last1=Johnston|first1=Jake|date=January 16, 2014|title=Outsourcing Haiti: How disaster relief became a disaster of its own|url=http://www.bostonreview.net/world/jake-johnston-haiti-earthquake-aid-caracol|access-date=September 22, 2016|website=Boston Review}}</ref> On May 24, 2010, the Haiti Economic Lift Program (HELP) was signed into US law, ensuring preferential tariffs for Haitian-produced garments.<ref>{{cite web|title=HR 5160: Haiti Economic Lift Program|url=https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/111/hr5160/summary|access-date=14 October 2016|website=govtrack}}</ref>  On October 22, 2012 [[Hillary Clinton]] gave the keynote speech as acting US Secretary of State for the opening of the industrial park.<ref name="Johnston" /><ref>{{cite web|author1=Hillary Clinton|date=October 22, 2012|title=Remarks at the Caracol Industrial Park Opening Ceremony|url=https://2009-2017.state.gov/secretary/20092013clinton/rm/2012/10/199451.htm|access-date=September 23, 2016|website=US Department of State}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|author1=Susana Ferreira|author2=Andrew Quinn|date=22 October 2012|title=Clintons preside at star-studded opening of Haitian industrial park|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-haiti-clinton-caracol-idUSBRE89M00L20121023|access-date=14 October 2016|website=Reuters}}</ref>

==== Projections ====
The anchor tenant is S & H Global, S.A, a subsidiary of Sae-A Trading Co. Ltd., a global [[Clothing technology#Production|clothing manufacturer]] headquartered in South Korea.<ref name="MHSS">{{cite news|title=New industrial park in Haiti|newspaper=The Miami Herald|format=Slide 4 of slideshow|url=http://www.miamiherald.com/2012/06/04/2832927/new-industrial-park-in-haiti.html|access-date=July 6, 2012}}</ref><ref name="MH060412">{{cite news|author=Jacqueline Charles|date=June 4, 2012|title=New industrial park in northern Haiti sparks controversy|newspaper=The Miami Herald|url=https://www.miamiherald.com/latest-news/article1940373.html|access-date=August 24, 2019}}</ref> It began operations in the fall of 2012; a work force of 20,000 was projected for that year.<ref name="Sontag">{{cite news|author=Deborah Sontag|date=July 5, 2012|title=Earthquake Relief Where Haiti Wasn't Broken|newspaper=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/07/06/world/americas/earthquake-relief-where-haiti-wasnt-broken.html|access-date=August 24, 2019}}</ref> The eventual workforce was projected to approach 60,000 by 2017 and ultimately 100,000 workers.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Katz|first=Jonathan M.|title=The King and Queen of Haiti|url=https://www.politico.com/magazine/story/2015/05/clinton-foundation-haiti-117368/|url-status=live|access-date=2021-05-28|website=[[POLITICO Magazine]]|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150904003923/http://www.politico.com/magazine/story/2015/05/clinton-foundation-haiti-117368|archive-date=2015-09-04}}</ref> This would result in an estimated tenfold expansion of population in the area to about 300,000 from its present 30,000. Social and environmental disruption was anticipated as the result of this hastily planned project.<ref name="MH060412" /><ref name="Sontag" />

==== Construction ====
In 2012 the Caracol Industrial Park was built on a square mile, 600 acre, 252 hectare site near Caracol over a former [[labor camp]] used by [[United States Marines]] during the [[United States occupation of Haiti]].<ref name=":0" /> The facility has some of the best constructed infrastructure in Haiti.<ref name=":0" /> The $300 million project, which was to include a 10-megawatt power plant, road, a water-treatment plant, worker housing in neighboring communities, and development of a port in nearby [[Fort-Liberté#Fort-Libert.C3.A9 Bay|Fort-Liberté]], was built with hurricane relief funds, a loan from the [[Inter-American Development Bank]],<ref name="IDB112811">{{cite web|title=Haiti and its partners lay the foundation stone for the Caracol Industrial Park|url=http://www.iadb.org/en/news/news-releases/2011-11-28/caracol-industrial-park-in-haiti,9724.html|publisher=Inter-American Development Bank|access-date=July 6, 2012|format=Press release|date=November 28, 2011|archive-date=April 3, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120403022631/http://www.iadb.org/en/news/news-releases/2011-11-28/caracol-industrial-park-in-haiti,9724.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> contributions by the United States government, and the [[Clinton Foundation]].<ref name="KQ_Haiti">{{cite web|last1=Katz|first1=Jonathan|title=The King and Queen of Haiti|url=https://www.politico.com/magazine/story/2015/05/clinton-foundation-haiti-117368/|website=Politico|access-date=September 21, 2016|date=May 4, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|last1=Katz|first1=Jonathan|title=The Big Truck That Went By: How the World Came to Save Haiti and Left Behind a Disaster|date=2013|publisher=St. Martin's Press|isbn=978-0-230-34187-6}}</ref>  As of 2013, 1.7 of the projected 10 megawatts were being supplied by the power plant.<ref name="GenderAction">{{cite web|url=http://www.genderaction.org/publications/caracol.pdf|title=Caracol Industrial Park Social and Gender Impacts of Year One of Haiti's newest IFI - funded Industrial Park|author1=Claire Lauterbach|author2=Elaine Zuckerman|date=2013|website=GenderAction|publisher=Gender Action|pages=18, 35|access-date=November 13, 2018}}</ref>

== Communal Sections ==
The commune consists of two [[communal section]]s, namely:
* [[Champin (communal section)|Champin]], urban and rural, containing the town of Caracol
* [[Glaudine]] or "Jacquesil", rural
