'''Puerto Inca''' is the largest of eleven [[Provinces of Peru|provinces]] of the [[Huánuco Region]] in [[Peru]].<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Nalvarte |first=W. |last2=Sabogal |first2=C. |last3=Galvan |first3=O. |last4=Marmillod |first4=D. |last5=Angulo |first5=W. |last6=Cordova |first6=N. |last7=Colan |first7=V. |date=2004 |title=Silvicultura en la Amazonia Peruana: diagnostico de experiencias en la region ucanyali y la provincia de Puerto Inca |url=https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=Silvicultura+en+la+Amazonia+Peruana%3A+diagnostico+de+experiencias+en+la+++region+ucanyali+y+la+provincia+de+Puerto+Inca&author=Nalvarte%2C+W.&publication_year=2004 |language=Spanish}}</ref> The capital of this province is the city of [[Puerto Inca]].

==Boundaries==
*'''North''': [[Ucayali Region]]
*'''East''': [[Ucayali Region]]
*'''South''': [[Pasco Region]]
*'''West''': [[Leoncio Prado Province]]

==Languages==
According to the [[Peru 2007 Census|2007 census]], [[Spanish language|Spanish]] was spoken by 89.5% of the population as their first language, while 5.5% spoke [[Quechua language|Quechua]], 1.9% spoke [[Asháninka language|Asháninka]], 0.3% spoke [[Aymara language|Aymara]], 2.6% spoke other indigenous languages and 0.0% spoke foreign languages. 

In the early 20th century, a number of Japanese migrated to the area and established the Kudo and Taba settlements for rice and coffee cultivation.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Irie |first=Toraji |last2=Himel |first2=William |date=1952 |title=History of Japanese Migration to Peru, Part III (Conclusion) |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/2508910 |journal=The Hispanic American Historical Review |volume=32 |issue=1 |pages=73–82 |doi=10.2307/2508910 |issn=0018-2168|url-access=subscription }}</ref>
