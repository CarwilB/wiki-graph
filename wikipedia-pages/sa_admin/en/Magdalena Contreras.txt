'''La Magdalena Contreras''' ({{IPA|es|maɣðaˈlena konˈtɾeɾas|-|MagdalenaContreras.ogg}}) is a [[Boroughs of Mexico City|borough]] ({{lang|es|demarcación territorial}}) in [[Mexico City]]. As of the 2010 census, it has a population of 239,086 inhabitants and is the third-least populous of Mexico City's boroughs. It lies at an elevation of {{convert|2365|m|ft|0|abbr=on}} above sea level.<ref>[http://mapserver.inegi.org.mx/mgn2k/?s=geo&c=1223 2010 census tables: INEGI] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130502025339/http://mapserver.inegi.org.mx/mgn2k/?s=geo&c=1223 |date=2013-05-02 }}</ref> It is named after two historically important communities—La Magdalena Atlitic and Colonia Contreras. The northern end of the borough is urbanized. The rest of Magdalena Contreras, with its mountains and ravines, is designated as a conservation zone. However, urban sprawl has put pressure on these conservation areas. In an effort to preserve the area's forests and natural resources, the borough government has started promoting [[ecotourism]].

==History==
{{more citations needed section|date=October 2018}}
The borough takes its name from the La Magdalena Atlitic, one of four communities with pre-Hispanic roots, and the Contreras neighborhood, an area noted for its [[textile mill]]s until the 20th century.<ref name=mexicocity>{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicocity.gob.mx/contenido.php?cat=30900&sub=10 |title=Magdalena Contreras |publisher=Secretaria de Turismo, Distrito Federal |location=Mexico City |language=es |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130814200114/http://mexicocity.gob.mx/contenido.php?cat=30900&sub=10 |archive-date=2013-08-14 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Human settlements in the area date to between {{nowrap|c. 500 – c. 200 BCE}}, or the latter [[Mesoamerican chronology#Preclassic Era or Formative Period|Preclassic period]]. These settlements were mostly located in the Contreras area and were under the control of [[Cuicuilco]] .<ref name=mchistoria>{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/historia |title=Historia |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=History |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130715071933/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/historia |archive-date=2013-07-15 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref name=encmuc>{{cite web |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM09DF/delegaciones/09008a.html |title=La Magdalena Contreras |publisher=INAFED |location=Mexico |work=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México Distrito Federal |year=2010 |language=es |access-date=July 10, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140504055128/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM09DF/delegaciones/09008a.html |archive-date=May 4, 2014 }}</ref> After Cuicuilco was destroyed by an eruption of the [[Xitle]] volcano, inhabitants fled to higher elevations. The higher elevations were inhabited by the [[Otomi people|Otomi]] or the [[Chichimeca]] who were hunter-gatherers that lived in a stateless society. These peoples existed alongside the [[Nahuas|Nahuatlaca]]. The [[Tepanec]] eventually came to dominate the area, making it part of the Coyoacán dominion. With the rise of the [[Aztec]], the Tepanec were conquered and the area was renamed Atlitic—stone that drinks water—after a large rock which rose above a small lake.<ref name=mchistoria/> Four settlements date from the pre-Hispanic period, San Bernabé Ocotepec, San Nicolás Totolapan, La Magdalena Atlitic and [[San Jerónimo Lídice|San Jeronimo Aculco]].<ref name=encmuc/>

After the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire]], the [[Dominican Order|Dominicans]] established a town and church dedicated to [[Mary Magdalene]] and changed the name of the area to Magdalena (de) Atlitic. The first to take advantage of the waters of the Río Magdalena, Jerónimo de León established the first water-powered saw mill in 1543.<ref name=mchistoria/> In the 17th century the Contreras family established a factory in the location that bears their name.<ref name=barriomagico>{{cite web |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/santa-maria-magdalena-atlitic.html |title= Santa María Magdalena Atlitic |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine | location=Mexico City  |language=es  |access-date=July 10, 2013 }}</ref> At the end of the 18th century, a group of Spanish industrialists founded a textile factory which caused an increase in the economy and the population of the area.<ref name=mchistoria/> By the end of the 19th century, the river was powering four textile factories named El Águila Mexicana, Tizapán, Santa Teresa, and Loreto.<ref name=mchistoria/> These factories produced wool, cotton and [[Cashmere wool|cashmere]] thread, and cloth of various types and by the end of the century had the use of the Mexico City–[[Cuernavaca]] rail line for shipping.<ref name=barriomagico/> However, from the colonial period through most of the 19th century, the area was mostly populated by farm and textile laborers who lived in poor conditions.<ref name=encmuc/>

The modern borough was a result of several reorganizations of the Federal District of Mexico City at the beginning of the 20th century. Initially the area was governed by a municipality system, but then by presidential decree in 1928 the municipality was abolished and it became one of the boroughs of Mexico City.<ref name=mcgeo>{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/geografia |title=Geografía |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Geography |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130715073214/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/geografia |archive-date=2013-07-15 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

At the beginning of the 20th century, there was a political power struggle as part of the [[Mexican Revolution]]. The "Club Democrático Antireelectionista Vicente Guerrero" formed in 1911 to fight against the power of local strongmen.<ref name=encmuc/>

The Mexico City–Cuernavaca rail line continued to operate through most of the 20th century with train "501" noted for carrying famous passengers between the two destinations. The line was officially shut down in 1997. The Contreras station was converted into a park.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=barriomagico/>

The latter 20th century is marked by population growth, especially in the borough's north end. In 1963, an extension of the [[Anillo Periférico]] [[ring road]] was constructed through the borough. The road brought people and prompted the development of housing projects in the 1970s for the city's growing population. These developments include Santa Teresa, Pedregal II, Pueblo Nuevo, Potrerillo, El Rosal, El Tanque, and El Toro, heading west from the roadway.<ref name=mchistoria/>

In 1900, the area was completely rural, with a population of only 8,150 people mostly located in the town of La Magdalena. The borough's population began growing quickly in the 1940s to over 40,000 in 1960, but by 1990 the population had reached over 195,000. Population growth continues but at a slower pace: 222,000 by 2000 and 229,000 in 2005.<ref name="encmuc" /><ref name="mcdemo">{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/demografia |title=Demografía |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Demographics |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130715072104/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/demografia |archive-date=2013-07-15 |url-status=dead }}</ref> There is limited space for housing projects due to the geography of the area but population growth has prompted [[Squatting|illegal settlement]]s in conservation areas and in steep ravines where landslides are a danger.<ref name="mchistoria" /><ref name="encmuc" />

==Geography==
{{more citations needed section|date=October 2018}}
[[File:Dinamos 2009.JPG|thumb|Canyon walls in Los Dinamos]]
Magdalena Contreras is located in the southwest area of the Federal District of Mexico City. It is bordered to the north, south, and east by the boroughs of [[Álvaro Obregón, D.F.|Álvaro Obregón]] and [[Tlalpan]] with the [[State of Mexico]] to the west.<ref name=mcgeo/> It has a territory of {{convert|7458.43|ha|sqmi|1|abbr=on}} accounting for 5.1% of the Federal District.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mcgeo/>  It has an average altitude of {{convert|2,510|m|ft|0|abbr=on}} above sea level. The borough is divided into forty seven official neighborhoods called colonias.<ref name=encmuc/> The oldest of these are La Magdalena Contrereas, [[San Jerónimo Lídice|San Jerónimo Aculco]], San Bernabé Ocotepec, and San Nicolás Totolapan.<ref name=mcgeo/>

Magdalena Contreras lies in the southwest area of the [[Valley of Mexico]] along the lower eastern slopes of the [[Sierra de las Cruces]] which is a volcanic mountain range.<ref name=mcgeo/> The geographic features of the borough stretch from foothills to the lower mountains of the volcanic range. The hills and mountains are separated by ravines and canyons.<ref name=mcgeo/> Major elevations include Cerro Panza ({{convert|3,600|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Nezehuiloya ({{convert|3,760|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Pico Acoconetla ({{convert|3,400|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Cerro Palmitas ({{convert|3,700|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Cerro Palmas ({{convert|3,789|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Piedras Encimadas ({{convert|3,200|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), El Aguajito ({{convert|2,350|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Tarumba ({{convert|3,470|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), [[Cerro del Judío]] ({{convert|2,770|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}), Cerro Sasacapa ({{convert|3,250|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}),  and Cerro San Miguel ({{convert|3,630|m|ft|0|disp=sqbr|abbr=on}}) .<ref name=mcgeo/> Principle canyons and ravines which are located in the center of the borough include Tlalpuente, Cainotitas, Atzoma, and Tejocote.<ref name=encmuc/>
[[File:Creek (Los Dinamos).jpg|thumb|Creek in Los Dinamos]]
The ravines and canyons are carved by streams and run off from freshwater springs. Some of these flow year round and others only during the summer rainy season. The most important springs are Pericos, Mal Paso, Apapxtla, Las Ventanas, Cieneguillas, Los Cuervos, and San Miguel. There are two major flows of water—the Río Magdalena and the Río Eslava.<ref name=mcgeo/> The Río Magdalena is the only free flowing river in the Federal District. It begins on the slopes of the Cerro La Palma in the [[Cuajimalpa borough]]. Some of the water is captured at a section of the Los Dinamos park to be treated at a plant but the rest flows down to the floor of the valley.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mcgeo/> The borough has little in the way of structured drainage for wastewater and rainwater. Much of the wastewater flows openly which has contaminated local streams, the Río Magdalena, and the supply of drinking water. The problem is serious enough in the urbanized area to affect the population's health.<ref name=ambiental>{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/ecosistema |title=Problemática Ambiental |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Environmental Problems |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130713122513/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/ecosistema |archive-date=2013-07-13 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Climate===
There are three main climates found in the borough. The urbanized part is temperate and semi-moist. Conservation areas between {{convert|2,900|-|3,500|m|ft|abbr=on}} above sea level are semi-cold and semi-moist, and those areas above {{convert|3,700|m|ft|abbr=on}} are semi-cold and moist. All areas receive most of their rainfall during the summer rainy season particularly in July. This precipitation can often take the form of hail, with fog common during the summer and in November and December. Snow is rare.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mcgeo/>

===Natural resources===
Wildlife and native flora are mostly restricted to the conservation areas of the southern two thirds of the borough.<ref name=encmuc/> The vegetation of this conservation area is an important source for oxygenation in the Valley of Mexico.<ref name=mchistoria/>  Vegetation varies by altitude. Tree species include oaks, [[Quercus agrifolia|holm oak]]s, and pines. Bushes, brush, and other vegetation is mostly deciduous and includes plants from the legume and cactus families. A number of ravines and some of the higher altitudes have species adapted to wetter climes including [[Alnus jorullensis]], [[Salix bonpladiana]], [[Fraxinus uhdei]], [[Buddlei cordata]], [[Pronus capuli]], and [[Taxodium mucronatum]]. In the pre-Hispanic period, fauna was highly varied; however, many species, such as wildcats, wolves, coyotes, anteaters and many others, are no longer found due to human settlement and habitat loss. Smaller mammals such as rabbits and squirrels still remain, especially in the higher elevations. Various types of birds such as hummingbirds, carpenter birds, and more, along with various lizards, snakes, and amphibians remain.<ref name=mcgeo/>

The borough has important ecological and tourist centers but these are facing degradation from [[urban sprawl]].<ref name=mchistoria/><ref name=ambiental/> The area is 73% under conservation with 18% in urban space. An estimated 3.5% of the conservation area has illegal settlements.<ref name=encmuc/>  The illegal settlements are problematic for the borough with most environmental damage occurring in or near them. For environmental and safety reasons, a significant problem is the [[illegal construction]] of residences on the steep slopes of ravines. These illegal settlements also generate a large number of dumps of solid and liquid waste into ravines which pollute the water supply.<ref name=ambiental/> The borough states that there are 106 areas of construction, about 1,000 residences, at high risk of landslides during the rainy season.<ref name=deslaves>{{cite news |url= http://www.eluniversaldf.mx/magdalenacontreras/detectan-106-puntos-de-riesgo-por-deslaves-en-contreras.html  |title= Detectan 106 puntos de riesgo por deslaves en Contreras |newspaper=El Universal | location=Mexico City |date=April 9, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Detect 106 risk areas for landslides in Contreras |access-date=July 10, 2013 }}</ref><ref name=1000viv>{{cite news |url= http://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/component/content/article/47-politicasociedad/17966-lluvias-afectan-1000-viviendas-en-magdalena-contreras.html |title= Lluvias afectan 1,000 viviendas en Magdalena Contreras |publisher=El Financiero | location=Mexico City |language=es |date=June 10, 2013  |trans-title=Rains affect 1,000 residences in Magdalena Contreras |access-date=July 10, 2013 }}</ref> Efforts to combat these problems include relocating those residing in illegal settlements and developing ecotourism and other programs for those that own rural areas of the borough.<ref name=ambiental/>

===Communities===
{{more citations needed section|date=October 2018}}
[[File:IglesiaConcepcionContreras10.JPG|thumb|Concepción church and Casa de Bellas Artes]]
[[File:Church of San Jerónimo Aculco, San Jerónimo Lídice, Magdalena Contreras, Mexico City.JPG|thumb|Church of San Jerónimo Aculco, San Jerónimo Lídice]]
The four oldest communities in the borough are La Magdalena Atlitic, San Nicolás Totolapan, [[San Jerónimo Lídice|San Jerónimo Aculco]], and San Barnabé Ocotepec, all of pre-Hispanic origin. The modern community of La Magdalena Atlitic is centered on a church which was founded in the 16th century as a hermitage.<ref name=barriomagico/><ref name=iglesias>{{cite web|url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/iglesias-representativas |title=Iglesias Representativas |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Representative Churches |access-date=July 10, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130714074216/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/iglesias-representativas |archive-date=July 14, 2013 }}</ref> The current structure dates to 1760. In 1932, it was named a national monument. The church has a [[Baroque architecture|Baroque]] facade with raised patterns, fronted by a stone paved atrium which has a fountain with an image of the [[Archangel Michael]]. The main portal is flanked by [[doric order|Doric]] [[pilaster]]s holding up an arch and a [[cornice]] to the choir window.<ref name=iglesias/> Inside, at the base of the arches over the main altar, there are feather designs unique to Mexico.<ref name=barriomagico/>

The town of San Nicolás Totolapan is centered on the Plaza Cívica Benito Juárez.<ref name=plazascivicas>{{cite web|url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/plazas-civicas |title=Plazas Civicas |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Town squares |access-date=July 10, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130713121112/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/plazas-civicas |archive-date=July 13, 2013 }}</ref> Its church was founded in 1535 over a former pre-Hispanic ceremonial center. It has as simple facade and a Baroque bell tower surrounded by gardens that contains a stone with inscriptions.<ref name=iglesias/> [[San Jerónimo Lídice|San Jerónimo Aculco]] has a plaza called Plaza Cívica Lidice, named after a [[Lidice|Czech town]] that attacked the Nazis in 1942. It contains Rosedal de Paz (Rose Garden of Peace) and mural called {{lang|es|Campos de Luz y Muerte}} (Fields of Light and Death) by Ariosto Otero.<ref name=plazascivicas/> The town of San Jerónimo Aculco dates back to the [[Toltec]] period. Its church was built in the 16th century by the Franciscans and maintains its open chapel and original baptismal font. Its facade is Baroque with sculpted sandstone. Its atrium dates to the 18th century. The San Baranbé Ocotepec church was built in the 16th century as part of evangelization efforts and dedicated to Barnabas. Its main altar is Baroque and there are archeological remains in its atrium.<ref name=iglesias/>

History and population growth have led to other communities being established in the area. In the colonial period up until the 19th century, the main industry was textiles. Textile factories were built in the area now known as Colonia Contreras. The grounds of the former El Águila [[textile mill]] are now the Foro Cultural (Cultural Forum), inaugurated in 1979. Remains of the mill's installations, such as its smokestack, still remain, but it is now a wooded area with an auditorium, gallery, offices, workshops and libraries.<ref name=barriomagico/><ref name=centroscul>{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/centros-culturales |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130713122607/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/centros-culturales |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 13, 2013 |title=Centros culturales |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Cultural centers |access-date=July 10, 2013 }}</ref> Near the Foro is the Purísima Concepción Church. It was founded in the 16th century, but the current structure was built in the 17th by the Contreras family, owners of the El Águila mill. It offered mass to textile workers.<ref name=iglesias/> The area's former rail station, Estación Ferrocarril de Cuernavaca, is now a park. It contains a large mural by Ariosto Otero called {{lang|es|El viaje del siglo veinte}} (Voyage of the 20th Century) that features the "501" train. This train carried famous passengers such as [[Diego Rivera]], [[Tin Tan]], and [[Lilia Prado]].<ref name=barriomagico/>

Although named for the Magdalena and Contreras areas, the borough's main institutions are in Colonial Barranca Seca. These include the civic plaza, the municipal building, and the Centro Cultural La Magdalena Contreras, which is in a former 1940s movie theater .<ref name=iglesias/><ref name=plazascivicas/> Other notable, modern communities include the Unidad Habitacional de Servicios Sociales, which was established in 1960,<ref name=plazascivicas/> Ampliación Lomas de San Bernabé, El Tanque, and La Malinche.<ref name=encmuc/>

==Economy==
{{more citations needed section|date=October 2018}}
The borough is mostly residential in the northern third and mostly rural, agricultural, and conservation area in the south. The major population centers are found in the lower elevations of the central section of the borough.<ref name=mcgeo/>

For both environmental and economic reasons, ecotourism is an important aspect of the borough economy.<ref name=mcecoturismo>{{cite web |url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/ecoturismo |title=Ecoturismo |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Ecotourism |access-date=July 10, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130824054226/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/turismo/ecoturismo |archive-date=2013-08-24 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The borough government has promoted this development over the past twenty years by encouraging major landholders to develop ecotourism parks and other measures.<ref name=encmuc/> It established a tourism corridor mostly focused on the older communities and natural areas.<ref name=contara>{{cite news |title= Contará Magdalena Contreras con un Barrio Mágico  |publisher=NOTIMEX | location=Mexico City  |language=es  |trans-title=Magdalena Contreras will have a Magical Neighborhood|date=July 8, 2011 }}</ref> Magdalena Atlitic was declared a [[Barrios Mágicos of Mexico City|Barrio Magico]] with a "tourism market" located just outside the entrance to Los Dinamos.<ref name=barriomagico/><ref name=convertir>{{cite news |url=http://mexico.diariocritico.com/noticia/leticia-quezada/miguel-torruco/4006791 |title=Magdalena Contreras, se convertirá en un polo turístico a nivel nacional e internacional |publisher=Diario Crítico |location=Mexico City |date=March 26, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Magdalena Contreras, will convert into a national and international tourist attraction |access-date=July 10, 2013 to encourage the area's reputation for ecology, the Feria de la Trucha y la Quesadilla (Trout and [[Quesadilla]] Fair) occurs each year in this borough.<ref name=convertir/><ref name=canada>{{cite The borough has several major ecotourism areas. The largest of these is Los Dinamos Park which is part of the Bosques de la Cañada de Contreras conservation area. It has an area of {{convert|2,429|ha|sqmi|0|abbr=on}} that encompass a series of canyons formed by streams that eventually empty into Río Magdalena. The longest of these canyons is {{convert|12|m|ft|1|abbr=on}}. The park has over {{convert|26|km|mi|1|abbr=on}} of hiking paths which connect with those of San Nicolás Totolapan.The park also contains the remains of several former textile factories which used the rivers for power.<ref name=mcecoturismo/>

The [[ejido]] of San Nicolás Totolapan has two main ecotourism areas, one called ''Parque Ecoturístico'' and the other called ''Valle de Monte Alegre'' with a total area of {{convert|1,700|ha|sqmi|0|abbr=on}}. The ejido is located in the south of the borough, on the northwest slope of the Ajusco Volcano.<ref name=mexicocity/><ref name=mcecoturismo/> The San Bernabé Ocotepec Ecotourism Park is located on the Cajetes and Meyuca mountains next to the community of the same name. It is {{convert|337.1|ha|sqmi|1|abbr=on}} of pine and oyamel fir forest and was established in 1998 to block the advance of urban sprawl. The Mazatepetl Eco-archeological Park is located on the San Bernabé Ocotepec ejido on a large hill called Cerro del Judío. It contains a restored pyramid originally constructed by the Otomi between 1200&nbsp;CE and 1380&nbsp;CE. Mazatepetl is one of oldest stone constructions in the Valley of Mexico.<ref name=mexicocity/><ref name=mcecoturismo/>

==Culture==
Some of Magdalena Contrera's rural character is still preserved in the observances of religious festivals. The Day of the Cross (May 3) is a popular festival in the borough, especially in the La Cruz neighborhood and the Cerro de Judío. During [[Holy Week in Mexico|Holy Week]], the custom of erecting altars in honor of the [[Virgin of Sorrows]] is still observed. There is a traditional passion play depicting the passion and crucifixion of Christ during the week, with the main event on [[Good Friday]] at the Cerro de Judío.<ref name=festivales>{{cite web|url=http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/festividades |title=Festividades |publisher=Borough of Magdalena Contreras |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Festivities |access-date=July 10, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130715071928/http://magdalenacontreras.gob.mx/la-magdalena-contreras/festividades |archive-date=July 15, 2013 }}</ref>  In addition, it is traditional to spend [[Holy Saturday]] at Los Dinamos.<ref name=festivales/>  Holy Week events have drawn up to 80,000 people at Los Dinamos.<ref name=dosvia>{{cite news |title= Escenificarán dos Vía-Crucis en la delegación Magdalena Contreras |publisher=NOTIMEX | location=Mexico City  |language=es  |trans-title=Will have two passion plays in the Magdalena Contreras borough|date=March 28, 2009 }}</ref> In addition, the area has a major annual pilgrimage to [[Chalma, Malinalco|Chalma]] during this week.<ref name=festivales/>

Another major traditional observance is [[Day of the Dead]].<ref name=festivales/>  This holiday has drawn up to 90,000 people to the borough, mostly to the cemeteries of San Bernabé, San Jerónimo and San Francisco.<ref name=90mil>{{cite news |title= Atenderá La Magdalena Contreras a 90 mil personas por Día de Muertos.|publisher=NOTIMEX | location=Mexico City  |language=es  |trans-title=90,000 people will visite Magalena Contreras for Day of the Dead|date=October 19, 2008 }}</ref>
