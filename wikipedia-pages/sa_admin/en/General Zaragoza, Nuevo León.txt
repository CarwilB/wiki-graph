'''General Zaragoza''' is a municipality and town of the northeastern Mexican state of [[Nuevo León]].  It is located in the south-eastern part of the state. In 2023, Zaragoza was designated a [[Pueblos Mágicos|Pueblo Mágico]] by the Mexican government, recognizing its cultural and historical importance.<ref>{{Cite web |title=These are Mexico's newest Magical Towns |url=https://www.travelweekly.com/Mexico-Travel/Insights/Mexico-names-new-Magical-Towns |access-date=2025-09-14 |website=www.travelweekly.com |language=en}}</ref> The town is at {{coord|23|58|25|N|99|46|23|W}}. The municipality has a total area of 508 square miles (1,315&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>) and had a population of 6,282 in 2020.  Most of the population lives in the town of Zaragoza.  The elevation of Zaragoza is 4,520 feet (1,379&nbsp;m).<ref>"Nuevo León" ''Instituto Naciona de Estadistica y Geographia'' http://www3.inegi.org.mx/sistemas/mexicocifras/default.aspx?e=19, accessed 23 Feb 2013</ref>  Zaragoza is bordered by [[Aramberri, Nuevo Leon|Aramberri]], on the south and east by [[Hidalgo, Tamaulipas]], and on the west by [[Doctor Arroyo]].

Zaragoza is named in honor of General [[Ignacio Zaragoza]] who fought against the French invaders at the [[Battle of Puebla]] on May 5, 1862.

==History==

The original inhabitants of the Zaragoza region were nomadic Indian bands the Spanish called Negritos or Bozalos and Janambres. In 1626, a [[Franciscan]] priest, Lorenzo Cantú from the [[convent]] at [[Charcas, San Luis Potosi|Charcas]], followed a group of Indians to the valley of the Rio Blanco where he founded the Misión de San Josè del Rio Blanco near the site of Zaragoza. In 1866, the municipality of Villa de General Zaragoza was created by the government.<ref>''Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de Mexico''

== Geography ==
Zaragoza is at the head of the semi-arid valley of the Rio Blanco (White River), the uppermost part of the [[Soto La Marina River]] system. Some of the highest mountains of the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]] are in the Zaragoza municipality.  Cerro Zaragoza which rises to 11,352 feet (3,449&nbsp;m) is three miles northeast of Zaragoza town.  Cerro Peña Nevada, 11,682 feet (3,562&nbsp;m) is nine miles southwest of Zaragoza town, just across the border in [[Tamaulipas]].<ref>Google Earth</ref>  Pine and Oak forests cloak the mountains.  One to two miles south of town are three waterfalls—15, 20, and 25 meters high (49, 65, and 82 feet high) -- which are tourist attractions.<ref>"El Rio Blanco", http://www.sabinashidalgo.net/articulos/de-solares-y-resolanas/7048-el-rio-blanco, accessed 24 Feb 2013</ref>
=== Climate ===
In a semi-arid zone with precipitation at most locations less than 20 inches (500mm), Zaragoza is well-favored with rainfall with 28.7 inches (730mm) annually. Some of the mountains around Zaragoza receive more precipitation, including snow at higher elevations, than the town and valley.  Zaragoza Este, one mile east of the town, at an elevation of 5,830 feet (1,777&nbsp;m), receives 49.2 inches (1250&nbsp;mm) of precipitation annually.<ref>"Zaragoza-Este, Nuevo León" Weatherbase.  http://weatherbase.com</ref>   Throughout the area most precipitation falls in the summer.  The rainy season usually begins at the end of April and continues until the end of September.  In the region around Zaragoza, August and September are usually the rainiest months. Temperatures are normally mild, although occasional days are very hot and the temperature falls below freezing several times each year.

The climatic classification of Zaragoza is Bsk (Koppen) and Bsak (Trewartha).  Higher elevations near the town are cooler and more humid, some becoming Cw climates.
