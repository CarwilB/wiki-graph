'''San Cosme y Damián''' is a [[Districts of Paraguay|district]] in the [[Itapúa Department]] of [[Paraguay]]. Located 80&nbsp;km west of [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnación]], it is close to places like [[Ayolas, Paraguay|Ayolas]], [[General Artigas]] or [[Isla Apipé]], in Southern Paraguay and Northern [[Argentina]], along the [[Paraná River]], at the [[Argentina–Paraguay border]]. It covers an area of 800&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> and has an estimated population of over 10,000 in 2023.<ref name=":02">{{Cite web |title=San Cosme y Damián – Otro sitio más de Gobernaciones y Municipios del Paraguay |url=https://www.municipios.gov.py/sancosmeydamian/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230331090344/https://www.municipios.gov.py/sancosmeydamian/ |archive-date=March 31, 2023 |access-date=2023-07-03 |language=es}}</ref><ref>Dpto. == History ==
[[File:San_Cosme_y_Damian_-_Iglesia_de_la_Reduccion.jpg|thumb|Mission San Cosme y Damian church]]
The name was given by [[Jesuits]] after [[Saints Cosmas and Damian]], twin brothers from third-century who practiced medicine.<ref name=":02" />

The [[Jesuit missions among the Guaraní|Jesuit ruins]] found in San Cosme y Damián are the remains of some 30 missions, also known as reductions (Spanish: ''reducciones''), found in the [[Río de la Plata]] region. The San Cosme y Damián mission was one of seven missions located in Paraguay; the other missions are found in modern day [[Argentina]] and [[Brazil]]. The Jesuit mission of San Cosme y Damián was founded in 1632 by Father Adriano Formoso. The settlers had to move several times before ultimately establishing themselves upon the north bank of the [[Paraná River]] in 1718.<ref name=":02" /><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Centre |first=UNESCO World Heritage |title=Jesuit Mission of San Cosme y San Damián |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6613/ |access-date=2023-07-06 |website=UNESCO World Heritage Centre |language=en}}</ref>

The ruins of the San Cosme y San Damián mission holds several well-preserved artifacts and archaeological finds. These ruins are the only ones in Paraguay to still have a functional church, which has been continually used to this day as a place of worship and as a community centre. The church also has original wood carvings, a number of them even with their original colouring. Some of these carvings depict [[Jesus]] with indigenous features. The school and priests' rooms are also well-preserved. One can still observe the original painted ceiling and stone ornaments made by the inhabitants. As of April 2022, there remains a side of the mission that has not been excavated.<ref name=":1" />

During the 18th century, Mission San Cosme y San Damián was also the site of an observatory, due to the work of Father Buenaventura Suárez. Born in [[Santa Fe, Argentina]] in 1678, Father Suárez came to San Cosme y Damián in 1703. He stayed and worked there till his death in 1750. With the help of the [[Guaraní people]], he built several astronomical devices such as [[Sundial|sundials]], [[Telescope|telescopes]], [[Quadrant (instrument)|quadrants]], etc. Although rudimentary in nature, these devices were completely functional and accurate and allowed Father Suárez to make many observations. He sent his observations of eclipses, satellites of Jupiter, and the rings of Saturn to European scientists who were intrigued by astronomical data from the [[Southern Hemisphere]]. Some of his findings were even published in European journals.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=de Asúa |first=Miguel |date=December 2004 |title=The publication of the astronomical observations of Buenaventura Suárez SJ (1679-1750) in European scientific journals |url=https://adsabs.harvard.edu/full/2004JAHH....7...81D |journal=Journal of Astronomical History and Heritage |volume=7 |issue=2 |pages=81–84 |doi=10.3724/SP.J.1440-2807.2004.02.03 |bibcode=2004JAHH....7...81D |issn=1440-2807}}</ref> Father Suárez's work culminated in the publication of his book ''Lunario de un Siglo'' (English: ''Lunar Centenar'') in 1743. This was an astronomical calendar of a century, 1740-1840. This book predicted with great accuracy the eclipses that would occur in this time period and also contained geographical data of 70 cities. Nearly all of his instruments have been lost, with only the sundial still remaining in the schoolyard.<ref name=":1" /><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Troche-Boggino |first=A.E. |date=1998 |title=Centre for Astronomy for Paraguay |url=https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/cgi-bin/nph-iarticle_query?1998UNPSA...9..167T&defaultprint=YES&filetype=.pdf |journal=Selected Papers on Space Science Education, Remote Sensing, and Small Satellites. Seminars of the United Nations Programme on Space Applications |volume=9 |pages=167|bibcode=1998UNPSA...9..167T }}</ref>

Other priests who played an important role in the development of San Cosme y Damián include Fathers Adolfo Scal, Johan Gilde, Unger and Taddaeus Enis. Father Enis was especially famous for leading the native population in battles against the [[Portuguese Empire|Portuguese]].<ref name=":02" />

== Geography ==
[[File:Dunas_de_San_Cosme_y_Damián.jpg|thumb|Sand dunes of San Cosme y Damián, from the island of [[Ybycuí]]]]
To the north of San Cosme y Damián is the district of [[Coronel Bogado]]. To the west is [[Misiones Department]] and the district of [[General Delgado]]. The [[Paraná River|Paraná river]] runs through the southern border and separates the district from [[Argentina]]. Major towns in this district include Tiburcio Bogado, Lomas Valentinas, San Lorenzo (Colonia Voluntad), Cambyretá, Tiburcio Bogado, Pirity, Potrero Yvate, San Mauricio and Atinguy.<ref name=":02" />

The island [[Ybycuí]] is found in the Paraná river, near the district. The island is well known for containing sand dunes, which are the remains of [[Yacyretá Island]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Dunas de San Cosme y Damián - Isla Ybycuí |url=https://visitaparaguay.com.py/lugar/405/dunas-de-san-cosme-y-damian-isla-ybycui |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230706000039/https://visitaparaguay.com.py/lugar/405/dunas-de-san-cosme-y-damian-isla-ybycui |archive-date=July 6, 2023 |access-date=2023-07-05 |website=Visita Paraguay |language=es}}</ref>

== Economy ==
The principal economic activities are farming, livestock and fishing. The agricultural output largely includes cotton, maize, cassava, rice, bean, soybean and sugarcane and most of the livestock is bovine, chicken and pig. Like many rural communities, the district suffers from [[Human capital flight|brain drain]] as many of the youth will leave to study or work in nearby Argentina or more urban areas of Paraguay like [[Asunción]], [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnación]] and [[Ciudad del Este]].<ref name=":02" />

Tourism also contributes to the economy. In 2015, over 16,000 people went to visit the [[Jesuit missions among the Guaraní]] in San Cosme y Damián and over 17,000 were recorded in 2016.<ref>{{Cite web| title=Informe de Visitas a las Misiones Jesuíticas Guaraní 2016 | language=es | trans-title=Report of Visits to the Jesuit Guaraní Missions 2016 | url=https://www.observatorionacionaldeturismo.gov.py/application/files/4714/9141/7297/INFORME_ANUAL_2016_-_3_MISIONES.pdf | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220521125247/https://www.observatorionacionaldeturismo.gov.py/application/files/4714/9141/7297/INFORME_ANUAL_2016_-_3_MISIONES.pdf | archive-date=2022-05-21}}</ref>
