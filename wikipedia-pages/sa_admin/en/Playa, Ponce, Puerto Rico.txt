Barrio '''Playa''', also known as '''Playa de Ponce''', '''Ponce Playa''', or  '''La Playa''', is one of the thirty-one [[barrio]]s that comprise the [[municipality]] of [[Ponce, Puerto Rico]]. Along with [[Bucaná]], [[Canas (Ponce)|Canas]], [[Vayas]], and [[Capitanejo (Ponce)|Capitanejo]], Playa is one of the municipality's five coastal barrios. Barrio Playa also incorporates several islands, the largest of which is [[Caja de Muertos, Puerto Rico|Caja de Muertos]].<ref>[http://puertorico.hometownlocator.com/maps/countymap,cfips,113,c,ponce.cfm ''Map of Ponce Municipio, Puerto Rico.''] United States Geological Survey. Home Town Locator. Retrieved 6 June 2011.</ref> It was founded in 1831.<ref>[http://freepages.genealogy.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~poncepr/barrios.html ''Barrios de Ponce.''] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150930083458/http://freepages.genealogy.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~poncepr/barrios.html |date=30 September 2015 }} Antepasados Esclavos.(From: Pedro Tomás de Córdoba. Memorias geográficas, históricas, económicas y estadísticas de la Isla de Puerto Rico.) Retrieved 28 November 2014.</ref>

==Location==
Playa is an urban barrio located in the southern region of the municipality, within the limits of the city of Ponce, south of the traditional center of the city at [[Plaza Las Delicias]], and on the shores of the [[Caribbean Sea]]. It is located at 17.9839°N 66.6128°W, and it has an elevation of 10 feet.<ref>[http://www.trails.com/topo.aspx?panerror=true&z=19&e=759173.152588761&n=1993318.14420084&s=50&size=s&style=drgsr U.S. Geological Survey. Topographic Map of Ponce. Map Styles: Map and Shaded. Trails.com. From: USGS. Topo Map. Projection: NAD83/WGS84. ''Coordinates and Elevation of Barrio Playa.''] Retrieved 25 February 2010.</ref>  The [[toponymy]], or origin of the name, describes the geographic area the barrio occupies in southern Ponce and facing the [[Caribbean Sea]].<ref>Government of the Municipality of Ponce. Periodico "El Señorial".  Special issue: Carnaval Ponceño 2013. February 2013. Page 17. Ponce, Puerto Rico.</ref>

General Purpose Population Data, Census 2000. Unidad de Sistemas de Información Geográfica, Área de Tecnología de Información Gubernamental, Oficina de Gerencia y Presupuesto. ==Features and demographics==
Unlike most other barrios of Ponce, Playa's landscape is entirely flat. Playa also has the second longest coastline of all five of Ponce's coastal barrios, after [[Canas (Ponce)|Canas]]. In 2000, the population of Playa was 4,761 persons, and it had a density of 1,031 persons per square mile.<ref name="factfinder.census.gov"/> The communities of Villa del Carmen, Valle Real, Villa Tabaiba, Salistral, El Caribe, Puerto Viejo, Los Meros, Amalia Marin, Lirios del Sur, and Playa proper are found here.<ref name="trails.com"/><ref name="ponceweb.org">[http://www.ponceweb.org/Anuncio/Barriosdeponce.pdf Autonomous Municipality of Ponce. Office of Territorial Planning. (Municipio Autonomo de Ponce. Oficina de Ordenacion Territorial.) Mapa de Localizacion Limite de Barrios de Ponce.] Retrieved 20 February 2010.</ref>

In 2010, the population of Playa was 14,077 persons, and it had a density of 3,206.6 persons per square mile.<ref name= "2010 Census">{{cite book|title=Puerto Rico:2010:population and housing unit counts.pdf | url = https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/gpo35934/cph-2-53.pdf|year=2010|publisher=U.S. Dept. of Commerce, Economics and Statistics Administration, U.S. Census Bureau}}</ref>

This barrio, or ward, has its own [[plaza]] giving it the character of a municipality within a municipality. It has a residential area, a tourist area called [[Paseo Tablado La Guancha|La Guancha]], and a harbor, ''[[Port of the Americas (Port of Ponce)|Puerto de Ponce]]''.

==History==
[[File:Playa Harbour, Ponce 02.jpg|thumb|left|300px|Deserted street in harbor warehouse area with [[Rail transport in Puerto Rico|defunct street rail line]] of Playa]]
As the major means of external communication for the settlement that was to become the city of Ponce, barrio Playa has a history that is as old as the history of the city of Ponce itself, thus dating back to the 16th century. During the 16th century, barrio Playa was called ''Montones''.<ref>Eli D. Oquendo-Rodriguez. Pablo L. Crespo-Vargas, editor. ''A Orillas del Mar Caribe: Boceto historico de la Playa de Ponce - Desde sus primeros habitantes hasta principios del siglo XX.'' First edition. June, 2017. Editorial Akelarre. Centro de Estudios e Investigaciones del Sur Oeste de Puerto Rico (CEISCO). Lajas, Puerto Rico. Page 21. {{ISBN|978-1547284931}}</ref> Subsequently, during the 17th and 18th centuries, significant contraband took place on these shores as well as attempts to attack and ransack the Playa settlement. A lookout post was set up in [[Cruceta El Vigia|El Vigia]] to warn the city of the need of help in the Playa harbor, a port settlement at the time.

By the 1830s, la Playa had one of the best roads in Puerto Rico, connecting the shore settlement to the city proper, and it was the center of Ponce's commercial activity. It subsequently also got the first phone lines in Ponce and was also the first one to get railroad service from downtown Ponce.<ref name="Aida Belen Rivera Ruiz 2008. Page 15">Aida Belen Rivera Ruiz, Certifying Official, and Juan Llanes Santos, Preparer, Puerto Rico Historic Preservation Office. (San Juan, Puerto Rico) 26 February 2008. In ''National Register of Historic Places Inventory-Nomination Form – McCabe Memorial Church''. United States Department of the Interior. National Park Service. (Washington, D.C.) Section 8. Page 15. Listing Reference Number 80000283. Published on 11 April 2008.</ref>

Unfortunately, in 1845 a significant fire occurred in La Playa.<ref name="Ponce. 1913. Page 194">''Verdadera y Auténtica Historia de la Ciudad de Ponce.''' By Dr. Eduardo Neumann. 1913. (In Spanish) Reprinted by the Instituto de Cultura Puertorriqueña (1987)Page 194.</ref> The fire destroyed Playa and most of the Ponce vicinity. It significantly damaged the [[U.S. Custom House (Ponce, Puerto Rico)|Spanish Customs House in Ponce]], this being one of the few building left standing after the fire.<ref>James C. Massey, Exec. Vice Pres., and Shirley Maxwell, Associate, National Preservation Institute (National Building Museum) Washington, D.C. and the Federal Historic Preservation Office, U.S. Department of the Treasury. (Washington, D.C.) 7 January 1988. In ''National Register of Historic Places Registration Form – U.S. Custom House, Ponce''. United States Department of the Interior. National Park Service. (Washington, D.C.) Section 8, Page 3. Listing Reference Number 88000073. 10 February 1988.</ref> The fire burned down the major buildings of the "Marina de Ponce".<ref name="Ponce. 1913. Page 194"/> This moved Puerto Rico governor Conde de Marisol to create a new voluntary fire-fighting organization island-wide.{{citation needed|date=June 2021}}

In 1887 the Spanish government built a lighthouse at [[Caja de Muertos Light]] on an island by the same name just off the coast of barrio Playa.<ref>''National Register of Historic Places Inventory-Nomination Form – Isla Caja de Muertos Light''. United States Department of the Interior. National Park Service. (Washington, D.C.) Page 2. Listing Reference Number 81000690. 22 October 1981.</ref> This was followed by the building of [[Cardona Island Light|Cayo Cardona]] lighthouse in 1889, on a small island at the entrance of the Ponce harbor.<ref>''National Register of Historic Places Inventory-Nomination Form – Cayo Cardona Light''. United States Department of the Interior. National Park Service. (Washington, D.C.) Page 2. Listing Reference Number 81000691. 22 October 1981.</ref> Both of these islands are part of barrio Playa.

By 1913, Playa was "a dynamic neighborhood with a self-sustained urban development with a population of 5,169 distributed through a residential area dominated by wooden houses, sugar cane farms, churches, schools, hospitals, a cemetery and local indiustries that promoted the formation of a strong artisan and industrial workers class."<ref name="Aida Belen Rivera Ruiz 2008. Page 15"/> Playa is considered Puerto Rico's first planned [[suburb]]an area.<ref>Diana Lopez Sotomayor. Edificio Municipal de la Playa de Ponce. National Register of Historic Places. Registration Form. Number 13000639. 3 July 2013. p.7.</ref>

In 1929, La Playa was the place where Dr. [[Manuel de la Pila Iglesias]] worked for the U.S. federal government with such distinction as to earn him the title "physician of physicians."<ref name="galenusrevista.com">[http://www.galenusrevista.com/El-Dr-Manuel-de-la-Pila-Iglesias ''El Dr. Manuel de la Pila Iglesias: Ilustre médico de Ponce y Puerto Rico.''] Norman Maldonado, MD. ''GALENUS: Revista para los Medicos de Puerto Rico.'' Volume 9. Year 2. Number 2. (March–April 2009) Guaynabo, Puerto Rico: MW World PR Corp. Accessed 28 April 2018.</ref>

Today, Playa is a mostly low-income barrio of Ponce, and thus this is where [[Isolina Ferré]] founded her "Centro de Orientacion de la Playa" hospital and school that would earn her the U.S. [[Presidential Medal of Freedom]] in 1999. The Ponce Playa Diagnostic and Treatment Center was also founded in barrio Playa de Ponce.{{citation needed|date=June 2021}}

==Landmarks==
[[File:Research Bldg at Ponce School of Medicine in Barrio Playa in Ponce, PR (DSC00769).jpg|thumb|right|300px|[[Ponce Health Sciences University]] on [[Puerto Rico Highway 2#Ponce|PR-2]], Barrio Playa]]

Playa is home to a mix of Ponce and Puerto Rico landmarks: [[Puerto Rico Iron Works]] foundry, [[Plaza del Caribe]] mall, [[Club Náutico de Ponce]] yacht club, [[Paseo Tablado La Guancha|La Guancha boardwalk]], and [[Caja de Muertos, Puerto Rico|Caja de Muertos]] natural reserve are all located in Barrio Playa. The historic [[Cardona Island Light]], [[Caja de Muertos Light]]house, [[United States Customs House (Ponce, Puerto Rico)|United States Custom House]] and [[McCabe Memorial Church]],<ref>[http://www.topozone.com/map.asp?lon=-66.5623943&lat=18.0060765&datum=nad83 McCabe Memorial Church is located in Barrio Playa] Retrieved 3 December 2009.</ref> all [[National Register of Historic Places]] listed landmarks, are also located in Barrio Playa. Playa also has the most important Caribbean seaport in Puerto Rico, called [[Rafael Cordero Santiago Port of the Americas|Port of the Americas]].<ref>[http://latintrade.com/2009/06/port-of-the-americas-puerto-rico ''A New Transshipment Hub Underway in Puerto Rico.''] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130921054249/http://latintrade.com/2009/06/port-of-the-americas-puerto-rico |date=2013-09-21 }} Richard Westlund. Latin Trade. 1 June 2009. Retrieved 19 September 2013.</ref>
