'''Tamarindo''' is a [[Districts of Costa Rica|district]] of the [[Santa Cruz (canton), Costa Rica|Santa Cruz]] canton, in the [[Guanacaste Province|Guanacaste]] province of [[Costa Rica]], located on the [[Nicoya Peninsula]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019 |archive-date=26 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201026094325/http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book|language=es|title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf|access-date=17 September 2020|archive-date=3 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210603061212/https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref> 

The town of Tamarindo is the largest developed beach town in [[Guanacaste Province|Guanacaste]]. Once a small [[fishing village]], it has boomed in the 21st century with [[surfing]] and [[eco-tourism]], and is now popular with surfers, digital nomads, and expatriates.

== History ==
Tamarindo began as a small village of several families who largely depended on fishing.

In 1985, Texas businessman Russell Wenrich purchased land in the village of Tamarindo and began to develop beachside cabanas. Five years later, Wenrich met [[Robert August]], star of the 1966 surfing documentary ''[[The Endless Summer]],'' who moved to town. Still later, Wenrich persuaded the Costa Rican tourism agency to subsidize filming in Tamarindo for the 1994 sequel ''[[The Endless Summer II]]''. This film, which began with scenes from Tamarindo, is generally credited for launching a tourism boom that transformed a small fishing village into the tourism center it is today.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2021-07-04 |title=Surf Sunday: Watch 'Endless Summer II,' A film that popularized Tamarindo |url=https://ticotimes.net/2021/07/04/tbt-watch-endless-summer-ii-the-documentary-that-popularized-tamarindo-2 |access-date=2024-03-21 |website=The Tico Times {{!}} Costa Rica News {{!}} Travel {{!}} Real Estate |language=en-US |archive-date=2024-03-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240320203807/https://ticotimes.net/2021/07/04/tbt-watch-endless-summer-ii-the-documentary-that-popularized-tamarindo-2 |url-status=live }}</ref> 

Tamarindo was incorporated as a town on November 27, 1995, by ''Decreto Ejecutivo'' (English: Executive Order) 24820-G.<ref name="dta2017" />

"Another watershed moment came in 2003 when the nearby airport at Liberia started handling international flights, putting the town within five hours reach of Miami," ''[[Wavelength (magazine)|Wavelength]]'' magazine wrote in 2020.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gartside |first=Luke |date=2020-08-19 |title=After The Endless Summer II: What Happened To The Surf Destinations Featured In The Film? |url=https://wavelengthmag.com/after-the-endless-summer-ii-what-happened-to-the-surf-destinations-featured-in-the-film/ |access-date=2024-04-01 |website=Wavelength Surf Magazine - since 1981 |language=en-GB |archive-date=2024-04-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240401150441/https://wavelengthmag.com/after-the-endless-summer-ii-what-happened-to-the-surf-destinations-featured-in-the-film/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Geography ==
Tamarindo has an area of {{Convert|125.86|km2|sqmi}}<ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=24 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024225203/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{Convert|27|m|ft}}.<ref name="divadm" />

The nearby Las Baulas National Marine Park is an important nesting site for the endangered leatherback sea turtle (''Dermochelys coriacea'')<ref>{{Cite web |title=Тамариндо (Гуанакасте): населённый пункт, курорт и природный регион Коста-Рики |url=https://visit-tamarindo.ru/ |access-date=2026-04-30 |website=visit-tamarindo.ru}}</ref>.

==Settlements==
Besides Tamarindo, towns in the district include Villarreal, Santa Rosa, Garita Nueva, Hernández, Hucus, Flamingo and San José de Pinilla.<ref name=database>{{cite web|title=Tamarindo|url=http://www.costaricadatabase.com/crdb/city.asp?action=2&q=TAMARINDO&b=SANTA+CRUZ&p=5|accessdate=2015-03-28|archive-date=2015-04-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402130413/http://www.costaricadatabase.com/crdb/city.asp?action=2&q=TAMARINDO&b=SANTA+CRUZ&p=5|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Villareal===

The nearby town of Villareal has a public elementary school and high school, two full-size soccer fields, multiple restaurants, two large stores, multiple hardware stores, a police station, a church, several car-repair shops, and a gas station.

===Langosta===

Just east of the centre of Tamarindo, there is a three-way intersection. To the left is the road that leads to the [[Banco Nacional de Costa Rica]], and also a secondary exit from Tamarindo. To the right is the road that leads to Playa Langosta.

===Playa Grande===

To the north of Tamarindo is Playa Grande, typically accessed by crossing the [[Las Baulas National Marine Park|marine park estuary]] via a water taxi, or via a 17km drive through Villareal.

==Tourism==
===Beaches===

The beaches in the area are generally clean and recent efforts by the government and local business organizations are proving themselves. While the beach has not regained its Blue Flag Status,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.blueflag.org/ |title=Home |website=blueflag.org |access-date=2008-09-29 |archive-date=2008-05-29 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080529165244/http://www.blueflag.org/blueflag |url-status=live }}</ref> in September 2008 it did get a clean bill of health from the Costa Rican government. The town is trying to regain its Blue Flag Status.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.tamarindonews.com/Tamarindo_Community_News/tamarindo_let_s_get_the_blue_flag.html |title=Tamarindo News |access-date=2010-08-20 |archive-date=2010-08-09 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100809185048/http://tamarindonews.com/Tamarindo_Community_News/tamarindo_let_s_get_the_blue_flag.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Surfing===

Tamarindo and nearby areas are very popular for [[surfing]], and feature waves from beginner to advanced.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.surfline.com/travel/zones/north-costa-rica/surf-guide/tamarindo/5842041f4e65fad6a7708b9b |title=Tamarindo Surf Guide - Surfline |access-date=2024-05-21 |archive-date=2024-05-21 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240521024740/https://www.surfline.com/travel/zones/north-costa-rica/surf-guide/tamarindo/5842041f4e65fad6a7708b9b |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Fishing===
Tamarindo is known for world-class fishing, and a variety of captains and charter services are available. Costa Rica requires a [https://www.incopesca.go.cr/carnets/tramites/SolicitudTraducido.aspx fishing license] from the INCOPESCA (Instituto Costarricense de Pesca y Acuicultura), the government agency that manages, regulates and promotes fisheries and aquaculture.<ref>{{Cite web | date=2022-10-15 | title=Reseña Histórica de INCOPESCA | url=https://www.incopesca.go.cr/acerca_incopesca/index.aspx | access-date=2022-10-15 | website=Instituto Costarricense de Pesca y Acuicultura | language=es | archive-date=2022-10-15 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221015170302/https://www.incopesca.go.cr/acerca_incopesca/index.aspx | url-status=live }}</ref>

===Scuba===

The [[Santa Catalinas Islands | Catalinas Islands]] are one of the most popular spots for scuba diving, due to its wide variety of sea life and its views.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://ticotimes.net/2021/10/17/visiting-the-catalina-islands-of-costa-rica |title=Visiting the Catalina Islands of Costa Rica |date=17 October 2021 |access-date=2024-03-23 |archive-date=2024-03-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240323071734/https://ticotimes.net/2021/10/17/visiting-the-catalina-islands-of-costa-rica |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Golf===

Golf courses include the Hacienda Pinilla Golf Club, the Reserva Conchal Beach Resort, and Four Seasons Resort Peninsula Papagayo.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.coldwellbankertamarindo.com/article/golf-courses-near-tamarindo-costa-rica |title=Golf Courses Near Tamarindo Costa Rica |access-date=2024-03-23 |archive-date=2024-03-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240323005021/https://www.coldwellbankertamarindo.com/article/golf-courses-near-tamarindo-costa-rica |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Weather ==
The town of Tamarindo has essentially two seasons: the wet, generally May to November; and the dry, December to April.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://iguanasurf.net/blog/tamarindo-surf-vacation-how-to-pick-when-to-go/ |title=Picking the Season for Your Tamarindo Surf Vacation &#124; Iguana Surf |date=19 March 2020 |access-date=2024-03-20 |archive-date=2024-03-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240320213512/https://iguanasurf.net/blog/tamarindo-surf-vacation-how-to-pick-when-to-go/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Airport===
Tamarindo is the most accessible location along the northern Pacific coast of Costa Rica with an [[Tamarindo Airport|airstrip]].

[[Daniel Oduber Quirós International Airport|Liberia International Airport]] is the closest international airport to Tamarindo, about an hour away.

===Public transportation===
There is a scheduled daily bus service to and from San José, as well as a paved highway to San José, with driving time of 3.5 to 6 hours depending on traffic.
