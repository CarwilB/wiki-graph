'''Las Lajas''' ("The Flagstones") is a town in [[Neuquén Province]], Argentina, and the capital of [[Picunches Department]]. The town, at an altitude of {{convert|900|m|ft}} [[above mean sea level]], is located on the right shore of [[Agrio River]] at the Andean foothills. National Routes 22 and 40 intersect in this area of the central Neuquén Province. The Cuchillo Curá range, considered the most important underground ecosystem in Argentina, extends approximately {{convert|10|km|mi}} from Las Lajas.

Before the [[Araucanization of Patagonia]], Las Lajas was originally populated by [[Pehuenche]] and [[Picunche]] natives. In 1788, the first expedition into the territory, under the command Francisco Esquivel y Aldao, reached Las Lajas. In 1882, commander Ortega{{who|date=November 2011}} established a fort for the Argentinian Army. The town was established on 8 February 1897, by General Enrique Godoy, the Argentinian Army's 4th Division commander. To take possession of the region and protect the inhabitants from the natives, the town became the headquarters of the 8th and 9th Cavalry Regiments. It was named "Las Lajas" after the creek Las Lajitas, whose bed soil is mainly composed of the stones. Governor Alsina and the inhabitants of the town requested to move the National Territory's capital from [[Chos Malal]] to Las Lajas. The capital was ultimately moved to Neuquén city in 1902. The town received its current status of second-category municipality in 1967.

The fodder production is largest agricultural activity in the region. The soils of the area remain unexplored, and for the lack of technical information, subsequently the oil and natural gas invest in locations such as Loma de La Lata. Another major aspect of the town is the Argentina Army's 21 Mountain Infantry Regiment. Following its closure of in the late 1990s, the regiment re-opened in 2004 with a diminution of the personnel to 250 soldiers as of 2011, out of an original of 1,000. This increased the number of customers and raised the solicitations for commercial licenses. The opening of the Pino Hachado Pass increased the quantity of vehicles and people that go through the town.

==History==
Archeological evidences found at Mallín del Tromen suggest that the [[Picunches Department]] was populated at around 4500 [[Before Present|BP]].<ref>{{cite book |work=National Congress of Argentine Archeology|year=1984 |title=Arqueología argentina en los inicios de un nuevo siglo 
|place=Rosario, Argentina|publisher=Editorial Laborde|isbn=978-987-1315-20-8|pages=287–634}}</ref> Before the [[Araucanization of Patagonia]], which took place sometime between 1550 and 1850, this region of the current Neuquén Province was inhabited by [[Picunche]] and [[Pehuenches]] natives.<ref>{{cite book|title=Historia del Neuquén|publisher=Pangera Editorial|page=27|language=Spanish}}</ref> In 1788, the first expedition into the territory of the current province reached the Las Lajas area under order of commander Francisco Esquivel y Aldao, who traveled {{convert|2000|km|mi|0}} from [[Mendoza, Argentina|Mendoza]] with his men.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://w1.lmneuquen.com.ar/05-06-15/n_sociedad1.asp|title=Cincuenta años de Provincia|work=La Mañana Neuquén|date=15 June 2005|access-date=29 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> 
[[File:LasLajasArgentina entrance.jpg|left|thumb|250px|Entrance to Las Lajas]]
The first fort of the Argentinian army in the area was built by commander Ortega around 1882.<ref name=rio2>{{cite news|url=http://www1.rionegro.com.ar/viajes/lugar.php?id=305|title=Sospechada gestión de Alsina por Las Lajas|work=Río Negro|date=12 January 2003|access-date=22 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> The local postal office started working in 1886 and the telegraph arrived one year later.<ref name=rio/> On 8 February 1897, the Argentinian Army's 4th Division Commander General Enrique Godoy ordered the establishment of the town as the headquarters of the 8 and 9 Cavalry Regiments. It was named Las Lajas ("The Flagstones" in Spanish) after the near creek Las Lajitas, whose [[Bed (geology)|bed]] possesses abundance of that kind of stone.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.rionegro.com.ar/viajes/listado.php?idlocalidad=47|title=Localidades: Las Lajas|work=Río Negro|access-date=21 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> The regiments were there to protect the colonists after the occupation of the native lands, and to take the control of the region. Two years later, an [[Agrio River]] flood destroyed the hamlet, which had to be reconstructed on the upper part of the valley. Those lands belonged to the later Governor Juán Alsina, who exchanged them for lands in [[Primeros Pinos]] and Pino Hachado.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lmneuquen.com.ar/noticias/2011/2/27/99569.php |title=Febrero, un mes repleto de celebraciones para la Provincia |work=La Mañana Neuquén |date=2 February 2011 |access-date=4 August 2011 |language=Spanish |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111002223753/http://www.lmneuquen.com.ar/noticias/2011/2/27/99569.php |archive-date= 2 October 2011 }}</ref>

The local court was opened on 23 January 1901, and the following year the first police station was established.<ref name=rio>{{cite web|url=http://www1.rionegro.com.ar/viajes/lugar.php?id=304|title=Las Lajas: Generalidades|work=Río Negro|access-date=21 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> In 1903, Governor Alsina and local ranchers wanted to move the National Territory's capital from [[Chos Malal]] to Las Lajas, predicating that its location was more accessible for the rest of the Territory's towns in comparison with the previous city.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://investigadores.uncoma.edu.ar/cehepyc/publicaciones/Trabajo_Traslado_de_la_capital_Nqn..pdf|title=Confluencia de voluntades o coincidencia de intereses: El traslado del Neuquén a Confluencia|work=National University of Comahue |access-date=22 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> The capital was ultimately moved to Neuquén city in 1902 with the support of Carlos Governor Bouquet Roldán and landowners of the [[Confluencia Department]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.eculturismo.com.ar/Textos/ChosMalal.pdf|title=Pioneros de un sueño|work=Andacollo's forests' division|access-date=22 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref>

In 1933, the province's government designated the [[ejido]] for the developing committee. Due to its sustained development, the town received its current status of second-category municipality in 1967.<ref name=nqn>{{cite web|url=http://www.neuquen.com/localidades_laslajas.php|title=Las Lajas' page on Neuquén province's tourist site|work=Neuquén province's tourist site|publisher=Neuquén.com SA|access-date=23 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref>

In 1996, a popular but unsuccessful riot tried to prevent the closure of the 21 Mountain Infantry Regiment which was crucial to the town's economy. It remained closed for seven years until its reopening in 2004 with a considerably lower number of men in comparison to its past.<ref name=mil/>

==Geography==
Las Lajas is located on the right shore of [[Agrio River]] at the Andean foothills, where [[National Route 22 (Argentina)|National Routes 22]] and [[National Route 40 (Argentina)|40]]. The [[Pino Hachado Pass]], which joins Argentina and Chile, is {{Convert|52|km|mi}} west of the town. Las Lajas is {{convert|58|km|mi|0}} from [[Zapala]], {{convert|242|km|mi|0}} from [[Neuquén, Argentina|Neuquén City]] and {{convert|1455|km|mi|0}} from [[Buenos Aires]]. It has an elevation {{convert|703|m|ft|0}} [[above mean sea level]] (AMSL).<ref name="tur">{{cite web|url=http://argentinaturismo.org/neuquen/bajada-del-agrio/4/que-hacer-y-que-visitar-en-bajada-del-agrio/|title=Qué hacer y qué visitar en la bajada del Agrio|work=Argentina Turismo|access-date=21 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> The local poorly developed soils are composed of a low percentage in organic matter. They belong to the [[entisol]] and [[aridisols|aridisol]] orders of the [[USDA soil taxonomy]].<ref>{{cite web|url =http://www.inta.gov.ar/bariloche/ssd/contenido/patagonia.htm|title =Bariloche Experimental Agrucultural Station|work=Argentinian Institute of Agricultural Technology|access-date =25 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> Besides the Agrio River, waterways in the area include the Las Lajitas and Liu Cullín Creeks.<ref>{{cite journal |journal=Journal of Argentina's Geological Association|year=1973 |title=Geological draft of Las Lajas Area 
|place=Buenos Aires, Argentina|publisher=Argentina's Geological Association|volume=28|number=2|pages=109&ndash;110|language=Spanish}}</ref>

===Climate===
Las Lajas lies within an arid area. The annual precipitation is around {{convert|311|mm|in|0}}, being the values higher during winter months{{Clarify|Is there a stat missing?|date=November 2011}}. The average yearly [[vapor pressure]] values are relatively low. The average humidity values are above 60 percent during winter, while they are considerable lower during summer. The relative humidity amplitude averages 30 percent yearly. Although the town is located at the same latitude as Neuquén City, the area is higher and the temperature colder. Average temperatures range from {{convert|14|°C|°F}} in January to {{convert|0|°C|°F}} in July. The daily amplitude is significant, especially in summer. However, considerable interdiurnal variations occur. The highest temperature ever recorded in the area is around {{convert|40|°C|°F}} in summer and the lowest around {{convert|-16|°C|°F}} in winter. The winds range from moderate to strong, having more intensity in the south. The main [[wind direction]]s are west, northwest and southwest. The clouds cover up to 60 percent of the sky in winter and 30 percent in summer.<ref>{{cite web |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150220015016/http://www.mineria.gob.ar/estudios/irn/neuquen/N-2.asp |archive-date=20 February 2015 |url = http://www.mineria.gob.ar/estudios/irn/neuquen/n-2.asp|title = Provincia del Neuquén: Clima y Meteorología|work=Argentina's Mining Secretariat|publisher=Argentina's National Mining Direction|access-date =16 November 2007}}</ref>

=== Cave systems ===
The Cuchillo Curá Range extends around {{convert|10|km|mi|0}} from Las Lajas, on National Route 40. Located at {{convert|900|m|ft|0}} AMSL, it is considered the most important underground ecosystem in Argentina.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.rionegro.com.ar/viajes/lugar.php?id=306|title=Las Lajas: Cavernas|work=Río Negro|access-date=29 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> The system is composed of the caves El Gendarme, El Templo, El Arenal and Los Cabritos. It possesses a gallery area of {{Convert|4800|m2|sqft}}.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.argentour.com/es/provincia/neuquen/las_lajas.php|title=Las Lajas|work=Argentour|access-date=29 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref>

The other important cave in the area is the Cueva del León, which is situated on the Crodón Curimil to {{convert|15|km|mi|0}} from the town. It was formed in [[plaster]] during the [[Late Jurassic]]. It is {{Convert|631|m|ft}} long and consists of two chambers named the "Great Chamber" and "Chamber of the Lake".<ref name=nqn/>

== Demographics ==
The 1991 Argentinian census registered a total of 3,428 inhabitants in Las Lajas. The quantity increased by 7.8%, reaching the 4,673 in 2001.{{Clarify|How is an increase of over a 1000 people to 4,673 a 7.8% increase?|date=November 2011}}<ref>{{cite web|url =http://www2.neuquen.gov.ar/dgecy/imprimir.asp?Imprimir=010964:%20Poblaci%F3n%20censada%20por%20a%F1o%20seg%FAn%20municipio%20y%20comisi%F3n%20de%20fomento.%20A%F1os%201991%20y%202001|title =Registers of the 1991 and 2001 censuses for the Neuquén Province's municipalities|work=Neuquén Province's Statistics' Direction|publisher=Argentina's National Mining Direction|access-date =16 November 2007}}</ref> As of 2010, it was estimated that the town had a permanent population of 4,673, which increased up to 7,000 if non-permanent residents were considered.<ref>{{cite news|url =http://www.neuquen.com/noticias.php?titulo=las_lajas&fecha=2010-02-08|title =Las Lajas' 113th Anniversary|work=Neuquén Province's Statistics' Direction|publisher=Neuquén.com SA|date=2 August 2010|access-date =23 September 2011}}</ref>

== Economy ==
The [[fodder]] production is largest agricultural activity in the region. It is recognized by the quality of its grass, especially of the [[alfalfa]]. Villa Rural San Demetrio and Colonia Juán Ignacio Alsina are the main establishments.<ref name=pasto/>

The closing of Argentina Army's 21 Mountain Infantry in the late 1990s caused the closure of businesses and emigration of inhabitants to other towns. In 2004, the Regiment was reopened increasing the people flux around local businesses by 20 percent and commercial licenses quantity by 12 percent. Although the Regiment's positive impact on the town's economy, it produced a lower income with a personnel of 250 soldiers as of 2011 in comparison to the 1,000 men who served there prior to its closure.<ref name=mil>{{cite news|url =http://w1.lmneuquen.com.ar/05-06-13/n_regionales4.asp|first=Diana|last=Nieto|title =Con el RIM 21 resurge Las Lajas|work=La Mañana Neuquén|date=28 September 2011|access-date =28 September 2011}}</ref> The opening of the Pino Hachado Pass increased the quantity of vehicles and people that go through the town.<ref name=rio/>

Since the area's soil remains unexplored and lacks availability of technical information, the oil and natural gas companies opt for investing in known and traditional locations like [[Loma de La Lata]].<ref>{{cite book|first=Federico|last=Bernal|title=Petróleo, Estado y Soberanía: Hacia la empresa multiestatal latinoamericana de hidrocarburos|page=177|publisher=Biblos Editorial|year=2005|isbn=950-786-457-1|language=Spanish}}</ref>

The town's economy saw itself benefited by the installment of the [[China National Space Administration|CNSA]] [[Espacio Lejano Station|Espacio Lejano]] deep space station, located in Bajada del Agrio, about 60&nbsp;km on the provincial route 33. Over 300 local workers were employed for its construction.<ref>{{Cite news|date=26 February 2015|title=Argentinian congress approves deal with China on satellite space station|url=http://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/feb/26/argentina-congress-china-satellite-space-station|access-date=2 February 2021|work=The Guardian}}</ref> Although the Chinese staff of the station rarely visits the town, María Espinosa, the mayor by 2019, noted that the facilities have had a positive impact on the local economy.<ref>{{Cite news|date=31 January 2019|last=Garrison|first=Cassandra|title=Argentinian congress approves deal with China on satellite space station|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-space-argentina-china-insight-idUSKCN1PP0I2|access-date=2 February 2021|work=Reuters}}</ref>

== Tourism ==
Las Lajas is a hub for travelers that head to some of the province's touristic spots, including the northern hot springs' area in the municipality of [[Caviahue-Copahue]], as well as the ones headed to [[Chile]] through the Pino Hachado Pass.<ref>{{cite web|url =http://bariloche.inta.gov.ar/ssd/valles/neuquen/ig/POP/lajas_pop.htm|title =Las Lajas|work=Argentinian Institute of Agricultural Technology|access-date =16 November 2007}}</ref> The area offers possibilities to practice different activities both in summer and winter, such as hiking, fishing, camping and skiing.<ref name=rio/> Its natural surroundings include an [[Araucaria]] forest, the Cuchillo Curá cave system and the Agrio river. In Las Lajas work accommodation facilities and other services for tourists like bars and restaurants.<ref>{{cite web|url =http://www.argentinaturismo.com.ar/laslajas/|title =Las Lajas, Neuquén|work=Argentina's Official Tourism Site|access-date=23 September 2011}}</ref>

There are a number of attractions in Las Lajas. Among them is a local museum at the junction between the National Route 22 and the Avenida del Trabajador Street. It exhibits historic objects and archaeological pieces. And, since Las Lajas is a [[speleology]] center, the museum also exhibits photos of the Cuchillo Curá y Cueva del León cave systems.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.neuquentur.gob.ar/turismo-historico-y-museos/1610/museo-municipal-de-las-lajas|title=Turismo Histórico y Museos|work=Neuquén Province's Official Tourism Site|access-date=25 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> During October and November, cultural meetings gather musicians, poets, painters and other kinds of artists. The shores of the Agrio River and of the Liu Cullín and Las Lajitas creeks are popular spots for camping and fishing. During winter months it is possible to ski in the slopes of the near snowpark.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.patagonia.com.ar/Las+Lajas/197_Las+Lajas+y+Pino+Hachado.html|title=Las Lajas y Pino Hachado|work=Patagonia.com.ar|access-date=25 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref> Every year in April the Grass Festival, which honors the farmers dedicated to the fodder crop, is held. During the Festival, local meals and artwork are sold, and night folk music spectacles are celebrated.<ref name=pasto>{{cite web|url=http://www.patagonia.com.ar/Las+Lajas/802_Fiesta+Provincial+del+Pasto.html|title=Las Lajas y Pino Hachado|work=Patagonia.com.ar|access-date=28 September 2011|language=Spanish}}</ref>
