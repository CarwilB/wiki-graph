'''Osorno Province''' ({{langx|es|Provincia de Osorno}}) is one of the four [[Provinces of Chile|provinces]] in the southern [[Chile]]an [[Regions of Chile|region]] of [[Los Lagos Region|Los Lagos]] (X). The province has an area of {{convert|9223.7|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and a population of 221,496 distributed across seven [[Communes of Chile|communes]] ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''comunas''). The provincial [[Capital (political)|capital]] is the city of [[Osorno, Chile|Osorno]].

Osorno is a city located in southern Chile a territory of northern Patagonia in the Los Lagos Region. The Chilean Patagonia starts at Latitude 39° South in Valdivia and then it continues through the Chiloe Island and the fiords that continue its course south through the Pacific Ocean and the Cordillera de los Andes until it reaches Latitude 56° South at Cape Horn.The province possesses one of the most frequented [[List of mountain passes of Chile|mountain passes of Chile]], the [[Cardenal Antonio Samoré Pass]] which links the province with [[Villa La Angostura]] and [[San Carlos de Bariloche]] in [[Argentina]].

==Administration==
As a province, Osorno is a second-level [[Administrative divisions of Chile|administrative division]], governed by a provincial [[governor]] who is appointed by the [[President of Chile|president]]. The Osorno Province comprises seven communes; each commune is governed by its own municipality, headed by an [[alcalde]]:

*[[Osorno, Chile|Osorno]]
*[[Puerto Octay]]
*[[Purranque]]
*[[Puyehue, Chile|Puyehue]]
*[[Río Negro, Chile|Río Negro]]
*[[San Pablo, Chile|San Pablo]]
*[[San Juan de la Costa]]

==History==
The first inhabitants of the area were the indigenous [[Huilliche]] people. Osorno also featured largely in [[German-Chilean|German Colonization in Chile]], which took place in the 19th century, when the Chilean government brought [[Germans|German]] settlers to assist in the growth of the region.

== Ethnocultural demography==
{{further|German colonization of Valdivia, Osorno and Llanquihue}}
{{More citations needed section|date=July 2010}}
The Osorno province owes its legacy to fairly recent Chilean settlement, when the government subdued the region's indigenous [[Mapuche]] peoples in the mid-19th century and opened the land to Chilean and European immigration soon to follow. Large percentage of locals in Osorno are descendants of Spanish (the livestock grazing industry owes its foundation to the [[Basque people|Basques]]) and other European immigrants.

In Osorno, there are historic ties and bonds with the [[Dutch people|Dutch]], [[British people|British]] esp. [[Scottish people|Scots]] with some [[Irish people|Irish]] and [[English people|English]], [[French people|French]], [[Germans]] including [[Austrians]] and [[Swiss people|Swiss]], [[Italians]], [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]], ex-[[Yugoslavians]], [[Greeks]] and from the Middle East are [[Arab Chilean|Arabs]] and [[Palestinian Chilean|Palestinians]]. These ethnic groups came in the late 19th century and early 20th century  period.

The provincial cultural heritage is shaped by [[Huilliche]], [[Spanish Chileans|Spanish]], and [[German Chilean|German]] influences. Around 1850, the government of Chile began inviting German settlers to the colony to promote growth in the region; the settlers found Osorno's climate and geography to be very similar to their own. With their help, Osorno was made the home of the national cattle ranch of Chile, boosting the regional economy significantly. Present-day Osorno has preserved 19th-century architecture and urban layout, represented by six houses which have been designated [[National Monuments of Chile|national monuments]].<ref name=usa>{{cite web |url=http://www.chile-usa.org/photo4.htm |title=South; Araucaria, Lakes & Volcanoes |access-date=2007-06-06 |author=Embassy of Chile, Washington, DC |work=Chile}}</ref>

==Economic activity==
The main economic activity in the province is livestock, Osorno being one of the most important agricultural centers of Chile, which is identified with the slogan "Land of Milk and Meat of Chile". Another important economic activity is tourism.

Tourist destinations in the province include:

*Lake and mountainous communes of Puerto Octay and Puyehue, where the German migrated influence is evident; mainly in construction and catering. The main tourist attractions in the area are its lakes: [[Llanquihue Lake|Llanquihue]], [[Puyehue Lake|Puyehue]] and [[Rupanco Lake|Rupanco]] with multiple beaches each; [[Puyehue National Park]] (tourist centre which has the ''Hot spring and Baths'' of [[Puyehue Hot Springs|Puyehue]] and [[Aguas Calientes, Chile|Aguas Calientes]]; and [[Antillanca ski resort]]).
*Communes central of Osorno and San Pablo, where Osorno city identifies itself with the slogan "City-Park of Chile". Osorno city is also a tourist centre in the province.
*Coastal communes of [[Purranque]], [[Río Negro, Chile|Rio Negro]] and [[San Juan de la Costa]], presenting a wide coastal area, rich in natural resources, and ecotourism and ethnotourism.{{citation needed|date=December 2018}} Attractions include the beaches at [[Pucatrihue]] and [[Maicolpue]]. Also this zone presents wildlife reserves created by the original inhabitants of ethnic [[Huilliche]], such as the Network of Community [[Parks Mapu Lahual]], associated with marine and coastal protected area [[Lafken Mapu Lahual]].
