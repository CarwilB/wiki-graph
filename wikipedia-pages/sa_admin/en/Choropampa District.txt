'''Choropampa District''' is one of nineteen [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of the [[Chota Province]], [[Peru]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=April 23, 2008 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> It is located in the Northern Peruvian highlands and renowned for its gold reserves, with South America's largest goldmine operations.

An elemental mercury spill occurred in June 2000 along a road that passed through the three villages Choropampa,<ref>[https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2020/apr/02/the-village-still-suffering-from-peru-mercury-spill-fallout-after-20-years The village still suffering from Peru mercury spill fallout – after 20 years] The Guardian, 2020</ref> Magdalena and San Juan from a truck contracted by [[Yanacocha]] mining; the Compliance/Advisor Ombudsman of the [[International Finance Corporation]] /Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency investigated, as described in the 2006 exit report.<ref name="cao">{{cite web | url=http://www.cao-ombudsman.org/cases/document-links/documents/Exitreportenglish02-7-06-final.pdf | title=Exit Report Regarding two complaints filed with the CAO in relation to Minera Yanacocha Cajamarca, Peru | publisher=International Finance Corporation/ Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency | date=February 2006 | accessdate=5 May 2016 | author=International Finance Corporation/ Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency | pages=8 | archive-date=26 February 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226175805/http://www.cao-ombudsman.org/cases/document-links/documents/Exitreportenglish02-7-06-final.pdf | url-status=dead }}</ref>
