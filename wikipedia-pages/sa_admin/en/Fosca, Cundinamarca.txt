'''Fosca''' is a municipality and town of [[Colombia]] in the [[Eastern Province, Cundinamarca|Eastern Province]], part of the [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]]. It is located in the [[Ubaque]] Valley with the urban centre at a distance of {{convert|62|km|mi}} from the capital [[Bogotá]] at an altitude of {{convert|2080|m|ft}}. Fosca borders [[Cáqueza]] and [[Une]] in the north, [[Quetame]] in the east, [[Guayabetal]] in the south and [[Gutiérrez, Cundinamarca|Gutiérrez]] in the west.<ref name=websiteFosca>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.fosca-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Fosca]{{Dead link|date=December 2019 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

== History ==
In the time before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]], Fosca was inhabited by the Mau or Maco and Guaypi or Buchipa indigenous tribe.<ref name=websiteFosca/> The [[Muisca people|Muisca]], living north from the area, had a [[Muisca warfare#Fortifications|fortification]] of [[guecha warrior]]s stationed in Fosca to defend themselves from the [[Guayupe people]] who inhabited the region south of Fosca.<ref name=RodriMontes_p1639>Rodríguez Montes, 2002, p.1639</ref>

About the discovery and foundation of Fosca three different versions are given; [[Nikolaus Federmann]] would have founded the town on September 1, 1538, captain Pedro de Limpias reached and discovered Fosca on his expedition from the [[Orinoquía Region|Llanos Orientales]] on February 5, 1538, or Juan de Valcárcel passed through the area and moved the town centre to a different location on February 5, 1627.<ref name=websiteFosca/>

== Economy ==
The economy of Fosca is concentrated around [[agriculture]] and [[livestock]] farming. Main agricultural products cultivated are [[maize]], [[potato]]es, [[coffee]], [[arracacha]], [[bean]]s and [[pea]]s.<ref name=websiteFosca/>
