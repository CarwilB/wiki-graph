'''Lima''' ({{IPA|es|ˈli.ma}}) is a [[List of districts of Lima|district of Lima]], [[Peru]]. It is the oldest district of the province and as such, vestiges of the city's [[Viceroyalty of Peru|Spanish period]] remain today in its [[Historic Centre of Lima|historic centre]], part of which was declared a [[UNESCO World Heritage Site]] in 1988 and contains the [[Cercado de Lima|foundational area]] once surrounded by the [[Walls of Lima|city's walls]].

== Etymology ==
{{see|Lima#Etymology}}
The name of the district comes from one of two sources: Either the Aymara language ''lima-limaq'' (meaning "yellow flower"), or the Spanish pronunciation of the [[Quechuan languages|Quechua]] word ''rimaq'' (meaning "talker", and actually written and pronounced ''limaq'' in the nearby [[Quechua languages#Geographic distribution|Quechua I]] languages). It is worth nothing that the same Quechua word is also the source of the name given to the river that feeds the city, the [[Rímac River]] (pronounced as in the politically dominant [[Quechua languages#Geographic distribution|Quechua II]] languages, with an "r" instead of an "l"). Early maps of Peru show both names (''Lima'' and ''Ciudad de los Reyes'') displayed jointly.

== History ==
{{main|History of Lima}}
[[File:Lima1750.jpg|thumb|left|1750 map of Lima and its [[Walls of Lima|walls]].]]
The city of Lima was founded by [[Francisco Pizarro]] on January 18, 1535, and given the name '''City of [[Biblical Magi|Kings]]'''.<ref name=ABC>{{Cite news |title=La inmortal flor de la canela |url=http://www.abc.es/especiales/index.asp?cid=7927 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040419115440/http://www.abc.es/especiales/index.asp?cid=7927 |archive-date=2004-04-19 |work=[[ABC (newspaper)|ABC]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Augustin |first=Reinhard |title=El Damero de Pizarro: El trazo y la forja de Lima |url=https://publicacioneslima.pe/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/munilibro-8.pdf |access-date=November 3, 2017 |language=es |year=2017 |publisher=Municipality of Lima |location=Lima |isbn=978-9972-726-13-2 |pages= }}</ref> Nevertheless, its original name persisted with time.

Under the [[Viceroyalty of Peru]], the authority of the [[viceroy]] as a representative of the [[Spanish monarchy]] was particularly important, since its appointment supposed an important ascent and the successful culmination of a race in the colonial administration. The entrances to [[Lima]] of the new viceroys were specially lavish. For the occasion, the streets were paved with silver bars from the [[Gate of Lima|gates of the city]] to the [[Government Palace, Peru|Palace of the Viceroy]].{{Citation needed|date=May 2011}}

In 1988, [[UNESCO]] declared the historic centre of Lima a [[World Heritage Site]] for its originality and high concentration of historic monuments constructed during the viceregal era.<ref name=WHC>{{Cite web |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/500/ |title=Historic Centre of Lima |website=[[World Heritage Convention]] |publisher=[[UNESCO]]}}</ref><ref name=ABC/> In 2023, it was expanded with two exclaves to include the [[Quinta and Molino de Presa]] and the [[Barrios Altos#Viceregal era|Ancient Reduction of Santiago Apostle of Cercado]].<ref name=WHC/>

== Politics ==
The district is the site of the city's [[Palacio Municipal de Lima|Municipal Palace]], which hosts the [[Metropolitan Municipality of Lima]].

=== Subdivisions ===
[[Image:Mapa distrito de Lima.png|thumb|right|Detailed map featuring important avenues.]]
Lima is divided into a number of neighbourhoods and one [[Populated centres of Peru|populated centre]].
*'''[[Cercado de Lima|Central Lima]]''' (known as the ''Cercado de Lima'' or as the ''Damero de Pizarro'') is limited by [[Avenida Alfonso Ugarte|Alfonso Ugarte Avenue]] to the west<ref name=Gonzalete>{{Cite news |title=Avenidas anchas, mentes estrechas |last=Torres |first=Gonzalo |date=2015-08-09 |url=https://elcomercio.pe/lima/avenidas-anchas-mentes-estrechas-gonzalo-torres-192108-noticia/ |work=[[El Comercio (Peru)|El Comercio]] |author-link=Gonzalo Torres (actor)}}</ref> and Huánuco street to the east. It is divided into West and East sides by the pedestrian [[Jirón de la Unión|Union Street]], from which ''cuadras'' ([[city block]]s) are numbered beginning at 100 and changing the first numbers at the next block. The main thoroughfares for cars and [[bus]]es are [[Avenida Tacna|Tacna Avenue]] on the West side and [[Avenida Abancay|Abancay Ave.]] on the East. Both are separated from the Jirón de la Unión by 4 blocks. The [[Plaza Mayor, Lima|Main Square]] is located on block 2 of the Jirón de la Unión, facing the [[Government Palace (Peru)|Government Palace]] and the [[Metropolitan Municipality of Lima]] ([[Palacio Municipal de Lima|City Hall]]).
*'''[[Barrios Altos]]''', which contains the district's oldest structures, is located to the east of the aforementioned centre.<ref name=Gonzalete/> Two cemeteries, [[El Ángel Cemetery|El Ángel]] and [[Cementerio Presbítero Matías Maestro|Presbítero Maestro]], form the eastern border with [[El Agustino]]. Parts of the long-demolished colonial city walls can be seen here. Abutting this to the southwest is the city's [[Chinatown, Lima|Chinatown]], dating back to the mid-1800s.
*'''Monserrate''' is located west of [[Tacna Avenue]],<ref name=Gonzalete/> also contained by [[Avenida Emancipación|Emancipación]] and [[Avenida Alfonso Ugarte|Alfonso Ugarte]] avenues.
*'''[[Santa Beatriz]]''' is located to the south, which contains residential buildings and the ''[[Park of the Reserve|Parque de la Reserva]]''. Santa Beatriz is locally famous for containing the buildings for the state TV network [[TV Perú]], and the top two private [[TV networks]], [[América Televisión]] (Channel 4) and [[Panamericana Televisión]] (Channel 5). Its main thoroughfare is [[Avenida Arequipa|Arequipa Avenue]], a narrow boulevard lined with trees of all sizes. Santa Beatriz is also home to the city (and country)'s main [[association football|football]] [[stadium]], the [[National Stadium of Peru]].
*The '''Industrial Area''', an industrial belt extending into neighbouring Callao Region, is located towards the west, home to the main industries in both city and country. Most of the area is covered by large blocks containing large factories. This area includes the [[University City of the National University of San Marcos]].

Lima is the site of a lone [[Populated centres of Peru|populated centre]] that is coterminous with the district:<ref>{{Cite book |title=Directorio Nacional de Centros Poblados. == Geography ==
The district has a total land [[area]] of 21.98&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. Its administrative centre is located at 154 meters above [[sea level]].

=== Boundaries ===
* North: The [[Rímac River]] marks the district's border with the [[San Martín de Porres District|San Martín de Porres]] and [[Rímac District|Rímac]] districts.
* East:  [[San Juan de Lurigancho]], [[El Agustino]] and [[San Luis District, Lima|San Luis]].
* South: [[La Victoria District, Lima|La Victoria]], [[Lince District|Lince]], [[Jesús María District, Lima|Jesús María]], [[Breña]] and [[Pueblo Libre]].
* West: [[San Miguel District, Lima|San Miguel District]]; and the [[Callao Region]] districts of [[Bellavista District|Bellavista]], [[Callao District|Callao]] and [[Carmen de la Legua Reynoso]].

== Demographics ==
According to a 2005 estimate by the [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática|INEI]], the district has 278,804 inhabitants and a population density of 15,736.9 persons/km<sup>2</sup>. In 1999, there were 75,595 households in the district.

The high point of Lima's religious calendar for the masses is a month of festivities in October dedicated to the [[Lord of Miracles]], during which take place several processions in the city.

== Culture ==
=== Landmarks ===
{{main|Historic Centre of Lima#List of sites}}
The [[Historic Centre of Lima]] is located within the district. The [[World Heritage Site]] declared by [[UNESCO]] in 1988 is divided in three sites, of which two are in the Cercado and Barrios Altos parts of Lima, while one is located in [[Rímac District]].

== Transport ==
The district is serviced by the [[Lima and Callao Metro]], as well as the [[Metropolitano (Lima)|Metropolitano bus system]].

The [[Metropolitano (Lima)|Metropolitano bus system]] operates two separate lines that join at Central Station, located under the [[Paseo de los Héroes Navales|Promenade of the Naval Heroes]].
*Route A runs a circuit along [[Avenida Emancipación]] and [[Jirón Lampa]], with four stations ([[Plaza Ramón Castilla|Ramón Castilla]], [[Avenida Tacna|Tacna]], [[Jirón de la Unión#Bus service|Jirón de la Unión]] and [[Avenida Nicolás de Piérola|Colmena]]).
*Route B runs a circuit along [[Avenida Alfonso Ugarte|Alfonso Ugarte]] and [[Avenida España (Lima)|Spain]] avenues, with three stations ([[Plaza Dos de Mayo|Dos de Mayo]], [[Jirón Quilca|Quilca]] and España).
*Route C, shared with [[La Victoria District, Lima|La Victoria]], travels through the entirety of [[Paseo de la República Avenue]], with two stations (Estadio Nacional and México) located within the district's limits.

As of 2026, the [[Lima and Callao Metro]] operates a single line (Line 1), with a second one under construction. It has two stations ([[Miguel Grau Avenue (Lima)|Grau]] and [[Cementerio Presbítero Matías Maestro|Presbítero Maestro]]), with a third one ([[Avenida 28 de Julio|28 de Julio]]) under construction. Another line (Line 2), also in construction, is projected to have six stations (Tingo María, La Alborada, Elio, San Marcos, 28 de Julio, and Nicolás Ayllón) that meet at a Central Station, to be integrated with that of the Metropolitano.
