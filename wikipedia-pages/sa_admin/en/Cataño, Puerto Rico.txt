'''Cataño''' ({{IPA|es|kaˈtaɲo}}) is a [[Cataño barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] on the northeastern [[coastal plain]] of [[Puerto Rico]]. It is west of the capital [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], east of [[Toa Baja, Puerto Rico|Toa Baja]], north of [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]] and [[Guaynabo, Puerto Rico|Guaynabo]], and south of [[San Juan Bay]]. Part of the [[San Juan–Bayamón–Caguas metropolitan area|San Juan metropolitan area]], Cataño is spread over 7 barrios and the downtown area and administrative center of [[Cataño barrio-pueblo|Cataño Pueblo]]. It is the smallest municipality of Puerto Rico by land area. 

==History==
[[File:Houses in the slum area known as El Fangitto (The Mud) in San Juan, Puerto Rico.tif|thumb|200px|Houses in the slum area known as El Fanguito ({{lang|es|fango}} means mud) in San Juan, Puerto Rico|left]]
[[Hernando de Cataño]] was chosen to offer his medical services in [[Puerto Rico]] during Francisco Bahamonde de Lugo's tenure as [[Governor of Puerto Rico]] (1564–1568). He was one of the first physicians who arrived in [[Puerto Rico]] during its [[colonization]]<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.municipiodecatano.com/included/historia.htm |title=Municipio de Cataño Puerto Rico el Pueblo que se nego a morir, Lancheros de Cataño, Antesalistas de San Juan |access-date=2009-03-31 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090401121923/http://www.municipiodecatano.com/included/historia.htm |archive-date=2009-04-01 |url-status=dead }}</ref> and, upon accepting his position, received as payment a piece of land across the [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] islet. From that time, the region started to be recognized by the name of its original owner. As people started establishing in the area, Cataño was declared as a [[barrio]] of [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]]. However, there wasn't much success in the town's development during these years due to its [[swamp]]-like terrain. Still, around 1690, a hermitage was established to allow residents to receive religious services without having to travel to Bayamón.

In the middle of the 19th century, a ferry company was founded to facilitate the transportation of merchandise and passengers through the [[San Juan Bay]]. This spurred a growth in the population of Cataño, transforming it into one of the most prosperous barrios of Bayamón. Still, attempts to separate themselves from Bayamón in 1839 were unsuccessful. On June 26, 1893, Bishop Antonio Puig y Montserrat separated the barrios of [[Cataño barrio-pueblo|Cataño]], [[Palo Seco, Toa Baja, Puerto Rico|Palo Seco]], and [[Palmas, Cataño, Puerto Rico|Palmas]] from Bayamón's parish and established an independent parish for the residents of these sectors. In 1927, Cataño was officially declared a municipality with the name '''Hato de Palmas de Cataño''', which overtime was simply shortened to Cataño.

Politics played a crucial part in the foundation of the town, since Bayamón was controlled by an administration with opposing ideologies to those of the island's Legislature. The separation of Cataño from Bayamón was a strategy to weaken that opposition.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=08060302&page=2 |title=Municipios: Cataño |access-date=2009-03-31 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110728173011/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=08060302&page=2 |archive-date=2011-07-28 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

With only {{convert|12.5|km2}} of territory, Cataño is the smallest municipality in Puerto Rico. It is less than half the size of [[Hormigueros, Puerto Rico|Hormigueros]], the next-smallest in area.

The people of Cataño were left in despair when [[Hurricane Maria|Hurricane María]] struck on September 20, 2017, destroying their infrastructure and homes. With winds of {{convert|240|km/h}} the hurricane destroyed and flooded an estimated 650 homes and the roads became rivers (flooded).<ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. Cataño, la vida antes y después del huracán María. | trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget. Cataño, life before and after Hurricane María. | newspaper=El Nuevo Día | date=2019-06-13 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/catano/ | language=es | access-date=2021-08-24 | archive-date=September 20, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210920212716/https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/catano/ | url-status=dead }}</ref> An estimated 80% of homes in the Juana Matos area of Cataño were destroyed.<ref>{{cite web | last=Leposa | first=Adam | title=After Hurricane Maria, First Commercial Flight Returns to San Juan | website=Travel Agent Central | date=2017-09-22 | url=https://www.travelagentcentral.com/transportation/after-hurricane-maria-first-commercial-flight-returns-to-san-juan | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref>

===Tourism===
One of the main tourist attractions in Cataño is the [[boardwalk (entertainment district)|boardwalk]] or {{lang|es|tablado}} that commands a view of the San Juan Bay, including views of [[Fort San Felipe del Morro]] on the opposing side. There are several monuments and sculptures along the boardwalk, including a monument to [[Taíno]] culture called "India Taína".

The [[Cathedral of Rum|Bacardi Distillery]] also offers tours of its facilities to visitors who want to learn about the rum manufacturing industry in the island and the [[Caribbean]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.atlasobscura.com/places/casa-bacardi|title=Casa Bacardí|website=Atlas Obscura|access-date=15 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190510132150/https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/casa-bacardi|archive-date=10 May 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

To stimulate local tourism, the [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]] launched the ''Voy Turistiendo'' ("I'm Touring") campaign, with a passport book and website. The Cataño page lists {{lang|es|Lancha de Cataño}}, {{lang|es|Malecón de Cataño}},  and {{lang|es|Casa Bacardí}}, as places of interest.<ref>{{cite book | title= Pasaporte: Voy Turisteando |publisher=Compañia de Turismo de Puerto Rico | year=2021| language=es}}</ref>

====Christopher Columbus statue====
The town gained notoriety in 1998, when Mayor Edwin Rivera Sierra traveled to Russia and acquired a huge statue of [[Christopher Columbus]] called "[[Birth of the New World]]". The statue Columbus by Tsereteli was designed by artist [[Zurab Tsereteli]] and would measure {{convert|350|ft|m}} when erected. Tsereteli had offered the statue to the United States as a gift in 1992 with the intention to use it for the celebrations of the 500th year of its voyage. However, it was rejected. The transportation of the statue from Russia to Cataño cost $2.4&nbsp;million. After arriving on the island, the 2,700 bronze pieces of the statue were scattered in a terrain awaiting for funds for the project, but Rivera Sierra was unable to garner enough public support or funding for it. The statue was installed in [[Arecibo]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.npr.org/2017/10/09/556597003/puerto-ricos-christopher-columbus-statue-survives-hurricane-maria|title=Puerto Rico's Christopher Columbus Statue Survives Hurricane Maria|website=NPR|access-date=15 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180123200652/https://www.npr.org/2017/10/09/556597003/puerto-ricos-christopher-columbus-statue-survives-hurricane-maria|archive-date=23 January 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Sports===
Cataño has a number of professional sports teams{{Citation needed|date=January 2011}}, and there are several important sports facilities located in the town, including the [[Orlando Cepeda|Perucho Cepeda]] Stadium, the Pedro Rodríguez Gaya Boxing Coliseum, and the Cosme Beitía Salamo Coliseum.

==Economy==
[[Image:DSC 0494wc.jpg|thumb|right|225px|Bacardi factory, located in Cataño]]
Due to its location, Cataño has always played an important role as a port to the island.{{Citation needed|date=January 2011}} Fishing has also been a main source of economy for centuries{{Citation needed|date=January 2011}}. [[Bacardi]], one of the largest [[rum]] manufacturers of the world, has a distillery in Cataño.

Other industries established in the town are refineries, commerce companies, transport and logistics, among others.

Despite its small size, Cataño has a large population when compared to municipalities of similar areas. This is perhaps due to its location near the capital of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]]. The population, according to the 2000 census, was 30,071 with a population density of {{convert|6014.2|/mi2|/km2|disp=preunit|people&nbsp;|people|order=flip|}}. Although the current population is almost the double of what it was in the 1950 census, the current census reflects a small decrease of inhabitants.

As a whole, Puerto Rico is populated mainly by people from a [[Mulatto]] (Of African and European descent) and [[Ethnic groups in Europe|European]] descent, with small groups of African and [[Asian people]]. Statistics taken from the 2000 census shows that 66.9% of Catañenses have Spanish or [[white people|White]] ancestry, 8.6% are [[black people|black]], 0.8% are [[Amerindian]] etc.

==Government==
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
After its initial establishment, Cataño belonged to the Bayamón region. From 1839 to 1845, there were some attempts to separate the barrio from Bayamón, but these were unsuccessful. However, on late 19th century, Bishop Antonio Puig y Montserrat managed to separate Cataño establishing their own parish. Cataño was finally declared a municipality on April 25, 1927, being its first mayor Alberto Dávila.

In 1987, Edwin Rivera Sierra was elected as Mayor of Cataño. He remained in the position for 16 years, quitting in 2003. He was replaced by [[Wilson Soto]], who was then officially elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2004|2004 elections in Puerto Rico]]. After losing a reelection bid in 2008 against [[José Rosario]], Soto was indicted on nine charges.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.vocero.com/noticia-17682-cargos_criminales_contra_wilson_soto.html |title=El Vocero – Cargos criminales contra Wilson Soto |access-date=2009-03-31 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090330041625/http://www.vocero.com/noticia-17682-cargos_criminales_contra_wilson_soto.html |archive-date=2009-03-30 }}</ref>

The current mayor of Cataño is [[Julio Alicea Vasallo]], of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP) who has served as mayor since December 19, 2021 after the resignation of former mayor Felix "El Cano" Delgado. Alicea Vasallo was then subsequently elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2024|2024 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district II]], which is represented by two senators. [[Migdalia Padilla]] and [[Carmelo Ríos Santiago]] have served as district senators since 2005.<ref>[http://ceepur.org/elecciones2008/CEE_Events/ELECCIONES_GENERALES_2008_4/ESCRUTINIO_GENERAL_8/default.html Elecciones Generales 2008: Escrutinio General] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111120101807/http://ceepur.org/elecciones2008/CEE_Events/ELECCIONES_GENERALES_2008_4/ESCRUTINIO_GENERAL_8/default.html |date=November 20, 2011 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag consists of nine horizontal stripes: four blue stripes and five white stripes (substituting for the silver color on the coat of arms). A white and green band traverses diagonally the drape in all its extension, from the upper hoist to the lower fly.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=CATAÑO | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/CATANO.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001100/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/CATANO.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=16 September 2020}}</ref>

The green color represents the [[palm tree]]s that are also present in the [[coat of arms]]. The flag was officially adopted during José Alvarez Brunet's tenure as mayor on September 5, 1974.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

===Coat of arms===
The coat of arms of Cataño consists of nine horizontal stripes of same the width: four blue and five silver. The colors of the coat and the flag represent the coat of arms of the family of Don Hernando de Cataño, an [[Hidalgo (Spanish nobility)|Hidalgo]] to whom the town owes its name. The color silver represents nobility and the color blue was used by hidalgos on their armories. It symbolizes royalty and serenity.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

On top of the coat of arms, there's a crown with three towers distinct of others coat of arms. The coat itself is surrounded by two green [[palm tree]]s, an allusion to one of the original names of the town: Hato de las Palmas de Cataño.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

===Name===
Aside of its name, derived from its original owner, Cataño has several [[nicknames]]. The city is known as "La Antesala de la Capital" (the [[Foyer]] of the Capital) because of its location across the bay from the capital-city of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]]. In the 1960s, the residents of Cataño jokingly called it "Fanguito Town" because of its 
many muddy streets and shacks built on stilts over tidal flats.<ref>{{cite book | url=https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B_q6VhhkczIYcnRmRFpacEVjblU | title=Slums and social insecurity, an appraisal of the effectiveness of housing policies in helping to eliminate poverty in the United States | publisher=Washington, U. S. Govt. Print. Office | author=Schorr, Alvin Louis | year=1963 | location=Washington DC | pages=8}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal | url=https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B_q6VhhkczIYXzhXaDhyam5nSEE | title=Caribbean Negritos Ramón Rivero, Blackface, and "Black" Voice in Puerto Rico | author=Rivero, Yeidy M. | journal=Television & New Media | year=2004 | volume=5 | issue=4 | pages=315–3 | doi=10.1177/1527476404268424| s2cid=144630039 | url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref>{{cite journal | url=https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B_q6VhhkczIYSWJjbVprSkZDLVU | title=Review: Review Essay: Estudios queer puertorriqueños | author=Torres, Daniel | journal=Chasqui | year=2009 | volume=38 | issue=2 | pages=141–147}}</ref><ref>{{cite book | url=https://archive.org/details/TheCrimeOfElFanguitoAnOpenLetterToPresidentTrumanOnPuertoRico | title=The crime of El Fanguito : an open letter to President Truman on Puerto Rico | publisher=New Century Publishers | author=Foster, William Z. | year=1948 | location=New York | access-date=2017-08-20 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160317043129/https://archive.org/details/TheCrimeOfElFanguitoAnOpenLetterToPresidentTrumanOnPuertoRico | archive-date=2016-03-17 | url-status=live }}</ref>

==Education==
Cataño has several public and private schools managed by the [[Puerto Rico Department of Education]].

==Transportation==
[[File:PARTIAL ELEVATION OF NORTHWEST PARAPET - Puente Blanco Bridge, Spanning Mosquito Control Channel, Barrio Palmas neighborhood, Catano, Catano Municipio, PR.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Puente Blanco Bridge, Barrio Palmas neighborhood, Cataño, Cataño Municipio, PR]]
[[Puerto Rico Highway 22]] provides access to Cataño from [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] or from other adjacent towns. Like most other towns in the island, it has a public transportation system consisting of public cars. Taxis are also available around the town.

Cataño also has a ferry service known as ''[[La Lancha de Cataño]]'', or the Ferry of Cataño. The ferry service, which has been working since 1853, operates a five-minute harbor route between Cataño and [[Old San Juan]], and vice versa daily. There is a large ferry terminal at Cataño, and tourists can enjoy the view of the [[El Morro Castle|Castillo del Morro]] and the large cruise ships docked at the old San Juan terminal during this journey.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/largahistoriaenlalanchadecatano-2196423/|title=Larga historia en la lancha de Cataño|newspaper=[[El Nuevo Día]]|first=Leysa|last=Caro|date=May 8, 2016|access-date=May 8, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160509112325/http://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/largahistoriaenlalanchadecatano-2196423/|archive-date=May 9, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

There are 16 bridges in Cataño.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Cataño Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/catano/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=20 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190221224045/http://bridgereports.com/pr/catano/|archive-date=21 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>
