'''Piracicaba''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|piɾɐsiˈkabɐ|lang}} {{IPA|pt-BR|piˌɾasiˈkabɐ|label=or}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the [[Interior of São Paulo|interior]] of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo state]], in the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]] of Brazil. It serves as the main city of the Metropolitan Region of Piracicaba (RMP) and is situated approximately {{convert|150|km|mi|abbr=on}} northwest of the state capital, [[São Paulo]]. Covering an area of just over {{convert|1378|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}, with around {{convert|169|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} classified as [[urban area]], Piracicaba has a population of 438,827 inhabitants, making it the [[List of municipalities in São Paulo|13th most populous municipality]] in São Paulo state.

Established in 1767 along the banks of the [[Piracicaba River (São Paulo)|Piracicaba River]], a vital water source for the region, Piracicaba saw significant agricultural development during the 19th century, particularly in sugarcane and coffee cultivation. However, the early 20th century brought economic decline due to the collapse of the [[Brazilian coffee cycle|coffee cycle]] and falling sugar prices, a situation that persisted until the onset of industrialization.

Piracicaba was among the first Brazilian cities to industrialize, with the establishment of factories in the metalworking and sugar production equipment sectors. This industrial activity expanded significantly in the 1970s with the [[Pró-Álcool]] program, which promoted the production of [[ethanol fuel|ethanol]] for automotive use in response to the [[1973 oil crisis|1973 global oil crisis]]. This initiative spurred substantial industrial growth in Piracicaba over subsequent decades, positioning it as the 34th largest GDP in Brazil in 2021. Today, it is a key industrial hub in the region and home to several universities.

Beyond its economic significance, Piracicaba is a prominent cultural center in its region. The Tupi Forest Reserve and Ártemis Spa are major environmental preservation areas, while Professor Phillipe Westin Park and parks along the Piracicaba River are notable urban attractions. The International Humor Exhibition of Piracicaba, held annually at the Central Mill, is one of the world's most significant [[cartoon]] events. The Central Mill, a former sugarcane mill, is now a protected historical and cultural site, serving as a venue for cultural, artistic, and recreational activities.

== Etymology ==
[[File:Etimologia de Piracicaba.svg|thumb|left|In [[Tupi language|Tupi]]: ''pirá sykaba''. ''Syk'' means "to arrive". Thus, ''syk-aba'' means "place of arrival". This term is in a [[Genitive case|genitive relationship]] with ''pirá'', meaning fish.]]
The name of the municipality derives from the [[Paulista General Language]], meaning "the place where fish arrive," formed by combining the terms ''pirá'' ("fish"), ''syk'' ("arrive"), ''ab'' (a suffix indicating the place where something occurs), and ''-a'' (a nominalizing suffix).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fflch.usp.br/dlcv/tupi/vocabulario.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FGXV9Wj?url=http://www.fflch.usp.br/dlcv/tupi/vocabulario.htm |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |title=Vocabulário tupi-português das lições |author=[[University of São Paulo]] (USP) |access-date=June 7, 2012 |language=pt}}</ref> It refers to the rapids of the [[Piracicaba River (São Paulo)|Piracicaba River]], which obstruct the migration (''[[piracema]]'') of fish.<ref name="História">{{cite web |url=http://www.urbal.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/caracteriza%E7%E3o/caracteriza%E7%E3o,%20historia%20e%20histsan/piracicabadireito/historiaecultpiracicaba.htm |title=Piracicaba/BR |date=August 29, 2006 |access-date=February 15, 2022 |author=URB-AL |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FEcEioN?url=http://www.urbal.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/caracteriza%E7%E3o/caracteriza%E7%E3o,%20historia%20e%20histsan/piracicabadireito/historiaecultpiracicaba.htm |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |language=pt}}</ref>

Notably, according to [[Eduardo de Almeida Navarro|Eduardo Navarro]] in his ''[[Dictionary of Old Tupi|Dicionário de Tupi Antigo]]'' (2013), the term originates from the [[Paulista General Language|General Language]], a historical development of [[Tupi language|Old Tupi]] and thus more recent. In Old Tupi, ''syk'' meant arriving by land (arriving by water, as with fish, would be ''îepotar''). The naming of Piracicaba in the 18th century aligns with the period when the General Language was spoken, rather than Old or Classical Tupi.<ref>{{cite book |last=Navarro |first=Eduardo de Almeida |title=Dicionário de Tupi Antigo |trans-title=Dictionary of Old Tupi |language=pt |date=2013 |page= |publisher=Global Editora |oclc= |isbn=9788526019331}}</ref>

=== Settlement and creation of the municipality ===
[[File:Almeida Júnior - Paisagem do Rio Piracicaba.JPG|thumb|left|''Landscape of the Piracicaba River'' by [[Almeida Júnior]]]]
The Piracicaba River Valley began to be settled by European descendants in the 17th century, as colonists ventured into the forest and occupied lands around the river, engaging in subsistence agriculture and timber extraction. The first confirmed record of a ''sesmaria'' (land grant) in the Piracicaba region dates to 1726, though there is evidence of a ''sesmaria'' granted as early as 1693 to Pedro de Morais Cavalcanti. Granted to Felipe Cardoso from Itu, the 1726 ''sesmaria'' was located about one kilometer from the Piracicaba River rapids, attracting various settlers and landholders to the area, which was partly traversed by roads leading to the village of [[Itu, São Paulo|Itu]] and the [[Cuiabá]] mines.<ref name=":2">{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> This small rural community is depicted in the "Map of the Captaincy of São Paulo," produced by Genoese cartographer Francesco Tosi Colombina in the 1750s, which shows a locality called the "new settlement of Piracicaba" on the right bank of the river.<ref>{{cite web |last=Colombina |first=Francesco Tosi |url=http://objdigital.bn.br/acervo_digital/div_cartografia/cart1033415/cart1033415.html |title=Mappa da capitania de S. Paulo, e seu sertão em que devem os descobertos, que lhe forão tomados para Minas Geraes, como tambem o camiho de Goyazes, com todos os seus pouzos, e passagens |trans-title=Map of the Captaincy of São Paulo and Its Hinterland, Including Discoveries Taken for Minas Gerais, as Well as the Route to Goiás, with All Its Wells and Passages |language=pt |date=c. 1750 |access-date=September 15, 2022 |website=Biblioteca Digital Luso-Brasileira |publication-date=2012 }}{{Dead link|date=March 2026 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}</ref>
[[File:Trecho do Mappa da Capitania de São Paulo c. 1750.jpg|thumb|310px|Excerpt from the Map of the Captaincy of São Paulo, produced in the 1750s by Francesco Tosi Colombina, showing the "New Settlement of Piracicaba."]]

In 1766, the governor of the [[Captaincy of São Paulo]], Dom Luís António de Sousa Botelho Mourão, decided to establish a settlement to support navigation along the [[Tietê River]] toward the [[Paraná River]] and to provide logistical support for the Fort of Iguatemi, located near the future border with [[Paraguay]].<ref>{{cite book |title=Autoridade e Conflito no Brasil Colonial: o governo do Morgado de Mateus em São Paulo |trans-title=Authority and Conflict in Colonial Brazil: The Government of Morgado de Mateus in São Paulo |language=pt |last=Bellotto |first=Heloísa |publisher=Secretaria de Estado de Cultura de São Paulo |year=2007 |location=São Paulo}}</ref> The settlement was intended for the confluence of the Piracicaba and Tietê rivers, near present-day [[Santa Maria da Serra]], but Captain Antônio Correa Barbosa, tasked with the mission, chose a site between seventy<ref>{{cite web |last=Bragança |first=Fábio |url=https://www.camarapiracicaba.sp.gov.br/1-de-agosto-de-1767-fundacao-de-piracicaba-31616 |title="1° de Agosto de 1767 - Fundação de Piracicaba" |trans-title="August 1, 1767 - Founding of Piracicaba" |language=pt |date=August 1, 2016 |access-date=September 15, 2022 |website=Câmara Municipal de Piracicaba }}</ref> and ninety<ref name=":1" /> kilometers from the confluence, already occupied by settlers and offering better access to other regional villages, particularly Itu. The settlement of Piracicaba was founded on August 1, 1767, on the right bank of the river, near the future location of the Central Mill and parts of Vila Rezende. As was customary in colonial times, settlements authorized to build a [[chapel]] were dedicated to a Christian saint, with Our Lady of Pleasures initially chosen, later changed to [[Anthony of Padua|Saint Anthony]].<ref name=":1" /><ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mário |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |pages=61–62 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> Politically, the settlement was linked to Itu, the nearest village. In 1774, it was elevated to ''[[freguesia]]'' status, with about 230 inhabitants and 45 houses, according to a 1775 census.<ref name="História"/><ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=66 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref>

In 1777, the Fort of Iguatemi was captured by the [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]], part of a broader military campaign that included the conquest of the [[Colonia del Sacramento|Colony of Sacramento]] in present-day [[Uruguay]] and the surrender of Portuguese fortresses on Santa Catarina Island.<ref>{{cite journal |title=Fortificações no Brazil |trans-title=Fortifications in Brazil |language=pt |date=1885 |journal=Revista do Instituto Histórico e Geográfico Brasileiro |publisher=Instituto Histórico e Geográfico Brasileiro |issue=Tomo XLVIII |last=Sousa |first=Augusto Fausto de |pages=5–140 |volume=Part 2}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=As Defesas da Ilha de Santa Catarina no Brasil-Colônia |trans-title=The Defenses of Santa Catarina Island in Colonial Brazil |language=pt |last=Cabral |first=Oswaldo |publisher=Conselho Federal de Cultura |year=1972 |location=Rio de Janeiro}}</ref> Consequently, the ''freguesia'' of Piracicaba likely experienced indirect impacts, as supporting the fort was a key local economic activity.<ref>{{cite book |title=Enciclopédia Delta Júnior |trans-title=Delta Júnior Encyclopedia |language=pt |publisher=Editora Delta |year=1967 |volume=10 |location=Rio de Janeiro |pages=1410–1411}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=78 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> Additionally, conflicts between Antonio Correa Barbosa and the first parish priest, Father João Manuel da Silva, led to the latter's transfer request in 1776, leaving Piracicaba without religious services for the next eight years.<ref name=":3" /> Tensions between the local population and Correa Barbosa were reportedly frequent, culminating in a collective petition against the so-called Founder of Piracicaba to the captain-general of the Captaincy of São Paulo in 1786.<ref name=":2" />

The uneven and infertile terrain on the left bank of the river prompted the relocation of the ''freguesia'' seat to the right bank in 1784. Historian Joaquim Silveira Melo notes, “With the abandonment of the Iguatemi colony, the reasons for the settlement’s existence on the right bank of the Piracicaba diminished; there was no longer a need for a shipyard to build canoes or for the river to serve as a barrier preventing soldiers and exiles from taking the road to Itu at night. Thus, Captain Director António Corrêa Barbosa and Vicar Friar Tomé de Jesus sent a petition to Captain-General Francisco da Cunha Menezes on June 6, 1784, requesting the settlement’s relocation to the opposite side, on the left bank.”<ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=83 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> In the following years, a new church and residences were built in the chosen area, part of Felipe Cardoso’s former ''sesmaria'', with agriculture expanding due to the fertile red soils on the left bank.<ref name=":4">{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mário |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=106 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> Records show multiple ''sesmarias'' granted in Piracicaba in 1795, indicating the expansion of cultivated areas around the ''freguesia''.<ref name=":4" />

In the early 19th century, the region developed through river navigation and [[sugarcane]] cultivation, heavily reliant on [[Slavery in Brazil|enslaved African labor]].<ref name="História" /><ref name=":0" /> In 1808, the ''freguesia''’s layout was planned under the orders of Captain-General Franca e Horta, with uniformly wide streets forming right angles and regular blocks to facilitate the demarcation of public and private lands.<ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mário |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |pages=132–133 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> In 1821, the ''freguesia'' was elevated to town status, named ''Vila Nova da Constituição'' in honor of the [[Portuguese Constitution of 1822]], then under approval. The new town was formed from territories previously belonging to the towns of Itu and Porto Feliz.<ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=110 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> With its new status and the growth of sugarcane cultivation, Vila Nova da Constituição experienced a significant economic boom. On August 11, 1822, the first meeting of the city council was held.<ref name="História" />
[[File:Engenho Piracicaba.jpg|thumb|left|Central Mill of Piracicaba, founded in 1881.]]

Piracicaba grew rapidly, becoming the main city in the region and influencing other towns that later became the cities of [[São Pedro, São Paulo|São Pedro]], [[Limeira]], [[Capivari]], [[Rio Claro, São Paulo|Rio Claro]], and [[Santa Bárbara d'Oeste]]. The city remained focused on [[sugarcane]] cultivation, overlooking the rise of coffee in Western São Paulo, which became the driving force of the São Paulo economy by the late 19th century. Due to sugarcane, the region became a major [[slavery|slaveholding]] hub in Western São Paulo, with a significant presence of enslaved and freed Black individuals.<ref name="História2">{{cite web |url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=8657&idContentSection=755 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FEdPcB0?url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=8657&idContentSection=755 |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |title=História de Piracicaba é definida pelo rio |trans-title=Piracicaba’s History Is Defined by the River |language=pt |author=José Pedro Martins |date=February 29, 2008 |publisher=A Província |access-date=May 1, 2012}}</ref> According to 1817 records, when Piracicaba was still a ''freguesia'', local farms and [[Engenho|sugarcane mills]] held about 890 enslaved Black individuals.<ref>{{cite book |url=http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |title=História da Fundação de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of the Founding of Piracicaba |language=pt |last=Neme |first=Mario |publisher=Equilíbrio/IHGP |year=2009 |location=Piracicaba |page=134 |isbn=9788561237202 |archive-date=2023-02-24 |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230224224532/http://www.ihgp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Historias-da-Fundacao-de-Piracicaba.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> By 1887, on the eve of the ''[[Lei Áurea]]'', approximately 5,663 [[African people|Africans]] and [[Afro-Brazilians]] were enslaved in Piracicaba, making it the third city—then with about 22,000 free inhabitants—with the most slaves in the [[Province of São Paulo]].<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{cite book |title=Almanach da Província de São Paulo Administrativo, Commercial e Industrial para 1888 |trans-title=Almanac of the Province of São Paulo Administrative, Commercial, and Industrial for 1888 |language=pt |last=Seckler |first=Jorge |publisher=Jorge Seckler & Comp. |year=1888 |location=São Paulo |page=543}}</ref> However, in 1847, Senator [[Nicolau Pereira de Campos Vergueiro]] (known as Senator Vergueiro), owner of the [[Ibicaba Farm]] (then part of Piracicaba’s territory), pioneered the use of waged immigrant labor to replace enslaved Africans, hiring Swiss and German families.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fazendaibicaba.com.br/r.php?l=historia |title=História |trans-title=History |language=pt |access-date=September 15, 2022}}</ref>

In 1877, Piracicaba received a railroad connection through the Companhia Ytuana de Estradas de Ferro to [[Itu, São Paulo|Itu]] and [[Jundiaí]], via [[Capivari]] and [[Indaiatuba]]. That same year, through the efforts of its then-councilor and future [[President of Brazil]], [[Prudente de Morais]], the city adopted the name "Piracicaba," abandoning the Portuguese designation ''Vila Nova da Constituição''.<ref name="História2"/> In 1881, the Central Mill of Piracicaba, founded on the banks of the Piracicaba River, became one of Brazil’s largest [[sugarcane mill]]s in the following years.<ref name="História2"/>

=== 20th and 21st centuries ===
[[File:Piracicaba-SP.jpg|thumb|The city today, with the [[Piracicaba River (São Paulo)|Piracicaba River]] in the foreground, along whose banks it developed.]]
By 1900, Piracicaba had established itself as one of the largest hubs in [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo state]]: it was the fourth largest city in the state, had electric lighting, telephone services, and, on land donated by Luiz Vicente de Sousa Queiroz, the foundation of the future [[College of Agriculture, University of São Paulo|Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture]] was begun. In 1922, 45 years after the arrival of the Companhia Ytuana railway, Piracicaba received a Companhia Paulista railway branch.<ref name="Estações_Piracicaba">{{Cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/p/piracicaba-cp.htm |title=Piracicaba Paulista |author=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |date=21 June 2011 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BORY7Ki?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/p/piracicaba-cp.htm |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

However, Piracicaba entered a prolonged period of stagnation and slight decline that affected the city for much of the 20th century. The end of the [[Brazilian coffee cycle|coffee cycle]] and the persistent drop in sugar prices caused the local economy to stagnate. In an attempt to reverse this, Piracicaba was among the first Brazilian cities to industrialize, with the opening of factories in the metalworking and sugar production equipment sectors. Industrialization, still heavily tied to the sugarcane cycle, prevented a steeper decline but did not halt stagnation. From the second half of the 20th century, the city faced another challenge: the growth of [[Campinas]] and its surrounding area (now the [[Campinas metropolitan area|Campinas Metropolitan Region]]).<ref name="ABG_Gênese">{{cite web |url=http://www.agb.org.br/evento/download.php?idTrabalho=1530 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FGJiMJk?url=http://www.agb.org.br/evento/download.php?idTrabalho=1530 |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |title=A gênese industrial do município de Piracicaba |trans-title=The Industrial Genesis of the Municipality of Piracicaba |language=pt |author=Lucas Possedente Emerique and Nathalia Bernardinetti |date=July 30, 2010 |publisher=Associação dos Geógrafos Brasileiros (ABG) |access-date=June 7, 2012}}</ref>
[[File:Vista aérea de Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|Aerial view of the municipality’s central region]]

From the 1970s, initiatives were launched to revitalize Piracicaba’s economy. The [[SP-308 (São Paulo highway)|Sugar Highway]] was built, connecting the city to the [[Rodovia Castelo Branco|Castelo Branco Highway]], providing a new route for product transport and maintaining Piracicaba’s influence in the [[Capivari]] region. The [[Rodovia Luís de Queiróz|Luiz de Queiroz Highway]] was widened to the [[Rodovia Anhanguera|Anhanguera Highway]], improving access and linking Piracicaba to São Paulo’s main interior highway. Industrial districts were established, and new companies arrived. Concurrently, the federal [[Pró-Álcool]] program, promoting automotive ethanol from sugarcane, modernized cultivation and revitalized the sugarcane industry.<ref name="FUMEP_História">{{cite web |url=http://portal.eep.br/EEP/cursos_histor.php?curso=tec |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FGLuq8r?url=http://portal.eep.br/EEP/cursos_histor.php?curso=tec |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |title=Hitórico |trans-title=History |language=pt |author=Fundação Municipal de Educação de Piracicaba (FUMEP) |access-date=June 7, 2012}}</ref> Other projects, such as the Santa Maria da Serra Dam (intended to resume river navigation and connect to the Paraná-Tietê Waterway), an ethanol pipeline, and a closer alignment with the [[Anhanguera Highway]], were not realized. Instead, the [[Rodovia dos Bandeirantes|Bandeirantes Highway]] was extended, passing through [[Santa Bárbara d'Oeste]]. Despite these setbacks, Piracicaba diversified its economy, overcoming stagnation and attracting significant investments over the last two decades.<ref name="ABG_Gênese"/><ref name="FUMEP_História"/>

On March 29, 2006, Piracicaba was struck by a historic [[supercell]] storm, accompanied by a tornado in the area of the Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture (Esalq) campus. Numerous trees were felled, with the Municipal Government reporting about 2,700 trees (3% of the city’s total) damaged, including approximately 500 on the [[College of Agriculture, University of São Paulo|Esalq]] campus and 400 on the Taquaral campus of the [[Methodist University of Piracicaba]] (UNIMEP). A [[state of emergency]] was declared for 60 days, with urban damages estimated at over R$1.5 million. Sugarcane production was severely affected, with 350,000 tons lost. Data from the Esalq weather station recorded maximum winds of 158.4&nbsp;km/h.<ref name=":02">{{cite journal |url=https://www.revistas.usp.br/geousp/article/view/74083 |title=CONDICIONANTES FÍSICOS E IMPACTOS DOS TORNADOS DO FINAL DE MARÇO DE 2006 NO INTERIOR PAULISTA |trans-title=Physical Conditions and Impacts of the Late March 2006 Tornadoes in the São Paulo Interior |language=pt |date=December 30, 2008 |access-date=May 8, 2023 |journal=GEOUSP Espaço e Tempo (Online) |issue=2 |last1=Nunes |first1=Luci Hidalgo |last2=Candido |first2=Daniel Henrique |pages=99–124 |doi=10.11606/issn.2179-0892.geousp.2008.74083 |issn=2179-0892}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://piracicaba.sp.gov.br/noticias/temporal-limpeza-deve-terminar-em-junho/ |title=TEMPORAL: Limpeza deve terminar em junho |trans-title=STORM: Cleanup Expected to Finish in June |language=pt |access-date=March 2, 2025 |website=Portal do Município de Piracicaba}}</ref>
[[File:Panorama Usina Costa Pinto Piracicaba SAO 10 2008.jpg|thumb|left|300px|View of the Costa Pinto Mill in Piracicaba, a factory that produces [[sugar]], [[ethanol]], and [[Alcohol (chemistry)|alcohol]].]]

The municipality has shown strong development indicators, reclaiming degraded areas and investing in [[biotechnology]] and export products for future growth. In 2012, Piracicaba had the second largest population and third largest economy in the Administrative Region of Campinas (surpassed only by [[Campinas]] and [[Jundiaí]]), ranking among the world’s top sugar and ethanol production hubs, with a significant industrial center and renowned universities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sp.senai.br/piracicabavila/Webforms/Interna.aspx?secao_id=47 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68FGMp1C7?url=http://www.sp.senai.br/piracicabavila/Webforms/Interna.aspx?secao_id=47 |archive-date=June 7, 2012 |title=Histórico de Piracicaba |trans-title=History of Piracicaba |language=pt |author=National Service for Industrial Training (SENAI) |access-date=June 7, 2012}}</ref>

In 2012, the city welcomed a [[Hyundai Motor Company|Hyundai]] factory, generating thousands of jobs and transforming the region, significantly boosting its industrial sector and making it one of the most prominent in São Paulo’s interior. According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], Piracicaba’s gross domestic product in 2012 was R$11.9 billion.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=353870&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7cpiracicaba%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Piracicaba |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Piracicaba |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> Other microregion seats, [[Limeira]] and [[Rio Claro, São Paulo|Rio Claro]], had GDPs of R$7.7 billion<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=352690&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7climeira%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Limeira |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Limeira |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> and R$5.8 billion,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=354390&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7crio-claro%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Rio Claro |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Rio Claro |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> respectively, in 2012. Neighboring mesoregion seats [[Campinas]], [[Ribeirão Preto]], and [[Araraquara]] had GDPs of R$42.8 billion,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=350950&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7ccampinas%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Campinas |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Campinas |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> R$20.3 billion,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=354340&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7cribeirao-preto%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Ribeirão Preto |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Ribeirão Preto |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> and R$5.7 billion,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=350320&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7cararaquara%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012 |title=IBGE Cidades - Araraquara |trans-title=IBGE Cities - Araraquara |language=pt |author=IBGE |access-date=June 30, 2012}}</ref> respectively. In June 2012, the Piracicaba Urban Agglomeration was established, listed by the IBGE in 2014 among Brazil’s 25 largest administrative regions by population. By 2015, it ranked [[List of metropolitan areas in Brazil|20th]], with 1.412 million inhabitants.

== Geography ==
[[File:Piracicaba (2).jpg|thumb|Partial view of the city with the Piracicaba River running through it. The urban area has a flatter and slightly undulating terrain.]]
The municipality of Piracicaba covers an area of {{convert|1378.069|km2|sqmi}},<ref name="IBGE_Piracicaba"/> of which {{convert|168.824|km2|sqmi}} is classified as urban.<ref name="Embrapa_Áreaurbana2015"/> According to the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] established by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) in 2017, Piracicaba belongs to the Intermediate Geographic Region of Campinas and the Immediate Geographic Region of Piracicaba.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Divisão Regional do Brasil |trans-title=Regional Division of Brazil |language=pt |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=29 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170905163107/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=5 September 2017}}</ref>

Piracicaba is located at 22°43′30″ south [[latitude]] and 47°38′56″ west [[longitude]],<ref name="Embrapa_SP">{{cite web|url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/sp.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67RMbAKXh?url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/sp.html |archive-date=5 May 2012 |title=São Paulo |trans-title=São Paulo |language=pt |publisher=Embrapa Monitoramento por Satélite|author-link=Embrapa Monitoramento por Satélite |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref> approximately 152 kilometers northwest of the state capital, [[São Paulo]]. It shares borders with the following municipalities: [[Saltinho, São Paulo|Saltinho]], [[Laranjal Paulista]], [[Rio das Pedras, São Paulo|Rio das Pedras]], and [[Tietê, São Paulo|Tietê]] to the south; [[Santa Bárbara d'Oeste]] to the southeast; [[Limeira]] and [[Iracemápolis]] to the east; [[Rio Claro, São Paulo|Rio Claro]] to the northeast; [[São Pedro, São Paulo|São Pedro]], [[Charqueada]], and [[Ipeúna]] to the north; [[Santa Maria da Serra]] to the northwest; [[Anhembi]] to the west; and [[Conchas]] to the southwest.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ipplap.com.br/docs/MAPA%20DE%20PIRACICABA%20E%20MUNICIPIOS%20VIZINHOS.pdf |title=Mapa de Piracicaba e municípios vizinhos |trans-title=Map of Piracicaba and neighboring municipalities |language=pt |publisher=Instituto de Pesquisas e Planejamento de Piracicaba (IPPLAP) |access-date=30 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210730205247/http://www.ipplap.com.br/docs/MAPA%20DE%20PIRACICABA%20E%20MUNICIPIOS%20VIZINHOS.pdf |archive-date=30 July 2021}}</ref>

=== Geomorphology and hydrography ===
[[File:Rio Piracicaba 2.jpg|thumb|Piracicaba River]]
[[File:Vista do Centro, a partir da Av. Pres. Kennedy - panoramio.jpg|thumb|View of the city center from Presidente Kennedy Avenue.]]
The terrain of Piracicaba is predominantly rugged, with a notable depression in the central part of its territory.<ref name="Ipef_Rural">{{cite web |author=Barreto |first=Alberto Pereira G. O. |last2=Sparovek |first2=Gerd |last3=Giannotti |first3=Mariana |date=2006 |title=Atlas Rural de Piracicaba |trans-title=Rural Atlas of Piracicaba |url=http://www.ipef.br/publicacoes/atlasrural/Atlas_Rural_de_Piracicaba_2006.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304031328/http://www.ipef.br/publicacoes/atlasrural/Atlas_Rural_de_Piracicaba_2006.pdf |archive-date=4 March 2016 |access-date=22 May 2012 |publisher=Instituto de Pesquisas e Estudos Florestais (IPEF) |language=pt}}</ref> This depression extends along the east-west course of the [[Piracicaba River (São Paulo)|Piracicaba River]], becoming more pronounced within the urban area at the river's waterfall. This region separates the watersheds of the Piracicaba and Tietê rivers.<ref name="Ipef_Rural"/> Valleys converging toward the [[Tietê River]] in the southwest and the Piracicaba River in the central region trend north-northwest, creating more pronounced depressions in these areas. The lowest elevations are approximately 420 meters, while the highest average around 780 meters. The average altitude of Piracicaba is 528 meters, situated in a significantly sloped area with an average slope of 7.8%.<ref name="Ipef_Rural"/>

Piracicaba exhibits a wide variety of soils, some of which are highly fertile, supporting the municipality's agricultural activities. This diversity results from the combination of different geological materials and the rugged terrain.<ref name="Ipef_Rural"/> The predominant soil types are [[latosols]], which have medium to clayey textures, are dense, and capable of retaining water. Their fertility varies but is generally low, requiring fertilization and soil management to achieve high productivity. In the northern, central, and southern regions, [[podzolic]] soils are prevalent, mixed with other types such as brunizems, [[leptosol]]s, and [[cambisols]], which are widely used in agriculture. In the western part of Piracicaba, soils are highly sandy, with low fertility, limited water retention capacity, and a propensity for erosion.<ref name="Ipef_Rural"/>

The terrain's ruggedness directly influences the drainage network, which, in turn, can alter the surface configuration. The hydrographic networks are more concentrated in the central and northern parts of Piracicaba, with a total drainage length of {{convert|2139|km|mi}} across the municipality. The main rivers flowing through the territory are the Piracicaba, Tietê, and [[Corumbataí River (Paraná)|Corumbataí]].<ref name="Ipef_Rural"/>

[[File:Antes da tempestade.jpg|thumb|right|Storm approaching Piracicaba on a spring night]]

=== Environment ===
[[File:Arco-íris Piracicaba.jpg|thumb|left|[[Rainbow]] in Piracicaba]]
The original and predominant vegetation in Piracicaba is the [[Atlantic Forest]], with remaining fragments of various [[phytogeographic]] units, including [[seasonal semideciduous forest]], [[swamp forest]], and seasonal deciduous forest. Some areas, particularly those with sandy soils prone to erosion, exhibit characteristics of ''[[cerrado]]''. The presence of these vegetation types is due to the [[edaphic]] characteristics, as well as the geological, geomorphological, and hydrological features of specific areas.<ref name="IPEF_vegetação">{{cite web|url=http://www.ipef.br/publicacoes/ctecnica/nr189.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rG3K9qR?url=http://www.ipef.br/publicacoes/ctecnica/nr189.pdf |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=A vegetação de Piracicaba e municípios do entorno |trans-title=The vegetation of Piracicaba and surrounding municipalities |language=pt |author=Ricardo Ribeiro Rodrigues |date=August 1999 |publisher=Instituto de Pesquisas e Estudos Florestais (IPEF) |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref>

Areas dominated by seasonal semideciduous forest were once prevalent but have been largely destroyed due to agricultural expansion, with remaining fragments limited to environmental preservation areas or inaccessible locations. Timber extraction during the 20th century, used for construction or railway development, further reduced these forests. [[Riparian forest]]s are primarily found along watercourses. Broadleaf hygrophilous forests occur in waterlogged soils with near-permanent flooding, restricted to areas with strong hydrological influence. Seasonal deciduous forests are found on litholic (typically shallow) and highly acidic soils with low water retention capacity during the dry season. ''Cerrado'' areas, located away from watercourses, particularly in elevated parts of the peripheral depression and the western plateau, are often mistaken for pastures or eroded areas.<ref name="IPEF_vegetação"/>

According to the [[2022 Brazilian census]] conducted by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), Piracicaba’s population was {{formatnum:423323}} inhabitants, with a population density of {{convert|307.19|PD/km2|PD/sqmi}}.<ref name="IBGE_Piracicaba"/>

The [[2010 Brazilian census]] reported {{formatnum:178538}} male and {{formatnum:186334}} female inhabitants. === Poverty and inequality ===
[[File:Comunidade Pantanal, Piracicaba - fev 2023.jpg|thumb|The Pantanal Community, located in the Jardim Itapuã neighborhood, is one of Piracicaba’s slums.]]
[[File:Casas de alto padrão em Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|Residential upper-middle class neighborhood in the city]]
According to IBGE data from 2003, Piracicaba’s [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.40, where 1.00 represents the highest inequality and 0.00 the lowest.<ref name="GINI" /> In that year, the poverty incidence, as measured by IBGE, was 15.24%, with a lower limit of 11.43%, an upper limit of 19.05%, and a subjective poverty incidence of 11.81%.<ref name="GINI">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=353870&nomemun=Piracicaba |title=Indicadores sociais dos municípios brasileiros |trans-title=Social indicators of Brazilian municipalities |language=pt |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |access-date=19 April 2011}}</ref> From 1991 to 2010, the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to half the minimum wage decreased by 7.0%. In 2010, 86.4% of the population lived above the poverty line, 8.4% were at the poverty line, and 5.1% were below it.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/PDF/gera_PDF.php?cidade=52400 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOPm6K8?url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/PDF/gera_PDF.php?cidade=52400 |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=Perfil municipal — Piracicaba - SP |trans-title=Municipal Profile — Piracicaba - SP |language=pt |publisher=Portal ODM |date=2010 |access-date=12 May 2012}}</ref> In 2000, the top 20% of the city’s wealthiest population accounted for 58.8% of the total municipal income, 19 times higher than the 3.1% share of the bottom 20%. In 1991, the bottom 20% had a 4.4% share, indicating an increase in social inequality from the early 1990s to 2000.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

In 2008, according to the municipal government, there were records of [[slums]], [[stilt houses]], and irregular settlements. In 2000, {{formatnum:14816}} inhabitants lived in substandard housing clusters.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> The emergence of these clusters in Piracicaba was driven by the economic conditions of the 1970s, coupled with the establishment of the Industrial District and the rural exodus, which led to significant migration to the city. This caused rapid urban growth but was accompanied by negative impacts on the population’s quality of life. Low wages and a lack of public housing policies exacerbated the situation in the 1980s and 1990s.<ref name="UFMG_Favelas">{{cite web|url=http://www.cantacantos.com.br/revista/index.php/geografias/article/view/60/42 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOR23EW?url=http://www.cantacantos.com.br/revista/index.php/geografias/article/view/60/42 |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=Obstáculos aos processos de regularização fundiária de favelas no Brasil: o caso de Piracicaba – SP |trans-title=Obstacles to the land regularization processes of slums in Brazil: the case of Piracicaba – SP |language=pt |author=Maria M. Funes, Maria Pozzi de Castro e Shimbo |date=9 December 2005 |publisher=Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (UFMG) |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref>

Efforts to address this situation included attempts to relocate slum residents, but community resistance led to the urbanization of some substandard clusters in certain cases. In 1990, the Piracicaba Municipal Housing Development Company (EMDHAP) was established to lead the municipality’s housing policy and slum eradication efforts. During the 1990s, approximately {{formatnum:1500}} families were relocated to planned subdivisions or housing complexes. In 1997, a land regularization process began in some slums not located in high-risk areas, and urban development projects were initiated in certain clusters. However, a significant portion of the population still lacks adequate infrastructure.<ref name="UFMG_Favelas"/> In 2021, Piracicaba had 76 recorded slums.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sampi.net.br/piracicaba/noticias/1759823/cidade/2021/05/piracicaba-contabiliza-76-favelas-e-pessoas-a-espera-de-moradia-digna|title=Piracicaba contabiliza 76 favelas e pessoas a espera de moradia digna|trans-title=Piracicaba records 76 slums and people awaiting decent housing|language=pt-br|date=2021-05-24|access-date=2024-07-17|website=sampi}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Catedral S Antonio Piracicaba 180611 REFON 1 PANO.jpg|thumb|Santo Antônio Cathedral]]
[[File:Capela D Bosco Piracicaba 180611 REFON 10.JPG|thumb|Dom Bosco Chapel]]
Piracicaba exhibits a rich diversity of religious practices, reflecting its cultural variety. While the city developed on a predominantly Catholic social foundation, influenced by both colonization and immigration—and with the majority of its residents still identifying as Catholic—numerous other religious denominations are present. These include dozens of Protestant denominations, as well as practices such as [[Buddhism]], [[Islam]], [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]], and others. In recent decades, Buddhism and Eastern religions have seen significant growth in the city. The Jewish, Mormon, and Afro-Brazilian religious communities are also notable. According to data from the [[2010 Brazilian census]] conducted by IBGE, Piracicaba’s population in that year consisted of: [[Roman Catholicism|Catholics]] (59.78%), [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]] (27.42%), [[irreligion|no religion]] (7.47%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (2.29%), and [[Jehovah’s Witnesses]] (0.95%). The remaining 2.09% of the resident population identified themselves with other religious denominations or had an undetermined religious affiliation.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2103&i=P&nome=on&qtu8=137&qtu14=3¬arodape=on&tab=2103&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&opc1=1&poc2=1&poc133=2&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&nivt=0&poc1=1&sec58=0&orp=7&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&sec1=0&opc2=1&opc133=2&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&orc2=4&opc58=1&orc133=6&qtu2=5&qtu15=4&sev=1000093&sec2=0&sec133=0&opp=1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&opn13=0&orc1=3&poc58=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&orc58=5&opn7=0&decm=99&ascendente=on&sep=38558&orn=1&qtu7=36&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Piracicaba&OpcCara=44&proc=1 |title=População residente _Religião |trans-title=Resident population _Religion |language=pt |publisher=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |date=2010 |access-date=13 December 2014}}</ref>

==== Roman Catholic Church ====
[[File:Igreja de Monte Alegre (2334060594).jpg|thumb|right|Monte Alegre Church, inaugurated in 1937 and designed by [[Alfredo Volpi]].]]
According to the [[Catholic Church]]’s organizational structure, Piracicaba is part of the [[Archdiocese of Campinas]], established on 19 April 1958, and serves as the seat of the [[Diocese of Piracicaba]]. The diocese, created as a parish on 21 June 1774 and officially established as a diocese on 26 February 1944, encompasses Piracicaba and 15 other municipalities. For pastoral purposes, it is divided into six pastoral regions, with 57 parishes and 4 quasi-parishes.<ref name="Diocese_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.diocesedepiracicaba.org.br/novo/capa.asp?secao=material&id=70 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOTGGgj?url=http://www.diocesedepiracicaba.org.br/novo/capa.asp?secao=material&id=70 |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=Diocese hoje |trans-title=Diocese today |language=pt |publisher=Diocese de Piracicaba |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref> The diocese’s establishment was driven by rapid population growth in the region during the early 20th century, which facilitated the development of Catholicism in the area.<ref name="Diocese_História"/>

The Santo Antônio Cathedral, Piracicaba’s cathedral, serves as the seat of the Diocese of Piracicaba. Originally built as a [[Neoclassicism|neoclassical]] parish church, it deteriorated over time. Between 1946 and 1948, it underwent renovations and was officially reopened on 27 December 1950.<ref name="Catedral_Histórico">{{cite web|url=http://www.catedraldepiracicaba.org.br/secoes/catedral/historico/historico.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOV7I9O?url=http://www.catedraldepiracicaba.org.br/secoes/catedral/historico/historico.htm |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=Histórico |trans-title=History |language=pt |publisher=Catedral de Piracicaba |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref>

==== Protestant churches ====
Piracicaba is home to a wide range of Protestant or Reformed denominations, including the Sara Nossa Terra Evangelical Community, Maranata Christian Church, [[Lutheran Church]], [[Presbyterian Church]], [[Methodist Church]],{{efn|The Methodist Church of Piracicaba was founded on 22 September 1881, making it the third Methodist church established in Brazil.<br>[http://www.aprovincia.com.br/memorial-piracicaba/almanaque/igreja-metodista-de-piracicaba-a-terceira-do-brasil/ Igreja Metodista de Piracicaba, 1881] - "A Província - Paixão por Piracicaba" (1 June 2013)}} [[Anglican Episcopal Church]], [[Baptist Church|Baptist churches]], [[Assembleias de Deus|Assemblies of God]], [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], [[World Church of God's Power|World Church of God’s Power]], [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], [[Christian Congregation in Brazil]], and others.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/> According to the 2010 Census, of Piracicaba’s Protestant population, 18% belonged to Pentecostal evangelical churches, 1.95% to mission-based evangelical churches, and 7.47% to other evangelical denominations. Other Christian groups included [[Jehovah’s Witnesses]], representing 0.95% of the resident population, and members of [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (Mormons), accounting for 0.28%.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/>

=== Ethnic composition ===
[[File:Fazendas em Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|right|Farms in the rural area of Piracicaba. The immigrants brought with them many advances in agriculture.]]
According to the 2022 IBGE Census, Piracicaba’s population was composed of {{formatnum:272080}} Whites (64.27%), {{formatnum:33333}} Blacks (7.87%), {{formatnum:115484}} ''[[Pardo]]s'' (27.28%), {{formatnum:2095}} Asians (0.49%), and 320 Indigenous people (0.08%).<ref name="SIDRA_Raça">{{cite web|url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/?utm_source=ibge&utm_medium=home&utm_campaign=portal|title=Panorama Censo 2022 Cor e Raça|trans-title=2022 Census Panorama: Color and Race|language=pt|date=22 December 2023|access-date=22 December 2023|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)}}</ref>

In 2010, according to IBGE, there were 892 emigrants from other parts of São Paulo state and Brazil.<ref>{{cite web |date=2010 |title=Tabela 3172 - Emigrantes internacionais, por sexo, segundo as Grandes Regiões e Unidades da Federação das pessoas com quem residiram antes de emigrarem |trans-title=Table 3172 - International emigrants, by sex, according to the Major Regions and Federation Units of the people they lived with before emigrating |url=https://ftp.ibge.gov.br/Censos/Censo_Demografico_2010/Resultados_do_Universo/tabelas_pdf/tab2.pdf |access-date=12 May 2012 |publisher=Sidra (Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática) |language=pt}}</ref>

Immigration was more common in the early 20th century and significantly contributed to agriculture, particularly by improving rural labor. According to São Paulo’s Secretariat of Internal Affairs at the time, in 1895, there were {{formatnum:1200}} immigrants in the city. Of the 744 births that year, 237 had Italian fathers, 23 had Spanish fathers, 10 had Portuguese fathers, and 7 had German fathers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=3887&idContentSection=710 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOWvTs1?url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=3887&idContentSection=710 |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=1900: Piracicaba em números oficiais |trans-title=1900: Piracicaba in official numbers |language=pt |author=Beatriz Elias |date=16 November 2006 |publisher=A Província |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref>

Italian immigration was the most significant in Piracicaba, though there were also notable communities of Germans, Swiss, Arabs, Spaniards, Portuguese, Japanese, and Blacks who initially worked as slaves on plantations. After the abolition of slavery, many remained in the city.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.abep.nepo.unicamp.br/encontro2010POSDEM/docs/ABEP_PD_2071.doc |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67rOYdWgq?url=http://www.abep.nepo.unicamp.br/encontro2010POSDEM/docs/ABEP_PD_2071.doc |archive-date=22 May 2012 |title=Influência dos emigrantes no espaço geográfico piracicabano |trans-title=Influence of emigrants on Piracicaba’s geographical space |language=pt |publisher=ABEP/NEPO/Unicamp |date=2010 |access-date=22 May 2012}}</ref>

=== Subdivisions ===
The city is divided into five districts in addition to the Seat District: Ártemis, Guamium, Ibitiruna, Santa Teresinha de Piracicaba, and Tupi. It is also organized into five regions (North, South, East, West, and Central),<ref name="IBGE_Censo2000">{{cite web|url=https://ftp.ibge.gov.br/Censos/Censo_Demografico_2000/Dados_do_Universo/Meso_Microregioes_Distritos_Subdistritos_Bairros/Sao_Paulo.zip |title=Censo Demografico 2000 - Microrregiões, distritos, subdistritos e bairros |trans-title=2000 Demographic Census - Microregions, districts, subdistricts, and neighborhoods |language=pt |publisher=[[Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]] (IBGE) |date=29 November 2001 |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref> and includes nearly 70 official and unofficial neighborhoods, villages, and subdivisions. The South Region, comprising neighborhoods such as Verde, Paulista, Água Branca, Campestre, Higienópolis, Jardim Califórnia, Jardim Caxambu, Jardim Elite, Monte Líbano, Nova América, and Pauliceia, is the second most populous, with {{formatnum:66486}} inhabitants.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.camarapiracicaba.sp.gov.br/camara07/index8.asp?id=7725 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68S3sDtas?url=http://www.camarapiracicaba.sp.gov.br/camara07/index8.asp?id=7725 |archive-date=16 June 2012 |title=Paiva propõe abertura da caixa nas regiões Norte e Sul de Piracicaba |trans-title=Paiva proposes opening the box in the North and South regions of Piracicaba |language=pt |author=Ademir Barbosa |date=1 October 2009 |publisher=Câmara Municipal |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ipplap.com.br/docs/mapa_reg_bairros.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68S3tsX39?url=http://www.ipplap.com.br/docs/mapa_reg_bairros.pdf |archive-date=16 June 2012 |title=Regiões e bairros |trans-title=Regions and neighborhoods |language=pt |publisher=Instituto de Pesquisa e Planejamento de Piracicaba (IPPLAP) |date=6 May 2005 |access-date=15 June 2012}}</ref>

In 2012, Piracicaba’s [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) was the highest in the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Piracicaba Microregion]], the 14th highest in São Paulo state, and the 52nd highest in Brazil. According to IBGE data for 2012, the municipality’s GDP was R${{formatnum:11887388}} thousand, with R${{formatnum:1962724}} thousand from net product taxes at current prices. The [[per capita GDP]] was R${{formatnum:32135.11}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=353870&idtema=134&search=sao-paulo%7cpiracicaba%7cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2012|title=Produto Interno Bruto 2012 |trans-title=Gross Domestic Product 2012 |language=pt |publisher=IBGE |date=2012 |access-date=29 January 2015}}</ref> In 2000, the [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) for income was 0.795, compared to Brazil’s 0.723 that year.<ref name="UFMS"/> Piracicaba ranks as the fifth city in São Paulo state for export value.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.corderovirtual.com.br/geral/imprima_mundo.php?codigo=171 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67sxBMs8V?url=http://www.corderovirtual.com.br/geral/imprima_mundo.php?codigo=171 |archive-date=23 May 2012 |title=Piracicaba é a 5ª no Estado em exportações |trans-title=Piracicaba is 5th in the state for exports |language=pt |publisher=Correio Virtual |date=19 January 2005 |access-date=23 May 2012}}</ref>

According to IBGE, in 2010, the city had {{formatnum:15055}} local units and {{formatnum:14522}} active companies and commercial establishments. The total number of persons employed was 138,654, with {{formatnum:118295}} categorized as salaried employees. Salaries and other remunerations totaled R${{formatnum:2925666}} thousand, and the average monthly salary in the municipality was 3.8 [[minimum wage]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=353870&nomemun=Piracicaba |title=Estatísticas do Cadastro Central de Empresas |trans-title=Central Business Register Statistics |language=pt |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=23 May 2012}}</ref> Until the 1950s, Piracicaba’s economy was heavily dependent on agriculture. This, combined with the rapid development of the [[Campinas metropolitan area|Campinas Metropolitan Region]] (RMC), led to financial challenges. However, the construction of highways and investments in agroindustry helped reverse this situation. The agricultural sector shifted focus to sugar and ethanol production. In addition to this significant agricultural activity, Piracicaba is a major industrial hub in the region and hosts several universities.<ref name="ABG_Gênese" /><ref name="mto_economia">{{cite web|url=http://www.muito.com.br/piracicaba/a-economia-de-piracicaba.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67swbdFOR?url=http://www.muito.com.br/piracicaba/a-economia-de-piracicaba.html |archive-date=23 May 2012 |title=A economia de Piracicaba |trans-title=The economy of Piracicaba |language=pt |publisher=Muito.com |access-date=23 May 2012}}</ref>

;'''Primary sector'''

According to IBGE, in 2012, agriculture was the least significant sector of Piracicaba’s economy. Of the city’s total GDP, R${{formatnum:171392}} thousand was the gross added value of agriculture.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In 2013, IBGE reported approximately {{formatnum:52477}} cattle, {{formatnum:1257}} horses, 165 buffaloes, {{formatnum:7240}} pigs, 319 goats, and {{formatnum:3965}} sheep. There were {{formatnum:4800000}} poultry, including {{formatnum:4709920}} roosters, pullets, chickens, and chicks, {{formatnum:90080}} hens, and 460 quails, producing {{formatnum:1323}} thousand dozen eggs and 8 thousand quail eggs. {{formatnum:6050}} cows were milked, yielding {{formatnum:10237}} thousand liters of milk. A total of 635 sheep were sheared, producing {{formatnum:1410}} kilograms of wool.<ref name="IBGE_Pecuária 2013">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=353870&idtema=135&search=sao-paulo%7cpiracicaba%7cpecuaria-2013 |title=Pecuária 2013 |trans-title=Livestock 2013 |language=pt |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2013 |access-date=29 January 2015}}</ref> In temporary crop farming, [[sugarcane]] was cultivated on {{formatnum:52800}} hectares, yielding {{formatnum:4224000}} tons in 2013. Watermelons were planted on 310 hectares, producing {{formatnum:9300}} tons, and corn was grown on {{formatnum:1790}} hectares, yielding {{formatnum:14130}} tons.<ref name="IBGE_Agricultura 2013">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=353870&idtema=137&search=sao-paulo%7cpiracicaba%7cproducao-agricola-municipal-lavoura-temporaria-2013 |title=Lavoura Temporária 2013 |trans-title=Temporary Crop 2013 |language=pt |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2013 |access-date=29 January 2015}}</ref>

Despite its limited economic contribution, Piracicaba is considered a potential hub for agroindustry. Sugarcane has long been central to the city’s economy. In the 1950s, the city relied heavily on it, and as the city grew, investments were made to create more jobs, including modernizing the harvest process and advancing biotechnology.<ref name="ABG_Gênese" /><ref name="mto_economia"/>

;'''Secondary sector'''
In 2012, industry was the second most significant sector of Piracicaba’s economy, contributing R${{formatnum:3248627}} thousand to the municipal GDP as the gross added value of the industrial sector.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Key industries include [[metallurgy]], mechanics, [[textiles]], [[food industry|food]], and [[fuel]]s (production of [[petrochemicals]] and [[ethanol]]). Sugarcane, the primary source of income for the primary sector, provides raw materials for ethanol and [[Alcohol (chemistry)|alcohol]] production, making Piracicaba one of the world’s leading sugar and ethanol hubs. These sectors saw significant growth in the latter half of the 20th century due to investments aimed at addressing unemployment.<ref name="ABG_Gênese" /><ref name="mto_economia"/>

One of the region’s main industrial parks is located in Piracicaba and played a crucial role in improving infrastructure and employment conditions in the late 20th century.<ref name="ABG_Gênese" /> The Uninorte Industrial District hosts 72 associated companies and covers nearly 1 million square meters. Its proximity to the Ring Road provides direct access to the [[Rodovia Anhanguera|Anhanguera Highway]] and [[Rodovia dos Bandeirantes|Bandeirantes Highway]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.distritoindustrialuninorte.com.br/index.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67swdbU2V?url=http://www.distritoindustrialuninorte.com.br/index.html |archive-date=23 May 2012 |title=História |trans-title=History |language=pt |publisher=Distrito Industrial Uninorte |date=2009 |access-date=23 May 2012}}</ref>

;'''Tertiary sector'''
In 2012, the provision of [[Tertiary sector of the economy|services]] contributed R${{formatnum:3248627}} thousand to Piracicaba’s GDP, making it the largest contributor to the municipal economy.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Commerce in the city began to develop and gain economic significance during the first half of the 20th century. In 1941, the Professional Association of Retail Trade was established, becoming a [[trade union]] in 1942 and known as SINCOMÉRCIO Piracicaba, which coordinates the commercial sector. In the 1970s, the [[Serviço Social do Comércio|Social Service of Commerce]] (SESC) arrived in Piracicaba, and in the 1980s, a National Commercial Learning Service (SENAC) unit was established.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sincomerciopiracicaba.com.br/2009/historia.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67swegWSx?url=http://www.sincomerciopiracicaba.com.br/2009/historia.php |archive-date=23 May 2012 |title=História |trans-title=History |language=pt |publisher=SINCOMÉRCIO Piracicaba |access-date=23 May 2012}}</ref>

== Education ==
[[File:Universidade de São Paulo - Campus de Piracicaba - SP - Escola Superior de Agricultura Luiz de Queiroz (ESALQ) - panoramio.jpg|thumb|left|Central building of the [[Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture]] (ESALQ) at the [[University of São Paulo]] (USP)]]

In 2009, the average Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) among public schools in Piracicaba was 4.0 (on a scale from 1 to 10), equivalent to the average for municipal and state schools across Brazil. Among private institutions, this index rises to 6.1.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> The [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) for education was 0.913 (classified as very high), compared to Brazil's 0.849.<ref name="UFMS"/>

In 2009, the municipality recorded approximately 73,658 enrollments in public and private school systems.<ref name="IBGE_Educação">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=educa&codmun=353870&nomemun=Piracicaba |title=Ensino, matrículas, docentes e rede escolar 2009 |access-date=24 May 2012 |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2009}}</ref> According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), in the same year, of the 115 primary schools, 54 were part of the municipal public system, 36 were state public schools, and 25 were private institutions. Among the 41 secondary schools, 46 were state public schools, 1 was a municipal public school, and 19 were private.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> In 2000, 4.1% of children aged 7 to 14 were not enrolled in primary education. The completion rate for youths aged 15 to 17 in that year was 61.2%. The literacy rate for the population aged 15 and older in 2010 was 96.9%. In 2006, for every 100 girls in primary education, there were 111 boys.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

== Science and technology ==
[[File:Laboratório de Análise Tecnológica de Cana-de-açúcar Dr. Paulo Rogério Palma de Oliveira, Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|right|Dr. Paulo Rogério Palma de Oliveira Sugarcane Technological Analysis Laboratory, inaugurated in December 2010 by the Secretariat of Agriculture and Supply.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.apta.sp.gov.br/noticias.php?id=3714 |title=Polo regional centro-sul ganha novo laboratório de tecnologia de cana-de-açúcar |last=Resende |first=José Venâncio de |date=30 November 2010 |publisher=Agência Paulista de Tecnologia dos Agronegócios (APTA) |access-date=24 May 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67uV3sS4B?url=http://www.apta.sp.gov.br/noticias.php?id=3714 |archive-date=24 May 2012}}</ref>]]

As a hub for scientific development, Piracicaba attracts numerous visitors interested in the technology being developed. Several high-complexity educational and research institutions, some internationally recognized, are based or represented in the city, including the [[Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture]] of the [[University of São Paulo]] (USP), the Piracicaba School of Dentistry of the [[State University of Campinas]], the [[Methodist University of Piracicaba]] (UNIMEP), [[Anhembi Morumbi University]], and the Piracicaba Campus of the Deputy Roque Trevisan Technology College (FATEC), as well as the Maria Imaculada Integrated Colleges, the Piracicaba Municipal Education Foundation (FUMEP), Piracicaba Technology College (FATEP), Dom Bosco Salesian College, [[Virtual University of the State of São Paulo|Virtual University of the State of São Paulo (UNIVESP)]], Anglo College, Anhanguera College, International University Center (UNINTER), and the [[Federal Institute of Education, Science and Technology]] (IFSP). The city is considered a regional center for professional training, offering a wide variety of technical courses.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.ipplap.com.br/acidade.php |title=A Cidade |author=Instituto de Pesquisas e Planejamento de Piracicaba (IPPLAP) |access-date=24 May 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67uTzRjT0?url=http://www.ipplap.com.br/acidade.php |archive-date=24 May 2012}}</ref>

In addition to academic institutions, the city hosts a significant technological park, the Piracicaba Technological Park, focused on technological information, facilitating integration among research centers, universities, and businesses, and supporting the development of entrepreneurial activities. Another prominent sector is the production of [[biofuel]]s, with the region accounting for approximately 65% of the equipment used in the national sugar-alcohol industry. The production of [[ethanol]], [[Alcohol (chemistry)|alcohol]], and [[biodiesel]] benefits both rural workers and research and biotechnology institutions.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.semdec.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/textos.aspx?SID=c215daf96fbbf130a7b0ce252c7ea1c9&id=81 |title=Parque Tecnológico de Piracicaba |author=Secretaria Municipal de Desenvolvimento Econômico |access-date=24 May 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67uU0z4Y7?url=http://www.semdec.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/textos.aspx?SID=c215daf96fbbf130a7b0ce252c7ea1c9&id=81 |archive-date=24 May 2012}}</ref>

To strengthen policies aimed at agribusiness, the Piracicaba Valley,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www4.esalq.usp.br/banco-de-noticias/vale-do-piracicaba-foi-lan%25C3%25A7ado-no-conselho-municipal-e-ci%25C3%25AAncia-e-tecnologia |title=Banco de Notícias |access-date=18 July 2016 |website=www4.esalq.usp.br |archive-date=24 April 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160424115245/http://www4.esalq.usp.br/banco-de-noticias/vale-do-piracicaba-foi-lan%C3%A7ado-no-conselho-municipal-e-ci%C3%AAncia-e-tecnologia |url-status=dead }}</ref> also known as AgTechValley, was recently launched. Inspired by [[Silicon Valley]], it has the [[College of Agriculture, University of São Paulo|Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture]] (ESALQ) as a reference.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/noticia/2016/04/baseado-vale-do-silicio-piracicaba-lanca-complexo-voltado-ao-setor-agro.html |title=Piracicaba quer sediar 'Vale do Silício' do agronegócio no campus da USP |date=19 April 2016 |access-date=18 July 2016}}</ref>

This project aims to bolster policies for the agribusiness sector, attracting investments to solidify the municipality’s prominent position in agricultural technology development.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/noticia/2016/05/projeto-busca-consolidar-piracicaba-como-vale-do-silicio-do-agronegocio.html |title=Projeto busca consolidar Piracicaba como 'Vale do Silício' do agronegócio |date=6 May 2016 |access-date=18 July 2016}}</ref> Notably, 65% of the equipment supplied to the sugar-alcohol industry originates from this municipality.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.valedopiracicaba.org/sobre-o-vale-do-piracicaba/ |title=O Vale | access-date=18 July 2016 |website=www.valedopiracicaba.org}}</ref>

[[File:Penitenciária de Piracicaba.jpg|thumb|left|"Nelson Furlan" Provisional Detention Center]]

== Housing, services, and communication ==
[[File:Rio Piracicaba.jpg|thumb|Night view of the Piracicaba River with the city in the background. Electricity supply is provided by [[Companhia Paulista de Força e Luz]] (CPFL Paulista).]]

According to the [[2022 Brazilian census]], in 2022, the city had 190,944 permanent private [[housing unit|households]], comprising 115,223 [[house]]s, 36,569 [[apartment]]s, 3,266 houses in villages or condominiums, 109 [[tenement]]s, and 261 households in degraded or unfinished structures. Per the IBGE, in 2022, 155,428 permanent private households were occupied, 24,501 were vacant, and 10,273 were for occasional use. Most of the municipality has access to treated water, electricity, sewage, urban cleaning, fixed telephony, and mobile telephony. In that year, 98.67% of households were served by the general water supply network; 99.75% had waste collection services; and 97.02% of residences were connected to the sewage network.<ref name="IBGE_DomicídiosPop">{{Cite web |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/indicadores.html?localidade=BR&tema=2 |title=Censo Demográfico 2022: Características dos Domicílios |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=23 February 2024 |access-date=25 February 2024}}</ref>

Piracicaba’s first water supply system began planning in 1824 by the Municipal Chamber. In May 1886, construction started on a 2-million-liter reservoir in the Alemães neighborhood, managed by the Piracicaba Hydraulic Company. The Municipal Water and Sewage Service (SEMAE Piracicaba) was established on 30 April 1969, under Municipal Law No. 1657, and is currently responsible for the city’s water supply, operating three intake stations: two on the Piracicaba River and one on the Corumbataí River.<ref name="Semae_História">{{Cite web |url=http://www.semaepiracicaba.org.br/?p=aGlzdG9yaWE= |title=História |author=Serviço Municipal de Água e Esgoto (SEMAE) |access-date=1 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686Vbdi6j?url=http://www.semaepiracicaba.org.br/?p=aGlzdG9yaWE= |archive-date=1 June 2012}}</ref> However, the Piracicaba River is experiencing reduced water levels. Urban, industrial, and agricultural development in the region has increased water demand beyond availability, with the river also supplying part of the [[Greater São Paulo|São Paulo Metropolitan Region]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://educorumbatai.blogspot.com.br/2009/11/recursos-hidricos-e-o-abastecimento-de.html |title=Recursos hídricos e o abastecimento de água em Piracicaba |author=Educorumbataí |date=18 November 2009 |access-date=1 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686Vcu1d5?url=http://educorumbatai.blogspot.com.br/2009/11/recursos-hidricos-e-o-abastecimento-de.html |archive-date=1 June 2012}}</ref> SEMAE also manages sewage collection, with waste treated at two sewage treatment plants (ETE): the smaller-capacity Dois Córregos Sewage Treatment Plant, inaugurated in December 1992, and the primary Piracicamirim Sewage Treatment Plant, opened in June 1998.<ref name="Semae_História"/>

[[File:Prédios de moradia popular em Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|left|Aerial view of public housing in Piracicaba]]

The [[Companhia Paulista de Força e Luz]] (CPFL Paulista) is responsible for electricity supply in Piracicaba, serving 234 other municipalities in the [[interior of São Paulo]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cpfl.com.br/Distribuiccedilatildeo/CPFLPaulista/tabid/93/Default.aspx |title=Companhia Paulista de Força e Luz - CPFL Paulista |author=[[Companhia Paulista de Força e Luz]] (CPFL) |access-date=1 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686Ve7v8d?url=http://www.cpfl.com.br/Distribuiccedilatildeo/CPFLPaulista/tabid/93/Default.aspx |archive-date=1 June 2012}}</ref> In 2010, 99.93% of the municipality’s households were served by this service.<ref name="IBGE_DomicídiosPop"/> [[Fiber optic|Fiber optic internet]] and [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]) services are offered by various [[Internet service provider|internet providers]]. For fixed telephony, the city was initially served by CIPATEL - Paulista Telecommunications Company, which built the first telephone exchange still in use today. In 1975, the [[Direct Distance Dialing|DDD]] system was implemented, and in 1977, services were taken over by [[Telesp|Telecomunicações de São Paulo (TELESP)]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19980114175120/http://www.telesp.com.br/paginas/06_casasua/060202_operacional.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=1998-01-14 |title=Área de atuação da Telesp em São Paulo |publisher=Página Oficial da Telesp (arquivada) |access-date=2025-05-08 }}</ref> In 1998, TELESP was privatized and sold to [[Telefônica Brasil|Telefônica]],<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://telefonica.mediagroup.com.br/pt/Empresa/Nossa_Historia.aspx |title=Nossa História |publisher=Telefônica / VIVO}}</ref> adopting the [[Vivo (telecommunications company)|Vivo]] brand in 2012.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/economia/negocios/noticia/2012/04/telefonica-conclui-troca-da-marca-por-vivo.html |title=Telefônica conclui troca da marca por Vivo |last=Gasparin |first=Gabriela |date=12 April 2012 |publisher=G1}}</ref> Piracicaba’s area code ([[Direct Distance Dialing|DDD]]) is 019,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://dddcidade.com.br/piracicaba/ |title=DDD |author=DDD Cidade |access-date=1 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686Vfa6RX?url=http://dddcidade.com.br/piracicaba/ |archive-date=1 June 2012}}</ref> and its [[Postal code|Postal Code]] (CEP) ranges from 13400-001 to 13439-999.<ref name="Correios_BuscaCEP">{{Cite web |url=http://www.buscacep.correios.com.br/sistemas/buscacep/buscaFaixaCep.cfm |title=Busca Faixa CEP |author=Empresa Brasileira de Correios e Telégrafos |access-date=29 July 2021}}</ref>

Several [[high-definition television]] (HDTV) channels are available, with major networks having affiliates in the city or nearby, such as [[EPTV Campinas]] ([[TV Globo]]),<ref>{{Cite web |author= |date=1 June 2012 |title=Piracicaba e Região |url=http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686ViRuAf?url=http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/ |archive-date=1 June 2012 |access-date=1 June 2012 |website=G1}}</ref> [[TH+ Record Campinas|TV Thathi Campinas]] ([[RecordTV]]),<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.sbtworld.com.br/2010/11/bomba-diretor-da-tvb-confirma.html |title=Diretor da TVB confirma negociações com a Tv Record |last=Cardoso |first=Júnior |date=30 November 2010 |publisher=SBD World |access-date=1 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/686VgXZa5?url=http://www.sbtworld.com.br/2010/11/bomba-diretor-da-tvb-confirma.html |archive-date=1 June 2012}}</ref> Band Mais, VTV ([[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]), TV Jornal, and TV Ativa. Piracicaba also has several newspapers, including A Tribuna de Piracicaba, Gazeta de Piracicaba, and Jornal de Piracicaba. == Transport ==
[[File:Piracicaba footbridge01 2013-09-25.jpg|thumb|Pedestrian bridge over the Piracicaba River]]
[[File:Avenidas em Piracicaba SP.jpg|thumb|right|Intersection of avenues in Piracicaba]]

The city’s airport, [[Piracicaba Airport|Comendador Pedro Morganti State Airport]], is managed by the [[DAESP|São Paulo State Aviation Department]] (DAESP) and handles only small-scale flights, serving domestic non-commercial operations.<ref name="Abrates_Info">{{Cite web |url=http://www.abrates.org.br/eventos/evento.php?evento_id=8&pagina=informacoes_gerais |title=Informações gerais |author=Abrates |date=2012 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BONT1G8?url=http://www.abrates.org.br/eventos/evento.php?evento_id=8&pagina=informacoes_gerais|archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref> It features an asphalt runway, 1,000 meters long with nighttime lighting, a 175 m² passenger terminal, and parking for 65 vehicles. The airport is also home to charter flights, skydiving schools and a pilot training school.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.rac.com.br/noticias/nacional/78949/2011/03/27/mato-toma-conta-de-aeroporto-de-piracicaba.html |title=Mato toma conta de aeroporto de Piracicaba |author=Rac.com.br |date=27 March 2011 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOP1PLP?url=http://www.rac.com.br/noticias/nacional/78949/2011/03/27/mato-toma-conta-de-aeroporto-de-piracicaba.html |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

The first railway to reach Piracicaba was the Piracicaba Branch of the Ytuana Railway Company, with the city’s first railway station inaugurated on 20 February 1877, when Piracicaba consisted of only four blocks and public lighting relied on kerosene lamps. The first train arrived on 19 May of that year. A new station was opened on 6 January 1885, renovated in 1943, and served as the main station until the early 1990s, when it was replaced by the Urban Bus Terminal. During its operation, it was managed by the Ytuana Railway Company, the Sorocabana Railway, and the [[Ferrovia Paulista S/A]] (FEPASA).<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/p/piracicaba.html |title=Piracicaba (antiga Constituição) |author=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |date=7 August 2010 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOPuPKj?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/p/piracicaba.html |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

In 1922, 45 years after the arrival of the Ytuana Railway Company, the Piracicaba Branch of the Paulista Railway Company reached Piracicaba, with the significant Piracicaba Paulista Station inaugurated on 30 July 1922. The station was owned by the [[Companhia Paulista de Estradas de Ferro|Paulista Railway Company]] until 1971 and by FEPASA until 1998, when it was deactivated. Today, the site houses municipal parks and sports courts.<ref name="Estações_Piracicaba"/>

Rail transport in Piracicaba, as in much of São Paulo state, declined due to the rise of road and air transport, particularly in the first half of the 1990s.<ref name="Estações_Piracicaba"/> Currently, there are only projects aimed at preserving the heritage of the city’s main stations.<ref>{{Cite web |author=((Conselho de Defesa do Patrimônio Histórico, Arqueológico, Artístico e Turístico do Estado (CONDEPHAAT))) |date=7 December 2009 |title=Ordem do Dia |url=http://www.cultura.sp.gov.br/StaticFiles/SEC/Condephaat/Delibera%C3%A7%C3%A3o/deliberacoes%2007122009.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOm6qSF?url=http://www.cultura.sp.gov.br/StaticFiles/SEC/Condephaat/Delibera%C3%A7%C3%A3o/deliberacoes%2007122009.pdf |archive-date=5 June 2012 |access-date=4 June 2012}}</ref>

Piracicaba has a robust road network connecting it to various cities in the [[interior of São Paulo]] and the state capital, with access to major state and national highways via paved, dual-lane secondary roads, such as the [[Rodovia dos Bandeirantes|Bandeirantes Highway]] and the [[Anhanguera Highway]]. The Piracicaba Bus Terminal is one of the region’s main terminals, inaugurated in the 1990s,<ref name="Estações_Piracicaba"/> with passenger traffic increasing by 30% to 40% during holidays and peak travel seasons.<ref>{{Cite web |author= |date=6 April 2012 |title=Movimento na rodoviária cresce 40% antes da Páscoa em Piracicaba, SP |url=http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/noticia/2012/04/movimento-na-rodoviaria-cresce-40-antes-da-pascoa-em-piracicaba-sp.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOTRB9E?url=http://www.cultura.sp.gov.br/StaticFiles/SEC/Condephaat/Delibera%C3%A7%C3%A3o/deliberacoes%2007122009.pdf |archive-date=5 June 2012 |access-date=4 June 2012 |website=G1}}</ref> The following highways pass through the municipality:<ref name=".com_Acesso">{{Cite web |url=http://www.setur.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/index.php/rodovias-de-acesso.html |title=Rodovias de acesso |author=Secretaria Municipal de Turismo (SETUR) |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOURDvt?url=http://www.setur.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/index.php/rodovias-de-acesso.html |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref> [[Rodovia Luís de Queiróz|Luiz de Queiroz Highway]] ([[SP-304]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Geraldo de Barros Highway ([[SP-304]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Fausto Santomauro Highway ([[SP-127 (São Paulo highway)|SP-127]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Cornélio Pires Highway ([[SP-127 (São Paulo highway)|SP-127]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Deputy Laércio Corte Highway ([[SP-147 (São Paulo highway)|SP-147]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Samuel de Castro Neves Highway ([[SP-147 (São Paulo highway)|SP-147]]);<ref name=".com_Acesso"/> Sugar Highway ([[SP-308 (São Paulo highway)|SP-308]]).<ref name=".com_Acesso"/>

[[File:Rotatória da Av. Independência - panoramio.jpg|thumb|Independence Avenue]]

The Municipal Transit and Transport Secretariat (Semuttran) oversees the control and maintenance of the municipality’s traffic, from monitoring public roads and driver and pedestrian behavior to developing traffic engineering projects, paving, constructing roadworks, and managing services such as taxis, alternative transport, buses, chartered vehicles, and school transport.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.sjc.sp.gov.br/secretarias/transportes.aspx |title=Transportes |author=Prefeitura |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/67jh6RdO3?url=http://www.sjc.sp.gov.br/secretarias/transportes.aspx |archive-date=17 May 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/14209/faleconosco |title=Tem início amanhã o 46°Fórum Paulista de Transporte Urbano e Trânsito |last=Bianchini |first=Flávia |date=20 March 2012 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOWxtaD?url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/14209/faleconosco |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

In 2014, the municipal vehicle fleet totaled 277,008 vehicles, including 167,039 cars, 7,920 trucks, 2,582 tractor-trucks, 21,047 pickups, 9,747 vans, 951 minibuses, 49,681 motorcycles, 5,743 scooters, 1,484 buses, 56 wheeled tractors, and 1,614 utility vehicles.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=353870&idtema=139&search=sao-paulo|title=IBGE - Frota de Piracicaba em 2014 |author=IBGE |publisher=IBGE |access-date=16 May 2015}}</ref> Dual-lane paved avenues and numerous traffic lights facilitate city traffic, but the increase in vehicles over the past decade has led to increasingly slow traffic, particularly in the municipal seat. Additionally, finding parking spaces in the commercial center has become challenging, causing some losses to local commerce.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://suacidade.org/piracicaba/prefeitura-inicia-obras-entre-as-avenidas-piracicamirim-prof-alberto-vollet-sachs-e-rio-das-pedras |title=Piracicaba: Prefeitura inicia obras entre as avenidas Piracicamirim, Prof. Alberto Vollet Sachs e Rio das Pedras |author=Sua Cidade |date=11 May 2012 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOXtDM3?url=http://suacidade.org/piracicaba/prefeitura-inicia-obras-entre-as-avenidas-piracicamirim-prof-alberto-vollet-sachs-e-rio-das-pedras |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

The city has dozens of urban and intercity bus lines, connecting nearby cities such as [[Mombuca]], [[Tietê, São Paulo|Tietê]], [[Laranjal Paulista]], [[Anhembi]], [[Cordeirópolis]], [[Cerquilho]], [[Elias Fausto]], [[Torrinha]], and [[Conchas]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://viacaoandrenix.blogspot.com.br/2011/03/secretaria-de-transportes-de-piracicaba.html |title=Secretaria de Transportes de Piracicaba oferece linhas Sub-Urbanas para o Grupo VRD |author=Grupo VRD |date=21 March 2011 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOZBgmJ?url=http://viacaoandrenix.blogspot.com.br/2011/03/secretaria-de-transportes-de-piracicaba.html |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref> As of August 2011, 220 of the municipal bus fleet were adapted for people with physical disabilities, with the public transport system serving an average of 2.9 million passengers.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://meutransporte.blogspot.com.br/2011/08/frota-do-transporte-publico-de.html |title=Frota do transporte público de Piracicaba |author=Jornal de Piracicaba |date=13 August 2011 |access-date=4 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68BOasVgh?url=http://meutransporte.blogspot.com.br/2011/08/frota-do-transporte-publico-de.html |archive-date=5 June 2012}}</ref>

The Secretariat of Cultural Action of the Municipality of Piracicaba (SEMAC) is responsible for the city’s cultural sector, tasked with planning and executing the municipality’s cultural policy through programs, projects, and activities aimed at cultural development. It was established under Decree No. 1,655 of 22 January 1992.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/secretaria/apresentacao-missao-valores/ |title=Apresentação / Missão / Valores |author=Secretaria da Ação Cultural da Prefeitura de Piracicaba (SEMAC) |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9g7i3Z?url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/secretaria/apresentacao-missao-valores/ |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The Sports, Leisure, and Motor Activities Secretariat (SELAM) also oversees specific areas of Piracicaba’s culture, such as leisure activities and sports practices.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/sobre-a-selam/137-sobre-a-selam.html |title=Sobre a SELAM |author=Secretaria de Esportes, Lazer e Atividades Motoras (SELAM) |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9h5oCj?url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/sobre-a-selam/137-sobre-a-selam.html |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

=== Performing arts ===
[[File:Paixao3.jpg|thumb|Staging of the “[[Passion of Jesus|Passion of Christ]]” in Piracicaba]]

The city boasts several venues dedicated to cultural events in theater and music. The "Dr. Losso Netto" Municipal Theater, inaugurated on 19 August 1978 as the Piracicaba Municipal Theater, was renamed in April 1993 to honor a journalist who contributed to the growth of the city’s journalism sector. With two large halls, it has infrastructure for diverse events, including dance, music, theater performances, and lectures.<ref name="SEMAC_Teatro">{{Cite web |url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/teatro/?page_id=19 |title=Teatro Municipal "Dr. Losso Netto" |author=Secretaria da Ação Cultural da Prefeitura de Piracicaba (SEMAC) |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9kozLt?url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/teatro/?page_id=19 |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The Unimep Theater, part of the [[Methodist University of Piracicaba]], showcases a variety of artistic performances produced by the Unimep academic community and other Brazilian and international art groups.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.unimep.br/gdc_setores.php?fid=84 |title=Teatro Unimep |author=Universidade Metodista de Piracicaba |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68ESHTMh8?url=http://www.unimep.br/gdc_setores.php?fid=84 |archive-date=7 June 2012}}</ref>

The "Ricardo Ferraz de Arruda Pinto" Municipal Public Library, established on 2 May 1939 with a collection of 837 books, was one of the first in the [[interior of São Paulo]]. Relocated in February 1989, it moved to a purpose-built facility in 2009. Its current collection exceeds 70,000 volumes, and the new 2,770 m² building includes an amphitheater, Wi-Fi, a video room, a computer lab, a newspaper library, a [[microfilming]] section, and a children’s library.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/biblioteca/?page_id=19 |title=Biblioteca Pública Municipal |author=Secretaria da Ação Cultural da Prefeitura de Piracicaba (SEMAC) |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9m1KOs?url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/biblioteca/?page_id=19 |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> SEMAC also operates six cultural centers across the city, offering courses and activities related to crafts, music, dance, and cuisine.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/6707/acao-cultural |title=Ação Cultural |author=Prefeitura |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9nUWsR?url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/6707/acao-cultural |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> These activities are also held at the "Miguel Arcanjo Benício Assumpção Dutra" Municipal Art Gallery, located in the city center.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/19611/pinacoteca-abre-mostras-com-selecionados-no-mapa-cultural-paulista |title=Pinacoteca abre mostras com selecionados no Mapa Cultural Paulista |author=Prefeitura |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9pN3fq?url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/goto/store/texto/19611/pinacoteca-abre-mostras-com-selecionados-no-mapa-cultural-paulista |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>
[[File:Pira1.jpg|thumb|left|250px|Plaque of the International Humor Exhibition at the Central Mill of Piracicaba]]

The Cultural Secretariat promotes various festivals and competitions, including the Piracicaba National Theater Festival (ENTEPIRA), featuring performances by numerous theater groups, the Piracicaba Dance Festival (PIRADANÇA), showcasing competitive and guest performances in genres such as [[ballet]], [[contemporary dance]], [[Jazz dance|jazz]], [[street dance]], [[ballroom dance]], and folk and ethnic dances, as well as the National Choral Meeting (ENACOPI) and the Literary Salon.<ref name="SEMAC_Teatro"/> Traditional festivals include the Feast of the Divine [[Holy Spirit]], held since 1826; the Green Corn Festival, with country music concerts and folk group performances; the Festival of Nations, with settings, food and drink typical of the countries represented; the [[Polenta]] Festival, celebrating Trentino-Tyrolese immigration to Piracicaba; and the São João de Tupi Festival, in the style of a ''[[Quadrille|quadrilha]]'', with concerts, bonfires, traditional games, and typical food and drink, including a ritual in which the locals walk barefoot over hot coals.<ref name="SEMAC_Festas">{{Cite web |url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/agenda/festas-tradicionais/ |title=Festas Tradicionais |author=Secretaria da Ação Cultural da Prefeitura de Piracicaba (SEMAC) |access-date=6 June 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9rBuTG?url=http://semac.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/website/agenda/festas-tradicionais/ |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

The Piracicaba International Humor Exhibition is an annual graphic humor festival held since 1974 at the Piracicaba Central Mill, initially as a form of resistance by graphic artists against Brazil’s [[Military dictatorship in Brazil|military dictatorship]]. Now considered one of the most important graphic arts salons in the world, the competition is divided into four categories: cartoon, editorial cartoon, comic strip and caricature.<ref name="Salão">{{Cite web |url=http://salaodehumor.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/humor/historia.html |title=Salão Internacional de Humor de Piracicaba |author=Secretaria da Ação Cultural da Prefeitura de Piracicaba (SEMAC) |access-date=1 May 2012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68E9sLNBB?url=http://salaodehumor.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/humor/historia.html |archive-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

== Cultural, architectural, and natural attractions ==
[[File:Ponte Pênsil - panoramio.jpg|thumb|right|Suspension Bridge]]
[[File:Museu da Água 1.jpg|thumb|Piracicaba Water Museum]]
[[File:Festa da Polenta Santa Olímpia.jpeg|thumb|Santa Olímpia Polenta Festival]]

In addition to its scenic attractions, Piracicaba boasts a wealth of historical monuments, natural sites, and places to visit. Along the banks of the Piracicaba River, notable landmarks include: the Settler's House, a [[Wattle and daub|wattle-and-daub]] mansion built in the early 19th century, symbolizing the passage of ''[[bandeirantes]]'' through the region;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-casa-do-povoador-73/ |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> the Mirante Park, established in 1895, offering a privileged view of the river’s rapids;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-mirante-71/ |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-area-lazer-65/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQ38Kxa?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-area-lazer-65/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 and an arena theater.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-rua-do-porto-61/ |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> Within the [[College of Agriculture, University of São Paulo|Luiz de Queiroz College of Agriculture]] (ESALQ), several attractions can be found, including the ESALQ Library, the Professor Phillipe Westin Cabral de Vasconcelos Park, the ESALQ reflecting pools, a commemorative plaque in front of the main building, and the stained glass windows of the noble hall.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=7928&idContentSection=709 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQ5KVHf?url=http://www.aprovincia.com/padrao.aspx?texto.aspx?idContent=7928&idContentSection=709 |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=A bela e gloriosa ESALQ com Square, established in 1888 with trees planted by prominent citizens of the time; the Municipal Market, constructed in 1882 and inaugurated on 5 July 1888;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.mercadomunicipalpiracicaba.com.br/mercado.php |title=Mercado Municipal de Piracicaba, desde 1888 |date=2012 |access-date=13 December 2014}}</ref> the Mirante, built in the late 19th century by the Baron of Rezende along the Piracicaba River (its kiosk was constructed between 1906 and 1907), which was visited by [[Isabel, Princess Imperial of Brazil|Isabel]] in 1888; the Piracicaba School, inaugurated in 1881 as a school founded by American missionary [[Martha Watts]]; the Paulista Station, built in 1902,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://emdhap.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/noticias/128--inauguracao-do-centro-cultural-da-estacao-da-paulista-foi-marcada-pela-emocao-.html |title=Estação da Paulista, Piracicaba, 1922 |date=}}</ref> now serving as a vast leisure space; and one of the city’s greatest artistic and religious treasures, the Passage of the Lord of the Garden, created in 1883 by artist Miguelzinho The Secretariat of Cultural Action of the Piracicaba City Hall (SEMAC) oversees the city’s public cultural spaces, including its museums. The Piracicaba Water Museum chronicles the history of the city’s water collection and pumping system, displaying hydraulic pumps, antique water meters, and panels with photographs of services provided since the first Water Collection and Pumping Station.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-da-agua-64/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQ8L9rg?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-da-agua-64/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Museu da Água de Piracicaba (Museu da Água) |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The Prudente de Moraes Historical and Educational Museum showcases the home of [[Prudente de Morais]], Brazil’s third president, built in 1870, and documents the history of the [[Constitutionalist Revolution|Constitutionalist Revolution of 1932]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-prudente-49/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQ9IiDD?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-prudente-49/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Museu Histórico e Pedagógico (Museu Prudente de Moraes) |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The Luiz de Queiroz Museum and Center for Science, Education, and Arts, part of [[College of Agriculture, University of São Paulo|ESALQ]], is housed in the former residence of the school’s deans and directors.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-luiz-57/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQAJtBd?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-museu-luiz-57/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Museu e Centro de Ciência, Educação e Artes "Luiz de Queiroz" (Museu Luiz de Queiroz) |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The Central Mill of Piracicaba, built in 1881 by Estêvão Ribeiro de Sousa Resende, Baron of Rezende, aimed to replace [[Slavery in Brazil|slave labor]] with salaried work and mechanization, but was sold in 1899. Deactivated by the city hall in 1974 and recognized as a historical heritage site, it now serves as a significant cultural, artistic, and recreational space.<ref name="Estações_Piracicaba"/>

In rural tourism, the neighborhoods of [[Santa Olímpia (village)|Santa Olímpia]] and Santana form the Tyrolean Colony of Piracicaba, established over a century ago by immigrants from [[Tyrol]] (the [[Trento]] region, which belonged to Austria until 1919 and is now part of [[Italy]]).<ref name="SEMAC_Festas"/> Other prominent green areas include the Tupi Forest Garden, a 200-hectare environmental conservation area offering trails for hiking;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-horto-9/ |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> the Baths of Artemis, a place for the consumption of medicinal waters and bathing in sulphurous springs;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-balneario-de-artemis-11/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQCF08e?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-balneario-de-artemis-11/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Balneário de Ártemis |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> and the Children's Paradise, established on 7 January 1976, where environmental campaigns aimed at children are held.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-paraiso-da-crianca-55/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQD4fKX?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-paraiso-da-crianca-55/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Paraíso das Crianças e a Educação Ambiental (Paraiso da Criança) |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> This site is adjacent to the Piracicaba Municipal Zoo, founded on 18 August 1972, which currently spans approximately 36,000 m² and houses around 200 animals.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-zoologico-69/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQDxtUu?url=http://www.piracicaba.tur.br/piracicaba-zoologico-69/ |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Zoológico Municipal de Piracicaba (Zoológico) |author=Piracicaba.Tur |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

Piracicaba has three cemeteries: the Saudade Cemetery, built in 1849, the city’s most traditional necropolis, where prominent citizens are buried;<ref>[http://g1.globo.com/sp/piracicaba-regiao/fotos/2013/11/veja-tumulos-famosos-do-cemiterio-da-saudade-em-piracicaba.html Veja túmulos 'famosos' do Cemitério da Saudade em Piracicaba | G1 Piracicaba e região]</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/cemiterio+da+saudade+registra+grande+procura+por+dados+de+antepassados.aspx |title=Cemitério da Saudade, em Piracicaba/SP |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-date=2022-11-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221128153056/http://www.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/cemiterio%20da%20saudade%20registra%20grande%20procura%20por%20dados%20de%20antepassados.aspx |url-status=dead }}</ref> the Vila Rezende Cemetery, founded in 1974, the city’s newest necropolis;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://sedema.wixsite.com/sedema/sedema-cemiterios |title=Cemitério da Vila Rezende {{!}} Piracicaba/SP |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-date=2017-12-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201033040/http://sedema.wixsite.com/sedema/sedema-cemiterios |url-status=dead }}</ref> and the [[rural cemetery]] Resurrection Park, a private necropolis established in 1971.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.parquedaressurreicao.com.br/quemsomos.asp |title=Cemitério Parque da Ressurreição em Piracicaba/SP |access-date=2025-05-08 |archive-date=2021-12-02 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211202123355/http://www.parquedaressurreicao.com.br/quemsomos.asp |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Piracicaba’s gastronomy is renowned throughout Brazil for its distinctive ''[[pamonha]]s''.

== Sports ==
[[File:Estádio XV.jpg|thumb|left|[[Estádio Barão da Serra Negra]], home of [[Esporte Clube XV de Novembro (Piracicaba)|XV de Piracicaba]]]]
[[File:Ginásio Municipal Waldemar Blatkauskas.jpg|thumb|left|Waldemar Blatkauskas Municipal Gymnasium]]

The city is home to several [[sports club]]s, including the [[Esporte Clube XV de Novembro (Piracicaba)|Esporte Clube XV de Novembro]], founded on 15 November 1913, which is the city’s main club. In the 1960s, under the leadership of [[Romeu Italo Ripoli]], it competed in international matches in Europe and Asia.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://terceirotempo.bol.uol.com.br/quefimlevou/qfl/sobre/romeu-italo-ripoli-4219.html |title=Que fim levou Romeu Italo Ripoli |author=Terceiro Tempo - Milton Neves |access-date=6 May 2013}}</ref>

Within Brazil, XV de Piracicaba has primarily competed in the divisions of the [[Campeonato Paulista]] and the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|Campeonato Brasileiro]], winning the [[1995 Campeonato Brasileiro Série C]] and, more recently, the 2011 Campeonato Paulista - Série A2.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clube15.com.br/hist15.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQF3xcB?url=http://www.clube15.com.br/hist15.htm |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Uma história que se confunde com a história da própria cidade |author=Esporte Clube XV de Novembro |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> Their greatest success in top level competitions was the second place finish in the first division of the Campeonato Paulista in 1976, also under [[Romeu Italo Ripoli|Ripoli]]’s leadership.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.gazetaesportiva.net/noticia/2012/02/palmeiras/em-76-verdao-foi-campeao-com-recorde-diante-do-xv-de-zagueiro-entregador.html |title=Campeonato Paulista de 1976 |author=A Gazeta Esportiva |access-date=6 May 2013}}</ref>

Other clubs that have achieved at least regional or state-level recognition include the Clube Atlético Piracicabano<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clubeatletico.com.br/atletico.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQFy1OL?url=http://www.clubeatletico.com.br/atletico.html |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Clube Atlético Piracicabano - 98 de Tradição |author=Clube Atlético Piracicabano |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> and MAF Futebol Clube.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://saopaulo.entrei.net/empresa/maf-futebol-clube/769653.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQHAgDW?url=http://saopaulo.entrei.net/empresa/maf-futebol-clube/769653.html |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=MAF Futebol Clube |author=Entrei Net |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> The main football stadiums are the Estádio Doutor Kok, with a capacity of 1,500 people,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.conteudoesportivo.com.br/html/futebol/estadios/est_frame.asp?codestadio=20115&UF=SP&FIXO=0&pais=0&NomeUF=S%E3o%20Paulo |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQI6AuQ?url=http://www.conteudoesportivo.com.br/html/futebol/estadios/est_frame.asp?codestadio=20115&UF=SP&FIXO=0&pais=0&NomeUF=S%E3o%20Paulo |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Estádio Doutor Kok |author=Conteúdo Esportivo |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> and the [[Estádio Barão da Serra Negra]], which can hold over 25,000 spectators.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/centros-esportivos-e-de-lazer/73-estadio-municipal-barao-de-serra-negra/93-historico.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQJ6dwv?url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/centros-esportivos-e-de-lazer/73-estadio-municipal-barao-de-serra-negra/93-historico.html |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Estádio Municipal Barão de Serra Negra |author=Antônio Carlos Zinsly de Mattos |date=2 March 2010 |publisher=Secretaria de Esportes, Lazer e Atividades Motoras (SELAM) |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

Beyond football, Piracicaba offers numerous facilities for other sports. The city is home to the only racetrack recognized by the [[Brazilian Automobile Confederation]] and the São Paulo Automobile Federation in the [[interior of São Paulo]], as well as the Professor José Carlos Callado Hebling Sports Complex, which includes a multi-sport gymnasium, official beach volleyball courts with fencing, official ''malha'' courts (a traditional game similar to [[quoits]]), and a grandstand with a capacity for over 700 people. In 2010, the city had approximately 10 sports centers registered with the Department of Sports, Leisure, and Physical Activities (SELAM).<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/centros-esportivos-e-de-lazer/78-outros-equipamentos-esportivos/104-relacao-dos-equipamentos.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQKJ4jZ?url=http://www.selam.piracicaba.sp.gov.br/site/centros-esportivos-e-de-lazer/78-outros-equipamentos-esportivos/104-relacao-dos-equipamentos.html |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Relação dos Equipamentos |author=Secretaria de Esportes, Lazer e Atividades Motoras (SELAM) |date=2010 |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>

== Media ==
[[File:Centro de Piracicaba (1).jpg|thumb|right|Partial view of downtown Piracicaba]]

The city has three main newspapers: the ''Jornal de Piracicaba'', founded in 1900, one of the few newspapers in [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo state]] over 100 years old;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.apj.inf.br/detalhe_associados.php?codigo=11 |title=Jornal de Piracicaba, 100 anos em Piracicaba. |access-date=23 July 2014 |archive-date=22 February 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222170917/http://www.apj.inf.br/detalhe_associados.php?codigo=11 |url-status=dead }}</ref> the ''Gazeta de Piracicaba'', which began in 1882, ceased in 1935, and resumed circulation in 2003;<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.gruporac.com.br/gz-piracicaba/ |title=Jornal A Gazeta de Piracicaba |publisher=Grupo RAC |access-date=23 July 2014 |archive-date=27 July 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140727040637/http://www.gruporac.com.br/gz-piracicaba/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> and ''A Tribuna Piracicabana'', established in 1974.<ref>[http://www.linguagemviva.com.br/tribuna.html A Tribuna Piracicabana, história, 40 anos em Piracicaba.]</ref> In addition to these, Piracicaba has several neighborhood newspapers with limited circulation, focusing exclusively on local content.

The city is also home to locally produced magazines, such as ''Trifatto'',<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.trifatto.com.br/revista. |title=Revista Trifatto, atendendo a cidade de Piracicaba, desde 2007. |access-date=23 July 2014}}</ref> and ''Arraso''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Revista Trifatto, Piracicaba e região. |url=https://view.officeapps.live.com/op/view.aspx?src=https%3A%2F%2Fsiave.camarapiracicaba.sp.gov.br%2Farquivo%3FId%3D321460&wdOrigin=BROWSELINK |access-date=23 July 2014}}</ref>

== Holidays ==
Piracicaba observes four [[Public holiday|municipal holidays]], eight national holidays, and six optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the feast of [[Anthony of Padua|Saint Anthony]], the city’s patron saint, on 13 June; [[Black Awareness Day]], on 20 November; and the feast of [[Immaculate Conception|Our Lady of Conception]], on 8 December.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://buenoecostanze.adv.br/utilidades/feriados-municipais/26133-feriados-municipais.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68EQLGOms?url=http://buenoecostanze.adv.br/utilidades/feriados-municipais/26133-feriados-municipais.html |archive-date=7 June 2012 |title=Feriados municipais |author=Bueno e Costanze |date=10 May 2011 |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref> According to federal law nº 9.093, enacted on 12 September 1995, municipalities may have up to four religious municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{Cite web |url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tscUhIs?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref><ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaLei">{{Cite web |url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tsfGoVI?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Lei Nº 9.093, de 12 de setembro de 1995 |author=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=6 June 2012}}</ref>
