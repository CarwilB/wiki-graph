'''San Fernando''' is a city located in the Mexican [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Tamaulipas]]; it serves as the seat of the surrounding [[Municipalities of Tamaulipas|municipality]] of the same name. It is about {{convert|85|mi|km}} away from [[Brownsville, Texas]], United States.<ref>Schiller, Dane. "[http://www.mysanantonio.com/news/local_news/article/Zeta-captives-have-to-fight-or-die-1420451.php#next Zeta captives have to fight or die]." ''[[Houston Chronicle]]'' at the ''[[San Antonio Express-News]]''. Updated Wednesday June 15, 2011. Retrieved on January 4, 2012.</ref> The municipality has a population of 57,220, while the city itself has a population of 29,665.

==History==
===Massacres===
San Fernando, Tamaulipas, is notorious for experiencing two of the largest recorded [[list of massacres in Mexico|massacres]] of the [[Mexican drug war]]. The first [[massacre]], known as the [[2010 San Fernando massacre]], occurred following a gunfight in Tamaulipas between drug cartel gunmen and Mexican authorities, in which three gunmen and a marine were killed.<ref name="Reuters 72" /> After the authorities patrolled the nearby area, they discovered 72 bodies in a remote ranch in Tamaulipas.<ref>{{cite news |title=72 bodies found on ranch in Mexico |url=http://www.rte.ie/news/2010/0825/mexico.html |access-date=August 26, 2010 |newspaper=[[RTÉ News and Current Affairs|RTÉ News]] |date=August 25, 2010 |archive-date=August 27, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100827181822/http://www.rte.ie/news/2010/0825/mexico.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The bodies were found in a room, some of which were piled up on top of each other.<ref name="BBC 72" /> It was "the biggest single discovery of its kind" in the ongoing drug war.<ref name="Reuters 72">{{cite news |title=Drug hitmen dump 72 bodies at Mexican ranch |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/idUSTRE67O2NF20100826 |access-date=August 26, 2010 |newspaper=[[Reuters]] |date=August 25, 2010 |author=Robin Emmott |author2=Patrick Rucker |author3=Miguel Angel Gutierrez |archive-date=August 26, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100826173721/http://www.reuters.com/article/idUSTRE67O2NF20100826 |url-status=live }}</ref> The 58 men and 14 women were believed to be undocumented migrants from South and Central America trying to cross the border to the United States.<ref name="BBC 72">{{cite news |title=Murdered bodies found in Mexico 'were migrants' |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-11090563 |access-date=August 26, 2010 |newspaper=[[BBC News]] |date=August 25, 2010 |archive-date=August 26, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100826035137/http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-11090563 |url-status=live }}</ref> A surviving migrant claims that the migrants were kidnapped by the [[Los Zetas]] cartel and killed for refusing to do work for them.<ref>{{cite news |title=Survivor: Drug gang massacred 72 migrants in northern Mexico |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2010/08/25/AR2010082506776.html |access-date=August 26, 2010 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=August 25, 2010 |author=William Booth |archive-date=October 10, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121010161653/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2010/08/25/AR2010082506776.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Twenty one rifles, 101 ammunition clips, four bullet-proof vests, camouflage uniforms and four vehicles were seized by officials.<ref>{{cite news |title=72 bodies found in Mexico ranch |url=http://www.hindustantimes.com/72-bodies-found-in-Mexico-ranch/Article1-591920.aspx |access-date=August 26, 2010 |newspaper=[[Hindustan Times]] |date=August 26, 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100828150704/http://www.hindustantimes.com/72-bodies-found-in-Mexico-ranch/Article1-591920.aspx |archive-date=August 28, 2010 }}</ref>

The second massacre, the [[2011 San Fernando massacre]], was discovered after Mexican authorities exhumed more than 40 [[mass grave]]s, with a final body count of 193 corpses.<ref>{{cite news |title=Hallan 7 nuevas narcofosas en San Fernando Tamaulipas suman 193 cadáveres |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KVt2RRlRmEE |newspaper=Milenio TV |date=June 7, 2011 }}</ref>

The massacres and the Mexican government's response are the subject of ''San Fernando: Última Parada'', a book written by journalist [[Marcela Turati]].<ref>{{Cite book |last=Turati |first=Marcela |title=San Fernando:última parada: viaje al crimen autorizado en Tamaulipas |date=2023 |publisher=Penguin Random House Grupo Editorial |isbn=978-607-38-1205-4 |location=México }}</ref>

==Climate==
San Fernando has a [[humid subtropical climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]]: ''Cfa'') bordering closely with a [[hot semi-arid climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]]: ''BSh'').
