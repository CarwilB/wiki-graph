'''Mérida Municipality''' is one of the 106 [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipalities]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Yucatán (state)|Yucatán]] containing 858.41&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> of land with the head or seat being the city of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]]. Because the archaeological remains of the [[Maya civilization|Maya]] reminded the Spaniards of the ancient city of [[Mérida, Spain]], which was marked by [[Augusta Emerita|Roman archaeological sites]], they renamed the site of T-hó after the Spanish city.<ref name="municipios"/>

==History==
What now constitutes the head of the municipality of Mérida, was a pre-Hispanic Itza Mayan town called T-hó ([[Yucatec Maya language]] meaning "five hills"), which was founded around the 12th century AD.<ref name=municipios /> By the time of the Spanish arrival, the city was virtually abandoned, though still used as a ceremonial center and its remnant buildings were of impressive monumental scale. [[Francisco de Montejo the Younger]] established the city of Mérida on the site on 6 January 1542.<ref name="Barteet (2007)">{{cite book|last1=Barteet|first1=C. Cody|title=Colonial contradictions in the Casa de Montejo in Mérida, Yucatan Space, society, and self-representation at the edge of viceregal Mexico|date=2007|publisher=State University of New York|location=Binghamton, New York|isbn=978-0-549-13267-7|page=62|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Xc1kRZTDsysC&pg=PR4|access-date=31 July 2015}}</ref>
In the first year of the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán|conquest]], Montejo ordered the establishment of 54 [[encomiendas]] in favor of his soldiers and confirmed the three principal municipalities to be [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]], [[Valladolid, Yucatán|Valladolid]], and [[Campeche, Campeche|San Francisco de Campeche]].<ref name="Pinet Plasencia (1998)">{{cite book|editor-last1=Pinet Plasencia|editor-first1=Adela |title=La Península de Yucatán en el Archivo General de la Nación|date=1998|publisher=Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México|location=San Cristóbal de las Casas, Chiapas|isbn=978-9-683-65757-2|page=95|edition=1st|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=l0L5_lzURQkC&pg=PA94|access-date=31 July 2015|language=es}}</ref>

Overlapping jurisdictions occurred for the area at various times with the administration being under the [[Real Audiencia of Guatemala|Audiencia de Guatemala]] in 1549, but also the [[Viceroyalty of New Spain]] from 1549-1552. Then between 1552 and 1561 the area returned to the Audiencia de Guatemala and between 1561 and 1565 was again administered under the Viceroyalty of New Spain.<ref name="state divisions">{{cite web|title=Estado de Yucatán. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995|url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/integracion/pais/divi_terri/1810-1985/yuc/YUCATAN.pdf|website=inegi|publisher=Instituto Nacional de Estadística, Geografía e Informática|access-date=31 July 2015|location=Aguascalientes, Mexico|language=es|date=1996|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151223101757/http://www.inegi.org.mx/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/integracion/pais/divi_terri/1810-1985/yuc/YUCATAN.pdf|archive-date=23 December 2015}}</ref> In 1617, Yucatán became a [[Captaincy General of Yucatán|Captaincy General]] in its own stead.<ref name="Captaincy General">{{cite web|title=New Spain AD 1535 - 1821|url=http://www.historyfiles.co.uk/KingListsAmericas/CentralMexico.htm|website=History Files UK|publisher=Kessler Associates|access-date=31 July 2015}}</ref>

Yucatán declared its independence from the Spanish Crown in 1821<ref name=municipios /> and in 1825, the area was distributed into 15 administrative partitions including Bacalar, Campeche, Hecelchakán, Hunucmá, Ichmul, Isla del Carmen, Izamal, Lerma, Mama, Mérida, Oxkutzcab, Seibaplaya, Sotuta, Tizimín and Valladolid. In 1840, Yucatán declared itself to be a free, independent nation<ref>''Estado de Yucatán. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995'', p 69</ref> with its capital located in Mérida. The following year treaties were signed for the peninsula to rejoin Mexico, but by 1842, independence was again declared. In 1843, a new treaty of reinstatement was signed, but in 1845 the peninsula withdrew because Mexico had not complied with the terms of the 1843 agreement. Finally in 1846 the peninsula agreed to reintegrate into Mexico<ref>''Estado de Yucatán. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995'', p 70</ref> but the outbreak of the [[Caste War of Yucatán|Caste War]], in 1847, an indigenous rebellion that took place throughout the Yucatán, delayed the full implementation until 1849.<ref name="caste war">{{cite web|title=Campeche|url=http://inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM04campeche/index.html|website=INAFED|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=31 July 2015|language=es}}</ref>

The Constitution of 1850, redrafted the administrative divisions and made headquarters for the 17 partitions at: Bacalar, Bolonchenticul, Campeche, Espita, Hequelchakan, Isla del Carmen, Izamal, Maxcanú, Mérida, Motul, Peto, Seibaplaya, Sotuta, Tekax, Ticul, Tizimin, and  Valladolid.<ref>''Estado de Yucatán. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995'', p 73</ref> In 1918, the modern municipality of Mérida was confirmed as the seat of the municipio and capital of the State.<ref name=municipios />

==Governance==
The municipal president is elected for a three-year term. The town council has seventeen councilpersons, who serve as Secretary and councilors of Markets, Utilities and Public Safety; Administration; Social Development; Public Security; Entertainment; Urban Development and Ecology; Public Works; Youth And Sports; Women; Health and Disability; Economic Development; Tourism; Heritage; Education; and Culture.<ref name=governance>{{cite web|title=Mérida| url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|website=inafed|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=31 July 2015|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es}}</ref>

The Municipal Council administers the business of the municipality. It is responsible for budgeting and expenditures and producing all required reports for all branches of the municipal administration. Annually it determines educational standards for schools.<ref name=governance />

The Police Commissioners ensure public order and safety. They are tasked with enforcing regulations, distributing materials and administering rulings of general compliance issued by the council.<ref name=governance />

==Geography==
The land throughout the municipality is virtually flat, without any areas of the elevation, and like much of the [[Yucatan Peninsula]] has no surface water streams. There are [[cenotes]] both underground and at the surface (collapsed caverns).  The climate is semi-humid, with temperature range between a maximum of 40&nbsp;°C and minimum 14&nbsp;°C.<ref name=governance />

==Communities==
The head of the municipality is [[Mérida, Yucatán]]. ==Local festivals==
Every year on 6 January the founding of the city is acknowledged in a citywide festival. Also annually from 27 September to 14 October is a celebration in honor of Santo Cristo de las Ampollas.<ref name="municipios"/>
