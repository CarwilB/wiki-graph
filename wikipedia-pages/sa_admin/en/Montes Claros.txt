'''Montes Claros''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the northern region of the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]]. Situated north of the state capital, [[Belo Horizonte]], it lies approximately {{convert|422|km|mi|abbr=on}} away. The municipality spans an area of {{convert|3589.811|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}, with {{convert|73.51|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} within its [[urban area]]. As of the 2022 census, its population was {{formatnum:414240}}, making it the fifth most populous municipality in Minas Gerais.

Montes Claros achieved emancipation in the 19th century and has long relied on industry and commerce as key economic drivers, establishing itself as a regional industrial hub. The municipality is currently divided into ten districts and further subdivided into approximately 200 neighborhoods and villages.<ref name="GrandeBrasil_MontesClaros"/> It boasts a variety of natural, historical, and cultural attractions, including the Milton Prates Municipal Park, Guimarães Rosa Park, and Sapucaia Park, which are significant green spaces, as well as notable structures such as the Cathedral of [[Our Lady of Aparecida]] and the Small Church of the Little Hills, alongside numerous [[Archaeological site|archaeological sites]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/>

== History ==
=== Origins ===
Until the 1660s, the lands now comprising Montes Claros were inhabited solely by the [[Indigenous peoples of Brazil|indigenous]] Anais and Tapuia peoples. Around 1554, the Espinosa Expedition, consisting of 12 ''[[bandeirantes]]'', explored the region in search of precious stones, venturing into the northern backlands of the Captaincy of São Paulo and Minas de Ouro. However, these explorers did not establish permanent settlements.

In 1674, [[Fernão Dias Pais]], known as the "Emerald Hunter", led a [[Bandeirantes|''bandeira'']] to explore the region for precious stones.<ref name="Prefeitura_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.montesclaros.mg.gov.br/cidade/aspectosgerais/historia.htm |title=History |author=Montes Claros Municipal Government |access-date=May 11, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210223144526/http://www.montesclaros.mg.gov.br/cidade/aspectosgerais/historia.htm |archive-date=February 23, 2021}}</ref> Antônio Gonçalves Figueira, a member of Fernão Dias’ expedition, accompanied it to the banks of the [[Paraopeba River]], where, alongside Matias Cardoso de Almeida, he parted ways with the leader, who later returned to São Paulo two years later. There, Antônio and Matias established farms, which grew into settlements, displaced indigenous populations, and continued exploiting the region’s resources.

By a royal charter dated April 12, 1707, Antônio Gonçalves Figueira received a ''sesmaria'' grant of one league in width by three in length, forming the Montes Claros Farm (one of three farms) located at the headwaters of the [[Verde Grande River]] on its left bank. To access markets for cattle, Gonçalves Figueira built roads to Tranqueiras in Bahia and the [[São Francisco River]].<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> A native of São Paulo, Gonçalves Figueira later returned to his home region, entrusting the Montes Claros Farm to his brother Manuel Afonso de Siqueira, who owned neighboring farms.

Later, Gonçalves Figueira’s descendants sold portions of the Montes Claros Farm. Ensign José Lopes de Carvalho, whose nephew married Manuel Afonso de Siqueira’s granddaughter, acquired part of the farm and built a chapel, now the Nossa Senhora da Conceição and São José de Montes Claros Matrix Church. Around this chapel, the Arraial das Formigas developed, the second settlement on the Montes Claros Farm, which years later became the city bearing the same name.<ref>DE PAULA, Hermes. ''Montes Claros - sua história sua gente, seus costumes'', Belo Horizonte, 1979</ref>

[[File:Casa do Alferes José Lopes de Carvalho.jpg|thumb|upright=1.3| House of Ensign José Lopes de Carvalho]]

=== Administrative evolution ===
One hundred twenty-four years after Antônio Gonçalves Figueira obtained the ''sesmaria'', the village had developed sufficiently to gain independence, separating from the municipality of Serro Frio (now [[Serro]]). Through the efforts of local political leaders, the municipality was established by the Law of October 13, 1831, named Montes Claros de Formigas. Its independent municipal life began in 1832 with the election and inauguration of the first Municipal Chamber.

By 1857, the then-village (municipality) of Montes Claros had just over 2,000 inhabitants, with infrastructure typical of most municipalities in the province. The municipality of Montes Claros de Formigas had existed since 1831 and began its independent municipal administration in 1832 with the inauguration of the Municipal Chamber and the election of its Executive Agent, equivalent to a mayor. Under Law 802 of July 3, 1857, the village was elevated to city status, officially adopting the name Montes Claros.[9] Despite starting its independent municipal life in 1832, the city traditionally celebrates its anniversary on July 3, referencing its elevation to city status rather than October 16, 1832, the date of its emancipation from Serro. This history was clarified in the article “The Invention of July 3” published in the journal Unimontes Científica. 

Under provincial law no. 1398 of November 27, 1867, and state law no. 2 of September 14, 1891, the district of Brejos das Almas (formerly São Gonçalo do Brejo das Almas) was created, becoming the municipality’s first district. Over time, Montes Claros’ territory underwent several territorial losses and administrative reorganizations, eventually comprising its current districts in the 1980s: Aparecida do Mundo Novo, Ermidinha, Miralta, Nova Esperança, Panorâmica, Santa Rosa de Lima, São João da Vereda, São Pedro de Garça, and Vila Nova de Minas.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico">{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/montesclaros.pdf |title=History |author=Encyclopedia of Brazilian Municipalities |date=2007 |publisher=IBGE Library |access-date=May 11, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171121212442/https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/montesclaros.pdf |archive-date=November 21, 2017}}</ref>

=== After the foundation ===
Following political emancipation, population growth necessitated investments in municipal urban infrastructure.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> In 1871, the Charity Hospital, later known as the [[Santa Casa da Misericórdia|Santa Casa de Caridade]], was established. On February 2, 1880, the Montes Claros Normal School was founded. On February 24, 1884, the first issue of the weekly newspaper “Correio do Norte” was published. On September 14, 1886, the Santa Cruz Chapel, commonly known as the Morrinho Chapel, was inaugurated. On October 27, 1892, the city’s first telegraph line was established.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>
[[File:Primeiro carro de Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|left|First automobiles in the city, 1920.]]

The industry in Montes Claros began with the Cedro textile factory in 1882, which was destroyed by a major fire on July 25, 1889. It provided primary education for its workers.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/5yc8CAoMF |title=Vasco Azevedo and Primary Education for Textile Industry Workers in Early 20th Century Minas Gerais |author=Daqui de Pitangui |date=March 26, 2011 |access-date=May 11, 2011}}</ref> In commerce, the Municipal Market, inaugurated on September 3, 1899, stood out. Located in what is now Dr. Carlos Versiani Square, it was an imposing structure with a tall tower housing a clock donated by Carlota Versiani, which chimed hourly.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> Throughout the 20th century, notable developments included the arrival of electricity on January 20, 1917, the first automobile on November 10, 1920, the establishment of potable water services on December 18, 1938, and the installation of intercity telephone services on June 30, 1956.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

=== Economic and demographic growth ===
Montes Claros experienced significant industrialization starting in the 1970s. Industrial activities, supported by fiscal and financial incentives from federal, state, and municipal governments through the [[Superintendency for the Development of the Northeast]] (SUDENE), attracted substantial migration, leading to unplanned urban growth. This rapid urbanization, coupled with inadequate planning, resulted in intra-urban spatial disparities, with several areas marked by poverty.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fobservatoriogeograficoamericalatina.org.mx%2Fegal10%2FGeografiasocioeconomica%2FGeografiadelapoblacion%2F29.pdf&date=2011-06-06 |title=Territorial Expansion and Spaces of Poverty in the City of Montes Claros |author=Marcos Esdras Leite and Anete Marília Pereira |date=March 26, 2005 |publisher=Geographical Observatory of Latin America |access-date=June 6, 2011}}</ref> In the 1980s, significant urban changes occurred, including the occupation of vacant urban areas in the southern region, avenue renovations to improve traffic flow, [[verticalization]] in the central area, shifts in the spatial distribution of activities, and peripheral expansion.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/>

Over time, efforts to reduce poverty levels were implemented. In 1970, 74.79% of the population lived in poverty, which decreased to 33.17% by 2001.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/> The rural landscape has increasingly been replaced by [[urban area|urban zones]] to accommodate the demands of urban expansion driven by growing productive activities (industry, commerce, and services) and rising housing needs due to population concentration. The boundary between rural and urban areas is becoming less distinct, with the rural population declining annually.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/><ref name="População_Sexo">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.cnm.org.br%2Fdemografia%2Fmu_dem_pop_total.asp%3FiIdMun%3D100131502+&date=2011-05-11 |title=Demography - Total Population |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM) |date=2008 |access-date=May 11, 2011}}</ref>

== Geography ==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], Montes Claros covers an area of {{convert|3589.811|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}, with {{convert|73.51|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} constituting the urban area.<ref name="IBGE_MontesClaros"/> It is located at 16°44'06" south [[latitude]] and 43°51'43" west [[longitude]], approximately {{convert|422|km|mi|abbr=on}} north of the state capital. Its neighboring municipalities are [[São João da Ponte]] to the north; [[Capitão Enéas]] to the northeast; [[Francisco Sá, Minas Gerais|Francisco Sá]] to the east; [[Juramento]] and [[Glaucilândia]] to the southeast; [[Bocaiúva|Bocaiuva]] and [[Engenheiro Navarro]] to the south; [[Claro dos Poções]] to the southwest; [[São João da Lagoa]] and [[Coração de Jesus]] to the west; and [[Mirabela]] and [[Patis, Minas Gerais|Patis]] to the northwest.<ref name="cidadesnet.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fcidadesnet.com%2Fmunicipios%2Fmontesclaros.htm+&date=2011-05-14 |title=Montes Claros - MG |author=Cidades.Net |access-date=May 14, 2011}}</ref><ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/>

[[File:Fazenda em Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|right|Pastures on undulating terrain in the rural area of Montes Claros.]]
Under the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] effective since 2017, established by the IBGE,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/geociencias-novoportal/cartas-e-mapas/redes-geograficas/2231-np-divisoes-regionais-do-brasil/15778-divisoes-regionais-do-brasil.html |title=Regional Division of Brazil |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=November 21, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171121155206/https://www.ibge.gov.br/geociencias-novoportal/cartas-e-mapas/redes-geograficas/2231-np-divisoes-regionais-do-brasil/15778-divisoes-regionais-do-brasil.html |archive-date=November 21, 2017}}</ref> the municipality belongs to the Intermediate and Immediate [[List of Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions of Minas Gerais|Geographic Regions]] of Montes Claros.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{cite web |url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Database of Municipalities in the Immediate and Intermediate Geographical Regions of Brazil |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=November 21, 2017}}</ref> Previously, under the division into [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregions]] and [[Microregions of Brazil|microregions]], it was part of the Montes Claros microregion within the North of Minas mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=Brazilian Territorial Division 2016 |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=November 21, 2017}}</ref>

The city center has an average elevation of {{convert|655.21|m|ft|abbr=on}}. The highest point in the municipality is Morro Vermelho, reaching {{convert|1075|m|ft|abbr=on}}. The terrain is predominantly undulating, characterized by rolling hills and mountains. The lowest elevation, {{convert|502|m|ft|abbr=on}}, is at the [[River mouth|mouth]] of the Ribeirão do Ouro.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> Approximately 60% of the municipal terrain is undulating, 30% is flat, and 10% is mountainous.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/>

Montes Claros lies within the São Francisco River Basin, and is traversed by the Vieira River, Cedro River,<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> [[Verde Grande River]], [[Pacuí River (São Francisco River tributary)|Pacuí River]], São Lamberto River,<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fgeografia.htm+&date=2011-05-14 |title=Geography |author=Montes Claros Municipal Government |access-date=May 14, 2011}}</ref> and Riachão River.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.atlasdasaguas.ufv.br/alto_e_medio_sao_francisco/impacto_ambiental_relevante_na_sub_bacia_do_rio_riachao_norte_de_minas_gerais.html |title=Significant Environmental Impact in the Riachão River Sub-Basin, Northern Minas Gerais |author=Water Atlas |date=2010 |access-date=June 7, 2011}}</ref> The municipality also features several [[lagoons]]: Tiriricas, Lagoão, Periperi, São João, Brejão, Garça, Vereda dos Caetanos, Mombuca, São Jorge, Freitas, Matos, and Barreiro. The soil predominantly consists of ancient [[Precambrian]] formations, with occurrences of [[siltstone]], [[slate]], [[limestone]], [[phyllite]], [[calcite]], [[galena]], [[iron ore]], [[potassium nitrate]], [[rock crystal]], and [[alluvial gold]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/>

=== Geology ===
A [[fault (geology)|geological fault]] surrounds the city, identified as the cause of seismic activity. Extending approximately {{convert|3|km|mi|abbr=on}} from the Vila Atlântica neighborhood to the Serra do Mel, it lies at a depth of {{convert|1.5|to|2|km|mi|abbr=on}}. Due to this fault, the ground experiences near-continuous tremors, according to reports from professors at the Department of Geophysics at the [[University of São Paulo]] (USP) and the Seismological Observatory at the [[University of Brasília]] (UnB).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bhaz.com.br/bombeiros-receberam-mais-de-500-ligacoes-apos-tremores-de-terra-registrados-em-montes-claros/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6D2Ob5myA?url=http://www.bhaz.com.br/bombeiros-receberam-mais-de-500-ligacoes-apos-tremores-de-terra-registrados-em-montes-claros/ |archive-date=December 19, 2012 |title=Fire Department Received Over 500 Calls After Earthquakes Recorded in Montes Claros |author=Belo Horizonte de A a Z |date=December 19, 2012 |access-date=December 19, 2012 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The most intense earthquake recorded reached approximately 4.2 on the [[Richter scale]], occurring on May 19, 2012, affecting 60 homes, six of which were declared uninhabitable and two interdicted by the city’s Civil Defense, leaving families displaced. Tremors exceeding 5 on the Richter scale are considered unlikely and unpredictable but possible. However, as tremors last only seconds, they pose no significant risk of severe damage. Seismographs recorded 174 earthquakes in Montes Claros from July to December 2012.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://exame.abril.com.br/meio-ambiente-e-energia/noticias/montes-claros-registra-pelo-menos-dois-tremores-de-terra |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6D2Odpz7g?url=http://exame.abril.com.br/meio-ambiente-e-energia/noticias/montes-claros-registra-pelo-menos-dois-tremores-de-terra |archive-date=December 19, 2012 |title=Montes Claros Records At Least Two Earthquakes |author=Paula Laboissière |date=December 19, 2012 |publisher=Revista Exame |access-date=December 19, 2012 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The climate of Montes Claros is classified as [[tropical climate|tropical]] (type ''Aw'' according to the [[Köppen climate classification|Köppen system]]),<ref name="vetmedunivienna">{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fkoeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at%2Fpics%2Fkottek_et_al_2006.gif&date=2011-04-24 |title=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger Climate Classification |publisher=Institute for Veterinary Public Health |access-date=May 14, 2011}}</ref> characterized by dry, mild [[winter]]s and rainy [[summer]]s with high [[temperature]]s, with an annual [[precipitation]] index of approximately {{convert|1000|mm|in|abbr=on}}. During the [[dry season]], [[relative humidity]] levels often drop significantly, frequently falling below 30%,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jornalmontesclaros.com.br/2014/07/09/norte-de-minas-subiu-para-129-o-numero-de-municipios-mineiros-em-situacao-de-emergencia-por-causa-da-seca.html |title=Northern Minas - Number of Municipalities in Emergency Due to Drought Rises to 129 |date=July 9, 2014 |publisher=Jornal Montes Claros |access-date=August 20, 2014 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140821004815/http://jornalmontesclaros.com.br/2014/07/09/norte-de-minas-subiu-para-129-o-numero-de-municipios-mineiros-em-situacao-de-emergencia-por-causa-da-seca.html |archive-date=August 21, 2014}}</ref> and sometimes as low as 20%,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://montesclaros.com/noticias.asp?codigo=45023 |title=WHO Calls for Alerts for Humidity Below 20%; It Reached 13% in Montes Claros |date=August 1, 2009 |access-date=August 20, 2014 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140821004232/http://montesclaros.com/noticias.asp?codigo=45023 |archive-date=August 21, 2014}}</ref> well below the 60% recommended by the [[World Health Organization]] (WHO).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.climatempo.com.br/destaques/2011/07/13/tempo-de-seca-no-brasil-atencao-com-a-baixa-umidade-do-ar/ |title=Dry Season in Brazil: Attention to Low Humidity |author=Climatempo |date=July 13, 2011 |access-date=August 15, 2011}}</ref> During this period, [[wildfire]]s become more frequent, particularly in rural areas, contributing to air quality degradation due to pollutant emissions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.gazetanortemineira.com.br%2Fnoticias.php%3Fid%3D3703+&date=2011-05-14 |title=Wildfires Cause Concern in Northern Minas |author=Paula Machado |date=September 17, 2009 |publisher=Jornal Gazeta Norte Mineira |access-date=May 14, 2011}}</ref>

=== Ecology and environment ===
Montes Claros is part of the [[Cerrado]] biome, featuring deciduous and semi-deciduous cerrado, with minor occurrences of evergreen cerrado and [[Caatinga]] hipoxerophytic vegetation. The regional [[flora]] includes species such as ''[[Tabebuia]]'', ''[[Caryocar brasiliense]]'', ''[[Aegiphila fluminensis]]'', ''[[Hymenaea courbaril]]'', ''[[Bromelia laciniosa]]'', ''[[Schinopsis brasiliensis]]'', and ''[[ceiba speciosa]]'', alongside a rich variety of medicinal plants.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/>

Under Law No. 3754 of June 15, 2007, which establishes the municipal policy for the protection, preservation, conservation, control, and recovery of Montes Claros’ environment, the Municipal Council for Environmental Protection and Conservation (CODEMA) is the public agency responsible for protecting, conserving, and improving the city's environment. It also oversees the management of the municipality’s Environmental Protection Areas.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fpublica_legais%2Fleis_pdf%2Fleis-2007%2Fjun-07%2Flei-3754-07.pdf+&date=2011-05-14 |title=Law No. 3754 of June 15, 2007 |author=Montes Claros Municipal Government |date=June 15, 2007 |access-date=May 14, 2011}}</ref>

== Subdivisions ==
Montes Claros is divided into ten districts: Aparecida do Mundo Novo, Ermidinha, Miralta, Montes Claros (Seat), Nova Esperança, Panorâmica, Santa Rosa de Lima, São João da Vereda, São Pedro de Garça, and Vila Nova de Minas.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> In 2000, their populations were 953, {{formatnum:1614}}, 827, {{formatnum:293903}}, {{formatnum:2718}}, 222, {{formatnum:2873}}, {{formatnum:1386}}, {{formatnum:1051}}, and {{formatnum:1400}}, respectively, according to the IBGE census of that year.<ref name="IBGE_Censo2000">{{cite web|url=ftp://ftp.ibge.gov.br/Censos/Censo_Demografico_2000/Dados_do_Universo/Meso_Microregioes_Distritos_Subdistritos_Bairros/Minas_Gerais.zip |title=2000 Demographic Census - Microregions, Districts, Subdistricts, and Neighborhoods |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) |date=November 29, 2001 |access-date=May 20, 2011}}</ref> Throughout the 20th century, several districts were created, elevated to city status, or abolished, with the last change occurring on October 8, 1982, with the creation of the Aparecida do Mundo Novo district.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/>

Currently, Montes Claros has approximately 160 neighborhoods, according to the “Grande Brasil” portal.<ref name="GrandeBrasil_MontesClaros">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.grandebrasil.com.br%2FBrasil%2FCidades%2F0d1F%2FMunicipio_De_Montes_Claros_Estado_De_Minas_Gerais_mg&date=2011-05-20 |title=Municipality of Montes Claros, Minas Gerais (MG) |author=Grande Brasil |access-date=May 20, 2011}}</ref> One of the most populous is Major Prates, home to approximately 25,000 people.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fintertvonline.globo.com%2Fmg%2Fnoticias.php%3Fid%3D12717&date=2011-05-20 |title=Major Prates Requests More Security |author=In360 |access-date=May 20, 2011}}</ref>

The first population estimate was recorded when Montes Claros was known as Arraial das Formigas. In 1817, [[Augustin Saint-Hilaire|Saint-Hilaire]] wrote during a visit: “''This settlement, which may currently comprise two hundred houses and over eight hundred souls, is certainly one of the most beautiful I have seen in the Province of Minas''.”<ref>SAINT HILAIRE, A. ''Viagem pelas províncias do Rio de Janeiro e Minas Gerais''. Belo Horizonte: Itatiaia, 2000</ref>

According to the 1832 Imperial Brazilian Census, Montes Claros had {{formatnum:3350}} inhabitants, including 499 enslaved individuals (approximately 0.24% of the total in the Province of Minas Gerais).<ref>Botelho, Tarcísio Rodrigues. 1997. “Batismo E Compadrio De Escravos: Montes Claros (MG), século XIX”. ''Locus: Revista De História'' 3</ref>

The 1872 General Census of the Empire recorded {{formatnum:8862}} free individuals and {{formatnum:1143}} enslaved individuals in Montes Claros, totaling {{formatnum:10005}} inhabitants in the seat. Including districts and rural areas, the total population was {{formatnum:40317}}.<ref>BRASIL, Recenseamento Geral do Império, 1872</ref>

On October 30, 1884, the Jequitaí district was elevated to the status of “City of Jequitaí,” reducing Montes Claros’ total population. In 1897, a study by Judge Antônio Augusto Velloso estimated the municipal population at 15,000, with districts and rural areas totaling about 42,000.<ref>VELLOSO, Antônio Augusto. ''Corografia Mineira''. Belo Horizonte, 1897</ref>

On September 7, 1923, the Brejo das Almas district was elevated to the city of [[Francisco Sá, Minas Gerais|Francisco Sá]]. On September 10, 1925, the [[Coração de Jesus]] district was elevated to city status, impacting Montes Claros’ total population count.

In 1940, the IBGE recorded {{formatnum:61532}} inhabitants, including {{formatnum:28798}} whites, {{formatnum:10326}} blacks, 2 Asians, {{formatnum:22396}} mixed-race individuals, and 10 undeclared.<ref>Recenseamento 1940 IBGE https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/65/cd_1940_p13_t2_mg.pdf</ref> The 1950 census counted {{formatnum:71736}} inhabitants.

On December 12, 1953, the [[Juramento]] district was separated from Montes Claros and elevated to municipal status, leading to a population decrease in the 1955 census.

In 2000, according to IBGE census data, the population consisted of {{formatnum:131231}} whites (42.75%), {{formatnum:16691}} blacks (5.44%), {{formatnum:156126}} mixed-race individuals (50.86%), 260 Asians (0.08%), {{formatnum:1153}} indigenous (0.38%), and {{formatnum:1485}} undeclared (0.48%).<ref name="SIDRA_Raça">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P |title=Population of Montes Claros by Race and Color |author=Sidra (IBGE Automatic Data Recovery System) |date=2000 |access-date=May 21, 2011}}</ref>

In 2010, the IBGE counted {{formatnum:361971}} inhabitants, making Montes Claros the sixth most populous municipality in the state and the 62nd in the country, with a [[population density]] of 101.05 inhabitants per km².<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/tabelas_pdf/total_populacao_minas_gerais.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62vRN1jFU?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/tabelas_pdf/total_populacao_minas_gerais.pdf |archive-date=November 3, 2011 |title=2010 Census - Minas Gerais |author=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) |date=November 29, 2010 |access-date=September 6, 2013 |url-status=live}}</ref> According to the 2010 census, {{formatnum:174281}} were men and {{formatnum:187690}} were women. Additionally, {{formatnum:344479}} lived in the urban area and {{formatnum:17492}} in the [[rural area]].<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010" />

A 2010 genetic study revealed the population’s genetic composition as 52% European, 39% African, and 9.0% indigenous.<ref>{{Cite web| title=Development of two multiplex mini-sequencing panels of ancestry informative SNPs for studies in Latin Americans: an application to populations of the State of Minas Gerais (Brazil) | url=http://www.funpecrp.com.br/gmr/year2010/vol9-4/pdf/gmr911.pdf | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131228122241/http://www.funpecrp.com.br/gmr/year2010/vol9-4/pdf/gmr911.pdf | archive-date=2013-12-28}}</ref>

According to the 2022 IBGE census, the population self-identified as 60% mixed-race, 28% white, 10% black, 0.11% indigenous, and 0.1% Asian.<ref>{{Cite web | title=Censo 2022: veja quais são os municípios mais amarelos, brancos, indígenas, pardos e pretos do Brasil {{!}} Censo {{!}} G1 | url=https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2023/12/22/censo-2022-veja-quais-sao-os-municipios-mais-amarelos-brancos-indigenas-pardos-e-pretos-do-brasil.ghtml | access-date=2025-07-23 | website=g1.globo.com}}</ref>

=== Human Development Index ===
As in much of Brazil, the rapid population growth in Montes Claros during the 1970s, driven by intense migration and poor planning, led to intra-urban spatial disparities, with several areas marked by poverty.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/> Until around 1970, urban occupation was limited to the city center and adjacent neighborhoods. From that period, significant urban changes occurred, with the northern, eastern, and southern zones experiencing more intense population growth than the wealthier western zone. Urban expansion was uneven, resulting in persistent urban voids alongside underdeveloped neighborhoods and [[slums]].<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/>

In the urban area, population distribution correlates closely with residents’ socioeconomic status, contributing to [[economic inequality]] in the city. Neighborhoods near the center are home to higher-income populations, while peripheral areas house the most underdeveloped communities.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/> According to the IBGE, in 2002, the Living Conditions Index (ICV) was highest in the Cidade Santa Maria (0.81), Todos os Santos (0.68), and São Luís (0.73) neighborhoods, located centrally, and lowest in Vila Mauriceia (0.31), Vera Cruz (0.37), and Vila Atlântida (0.27), all situated in peripheral areas.<ref name="Observatório_Crescimento"/>

In 2009, the IBGE reported a [[Gini coefficient]] of 0.41, indicating social inequality, with 1.00 being the worst and 0.00 the best.<ref name="GINI" /> Poverty incidence, as measured by the IBGE, was 31.37%, with a lower limit of 21.74%, an upper limit of 41.00%, and subjective poverty incidence at 26.03%.<ref name="GINI">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fcidadesat%2Fxtras%2Fcsv.php%3Ftabela%3Dmpobreza2003%26codmun%3D314330%26nomemun%3DMontes%2520Claros+&date=2011-05-17 |title=Social Indicators of Brazilian Municipalities |publisher=IBGE |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref>

The Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Montes Claros is considered high by the United Nations Development Programme ([[United Nations Development Programme|UNDP]]), at 0.770, ranking 227th among Brazil’s {{formatnum:5565}} municipalities. Most of the city’s indicators are average and align closely with national averages, according to the UNDP.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Igreja Matriz de Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|left|Montes Claros Matrix Church.]]
Reflecting the cultural diversity of Montes Claros, the city hosts a variety of [[Religious denomination|religious denominations]]. While it developed within a predominantly [[Catholicism|Catholic]] social framework, numerous [[Protestantism|Protestant]] denominations are now present.<ref name="Religião"/>

Located in the world’s most Catholic country by absolute numbers, the [[Catholic Church]] in Montes Claros had its legal status recognized by the federal government in October 2009,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Foglobo.globo.com%2Feconomia%2Fmat%2F2009%2F10%2F08%2Fsenado-aprova-acordo-com-vaticano-767959739.asp&date=2011-04-13 |title=Senate Approves Agreement with the Vatican |author=Cristiane Agostine |publisher=O Globo |date=October 8, 2009 |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> despite Brazil being an officially secular state.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://jus.com.br/artigos/8519/brasil-estado-laico-e-a-inconstitucionalidade-da-existencia-de-simbolos-religiosos-em-predios-publicos |title=Brazil: Secular State and the Unconstitutionality of Religious Symbols in Public Buildings |author=Fernando Fonseca de Queiroz |date=October 2005 |publisher=Jus Navigandi |access-date=July 20, 2011}}</ref>

The municipality is home to various evangelical and Reformed denominations, including the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembly of God]], Maranata Christian Church, [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian Church]], [[Baptist Church|Baptist churches]], [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], and [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], among others. According to the 2000 IBGE census, the population comprises [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (77.13%), [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (15.58%), [[Irreligion|non-religious individuals]] (4.02%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (0.66%), and 2.61% distributed among other religions.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/5yl0zVw22 |title=Religion |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref>

== Politics and administration ==
Under the [[Constitution of Brazil|1988 Constitution]], Montes Claros operates within the framework of a federative presidential republic, inspired by the [[United States]] model but following the [[Roman law|Roman]]-[[Germanic law|Germanic]] tradition of [[positive law]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.oas.org%2Fjuridico%2Fmla%2Fen%2Fbra%2Fen_bra-int-des-ordrjur.html&date=2011-04-13 |title=The Brazilian Legal System |language=English |author=Organization of American States (OAS) |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> Municipal administration is divided between the [[executive power]] and [[legislative power]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fjusvi.com%2Fartigos%2F20074&date=2011-04-13 |title=The Municipal Public Power and the Constitutional Obligation to Create an Internal Control System |author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima |date=February 8, 2006 |publisher=JusVi |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref>

Before 1930, municipalities were led by municipal chamber presidents, also known as executive agents or intendants. Following the [[Revolution of 1930|1930 Revolution]], municipal powers were separated into executive and legislative branches.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.camarabp.sp.gov.br%2Fconteudo%2Fcamara-municipal-da-estancia-de-braganca-paulista-&date=2011-04-13 |title=Bragança Paulista Municipal Chamber |author=Bragança Paulista Municipal Chamber |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> The first intendant was José Pinheiro Neves, and the first executive leader and [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]] was Orlando Ferreira Pinto. Over 29 terms, 25 mayors and 22 executive agents have led Montes Claros.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fprefeitos.htm+&date=2011-05-17 |title=Former Mayors |author=Montes Claros Municipal Government |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> In 2008, Luiz Tadeu Leite of the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB) won the [[2008 Brazilian municipal elections|municipal elections]] with 96,374 votes (52.58% of valid votes) in the second round, which was necessary because the city has over 200,000 voters and Tadeu did not secure over 50% in the first round.<ref name="G1_Eleições2008">{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fg1.globo.com%2FEleicoes2008%2F0%2C%2CMUL830781-15721%2C00-LUIS%2BTADEU%2BLEITE%2BE%2BELEITO%2BPREFEITO%2BDE%2BMONTES%2BCLAROS%2BMG.html+&date=2011-05-17 |title=Luis Tadeu Leite Elected Mayor of Montes Claros (MG) |author=G1 |date=October 26, 2008 |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref>

The [[legislative power]] is exercised by the [[Câmara municipal|Municipal Chamber]], comprising 15 councilors elected for four-year terms, in accordance with Article 29 of the Constitution.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.dji.com.br%2Fconstituicao_federal%2Fcf029a031.htm&date=2011-04-13 |author=DJI |title=Federal Constitution - CF - 1988 / Art. 29 |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the Executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law).

Montes Claros is governed by an organic law enacted on February 1, 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fprefeitura%2Flegislacao%2Fpaginas%2Farquivos%2Flei_organica_municipal.pdf&date=2011-05-17 |title=Organic Law of the Municipality of Montes Claros |author=Municipal Chamber |date=February 1, 2007 |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> The city also serves as the seat of a [[Comarca|''comarca'']].<ref name="comarca">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.tjmg.jus.br%2Fjuridico%2Fsf%2Fcomarcas.html&date=2011-04-25 |title=List of Comarcas of the State of Minas Gerais |author=Minas Gerais Court of Justice |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref> In 2010, the municipality had 238,405 voters, a 10.9% increase from 2006.<ref name="Eleitores">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fintertvonline.globo.com%2Fmg%2Fnoticias.php%3Fid%3D7690+&date=2011-05-17 |title=Number of Voters Grows in Montes Claros |author=In360 |date=July 23, 2010 |access-date=May 15, 2011}}</ref>

== Economy ==
Montes Claros has the largest [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) in its microregion,<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> excelling in the [[tertiary sector|service sector]]. According to 2020 IBGE data, the municipality’s GDP was {{formatnum:9686454.28|million}} [[Brazilian real|reais]].<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{cite web|url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/estatisticas-novoportal/economicas/contas-nacionais/9088-produto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios.html?t=destaques&c=3143302date=2019-02-16 |title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities |date=2020 |access-date=March 14, 2023 |author=IBGE - Montes Claros}}</ref> Of this, {{formatnum:1056788.90}} million reais are taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> The per capita GDP is {{formatnum:23426.26}} reais,<ref name="IBGE_PIB" /> and the income HDI is 0.707.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

In 2008, the city had 9,497 local units, 9,127 companies and active commercial establishments, and {{formatnum:130045}} workers, with a total workforce of 70,691 and {{formatnum:59354}} salaried employees. Salaries and other remunerations totaled {{formatnum:691296}} reais, with an average monthly wage of 2.2 [[minimum wages]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fcidadesat%2Fxtras%2Fcsv.php%3Ftabela%3Dempresas%26codmun%3D314330%26nomemun%3DMontes%2520Claros+&date=2011-05-17 |title=Central Business Register Statistics |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=May 17, 2011}}</ref>

=== Sectors ===
The economy of Montes Claros is diversified across agricultural, industrial, and service activities. The primary economic driver is the tertiary sector, encompassing various commerce and service segments, such as education and healthcare. The secondary sector follows, with large-scale industrial complexes and small to medium-sized production units.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/>

; '''Secondary'''
The industry is currently the second most significant sector, contributing {{formatnum:774539}} million reais to the gross value added of the GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> The city is home to an [[industrial district]] spanning 5.2 million m², managed by the Minas Gerais Economic Development Company (CODEMIG),<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> one of the most important industrial complexes in Minas Gerais. Plans are underway for a second industrial district with a projected area of 1.5 million m². A [[Petrobras]] [[biodiesel]] plant, established in 2008, is also operational.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/5yl1Cya15 |title=Collection of Information about the Municipality of Montes Claros |author=Montes Claros Municipal Government |access-date=May 17, 2011}}</ref>

In 2011, the municipal government reported 954 industrial establishments and {{formatnum:9912}} workers in the industrial sector.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/> Major companies with industrial facilities include Coteminas, [[Lafarge (company)|Lafarge]], [[Novo Nordisk]], [[Nestlé]], and [[Petrobras]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral" />

=== Tax and financial incentives ===
Although located in Brazil’s [[Southeast Region of Brazil|Southeast Region]], Montes Claros, due to its edaphoclimatic, economic, social, and cultural characteristics, is included in the Minas Gerais area of the [[Superintendency for the Development of the Northeast]] (SUDENE), receiving tax and financial incentives from this regional development agency. Through SUDENE, the city benefits from investment projects funded by the Northeast Development Fund, covering up to 60% of total fixed and circulating investments.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/> SUDENE also provides exemptions from the Additional Freight for the Brazilian Merchant Navy (AFRMM) and the Tax on Financial Operations (IOF).<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/>

The city is served by an extensive banking network, including private financial institutions and credit cooperatives, as well as branches of [[Banco do Brasil]], [[Caixa Econômica Federal]], and [[Banco do Nordeste]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/> Operating with various credit arrangements under the Northeast Constitutional Financing Fund (FNE), and transfers from the [[National Bank for Economic and Social Development]] (BNDES) and the Worker Support Fund (FAT), Banco do Nordeste is, according to the municipal government, the primary financial institution supporting the region’s economic activities.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geral"/>

== Infrastructure ==
[[File:Praça da Matriz, Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|left|Praça da Matriz, located in the city center.]]
In the year 2000, the municipality had 75,676 [[Household|households]], comprising [[Apartment|apartments]], [[House|houses]], and individual rooms. Of these, 59,703 were owned properties, with 56,714 fully paid off (74.94%), 2,989 in the process of acquisition (3.95%), and 10,103 [[Renting|rented]] (13.35%). Additionally, 5,553 properties were provided free of charge, with 1,190 supplied by employers (1.57%) and 4,363 provided through other means (5.77%). A further 317 households were occupied in other ways (0.40%).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.cnm.org.br%2Finfra%2Fmu_infra_domicilio.asp%3FiIdMun%3D100131502+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Infra-Estrutura - Domicílios Particulares Permanentes e Moradores |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> The municipality has treated water, [[electricity]], [[sewerage]], [[Waste management|urban cleaning services]], [[Landline|fixed-line telephony]], and [[Mobile phone|mobile telephony]]. In 2000, 92.77% of households were served by the [[Water supply network|public water supply network]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.cnm.org.br%2Finfra%2Fmu_infra_abastecimento.asp%3FiIdMun%3D100131502+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Infra-Estrutura - Abastecimento de Água |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> 90.04% of residences had waste collected by municipal cleaning services;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.cnm.org.br%2Finfra%2Fmu_infra_lixo.asp%3FiIdMun%3D100131502&date=2011-05-18 |title=Infra-Estrutura - Destino do Lixo |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> and 85.52% of homes were connected to the general sewer or stormwater drainage system.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.cnm.org.br%2Finfra%2Fmu_infra_esgotamento.asp%3FiIdMun%3D100131502+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Infra-Estrutura - Esgotamento Sanitário |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref>

=== Healthcare ===
In 2009, Montes Claros had 224 healthcare facilities, including hospitals, [[Emergency department|emergency rooms]], [[Health center|health centers]], and [[Dentistry|dental services]], with 83 being public and 141 private. These facilities provided 921 hospital beds for [[inpatient care]], with 241 in public institutions and 680 in private ones.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fcidadesat%2Fxtras%2Fcsv.php%3Ftabela%3Dassismed%26codmun%3D314330%26nomemun%3DMontes%2520Claros+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Serviços de Saúde 2009 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> The city is home to six general hospitals: one public, two private, and three [[Philanthropy|philanthropic]]. Montes Claros also employs 8,780 healthcare professionals. In 2008, the city recorded 5,167 live births, with 7.7% being premature, 38.5% delivered via [[caesarean section]], and 16.8% born to mothers aged 10 to 19 years (0.5% to mothers aged 10 to 14). The crude birth rate in that year was 14.4 per 1,000 inhabitants.<ref name="Datasus_Saúde">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Ftabnet.datasus.gov.br%2Ftabdata%2Fcadernos%2FMG%2FMG_Montes_Claros_Geral.xls+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Caderno de Informações de Saúde - Informações Gerais |author=DATASUS |format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]] |date=10 April 2010 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> The [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) for longevity in Montes Claros is 0.868.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

Notable hospitals in the city include Aroldo Tourinho, Clemente de Farias (University Hospital), Fundação Hospitalar Dilson de Quadros Godinho (São Lucas), Alpheu de Quadros, and Santa Casa.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/5ymlEM1zW |title=Hospitais de Montes Claros recebem recursos para urgência e emergência |author=Hoje em Dia |date=7 April 2011 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> The Santa Casa de Montes Claros, officially known as Irmandade Nossa Senhora das Mercês, is considered the largest healthcare facility in the northern region of Minas Gerais.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.jusbrasil.com.br%2Fnoticias%2F210799%2Fsanta-casa-de-montes-claros-recebe-recursos-do-pro-hosp&date=2011-05-18 |title=Santa Casa de Montes Claros recebe recursos do Pro-Hosp |author=JusBrasil |date=19 November 2008 |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref>

=== Education ===

Montes Claros has [[School|schools]] in all its regions. Residents of rural areas have easy access to schools in nearby urban neighborhoods due to the high level of urbanization. The average Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) among public schools in Montes Claros in 2009 was 4.85, surpassing the national average of 4.0 for municipal and state schools across Brazil.<ref name="IDEB_Índice">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fsistemasideb.inep.gov.br%2Fresultado%2F&date=2011-04-13 |title=Índice de Desenvolvimento da Educação Básica (IDEB) - Resultados e Metas |author=Federal Government |access-date=18 May 2011 }}</ref> In 2009, the municipality recorded approximately 83,846 enrollments, 4,586 teachers, and 342 schools in both public and private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> The [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) for education was 0.744, classified as high.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

=== Public safety and crime ===
[[File:55bi.png|thumb|right|100px|Emblem of the 55th Infantry Battalion.]]
As in most medium and large Brazilian municipalities, crime remains a significant issue in Montes Claros. In 2008, the [[homicide]] rate was 28.7 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 34th in the state and 534th nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FHTN.xls&date=2011-02-28 |title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de homicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=18 May 2011 |format=xls |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FHTN.xls |archive-date=2011-02-28}}</ref> The [[suicide]] rate for the same year was 8.1 per 100,000 inhabitants, placing it 81st in the state and 570th nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FSTN.xls&date=2011-02-28 |title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de suicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=18 May 2011 |format=xls |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FSTN.xls |archive-date=2011-02-28}}</ref> The rate of deaths due to traffic accidents was 33.8 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 45th in the state and 389th nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FTTN.xls&date=2011-02-28 |title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de Óbitos Ac.Transporte nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=18 May 2011 |format=xls |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.sangari.com%2Fmapadaviolencia%2Ftabelas2011%2FTTN.xls |archive-date=2011-02-28}}</ref>

According to the [[Military Police of Minas Gerais State]] (PMMG), the city's battalion also serves 23 other municipalities in northern Minas Gerais, increasing demands for additional police personnel in Montes Claros. PMMG data from 2009 indicates that 74% of the prison population consisted of young individuals, with many arrests linked to [[drug trafficking]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.almg.gov.br%2Fnot%2Fbancodenoticias%2Fnot_754815.asp+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Montes Claros reivindica aumento de efetivos policiais |author=[[Legislative Assembly of Minas Gerais]] (ALMG) |date=1 September 2009 |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.almg.gov.br%2Fnot%2Fbancodenoticias%2Fnot_754815.asp+ |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref> The city is also home to the 55th Infantry Battalion (Dionísio Cerqueira Battalion, or 55th BI), a [[Brazilian Army]] unit established on 19 April 1851, subordinate to the 4th Military Region.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.exercito.gov.br%2Fweb%2Fguest%2F1117&date=2011-05-21 |title=55º Batalhão de Infantaria |author=[[Brazilian Army]] |access-date=21 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.exercito.gov.br%2Fweb%2Fguest%2F1117 |archive-date=2011-05-21}}</ref>

=== Services and communications ===
Water supply and sewage collection in Montes Claros, as in the broader region, are managed by Copasa (Minas Gerais Sanitation Company). Electricity distribution, as is the case in most of Minas Gerais, is provided by [[Cemig]] (Minas Gerais Energy Company). In 2003, there were {{formatnum:101502}} electricity consumers, with a total consumption of {{formatnum:368217506}} kWh.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" />

Internet services, including dial-up and [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]), are offered by various free and paid [[Internet service provider|ISPs]]. Mobile phone services are provided by multiple operators. The area code ([[Direct distance dialing|DDD]]) for Montes Claros is 038.<ref name="Guiatel_DDD">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.guiatel.com.br%2Finfo_mg2.htm+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Minas Gerais |author=Guiatel |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.guiatel.com.br%2Finfo_mg2.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref> The [[Postal code|Postal Code]] (CEP) ranges from 39400-001 to 39409-999.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correios.com.br/ |title=CEP de cidades brasileiras |author=[[Correios]] |access-date=18 May 2011}}</ref> On 10 November 2008, Montes Claros and other cities with the 038 area code gained access to [[Mobile number portability|<span title="Number portability">number portability</span>]], allowing users to switch operators without changing their phone numbers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fmontesclaros.com%2Fnoticias.asp%3Fcodigo%3D41297+&date=2011-05-18 |title=Portabilidade numérica (que para nós chegou em 10 de novembro) acrescenta hoje mais 13 milhões de usuários |author=Montes Claros.com |date=12 January 2009 |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fmontesclaros.com%2Fnoticias.asp%3Fcodigo%3D41297+ |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref>

The city has both print and digital newspapers in circulation. In 2004, there were four print newspapers. In 2001, according to the Minas Gerais Radio and TV Association (AMIRT) and Telemig, there were eight radio stations, a number that has since increased. Notable radio stations include 98 FM, the first in northern Minas Gerais, established in May 1981;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fmontesclaros.com%2F98%2F&date=2011-05-18 |title=Rádio 98 FM |author=Montes Claros.com |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fmontesclaros.com%2F98%2F |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref> Rádio Unimontes 101.1 FM;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.unimontes.br%2Fradio%2Fradio.php&date=2011-05-18 |title=Rádio |author=State University of Montes Claros (Unimontes) |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.unimontes.br%2Fradio%2Fradio.php |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref> and [[Rede Transamérica|Rádio Transamérica Pop]] 95.1 FM.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.radios.com.br%2Faovivo%2FRadio-Transamerica-Pop-95.1-FM%2F15718&date=2011-05-18 |title=Rádio Transamérica Pop 95.1 FM - Montes Claros / Minas Gerais |author=Rádios.com |access-date=18 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.radios.com.br%2Faovivo%2FRadio-Transamerica-Pop-95.1-FM%2F15718 |archive-date=2011-05-18}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===
[[File:Estrada rural em Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|left|Dirt road in the rural area.]]

Due to the scarcity of rivers, Montes Claros has little tradition of water-based transportation. The city was once served by the [[Estrada de Ferro Central do Brasil|Central do Brasil Railway]], operational from 1858 to 1969, with passenger services at the Montes Claros station continuing until 1996, connecting to [[Monte Azul]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.estacoesferroviarias.com.br%2Ftrens_mg%2Ftremsertao.htm+&date=2011-05-19 |title=Trem do Sertão |author=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.estacoesferroviarias.com.br%2Ftrens_mg%2Ftremsertao.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref> Today, the station is used solely for freight trains.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efcb_mg_linhacentro/montesclaros.htm |title=Montes Claros -- Estações Ferroviárias do Estado de Minas Gerais |access-date=2 August 2020 |website=www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br}}</ref> The city has easy access to major highways, including [[BR-135 (Brazil highway)|BR-135]], connecting mid-northern Brazil ([[Maranhão]]) to Belo Horizonte; [[BR-365 (Brazil highway)|BR-365]], linking Montes Claros to [[Pirapora]] and [[Uberlândia]]; [[BR-251 (Brazil highway)|BR-251]], extending from [[Bahia]] to [[Mato Grosso]]; and [[BR-122 (Brazil highway)|BR-122]], running from [[Fortaleza]], [[Ceará]], to Montes Claros at the BR-251 junction.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /><ref>{{cite web|url=http://maps.google.com.br/ |title=Google Maps |access-date=19 May 2011}}</ref> The [[Montes Claros Airport|Mário Ribeiro Airport]], inaugurated on 18 December 1939, features a 45-meter-wide, 2,100-meter-long asphalt runway and an annual capacity of 70,000 passengers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.montesclaros.mg.gov.br/cidade/voos.htm |title=Aeroporto Mário Ribeiro |author=Prefeitura |access-date=19 May 2011}}</ref> Additionally, there are two smaller privately managed airports, each with a 1,150-meter runway.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" />

In 2009, the municipal vehicle fleet totaled {{formatnum:120436}} vehicles, including {{formatnum:53200}} cars, {{formatnum:3769}} trucks, 398 tractor trucks, {{formatnum:8079}} pickups, 204 minibuses, {{formatnum:47095}} motorcycles, {{formatnum:6765}} mopeds, 917 buses, and nine wheeled tractors.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fcidadesat%2Fxtras%2Fcsv.php%3Ftabela%3Dfrota%26codmun%3D314330%26nomemun%3DMontes%2520Claros+&date=2011-05-19 |title=Frota 2009 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fcidadesat%2Fxtras%2Fcsv.php%3Ftabela%3Dfrota%26codmun%3D314330%26nomemun%3DMontes%2520Claros+ |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref> Paved, divided avenues and numerous [[traffic light]]s facilitate urban [[traffic]], but the rapid increase in vehicles over the past decade has led to slower traffic, particularly in the city center. Finding parking spaces in the commercial district has also become challenging, impacting local businesses.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.bemnanet.com.br%2Fmaterias.php%3Ftexto%3D4893&date=2011-05-19 |title=Trânsito de Montes Claros está cada vez mais devagar, quase parando |author=Bem Na Net |date=4 March 2011 |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.bemnanet.com.br%2Fmaterias.php%3Ftexto%3D4893 |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref>

The Municipal Company for Planning, Management, and Education in Traffic and Transportation (McTrans) regulates public transportation, manages traffic, and issues fines through its traffic officers for violations. Electronic speed radars on major avenues assist in enforcement.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fmctrans%2Findex.htm+&date=2011-05-19 |title=Empresa Municipal de Planejamento, Gestão e Educação em Trânsito e Transportes de Montes Claros - MCTRANS |author=Prefeitura |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fmctrans%2Findex.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref> Public transportation is operated by Transmoc and Alprino.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Ftransmoc.blogspot.com%2F2007%2F08%2Ftransmoc-vence-licitao.html&date=2011-05-19 |title=Transmoc vence a licitação |author=Transmoc |date=11 August 2007 |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Ftransmoc.blogspot.com%2F2007%2F08%2Ftransmoc-vence-licitao.html |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref> The Association of Urban Public Transport Companies of Montes Claros (ATCMC), founded on 23 February 1988, unites companies involved in municipal public transport.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.atcmc.com.br%2Fnovo%2F%3F%2Finstitucional%2F&date=2011-05-19 |title=Associação das Empresas de Transporte Coletivo Urbano de Montes Claros |author=Associação das Empresas de Transporte Coletivo Urbano de Montes Claros (ATCMC) |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.atcmc.com.br%2Fnovo%2F%3F%2Finstitucional%2F |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref> The Hildeberto Alves de Freitas Bus Terminal, a key regional [[bus terminal]], was inaugurated on 3 October 1980.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Frodoviaria.htm&date=2011-05-19 |title=Terminal Rodoviário "Hildeberto Alves de Freitas" |author=Prefeitura |access-date=19 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Frodoviaria.htm |archive-date=2011-05-19}}</ref>

== Culture ==
The Municipal Secretariat of Culture oversees cultural activities in Montes Claros, aiming to develop and implement cultural policies through programs, projects, and activities. Affiliated with the Mayor's Office, it operates as part of the municipality's indirect administration, with administrative and financial autonomy ensured by budgetary allocations, its own assets, revenue application, and contracts with other institutions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcultura%2Findex.htm+&date=2011-05-20 |title=Secretaria Municipal de Cultura |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcultura%2Findex.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-20}}</ref>

=== Tourism and events ===
[[File:Vista noturna da Praça da Matriz, Montes Claros MG.jpg|thumb|right|Night view of Praça da Matriz.]]
[[File:Igreja dos Morrinhos.jpg|thumb|right|Morrinhos Church.]]
Montes Claros boasts numerous tourist attractions, including the Milton Prates Municipal Park, a large green space housing the Municipal Zoo; Sapucaia Park, located in the Ibituruna Mountain Range, a forested reserve ideal for extreme sports; the Guimarães Rosa Park, established by Municipal Law No. 793 on 7 August 1989, one of the largest green areas in the urban perimeter; Lapa Encantada, featuring waterfalls and 1 km of underground rivers; Engenho Cave, open for visitation; the Lapa Grande Complex, home to a 3-km cave among the largest in Minas Gerais, containing fossilized animal remains; and landmarks such as the Our Lady of Aparecida Cathedral, standing at 65.08 meters, the Matrix Church, and the Morrinhos Church.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fturismo.htm+&date=2011-05-20 |title=Turismo |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fturismo.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-20}}</ref> The city also has 164 cataloged archaeological sites, with the Lapa Grande Speleological Complex standing out for its archaeological significance, featuring rare volcanic [[Speleothem|speleothems]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/>

To boost local socioeconomic development, the Montes Claros municipality, often in partnership with local businesses, invests in festivals and events. These attractions draw visitors from other cities, which requires improved infrastructure and professionalization of the sector. This benefits both tourists and residents. Events occur year-round, including the [[Pequi]] Festival in January; Carnamontes in February or March; the International [[Folk dance|Folk Dance]] Festival in May; [[Festa Junina|June Festivals]] in June or July; the Agricultural Exposition at João Alencar Athayde Park in July; the August Festivals (Catopês); the National Industry, Commerce, and Services Fair (FENICS) in August; and the Psiu Poético National Poetry Salon in October.<ref name="Prefeitura_Cultura"/>

=== Sports ===
As in much of Brazil, [[Association football|football]] is the most popular sport in Montes Claros. The city is home to several clubs, including Montes Claros Futebol Clube, founded on 28 August 1990; Funorte Esporte Clube, established on 4 May 2007; Associação Atlética Cassimiro de Abreu, founded on 28 May 1948; and Associação Desportiva Ateneu, created on 1 May 1947. Football stadiums include João Rebello Stadium, with a capacity of 4,550; José Maria de Melo Stadium, holding 3,346; Dr. Rubens Durães Peres Stadium, with 1,000 seats; Ivani Martins Pereira Stadium, also with 1,000 seats; and various smaller fields throughout the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Esportes">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fesportes.htm+&date=2011-05-20 |title=Esportes |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.montesclaros.mg.gov.br%2Fcidade%2Faspectosgerais%2Fesportes.htm+ |archive-date=2011-05-20}}</ref>

Montes Claros also excels in other sports, such as [[volleyball]]. The [[FUNADEM Montes Claros|Foundation for Educational Development of Montes Claros]] (FUNADEM), established in 2009, gained national recognition by winning the International Volleyball Circuit, a friendly event held in the city in 2009 with teams from other countries.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.jusbrasil.com.br%2Fpolitica%2F3974729%2Ffunadem-montes-claros-invicto-no-circuito-internacional-de-volei&date=2011-05-20 |title=Funadem/Montes Claros invicto no Circuito Internacional de Vôlei |author=JusBrasil |date=24 August 2009 |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.jusbrasil.com.br%2Fpolitica%2F3974729%2Ffunadem-montes-claros-invicto-no-circuito-internacional-de-volei |archive-date=2011-05-20}}</ref> The city has five sports arenas, the largest being the Presidente Tancredo Neves Sports Complex, with a capacity of 12,000; six public spaces designated for walking tracks; and 15 social clubs.<ref name="Prefeitura_Esportes"/>

=== Holidays ===
Montes Claros observes three municipal holidays, eight national holidays, and three optional holidays. The municipal holidays are [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], celebrated on the Thursday following the Sunday of the [[Trinity|Holy Trinity]]; the anniversary of the city's emancipation on 3 July; and [[Black Awareness Day]] on 20 November.<ref>Holiday established by Municipal Law 3.897, of 27 December 2007.</ref> According to Federal Law No. 9,093, enacted on September 12, 1995, municipalities may designate up to four municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.guiatrabalhista.com.br%2Ftematicas%2Fcarnaval.htm&date=2011-03-03 |title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.guiatrabalhista.com.br%2Ftematicas%2Fcarnaval.htm |archive-date=2011-03-03}}</ref><ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaLei">{{cite web|url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.planalto.gov.br%2Fccivil_03%2FLeis%2FL9093.htm&date=2011-03-03 |title=Lei n.º 9.093, de 12 de setembro de 1995 |author=Presidência da República |access-date=20 May 2011 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.planalto.gov.br%2Fccivil_03%2FLeis%2FL9093.htm |archive-date=2011-03-03}}</ref>
