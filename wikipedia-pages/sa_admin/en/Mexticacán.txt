'''Mexticacán''' is a [[town]] and [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] in the Southern Zone of Los Altos Region of [[Jalisco]]. Mexticacán comes from the [[Nahuatl]] language and means "place where the temple for the worship of the moon".

==History==
===Early history===
The first settlers of Mexticacán were [[Tecuexe]], who placed their villages at the foot of the mountains, where there were real strengths to fend off the [[Chichimeca]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.indigenouspeople.net/jalisco.htm|title=Sixtenth Century Indigenous Jalisco|author=John P. Schmal|access-date=February 20, 2019}}</ref> Those settlers were known to engaged in the manufacture of [[earthenware]] clay during break from work. Due to their late night engagements they became known as "Men who work in the moonlight".

===7th century===
By the early seventh century, was a sweeping immigration throughout the region and particularly in villages [[Tecuexe]]; [[Caxcan]], peregrinante home of the [[Nahuas]] fought bravely against the bellicose Tecuexe who in the meantime fought against [[Zacatecas]], [[Guachichil]]es and others without rest or respite. This war raged for more than a century with an effort to expand the sphere of influence of dominant tribe in Mexticacán. Before the conquest this place was the ''[[cacicazgo]]'' of Mexticacán.

[[Nuño de Guzmán]] expedition through this area trying to bring the Aboriginal Nochistlán Zacatecas, where he was about to perish in an ambush that he tended in the rock of [[Nochistlán]]. On Christmas 1531 the city's population was conquered by the Spanish conquistador [[Cristóbal de Oñate]] who was sent by Nuno de Guzman.<ref>{{cite web|title=Mexico: The Best Records in the World|author=John P. Schmal|url=http://www.somosprimos.com/schmal/jal.htm|access-date=February 20, 2019}}</ref>

===19th century===
In 1825 Mexticacán has formed its first council. On January 12, 1836 the city became a Section municipal and by March 13, 1837 it became a part of the [[Teocaltiche]]. Subsequently, from 1869 to 1895 the city became the [[Canton (country subdivision)|11th canton]] of Teocaltiche. On April 19, 1879 the city was awarded the title of village people of Mexticacán.

===20th century===
In the beginning of the 20th century the city got interested into trading with the [[United States]] and [[Europe]]. The city, back then, was known for its [[pallet crafts]].<ref name=uj>{{cite news|author=Roberto Larios|date=June 27, 2017|access-date=February 20, 2019|url=http://www.unionjalisco.mx/articulo/2017/06/27/turismo/mexticacan-la-cuna-del-helado-y-la-paleta|title=Mexticacán, la cuna del helado y la paleta|trans-title=Mexticacán, the cradle of ice cream and the palette|language=es|work=Unión Jalisco}}</ref> In 1940, the city had invested into production of [[ice cream]], which was brought to Mexticacán by Genaro Jáuregui, an entrepreneur from the city who also promoted the generation of electric power and radio.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.informador.mx/suplementos/Viaje-y-aventura-en-Mexticacan-20180505-0108.html|title=Viaje y aventura en Mexticacán|trans-title=Travel and adventure in Mexticacán|author=Francisco González|date=May 6, 2018|access-date=February 20, 2019}}</ref> By 1950s Mexticacán began entering other cities of the country such as [[Aguascalientes]], [[Durango]], [[León, Guanajuato|León]], [[Monterrey]], [[Veracruz (city)|Veracruz]] and many more. In 1970s Mexticacán had fully achieved its internationalization when it opened its hygiene business in [[Panama]].<ref name=uj/>

===21st century===
Recently, Club Mexticacan, was established as a non-profit organization in Northern California to help those in need, such as children and the elderly. In a little less than a year, major accomplishments have been achieved; two vehicles have been purchased to transport the disabled children to special education classes and hundreds of toys, blankets, and shoes have been handed out.

According to the Institute of Transparency and Public Information, Mexticacán became the most opaque city in Jalisco in 2017 because their government had failed to publish fundamental information on their website.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.notisistema.com/noticias/mexticacan-el-municipio-mas-opaco-de-jalisco/|title=Mexticacán, el municipio más opaco de Jalisco|trans-title=Mexticacán, the most opaque municipality of Jalisco|author=David Correa Aldrete|language=es|date=October 4, 2017|access-date=February 20, 2019}}</ref>
