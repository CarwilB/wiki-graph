'''Lajas''' ({{IPA|es|ˈlaxas}}, {{IPA|es|ˈlahaʔ|local}}) is a [[Lajas barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] located in the [[Lajas Valley]] in southwestern [[Puerto Rico]], bordering the [[Caribbean Sea]], south of [[San Germán, Puerto Rico|San Germán]] and [[Sabana Grande, Puerto Rico|Sabana Grande]]; east of [[Cabo Rojo, Puerto Rico|Cabo Rojo]]; and west of [[Guánica, Puerto Rico|Guánica]]. Lajas is spread over 11 barrios plus [[Lajas barrio-pueblo|Lajas Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Germán-Cabo Rojo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

==History==
Located in the [[Lajas Valley]], the town was founded in 1883 by the [[Xueta]] [[Teodoro Jácome Pagán y Cancel|Teodoro Jácome Pagán]]. Xuetes ([[Chuetas]]) were [[Mallorca]]n [[Jews]] ([[Sephardic]] Catalan Jews). Some families changed their names from ''Jácome'' to the Castilian form [[Santiago (surname)|Santiago]]. [[Jácome]] is the [[Balearic dialect|Mallorquin]] form of [[James (given name)|James]] (Ia'akov). [[Catalonia|Catalan]] Jews from Mallorca were part of the early settlers in the south of [[Borikén]] (Puerto Rico).

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a colony of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Lajas was 8,789.<ref>{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office| url=https://archive.org/details/reportoncensusof00unitiala/page/160/mode/2up |year=1900|publisher=Washington : Govt. print. off.|page=160}}</ref>

The village of [[La Parguera]] is a popular tourist destination to see the famous [[Bahía Fosforescente]] (Phosphorescent Bay) and its numerous keys and islets.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lajaspr.com/engLaParguera.htm|title=La Parguera|website=Lajas PR|access-date=2019-03-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161028180007/http://www.lajaspr.com/engLaParguera.htm|archive-date=2016-10-28|url-status=live}}</ref> Parador La Parguera was founded by Puerto Rican comedian [[Henry LaFont (comedian)|Henry LaFont]] (Julio Pancorbo Ortiz).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.elvocero.com/actualidad/otros/fallece-henry-lafont-fundador-del-parador-villa-parguera/article_829c7d84-aef0-11ed-b8d0-6bfd2584cb26.html|title=Fallece Henry Lafont: fundador del Parador Villa Parguera|first=Redacción, EL|last=VOCERO|website=El Vocero de Puerto Rico|access-date=February 18, 2023|archive-date=February 17, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230217233250/https://www.elvocero.com/actualidad/otros/fallece-henry-lafont-fundador-del-parador-villa-parguera/article_829c7d84-aef0-11ed-b8d0-6bfd2584cb26.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

People from the El Combate community in barrio [[Boquerón, Cabo Rojo, Puerto Rico|Boquerón]] are known as ''mata con hacha'' ("those who kill with axes") based on [[folklore]] about a fight over the ''salinas'', where those from [[Cabo Rojo, Puerto Rico|Cabo Rojo]] fought with axes against people from the adjacent town of Lajas. Because the people from Lajas apparently fought back by throwing stones they are known as ''tira piedras'' ("those who throw stones").<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caborojopr.net/brevehistoria.htm|title=Página Oficial Municipio Autónomo de Cabo Rojo|website=Cabo Rojo PR|access-date=2019-03-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190404121343/http://www.caborojopr.net/brevehistoria.htm|archive-date=2019-04-04|url-status=live}}</ref>

On September 20, 2017 [[Hurricane Maria]] struck Puerto Rico.  In Lajas, over 270 residences lost their roofs. The hurricane destroyed Lajas' pineapple industry.<ref>{{cite news| title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. Arrasadas las cosechas de piñas en Lajas| trans-title=Pineapple crops destroyed in Lajas| newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13| url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/lajas/| language=es| access-date=2022-09-11| archive-date=September 12, 2022| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220912005741/https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/lajas/| url-status=live}}</ref>

===Special Communities===
{{See also|Puerto Rico Office for Socioeconomic and Community Development}}
{{lang|es|Comunidades Especiales de Puerto Rico}} (Special Communities of Puerto Rico) are marginalized communities whose citizens are experiencing a certain amount of [[social exclusion]]. A map shows these communities occur in nearly every municipality of the commonwealth. Of the 742 places that were on the list in 2014, the following barrios, communities, sectors, or neighborhoods were in Lajas: El Papayo, El Tendal, Sector Sabana Yeguas, La Haya, Las Cuevas, Los Jovillos, Maguayo, Piñalejos, and Tokio.<ref>{{Citation|author1=Rivera Quintero, Marcia|title=El vuelo de la esperanza:Proyecto de las Comunidades Especiales Puerto Rico, 1997-2004|year=2014 |publication-date=2014| publisher=San Juan, Puerto Rico Fundación Sila M. ==Tourism==
Lajas is famous for its main touristic attraction, [[Bahía Fosforescente]] (La Parguera), a place where [[Bioluminescence|bioluminescent]] [[dinoflagellate]]s of different colors appear when the water moves. The origin of the colored lights is the object of many legends. Lajas is also a fishing town.

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Lajas celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in February. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref name="J.D. 2006">{{cite web | author=J.D. | title=Lajas | website=Link To Puerto Rico.com | date=2006-05-02 | url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/lajas.html# | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140314185714/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/lajas.html | archive-date=2014-03-14 | url-status=live }}</ref> The festival has featured live performances by well-known artists such as [[Tito Rojas]], and [[Arcángel]].<ref name="Fiestas">{{cite web |title=Fiestas Patronales en honor a la Virgen de la Candelaria |url=http://2.bp.blogspot.com/-UAS1GP9_5bg/Ut6mrdwyXfI/AAAAAAAABkA/dNCR6I76uu4/s1600/1535550_686110561439814_465835766_n+(1).jpg |website=sondeaquiprnet |publisher=El Gobierno Municipal de Lajas |access-date=14 August 2023 |archive-date=August 14, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230814160636/http://2.bp.blogspot.com/-UAS1GP9_5bg/Ut6mrdwyXfI/AAAAAAAABkA/dNCR6I76uu4/s1600/1535550_686110561439814_465835766_n+%281%29.jpg |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
Like all municipalities in [[Puerto Rico]], Lajas is administered by a mayor. The current mayor is Jayson Martínez, from the [[New Progressive Party (Puerto Rico)|New Progressive Party]] (PNP). Martínez was first elected at the [[2020 Puerto Rican general election|2020 general election]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district V]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Marially González Huertas]] and [[Jamie Barlucea]], from the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] and [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]], respectively, were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241115225812/https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml |date=November 15, 2024 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
Lajas {{lang|es|municipio}} or Municipality has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15 | archive-date=January 20, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210120152958/http://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | url-status=live }}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag consists of three horizontal stripes; the top one is green, the center one is white and the bottom one is light yellow. A vertical white stripe down the left makes a white cross. In the center of the cross is a ripe yellow pineapple. The cross is surrounded by eleven gold stars; five in the top part and six in the lower part of the cross, in the form of a circle.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=LAJAS | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/LAJAS.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001106/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/LAJAS.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
It is gold with a green band crossing it diagonally right to left; gold stands for the wealth of the land and green for the beauty of the valley, which is a gift from mother nature to Lajas. The band is adorned, at each end, with a pineapple bordered in gold and black. In the center of the band, also in gold, a marine shell. In the top left there is a red cardinal's hat and in the bottom a red anchor with green; the shield has a 3 tower castle, each one with two windows and a door. On the bottom, outside the shield, a banner with the inscription ''"Ciudad Cardenalicia"'' (Cardinal City). This, for Cardinal Luis Aponte Martínez, born in Lajas, who was the first and only Cardinal of the Catholic Church from Puerto Rico. The banner and the inscription appear in black.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />
