'''Ceará-Mirim''' is a city in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Norte]] in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast region]] of Brazil. With an area of {{Convert|724.838|km²|sp=us}}, of which {{Convert|28.5233|km²}} is urban, it is located 30 km from [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]], the state capital, and 1,771 km from [[Brasília]], the federal capital. Its population in the [[2022 Brazilian census|2022 demographic census]] was 79,115 inhabitants, according to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), ranking as the sixth most populous municipality in the state of Rio Grande do Norte.

== Etymology ==
The origin of the city name is unknown. [[Câmara Cascudo]], one of the most important historians in Brazil, gives some alternatives from other authors, with all the alternatives leading to the [[Tupi language]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Cascudo |first=Luiz da Câmara |author-link=Câmara Cascudo |url=http://archive.org/details/nomes-da-terra-lui-s-da-ca-mara-cascudo |title=Nomes da terra: Luís da Câmara Cascudo |date= |publisher=Fundação José Augusto |year=1968 |language=pt |via=the [[Internet Archive]]}}</ref> [[José de Alencar]] suggests to us that Ceará comes from the Tupi expression ''cê-ará'', meaning "the parrot speaks" or "the parrot sings". [[Teodoro Fernandes Sampaio|Teodoro Sampaio]] gives us ''ceará'' or ''cemo-ará'', which is "the parrot leaves" or "the parrot's departure". There are also the versions of Paulino Nogueira and João Brigido, who suggest, respectively, ''çoô-ará'' and ''ciri-ará'', which mean "truly hunting time" and "white crab", respectively.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Melo |first=Protásio Pinheiro de |url=https://www.google.com.br/books/edition/Contribui%25C3%25A7%25C3%25A3o_Ind%25C3%25ADgena_%25C3%2580_Fala_Norte_r/6_srAAAAYAAJ?hl=pt-BR&gbpv=0&bsq=%2522cear%25C3%25A1-mirim%2522%2520%2522c%25C3%25AA-ar%25C3%25A1%2522 |title=Contribuição Indígena À Fala Norte-rio-grandense |date=1971 |publisher=Imprensa Universitária |pages=19 |language=pt-BR}}</ref>

== History ==
The origins of the settlement of Ceará-Mirim are linked to the [[Potiguara]] Indigenous people, who lived along the banks of the Rio Pequeno, which was later renamed the [[Ceará-Mirim River]]. The Potiguara engaged in clandestine trade of [[Paubrasilia|brazilwood]] with French and Spanish merchants, and eventually with the Portuguese colonizers, exchanging the valuable timber for various goods. The brazilwood, which was abundant in the region, was transported via a rudimentary navigation system using the waters of the Gramoré River.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante">{{cite web |author=Institute of Economic Development and Environment of Rio Grande do Norte (IDEMA-RN) |date=2013 |title=Perfil do seu município: Ceará-Mirim |url=http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/idema/DOC/DOC000000000016638.PDF |url-status= |archive-url= |archive-date= |access-date=10 February 2022 }}{{Dead link|date=February 2026 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}</ref>

The Portuguese, in collaboration with [[Filipe Camarão]], undertook efforts to organize a settlement in the area. A convent was established in the village of [[Guajiru]], and on lands granted to the Jesuit priests of the Society of Jesus, they constructed a church, a jail, and a town hall. Through their work in organizing the community, the Jesuits earned the trust and admiration of the local Indigenous population.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" />

While the Indigenous people supported the presence of the Jesuits, the Portuguese colonists did not, as they were primarily interested in the fertile lands of the valley. They sought to remove the Jesuits, whose moral authority and influence posed a challenge to colonial ambitions. A [[Royal charter|Royal Charter]] issued by the [[Marquis of Pombal (title)|Marquis of Pombal]] eventually banned Jesuits from participating in the administration and education of the settlement, without providing a clear reason. Following the expulsion of the Jesuits, the Portuguese colonists assumed control, free from religious oversight.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" />

Under pressure from the colonists, the Indigenous population began selling their lands to outsiders. Around this period, enslaved Africans were brought to the region, marking the beginning of forced labor and the establishment of [[Engenho|sugarcane plantations]] (''engenhos''), which would go on to dominate the local economy and shape the historical trajectory of the Ceará-Mirim Valley. A distinct local society emerged, centered around the powerful sugar plantation owners who were aware of their economic dominance and lived in a manner marked by aristocratic refinement and elegance.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" /> By the end of the 19th century, the valley prospered through sugarcane production. A culture of wealth and opulence took hold, with aristocratic balls, silk-lined carriages, and lavish festivities reflecting the patriarchal and slave-based phase of the sugar economy.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" />

The municipality was officially created on 3 September 1759 by royal charter and formally established on 3 May 1760 in the former village of Guajiru, with its administrative seat in the town of the [[Extremoz]]. On 18 August 1885, the seat was transferred to the settlement of Boca da Mata, which was renamed the town of Ceará-Mirim. However, the transfer was suspended by Law No. 345 on 4 September 1856 and later reinstated by Law No. 370 on 30 July 1858. On 9 June 1882, through Law No. 837, Ceará-Mirim was granted city status.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" /> In the letters of the Ouvidor Domingos Monteiro da Rocha, in July 1757, he included the Ceará-Mirim Village, where he says "with many residents". The first school emerged only in 1858, installed at Bôca da Mata, Extremoz county. The first meeting of the [[city council]] occurred on 14 October 1858, in Ceará-Mirim Village.<ref name=":03">{{Cite book |last=Cascudo |first=Luiz da Câmara |author-link=Câmara Cascudo |url=http://archive.org/details/nomes-da-terra-lui-s-da-ca-mara-cascudo |title=Nomes da terra: Luís da Câmara Cascudo |date= |publisher=Fundação José Augusto |year=1968 |language=pt |via=the [[Internet Archive]]}}</ref>

== Geography ==
The territory of Ceará-Mirim covers {{Convert|724.838|km²|sp=us}},<ref>{{Cite web |last=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Brasil / Rio Grande do Norte / Ceará-Mirim |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/panorama |access-date=2025-06-18}}</ref> of which {{Convert|28.5233|km²}} constitutes the urban area.<ref name="Tabela/8418">{{cite web |last=IBGE |year=2019 |title=Tabela 8418 - Áreas urbanizadas, Loteamento vazio, Área total mapeada e Subcategorias |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/8418#/n3/23/n6/2402600/v/12749/p/all/d/v12749%204/l/v,p,t/resultado |access-date=16 December 2024 |publisher=}}</ref> It sits at an average altitude of 33 meters above [[sea level]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Rio Grande do Norte |url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/rn.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070612212935/http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/rn.html |archive-date=2007-06-12 |access-date=2025-06-18 |website= |publisher=[[Brazilian Agricultural Research Corporation]]}}</ref> Ceará-Mirim borders these municipalities: to the north, [[Maxaranguape]]; to the south, [[São Gonçalo do Amarante, Rio Grande do Norte|São Gonçalo do Amarante]], [[Extremoz]], and [[Ielmo Marinho]]; to the east, Extremoz and Maxaranguape; and to the west, [[Taipu, Rio Grande do Norte|Taipu]].<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" /> The city is located 30 km from the state capital Natal,<ref name="distancia">{{cite web |title=Distância entre Natal - RN, Brazil e Ceará-Mirim - RN, Brazil |url=https://www.distanciaentreascidades.com.br/distancia-de-natal-rn-brazil-ate-ceara-mirim-rn |access-date=14 December 2024 |website=Distância Entre as Cidades}}</ref> and 1,771 km from the federal capital [[Brasília]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Distância entre Brasilia - DF, Brazil e Ceará-Mirim - Rio Grande do Norte, Brazil |url=https://www.distanciaentreascidades.com.br/distancia-de-brasilia-df-brazil-ate-ceara-mirim-rn-brazil |access-date=25 July 2014 |website=Distância Entre as Cidades}}</ref>

== Demographics ==
In the [[2022 Brazilian census|2022 census]], the municipality had a population of 79,115 inhabitants and ranked sixth in the state that year (out of 167 municipalities),<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Sinopse / População residente ( Unidade: pessoas ) / 2022 |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122232 |access-date=2024-09-06}}</ref> with 50.36% female and 49.64% male,<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Tabela 9514 - População residente, por sexo, idade e forma de declaração da idade |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9514#/n6/2402600/v/all/p/all/c2/all/c287/100362/c286/113635/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c2+c287,t+c286/resultado |access-date=2024-12-16}}</ref> resulting in a sex ratio of 98.56 (9,856 men for every 10,000 women),<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=População residente / Sexo / Razão de sexo |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122248 |access-date=2024-09-06}}</ref> compared to 68,141 inhabitants in the [[2010 Brazilian census|2010 census]] (52.09% living in the urban area),<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2010 |title=Tabela 608 - População residente, por situação do domicílio e sexo |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/608#/n6/2402600/v/all/p/all/c1/all/c2/all/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c1,t+c2/resultado |access-date=December 22, 2024}}</ref> when it held the sixth state position.<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2010 |title=Sinopse / População residente ( Unidade: pessoas ) / 2010 |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/23/27652?detalhes=true&tipo=ranking&indicador=27665 |access-date=2024-09-06}}</ref> Between the 2010 and 2022 censuses, the population of Ceará-Mirim changed at an annual geometric growth rate of 1.25%.<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=População residente / Taxa de crescimento geométrico ( Unidade: % ) |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122278 |access-date=2024-09-06}}</ref> Regarding [[age group]] in the 2022 census, 68.59% of the inhabitants were between 15 and 64 years old, 22.53% were under fifteen, and 8.89% were 65 or older.<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Tabela 9514 - População residente, por sexo, idade e forma de declaração da idade |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9514#/n6/2402600/v/all/p/all/c2/6794/c287/6653,49108,49109,60040,60041,93070,93084,93085,93086,93087,93088,93089,93090,93091,93092,93093,93094,93095,93096,93097,93098,100362/c286/113635/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c2+c287,t+c286/resultado |access-date=December 22, 2024}}</ref> The [[population density]] in 2022 was 109.15 inhabitants per square kilometer.<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Território / Densidade demográfica ( Unidade: habitantes por quilômetro quadrado ) |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122231 |access-date=2024-09-06}}</ref> There were 24,896 housing units with an average of 3.13 inhabitants per household.<ref>{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2022 |title=Tabela 4712 - The municipality's [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) was considered medium, according to data from the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP). According to the 2010 report published in 2013, its value was 0.616, ranking 132nd in the state and 4,540th nationally (out of 5,565 municipalities)<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010">{{cite web |date=2010 |title=Ranking decrescente do IDH-M dos municípios do Brasil |url=http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-Municipios-2010.aspx |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160610114916/http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-Municipios-2010.aspx |archive-date=June 10, 2016 |access-date=October 11, 2010 |work=Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano |publisher=United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)}}</ref>, and the [[Gini coefficient]] rose from 0.42 in 2003<ref name="Gini2003">{{cite web |author=IBGE |date=2003 |title=Mapa de pobreza e desigualdade |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/ceara-mirim/pesquisa/36/30246?ano=2003 |access-date=2024-12-21}}</ref> to 0.52 in 2010.<ref>{{Cite web |last=IBGE |date=2010 |title=Índice de Gini da renda domiciliar per capita segundo Município |url=http://tabnet.datasus.gov.br/cgi/ibge/censo/cnv/ginibr.def |access-date=17 June 2025 |website=DataSUS}}</ref> Considering only the longevity index, its value is 0.774, the income index is 0.599, and the education index is 0.505.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010" />
