'''Talca Province''' ({{langx|es|Provincia de Talca}}) is one of three [[Provinces of Chile|provinces]] of the [[Maule Region]] in Chile. It spans an area of {{cvt|9937.8|km2}}. Its capital is [[Talca, Chile|Talca]]. It had a population of 445,021 inhabitants as per the [[2024 Chilean census]].

==History==
{{see|Founding of Talca}}
A settlement at Talca was first founded in 1692, which had been occupied by the indigenous people, and was abandoned in 1717 due to an uprising of the indigenous groups. On 12 May 1742, it was re-founded as San Agustín de Talca on the order of [[José Antonio Manso de Velasco]], the [[Royal Governor of Chile]]. In the nineteenth century, it grew into a major commercial and agricultural center due to the export of [[wheat]] from the Maule basin, while [[wine]] production developed later. The development was accompanied by the expansion of railway network, and food processing industries.<ref name="Hist1"/> However, the economic growth declined in the early twentieth century, and it was further devastated by an [[1928 Talca earthquake|earthquake]] in 1928, which destroyed much of the region.<ref name="Hist1">{{cite web|url=https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-804.html|title=Economía agrícola y sociedad tradicional, Talca (1742-1928)|lang=es|work=Memoria Chilena|trans-title=Agricultural economy and traditional society, Talca (1742-1928)}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/hazel/view/hazards/earthquake/event-more-info/3378|title=Earthquake Information, Talca 1928 |work=National Centre for Earthquake Information|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

The [[Maule Region]] was established in 1970. It is divided into four provinces&ndash;Talca, [[Curicó Province|Curicó]], [[Linares Province|Linares]], and [[Cauquenes Province|Cauquenes]], which are further divided into 30 [[Communes of Chile|communes]].<ref name="Hist">{{cite web|url=https://www.descentralizachile.cl/region_del_maule/|lang=es|title=Region de Maule|trans-title=Maule Region|work=[[Ministry of the Interior (Chile)]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

==Geography==
Talca Province is one of the four provinces of the [[Maule Region]] in Chile.<ref name="Hist"/><ref name="Admin">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/chile/mun/admin/|title=Administrative division of Chile|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 May 2026}}</ref> It covers an area of {{cvt|9937.8|km2}}.<ref name="INE"/> Almost three-fourth of the land area of the province belongs to the [[Maule River]] basin, with the remaining forming part of the [[Mataquito River]] basin and the coastal lands.<ref name="MN">{{cite web|url=https://simbio.mma.gob.cl/DPA/DetailsProvincia/38|lang=es|title=Provincia details, Talca Provincia|trans-title=Details of the Province, Talca Province|work=[[Ministry of the Interior (Chile)]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

Talca is located in the central [[Maule Valley]] region of Chile, and is the largest wine making region in Chile. The region has clayey and [[pumice]] based soils, where [[red wine]] grapes are grown.<ref name="Brit">{{cite web|url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Maule|title=Maule|work=[[Britannica]]|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.winetourism.com/wine-appellation/maule-valley/|title=Maule valley|work=Wine tourism|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref> It has a warm [[Mediterranean climate]] ([[Koppen classification]]: Csb) with an average annual temperature of {{cvt|18.66|C}}. The region receives approximately {{cvt|8.44|cm}} of rainfall annually on average.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://weatherandclimate.com/chile/maule/talca|title=Talca|work=Weather and Climate|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref>

==Administration==
As a province, Talca is a second-level [[administrative division of Chile]], governed by a provincial governor.<ref name="Admin"/><ref name="Gobernadores">{{cite web|url=http://www.subdere.gov.cl/1510/w3-article-67516.html |access-date=18 March 2011 |title=Gobierno de Chile: Gobernadores |work=[[Government of Chile]] |language=es |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110707010623/http://www.subdere.gov.cl/1510/w3-article-67516.html |archive-date=7 July 2011 |df=dmy }}</ref> It is further subdivided into ten [[Communes of Chile|communes]] (''comunas'')&ndash;[[Talca]], [[San Clemente, Chile|San Clemente]], [[Pelarco]],  [[Pencahue]], [[Maule, Chile|Maule]], [[San Rafael, Chile|San Rafael]], [[Curepto]], [[Constitución, Chile|Constitución]],  [[Empedrado, Chile|Empedrado]], and [[Río Claro, Chile|Río Claro]].<ref name="Admin"/><ref name="MN"/> The city of [[Talca, Chile|Talca]] serves as the capital of the Maule region and the province.<ref name="Hist"/><ref name="Brit"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://postal-codes.cybo.com/chile/talca/|title=Talca|work=Cybo|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

==Demographics==
According to the [[2024 Chilean census]], the province had a population of 445,021 inhabitants. The population consisted of 229,040 females (51.5%) and 215,981 males (48.5%). About 18.3% of the population was below the age of 15 years, 67.4% belonged to the age group of 15–64 years, and 14.2% was aged 65 years or older. The province had an urban population of 352,528 inhabitants (79.2%) and a rural population of 92,493 inhabitants (20.8%). Most of the residents were born in Chile, accounting for 423,486 inhabitants (95.2%). Non-indigenous people formed the majority of the population with 428,606 inhabitants (96.3%), while 16,391 inhabitants (3.7%) identified themselves as belonging to indigenous groups. [[Roman Catholics]] formed the largest religious group with 222,678 adherents (61.5%), followed by 70,748 inhabitants (19.5%) indicating no religious affiliation, and [[Evangelicals]] or [[Protestants]] with 58,599 adherents (16.2%).<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/chile/mun/admin/071__talca/|title=Talca Province|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite report|url=https://storage.googleapis.com/bktdescargascenso2024/viviendas_censo2024.zip|title=Chilean Census 2024, Results|work=[[National Statistics Institute (Chile)]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>
