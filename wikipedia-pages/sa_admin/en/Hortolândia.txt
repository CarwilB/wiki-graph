'''Hortolândia''' is a Brazilian municipality in the interior of the state of São Paulo. It is part of the [[Metropolitan Region of Campinas]]<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.agemcamp.sp.gov.br/rmc/ |title=Região Metropolitana de Campinas |access-date=2015-09-29 |archive-date=2015-09-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150930171441/http://www.agemcamp.sp.gov.br/rmc/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> and the [[List of microregions of São Paulo (state)|Mesoregion and Microregion of Campinas]]. It is located northwest of [[São Paulo|the state capital]], about 110&nbsp;km away. It is part of the [[São Paulo macrometropolis]], which exceeds 29 million inhabitants and makes up approximately 75 percent of the state's population. The metropolitan regions of Campinas and São Paulo form the first megalopolis in the southern hemisphere.<ref name="Estadão_Conurbação">{{cite web |url=http://www.estadao.com.br/megacidades/sp_mancha.shtm |title=A primeira macrometrópole do hemisfério sul |trans-title=The first macrometropolis of the Southern Hemisphere |author=Diego Zanchetta |date=August 3, 2008 |website=Estadão |language=pt |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080806030355/http://www.estadao.com.br/megacidades/sp_mancha.shtm |archive-date=2008-08-06 |url-status=dead |access-date=May 6, 2012}}</ref>  It is bordered by [[Sumaré]], to the north; [[Monte Mor]], to the south and west; and [[Campinas]], to the east.<ref name="NossoSP_Hortolândia" />

Hortolândia was founded in 1991, splitting from Sumaré, and the privileged location and proximity to major industrial centers in the country caused the municipality to undergo a rapid demographic and industrial development. Hortolândia is considered a [[technopole]] and has several high tech companies, including [[IBM]]. These activities make the city have the 76th largest nominal municipal GDP in Brazil, with BR$12.9 billion in 2017.<ref name="IBGE_PIB2">{{cite web

== History ==

=== Origins ===
[[File:Hortolândia antiga.jpg|left|thumb|Jacuba in the 19th century]]
In 1798, lands were donated by the Portuguese Crown to José Teixeira Nogueira, an important mill owner in the region. He brought coffee for the first time to where the city of Hortolândia is today, whose work on the farms was based on slavery. After the slaves were freed, lands were donated to them, but stolen by an American doctor. Some areas were even renegotiated, but those that were made available did not favor coffee, so [[cotton]], [[sugarcane]] and [[Animal husbandry|cattle breeding]] began to be grown. The place, which served as a stopping point for [[tropeiro]], settlers and slaves, came to be called '''Jacuba''' (a [[Tupi–Guarani languages|Tupi-Guarani]] word meaning "hot water"), or the '''Sítio de Jacuba''', since these travelers took advantage of the waters of the streams and the shade of the trees to rest and feed themselves.<ref name="Prefeitura_História3">{{cite web

The settlement began to take utterance when the telegraph office was inaugurated in 1896. Later, in 1917, the Jacuba telegraph post became a railroad station. Only in 1947 did its growth begin, with the approval of the Ortolândia Park subdivision, owned by João Ortolan. In December 1953, the District of Santa Cruz, which Jacuba was part of, was split from [[Campinas]] to become the municipality of Sumaré; as such, Jacuba went from a village to a [[District#Brazil|district]].

=== As a city ===
The municipality of Hortolândia is located in a strategic position, between major poles of development. Due to its privileged position, the region attracts large industrial organizations, besides being surrounded by large universities. The geographical location of the city is to the west of Campinas, bordering also the cities of Sumaré and [[Monte Mor]]. Hortolândia is the smallest municipality in the Campinas metropolitan area. The main river that cuts through the town is the [[Ribeirão Jacuba]] [<nowiki/>[[:pt:Ribeirão Jacuba|pt]]]. The city has benefited economically from being along the [[Rodovia Anhanguera]], bordering Campinas, and being close to the [[Viracopos International Airport]]. More recently, a continuation of [[Rodovia dos Bandeirantes]] was implemented through the city, in the region of Jardim Amanda. This highway allowed important access to the municipality through the cloverleaf at the junction with Rodovia Jornalista Francisco Aguirre Proença in an area near the IBM do Brasil company.<ref name="Prefeitura_História3"/><ref name="Uol_Hortolândia">{{cite web

== Geography ==
=== Metropolitan region ===
{{Main|Campinas metropolitan area}}
The intense process of [[conurbation]] currently underway in the region has been creating a [[metropolis]] whose center is the city of [[Campinas]], reaching several municipalities, such as [[Sumaré]], [[Indaiatuba]], [[Americana, São Paulo|Americana]], [[Santa Bárbara d'Oeste]], [[Valinhos]], [[Itatiba]] and [[Paulínia]], in addition to Hortolândia. The Campinas Metropolitan Region (RMC) was created by State Complementary Law 870, of June 19, 2000, and is currently composed of 19 municipalities, being the [[List of metropolitan areas in Brazil by population|ninth-largest urban agglomeration in Brazil]], with 2,798,477 inhabitants.<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010"/> It is one of the most dynamic in the Brazilian economic scenario and represents 2.7% of the national [[Gross domestic product]] and 7.83% of the GDP of the state of São Paulo, that is, about 77.7 billion [[Brazilian real|reais]] per year.<ref name="IBGE_PIB-RM">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/economia/pibmunicipios/2004_2008/ |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.ibge.gov.br%2Fhome%2Festatistica%2Feconomia%2Fpibmunicipios%2F2004_2008%2F&date=2011-06-19 |archive-date=June 19, 2011 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios 2004-2008 |trans-title=Gross Domestic Product of the Municipalities 2004–2008 |author=[[Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]] (IBGE) |access-date=May 6, 2012}}</ref>

Hortolândia is part of the so-called "[[Expanded Metropolitan Complex]]", which exceeds 29 million inhabitants, approximately 75 percent of the entire population of the state of São Paulo. The metropolitan regions of Campinas and [[Greater São Paulo|São Paulo]] already form the first [[megalopolis]] (or macrometropolis) in the [[Southern Hemisphere]], uniting 65 municipalities that together contain more than 12% of the Brazilian population.<ref name="Estadão_Conurbação"/>

[[File:Área_verde_no_B._Jardim_Nova_Europa,_Hortolândia_SP.jpg|left|thumb|Green areas in Hortolândia.]]

=== Poverty, inequality and growth ===
According to the IBGE, in 2003 the [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[economic inequality|social inequality]], was 0.40, where 1.00 is the worst number and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="GINI" /> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[economic inequality|social inequality]], is 0.39, where 1.00 is the worst number and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="GINI" /> The incidence of poverty, measured by the IBGE, is 24.01%; the lower limit of poverty incidence is 17.16%, the upper limit is 30.87%, and the incidence of subjective poverty is 18.48%.<ref name="GINI">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Mapa de Pobreza e Desigualdade - Municípios Brasileiros 2003 |trans-title=Poverty and Inequality Map - Brazilian Municipalities 2003 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2003 |access-date=July 29, 2012}}</ref>

In 2010, 81.3% of the population lived above the poverty line, 6.0% were on the poverty line and 12.7% were below it.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/PDF/gera_PDF.php?cidade=50240
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/69Wz9jhC9
 |archive-date=July 29, 2012 |title=Perfil municipal - Hortolândia - SP |trans-title=Municipal profile - Hortolândia - SP |author=Portal ODM |date=2010 |access-date=July 29, 2012}}</ref> In 2000, the share of the wealthiest 20% of the city's population in total municipal income was 50.4%, 14 times higher than that of the poorest 20%, which was 3.6%; in 1991 the share of the poorest 20% was 5.0%, that is, from the beginning of the 1990s to the year 2000 there was growth in social inequality in the city.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>
[[File:Minha Casa Minha Vida em Hortolândia SP.jpg|thumb|left|Banner announcing construction works for the [[Minha Casa, Minha Vida]] project in Hortolândia.]]

According to the city government, in 2008 there were records of irregular subdivisions, slums, mocambos, stilt houses, or similar settlements, and {{formatnum:2934}} inhabitants lived in them.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> Many of these are people who came from other cities or even states in search of better life opportunities in Hortolândia, but did not find work and ended up settling in subnormal clusters. According to the IBGE, in 2010 the city's favelas were Jardim Estrela, Jardim do Bosque, Jardim do Lago, Sumarezinho and an extension of Vila Real, but the city government recognizes only Vila Real as a subnormal cluster.<ref name="Liberal_Favelas">{{cite web|url=http://www.oliberalvirtual.com.br/noticia/A99F9DF1B77-ibge_aponta_rpt_com_42_mil_pessoas_morando_em_favelas
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/69WzB1AE8
 |archive-date=July 29, 2012 |title=IBGE aponta RPT com 4,2 mil pessoas morando em favelas |trans-title=IBGE indicates the RPT has 4,200 people living in favelas |author=Valdecir Cremon |date=December 21, 2011 |work=O Liberal |access-date=July 29, 2012}}</ref> To reverse the situation there was an attempt to remove favela residents to new houses or even grant housing assistance to some families. Other projects include the construction of housing complexes (COHABs) in vacant spaces in the city, in accordance with the guidelines of the Master Plan of Hortolândia.<ref name="Liberal_Favelas"/>

=== Religion ===
Most residents of Hortolândia declare themselves Catholic, although today it is possible to find dozens of different Protestant denominations in the city, as well as the practice of [[Buddhism]], [[Islam]], [[Espiritismo|Spiritism]], among others. The Jewish, Latter-day Saint, and Afro-Brazilian religious communities are also significant. According to data from the 2010 census carried out by the IBGE, the population of Hortolândia is composed of: [[Catholic Church|Catholics]] (49.43%), [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]] (35.76%), [[Irreligion|people with no religion]] (9.97%), [[Espiritismo|Spiritists]] (1.10%) and the remainder divided among other religions.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=11&i=P&c=2094

==== Reformed churches ====
The city has the most diverse Protestant or Reformed creeds, such as the [[Lutheranism|Lutheran Church]], the [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian Church]], the [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], the [[Anglican Communion|Anglican Episcopal Church]], the [[Baptists|Baptist churches]], and the [[Methodism|Methodist Church]], as well as [[Pentecostalism|Pentecostal]] denominations such as the [[Assemblies of God in Brazil|Assemblies of God]], Christian Congregation in Brazil and [[neo-Pentecostal]] movements such as Comunidade Cristã Novo Nascimento, [[Sara Nossa Terra|Comunidade Evangélica Sara Nossa Terra]], the [[The Foursquare Church|Igreja do Evangelho Quadrangular]], the [[World Church of the Power of God]], and the [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], among others.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/><ref name="IBGE_Religião"/> As noted above, according to the IBGE, in 2010 35.76% of the population were Protestants.<ref name="IBGE_Religião"/> Of this total, 9.52% belong to Evangelical churches of Pentecostal origin; 2.87% belong to missionary Evangelical churches; 0.64% belong to Evangelical churches without institutional affiliation; and 0.39% belong to other Evangelical religions.<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/><ref name="IBGE_Religião"/>

There are also Christians of several other denominations, such as [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (who represent 1.25% of the inhabitants) and members of [[the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (0.20%).<ref name="SIDRA_Religião"/><ref name="IBGE_Religião"/>

== Politics and administration ==
[[File:Palácio das àguas - Prefeitura Municipal de Hortolândia - panoramio.jpg|thumb|left|Palácio das Águas - Municipal Government of Hortolândia.]]
The municipal administration is carried out by the [[executive branch]] and the [[legislative branch]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/67UGgjcdE

Lima |date=February 8, 2006 |work=JusVi |access-date=July 29, 2012}}</ref> The first to govern the municipality was Luís Antônio Dias da Silva, of the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB), who was elected after the first elections were held in the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The current mayor is José Nazareno Zezé Gomes, of the [[Republicanos (Brazil)|Republicanos]], who assumed office as mayor after the death of elected mayor Angelo Augusto Perugini due to complications from COVID-19, who had won the [[2020 Brazilian municipal elections|2020 Brazilian municipal elections]]. Because Hortolândia had fewer than 200 thousand voters and none of the candidates received less than 50% of the total votes in the first round, there was no runoff. In February 2021, the city's mayor Angelo Augusto Perugini was removed from office by the City Council after testing positive for COVID-19 and being admitted to Samaritano Hospital in Campinas, then transferred to a hospital in São Paulo; after being hospitalized for two months, the elected mayor died on April 1, 2021. The city's vice mayor, José Nazareno Zeze Gomes (Zezé), officially assumed office.<ref name="Prefeito">{{cite web |url=http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-prefeito-hortolandia-sp/
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/6DvDxOBaD

The [[legislative branch]] is constituted by the [[municipal chamber of Brazil|council]], composed of 19 councilors elected for four-year terms (in observance of article 29 of the [[Constitution of Brazil|Constitution]]<ref name="Constituição">{{cite web |url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm

 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/6DvDyLPuz
 |archive-date=January 24, 2013 |title=Candidatos a Vereador Hortolândia/SP |trans-title=Candidates for Councilor of Hortolândia/SP |author= |date=October 7, 2012 |access-date=January 24, 2013}}</ref> six seats for the [[Workers' Party (Brazil)|Workers' Party]] (PT); three seats for the [[Brazilian Socialist Party]] (PSB); three seats for the [[Republican Progressive Party]] (PRP); two seats for the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB); two seats for the [[Free Fatherland Party]] (PPL); one seat for the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB); one for the [[Green Party (Brazil)|Green Party]]; and one for the [[Party of the Republic (Brazil)|Party of the Republic]] (PR).<ref name="vereadores"/> It is the responsibility of the chamber to draft and vote on laws fundamental to the administration and the executive, especially the participatory budget ([[Budget Guidelines Law]]).<ref name="Constituição"/>

== Economy ==
[[File:Camelôs em Hortolândia SP.jpg|thumb|right|[[Street market]] in Hortolândia.]]
The [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) of Hortolândia is the fifth largest in the [[Metropolitan Region of Campinas]], the [[List of municipalities in São Paulo by GDP|27th largest in the state of São Paulo]] and the [[List of municipalities of Brazil by GDP|89th in the entire country]].<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/sp/hortolandia/pesquisa/38/47001?tipo=ranking&indicador=47001 |title=PIB a preços correntes |trans-title=GDP at current prices |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=2019 |access-date=December 17, 2021}}</ref> According to IBGE data referring to 2010, the municipality's GDP was R$ {{formatnum:6226404}} million.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> {{formatnum:490274}} thousand are taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices.<ref name="IBGE@_PIB">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pibmunic&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios |trans-title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref> The [[GDP per capita|per capita GDP]] is R$ {{formatnum:23588.03}}<ref name="IBGE@_PIB" /> and in 2000 the income [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) was 0.7, while that of Brazil in that year was 0.723.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/>

According to the IBGE, in 2010 the city had {{formatnum:4736}} local units and {{formatnum:4674}} active companies and commercial establishments. {{formatnum:50188}} workers were classified as total employed personnel and {{formatnum:44204}} were categorized as salaried employed personnel. [[Salary|Salaries]] together with other remuneration totaled {{formatnum:1587615}} reais and the average monthly salary in the entire municipality was 6.1 [[minimum wage]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Estatísticas do Cadastro Central de Empresas |trans-title=Statistics of the Central Register of Companies |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref> The unemployment rate of the economically active population is 4%.<ref name="Comercial_Comércio"/>

;'''Primary sector'''
Agriculture is the least relevant sector of Hortolândia's economy. Of the city's total GDP, {{formatnum:4152}} thousand reais are the gross added value of agriculture and livestock.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> According to the IBGE in 2010, the municipality had about 100 cattle, 24 horses and 39 pigs. Twelve cows were milked, and milk production reached 22 thousand liters that year. There were no birds being raised for agricultural purposes<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2010&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Pecuária 2010 |trans-title=Livestock 2010 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref> and there were also no large [[agriculture|crops]] producing for profit, according to the IBGE.<ref name="agricultura">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2010&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Lavoura Temporária 2010 |trans-title=Temporary Crops 2010 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavperm2010&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia
 |title=Lavoura Permanente 2010 |trans-title=Permanent Crops 2010 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref> The city stands out only in family farming, as it is one of the cities in the Metropolitan Region of Campinas that buys the most products coming directly from small farmers in the region, supplying food for the city's Community Kitchen, and there are 5 thousand families registered with Hortolândia's welfare entities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.hortolandia.com/novo_site/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=3097&Itemid=203
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/69XnByBJl
 |archive-date=July 30, 2012 |title=Banco de Alimentos de Hortolândia fortalece Agricultura Familiar |trans-title=Hortolândia Food Bank strengthens Family Farming |author=Hortolândia.com |date=November 3, 2010 |access-date=July 30, 2012}}</ref>

Around 1866, the area of the current municipality was divided into agricultural properties. This region, which then belonged to Campinas, stood out in the production of coffee, cotton and sugar, in addition to subsistence crops. However, in the mid-1970s, a major industrialization process began there (now Sumaré), with tax incentives, until agriculture almost completely disappeared.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

;'''Secondary sector'''
[[File:Usina de reciclagem em Hortolândia SP.jpg|thumb|left|Waste recycling plant in Hortolândia.]]
[[Industry (economics)|Industry]], at present, is the second most relevant sector for the municipality's economy. {{formatnum:1605035}} reais of municipal GDP are gross value added by industry (secondary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Its industrial structure is also formed by high-technology companies that require specialized labor. With more than 120 years of historical records, eighteen of them as a municipality, Hortolândia has consolidated itself in the Metropolitan Region of Campinas (RMC) as a city with great economic development. The city is home to the multinational [[IBM]], which settled there in 1972. The company is located in the Tech Town industrial condominium, which houses other large enterprises.<ref name="Prefeitura_História" /><ref name="epoca">{{cite web |url=http://revistaepoca.globo.com/Revista/Epoca/0,,EDG76118-6012,00.html

[[File:EMS - panoramio.jpg|thumb|right|Headquarters of EMS in Hortolândia]]
Industrial development is due to the city's privileged location within the state. Hortolândia's proximity to [[Viracopos International Airport]], the presence of important highways around it and the fact that it is in a region with a high concentration of development in the country, considered a [[science|scientific]] and industrial hub, are primary and decisive factors in attracting enterprises.<ref name="netlegis">{{cite web |url=http://www.netlegis.com.br/imprimirPage.jsp?imp=/detalhesDestaquesP.jsp?cod=31248&imprimir=sim

Between 2005 and 2008, the size of the municipality's economy grew 105%, reaching R$ 3.5 billion.<ref name="hortooficial">{{cite web |url=http://www.hortolandia.sp.gov.br/wps/portal/!ut/p/c0/04_SB8K8xLLM9MSSzPy8xBz9CP0os3hHDxdTZyMvYwN_TzMnAyMvA2MnD48gIwMPI_2CbEdFAAFp9wY!/?WCM_GLOBAL_CONTEXT=/wps/wcm/connect/Hortolandia/hortolandia/noticias/novo+perfil+economico+de+hortolandia+atrai+condominios+residenciais+de+alto+padrao

;'''Tertiary sector'''
The provision of [[service (economics)|services]] yields {{formatnum:2756277}} thousand reais to the municipal GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In recent years, growth in the local trade and service sector has been perceived. According to the General Registry of Employed and Unemployed Persons (CAGED), trade is currently responsible for generating most of the formally registered jobs in the city.<ref name="Comercial_Comércio">{{cite web|url=http://comercialhortolandia.com/hortolandia/emprego-em-hortolandia-cresce-com-comercio

Large retail companies, major commercial establishments and multinational companies in the commerce sector have recently set up in the city, such as [[Tenda Atacado]], [[Pernambucanas]], [[Lojas Americanas|Americanas]], [[Marisa (retailer)|Marisa]], [[McDonald's]], [[Burger King]], Griletto, and [[Subway (restaurant)|Subway]], among others, inside Shopping Hortolândia, at the end of 2011, and [[Magazine Luiza]] located next to Supermercado Paulistão Hortolândia.<ref name="Comercial_Comércio"/> In Hortolândia's Commercial Center a significant share of local commercial activity is concentrated, and profits grow by up to 30% at special times, such as Christmas. In 2009, the municipality had 37 health establishments among hospitals, [[emergency department|emergency rooms]], [[clinic|health posts]] and [[dentistry|dental services]], 24 of them public and 13 private. In them the city had 68 beds for [[hospitalization]], all of them in public facilities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia

their deliveries assisted by qualified health professionals.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> In that same year, 15.1% of all pregnant women were girls under 20 years old.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> {{formatnum:5464}} children were weighed by the [[Family Health Program]], and 0.5% of them were malnourished.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

=== Education ===
[[File:Escola Técnica de Hortolândia - panoramio.jpg|thumb|left|Technical School of Hortolândia]]
The average [[Basic Education Development Index]] (IDEB) among Hortolândia's public schools was, in 2009, 5.0 (on an evaluation scale from 1 to 10), with the score obtained by 5th-year students (former 4th grade) being 5.4 and by 9th-year students (former 8th grade) being 4.6; the value for public schools throughout Brazil was 4.0. The IDEB for the 5th year was the 1086th highest in the country, while that of the 9th year was the 656th.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/> The Human Development Index (HDI) value for education was 0.883 (classified as high), while that of Brazil is 0.849.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

The municipality had, in 2009, approximately {{formatnum:45584}} enrollments in public and private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> According to the IBGE, in that same year, of the 69 elementary schools, 26 belonged to the state public network, 34 to the municipal public network and 9 were private schools. Among the 25 secondary education institutions, 22 belonged to the state public network and 3 to private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> In 2010, 16.0% of children aged 7 to 14 were not attending elementary school. The completion rate among young people aged 15 to 17 that year was 66.2%. The literacy rate of the population aged 15 or older, in 2010, was 99.1%. In 2006, for every 100 girls in elementary school, there were 102 boys.<ref name="ODM_Perfil"/>

=== Science and technology ===
Hortolândia has one technical school, [[ETEC Hortolândia]], consisting of technical courses in the areas of [[computer science]], [[business administration|administration]], [[secretarial studies|secretarial studies]] and [[nutrition]]. The computer science course stands out, qualifying technical professionals in the area of programming. Curricular components of [[software]] development are taught in the [[C (programming language)|C]] and [[Java (programming language)|Java]] languages. Graduates leave qualified for the labor market in information technology, especially in software programming.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.etechortolandia.com.br/novo/cursos.php?clean=y
 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/69YHgCSj3

In the [[Metropolitan Region of Campinas|RMC]], only Hortolândia and [[Campinas]] were selected for implementation of the new units in the [[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|MEC]] plan in 2010.<ref>Projetos Prioritários SETEC, Ministério da Educação, pp 5-9, 2008</ref>

The city is also home to a [[technology park]], whose objective is to collaborate in the scientific and technological development of the region, attracting companies that invest in research and development of products aimed at the industrial and technological sectors, such as the Service Center of [[IBM]], [[Dell]] Computers and the Chinese company [[ZTE]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://comercialhortolandia.com/hortolandia/hortolandia-apresenta-polo-tecnologico-a-turistas

Of the total number of households, {{formatnum:39496}} were owner-occupied properties, of which {{formatnum:37012}} were fully paid off, {{formatnum:2484}} were being purchased and {{formatnum:11526}} were [[renting|rented]]; {{formatnum:3227}} properties were ceded, 274 by employers and {{formatnum:2953}} ceded in another manner. {{formatnum:1144}} were occupied in another way. A large part of the municipality has treated water, [[electricity]], [[sewage]], [[municipal solid waste|urban cleaning]], [[landline|fixed telephony]] and [[cellular network|mobile telephony]]. In that year, 98.14% of households were served by the general [[drinking water|water supply network]]; 99.64% of homes had some type of garbage collection and 99.6% of residences had a bathroom for exclusive use of the household.<ref name="IBGE_DomicípiosPop">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=result_univer_censo2010&codmun=351907&nomemun=Hortol%E2ndia

=== Transport ===
[[File:Linha Férrea1.jpg|thumb|Railway line that passes through Hortolândia.]]
; Aviation
Hortolândia does not have commercial [[airport]]s in its territory; however, [[Viracopos International Airport]] is located in the municipality of Campinas, about 30 km from the center of Hortolândia, in the same [[Metropolitan Region of Campinas]], serving the entire region with daily flights to [[São Paulo]] and other destinations in Brazil and the world.<ref name="Prefeitura_História" /> The territory of the current municipality has had railways since 1916, and the city's main railway station, Hortolândia Station (formerly Jacuba Station), was inaugurated that year as a telegraphic post and transformed into a stopping point on April 1, 1917. At that time it belonged to the [[Companhia Paulista de Estradas de Ferro]], which administered the railway until 1971, when the company responsible became [[Ferrovia Paulista S/A|Ferrovia Paulista S/A (FEPASA)]]. In the early 1990s rail transport began to decline in the region, as in much of the state of São Paulo and Brazil, and the station was deactivated in 1991. It is currently abandoned, but there are projects to restore the building.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/h/hortolandia.htm

However, they have been reduced to a few daily freight train trips, with diesel-powered locomotives at very low speed, transporting cargo to other cities in São Paulo.<ref name="Horto_Trans_Tri">{{cite web |url=http://www.intelog.net/site/default.asp?TroncoID=907492&SecaoID=508074&SubsecaoID=538090&Template=../artigosnoticias/user_exibir.asp&ID=918453

Hortolândia also has a municipal band, which frequently performs at public events organized by the municipal government and by the band itself, in events offered free of charge to the population. Created in 1994 by Municipal Law No. 188, the Municipal Band of Hortolândia is one of the founding pillars of CEMMH (Municipal Music Education Center of Hortolândia). Formed by instruments from the woodwind, brass and percussion families, it offers an eclectic repertoire that covers all musical genres of an erudite and popular nature. At CEMMH, free courses are offered for all young people who wish to learn music and some wind and percussion instrument. It also includes some chamber groups, separated by instruments, such as the saxophone group and flute group, which also perform, like the band, at events held by the municipality's municipal government.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cemmh.webnode.com.br/banda-municipal/
