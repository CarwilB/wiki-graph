'''Úmbita''' is a town and municipality in the [[Márquez Province]], part of the [[Colombia]]n [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]]. Úmbita is situated on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]] at a distance of {{convert|70|km|mi}} from department capital [[Tunja]] and borders [[Nuevo Colón, Boyacá|Nuevo Colón]] and [[Tibaná]] in the north, [[Chinavita]] in the east, [[La Capilla]] and [[Tibiritá]] ([[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]]) in the south and in the west [[Villapinzón]] (Cundinamarca) and [[Turmequé]]. The altitude within the municipality ranges from {{convert|1600|m|ft}} to {{convert|3400|m|ft}}.<ref name=websiteUmbita>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.umbita-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Úmbita]</ref>

== Etymology ==
The name Úmbita is [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]] and means "Your point, your summit, summit of the farmlands".<ref name=excelsio>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.excelsio.net/2011/11/sabe-el-origen-del-nombre-de-su.html Etymology Úmbita] - Excelsio.net</ref>

== History ==
Úmbita in the centuries before the arrival of the Spanish [[conquistador]]es was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]], organized in a loose [[Muisca Confederation|confederation]] of different rulers; ''[[zaque]]s'' in [[Tunja|Hunza]], ''[[zipa]]s'' in [[Bogotá|Bacatá]] and other rulers of lower levels such as ''[[cacique]]''s. Úmbita was ruled by ''cacique'' Cochonuba.<ref name=websiteUmbita/>

The municipality was part of a larger division within Boyacá, called Chivití, shared with the present-day municipality of Nuevo Colón. In 1778 they were split and Úmbita was properly founded on November 17, 1779.<ref name=websiteUmbita/>

== Economy ==
Úmbita is a very rural municipality with 90% of the inhabitants living outside of the urban center and their economy is focused on [[agriculture]]. It is one of the five most productive [[potato]] farming villages in Boyacá. Other agricultural products are [[maize]], [[pea]]s, [[bean]]s, [[arracacha]], [[yuca]], [[sugar cane]], [[avocado]]es, [[banana]]s and [[coffee]]. In recent years the fruit industry has grown with fruits such as [[prune]]s, [[apple]]s, [[peach]]es, [[blackberry|blackberries]], [[pear]]s, [[tree tomato]]es and the typical Colombian fruits [[curuba]] and [[uchuva]]. Also medicinal plants such as [[Mentha spicata|mint]], [[chamomile]], plantain leaves and [[nettle]] are cultivated.<ref name=websiteUmbita/>
