The '''Rosario de Perijá Municipality''' is located in [[Zulia]], [[Venezuela]]. It was founded in 1989. The municipality has a total surface of 3,543&nbsp;km² and it has some 67,712 inhabitant (2001 census). Its capital is [[Villa del Rosario, Venezuela|Villa del Rosario]]. The municipality is made of three civil parishes: Donaldo García (7,489 inhabitants), El Rosario (53,953 inhabitants) and Sixto Zambrano (6,270 inhabitants).

The local economy is based primarily on cattle raising. There is also an important exploitation of limestone for the cement industry.

== Population ==

The majority of the population is made up of average mixed-race Venezuelans, but there are Native American communities in the Western and Northern parts: [[Yukpa]] and [[Wayuu]] Indians. There is also a small community of Japrería Indians.

There has been a serious conflict for several decades between the Native American population and the cattle ranchers for the use of the lands.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bibliojuridica.org/libros/6/2727/5.pdf |title=Archived copy |accessdate=2010-10-05 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110728033738/http://www.bibliojuridica.org/libros/6/2727/5.pdf |archivedate=2011-07-28 }} Pueblos Originarios del Zulia</ref>

== Geography ==
=== Climate ===
Minimum monthly temperature varies with elevation between 6.9 and 23.4&nbsp;°C while maximum temperatures are between 17.9 and 33.4&nbsp;°C. Annual precipitation is between 1228 and 2151&nbsp;mm, with most rainfall between September and November, and the driest quarter between January and March.<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer-Paris|first1=José R.|title=Caracterización ambiental de la ruta de NeoMapas: NM05 Rosario de Perijá, estado Zulia (CNEB d02)|journal=Figshare|date=2017|doi=10.6084/m9.figshare.4733107.v1}}</ref>

== Fauna and flora ==

The municipality of Rosario de Perijá lies in the Cordillera Oriental Montane Forests ecoregion, and the major natural habitat type is [[tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests]].<ref name="TNCecoreg">''The Nature Conservancy'' (2009) TNC terrestrial ecoregions, [http://maps.tnc.org/files/metadata/TerrEcos.xml]. Based on: Olson, D. M. and E. Dinerstein. 2002. The Global 200: Priority ecoregions for global conservation. (PDF file) Annals of the Missouri Botanical Garden 89:125-126.</ref>

Common birds include Great Egret (''[[Ardea alba]]''), Mealy Parrot (''[[Amazona farinosa]]''), Bicolored Wren (''[[Campylorhynchus griseus]]''), Brown-throated Parakeet (''[[Aratinga pertinax]]''), Tropical Kingbird (''[[Tyrannus melancholicus]]''), Carib Grackle (''[[Quiscalus lugubris]]'').<ref name='NeoMapas'>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer-Paris|first1=José Rafael|last2=Rodríguez|first2=Jon Paul|last3=Good|first3=Tatjana C.|last4=Sánchez-Mercado|first4=Ada|last5=Rodríguez-Clark|first5=Kathryn M.|last6=Rodríguez|first6=Gustavo A.|last7=Solís|first7=Ángel|title=Systematic, large-scale national biodiversity surveys: NeoMaps as a model for tropical regions|journal= Diversity and Distributions| date=2013|volume=19|issue=2|pages=215–231|doi=10.1111/ddi.12012|s2cid=84741871 |url=https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235954117}}</ref> At least 28 species of [[dung beetles]] have been reported in systematic surveys, including ''[[Canthon.mutabilis]]'', ''[[Canthidium.lebasi]]'', ''[[Onthophagus.marginicollis]]'', ''[[Dichotomius.agenor]]'', ''[[Coprophanaeus.gamezi]]'', ''[[Canthon.lituratus]]''.<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer-Paris|first1=José R.|last2=Rodríguez|first2=Jon Paul|last3=Sánchez-Mercado|first3=Ada|title=Optimización del muestreo de invertebrados tropicales: Un ejemplo con escarabajos coprófagos (Coleoptera: Scarabaeinae) en Venezuela|journal=Revista de Biología Tropical|date=2013|volume=61|issue=1|pages=89–100|doi=10.15517/rbt.v61i1.10941|doi-access=free}}</ref>
