'''Lagunillas Municipality''' is one of the 21 [[Municipalities of Venezuela|municipalities]] that make up the [[States of Venezuela|state]] of [[Zulia]], [[Venezuela]]. Found on the eastern coast of [[Lake Maracaibo]], it is notable for being the lowest-lying point in Venezuela, sitting at 8 meters below [[sea level]]. This unique geography requires a massive 49-kilometer dike system to protect its urban population from [[flooding]]. The estimated population is over 225,000 residents with a large majority in its [[shire town]] at [[Ciudad Ojeda]]. Locally, the municipality is governed by Mayor José Mosquera, who was first elected in 2021 and re-elected for the 2025–2029 term.

==Geography==
The Lagunillas Municipality is found on the eastern coast of [[Lake Maracaibo]] in the [[States of Venezuela|state]] of [[Zulia]]. The region's terrain is characterized by a flat relief predominantly covered by dry forests. A defining geographical feature of Lagunillas is its extreme low [[elevation]] below sea level. This negative altitude creates a highly challenging environment that is susceptible to [[flooding]] from the neighboring lake. To protect the [[urban center]] from major floods, a massive 49-kilometer-long wall, known as the ''[[Muro de Lagunillas]]'', serves as a barrier between the municipality and Lake Maracaibo. Originally built by the [[oil industry]] in the 1940s, this protective dike system faces ongoing concerns regarding a lack of proper maintenance, which could potentially weaken the structure over time and elevate the risk of inundation.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.clarin.com/informacion-general/geografia-curiosa-ciudad-baja-latinoamerica_0_qH6viAa98t.html|title=Geografía curiosa: esta es la ciudad más baja de Latinoamérica|trans-title=Curious Geography: This is the Lowest City in Latin America|lang=es|newspaper=[[Clarín (Argentine newspaper)|Clarín]]|date=12 October 2025|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the [[2011 Venezuelan census]], The population of the Lagunillas Municipality was 205,929 people, accounting for 5.37% of the total population of the state of [[Zulia]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/23__zulia/|title=Zulia (State in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref> A large majority of the population (67.9%) resides in [[Ciudad Ojeda]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/zulia/2310__lagunillas/|title=Urban Localities in Lagunillas (Zulia, Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=25 June 2017|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref> the [[shire town]] of the municipality.<ref>{{cite journal|url=http://www.ulpiano.org.ve/revistas/bases/artic/texto/RVGP/3/RVGP_2012_3_111-143.pdf|title=Diagnóstico en El Área De Desarrollo De La Gestión Pública Local Del Municipio Lagunillas Del Estado Zulia|trans-title=Diagnosis in the Area of Development of Local Public Management of the Lagunillas Municipality of Zulia State|lang=es|first=Yusmila|last=De Nicolo de Ocando|journal=Venezuelan Journal of Social Sciences|pages=111–143|publisher=[[University of Zulia]]|date=January–December 2012|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>

By June 2019, official projections from the [[Statistics National Institute (Venezuela)|Venezuelan Statistics National Institute]] estimated the population of Lagunillas as 225,745 people, representing an [[annual growth rate]] of 1.2% since 2011 and showing a population density of 220.5 inhabitants/km². However, these projections do not account for the impact of emigration linked to the country's recent economic and political circumstances.<ref name="CityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/zulia/2310__lagunillas/|title=Lagunillas (Municipality in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>

The [[gender]] distribution of the population showed a very even balance with 101,796 men and 101,639 women (approximately 50% each). The [[age distribution]] showed that the largest segment of the population was aged 15 to 64, comprising 67.5% of the people. Younger people aged 0 to 14 made up 27.6% of the population, while those aged 65 and older accounted for the remaining 4.9%. The municipality is almost completely urbanized, with 95.7% of the inhabitants (194,679) living in [[urban centers]] compared to only 4.3% (8,756) in [[rural areas]].<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Ethnically, the municipality identified as predominantly [[Mestizo]] (50.6%) and [[White people]] (46.1%). Minority groups included 2.6% [[Afro-Venezuelans]] as well as 0.5% indigenous people and 0.2% belonging to other ethnic groups. The [[literacy rate]] was 97%, with 5,057 inhabitants of Lagunillas not able to read or write.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

==Government==
Lagunillas Municipality is led by the mayor José Mosquera. He initially came to office after winning the 2021 municipal elections, running with the backing of the [[Democratic Unity Roundtable]] (MUD) coalition. He is currently a member of the [[Un Nuevo Tiempo]] (UNT) political party, which is led by [[Manuel Rosales]], the governor of [[Zulia]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://elestimulo.com/elecciones-2024/2024-08-06/jose-mosquera-alcalde-lagunillas-zulia-libre/|title=Zulia: José Mosquera, alcalde de Lagunillas, fue liberado tras pasar una semana preso|trans-title=Zulia: José Mosquera, Mayor of Lagunillas, Released After a Week in Jail|lang=es|newspaper=[[El Estimulo]]|date=6 August 2024|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref> On 27 July 2025, running as the candidate for the [[Democratic Alliance (Venezuela)|Democratic Alliance]], Mosquera was re-elected to serve a second consecutive term as mayor for the 2025–2029 period.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://elorientaldemonagas.com/zulia-jose-mosquera-reelecto-como-alcalde-de-lagunillas-para-el-periodo-2025-2029/|title=Zulia {{!}} José Mosquera reelecto como alcalde de Lagunillas para el período 2025-2029|trans-title=Zulia {{!}} José Mosquera re-elected as mayor of Lagunillas for the 2025-2029 term|lang=es|first=Heidi|last=Campos|newspaper=[[El Oriental de Monagas]]|date=28 July 2025|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>
