'''  San Bartolomé Quialana ''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 49.76&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. 
It is part of the [[Tlacolula District, Oaxaca|Tlacolula District]] in the east of the [[Valles Centrales de Oaxaca|Valles Centrales Region]].

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 2,485.<ref>{{cite web|title=San Bartolomé Quialana|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20118a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=June 12, 2009}}</ref>

Women in the area wear traditional clothing.

== Language ==
Zapotec is an indigenous language spoken in the state of Oaxaca and it is a member of the Oto-Manguean language family. Instead of being a single, cohesive language, Zapotec is made up of numerous dialects, some of which are incomprehensible to one another.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-25 |title=Zapoteco Language: A Deep Insight Into Oaxaca's Indigenous Linguistic Heritage |url=https://languagexs.com/zapoteco-language-oaxaca-indigenous-heritage/ |access-date=2025-12-14 |language=en-US}}</ref> Dizhsa or Valley Zapotec is commonly spoken in San Bartolome Quialana alongside Spanish. Neighboring towns, such as San Lucas Quiavini, also speak Valley Zapotec but they are not the same. While they may sound similar, the accents and vocabulary differ from other Zapotec speakers or towns depending on where they are from.

==Etymology==
''Quialana'' is a [[Zapotec language|Zapotec]] word that means "black rocks" or "blackened rocks." This is associated with the fact that there is a rocky hill in the area which is called ''Picacho'' or ''Yubda'' (in Zapotec, "Sun Rock).

== Traditional clothing ==
[[File:WikiLovesMexico AGRTlacolulaMercado1.jpg|alt=|thumb|Women from San Bartolome Quialana in traditional attire, selling in the market of Tlacolula, Oaxaca ]]
The women of San Bartolome Quialana have two traditional ways of dressing. The first one is a more traditional attire, but both are worn day to day. The top half consists of a floral embroidered blouse underneath a floral blouse that is tied in the back. The bottom half consists of a skirt worn underneath a plaid wrap skirt that is held up by a handwoven belt<ref>{{Cite web |title=San Bartolome Quialana, is a Zapotec town on the outskirts of Tlacalula., it is one of a number of traditional villages in the district. |url=https://www.mexicantextiles.com/grouppages/groups/zapotecvalley/sanbartolome/index.htm |access-date=2025-12-24 |website=www.mexicantextiles.com}}</ref> which is tightly wrapped around the waist to prevent the wrap skirt from slipping off. Then it is topped off with a small apron that is tied around the waist over the skirt to prevent the wrap skirt from getting dirty. 
[[File:WikiLovesMexico AGRSnBartolome1.jpg|thumb|Young woman in her traditional attire embroidering a blouse. Photo taken by Amalia Fotógrafa]]
The second one is simpler. A skirt and plain undershirt worn underneath a floral dress topped off with a beautifully embroidered apron.
