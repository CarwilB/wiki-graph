'''Guayatá''' is a town and municipality in the [[Eastern Boyacá Province]], part of the [[Colombia]]n [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]]. Guayatá is situated on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]] at distances of {{convert|129|km|mi}} from the department capital [[Tunja]] and {{convert|132|km|mi}} from the national capital [[Bogotá]]. The urban centre is located at an altitude of {{convert|1767|m|ft}} and the altitude ranges from {{convert|1270|m|ft}} to {{convert|3080|m|ft}}.<ref name=websiteGuayata>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.guayata-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Guayatá]{{Dead link|date=January 2020 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

== Borders ==

== Etymology ==
The name Guayatá comes from [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]] and is either a combination of ''tá''; "land over there" or "farmfields" and ''Guaya'', a creek running through [[Tenza]] or from ''Guaitá''; "domain of the female ''cacique''".<ref name=websiteGuayata/><ref name=excelsio>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.excelsio.net/2011/11/sabe-el-origen-del-nombre-de-su.html Etymology Guayatá] - Excelsio.net</ref>

== History ==
In the times before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]] of the central highlands of the Colombian [[Andes]], the area around Guayatá was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]]. Organized in their loose [[Muisca Confederation]], they were an advanced [[Muisca agriculture|agricultural]] civilization. Within present-day Guayatá [[cotton]] was cultivated, important for the mantle making of the Muisca. Also feathers of hunted birds were traded in and around Guayatá.<ref name=websiteGuayata/>

At the time of the arrival of the Spanish [[conquistador]]es, in Guayatá [[Muisca economy|money]] was found that consisted of small pieces of cloth, [[gold]] or [[emerald]]s. The golden disks used as money were not decorated yet plain. Knowledge about the Muisca in the early colonial period has been provided by [[friar]] [[Pedro Simón]].<ref name=websiteGuayata/>

Modern Guayatá was founded on April 6, 1821, by Andrés José Medina Narciso; months before the dissolution of the Spanish colonial period and independence of the [[Republic of Gran Colombia]].<ref name=websiteGuayata/>

== Economy ==
Principal economic activity of Guayatá is [[agriculture]], mainly [[maize]], [[arracacha]], [[banana]]s, [[yuca]], [[bean]]s, [[potato]]es, [[coffee]], [[pumpkin]]s and [[pea]]s. Famous product of Guayatá are the bread rolls (''mogollas'').<ref name=websiteGuayata/>
