'''Juchipila''' ([[Caxcan]]: ''Xuchipilan'', "place of flowered nobles"<ref name=encyclopedia>{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/32023a.html |title=Juchipila |language=es |website=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=[[INAFED]] |access-date=11 October 2021 |archive-date=10 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210310185548/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/32023a.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>) is a [[Municipalities of Zacatecas|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Zacatecas]], located approximately {{convert|160|km}} southwest of the state capital [[Zacatecas City]].

==Geography==
The municipality of Juchipila is located at an elevation between {{convert|1800|and(-)|2700|m}} in the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] in southwestern Zacatecas. The municipality spans the canyon of the [[Juchipila River]], a tributary of the [[Río Grande de Santiago]], which is flanked by the [[Sierra de Morones]] to the west and the Sierra de Nochistlán to the east.<ref name=encyclopedia/> The municipality borders the municipalities of [[Apozol Municipality|Apozol]] to the north, [[Nochistlán de Mejía Municipality|Nochistlán de Mejía]] to the east, [[Moyahua de Estrada]] to the south, [[Mezquital del Oro]] to the southwest, [[Teúl de González Ortega Municipality|Teúl de González Ortega]] to the west, and [[Santa María de la Paz]] to the northwest.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/32/32023.pdf |title=Juchipila, Zacatecas |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos |language=es |publisher=INEGI |year=2009 |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|338.6|km2}} and comprises 0.4% of the state's area.<ref name=panorama/>

As of 2009, 15.8% of the land in Juchipila is used for agriculture. The remainder of the land cover consists of [[Tropical and subtropical dry broadleaf forests|tropical dry forest]] (47.4%), [[Sierra Madre Occidental pine–oak forests|temperate forest]] (28.9%), grassland (7.1%), and urban areas (0.7%).<ref name=compendium/>

Juchipila's climate ranges from semiarid in the Juchipila River canyon to temperate in the mountains surrounding it. Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|14|and(-)|24|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|700|and(-)|1000|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
[[File:Cerro de las ventanas.jpg|thumb|Cerro de las Ventanas, location of the archaeological site of Las Ventanas, as viewed from the town of Juchipila.]]
Prior to Spanish contact, Juchipila was a [[Caxcan]] ''[[taxtukayul]]'' called the {{ill|Señorío de Juchipila|es}} in Spanish sources.<ref name=plan>{{cite web |url=http://www.juchipila.gob.mx/41/Juchipila-PMD-2018-2021.pdf |title=Plan Municipal de Desarrollo de Juchipila, Zacatecas |language=es |pages=9–14, 37–40 |publisher=Municipality of Juchipila |date=2018 |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> The Caxcanes had invaded the area in the 13th century from Tuitlán, now known as [[La Quemada]].<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Weigand |first1=Phil C. |last2=Weigand |first2=Acelia Garcia |date=Spring 2000 |title=Huichol Society before the Arrival of the Spanish |journal=[[Journal of the Southwest]] |volume=42 |issue=1 |page=13 |id={{Gale|A64972838}}}}</ref>

The archaeological site of {{ill|Las Ventanas (archaeological site)|es|Las Ventanas (sitio arqueológico)|lt=Las Ventanas}}, also known as the Peñol de Juchipila, is located next to the village of [[El Remolino]], {{convert|5|km}} south of the municipal seat of Juchipila. Structures at the site date from the late [[Mesoamerican chronology#Classic period|Classic]] and [[Mesoamerican chronology#Postclassic period|Postclassic]] periods, and other structural and archaeological remains indicate inhabitation as early as the first centuries AD.<ref name=inah>{{cite web |url=https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/en/zonas-arqueologicas/zonas/1900-las-ventanas.html |title=Cerro de Las Ventanas |publisher=[[INAH]] |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> Las Ventanas was one of the most heavily fortified of ancient Mesoamerican sites. Its upper citadel, set above sheer cliffs and accessible only by a steep and narrow stairway with switchbacks, features some of the finest stonework of its kind in all of Mesoamerica. Beneath the citadel is a settlement zone covering more than {{convert|25|ha}}.<ref name=grove>{{cite encyclopedia |title=Peñol de Juchipila [Las Ventanas] |encyclopedia=[[Grove Art Online]] |year=2003 |last1=Weigand |first1=Phil C. |last2=Barnes |first2=Trent |publisher=[[Oxford University Press]] |doi=10.1093/gao/9781884446054.article.T066202 |url=https://doi.org/10.1093/gao/9781884446054.article.T066202 |access-date=11 October 2021|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

Xiuhtecuhtli, the leader of Juchipila, was based at Las Ventanas (then known as Tlatlan or Tlaltan) when the Spanish first arrived in the area around 1530–1531.<ref name=plan/><ref name=grove/> He and other Caxcan leaders led a vigorous resistance to Spanish colonization attempts throughout the 1530s, until the Spanish finally prevailed in the [[Mixtón War]] of 1540–1542.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref name=plan/>

Franciscan friars founded the first monastery in what is now the town of Juchipila around 1532. The church of St. Francis of Assisi, the first built in the region and one of the first in [[Nueva Galicia]], dates to 1627.<ref name=plan/>

From 1548 to 1786, Juchipila was designated an ''alcaldía mayor'' of Nueva Galicia, its territory extending from [[Villanueva, Zacatecas|Villaneuva]] in the north to Moyahua in the south. It then became a ''subdelegacíon'' under the Intendancy of Guadalajara until 1804, when it was transferred along with [[Aguascalientes]] to the Intendancy of Zacatecas.<ref name=plan/>

After Mexican independence, Juchipila became of the original eleven ''partidos'' in the 1825 Constitution of Zacatecas, losing its eastern territories to Aguascalientes and its northern territories to the new ''partido'' of Villanueva. The ''partido'' of Nochistlán was separated from Juchipila in 1852.<ref name=plan/> When ''partidos'' were abolished in 1916, Juchipila was divided into the municipalities of Apozol, Juchipila proper, Moyahua, and Mezquital del Oro.<ref name=plan/><ref name=division>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Zacatecas. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |url= http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/integracion/pais/divi_terri/1810-1985/zac/ZACATECAS.pdf |language=es |pages=59, 117, 141 |location=Mexico |publisher=[[INEGI]] |isbn=970-13-1519-7 |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref>

==Administration==
The municipal government of Juchipila comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and ten trustees (''regidores''), six elected by [[relative majority]] and four by [[proportional representation]].<ref name=encyclopedia/> The current president of the municipality is Jose Maria Castro Felix.<ref>{{cite web |last=Alvarado |first=Silvia |date=8 June 2021 |url=http://portico.mx/2021/06/08/quienes-ganaron-en-los-municipios-de-zacatecas |title=Va por Zacatecas gana más municipios |language=es |website=Pórtico |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2020 Mexican Census, Juchipila recorded a population of 12,251 inhabitants living in 3888 households.<ref name=panorama>{{cite book |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/702825198053.pdf |title=Panorama sociodemográfico de Zacatecas. Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 |pages=60–61 |language=es |publisher=INEGI |year=2021 |accessdate=11 October 2021}}</ref> The 2010 Census recorded a population of 12,284 inhabitants in Juchipila.<ref name=snim/>

There are 30 inhabited localities in the municipality,<ref name=panorama/> of which only the municipal seat, also called Juchipila, is classified as urban.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=32&mun=023 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Juchipila |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[SEDESOL]] |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> It recorded a population of 5836 inhabitants in the 2020 Census.<ref name=panorama/>

Juchipila and its neighbouring municipalities have experienced high rates of emigration to the United States since the second half of the 20th century.<ref>{{cite book |last=Padilla |first=Juan Manuel |url=https://ieez.org.mx/CAP/Doc/TM4.pdf |title=Cambio Demográfico, Desigualdad y Migración en la región Juchipila, Zacatecas |page=36 |language=es |publisher=Instituto Electoral del Estado de Zacatecas |year=2006 |accessdate=11 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last1=Padilla |first1=Juan Manuel |last2=Torres Ramirez |first2=Mireya A. |title=Zacatecan demographics at the turn of the 21st century |journal=Migration and Development |volume=10 |issue=19 |pages=103–125 |url=https://estudiosdeldesarrollo.mx/migracionydesarrollo/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/19ing-5.pdf |year=2012 |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref>

==Culture and education==
[[File:Xochipilli y Mercado 27 de septiembre.jpg|thumb|Statue of Xochipilli in front of the ''Mercado 27 de septiembre'' in the municipal seat of Juchipila.]]

The oldest festival in Juchipila is the Fiesta de Xúchitl, celebrated in June on the eve of the octave of Corpus Christi. The tradition has pre-Hispanic roots and was originally dedicated to [[Xōchipilli]], the god of flowers, music, song and dance, being celebrated near the summer solstice and at the onset of the wet season.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.catalogoculturalzacatecas.mx/fiesta-de-xuchitl/ |title=Fiesta de Xúchitl |language=es |website=Catálogo Cultural de Zacatecas |publisher=Instituto Zacatecano de Cultura Ramón López Velarde |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://sic.gob.mx/ficha.php?table=festividad&table_id=1428 |title=Fiesta del Xuchitl |language=es |website=Sistema de Información Cultural |publisher=[[Secretariat of Culture]] of Mexico |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> It was declared [[intangible cultural heritage]] of the state of Zacatecas in 2015.<ref>{{cite web |date=3 July 2015 |url=https://www.congresozac.gob.mx/p/10959 |title=FIESTA DE LAS FLORES (XÚCHITL) DE JUCHIPILA YA ES PATRIMONIO CULTURAL INMATERIAL |language=es |publisher=[[Congress of Zacatecas]] |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> Another notable festival celebrated in Juchipila is the Festival of the {{ill|Tastoanes|es}}, held sometime around the feast day of [[James the Great|Saint James]] at the end of July.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.catalogoculturalzacatecas.mx/fiesta-de-los-tastoanes/ |title=Fiesta de los Tastoanes |language=es |website=Catálogo Cultural de Zacatecas |publisher=Instituto Zacatecano de Cultura Ramón López Velarde |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref>

The first investigations of the archaeological site of Cerro de Las Ventanas were made by [[Aleš Hrdlička]], who visited the site in 1898 and 1902.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Medrano E. |first1=Angélica María |last2=Hurtado |first2=María Honoria de Jesús |date=February 1996 |title=Análisis de restos arqueológicos de Las Ventanas |language=es |journal=Estudios Jaliscienses |issue=23 |pages=5–20 |url=http://www.estudiosjaliscienses.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/23-An%C3%A1lisis-de-restos-arqueol%C3%B3gicos-de-Las-Ventanas.pdf |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> The site was opened to the public in August 2019,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://inah.gob.mx/boletines/8345-abrira-cerro-de-las-ventanas-la-cuarta-zona-arqueologica-de-zacatecas |title=Abrirá Cerro de Las Ventanas, la cuarta zona arqueológica de Zacatecas |language=es |publisher=INAH |date=1 August 2019 |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> although it was closed during the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico|COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name=inah/>

The Polytechnic University of Southern Zacatecas (''Universidad Politécnica del Sur de Zacatecas'', UPSZ) is located in the village of El Remolino just south of the municipal seat of Juchipila.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://upsz.edu.mx/inicio |title=Universidad Politécnica del Sur de Zacatecas |language=es |publisher=Universidad Politécnica del Sur de Zacatecas |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref>

==Economy and infrastructure==
The main economic activities in Juchipila include commerce, services, agriculture and cattle farming. Important crops grown include corn, ornamental plants, and agave.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref name=plan/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/zacatecas-hopes-for-2000-people/ |title=Zacatecas hopes for 2,000 people to set new mezcal tasting record |date=21 August 2019 |website=Mexico News Daily |access-date=11 October 2021}}</ref> Remittances from the United States also form a large part of the municipality's income.<ref>{{cite book |last=Nevins |first=Joseph |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DlAaAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA132 |title=Dying to Live: A Story of U.S. Immigration in an Age of Global Apartheid |page=132 |publisher=City Lights Publishers |year=2013 |isbn=9780872866416 |accessdate=11 October 2021}}</ref>

[[Mexican Federal Highway 23|Federal Highway 23]] runs north–south through the municipality, connecting it to [[Jerez de García Salinas|Jerez]] and [[Fresnillo]] in the north, and to [[Zapopan]] and [[Guadalajara]] in the south.
