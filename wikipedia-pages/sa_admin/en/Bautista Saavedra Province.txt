'''Bautista Saavedra''' is one of the twenty provinces of the [[Bolivia]]n [[La Paz Department, Bolivia|La Paz Department]]  situated in the northwestern parts of the department. It was created on November 17, 1948 in honor of [[Bautista Saavedra|Bautista Saavedra Mallea]] (1870-1939) who was Bolivia's president from 1920 to 1925. The capital of the province is [[Charazani]].

The region is famous for the [[Kallawaya]] culture with its traditional medicine practices which was declared a [[Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity|Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Patrimony of the Humanity]] by the [[UNESCO]] on November 7, 2003.

== Location ==
Bautista Saavedra Province is located between 14° 45' and 15° 20' [[Latitude|South]] and between 68° 18' and 69° 12' [[Longitude|West]]. It extends over 65&nbsp;km from north to south, and up to 90&nbsp;km from west to east.

The province is situated on the Bolivian [[Altiplano]] northeast of [[Lake Titicaca]] and borders [[Franz Tamayo Province]] in the northeast to northwest, [[Peru]] in the west, [[Eliodoro Camacho Province]] in the southwest, and [[Muñecas Province]] and [[Larecaja Province]] in the southeast. [[Madidi|Madidi National Park]] is partially in this province.

== Population ==
The population of Bautista Saavedra Province has increased by 30% over the recent two decades:
*1992: 9,995 inhabitants ([[census]])
*2001: 11,475 inhabitants ([[census]])
*2005: 12,437 inhabitants (est.)<ref>[http://www.ine.gov.bo/pdf/boletin/np_2005_75.pdf Instituto Nacional de Estadística] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070825045345/http://www.ine.gov.bo/pdf/boletin/np_2005_75.pdf |date=August 25, 2007 }}</ref>
*2010: 12,851 inhabitants (est.)<ref>[http://www.ine.gov.bo/PDF/DIFD/ProyeccionesPoblacionProvinciasMunicipiosLaPaz.pdf Instituto Nacional de Estadística - Proyecciones] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080911211201/http://www.ine.gov.bo/PDF/DIFD/ProyeccionesPoblacionProvinciasMunicipiosLaPaz.pdf |date=September 11, 2008 }}</ref>

41.3% of the population are younger than 15 years old. ''(1992)''

46.6% of the population speak [[Spanish Language|Spanish]], 89.3% speak [[Quechua language|Quechua]], and 36.1% [[Aymara people|Aymara]]. ''(2001)''

The [[Literacy|literacy rate]] of the province's population is 55.2%. ''(1992)''

94.3% of the population have no access to electricity, 93.3% have no sanitary facilities. ''(1992)''

89.1% of the population are Catholics, 5.7% are Protestants. ''(1992)''

== Division ==
The province comprises two [[Municipalities of Bolivia|municipalities]]:
*[[General Juan José Pérez Municipality]] or Charazani Municipality - 9,841 inhabitants (''est. 2005'')
*[[Curva Municipality]] - 2,596 inhabitants (''est. 2005'')
