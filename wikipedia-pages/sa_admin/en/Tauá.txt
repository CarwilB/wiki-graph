'''Tauá''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the [[states of Brazil|state]] of [[Ceará]] in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast region]] of [[Brazil]]. In 2020 it had an estimated population of 59,062 people. It is one of the largest municipalities in the state, with an area of {{convert|4018.188|km2}}.

==History==
The [[toponymy]] of Tauá refers to an indigenous word meaning "yellow clay and loam" in the [[Tupi language]].<ref name="DioceseCrateus">{{cite web|url=http://www.diocesedecrateus.org.br/paroquias_select.php?sel=6|title=Paróquia Nossa Senhora do Rosário - Tauá|publisher=Diocese of Crateus|language=pt|access-date=16 October 2012}}</ref> Its origins date from at least the early 18th century. On December 14, 1801,  Ouvidor Gregório José da Silva went to the small settlement of Tauá to raise the site to a town.<ref name="SantosLeitão2002">{{cite book|last1=Santos|first1=Arnaldo|last2=Leitão|first2=Juarez|title=Verbo cívico: visão histórico-sociológica da Assembléia Legislativa do Ceará|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=LSwPAAAAYAAJ|access-date=12 October 2012|year=2002|publisher=Expert Editora}}</ref> On May 3, 1802, it became a formal settlement.<ref name="Chandler1972">{{cite book|last=Chandler|first=Billy Jaynes|title=Latin American Monographs|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=dqE1AAAAIAAJ|access-date=15 October 2012|year=1972|publisher=University of Florida Press, 1957-c1984.|page=37}}</ref> On August 17, 1832, it was given [[Freguesia (Portugal)|''freguesia'']] status.<ref name="DioceseCrateus" /> On December 2, 1889, the site was renamed São João do Príncipe dos Inhamuns. It received the classification of city on August 2, 1929, returning to its former name.<ref name="Historia">{{cite web|url=http://www.taua.ce.gov.br/pagina/historia-de-taua|title=Historia de Tauá|publisher=Government of Tauá|access-date=12 October 2012|language=pt}}</ref>

==Geography==
The city is located {{convert|337|km}} away from the capital of Ceará State, [[Fortaleza]]. The town lies along [[BR-020]], {{convert|60.8|km}} by road northeast of [[Parambu]]. Districts of the municipality include [[Marrecas]], [[Barra Nova]], [[Trici]], [[Marruás]], [[Carrapateiras]], [[Inhamuns]], and [[Santa Tereza, Ceará|Santa Tereza]]. The [[Jaguaribe River]]'s headwaters are located in the Tauá districts of Trici and Carrapateiras.<ref name="Gaiser2003">{{cite book|last=Gaiser|first=Thomas|title=Global Change and Regional Impacts: Water Availability and Vulnerability of Ecosystems and Society in the Semiarid Northeast of Brazil|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=3SHPT7I_l5gC&pg=PA71|access-date=14 October 2012|year=2003|publisher=Springer|isbn=978-3-540-43824-3|pages=71, 76, 309–}}</ref> At Tauá, the Jaguaribe is sandy and rather narrow, {{convert|50|-|100|m}} in width. The course of the river between Tauá to the mouth of the [[Salgado River (Ceará)|Salgado River]] is approximately {{convert|250|km}}.<ref name="Pamphlets1902">{{cite book|last=Pamphlets|first=Raymond C.|title=Pamphlets on Biology: Kofoid collection|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=1EYXAQAAIAAJ&pg=PA507|access-date=15 October 2012|edition=Public domain|year=1902|publisher=Proceedings of the United States National Museum|pages=507–}}</ref>

The biggest natural landmark is the mountain range, [[Serrote Quinamuiú]]. It became protected by municipal law in 2005. In 2011, the unauthorized extraction of precious stones, such as [[amethyst]], became a matter of concern to environmentalists and the local government as the use of [[dynamite]] caused serious damage to the mountain's physical structure.<ref name="Diario2011">{{cite news|url=http://diariodonordeste.globo.com/materia.asp?codigo=941977|title=Serrote do Quinamuiú em debate|date=March 2, 2011|publisher=Diario do Nordeste|access-date=16 October 2012}}</ref>

==Economy==
According to the government statistics in 1996, the per capita income in Tauá was far below the minimum established income. Carrapateiras suffers from [[overgrazing]] and poor market accessibility.<ref name="Gaiser2003" /> The municipality contains the first solar photovoltaic power plant to generate electricity on a commercial scale in Brazil, [[MPX Tauá]], inaugurated in August 2011. Tauá has three local radio stations, [[Rádio Difusora dos Inhamuns]], [[Rádio Trici FM]], and [[Cultura dos Inhamuns 960]].<ref name="culturaam960">{{cite web|url=http://culturaam960.com.br/|title=Cultura do Inhamuns 960|publisher=RÁDIO CULTURA DOS INHAMUNS |access-date=16 October 2012}}</ref>

==Transportation==
The city is served by [[Tauá Airport|Pedro Teixeira Castelo Airport]].

==Landmarks==
The municipality has three paleontological and archeological sites which can be visited but can only be explored by researchers and professionals with a permit. A donation by Sergeant Major José Rodrigues de Matos enabled the construction of a chapel dedicated to Nossa Senhora do Rosário (Our Lady of the Rosary), located in the municipal cemetery. Igreja de Nossa Senhora do Rosário (Church of Our Lady of the Rosary) was inaugurated on October 17, 1762. Originally constructed with a cylindrical domed roof, it was expanded in 1906. The structure is listed as a protected cultural monument by the Brazilian government. The Igreja de Jesus (Church of Jesus) in the Tauá district of Marrecas was built in the early eighteenth century, around 1717. The first [[parish]] priest was Father Fructuoso Ribeiro Dias.<ref name="DioceseCrateus" />

==Culture==
The feast of [[Jesus]], [[Virgin Mary|Mary]] and [[Saint Joseph|Joseph]] is held every year in April, one of the biggest religious events in the state of Ceará. Our Lady of the Rosary, whose day is celebrated on October 7, is the town's [[patron saint]].<ref name="Historia" />
