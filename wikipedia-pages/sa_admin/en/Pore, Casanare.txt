'''Pore''' is a town and municipality in the [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[Casanare Department|Casanare]], [[Colombia]]. 

Pore, which played a vital role during the [[History of Colombia|independence movement]], was the 18th town to be declared a ''[[Pueblo Patrimonio (Colombia)|Pueblo Patrimonio de Colombia]]'' (Heritage Town of Colombia) on March 1, 2021.<ref>{{cite news |title=Así es Pore, Casanare, el nuevo municipio colombiano catalogado Patrimonio Cultural |url=https://www.infobae.com/america/colombia/2021/03/01/asi-es-pore-casanare-el-nuevo-municipio-colombiano-catalogado-patrimonio-cultural/ |access-date=March 1, 2021 |work=infobae |publisher=Infobae |date=March 1, 2021 |language=es-ES}}</ref> It is the first municipality east of the [[Andes]] to be inducted into the ''Pueblo Patrimonio'' network.<ref>{{Cite web|date=Dec 2020|title=GUÍA: Red Turística de Pueblos Patrimonio de Colombia|url=https://fontur.com.co/sites/default/files/2020-12/GUIA_PUEBLOS_PATRIMONIO.pdf|access-date=29 Mar 2021|website=Fondo Nacional de Turismo de la República de Colombia - FONTUR}}</ref>

'''History'''

Pore was founded on November 5, 1644, by Adriano Vargas and Francisco Enciso. In that same century, the Jesuits managed to organize solid farms that, focused on cattle production, offered the region great economic development, a situation that, together with the commercial relations that it maintained with Venezuela and Tunja, in addition to the particular conditions of the national independence, led to its being assigned the title of capital of the province of Nueva Granada in 1818.

Pore was also the scene where Bolívar's troops met those of Santander to head towards the Boyacá bridge, where victory was achieved in the decisive battle for freedom.

Through Law 936 of December 30, 2004, it was declared a historical and cultural heritage of the nation.3
