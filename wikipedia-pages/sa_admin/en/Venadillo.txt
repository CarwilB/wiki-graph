'''Venadillo''' is a town and municipality in the [[Tolima Department|Tolima]] department of [[Colombia]].  The population of the municipality was 13,266 as of 2024.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=May 2024 |title=Municipio de Venadillo, Tolima |url=https://extension.unibague.edu.co/images/2024/extension/visor/VENADILLO.pdf}}</ref> Venadillo is 56 kilometres northeast of [[Ibagué]].<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last= |title=Municipio de Venadillo |url=https://tolima.gov.co/tolima/informacion-general/turismo/2023-municipio-de-venadillo |access-date= |website=Gobernación del Tolima |language=es}}</ref>

== History ==
The area where Venadillo was founded was historically inhabited by Citirques, Guambaimas, Colombaimas and Tolonies tribes of the [[Panche people|Panche]] [[Panche language|linguistic family]] and the Chocarí tribe of the [[Pijao people|Pijao]]. The name is derived from the local population of deer.<ref name=":2">Estadícas 2011-2014: Venadillo; Gobernación del Tolima, Unidos por la grandiza del Tolima; Secretaría de Planeción y TIC</ref>

Venadillo was founded as a hamlet on 2 September 1560 by Bartolomé de Frías y Carvajal, as a deer husbandry site. The town was expanded in its second foundation on 29 November 1596 through Venadillo's first serving mayor, Juan García de Herrera. Its third and final foundation was in 1710 under Manuel Antonio Maldonado Martínez.<ref name=":0" />

On 21 February 1863, Venadillo became a municipality, which went into effect on 1 January 1864.<ref name=":1" />

In the main park, the statue "Los Venados" commemorates Ponjo, a local tribal chief credited with domesticating the deer, and the namesake animal of Venadillo.<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" />

== Administrative division ==
Venadillo's urban centre has twelve [[barrio]]s. Its rural area is divided into three districts (Junín, Malabar, and Palmarosa), totalling 29 villages.<ref name=":1" />

== Economy ==
Venadillo's economy is primarily agricultural with the widespread farming of rice, corn, sorghum, and cotton.<ref name=":0" /> Venadillo also boasts a small tourist economy as a culinary centre for its distinct oatmeal and [[fritanga]], [[lechona]], [[buñuelo]]s, [[Guarapo (drink)|guarapo]], and whiskey.<ref name=":1" />
