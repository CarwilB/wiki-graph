'''Paraíso''' is a [[Municipalities of Mexico State|municipality]] in [[Tabasco]] in south-eastern [[Mexico]].<ref name="encmuc">{{cite web |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/tabasco/mpios/27014a.htm |title=Paraíso |year=2005 |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México Tabasco |publisher=Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal, Gobierno del Estado de Tabasco |location=Mexico |language=Spanish |accessdate=January 8, 2012 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070519103719/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/tabasco/mpios/27014a.htm |archivedate=May 19, 2007 }}</ref>
The municipal seat is the city of [[Paraíso, Tabasco|Paraíso]].

== Description ==
Paraíso is located in the Grijalva Region of Tabasco, in the subregion of [[Chontalpa]] in the north of the state.<ref name="towndos">{{cite web |url=http://www.puertodosbocas.com.mx/esp/Paraiso-Tabasco.htm |title= Paraíso Tabasco |publisher=Port of Dos Bocas |date=October 7, 2010 |location=Tabasco |language=Spanish |accessdate=January 8, 2012 }}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Centla Municipality|Centla]], Jalpa de Méndez, Comalcalco and [[Cárdenas, Tabasco|Cárdenas]] with the Gulf of Mexico to the north. It covers a territory of 577.55km2, the smallest in the state, and contains one city (the seat), one town, three villages, ten “colonias,” fourteen ejidos and 25 ranches. It also has seven “Rural Development Centers” in Chiltepec, Ejido Oriente, Francisco I. Madero, La Unión 2a Sección, Nicolás Bravo 1a Sección, Puerto Ceiba and Occidente San Francisco.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="towndos"/> The municipal government consists of a municipal president, a treasurer, and ten officials called “regidores.” The town has a population of 20,194. The municipality has 257 people who speak an indigenous language with languages such as Chontal, other Mayan dialects and Zapotec.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The local cuisine is rich in seafood especially oysters, crabs, shrimp, snails, and [[squid]]. Oysters are most common, mostly served pickled or smoked.<ref name="mexdes">{{cite web |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/paraiso-tabasco.-la-tierra-del-cacao.html |author=Giselle Yapur |title= Paraíso, Tabasco. La tierra del cacao |publisher= Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |trans-title=Paraíso, Tabasco: Land of Cacao |language=Spanish |accessdate=January 8, 2012 }}</ref> Other foods include [[iguana]], freshwater turtle, and grilled [[pejelagarto]] fish. Sweets include those made with coconut, pineapple and [[soursop]]. The most traditional drink of the area is [[pozol]], usually made with corn and cacao.<ref name="encmuc"/> 
[[File:Tabasco autopista a Dos Bocas.JPG|250px|thumb|Villahermosa-Comalcalco-Paraíso state highway.]]
The municipality has a number of local publications including newspapers El Casabel, Horizontes Nacionales, Opinión and one magazine called La Edición. Mass media comes from state, national and satellite outlets.<ref name="encmuc"/>

There are 138 schools from preschool to vocational education which include 59 preschools, 57 primary schools, 16 middle schools, three high schools, three vocational schools, two special education centers, and three work training programs. There are also eleven laboratories, thirty one workshops, nine scholastic libraries and twenty three public libraries.<ref name="encmuc"/> Paraíso's illiteracy rate fell to 4.2 percent in 2008 from 5.9% in 2005.<ref name="bandera">{{cite news |title= Izan bandera blanca de alfabetización en Paraíso, Tabasco |newspaper=NOTIMEX |location=Mexico |date=December 15, 2008 |language=Spanish |trans-title=Raise the white flag of literacy in Paraíso, Tabasco }}</ref>

The municipality has 129.6&nbsp;km of major roadway, most of which are state highway. There are eighteen vehicular bridges. The main highways include Federal highway 180, the Villahermosa-Comalcalco-Paraíso state highway and the Villahermosa-Jalpa de Méndez-Paraíso state highway. Seat transportation is mostly concentrated at the Dos Bocas port.<ref name="encmuc"/> There is a mall highway that links the town of Paraíso with the port of Dos Bocas. There used to be a road connection between Paraíso and the port of [[Sánchez Magallanes]], but this road has been washed out in places. A rail line between the Dos Bocas port and [[Villa Chontalpa]] is under construction.<ref name="towndos"/> There is an intercity bus terminal and local public transport consisting of vans, small buses and taxis.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Chiltepec has a population of about 3,700 people, located 18&nbsp;km outside of the municipal seat. Its main economic activities is the growing of coconut, fishing and tourism.<ref name="encmuc"/> It is a fishing port on the mouth of the González River, with the Playa Bruja and Playa Pirata.<ref name="towndos"/> The town has a rustic dock and boardwalk with benches and a lighthouse.<ref name="laregion">{{cite web |url=http://www.laregion.com.mx/tabasco/guia/municipios/paraiso.php |title=La Guia de Paraíso |year=2002 |publisher= |location=La Región newspaper Editorial Cibernética de México |language=Spanish |trans-title=The Paraíso Guide |accessdate=January 8, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111126053002/http://laregion.com.mx/tabasco/guia/municipios/paraiso.php |archive-date=November 26, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Quintín Arauz has a population of about 3,500 located 2.5&nbsp;km outside of the municipal seat. It is dedicated to the growing of coconut, cacao and pepper as well as the raising of pigs and domestic fowl.<ref name="encmuc"/>
Puerto Ceiba has a population of about 2,500 people and is six km from the municipal seat. Its main economic activities are fishing and tourism.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Libertad 1ª. Sección has a population of about 1,800 people located 5.5&nbsp;km outside of the municipal seat. Its main economic activities are the growing of coconut, cacao and pepper along with the raising of pigs and domestic fowl.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Francisco I. Madero has a population of about 2000 people, located 7.5&nbsp;km outside of the municipal seat. Its main economic activities are the growing of coconut, cacao and pepper along with the raising of pigs and domestic fowl.<ref name="encmuc"/>

El Bellote is a small community on the shore of the Mecoacán Lagoon which is dedicated to fishing and tourism.<ref name="towndos"/>
