'''San Andrés''' ({{IPA|es|ˌsan anˈdɾes}}) is the largest city and capital of the Colombian department of [[Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina|San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina]]. Covering a land area of {{cvt|27.586|km2}}, it had a population of 55,426 in 2005. Its economy is driven mainly by tourism, fishing, and agriculture. The island has a [[tropical climate]] with a marine ecosystem consisting of extensive [[coral reefs]] and diverse aquatic population, supported by various protected areas. 

== History ==
San Andres was colonized by the Europeans in the 17th century, and switched hands between various colonial powers for over 200 years. The [[British Empire|English]] and [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]] fought over it for years, which ended in 1786 after the signing of the [[Peace of Paris (1783)]] and [[Convention of London (1786)]], by which England recognized Spain’s sovereignty. In 1822, the islands were occupied by Colombia, and became part of the administration of the central government in 1866. In 1868, it became part of an independent territory of [[San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina]], and was made its capital in 1912. In 1991, it became the capital of the new department established by changes to the [[Colombian Constitution of 1991|Constitution of Colombia]].<ref name="Hist">{{cite web|url=https://www.islandstudies.com/files/2016/11/San-Andres-y-Providencia.pdf|title=San Andres|work=Island Studies|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref>

==Geography==
It is situated at the north end of [[San Andrés (island)|San Andrés Island]] in the [[Caribbean Sea]], and forms part of the Colombian archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina. It is located about {{cvt|710|km}} northwest of mainland Colombia, and {{cvt|180|km}} off the eastern coast of [[Nicaragua]].<ref name="Hist"/> Spread across an area of {{cvt|27.586|km2}}, it has a long coastline of {{cvt|36.12|km}}.<ref name="Isle">{{cite web|url=https://world-islands.net/details/isla-de-san-andres/|title=Isla San Andreas|work=World-Islands|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref> The topography consists of coastal low lands and central plains. The mean elevation is {{cvt|19|m}} with the highest elevation at about {{cvt|61|m}}.<ref name="Isle"/> San Andrés has a tree cover of 66%, with most vegetation composed of [[evergreen forests]].<ref name="Isle"/> It forms part of Colombia’s Seaflower marine conservation reserve, which included expanded protection in 2025 for remote atolls.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5166/|title=Seaflower reserve|work=[[UNESCO]]|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.nationalgeographic.org/colombia-creates-new-marine-reserve-protecting-coral-reefs-and-wildlife-in-the-caribbean-sea/|title=New marine reserve in Seaflower Biosphere|work=[[National Geographic]]|access-date=17 June 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://elpais.com/america-colombia/2025-06-12/colombia-crea-nuevas-zonas-para-proteger-sus-corales-del-caribe.html|title=3,800 km² marine area protected|work=El País|date=12 June 2025|access-date=17 June 2025}}</ref>

===Climate===
The island has a [[tropical monsoon climate]], bordering on a [[tropical savanna climate]], characterized by heavy rainfall, high humidity, and high temperatures. A distinct short dry season is followed by rainfall due to seasonal reversal of prevailing winds.<ref name="Isle"/>

== Demographics and economy ==
San Andreas had a population was 55,426 in 2005, with 80% mainland Colombians and 20% [[Afro-Caribbean]]s of [[Raizal]] descent.<ref name="Census"/> The economy is mainly sustained by [[tourism]] and commercial [[fishing]]. Due to the introduction of the tax free zone, it has led to a massive influx of immigrants both from the mainland Colombia as well as from other areas of the
Caribbean.<ref name="Hist"/> The [[Gustavo Rojas Pinilla International Airport]] serves the city, and there are no major ports in the vicinity.<ref name="Isle"/> Land transportation includes buses, and private vehicles, with small boats used to traverse along the coast.<ref name="Hist"/>
