'''Santa Cruz do Sul''' ({{Audio|Santa_cruz_do_sul.ogg|pronunciation}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the central region of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Sul]], approximately {{convert|155|km|mi}} from [[Porto Alegre]]. According to estimates by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), its population in 2024 was 138,104, making it the 14th most populous municipality in Rio Grande do Sul. Covering an area of {{convert|733.4|km2|mi2}}, it is situated in the Vale do Rio Pardo region, bordering the municipalities of [[Vera Cruz, Rio Grande do Sul|Vera Cruz]], [[Rio Pardo]], [[Sinimbu]], [[Venâncio Aires]], and [[Passo do Sobrado]]. The municipality has a [[temperate climate]], lies in a physiographic transition zone between the [[Brazilian Highlands]] and the [[Central Depression (Rio Grande do Sul)|Central Depression]], and features vegetation from both the [[Atlantic Forest]] and the [[Pampas]], with a predominance of [[Volcanic rock|volcanic rocks]].

Originally established as the Santa Cruz Colony on December 6, 1847, the city was officially founded on March 31, 1877, when it was emancipated from Rio Pardo. A significant hub of [[German colonization in Rio Grande do Sul]], the municipality is bilingual, with residents speaking both [[Portuguese language|Portuguese]] and [[German language|German]], particularly the [[Hunsrückisch dialect]]. Its economy has historically been tied to [[tobacco]], earning it the title of the world’s tobacco capital. The city experienced substantial economic growth, [[verticalization]], and [[rural exodus]] from the 20th century into the early 21st century. In 2018, its [[Gross Domestic Product|gross domestic product]] (GDP) reached 9.4 billion [[Brazilian real|reais]], ranking as the sixth largest in the state, while its [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) in 2010 was 0.733, classified as high.

Predominantly [[Catholic Church|Catholic]] and [[Evangelicalism|Evangelical]], Santa Cruz do Sul is home to the [[St. John the Baptist Cathedral, Santa Cruz do Sul|St. John the Baptist Cathedral]], the largest Gothic-style cathedral in [[South America]], and the Evangelical Lutheran Church, the largest Evangelical temple in Rio Grande do Sul. The city is home to the [[Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|University of Santa Cruz do Sul]], with 11,000 students enrolled in 52 undergraduate programs, alongside three other [[higher education]] institutions, 14 [[secondary education|high schools]], 114 [[primary education|elementary schools]], and three [[hospitals]]. It also has an [[Santa Cruz do Sul Airport|airport]] and a regional [[prison]].

With robust tourism infrastructure, Santa Cruz do Sul is renowned for hosting the largest [[Oktoberfest]] in Rio Grande do Sul, the [[Oktoberfest of Santa Cruz do Sul]], and one of the largest amateur art festivals in [[Latin America]], the Encontro de Arte e Tradição. The city is also home to the Santa Cruz do Sul International Raceway, as well as two professional [[association football|football]] clubs, [[Esporte Clube Avenida]] and [[Futebol Clube Santa Cruz]], and a professional [[basketball]] club, União Corinthians.

== History ==
[[File:Prefeitura Municipal de Santa Cruz do Sul, 1892.jpg|thumb|left|Inauguration ceremony of the City Hall in 1892]]

=== Founding and early years ===
The Santa Cruz Colony was established by provincial law on December 6, 1847,<ref name="PORTO, Aurélio 1934, p.168">PORTO, Aurélio. ''O trabalho alemão no Rio Grande do Sul'', Graf, Santa Terezinha, Porto Alegre, 1934, p.168.</ref> driven by the Rio Pardo Chamber’s desire to facilitate communication with the surrounding fields and promote regional commerce.<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE>{{cite book|title=Enciclopédia dos Municípios Brasileiros - Municípios do Estado do Rio Grande do Sul|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística|year=1959|url=https://servicodados.ibge.gov.br/Download/Download.ashx?http=1&u=biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv27295_34.pdf|pages=167–171|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171212084113/https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv27295_34.pdf|archive-date=12 December 2017}}</ref> The first settlers arrived in 1849<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|102}} and lived in rudimentary huts and shelters.<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE/> The colony was elevated to ''[[freguesia]]'' status on January 8, 1859.<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE/> In 1879, according to a survey by Carlos Trein Filho, 90.54% of the colony’s inhabitants, excluding Brazilians, originated from the [[Kingdom of Prussia]], with 42.53% from [[Pomerania]], 37.88% from the [[Rhineland]], 4.46% from [[Prussia]], 3.57% from [[Silesia]], 1.65% from [[Westphalia]], and 0.14% from [[Brandenburg]]. An additional 8.92% came from other German states, and 0.55% from other European regions.<ref name=Cunha>{{cite thesis|author=Jorge Luiz da Cunha|title=Os Colonos Alemães de Santa Cruz e a Fumicultura Santa Cruz do Sul; Rio Grande do Sul 1849 - 1881|type=Master's dissertation|year=1988|institution=[[Federal University of Paraná]]|location=[[Curitiba]], [[Paraná (state)|Paraná]]|url=http://www.acervodigital.ufpr.br/bitstream/handle/1884/27080/D%20-%20CUNHA%2C%20JORGE%20LUIZ%20DA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170907223838/http://www.acervodigital.ufpr.br/bitstream/handle/1884/27080/D%20-%20CUNHA%2C%20JORGE%20LUIZ%20DA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y|archive-date=7 September 2017}}</ref>{{rp|121}} The lands occupied by the colony were granted by the imperial government through the 1848 immigration incentive law.<ref name=monta>{{cite book|author=MONTALI, Lilia|title=Do núcleo colonial ao capitalismo monopolista: produção de fumo em Santa Cruz do Sul|pages=167|year=1979|publisher=Dissertação de Mestrado em Sociologia – Universidade de São Paulo}}</ref> The colonization aimed to revitalize the economy without merely replacing the former [[slavery|slave labor]].<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|103}} The immigrants settled in Colônia Picada Velha (''Alt Picade''),<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|102}} now known as Linha Santa Cruz.<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE/><ref name="PlanoDiretor"/>{{rp|6}}

In 1849, the area, then called Faxinal de João Faria, on lands owned by Antônio Martins da Cruz Jobim, Baron of Cambaí, was settled by five German families.<ref name="PORTO, Aurélio 1934, p.168"/> The colony’s first administrator was Evaristo Alves de Oliveira, and its first director was Johann Martin Buff, a German immigrant and engineer hailing from Rödelheim, near [[Frankfurt]].<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|99,102,103}} Although most immigrants were [[farmer]]s, many [[artisan]]s also settled in the colony,<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|129}} such as a group of 71 family heads who arrived in 1853, comprising 25 artisans and 46 farmers.<ref name=monta/> Despite challenges in land settlement—initially dense native forest—and financial and political difficulties reported by Oliveira and Buff,<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|104,105}} the colony grew rapidly: in 1849, it had 12 inhabitants; by 1852, 254; in 1853, an additional 692; and by 1859, a total of {{formatnum:2723}} residents.<ref name=monta/>

Initially, the settlers practiced [[polyculture]] for [[subsistence agriculture|subsistence]], cultivating seeds brought from Germany, such as [[potato]]es, [[oat]]s, and [[rye]], alongside local crops such as [[maize]], [[rice]], and [[bean]]s.<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|141-142}} However, the region soon became a hub for [[tobacco]] production.<ref name=monta/> Between 1859 and 1881, tobacco output surged from 14 to {{formatnum:1552}} tons, becoming the main export, with 95% of the harvest shipped to other regions.<ref name=monta/> The city was officially founded on March 31, 1877, gaining autonomy from Rio Pardo under law nº 1079. On September 28, 1878, the Municipal Chamber was established in a building at the corner of São Pedro and Taquarembó streets (now Marechal Floriano and 28 de Setembro).<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|7}} The inaugural session was presided over by councilor Joaquim José de Brito (Lieutenant Colonel Brito), but by the first regular session on October 15, Carlos Trein Filho assumed the presidency.<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|115}} One of the councilors at the first session, Pedro Werlang, distinguished himself in the [[Paraguayan War]], earning medals and honors, including the [[Imperial Order of the Rose]], which conferred upon him the rank of captain in the Brazilian imperial army.<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE/>

=== Expansion and modern times ===
[[File:UNISC06.jpg|thumb|left|Rectorate of UNISC, on the Santa Cruz campus]]
With emancipation, agricultural surpluses, and the presence of artisans and other professionals, the city developed a solid foundation for economic diversification and the emergence of a local middle [[bourgeoisie]]. Some small farmers achieved economic mobility, establishing small commercial and industrial enterprises.<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|156}}<ref name=nor>{{cite web|author=NORONHA, Andrius Estevam|title=Beneméritos empresários: história social de uma elite de origem imigrante do sul do Brasil, Santa Cruz do Sul, 1905-1966)|location=Porto Alegre|year=2012|pages=370|url=http://tede.pucrs.br/tde_busca/arquivo.php?codArquivo=4092|access-date=1 February 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130602174213/http://tede.pucrs.br/tde_busca/arquivo.php?codArquivo=4092|archive-date=2 June 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name=PrusEtAl/>{{rp|102}} In 1904, through mutual cooperation, the community founded the first local financial institution, the ''Caixa de Crédito Santa-Cruzense''.<ref name=nor/> This bank later expanded, becoming the Banco Agrícola Mercantil, which subsequently merged with the ''Banco Moreira Salles'' to form [[Unibanco]].<ref name=garcia>{{cite web|url=http://www.lume.ufrgs.br/bitstream/handle/10183/33270/000114367.pdf?sequence=1|author=GARCIA, Darcy|title=O sistema financeiro do Rio Grande do Sul: da criação da Caixa Econômica Estadual ao surgimento dos bancos múltiplos|publisher=Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Sul|location=Porto Alegre|year=1990}}</ref> In the following decades, urban improvements included the expansion of paved streets, access to electricity in 1906, telephony in 1907, and piped water in 1908.<ref name=gaztabaco23/><ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|80}}

In 1905, the Santa Cruz–Rio Pardo railway (Couto station) was inaugurated, boosting integration with [[Porto Alegre]] and facilitating the movement of goods and people.<ref name=PrusEtAl>{{cite journal|first1=Camila|last1=Prus|first2=Daniela Elisa|last2=Rachor|first3=Milton Roberto|last3=Keller|first4=Doris Maria Machado|last4=Bittencourt|title=As primeiras indústrias, a arquitetura utilitária e o espaço fabril em Santa Cruz do Sul|doi=10.17058/rjp.v6i2.7295|journal=Revista Jovens Pesquisadores|institution=Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|volume=6|issue=2|pages=99–114|year=2016|location=Santa Cruz do Sul|type=Undergraduate thesis|doi-access=free}}</ref>{{rp|102}} The railway station was established that same year by the provincial president, [[Borges de Medeiros]]. However, due to the decline of Brazil’s railway system, the line was discontinued in 1965.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|7}}<ref name=nor/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.iphae.rs.gov.br/Main.php?do=BensTombadosDetalhesAc&item=40600|title=IPHAE|access-date=11 February 2017|website=www.iphae.rs.gov.br}}</ref>

From 1917, with the establishment of ''The Brazilian Tobacco Corporation'', financed by [[British American Tobacco]],<ref name=gaztabaco23>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/o-tabaco-e-a-importancia-para-o-progresso-de-santa-cruz/|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|title=A importância da cultura do tabaco para o progresso de Santa Cruz|date=25 July 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230725190113/https://www.gaz.com.br/o-tabaco-e-a-importancia-para-o-progresso-de-santa-cruz/|archive-date=25 July 2023}}</ref><ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|80}} until 1965, the city experienced significant growth in the tobacco sector,<ref name=PrusEtAl/>{{rp|102}} earning the title of the "world’s tobacco capital."<ref name=EnciclopediaIBGE/><ref name=Fumo>{{cite web|url=http://www.editoragazeta.com.br/revistasantacruz/files/assets/basic-html/page60.html|title=Uma terra para servir de modelo|publisher=Editora Gazeta|access-date=27 May 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170908020656/http://www.editoragazeta.com.br/revistasantacruz/files/assets/basic-html/page60.html|archive-date=8 September 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/cotidian/ff04019918.htm|title=Cidade gaúcha é capital do fumo no Brasil|publisher=Folha de S.Paulo|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230429152417/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/cotidian/ff04019918.htm|archive-date=29 April 2023}}</ref> From 1918, medium and large companies dominated tobacco processing, beginning with the Companhia de Fumo Santa Cruz, a merger of six smaller firms. In 1919, the Anglo-Brazilian [[Souza Cruz]] arrived, followed by the German-Brazilian Tabacos Tatch in 1932, the Brazilian Companhia de Tabacos Sinimbu in 1948, and the French Meridional Tabacos in 1975. These companies expanded their influence, investing in seed enhancement technology, supporting rural producers, and setting production quotas and prices.<ref name=Paim>{{cite thesis|url=http://bibliodigital.unijui.edu.br:8080/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/2726/SANTA%20CRUZ%20DO%20SUL%20TCC%20.pdf?sequence=1|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180114021241/http://bibliodigital.unijui.edu.br:8080/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/2726/SANTA%20CRUZ%20DO%20SUL%20TCC%20.pdf?sequence=1|archive-date=14 January 2018|title=História, identidade e racismo na formação da sociedade santa-cruzense|author=Eduardo Tesche Paim|publisher=Universidade Regional do Noroeste do Estado do Rio Grande do Sul|type=Undergraduate thesis|year=2014}}</ref>{{rp|15}} By 1970, Santa Cruz boasted a robust industrial economy, with tobacco as its flagship.<ref name=Paim/>{{rp|17}}

In 1937, during the [[Vargas Era]] and amid [[World War II]], the federal government launched a nationalist campaign banning the use and teaching of German, closing German-language newspapers and cultural institutions, significantly impacting the region where German remains spoken and taught.<ref name=Paim/>{{rp|16}}<ref name=alemao/><ref name=alemao2/> This led to the closure of the newspaper ''Kolonie'' in 1941, which had operated since 1891. After the war, it resumed publication in Portuguese as ''Gazeta de Santa Cruz'', now known as Gazeta do Sul. Post-war sentiments subdued the city’s Germanic culture, which resurged in the 1970s.<ref name=Paim/>{{rp|17}}

In 1962, the Associação Pró-Ensino em Santa Cruz do Sul (APESC) began offering higher education courses. In 1980, it consolidated four colleges into the Faculdades Integradas de Santa Cruz do Sul (FISC). Construction of the current University of Santa Cruz do Sul (UNISC) campus began in 1982, with the project and accreditations completed in 1993. Today, UNISC operates campuses across several municipalities in [[Rio Grande do Sul]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.unisc.br/pt/home/a-universidade|title=A Universidade|publisher=UNISC|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170628065654/http://www.unisc.br/pt/home/a-universidade|archive-date=28 June 2017}}</ref> contributing to the municipality’s demographic growth.<ref name=Bozzetti/>

From 1970 to 2010, the city underwent significant [[verticalization]], starting in the central area. The relocation of FISC to the Universitário neighborhood and the development of the city’s [[industrial district]] spurred verticalization in other neighborhoods. Between 1970 and 1986, 899 vertical units—apartments, shops, and offices—were built. From 1987 to 1994, this number increased by 135.84%, and from 1995 to 2010, by 64%, totaling {{formatnum:3654}} units.<ref>{{cite journal|url=http://seer.ufrgs.br/index.php/bgg/article/download/40018/25538|title=Verticalização urbana em cidades médias: O caso de Santa Cruz do Sul - RS|author=Gabriel Anibal Santos de Oliveira|journal=Boletim Gaúcho de Geografia|volume=40|issue=2|pages=199–218|year=2013|publisher=Portal de Periódicos da Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Sul}}</ref> During this period, a [[rural exodus]] reduced the rural population from 62% in 1970 to 11% in 2010,<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|75,76}} alongside demographic growth driven partly by labor demand in the tobacco sector. The city also saw irregular occupation of peripheral areas.<ref name=Bozzetti/>

In 2020, a law was passed recognizing the [[German language]] as part of the municipality’s cultural heritage.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.portalarauto.com.br/Pages/184952/aprovada-a-lei-que-oficializa-a-lingua-alema-como-patrimonio-cultural-do-municipio-de-santa-cruz|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201009005057/https://www.portalarauto.com.br/Pages/184952/aprovada-a-lei-que-oficializa-a-lingua-alema-como-patrimonio-cultural-do-municipio-de-santa-cruz|url-status=dead|archive-date=2020-10-09|title=Aprovada a lei que oficializa a língua alemã como patrimônio cultural do município de Santa Cruz|website=Portal Arauto}}</ref>

Today, the city is expanding notably in its northern and northeastern regions, with the emergence of [[gated community|gated communities]], particularly upscale ones,<ref name=Bozzetti>{{cite journal|url=http://online.unisc.br/acadnet/anais/index.php/semic/article/view/11498|title=O Uso Do Geoprocessamento Na Análise Espacial Dos Condomínios E Loteamentos Fechados Em Santa Cruz Do Sul- RS|author=Jonis Bozzetti, Rogerio Leandro Lima Da Silveira|doi=10.17058/rjp.v4i1.4455|journal=Revista Jovens Pesquisadores|volume=4|issue=1|year=2014|pages=89–90|doi-access=free}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2017/06/10/96578-para_onde_santa_cruz_do_sul_avanca.html.php|title=Para onde Santa Cruz do Sul avança|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=10 June 2017}}</ref><ref name=CorreioExpansao/> alongside moderate growth in overall construction.<ref name=CorreioExpansao>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/Cidades/2018/08/660009/Obras-autorizadas-crescem-185-em-Santa-Cruz-do-Sul|title=Obras autorizadas crescem 185% em Santa Cruz do Sul|publisher=Correio do Povo|date=29 August 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180829165438/http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/Cidades/2018/08/660009/Obras-autorizadas-crescem-185-em-Santa-Cruz-do-Sul|archive-date=29 August 2018}}</ref>

The municipality lies in a physiographic transition zone between the Brazilian Highlands and the Central Depression, along the banks of the [[Pardinho River]].<ref name=Menezes>{{cite thesis|title=Zoneamento das áreas de risco de inundação na área urbana de Santa Cruz do Sul - RS|url=http://w3.ufsm.br/ppggeo/images/dissertacoes/dissertacoes_2014/Dissertacao_Daniel_Menezes.pdf|type=Master's dissertation|author=Daniel Junges Menezes|year=2014|location=Santa Maria|publisher=Universidade Federal de Santa Maria|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180304143616/http://w3.ufsm.br/ppggeo/images/dissertacoes/dissertacoes_2014/Dissertacao_Daniel_Menezes.pdf|archive-date=4 March 2018}}</ref>{{rp|20}}<ref name=Ruoso>{{cite thesis|first=Diamar|last=Ruoso|title=O Clima de Santa Cruz do Sul - RS e a percepção climática da população urbana|year=2007|type=Master's dissertation|url=http://cascavel.cpd.ufsm.br/tede/tde_arquivos/23/TDE-2007-10-24T174640Z-926/Publico/DIAMARRUOSO.pdf|institution=[[Universidade Federal de Santa Maria]]|location=[[Santa Maria, Rio Grande do Sul|Santa Maria]], Rio Grande do Sul}}</ref>{{rp|77}} It encompasses the [[Atlantic Forest]] and [[Pampas]] [[Biomes in Brazil|biomes]], with a landscape of valleys, hills, and undulations.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|7,10}} The predominant [[lithology]] consists of volcanic rocks.<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|66}}

The region includes three sub-[[drainage basin|watersheds]]—the Pardo, [[Taquari]]-Antas, and Lower Jacuí, with the Pardo being the most significant, fed primarily by the [[Pardinho River]]. In the Taquari-Antas watershed, the main water source is the Taquari Mirim River. The city also has several micro-watersheds for sewage drainage.<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|62}}<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|16}} Due to its geographic characteristics, parts of the municipality are prone to [[flooding]], with emergency and [[state of emergency|public calamity]] declarations recorded in 1984, 1993, 2005, 2009, 2010, and 2011.<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|89,90,128}}

A bordering area of native forest and environmental preservation, known as the Cinturão Verde (Green Belt), has restricted occupation.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|7,10}} Established under law nº 571 on December 2, 1957, and demarcated by decree nº 4117 on May 26, 1994, it spans 465 hectares. The area supports pollinator species, captures water, absorbs dust, and holds aesthetic value for the city.<ref name=domalberto_cinturao/> A partial survey identified 39 different species in the region.<ref>{{cite journal|url=http://online.unisc.br/acadnet/anais/index.php/semic/article/view/13525|title=Levantamento fitossociológico de um fragmento florestal do Cinturão Verde em Santa Cruz do Sul - RS|author=Carolina de Brito Borges, Jair Putzke|journal=Seminário de Iniciação Científica|publisher=Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|year=2015|pages=72 |issn=2318-8685}}</ref> A 2014 decree tightened environmental licensing for activities in the area,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/noticias/407/decreto-torna-mais-rigoroso-o-licenciamento-para-instalacao-de-atividades-na-area-do-cinturao-verde|title=Decreto torna mais rigoroso o licenciamento para instalação de atividades na área do Cinturão Verde|date=1 August 2014|publisher=Prefeitura de Santa Cruz do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190406170304/http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/noticias/407/decreto-torna-mais-rigoroso-o-licenciamento-para-instalacao-de-atividades-na-area-do-cinturao-verde|archive-date=6 April 2019}}</ref> though real estate development has reduced its size despite existing regulations.<ref name=domalberto_cinturao>{{cite web|url=http://www.domalberto.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/INSTRUMENTOS-FISCALIZADORES-DO-CINTUR%C3%83O-VERDE.pdf|title=Instrumentos fiscalizadores do Cinturão Verde de Santa Cruz do Sul (RS): Uma análise no período de 2010 a 2014|author=Miguel Muller Neto, Josirene Candido Loreno|publisher=Faculdade Dom Alberto|location=Santa Cruz do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190406164924/http://www.domalberto.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/INSTRUMENTOS-FISCALIZADORES-DO-CINTUR%C3%83O-VERDE.pdf|archive-date=6 April 2019}}</ref>

In the city center, particularly along Marechal Floriano Peixoto Street, the "túnel verde" (green tunnel) features a canopy of [[tipuana]] trees. These trees, some over 70 years old, are of different ages. Several of them have problems, and since 2019, some have been cut down or replaced.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/03/02/140943-hora_de_discutir_o_futuro_do_tunel_verde.html.php|title=Hora de discutir o futuro do Túnel Verde|author=Rodrigo Nascimento|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=2 March 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303040253/http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/03/02/140943-hora_de_discutir_o_futuro_do_tunel_verde.html.php|archive-date=3 March 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/03/22/142203-por_que_as_tipuanas_do_tunel_verde_estao_sendo_cortadas.html.php|title=Por que as tipuanas do Túnel Verde estão sendo cortadas|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=22 March 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190406171138/http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/03/22/142203-por_que_as_tipuanas_do_tunel_verde_estao_sendo_cortadas.html.php|archive-date=6 April 2019}}</ref>

=== Climate ===
Santa Cruz do Sul has a [[temperate climate]] (classified as ''Cfa'' under the [[Köppen climate classification]]), with an average annual temperature of {{convert|19.7|°C|°F}}.<ref name="Climatologia_SantaCruzdoSulRS"/> Absolute lows reach {{convert|1|°C|°F}}, and highs hit {{convert|41|°C|°F}}.<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|60}} Annual [[precipitation]] averages {{formatnum:1311}} millimeters (mm), evenly distributed throughout the year, though higher from October to April, with January being the wettest month ({{formatnum:159|mm}}).<ref name="Climatologia_SantaCruzdoSulRS"/> The city’s location results in [[orographic precipitation]].<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|60}} Winds average 1.5 to {{convert|2|m/s|mph}}.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|10}} The region is influenced by the Polar Atlantic, Tropical Atlantic, and Tropical Continental [[air mass]]es.<ref name=Menezes/>{{rp|71}} [[Snow]] is rare, with recorded events on August 18–19, 1965,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/zeborowsky/2017/08/21/101297-neve_em_santa_cruz.html.php|title=Neve em Santa Cruz|author=Gaz|date=21 August 2017|access-date=13 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171213102820/http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/zeborowsky/2017/08/21/101297-neve_em_santa_cruz.html.php|archive-date=13 December 2017}}</ref> and September 4, 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/cotidiano/ult95u125597.shtml|archive-url=https://archive.today/20171213103026/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/cotidiano/ult95u125597.shtml|archive-date=13 December 2017|title=Neve atinge ao menos 28 cidades do Sul do país|author=Folha de S.Paulo|date=4 September 2006|publisher=Folha Online|access-date=13 December 2017|url-status=live}}</ref>

According to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]], Santa Cruz do Sul had a population of {{formatnum:133230}}, making it the 14th most populous municipality in Rio Grande do Sul.<ref name=ibge22>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rs/santa-cruz-do-sul/panorama|title=Santa Cruz do Sul - Panorama|publisher=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]]|year=2022|access-date=9 December 2023}}</ref> An IBGE estimate for 2025 reported a population of 138,270, a  3.78% increase.<ref name="IBGE" /> In 2010, with {{formatnum:118374}} inhabitants, 88% resided in urban areas, and the [[population density]] was 161 inhabitants per square kilometer.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|18}}

In 2010, 86.12% of the population was White, 13.75% were Black or Brown, and the remaining 0.37% were Asian or Indigenous.<ref name=IBGE_TabelaReligiaoRaca/> Unlike its early years, by 2010, 99.76% of the permanent resident population were native-born Brazilians, with only 249 foreigners and 30 naturalized Brazilians.<ref name=IBGE_TabelaNac>{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/Tabela/1497|publisher=IBGE|title=Tabela 1497: População residente, por nacionalidade - Resultados Gerais da Amostra}}</ref>

The municipality’s HDI in 2010 was 0.733,<ref name="ATT_BOT_IDH_0522"/> considered high, while its IDESE (Socioeconomic Development Index) in 2020 was 0.814, also high.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.estado.rs.gov.br/upload/arquivos//lancamento-idese-2020-final.pdf|title=Idese - Principais resultados de 2020|publisher=Governo do Estado do Rio Grande do Sul|page=22|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230125131048/https://www.estado.rs.gov.br/upload/arquivos//lancamento-idese-2020-final.pdf|archive-date=25 January 2023}}</ref> Life expectancy at birth was 76.1 years—up from 69 in 1991—and the infant mortality rate was 11.8 per thousand, down from 21.5 in 1991.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|21}}

Approximately 58% of the adult population had not completed high school, with 4% having incomplete primary education and being illiterate, 38.2% having incomplete primary education but literate, and 16% with incomplete high school education. Meanwhile, 27.6% had completed high school or had some college education, and 14.2% had completed higher education.<ref name=Agenda2020/>

The proportion of the population living in poverty decreased from 16.14% in 1991 to 10.65% in 2000 and 3.68% in 2010. The [[Gini coefficient]] fell from 0.54 in 1991 to 0.53 in 2000 and 0.49 in 2010. Extreme poverty dropped from 3.76% in 1991 to 2.59% in 2000 and 0.96% in 2010. Vulnerability to poverty declined from 41.31% in 1991 to 25.68% in 2000 and 11.76% in 2010.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/pt/perfil_m/santa-cruz-do-sul_rs|title=Perfil - Santa Cruz do Sul, RS|publisher=Atlas Brasil|year=2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190406172210/http://www.atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/pt/perfil_m/santa-cruz-do-sul_rs|archive-date=6 April 2019}}</ref>

== Politics and administration ==
The [[Legislative power|legislative branch]] is exercised by the city council,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/a-camara|title=A Câmara|publisher=Câmara de Vereadores de Santa Cruz do Sul|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20181222092700/http://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/a-camara|archivedate=December 22, 2018}}</ref> while the [[Executive power|executive branch]] is managed by the mayor's office, supported by various secretariats.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/documento/lei-organica-municipal-15446|title=Lei Orgânica Municipal|at=Art. 56|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115185336/http://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/documento/lei-organica-municipal-15446|archivedate=January 15, 2019}}</ref> In 2019, the city had 16 secretariats<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/links/secretarias|title=Secretarias|publisher=Prefeitura de Santa Cruz do Sul|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20181230053037/http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/links/secretarias|archivedate=December 30, 2018}}</ref> and 18 municipal councils.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/secretarias/conselhos-municipais|title=Conselhos Municipais|publisher=Prefeitura de Santa Cruz do Sul|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20181230053818/http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/secretarias/conselhos-municipais|archivedate=December 30, 2018}}</ref>

The city council was established on September 28, 1878, at the corner of what are now Marechal Floriano and 28 de Setembro streets.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|7}} After several relocations, it has been housed since 2016 in a rented building on Fernando Abott Street. From 1878 to 1937, the council had 116 councilors. With the advent of the [[Estado Novo (Brazil)|Estado Novo]] in 1937, municipal councils were closed by [[Getúlio Vargas]]. After Vargas's ousting in 1945, legislative terms, typically lasting four years, resumed. From the first term, which ended in 1947, to the 17th term, concluding in 2020, the council had 275 councilors, totaling 391 since its founding, with some re-elected. The first woman elected as a councilor was Glória Dulce Jacobus in 1964, the youngest councilor was André Luiz Beck in 1988 at age 22, and the councilor with the most votes was Rodrigo Rabuske in 2024, with 4,789 votes.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Interativa|first1=Bravo|last2=Carolina|url=https://portalarauto.com.br/06-10-2024/rodrigo-rabuske-e-o-vereador-mais-votado-da-historia-de-santa-cruz/|title=Rodrigo Rabuske é o vereador mais votado da história de Santa Cruz|date=2024-10-06|accessdate=2025-04-14|website=Portal Arauto}}</ref>

Hermany had resigned as deputy mayor, and the council elected Francisco Carlos Smidt ([[Brazilian Social Democracy Party|PSDB]]) as mayor, a position he held for only ten days until Hermany took office.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/noticia/2020/12/21/apos-morte-de-telmo-kirst-francisco-carlos-smidt-e-eleito-prefeito-de-santa-cruz-do-sul.ghtml|title=Após morte de Telmo Kirst, Francisco Carlos Smidt é eleito prefeito de Santa Cruz do Sul|publisher=G1|date=December 21, 2020}}</ref>

Regarding the Fiscal Responsibility Law (LRF), the municipal administration has consistently achieved results within the defined limits. According to the FIRJAN Fiscal Management Index (IFGF), the city's management is above the national average,<ref name=gazfirjan21>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/estudo-mostra-que-santa-cruz-manteve-boa-situacao-fiscal-mas-investiu-pouco/|title=Estudo mostra que Santa Cruz manteve boa situação fiscal, mas investiu pouco|date=October 24, 2021|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211024131922/https://www.gaz.com.br/estudo-mostra-que-santa-cruz-manteve-boa-situacao-fiscal-mas-investiu-pouco/|archive-date=October 24, 2021}}</ref> having previously been classified as "in difficulty or critical".<ref name=Agenda2020/> However, the investment situation is still considered "critical" by the IFGF, meaning the municipality has not invested as much as it could with its available resources.<ref name=gazfirjan21/> Specifically, the municipality's IFGF scores, ranging from zero (worst) to one (best), were 0.7381 overall, 1.0 in autonomy, 1.0 in personnel expenses, 0.3496 in investments, and 0.6027 in liquidity. In 2020, the index showed the best historical results across all indicators except investments.<ref name=firjan>{{cite web|url=https://www.firjan.com.br/ifgf/consulta-ao-indice/consulta-ao-indice-grafico.htm?UF=RS&IdCidade=431680&Indicador=1&Ano=2020|title=IFGF Santa Cruz do Sul|publisher=Firjan|accessdate=October 24, 2021|archive-date=October 24, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211024132952/https://www.firjan.com.br/ifgf/consulta-ao-indice/consulta-ao-indice-grafico.htm?UF=RS&IdCidade=431680&Indicador=1&Ano=2020|url-status=dead}}</ref> According to the Municipal Governance Index of the Federal Administration Council (IGM/CFA), which evaluates finances, management, and performance, the city received scores of 3.96, 6.19, and 5.54 in 2021, 2022, and 2023, respectively. The 2023 score places the municipality at 127th out of 162 federally among cities with over 100,000 inhabitants, and tenth in the state within this group. A positive component of the score was finances, while a negative one was public safety.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-e-decimo-colocado-no-estado-em-ranking-federal-de-governanca/|title=Santa Cruz é décimo colocado no Estado em ranking federal de governança|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|author=Milena Bender|date=May 30, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230531140533/https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-e-decimo-colocado-no-estado-em-ranking-federal-de-governanca/|archive-date=May 31, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://igm.cfa.org.br/bi/|title=BI - IGM|publisher=Conselho Federal de Administração|year=2023}} <!-- Requires consulting the municipality --></ref>

The municipal government's debt in 2019 was 76.9 million [[Brazilian real|reais]], including court-ordered debt payments. Of this, 43.9 million came from loans, 17.1 million from a debt related to public lighting, 9.3 million from liabilities with the [[Instituto Nacional do Seguro Social|National Social Security Institute]] (INSS), 5.4 million from outstanding payments, and 1.1 million from judicial convictions. The largest creditors were [[Caixa Econômica Federal]], with 34.2 million, RGE, with 17.1 million, the [[Federal government of Brazil|federal government]] with 9.3 million, the Regional Development Bank of the Far South with 6.4 million, and [[Banco do Brasil]] with 3.1 million. Most of the debt was incurred during the municipal administrations of 2005–2008 under José Alberto Wenzel (PSDB) (8.5 million), 2009–2012 under Kelly Moraes ([[Brazilian Labour Party (1981)|PTB]]) (23.1 million), and 2013–2020 under Telmo Kirst ([[Progressive Party (Brazil)|PP]]) (12.1 million), with the public lighting debt dating back to the 1998–2006 administration of Sérgio Moraes (PDT).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/04/21/144221-qual_e_o_tamanho_da_divida_da_prefeitura_de_santa_cruz.html.php|title=Qual é o tamanho da dívida da Prefeitura de Santa Cruz|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=April 21, 2019|author=Pedro Garcia|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20190423014124/http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/04/21/144221-qual_e_o_tamanho_da_divida_da_prefeitura_de_santa_cruz.html.php|archivedate=April 23, 2019}}</ref>

== Subdivisions ==
The municipality is divided into nine districts, while the urban area was divided into 36 neighborhoods in 2010. The districts are Sede Municipal, Boa Vista, Monte Alverne, São Martinho, Saraiva, São José da Reserva, Rio Pardinho, Alto Paredão, and Área Anexada.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>

== Economy ==
[[File:Fumo-montage.jpg|thumb|From top to bottom, factories of [[Universal Leaf Tobacco|Universal]], [[British American Tobacco|BAT]], [[Philip Morris International|Philip Morris]], and [[Japan Tobacco International|JTI]], in the Industrial District of Santa Cruz do Sul. The latter three are among the five largest tobacco companies in the world, while Philip Morris accounted for more than half of the municipality's revenue.]]
[[File:Industria-montage.jpg|thumb|From top to bottom, factories of Imply, Mercur, and Mor, part of Santa Cruz's non-tobacco industry.]]
The main tobacco industries in Brazil are present in Santa Cruz do Sul. Among those established in the city are Souza Cruz, the market share leader in the country,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/negocios/british-american-tobacco-registra-oferta-para-fechar-capital-da-souza-cruz-15485135|title=British American Tobacco registra oferta para fechar capital da Souza Cruz|work=O Globo |date=March 3, 2015|accessdate=March 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230430214744/http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/negocios/british-american-tobacco-registra-oferta-para-fechar-capital-da-souza-cruz-15485135|archive-date=April 30, 2023}}</ref> and [[Philip Morris International|Philip Morris]], which in 2015 accounted for 54% of the municipality's Tax on Circulation of Goods and Services (ICMS) revenue,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jcrs.uol.com.br/site/noticia.php?codn=120778|publisher=Jornal do Comércio|title=Philip Morris inaugura nova fábrica em Santa Cruz do Sul|date=April 5, 2013|first=Clarisse|last=de Freitas|accessdate=March 23, 2015}}</ref> among others. In 2013 and 2023, the municipality exported approximately 1.5 billion dollars in goods, with tobacco-related products being the top thirteen items.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-volta-a-superar-us-15-bilhao-em-vendas-para-o-exterior/|title=Santa Cruz volta a superar US$ 1,5 bilhão em vendas para o exterior|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=January 23, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240123165515/https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-volta-a-superar-us-15-bilhao-em-vendas-para-o-exterior/|archive-date=January 23, 2024}}</ref>

The presence of these companies is supported by rural producers in Santa Cruz do Sul and neighboring cities such as Venâncio Aires, Vera Cruz, and Rio Pardo, for whom tobacco cultivation for processing is the primary source of income. Approximately 6,600 hectares of the municipality's area are dedicated to tobacco, yielding an annual production of 14,700 tons in the 2012/2013 harvest. Four thousand families in Santa Cruz are engaged in this activity.<ref>{{cite journal|publisher=Editora Gazeta|location=Santa Cruz do Sul|url=http://www.grupogaz.com.br/tratadas/flip/editora/revista_santa_cruz_2013/|year=2013|journal=Revista Santa Cruz|title=Lavoura dourada|accessdate=March 23, 2015|pages=44–45|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150404144251/http://www.grupogaz.com.br/tratadas/flip/editora/revista_santa_cruz_2013/|archivedate=April 4, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The tobacco industry has a strong presence in the region's socioeconomic life, providing technical, financial, and social program support to tobacco farmers. This creates a system of exchanges and loyalties between most farmers and the industry, particularly through the Association of Brazilian Tobacco Farmers.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|33}}<ref name="AfubraVerdeVida">{{cite web|url=http://www.afubra.com.br/verde-e-vida.html|title=Verde é vida|publisher=Afubra|quote=The work is carried out through lectures, research, experiments, environmental awareness programs (PSA) and socio-environmental action programs (PASA), collection of used oil; seed bank, environmental groups, scientific works, distribution of seedlings, and pedagogical didactic material|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20161015005418/http://www.afubra.com.br/verde-e-vida.html|archivedate=October 15, 2016}}</ref> In addition to tobacco, notable crops include [[maize]], [[rice]], [[cassava]], [[soybean]], [[bean]], [[olive oil]], [[horticulture]], [[floriculture]], [[sugarcane]], [[sweet potato]], [[potato]], and [[grape]]. Livestock farming is also present. In total, there are 4,365 rural properties with an average area of {{convert|12.7|ha}}.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|17}}

The city also has strong sectors in its economy, such as [[commerce]] and [[Tertiary sector of the economy|services]]. As a result, the commercial sector is currently represented by approximately 3,277 establishments and over 2,793 service-providing companies. In total, the municipality has 533 industries and 3,914 self-employed professionals. The largest companies in the municipality by added value in 2013 were Phillip Morris Brasil, Souza Cruz, [[Universal Leaf Tobacco]], [[Japan Tobacco International]], Metalúrgica Mor, Associated Tobacco do Brasil, Mercur, Excelsior Alimentos, Premium Tobacos do Brasil, and Xalingo.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|17}} Additionally, Imply, a technology company, was listed in 2018 among the 5,000 largest exporters in Brazil out of 1 million companies.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/04/12/143635-empresa_de_santa_cruz_esta_entre_as_maiores_exportadoras_do_brasil.html.php|title=Empresa de Santa Cruz está entre as maiores exportadoras do Brasil|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=April 12, 2019|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20190412232053/http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/04/12/143635-empresa_de_santa_cruz_esta_entre_as_maiores_exportadoras_do_brasil.html.php|archivedate=April 12, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://exame.abril.com.br/blog/esporte-executivo/imply-cresce-com-foco-em-tecnologia-para-o-esporte/|title=Imply cresce com foco em tecnologia para o Esporte|publisher=Exame|author=Vinicius Lordello|date=April 11, 2019}}</ref> Finally, other local enterprises that stood out in the past include Cia. de Fumos Santa Cruz, Máquinas Binz, and Máquinas Schreiner.<ref name=gaztabaco23/>

The region's GDP in 2013 was 6.67 billion [[Brazilian real|reais]], the eighth largest in the state, contributing 2% to its economy. The municipality's per capita GDP was 53,500 reais, compared to 29,657 reais for the state and 26,445 reais for the country.<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|32}} By 2017, the GDP reached 8.23 billion reais, with a per capita of 64,600 reais,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rs/santa-cruz-do-sul/pesquisa/38/46996|title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios - 2016|author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)|date=2017|accessdate=October 12, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190401220846/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rs/santa-cruz-do-sul/pesquisa/38/46996|archive-date=April 1, 2019}}</ref> and in 2018, it was 10.5 billion reais, with a per capita of 79,900 reais,<ref name="ATT_BOT_PIB_0522"/> making it the fifth largest economy in the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-volta-a-quinta-colocacao-no-ranking-do-pib-gaucho/|title=Santa Cruz volta à quinta colocação no ranking do PIB gaúcho|author=Pedro Garcia|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=December 16, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230408132832/https://www.gaz.com.br/santa-cruz-volta-a-quinta-colocacao-no-ranking-do-pib-gaucho/|archive-date=April 8, 2023}}</ref> The average income of the municipality's residents increased from 554.13 reais in 1991 to 1,036.87 reais in 2010,<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|18}} and 2,130.66 reais in 2020, compared to the national average of 1,310 reais in the same year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/mapa-da-riqueza-coloca-renda-de-santa-cruz-como-a-71a-maior-no-brasil/|title=Mapa da Riqueza coloca renda de Santa Cruz como a 71ª maior no Brasil|author=Otto Tesche|date=February 24, 2023|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230315152600/https://www.gaz.com.br/mapa-da-riqueza-coloca-renda-de-santa-cruz-como-a-71a-maior-no-brasil/|archive-date=March 15, 2023}}</ref> Extreme poverty decreased from 3.76% to 0.96% between 1991 and 2010. The main occupations of workers are in manufacturing, commerce, agriculture and livestock, construction, public service, transportation, education, private services, and other liberal professions and service providers.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|18}} Despite being an inland city, it ranked twentieth in a 2018 list of the best cities in Brazil for doing business.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2018/10/31/133316-santa_cruz_esta_entre_as_20_melhores_do_brasil_para_fazer_negocios.html.php|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=October 31, 2018|title=Santa Cruz está entre as 20 melhores do Brasil para fazer negócios|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20181101143151/http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2018/10/31/133316-santa_cruz_esta_entre_as_20_melhores_do_brasil_para_fazer_negocios.html.php|archivedate=November 1, 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://exame.abril.com.br/revista-exame/nao-da-para-se-acomodar/|title=As 100 melhores cidades do Brasil para investir em negócios|publisher=Exame|author=Fabiane Stefano and Suzana Liskauskas|date=October 25, 2018}}</ref>

=== Tourism ===
[[File:Parque da Gruta, Santa Cruz do Sul.jpg|thumb|right|The grotto at Adventure Park, a [[paleoburrow]] of the [[megafauna]] excavated over ten thousand years ago.<ref name=Gruta/>]]
The main event in Santa Cruz do Sul is the [[Oktoberfest of Santa Cruz do Sul]], a popular German festival held annually in October. The Oktoberfest of Santa Cruz do Sul is the second largest in Brazil, surpassed only by the one in [[Blumenau Oktoberfest|Blumenau]],<ref>{{cite web|publisher=Zero Hora|title=Um guia para as Oktoberfests no sul do país|date=October 14, 2014|accessdate=April 11, 2017|url=http://zh.clicrbs.com.br/rs/vida-e-estilo/viagem/noticia/2014/10/um-guia-para-as-oktoberfests-no-sul-do-pais-4620016.html}}</ref> and the largest in Rio Grande do Sul.<ref name=OktoberZH>{{cite web|url=http://zh.clicrbs.com.br/rs/vida-e-estilo/viagem/noticia/2015/10/outubro-e-mes-de-oktoberfest-saiba-quais-sao-e-escolha-a-sua-4862594.html|title=Outubro é mês de Oktoberfest: saiba quais são e escolha a sua|date=October 6, 2015|accessdate=January 21, 2017|publisher=Zero Hora}}</ref> Promoted locally as the "festival of joy,"<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/noticia/2014/10/shows-marcam-1-fim-de-semana-da-oktoberfest-de-santa-cruz-do-sul-rs.html|title=Shows marcam 1º fim de semana da Oktoberfest de Santa Cruz do Sul, RS|publisher=RBS TV / G1|date=October 11, 2014|accessdate=April 11, 2017}}</ref> its 2016 edition attracted approximately 150,000 paying visitors.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/oktoberfest/2016/10/16/82379-publico_da_32_oktoberfest_e_20_superior_ao_do_ano_passado.html.php|accessdate=January 30, 2017|title=Público da 32ª Oktoberfest é 20% superior ao do ano passado|author=Four Comunicação|publisher=Portal Gaz|date=October 16, 2016|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20161019011409/http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/oktoberfest/2016/10/16/82379-publico_da_32_oktoberfest_e_20_superior_ao_do_ano_passado.html.php|archivedate=October 19, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

The city also hosts the Art and Tradition Encounter, an event celebrating [[Gaucho culture|Gaucho traditions]], typically held in November,<ref name=PlanoDiretor/>{{rp|34}}<ref name=ENART_SCS>{{cite book|publisher=Revista Santa Cruz|date=2015|accessdate=April 11, 2017|title=Chimarrão, música e dança|url=http://www.editoragazeta.com.br/revistasantacruz/files/assets/basic-html/index.html#49|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170412143311/http://www.editoragazeta.com.br/revistasantacruz/files/assets/basic-html/index.html#49|archivedate=April 12, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> and is one of the largest of its kind in Latin America.<ref name=ENART_LA>{{cite journal|title=De corpo e alma na tradição|pages=14–15|journal=[[Zero Hora]]|last=Carapeços|first=Nathália|volume=53|issue=18626|date=November 19, 2016|accessdate=January 31, 2017|url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default2.jsp?uf=1&local=1&source=a8373371.xml&template=3898.dwt&edition=30122&section=4572|format=requires payment}}</ref> The 2016 edition featured six thousand participating artists.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/noticia/2016/11/ctg-tiarayu-e-o-grande-campeao-do-enart-2016-veja-todos-os-vencedores.html|title=CTG Tiarayú é o grande campeão do Enart 2016; veja mais vencedores|publisher=RBS TV / G1|date=November 20, 2016|accessdate=April 11, 2017}}</ref>

The city has robust infrastructure for events, earning it the Priority Seal for Tourism Development. It has sixteen hotels and five motels, offering a total of 1,900 beds. Over thirty restaurants, several colonial cafés, more than eight pizzerias, and numerous bars provide the community and tourists with a diverse culinary scene.<ref name=PlanoDiretor>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/epd/Fundamentacao_Diagnostico_naoconsolidado.pdf|title=Revisão do Plano Diretor de Desenvolvimento Social e Urbano|publisher=Secretaria Municipal de Planejamento e Gestão|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170907195653/http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/epd/Fundamentacao_Diagnostico_naoconsolidado.pdf|archivedate=September 7, 2017}}</ref>{{rp|34}}

== Infrastructure ==
The city has 40,540 registered households, of which 91.37% have water supply, 90.87% have basic sanitation, 99.76% have electricity, and 98.28% have garbage collection. Water supply and sewage treatment are managed by Companhia Riograndense de Saneamento (CORSAN), while electricity is provided by RGE Sul (formerly AES Sul). The city has public housing projects in nine neighborhoods, receiving funds from the [[Growth Acceleration Program]] (PAC). === Education ===
[[File:Faculdade Dom Alberto.jpg|thumb|Facade of Dom Alberto College in 2019, with the cathedral to the right]]
In 2014, the city had 114 primary schools with 30,925 enrolled students. Of these, 5% studied in rural areas, and the rest in urban areas. 19.6% attended private schools, while the remainder were in municipal or state schools. The illiteracy rate among the population over fifteen years old was 8.42% in 2010.<ref name=PlanoEducacao>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/download2014/smec_pme/Doc%20Base%20FINAL%20-%2024%20abril%202015.pdf|title=Plano Municipal de Educação|work=Prefeitura de Santa Cruz do Sul|year=2015|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20170909025325/http://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/download2014/smec_pme/Doc+Base+FINAL+-+24+abril+2015.pdf|archivedate=September 9, 2017|accessdate=September 9, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref>{{rp|25,26}} Additionally, 300 students are served in special education across all levels.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|48}}

In 2013, fourteen schools offered high school classes, with 3,806 students enrolled in one of the three years. Of these, four were private, including Colégio Mauá, part of the Synodal Education Network, founded on July 27, 1870, by the local community,<ref name=Paim/>{{rp|15}}<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.maua.g12.br/maua/historico|title=Histórico|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20171218224757/http://www.maua.g12.br/maua/historico|archivedate=December 18, 2017|publisher=Colégio Mauá}}</ref> Colégio Marista São Luís, founded in 1874<ref name=Paim/>{{rp|15}} and part of the Marista Network,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/biblioteca-catalogo.html?id=449330&view=detalhes|title=Colégio Marista São Luís : Santa Cruz do Sul, RS|publisher=IBGE}}</ref> and Colégio Educar-se, founded in 1984 by the institution that now manages UNISC.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://revista.uniamerica.br/index.php/pleiade/article/download/65/56|title=Escola Educar-se|author=Sonia Maria Dettenborn Luz|publisher=Revista Pleiade|volume=1|number=2|pages=119–131|year=2007}}</ref> The other institutions are state-run, with only one located in a rural area.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|43}} Among the state schools in the urban area are Colégio Goiás, founded in 1902,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2017/12/14/109434-goias_retoma_o_primeiro_lugar_entre_as_escolas_publicas_de_santa_cruz.html.php|title=Goiás retoma o primeiro lugar entre as escolas públicas de Santa Cruz|date=December 14, 2017|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|author=Heloísa Corrêa}}</ref> and Ernesto Alves State School.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.educacao.rs.gov.br/dados/cadastro_est_2011.pdf|title=Cadastro dos Estabelecimentos de Ensino - Rede ESTADUAL - RS|publisher=Governo do Estado do Rio Grande do Sul|year=2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211016105247/http://www.educacao.rs.gov.br/dados/cadastro_est_2011.pdf|archive-date=October 16, 2021}}</ref> Vocational high school courses are also offered at UNISC, with 211 students enrolled in 2014, at [[SENAI]], with 183 students in 2013, at the Santa Cruz Agricultural Family School, with 200 students in 2013, at Ideal School, with nine students in 2013, and at Colégio Marista, with 48 students in 2013.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|49}}

Higher education in the city is provided by the [[Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|University of Santa Cruz do Sul]], with eleven thousand students enrolled in 52 undergraduate programs in 2014, Dom Alberto College, named after Alberto Frederico Etges and located in the former Coração de Jesus College building,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.domalberto.edu.br/o-colegio/|title=O Colégio|author=Dom Alberto|work=Dom Alberto |accessdate=March 3, 2019|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180303081431/https://www.domalberto.edu.br/o-colegio/|archivedate=March 3, 2018}}</ref> with 2,500 students enrolled in three undergraduate programs, a branch of the [[Rio Grande do Sul State University]] (UERGS), with 36 students enrolled in two undergraduate programs, and a center of the [[Centro Universitário Ritter dos Reis|Ritter dos Reis University Center]] (UniRitter), with 361 students enrolled in undergraduate programs in 2013.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|45}}

In terms of the goals set by the [[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|Ministry of Education]] (MEC), the municipality performs well in the early grades but falls below the target in the final grades.<ref name=Agenda2020/>

=== Transportation ===

=== Security ===
The city's first prison was located in its central neighborhood—opened in 1910, it had wooden floors and ceilings and windows facing the sidewalk. To move inmates away from the center and enhance security, the municipality donated land to the state government in what is now the Faxinal Menino Deus neighborhood, then sparsely populated, for the construction of a new jail. Opened in 1977 with the transfer of prisoners from the center, the Santa Cruz Regional Prison has iron cells and masonry infrastructure,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/no-carnaval-de-1977-presos-de-santa-cruz-foram-transferidos-para-a-nova-cadeia/|title=No Carnaval de 1977, presos de Santa Cruz foram transferidos para a nova cadeia|publisher=Gazeta do Sul|date=February 20, 2023|author=Jose Augusto Borowsky|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230222044912/https://www.gaz.com.br/no-carnaval-de-1977-presos-de-santa-cruz-foram-transferidos-para-a-nova-cadeia/|archive-date=February 22, 2023}}</ref> with an official capacity of 168 inmates in 2017, but housing 261. == Culture ==
[[File:Bierwagen Oktoberfest Santa Cruz do Sul, 2013.jpeg|thumb|right|The ''bierwagen'' (beer wagon) at the Oktoberfest in 2013]]
[[File:ENART_2023.jpg|thumb|right|Traditional Gaucho dance performance at ENART 2023, held at the Arnão Sports Complex]]

The main languages spoken in the city are Portuguese, the official language of Brazil and used by the majority of the population, and German,<ref name=alemao2>{{cite journal|journal=Gazeta do Sul|first=Roseane|last=Bianca|title=Idioma é diferencial na hora da contratação|date=15 December 2012|access-date=23 March 2015|url=http://www.grupogaz.com.br/gazetadosul/noticia/383324-idioma_e_diferencial_na_hora_da_contratacao/edicao:2012-12-15.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304030617/http://www.grupogaz.com.br/gazetadosul/noticia/383324-idioma_e_diferencial_na_hora_da_contratacao/edicao:2012-12-15.html|archive-date=4 March 2016}}</ref> including dialects such as [[Hunsrückisch]],<ref name="alemao">{{cite web|url=http://www.topicos.net/fileadmin/pdf/2007/4/hunsrueck.pdf|title=Dialeto hunsrückisch do sul do Brasil ganhará atlas lingüístico|author=Alexandre Schossler|website=Landeskunde: Conhecendo o Brasil|date=April 2007|access-date=7 January 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428234420/http://www.topicos.net/fileadmin/pdf/2007/4/hunsrueck.pdf|archive-date=2014-04-28|url-status=dead}}</ref> which was predominant among the colony's earliest settlers.<ref name=Cunha/>{{rp|125}}

Among the city's cultural hubs is the Francisco José Frantz Cultural Center, located in the former municipal railway station, which hosts a variety of exhibitions.<ref name=TerraSCS/> In the city center, the Regina Simonis House of Arts hosts art exhibitions organized by the Santa Cruz do Sul Pró-Cultura Association, established in 1988 by volunteers. The association has occupied the current building since 1994 under a [[commodate]] agreement with the state government, the property's owner. Constructed in 1922 to house the Banco Pelotense, the [[Eclecticism in architecture|eclectic-style]] building spans 823 square meters in the city center and has also served as the [[Banrisul|Bank of the State of Rio Grande do Sul]] (from 1932 to 1982) and the State Tax Office (from 1983 to 1994). It is a designated heritage site. The architectural design, of unknown origin, is often attributed to the German engineer and architect [[Theo Wiederspahn]]. The facade features statues of figures from Roman mythology, centered around [[Mercury (mythology)|Mercury]]. The building's current name honors the artist Regina Simonis, born in 1900, the first Santa Cruz native to enroll in the Institute of Fine Arts of Rio Grande do Sul.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/um-monumento-de-rara-beleza-faz-100-anos/|title=Um monumento de rara beleza faz 100 anos|website=Gazeta do Sul|author=Paola Severo|date=13 March 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220314010708/https://www.gaz.com.br/um-monumento-de-rara-beleza-faz-100-anos/|archive-date=14 March 2022}}</ref> Other cultural venues include the Auditorium of the Municipal Secretariat of Education and Culture and the Camarin Space Theater in the Mauá School auditorium. Private cultural establishments include the Mauá School Theater, with 711 seats, the auditoriums of Dom Alberto and São Luís schools, and those at UNISC. The Oktoberfest Park pavilions host various events, and the Santa Cruz Park features an amphitheater with 800 seats.<ref name=PlanoEducacao/>{{rp|22,23}}

The city is also home to a public library and several private libraries open to the public, as well as a private museum with public access. The municipal public library had a collection of 21,000 works in 2012.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.riovalejornal.com.br/materias/4347-um_acervo_de_cultura_pra_voce|title=Um acervo de cultura pra and one of the most significant in the state's interior,<ref name=UniscBib>{{cite 2017,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.unisc.br/pt/servicosonline/biblioteca/estatisticas/acervo|title=Acervo|website=Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170718034124/http://www.unisc.br/pt/servicosonline/biblioteca/estatisticas/acervo|archive-date=18 July 2017}}</ref> along with periodicals, regional newspaper archives, multimedia collections, and materials in [[Braille]].<ref>{{cite The city is the home of the UNISC Chamber Orchestra, founded in 2005 initially as a school orchestra.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.unisc.br/pt/cultura/orquestra-de-camara-da-unisc/apresentacao|title=Cultura - Orquestra de Câmara da Unisc - Apresentação|website=Universidade de Santa Cruz do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181231174217/https://www.unisc.br/pt/cultura/orquestra-de-camara-da-unisc/apresentacao|archive-date=31 December 2018}}</ref> Since then, the group has performed in the city<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2019/03/19/141956-orquestra_da_unisc_abre_temporada_de_concertos_em_santa_cruz.html.php|title=Orquestra da Unisc abre temporada de concertos em Santa Cruz|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=19 March 2019}}</ref> and throughout the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.correiodopovo.com.br/arteagenda/orquestra-de-c%C3%A2mara-unisc-estreia-no-theatro-s%C3%A3o-pedro-nesta-quarta-1.248061|title=Orquestra de Câmara Unisc estreia no Theatro São Pedro nesta quarta|website=Correio do Povo|date=28 November 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190320004637/https://www.correiodopovo.com.br/arteagenda/orquestra-de-c%C3%A2mara-unisc-estreia-no-theatro-s%C3%A3o-pedro-nesta-quarta-1.248061|archive-date=20 March 2019}}</ref>

Since 2018, the city has annually hosted the Santa Cruz Film Festival, an event that showcases and awards contemporary regional and national film productions. The festival is organized by [[Serviço Social do Comércio|Sesc/RS]] – Santa Cruz do Sul Unit, UNISC – Communication Studies Programs, and Pé de Coelho Filmes.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://festivalsantacruzdecinema.com.br/sobre/ |title=Sobre o Festival Santa Cruz de Cinema|access-date=2022-09-17 |website=Festival Santa Cruz de Cinema}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.sesc-rs.com.br/noticias/inscricoes-para-o-5o-festival-santa-cruz-de-cinema-estao-abertas-ate-30-de-junho/ |title=SInscrições para o 5º Festival Santa Cruz de Cinema estão abertas|access-date=2022-09-17 |website=SESC |date=20 May 2022 }}</ref> Aimed at fostering film production and exhibition in the city, the festival brings Brazilian [[short films]] to Santa Cruz do Sul, aspiring to serve as a platform for the dissemination, premiere, and promotion of works from across the country. In addition to screenings, the festival offers workshops on audiovisual production, discussions, and tributes.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.gaz.com.br/5o-festival-santa-cruz-de-cinema-e-lancado-nesta-quinta-feira/ |title=5º Festival Santa Cruz de Cinema é lançado nesta quinta-feira|access-date=2022-09-17 |website=Gaz |date=12 May 2022 }}</ref>

== Sports ==
[[File:Poliesportivo_Arnao_BRA_EUA_26_02_23_wide.jpg|thumb|right|Arnão Sports Complex hosting a match between the [[Brazil men's national basketball team|Brazilian]] and [[United States men's national basketball team|American]] teams during the [[FIBA Basketball World Cup]] qualifiers in 2023]]
The Mayor Arno João Frantz Sports Complex, officially nicknamed Arnão in 2021 after being informally known as such, is one of the city's main [[Sports complex|sports complexes]]. Located in the Oktoberfest Park, it was inaugurated in 1992 by then-mayor Arno Frantz, after whom it is named.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/aprovado-projeto-que-batiza-o-poliesportivo-de-arnao/|title=Aprovado projeto que batiza o Poliesportivo de 'Arnão'|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=18 February 2021|author=Pedro Garcia}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/documento/projeto-de-lei-do-legislativo-no-58-l-2019-41830|title=Projeto de Lei do Legislativo Nº 58/L/2019|website=Câmara Municipal de Santa Cruz do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230227220724/https://www.camarasantacruz.rs.gov.br/documento/projeto-de-lei-do-legislativo-no-58-l-2019-41830|archive-date=27 February 2023}}</ref> Another sports complex is located at UNISC.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.unisc.br/es/infraestrutura-edu-fisica|title=Infraestrutura - Complexo Esportivo (Ginásio Poliesportivo, Piscina Térmica e Áreas Externas)|website=UNISC|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230227220531/https://www.unisc.br/es/infraestrutura-edu-fisica|archive-date=27 February 2023}}</ref> The city also hosts a [[tennis]] club<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/esportes/2016/09/22/80913-ajusc_e_tenis_clube_firmam_parceria_em_santa_cruz.html.php|title=Ajusc e Tênis Clube firmam parceria em Santa Cruz|date=September 22, 2016|website=Gazeta do Sul}}</ref> and a [[golf]] course.<ref name=TerraSCS/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/municipio/country-club|title=Country Club|date=23 August 2017|website=Prefeitura de Santa Cruz do Sul}}</ref>

Two professional [[Association football|football]] clubs are based in the city: [[Esporte Clube Avenida]] and [[Futebol Clube Santa Cruz]]. Both have stadiums in the city, namely the Estádio dos Eucaliptos, built in 1950 with a capacity of 3,000 spectators,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/municipio/estadio-dos-eucaliptos|title=Estádio dos Eucaliptos|website=Prefeitura Municipal|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190528064028/http://www.santacruz.rs.gov.br/municipio/estadio-dos-eucaliptos|archive-date=28 May 2019}}</ref> and the Estádio dos Plátanos. The first professional [[basketball]] club established in Santa Cruz was Sociedade Ginástica, founded in 1931. Currently, the Basquetebol do Esporte Clube União Corinthians, established in 1939, is the city's leading professional basketball club, with both men's and women's teams.<ref name=basquete>{{cite web |last=Weis |first=Gilmar |url=https://repositorio.unisc.br/jspui/bitstream/11624/291/1/GilmarFernandoWeis.pdf |title=O basquetebol em Santa Cruz do Sul |date=25 April 1996 |access-date=24 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230227222449/https://repositorio.unisc.br/jspui/bitstream/11624/291/1/GilmarFernandoWeis.pdf|archive-date=27 February 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/uniao-corinthians-e-o-campeao-gaucho-de-basquete/|title=União Corinthians é o campeão gaúcho de basquete|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=14 December 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210914143034/https://www.gaz.com.br/uniao-corinthians-e-o-campeao-gaucho-de-basquete/|archive-date=14 September 2021}}</ref>

Notable athletes from the city include gymnast [[Natália Scherer]], who represented Brazil in the [[rhythmic gymnastics]] finals at the [[2000 Summer Olympics]] in Sydney,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/esporte_local/2016/07/16/76657-talento_daqui_a_coreografia_da_vida_de_natalia.html.php|title=Talento daqui: A coreografia da vida de Natália|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=July 16, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/olimpiada2000/emcimadahora/outrosesportes/ult315u827.shtml|title=Brasileiras terminam em último na final da ginástica rítmica|website=[[Folha de S.Paulo]]|date=September 30, 2000}}</ref> golfer [[Adilson da Silva]], who competed for Brazil at the [[2015 Pan American Games]] in Toronto,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2016/08/04/77865-atleta_olimpico_e_cidadao_honorario_de_santa_cruz_do_sul.html.php|title=Atleta olímpico é Cidadão Honorário de Santa Cruz do Sul|date=April 8, 2016|website=Gazeta do Sul}}</ref> and other local residents and natives,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/esportes/2017/10/05/104506-padelistas_de_santa_cruz_do_sul_rumo_ao_mundial.html.php|title=Padelistas de Santa Cruz do Sul rumo ao Mundial|date=October 5, 2017|website=Gazeta do Sul}}</ref> such as [[Fabiano Peçanha]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://olimpiadas.uol.com.br/2008/atletas-brasileiros/atletismo/fabiano-pecanha.jhtm|title=Fabiano Peçanha|website=UOL|year=2008}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/esportes/2016/07/09/76208-fora_das_pistas_como_profissional_agora_os_objetivos_de_fabiano_pecanha_sao_outros.html.php|title=Fora das pistas como profissional, agora os objetivos de Fabiano Peçanha são outros|date=7 September 2016|website=Gazeta do Sul}}</ref>

== Leisure ==
Events celebrating both German and Gaucho cultures are traditionally held, with the most prominent being the Santa Cruz do Sul Oktoberfest and the Art and Tradition Encounter, respectively, along with their associated events. Other events include the [[Streuselkuchen|Cuca]] Festival, centered on the German dish, which in its 23rd edition in 2023 drew 80,000 attendees,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/organizacao-estima-publico-de-80-mil-pessoas-na-festa-das-cucas/|title=Organização estima público de 80 mil pessoas na Festa das Cucas|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=12 June 2023|author=Claudia Priebe|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230613190627/https://www.gaz.com.br/organizacao-estima-publico-de-80-mil-pessoas-na-festa-das-cucas/|archive-date=13 June 2023}}</ref> as well as events featuring beer producers from Rio Grande do Sul.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://gaz.com.br/conteudos/regional/2017/03/03/90536-festival_da_cerveja_artesanal_de_santa_cruz_acontece_em_setembro.html.php|title=Festival da Cerveja Artesanal de Santa Cruz acontece em setembro|website=Gazeta do Sul|date=March 3, 2017}}</ref> Additionally, [[Carnival]] has been celebrated in the city since 1896, featuring balls, street parties, and samba school parades. Today, the main [[Samba school|samba schools]] include Acadêmicos do União, founded in 1973 by members of the Sociedade Cultural e Beneficente (SCB) club, known as Uniãozinho, established in 1923 as a space for the city's Black community,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/uniaozinho-100-anos-de-um-simbolo-de-resistencia/|title=Uniãozinho: 100 anos de um símbolo de resistência|website=Gazeta do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230712060340/https://www.gaz.com.br/uniaozinho-100-anos-de-um-simbolo-de-resistencia/|archive-date=12 July 2023|date=1 July 2023|author=Caroline Garske}}</ref> as well as Imperadores do Ritmo, Imperatriz do Sol, Império da Zona Norte, and Unidos de Santa Cruz. Other notable street carnival groups from the early 20th century include Bam-Bam-Bam, Filhos do Inferno, and Turunas.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gaz.com.br/carnaval-de-rua-em-santa-cruz-soma-mais-de-100-anos-de-cultura/|title=Carnaval de rua em Santa Cruz soma mais de 100 anos de cultura|author=Caroline Garske|date=12 February 2023|website=Gazeta do Sul|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230218203640/https://www.gaz.com.br/carnaval-de-rua-em-santa-cruz-soma-mais-de-100-anos-de-cultura/|archive-date=18 February 2023}}</ref>

for residents and hosts various events.<ref>{{cite
