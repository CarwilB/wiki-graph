'''Quixadá''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|kiʃaˈda}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the state of [[Ceará]] in [[Brazil]]. It is known for its unusual rock formations, known locally as monoliths.

==Location==
Quixadá is located at around {{coord|4|58|15|S|39|0|54|W}}.
It was founded in 1870 and had a population of 80,605 residents in 2010, according to the Brazilian census. It is the seat of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Quixadá]].

Pre-historic cave paintings and other archaeological remains have been found in the vicinity.<ref name=UNESCO/>
The municipality contains the {{convert|16635|ha}} [[Quixadá Monoliths Natural Monument]], a fully protected area around and containing the highly unusual [[inselberg]]s known locally as monoliths, which are of considerable tourist interest.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|MN Monólitos de Quixadá – WWF}}|language=pt|title=MN Monólitos de Quixadá|publisher=WWF|url=http://observatorio.wwf.org.br/unidades/cadastro/207/|access-date=2016-05-21|archive-date=June 16, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160616202517/http://observatorio.wwf.org.br/unidades/cadastro/207/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|Monumento Natural Monólitos de Quixadá – Semace}}|language=pt
 |title=Monumento Natural Monólitos de Quixadá|date=September 24, 2010
 |publisher=Semace |url=http://www.semace.ce.gov.br/2010/12/1639/|access-date=2016-05-21}}</ref>
The Barragem do Cedro (Cedar Dam) was built between 1890 and 1906 using stone masonry, cement and steel, at the foot of the [[Pedra da Galinha Choca]] (Brooding Hen Rock), the most famous of the monoliths. On 30 January 2015 the dam was placed on the tentative list as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.<ref name=UNESCO>{{citation|ref={{harvid|Cedro Dam in the Quixadá Monoliths – UNESCO}} |title=Cedro Dam in the Quixadá Monoliths|publisher=UNESCO |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5998/|access-date=2016-05-21}}</ref>

==History==
Until the 1760s, the indigenous tribes of Kanindé and Jenipapo populated the territory that is known as Quixadá. The fact that it used to be the land of indigenous explains why Quixadá does not have a Portuguese name. The conflicts between these groups and the Portuguese led to the indigenous peoples' extinction.

==Sports==
[[Quixadá Futebol Clube]] is the town's soccer club.

Quixadá is a major [[paragliding]] spot in Brazil.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.paraglidingearth.com/pgearth/index.php?site=6801|title = Paragliding Earth}}</ref> It is famous for many flight distance world record breaks in the last years, and attracts pilots from around the world every November when potential record-breaking conditions prevail.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://xcmag.com/news/women’s-paragliding-distance-world-record-falls-in-brazil/|title=Women's Paragliding Distance World Record Falls in Brazil - Cross Country Magazine – In the Core since 1988|date=November 10, 2009|accessdate=November 23, 2022}}</ref>

[[Hang gliding]] and [[climbing]] are other [[extreme sport]]s popular in the area.

==Culture==
The best-known figure from Quixadá is [[Rachel de Queiroz]], a writer who portrayed the drought of 1915 in her novel "O Quinze (1930)," which later received national recognition. Queiroz was not born in Quixadá, but lived there for most of her life. 
In Quixadá, you can find three places dedicated to her: the Memorial Rachel de Queiroz, the former Chalé da Pedra; the Museu Rachel de Queiroz, which is located on her small farm, the Fazenda Não Me Deixes; and also the Rachel de Queiroz Cultural Center.

==Transportation==
Quixadá is served by [[Quixadá Airport]].
