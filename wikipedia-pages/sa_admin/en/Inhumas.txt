'''Inhumas''' ({{IPA|pt|ĩˈɲũmɐs|lang}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in central [[Goiás]] state, [[Brazil]]. The population was 52 866 (2019 estimate) in a total area of {{cvt|613.3|km2}} (10/10/2002). Inhumas is a large producer of sugarcane, poultry, milk, and meat.

==Distances and Location==
*Distance to the state capital: 54&nbsp;km.
*Distance to regional center ([[Anápolis]]): 73&nbsp;km.
*Highway connections: state highway GO-070 north from [[Goiânia]], through [[Goianira]] and then 22&nbsp;km. north.

Inhumas is on the [[Meia Ponte River]]. Neighboring municipalities are [[Itauçu]], [[Petrolina]], [[Damolândia]], [[Brazabrantes]], [[Caturaí]], and [[Araçu]].

==The economy==
Main agricultural activities were cattle raising (72,800 head in 2005) and agriculture. Because of the fertility of the soil and abundance of water Inhumas has a large and varied agricultural production. The main crops are sugarcane (373,000 tons), coffee, rice, soybeans, citrus fruits and bananas.
*Number of industrial establishments: 132
*Number of retail establishments: 486
*Banking establishments: Banco do Brasil S.A., BRADESCO S.A., Banco Itaú S.A., CEF, HSBC Bank Brasil S.A., Banco Multiplo (August 2007)
*Dairies: Laticínios Guerreiro Ltda. (22/05/2006)
*Industrial parks: Distrito Agroindustrial de Inhumas - DAI (June/2006)
*Distilleries: Centroálcool S/A - CENASA. (July/2007)
*Meat-packing plants: Frigorífico Vale da Goiabeira Ltda., Hiperboi Indústria e Comércio Ltda. (22/05/2006)

==Education and health==
The literacy rate was 87.8% and the infant mortality rate was 20.7 in 1,000 live births. There were 49 schools in the municipality and 5 small hospitals. There is a campus of the State University of Goiás (UEG) offering courses in zoo technology and nursing.

==History==
Inhumas was important as a stopping point on the Estrada Real linking the south with [[Goiás, Goiás|Goiás Velho]]. The first settlement was called Goiabeiras, because of the extensive groves of guava trees. It had fertile soil and attracted many immigrants, especially Syrians and Lebanese (Asmar, Sahium, Raiza, Chalub, Mahmud, Gebrim, Charter, Sebba, Nacruht), Italians (Pessoni, Qualhato, Serravali, Balestra, Quintanilha, Jácomo) and Japanese (Watanabe, Momonuke). The Syrian-Lebanese were the first to open commercial establishments in the 1930s. The Italians cultivated coffee and the Japanese worked on small farms producing garden vegetables. At one time the region was a major producer of coffee.

In 1896 Goiabeiras was elevated to the status of district in the municipality of [[Itaberaí]]. In 1908 the name was changed to "Inhumas" in homage to a local bird also known as inhuma or anhuma ([[Anhima cornuta]]).
In 1931 Inhumas was dismembered from Itaberaí to become a municipality. For the history see [https://web.archive.org/web/20060411085923/http://www.tudoin.com.br/portal/History.asp Tudoinhumas] and [https://web.archive.org/web/20080919083652/http://www.inhumas.go.gov.br/inhumas/historia.php Inhumas.go]
