'''Talamanca''' is a [[Cantons of Costa Rica|canton]] in the [[Limón Province|Limón]] province of [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The head city is [[Bribri, Costa Rica|Bribri]], located in [[Bratsi District|Bratsi]] district.

== History ==
{{See also|Cacicazgo of Talamanca|Kingdom of Talamanca}}
Talamanca was created on 20 May 1969 by decree 4339.<ref name="crevoterritorial" />

== Geography ==
Talamanca has an area of {{formatnum:2809.93}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |access-date=26 September 2020 |archive-date=16 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200916045223/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and a mean elevation of {{formatnum:29}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

The county is noted for its beautiful beaches, especially in [[Cahuita]] and [[Puerto Viejo de Talamanca|Puerto Viejo]], which are popular tourist locations.

Talamanca contains one of Costa Rica's three official border-crossing points with [[Panama]], the Sixaola-Guabito crossing.

For the [[Costa Rica 2011 Census|2011 census]], Talamanca had a population of {{formatnum:30712}} inhabitants.<ref name="censo2011">{{cite web |language=es |title=Censo. 2011. The county suffers from pervasive poverty. As of 2009, its human-development index is the lowest-ranked of all Costa Rican cantons.<ref>Álvaros, A.R. (2009, October 11). El miedo atracó en Limón. ''La Nación'' (Costa Rica). Retrieved from http://wvw.nacion.com/proa/2009/octubre/11/proa2113441.html</ref> While its most recent infant mortality rate is 12.89% (2009), it was as high as 22.5% (2003), and stayed above 15% between 2003 and 2007.<ref>Insituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos (INEC). (2009). ''Anuario Estadístico 2009''. Retrieved from http://www.inec.go.cr</ref> As of 2010, 52.3% of Talamanca inhabitants have access to sanitation (either piped or septic tank), and 75.2% are connected to electricity.<ref>Insituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos (INEC). (2010). ''Cuadro No 39: Indicadores de vividena, según provincia, cantón, y distrito''. Retrieved from http://www.inec.go.cr</ref>

The county is composed of four districts (see graphic below), with its capital city, [[Bribrí, Costa Rica|Bribrí]], located in the [[Bratsi District|Bratsi]] district. Talamanca houses the largest indigenous population in the country (at 11,062, or 34% of the county's population), which is composed principally of the [[Bribri people|Bribri]] and [[Cabécar people|Cabécar]] groups<ref name="inec">Insituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos (INEC). ==Conservation==
Eighty-eight percent of Talamanca's territory is protected. Fifty-five percent of this land falls under the [[Chirripó National Park|Chirripó]], [[La Amistad International Park|Amistad]], and [[Cahuita National Park|Cahuita]] National Parks;  and 2% belongs to the [[Jairo Mora Sandoval Gandoca-Manzanillo Mixed Wildlife Refuge]] (a major [[sea turtle]] nesting ground).<ref name="Gudiño">{{cite news |last1=Gudiño |first1=Arturo |title=Denuncian actividad minera de extranjeros en reserva indígena de Costa Rica |url=http://wvw.nacion.com/ln_ee/2007/junio/21/ultima-sr1141448.html |access-date=21 November 2020 |work=La Nación |date=21 June 2007 |language=es}}</ref>

In defense of these areas, for example, on July 27, 2011, the [[Ministry of Environment, Energy and Telecommunications|Ministerio de Ambiente, Energía y Telecomunicaciones]] (Minaet) carried out the demolition of two hotels (Las Palmas and Suerre), due to their occupation of 4 hectares of land within the Jairo Mora Sandoval Gandoca-Manzanillo Mixed Wildlife Refuge.
