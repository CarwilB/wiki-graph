'''Tlaxco''' ([[Nahuatl]]: "place of the [[Mesoamerican ballgame|ball game]]"<ref name=encyclopedia>{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM29tlaxcala/index.html |title=Tlaxco |language=es |website=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=[[INAFED]] |accessdate=17 November 2017}}</ref>) is a town and its surrounding [[Municipalities of Tlaxcala|municipality]] in the [[List of states of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Tlaxcala]].

==Geography==
The municipality of Tlaxco is located in northern Tlaxcala on the border with [[Hidalgo (state)|Hidalgo]] and [[Puebla]]. From east to west on its northern side, it borders the municipalities of [[Ixtacamaxtitlán]] and [[Chignahuapan]] in Puebla, and [[Almoloya, Hidalgo|Almoloya]] and [[Apan]] in Hidalgo. From east to west on its southern side, it borders the Tlaxcalan municipalities of [[Emiliano Zapata Municipality, Tlaxcala|Emiliano Zapata]], [[Lázaro Cárdenas Municipality, Tlaxcala|Lázaro Cárdenas]], [[Tetla de la Solidaridad]], [[Atlangatepec (municipality)|Atlangatepec]], [[Muñoz de Domingo Arenas]], [[Hueyotlipan]] and [[Benito Juárez Municipality, Tlaxcala|Benito Juárez]].<ref name=infocard/> Tlaxco is Tlaxcala's largest municipality: it covers an area of {{convert|575|km2}} and comprises 14.40% of the state's area.<ref name=snim/>

Situated in the [[Mexican Altiplano]], Tlaxco lies at an average altitude of {{convert|2540|m}} above sea level. The western and central parts of the municipality are relatively flat, while the northern and eastern parts of the municipality feature more rugged terrain.<ref name=encyclopedia/> The highest point in the municipality is the Cerro Peñas Coloradas located at {{coord|19.6653|N|98.0531|W|region:MX|display=inline}} with an elevation of {{convert|3360|m}} above sea level.<ref name=notebook/> The [[Zahuapan River]] and its tributaries drain the central-eastern part of the municipality.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Tlaxco, Tlaxcala |publisher=[[INEGI]] |accessdate=17 November 2017}}</ref> There are also two reservoirs in the municipality, Lázaro Cárdenas in the extreme east and El Muerto in the west.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref name=compendium/> Agricultural land comprises 65.43% of the municipality.<ref name=compendium/> Soils in the municipality include [[cambisol]]s, [[fluvisol]]s, [[lithosol]]s, [[gleysol]]s and [[andosol]]s.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

Tlaxco's climate is temperate sub-humid with the warmest months being March through May. June through September are the months with the most rain.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average annual precipitation ranges between 600 and 900 millimetres.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
Although minor settlements have been found in the Tlaxco area dating back to the Texoloc phase or Middle [[Mesoamerican chronology#Preclassic Era|Formative period]], sedentary settlement of the area properly began in the Tezoquipan phase or Late Formative period by proto-[[Teotihuacan]] peoples who share elements of the Tezoquipan culture. From 650 to 850 AD, northern Tlaxcala experienced a period of political instability known as the Acopinalco Complex, during which many different cultural groups competed for control over the area. By 900 AD a group of [[Huastec civilization|Huastec]] or [[Otomi people|Otomi]] people had prevailed, and subsequent cultural homogenization resulted in formation of the Tlaxco culture which would later become associated with the city of [[Tliliuhquitepec]].<ref name=encyclopedia/>

[[Franciscans]] had arrived in Tlaxco by 1614, which was then part of the municipality of [[Atlangatepec (municipality)|Atlangatepec]]. After 1820 Tlaxco began to exist as a separate administrative division of Tlaxcala, with only minor interruptions. By the end of the 19th century, Tlaxco had become an important centre for the production of [[pulque]]. [[Porfirio Díaz]] visited Tlaxco in January 1894 and inaugurated the municipal palace. The modern municipality of Tlaxco was established on 29 August 1945 with the publication of the Organic Law of Municipalities in the State of Tlaxcala. In 1956 a highway linking [[Apizaco]] and [[Chignahuapan]] via Tlaxco was completed.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and seven trustees (''regidores'') elected by [[proportional representation]].<ref name=encyclopedia/> The current president of the municipality is Gardenia Hernández Rodríguez.<ref name=officialsite/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Tlaxco recorded a population of 39,939 inhabitants living in 9356 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=29&mun=034 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Tlaxco |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[SEDESOL]] |accessdate=17 November 2017}}</ref> It recorded a population of 42,536 inhabitants in the 2015 Intercensal Survey.<ref name=survey/>

There are 155 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> two of which are classified as urban:
*Tlaxco, the municipal seat, which recorded a population of 14,806 inhabitants in the 2010 Census,<ref name=infocard/> and was designated a ''[[Pueblo Mágico|pueblo mágico]]'' by Mexico's [[Secretariat of Tourism (Mexico)|Secretariat of Tourism]] in 2015;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sectur.gob.mx/gobmx/pueblos-magicos/tlaxco-tlaxcala/ |title=Tlaxco, Tlaxcala |publisher=[[Secretariat of Tourism (Mexico)|SECTUR]] |date=16 March 2016 |accessdate=17 November 2017}}</ref> and
*San José Atotonilco, which had 3220 inhabitants in 2010.<ref name=infocard/>

==Economy==
The main economic activity in Tlaxco is agriculture.<ref name=encyclopedia/> The [[gross domestic product]] of the municipality was unofficially estimated at 1176.1 million [[Mexican peso]]s in 2010.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=González-Estrada |first1=Adrián |last2=Gallegos-Cedillo |first2=Gilberto |date=2014 |title=The gross domestic product of municipalities of Mexico: II.
