'''Pahuatlán''' ({{IPA|es|pawaˈtlan|lang|Pahuatlán.ogg}}), officially '''Pahuatlán del Valle''', is a town and [[municipalities of Puebla|municipality]] located in the northwest of the [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Puebla]] in central [[Mexico]]. The municipality is part of the [[Sierra Norte de Puebla|Sierra Norte region]] of the state, a steep mountainous area which receive significant moisture from the [[Gulf of Mexico]], and borders the states of [[Hidalgo (state)|Hidalgo]] and [[Veracruz]].

The town was founded by the [[Augustinian order|Augustinians]] when they built a small monastery in the town in 1532, with the area divided among ethnic [[Nahua peoples|Nahuas]] and [[Otomi people|Otomis]], both of which can still be found here today. Culturally, the municipality is best known for the [[amate|amate bark paper]] which is produced by the Otomis of the San Pablito community.

==The town==
The town of Pahuatlán is located on a small level space on the side of the Ahila Mountain about 1600 meters above sea level in the [[Sierra Norte de Puebla|Sierra Norte mountains of Puebla]] about fifty km from [[Tulancingo]] near the Hidalgo and Veracruz state borders.<ref name="atractivo">{{cite news |title= Pahuatlán, atractivo lugar turístico  |url= http://www.oem.com.mx/elsoldetulancingo/notas/n1995270.htm |author= Juan Manuel Aguirre |newspaper= El Sol de Tulancingo |location=Tulancingo, Mexico |date=March 9, 2011 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Pahuatlán, attractive tourist place }}</ref><ref name="encmuc">{{cite web |url= http://www.e-local.gob.mx/wb2/ELOCAL/EMM_Puebla |title=Pahuatlán |year=2010 |work= Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México-Estado de Puebla |publisher= INAFED Instituto para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal and SEGOB Secretaría de Gobernación |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=February 23, 2012 }}</ref><ref name="encuentros">{{cite news |title= Encuentros con Mexico / Una misteriosa region del amate  |author=Ricardo Diaz Muñoz |author2=Maryell Ortiz de Zarate |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=April 30, 2000 |page=2 |language=es |trans-title=Encounters with Mexico/A mysterious region of amate }}</ref> It is the center of commerce for the municipality of the same name, with a population of just over 3,000.<ref name="encmuc"/> The area conserves most of its traditional simple and austere architecture despite the lack of ordinances to that effect. The center of the town has stone paved roads. Traditional houses consist of two floors with wrought iron balconies and high roofs covered in red tile.<ref name="atractivo"/><ref name="encuentros"/><ref name="rincones">{{cite book |title=Puebla:Guía para descubrir los encantos del estado |trans-title=Puebla: Guide to discover the charms of the state |editor1-first=Victor Manuel |editor1-last=Jimenez Gonzalez |year=2010 |publisher=Editorial Oceano, S. L.|location=Barcelona |language=es |isbn=978-607-400-234-8|pages=61–62}}</ref> The town has been photographed by noted artist Angeles Torrejon for a series called "Vivir en la Sierra" (To live in the mountains). Another  regular photographer of the area is Ruben Pax.<ref name="finsemana">{{cite news |title= Fin de Semana/ Bienaventurados los que visitan Pahuatlan |author=Blanca Ruiz |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=May 11, 1997 |page=3 |language=es |trans-title=Weekend/Good adventure for those who visit Pahuatlán }}</ref>

The historic center of the town is its main plaza, which serves as a [[tianguis]] market on Sundays, selling handcrafts such as [[rebozo]]s, other textiles, fresh flowers, ceramics, leather goods and more, mostly from the surrounding rural communities.<ref name="finsemana"/>

This plaza is surrounded by the main civic and religious structures of the area, such as the municipal palace.<ref name="encuentros"/>  The main monumental structure in the municipality is the Santiago Apóstol parish from the 16th century. The patron saint of the municipality is the [[James, son of Zebedee|Apostle James the Great]], whose feast day is celebrated on 25 June here with masses, processions, fireworks and traditional dance including the [[Danza de los Voladores|Voladores]].<ref name="encmuc"/>
[[File:SantiagoApostolPahuatlan01.JPG|thumb|left|View of the parish church]]
The Casa de Cultura has paintings and literature related to the municipality.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The main handcrafts store is the Casa del Amate (House of [[Amate|Amate Paper]]) located on the main road heading towards the town center. In addition to selling the local paper, it also sells embroidered items and paintings.<ref name="finsemana"/> It was the workshop of painter Rafael Lechuga, whose art depicts the area and the local amate paper.<ref name="encuentros"/>

The Parque de los Muertos, according to tradition, is where soldiers loyal to [[Maximilian I of Mexico|Maximilian I]] were buried.<ref name="rincones"/>

The most traditional music of the area is [[huapango]]. Traditional dress for women includes a long black skirt with a white embroidered blouse and rebozo or [[quezquémetl]]. For men, it includes undyed cotton pants and shirt, a palm leaf hat, sandals and a machete. Traditional dishes of the area include [[mole (sauce)|mole poblano, pipián]], [[tamale]]s, fresh water shrimp and a type of ant called chicales.<ref name="encmuc"/> At night, a traditional snack is "[[mollete]]s," French bread with refried beans, [[longaniza]] sausage and cheese which is toasted.<ref name="encuentros"/> Pahuatlán is one of a number of communities that claims to be the origin of the [[Danza de los Voladores]].<ref name="encuentros"/> It hosted the fourth Danza de los Voladores ( International Encounter in 1998. The spectacle is mostly performed by the [[Nahua peoples|Nahuas]] in the area.<ref name="inaugura">{{cite news |title= Inaugura alcalde de Papantla el V Encuentro Internacional de Voladores: [1] |newspaper=NOTIMEX |location=Mexico City |date=June 20, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Mayor of Papantla inaugurates the V International Encounter of Voladores }}</ref>

It is a weekend getaway for residents of Tulancingo.<ref name="atractivo"/> There are two main hotels, one with three stars and the other with one. There are also a number of small guesthouses operated by families in their homes.<ref name="afirman">{{cite news |title= Afirman que Pahuatlán, Puebla, se mantiene firme tras deslave de 2007 |newspaper=NOTIMEX |location=Mexico City |date=March 13, 2008 |language=es |trans-title=Confirm that Pahuatlán, Puebla remains firm after the landslide of 2007 }}</ref> Locally produced coffee is available in the town's stores.<ref name="finsemana"/>

[[Carnival]] lasts for the eight days prior to [[Ash Wednesday]] to mark the end of "mundane life" in preparation for [[Lent]] and [[Holy Week]]. Participants are men dressed in multicolored outfits and wood masks who dance in the streets accompanied by live or recorded music. These are known as the "Huehues of Pahuatlán." The municipality supports the annual event in several ways including workshops for the making and improving of costumes and masks.<ref name="inicia">{{cite news |title= Inicia el tradicional Carnaval en Pahuatlán |url= http://pueblanoticias.com.mx/noticia/inicia-el-tradicional-carnaval-en-pahuatlan-16916/ |newspaper=Puebla Noticias |location=Mexico |date=15 February 2012 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Traditional Carnival in Pahutalán begina }}</ref>

Pahuatlán major festival is during Holy Week, which attracted an estimated 8,000 people in 2008. Religious events are complemented by the annual Festival Cultural de la Sierra which sponsors plays art exhibits and more.<ref name="atractivo"/><ref name="afirman"/>

Another important celebration marks the victory of General Lechuga's forces of the French, celebrated in January.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The town held its first film festival called the Encuentro Nacional de Festivales de Cine in 2012, sponsored by the Centro Internacional de Artes y Ciencias (Cinearte) and the Festival Internacional de Cine de Puebla (FICP).<ref name="realizaran">{{cite news |title= Realizarán primer Encuentro de Festivales de Cine |url= http://www.aztecanoticias.com.mx/notas/cultura/96760/realizaran-primer-encuentro-de-festivales-de-cine |agency=NOTIMEX |newspaper= Azteca Noticias |location=Mexico City |date=February 15, 2012 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Will hold the first Encounter of Film Festivals}}</ref>

==The municipality==
[[File:StreetSanPablito02.JPG|thumb|View of the main street in San Pablito]]
[[File:PahuatlanValley10.JPG|thumb|left|300px|View of the Pahuatlán Valley]]
The town of Pahuatlán is the seat of government for the municipality of the same name, which contains a population of 20,619 as of 2010,  divided into thirty four communities.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="microrreg">{{cite web |url=http://cat.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/default.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&valor=21109&varent=21&varmun=109 |title=Resumen Municipal |work=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |location=Mexico |language=es |trans-title=Summary of municipality |access-date=February 23, 2012 }}{{Dead link|date=April 2020 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> Together, the communities form an area of {{cvt|80.37|km2}}, nestled in the far northwest of the state of Puebla, bordering the Sierra Otomí-Tepahua mountains of Hidalgo.<ref name="atractivo"/> The municipality borders the municipalities of [[Tlacuilotepec (municipality)|Tlacuilotepec]], [[Naupan (municipality)|Naupan]] and [[Honey (municipality)|Honey]], bordering the state of Hidalgo on the north, south and west.<ref name="encmuc"/>  All of the communities of the municipality are classified as rural, with the exception of the seat and San Pablito.<ref name="microrreg"/>  The municipal government consists of a municipal president, an officer called a "síndico" and seven representatives called "regidores."<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipality is in the south of a cultural region known as [[La Huasteca]].<ref name="ofrenda">{{cite web |url= http://www.mna.inah.gob.mx/index.php/salas-de-exhibicion/temporales/expos-anteriores/80-ofrenda-de-dia-de-muertos-otomi.html |title= Ofrenda de día de muertos otomí de San Pablito, Pahuatlán, Puebla |publisher= Museo Nacional de Antropología |location=Mexico |language=es |trans-title=Otomi Day of the Dead altar in San Pablito, Pahuatlán, Puebla |access-date=February 23, 2012 }}</ref> Just under half of the population is indigenous, divided between ethnic Nahua and ethnic Otomí.<ref name="rincones"/><ref name="microrreg"/>
[[File:AmateSanPablito02.JPG|thumb|Amate paper wall hangings at the Gallery/Museum in San Pablito]]
After the town of Pahuatlán, the most populous community is [[San Pablito, Puebla|San Pablito]], with 2,760 people on a level area on the side of the Cerro del Guajalote Mountain near the Cerro del Brujo. Its Otomi name is Bite (meaning "below the mountain").<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="encuentros"/><ref name="sicgob">{{cite web |url= http://www.sic.gob.mx/ficha.php?table=festividad&table_id=509 |title= Fiesta del Pueblo en San Pablito Pahuatlán |work= Sistema de Información Cultural |publisher=CONACULTA |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=February 23, 2012 }}</ref> It is separated from the municipal seat by twelve km, and a deep ravine cut by the San Marcos River.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="mexchan">{{cite news |title= Mexico Channel / Donde los brujos se hacen de papel |author=Harry Miller  |newspaper=Mural |location=Guadalajara, Mexico |date=August 7, 2005 |page=3 |language=es |trans-title=Mexico Channel/Where the shamans makes paper }}</ref> The main economic activity here is [[Mexican handcrafts and folk art|handcrafts]], especially the production of a paper called "amate" made from the bark of a kind of fig tree that grows in the area.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="encuentros"/> The creation of this paper dates back to the pre Hispanic period and was and is used by the shamans of the area in rituals.<ref name="encuentros"/> The small town is the principal producer of amate paper in Mexico.<ref name="producen">{{cite news |title= Producen amate y contaminacion  |author=Patricia Cordero  |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=October 24, 2003 |page=3 |language=es |trans-title=Produce amate paper and pollution }}</ref> The paper is mostly made by women in their homes and is sold to artists and artisans all over the world. Most is bought by Nahua painters from the state of [[Guerrero]].<ref name="encuentros"/> On days when the paper is being manufactured, it is possible to hear the rhythmic thumping of stones pounding the fibers through the town. The production of the paper has caused environmental problems such as the overstripping of trees for bark and the use of chemicals which wind up in the [[Cazones River]]. The problematic chemicals are [[caustic soda]], used to soften the bark and industrial dyes.<ref name="producen"/>

The Fiesta del Pueblo or Town Festival occurs annually in San Pablito. It begins the week after Holy Week ends and lasts for fifteen days. Twelve saints are honored during this time, with each having his or her own day. The most important of these days is that dedicated to the [[Holy Trinity]] with the day honoring the towns patron saint, Saint Paul, coming second.<ref name="sicgob"/> San Pablito also is known for its colorful celebrations of [[Day of the Dead]]. This lasts from 31 October to 2 November, with different days dedicated to children, adults and saints.<ref name="ofrenda"/>

Cuauneutla is one of the oldest communities in the municipality.<ref name="inicia2">{{cite news |title= Inicia Feria Patronal de Cuauneutla en Pahuatlán |url= http://www.tzdigital.com.mx/tz/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1578:11-de-febrero-inicia-feria-patronal-de-cuauneutla-en-pahuatlan-&catid=50:regional&Itemid=27 |newspaper=TZ Digital Noticias |location=Puebla, Mexico |date=February 10, 2011 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Feast of the Patron Saint to begin in Cuauneutla in Pahuatlán }}</ref> It is located thirteen km from the municipal seat with a population of just over 700.  Its main economic activity is the production of coffee.<ref name="encmuc"/> In February, the community of Cuauneutla celebrates its patron, the Señor de la Agonía (Lord of Agony) with cultural, religious, sporting and social events.<ref name="inicia2"/>

Xolotla is located about 14&nbsp;km from the municipal seat with a population of about 2,400 people. Its main economic activity is embroidery and other handcrafts. Atla is located about ten km from the municipal seat with  a population of about 1,700 people. Its main economic activity is agriculture, especially the growing of coffee along with some handcrafts. Tlalcruz de Libres is located about 12&nbsp;km from the municipal seat with a population of just over 1,000 people. Its main economic activity is agriculture, especially the growing of coffee. Zoyatla de Guerrero is located about twelve km from the municipal seat with a population of under 1000. Its main economic activity is the growing of coffee. Atlantongo is located about 18&nbsp;km from the municipal seat with a population of about 680. Its main economic activity is the growing of chili peppers along with crafts.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Pahuatlán's main hospital is located down in the valley area near the river. It was opened in 2000, replacing a clinic built in 1970. It has a capacity of twelve beds and an emergency room. Flooding and shifting earth has damaged the structures with noticeable cracks in its walls. Much of the damage occurred in 2006 and 2007 as a result of [[Hurricane Dean|Hurricanes Dean]] and [[Hurricane Karl (2010)|Karl]].<ref name="operan">{{cite news |title= Operan hospitales entre el 'peligro' en Sierra Norte |author=Francisco Rivas |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=March 19, 2011 |page=20 |language=es |trans-title=Hospitals operate in the danger zone in the Sierra Norte }}</ref>

==History==

The name comes from [[Nahuatl]] and means “place of [[avocado]]s” or “place of fruit.” Its [[Otomi language|Otomi]] name is “Matsooni,” which is derived from the word for avocado.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="campus">{{cite web |url= http://www.unides.edu.mx/pahuatlan/ |title= Pahuatlán |publisher= Universidad del Desarrollo Pahuatlán |location= Puebla, Mexico |language= es |access-date= February 23, 2012 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110703092104/http://www.unides.edu.mx/pahuatlan/ |archive-date= July 3, 2011 |url-status= dead }}</ref>

During the pre Hispanic era, the area was inhabited by various ethnicities including the Nahuas, Otomis and Totonacas. The [[Aztec Empire]] invaded the area and nominally held it until the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|Spanish arrived]], but the rich resources of the area were in dispute between the local Otomi and Nahua peoples.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="rincones"/> The Spanish took over the area in 1522 and imposed the encomienda system.<ref name="encmuc"/>  The first evangelists to the area were the [[Augustinian order|Augustinians]]. They founded a small monastery in 1532, where the parish church, built in 1652, is found now.<ref name="encuentros"/> This is considered to be the founding of the town of Pahuatlán.<ref name="rincones"/>  The area come under the direct rule of the [[Spanish Crown]] in the 17th century. By 1750, it came under the jurisdiction of [[Huauchinango (municipality)|Huauchinango]] and remained so until 1860.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The major historical event to happen in this area occurred during the [[Second French intervention in Mexico|French Intervention in Mexico]]. On 28 January 1865, a battle occurred here between the French and their backers against troops under Coronel José Luis Lechuga.<ref name="encmuc"/>

In 1861, the municipality's official name was changed to Pahuatlán del Valle to honor General Don Leandro Valle.<ref name="encmuc"/>

As the town of Pahuatlán is located on the side of a steep hill, it is prone to landslides and other consequences of erosion. In October 2007, a fault line 800 meters long opened on one side of the town, causing the evacuations of over eighty families.<ref name="urgen">{{cite news |title= Urgen a desalojar Pahuatlán |author=Francisco Rivas |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=October 27, 2007 |page=16 |language=es |trans-title=Urged to evacuate Pahuatlán }}</ref> This negatively affected tourism in the area for a time.<ref name="afirman"/>

The municipal seat has applied to become a [[Pueblo Mágico]] in order to attract tourism to the area. To be accepted the state government invested 50 million [[Mexican peso|pesos]] in social infrastructure. This included the reconstruction and widening of the Atlantongo-Xolotla highway, which cost over 20 million pesos.<ref name="buscan">{{cite news |title= Buscan nominación de Pahuatlán como Pueblo Mágico |url= http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/notas/803174.html |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=October 22, 2011 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Pahuatlán seeks nomination as a Pueblo Mágico }}</ref>

==Geography==
[[File:Mountains106Pahuatlan.JPG|thumb|Steep mountainsides of the [[Sierra Norte of Puebla]] around Pahuatlán]]
[[File:RiverPahuatlanValley03.JPG|thumb|View of the San Marcos River]]

===Mountains===
Pahuatlán is in the northwestern [[Sierra Norte of Puebla]] [[mountain range]], at the intersection of the [[Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt]] and the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]], between the [[Mexican Plateau]] and the Gulf of Mexico coast. This area consists of a series of many individual mountain peaks formed in parallel lines. These mountains are separated by narrow valleys which frequently slope down towards the Gulf of Mexico. The terrain is steep and irregular with much of the municipality following the San Marcos and Mamiquetla rivers. Major peaks include the El Chile, Toxtla, Blanco, Tlazalotepec, De la Cruz, and Batalla de 28 Enero. The elevation of the municipality varies from {{convert|600|-|2000|m|ft}} above sea level.<ref name="encmuc"/>

===Climate===
There are two main climates in the municipalities, the temperate of the higher Sierra Norte and the hotter climate of the lower elevations nearer to the Gulf of Mexico The temperate climate has average annual temperatures which range from 12 to 18C. Most of the municipality falls under this designation. A small portion is in a semi hot climate with an average annual temperature of over 18C.<ref name="encmuc"/>
 
Both climates are humid, receiving large quantities of moist air from the Gulf,<ref name="encmuc"/> with the municipalities one of the most moist areas in the Sierra Norte of Puebla.<ref name="rincones"/> The steep terrain and wet weather of the area makes its prone to landslides and the opening of cracks in the ground.<ref name="urgen"/> In 1999, a twenty-meter fissure opened after a period of rain.<ref name="derrumbarse">{{cite news |title= La ciudad de Pahuatlán, por derrumbarse totalmente, señalan autoridades y vecinos |author= Javier Puga Martínez |url= http://www.lajornadadeoriente.com.mx/2007/10/24/puebla/pue107.php |newspaper=La Jornada de Oriente |location=Puebla, Mexico |date=October 24, 2007 |access-date=February 23, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=The city of Pahuatlán, about to collapse completely, indicate authorities and neighbors }}</ref> In 2007, a fault line opened in the ground for 800 meters from the municipal palace to Leandro Valle Schools, provoking several landslides and mandatory evacuations of over eighty families.<ref name="urgen"/> About four tons of earth and debris moved as a result.<ref name="derrumbarse"/>

===Natural history===
The area is part of several [[river basin]]s which are all [[Tributary|tributaries]] of the Gulf of Mexico. The rivers here are relatively recent in formation, winding with a number of steep drops. The two largest river basins are those of the Cazones and Tuxpan rivers with the principle water flows being the Mamiquetla, the Cuarco, Los Marías, Trinidad, Honey, Alcalman, Pahuatitla and Chixtla. Several of these eventually form the San Marcos, the main tributary of the Cazones.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Most of the municipality's natural vegetation [[ecosystem]]s have been eliminated. Some forests at the highest elevations remain, of the [[Sierra Madre Oriental pine-oak forests]] ecoregion. The lower elevations generally have only [[shrub]]s and other low growing vegetation. One major forest area is the perennial [[rainforest]] on the edges of the San Marcos River. Where natural vegetation still exists, wildlife such as deer, rabbits and opossum along with various bird and reptiles species are found.<ref name="encmuc"/>

There are caves at Angeles Tlacuilotepec, El Saltillo and Cazones, with [[cave painting]]s in the area between the Atla and Xolotla communities as well as a small archeological site at Saltillo.<ref name="encmuc"/>

==Socioeconomics==

Most of the municipality's residents lives in a state of socioeconomic marginalization, with thirty one communities classified as highly marginalized and two as very highly marginalized.<ref name="microrreg"/> The main economic activities are farming, especially coffee and the making of handcrafts, with commerce concentrated in the town of Pahuatlán proper.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipality is Mexico's main producer of [[amate|amate bark paper]], with its production concentrated in the community of San Pablito.<ref name="producen"/> Other handcrafts include hats, necklaces, embroidered clothing, baskets, semi precious stones and the weaving of wool.<ref name="encmuc"/> The [[Museo de Arte Popular, Mexico City|Museo de Arte Popular]] has sponsored a different craft for the area, the creation of beaded bands. The production and sale of these bands is not only to help support the artisan community of Pahuatlan but also to earn money for other museum projects.<ref name="apoyamap">{{cite news |title= Apoya MAP a artesanos  |author=Laura Castellanos  |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=September 2, 2005 |page=1 |language=es |trans-title=MAP supports artisans }}</ref>

However, despite this, most of the area's youth migrate to the United States for employment.<ref name="producen"/> This has left the area with mostly women. The economic recession of 2009 in the United States had a significant negative impact in the area with remittances sent back cut.<ref name="uswoes">{{cite news |title= U.S. Woes Cut Cash Flow From Mexican Migrants |newspaper=National Public Radio- All Things Considered |location=Washington, DC |date=November 3, 2009 }}</ref>

Although the area is not well known to tourists,<ref name="finsemana"/> efforts have been made to bring tourism to the municipality, principally through efforts to get the town of Pahuatlán designated as a “Pueblo Mágico.”<ref name="buscan"/>
