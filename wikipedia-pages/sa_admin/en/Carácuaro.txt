'''Carácuaro''' is a [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Michoacán]], located {{convert|77|km}} south of the state capital of [[Morelia]].

==Geography==
The municipality of Carácuaro is located in the [[Tierra Caliente (Mexico)|Tierra Caliente]] region of Michoacán at an altitude between {{convert|400|and(-)|1600|m}}. It borders the municipalities of [[Nocupétaro]] to the west, [[Villa Madero|Madero]] to the north, [[Tiquicheo]] to the east, [[Huetamo]] to the south and [[Turicato]] to the southwest.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Carácuaro, Michoacán de Ocampo |language=es |publisher=INEGI |accessdate=24 April 2018 |archive-date=4 August 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110804015215/http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |url-status=dead }}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|920.855|km2}}<ref name=infocard/> and comprises 1.6% of the state's area.<ref name=survey/> 

Carácuaro is located in the southern foothills of the [[Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt]], in the [[Balsas River]] basin. Tropical forests of [[parota]] and tepeguaje<ref name=encyclopedia/> cover 66% of the municipality.<ref name=compendium/>

Carácuaro's climate is tropical with rain in the summer.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|22|and(-)|28|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|700|and(-)|1000|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
Prior to the arrival of the Spanish, Carácuaro was a small [[Chichimeca]] village. The place name derives from the Chichimeca word ''carakua'', "place of the slope" or "place on the slope."<ref name=encyclopedia/> The Spanish founded a mission in Carácuaro in 1581. Originally a meat production centre for the towns of the [[Bajío]], it eventually became a centre of tropical fruit and sugarcane cultivation. [[José María Morelos]] served as the parish priest from 1799 until 1810, when he joined the rebels in the [[Mexican War of Independence]].<ref name=review>{{cite journal |last=Robinson |first=David J. |date=1997 |title=Carácuaro de Morelos: Historia de un pueblo de la tierra caliente |department=Review |jstor=2516744 |journal=The Hispanic American Historical Review |volume=77 |issue=3 |pages=523–524}}</ref> Carácuaro's significance declined after the war, despite it becoming a municipality on 1 February 1856.<ref>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Michoacán de Ocampo. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |pages=78, 110 |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |isbn=970-13-1501-4 |archive-date=2019-04-12 |access-date=2018-04-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190412021950/http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> After the [[Mexican Revolution]], emigration to central Mexico and the United States became common.<ref name=review/>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and seven trustees (''regidores''), four elected by [[relative majority]] and three by [[proportional representation]].<ref name=encyclopedia/> The current president of the municipality is Walter Gómez Gutiérrez.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Carácuaro recorded a population of 9212 inhabitants living in 2238 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=16&mun=013 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Carácuaro |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |accessdate=24 April 2018 |archive-date=25 April 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180425033546/http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=16&mun=013 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The 2015 Intercensal Survey estimated a population of 9485 inhabitants in Carácuaro.<ref name=survey/>

There are 176 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> of which only the municipal seat, known as Carácuaro de Morelos, is classified as urban. It recorded a population of 3653 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=catalog/>

==Economy==
The main economic activities in Carácuaro are farming and livestock production. The main crops grown are corn and sesame, while beef cattle and pigs are the main livestock raised.<ref>{{cite news |author=Carácuaro Municipal Council |url=http://caracuaro.gob.mx/contenidos/caracuaro/editor/files/2015/Cuarto/PLAN%20DE%20DESARROLLO%20MUNICIPAL_Periodico%20oficial%2021%20enero%202016.pdf |title=Plan de Desarrollo Municipal 2015–2018 |language=es |page=7 |newspaper=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Michoacán |publisher=Government of Michoacán |date=21 January 2016 |accessdate=24 April 2018}}</ref>

==Culture==
The ''Señor de Carácuaro'' or the Black Christ of Carácuaro is a large dark-coloured statue of Christ in the church of Saint Augustine.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ramírez Ortuño |first=Ángel |url=http://www.cambiodemichoacan.com.mx/nota-190710 |title=El Cristo Negro de Carácuaro, con sus múltiples y variadas historias |language=es |newspaper=Cambio de Michoacán |date=20 January 2013 |accessdate=24 April 2018}}</ref> Many miracles have been attributed to it and it is the destination of a popular pilgrimage route from [[Tacámbaro]] that takes place in the week around [[Ash Wednesday]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Rico |first=Rogelio |url=http://www.cambiodemichoacan.com.mx/columna-nc36626 |title=Fiesta de Ceniza en Carácuaro |language=es |newspaper=Cambio de Michoacán |date=25 January 2018 |accessdate=24 April 2018}}</ref>

The house where José María Morelos lived has been preserved as a library.<ref name=encyclopedia/>
