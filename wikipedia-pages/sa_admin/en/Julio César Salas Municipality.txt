The '''Julio César Salas Municipality''' is one of the 23 [[Municipalities of Venezuela|municipalities]] ([[municipio]]s) that makes up the [[Venezuela]]n state of [[Mérida (state)|Mérida]] and, according to a 2007 population estimate by the National Institute of Statistics of Venezuela, the municipality has a population of 15,300.<ref>http://www.ine.gob.ve/sintesisestadistica/estados/merida/cuadros/Poblacion6.xls {{dead link|date=April 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>  The town of [[Arapuey]] is the [[County seat|shire town]] of the Julio César Salas Municipality.<ref name="Merida_zip">http://www.ine.gob.ve/secciones/division/Merida.zip {{dead link|date=April 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==History==
Its origins date back to 1915 when the area was designated as Arapuey Village. With the construction of the [[Pan-American Highway]], it gained importance and became Julio César Salas Foreign Municipality, linked to the former Miranda District (today [[Miranda Municipality, Mérida|Miranda Municipality]]). Then, in 1988, it became Julio César Salas Autonomous Municipality. On January 15, 1992, the administration of Palmira [[Parish (administrative division)|Parish]] of Miranda Municipality was transferred to Julio César Salas Municipality. Historically, the northern territory has remained in dispute with [[Zulia State]] which sees it as an integral part.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

==Geography==
It is the northernmost municipality of Mérida. It is divided into two regions, a plateau formed by its proximity to [[Lake Maracaibo]] and another mountainous area located in the Venezuelan [[Andes]]. Elevations range from 80m in the north to 2,000m in the south, in the Andean foothills. The north is in a [[Tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests|tropical moist forest]] biome, and the rest of the municipality is a [[submontane moist forest]]. Temperatures vary between 25&nbsp;°C and 32.5&nbsp;°C and average annual precipitation is 1,000mm. The main waterways are the Alguacil, Arapuey and Quince rivers.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

==Demographics==
The Julio César Salas Municipality, according to a 2007 population estimate by the National Institute of Statistics of Venezuela, has a population of 15,300 (up from 12,569 in 2000).  This amounts to 1.8% of the state's population.<ref>http://www.ine.gob.ve/sintesisestadistica/estados/merida/cuadros/Poblacion5.xls {{dead link|date=April 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>  The municipality's population density is {{convert|75.7|PD/sqkm}}.<ref>http://www.ine.gob.ve/sintesisestadistica/estados/merida/cuadros/Poblacion4.xls {{dead link|date=April 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==Government==
The mayor of the Julio César Salas Municipality is Silvio Luis Torres Vasquez, elected on October 31, 2004, with 50% of the vote.<ref>{{Cite web| title=Alcalde de Municipio - Julio Cesar Salas | url=http://www.cne.gob.ve/regionales2004/12/alcaldedis=0&edo=12&mun=6&par=0&cen=0&mesa=0&cua=0.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120314125357/http://www.cne.gob.ve/regionales2004/12/alcaldedis=0&edo=12&mun=6&par=0&cen=0&mesa=0&cua=0.html | archive-date=2012-03-14}}</ref>  He replaced Antonio Vielma shortly after the elections.<ref>{{Cite web| url=http://www.cne.gob.ve/estadisticas/e016.pdf | title=Elecciones 30 de julio de 2000 directorio de alcaldes electos | language=es | trans-title=Elections July 30, 2000 Directory of elected mayors | accessdate=2008-03-15 | url-status=dead | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20061002063906/http://www.cne.gob.ve/estadisticas/e016.pdf | archivedate=2006-10-02}}</ref>  The municipality is divided into two [[parish]]es;  Julio César Salas and Palmira.<ref name="Merida_zip"/>
