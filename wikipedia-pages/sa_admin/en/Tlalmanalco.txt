'''Tlalmanalco''' is a [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] located in the far south-eastern part of the [[State of Mexico]]. The municipal seat and second largest town in the municipality is the town of '''Tlalmanalco de Velázquez'''  The name is from the [[Nahuatl]] language, meaning “flat area.” The municipality's seal shows flat land, with a pyramid on it, representing its pre-Hispanic history, surrounded by small mountains, which is how the area was represented in [[Aztec]] codices. The municipality is bordered by the municipalities of [[Chalco de Díaz Covarrubias|Chalco]], [[Ixtapaluca]], [[Cocotitlan]], [[Temamatla]], [[Tenango del Aire]], [[Ayapango]] and [[Amecameca]]. It also shares a border with the neighboring state of [[Puebla]]. Much of the municipality borders the [[Izta-Popo Zoquiapan National Park|Iztaccihuatl-Popocatepetl National Park]]. For this reason, [[Iztaccihuatl]] volcano dominates the landscape.<ref name="encmuc">{{cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15103a.htm |title=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de Mexico – Estado de Mexico – Tlalmanalco |language=es |access-date=2009-05-06 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070528124528/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15103a.htm |archive-date=May 28, 2007 }}</ref> The town has been designated as a “Pueblo con Encanto” (Town with Charm) by the government of the State of Mexico.<ref name="blog">{{cite web |url=http://app.edomex.gob.mx/blog/temas_categorias.jsp?id_cate=11&tema=Turismo |title=Blog del Estado de Mexico - Temas-Turismo |access-date=2009-05-05 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090131121621/http://app.edomex.gob.mx/blog/temas_categorias.jsp?id_cate=11&tema=Turismo |archive-date=2009-01-31 }}</ref>

==History==
According to archeological findings, there was a village stronghold in the area from about 3,100 to 600 B.C.<ref name="encmuc"/> The archeological zone is located just to the northwest of the main plaza of the town.<ref name="MexDesGuia">{{cite journal |last=Quintanar Hinojosa (ed) |first=Beatriz |date=Sep 2009 |title=Tlalmanalco |journal=Mexico Desconocido Guia Especial:Puebles Con Encanto del Bicentenario |issn=1870-9419 |pages=80–85 |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx }}</ref> Groups such as the [[Xochteca]], [[Cocolca]], [[Olmec-Xicallanca]] and [[Quiyahuizteca]] populated the area between the 10th and 13th centuries. However, these groups were later driven from here by the [[Toltec]]s and the [[Chichimeca]]s. A subgroup of these two tribes, called the [[Nonohualca]]s, arrived to the area in the 13th century and formed a city-state in what is now Tlalmanalco in 1336. This city-state formed an alliance with ethnically-related populations such as those in [[Amaquemecan]], [[Chimalhuacan]] and [[Tenango del Aire|Tenango Tepopollan]]. This area was one of the last to be conquered by the Aztecs, but after 100 years of fighting, the alliance succumbed in 1465.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The founding of the Spanish town occurred in 1525 by Friar Juan de Rivas, the same year that evangelization of the native population was begun by Friar [[Martin de Valencia]].<ref name="encmuc"/> The Annals of Chimalpain indicate that the indigenous shrine here was destroyed in 1525, and a church and open-air chapel were consecrated in 1532 even though the chapel was never completed.<ref name="guia">{{cite book |title=Guia turistica de Mexico - Estado de Mexico y Morelos |last=Bueno de Ariztegui (ed) |first=Patricia |year=1984 |publisher=Promex |location=Mexico City |language=es |isbn=968-34-0315-8 |pages=110–111 }}</ref> Tlalmanalco suffered the forced indentured servitude of the native population from 1550 to 1633, when this practice was abolished.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Attempts to industrialize the rural, farming economy began in 1858, when a copper [[smelting]] operation and various [[textile mill]]s were introduced, as well as a railroad line. One of the more important factories by the turn of the century was the San Rafael y Anexas, S.A. paper mill, located in the nearby village of San Rafael. This mill elevated the area economically, and was considered one of the most important industries in Mexico from 1930 to 1970.<ref name="encmuc"/>

==Notable sites==
[[File:SanLuisObispoChurchTlalMX.jpg|thumb|130px|Facade of the church]]
The '''Temple of San Luis Obispo''', with the adjoining [[Capilla Abierta]] (Open Chapel) were built in the 16th century by the [[Franciscans]] as part of the Monastery of San Luis.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="msn">{{cite web |url=http://mx.encarta.msn.com/encnet/refpages/RefArticle.aspx?refid=961536880 |title= MSN Encarta-Convento de San Luis (Tlalmanalco) |access-date=2009-05-06 }} {{Dead link|date=October 2010|bot=H3llBot}}</ref> The first church was built by Friar Juan de Rivas which opened in 1532.<ref name="encmuc"/> The larger complex was constructed between 1585 and 1591.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> The church is relatively simply decorated with only roses on the [[jamb]] of the main doors. In the central nave, there is a large relief of [[Francis of Assisi|Saint Francis]] with the [[stigmata]].<ref name="guia"/> The church has only one [[nave]] with a polygonal space for the main altar.<ref name="msn"/> This is covered by a [[barrel vault]] with false ribs painted on the ceiling.<ref name="guia"/> The interior has a lavish [[Churrigueresque|Mexican Baroque]] [[altarpiece]] done in [[Cedar wood|cedar]] representing the Visit of the Virgin.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> The paintings on this altar were done by Baltasar de Echave<ref name="guia"/> The cloister has frescos with scenes of flora and fauna and human figures, as well as portraits of Martin de Valencia and Saint Clare.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/><ref name="guia"/> Friar Martin de Valencia died in [[Ayotzingo]] and is interred in the Tlalmanalco church.<ref name="encmuc"/>
[[File:CapillaAbiertaTlalMX.jpg|thumb|left|250px|The Capilla Abierta]]
Tlalmanalco was one of the first sites to have an “open chapel,”  and is one of the very few places where one can be found intact today.<ref name="arts">{{cite web |last=Zavala y Alonso (ed) |first=Manuel |url=http://www.arts-history.mx/banco/index.php?id_nota=19072004175124 |title=Tlalmanalco y sus alrededores |language=es |access-date=2009-05-06 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110719093806/http://www.arts-history.mx/banco/index.php?id_nota=19072004175124 |archive-date=2011-07-19 }}</ref> In the open chapel was where mass was offered to natives who would not enter the dark confines of the typical church.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> The Capilla Abierta is done in Mexican Baroque style<ref name="encmuc"/> and is considered an interesting example of “tequitqui” art. The word “tequitqui” is from Nahuatl, meaning “vassal.” This expression was used to refer to artistic works, especially sculpture, that had Christian themes but was done by indigenous craftsmen. The Capilla Abierta has a trapezoidal layout, left incomplete. Five arches with capitals finished with reliefs and a [[frieze]] that follows the outline of the arches filled with human figures.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> These arches were reserved for persons of high social rank. The arch behind these was most likely reserved for the altar.<ref name="arts"/> The interior is “proto- renaissance” and decorated with Italian-style motifs and figures that seem to represent indigenous [[bestiary]]. Images includes [[Atlas (architecture)|telamons]], male goats (to represent lust) and a portrait of Friar Martin de Valencia, and [[Clare of Assisi|Saint Claire]].<ref name="msn"/> The mix of Christian and indigenous motifs has been studied by [[Gustavo Curiel]] and others. According to Curiel, the images seem to represent the clash between [[idolatry]], associated with the [[devil]] and Christianity in competition for the soul.<ref name="guia"/>

Nonohualca Museum  exhibits archeological pieces from the area around Tlalmanalco. Much of the work is stone sculpture, the best known of which is the Effigy of [[Xochipilli]].<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> This statue was found on the side of the volcano [[Popocatépetl]] near Tlalmanalco.<ref>Wasson, R. Gordon (1980) The Wondrous Mushroom</ref> however, this piece is in the [[Museo Nacional de Antropología|Museum of Anthropology]] in [[Mexico City]].<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="MexDesGuia"/>

==Culture==
===Gastronomy===
Dishes of the area include [[mixiote]], [[tlacoyo]]s with fava beans, pinto beans and [[requeson]], [[cecina (meat)|cecina]], green [[Mole (sauce)|mole]] with chicken or pork, and pork in [[mulato pepper|chile mulato]] but a very local specialty is [[Tamal (dish)|tamal]]es with [[capulin]]s (a type of cherry) and corn.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/>
===Festivals===
In October 2008, the town held its first International Festival of Antique Music, inviting vocalist [[Rita Guerrero]], flutist [[Horacio Franco]], the Alharaca Ensamble among others. The festival was a series of twelve concerts held in the most historically important sites of the town such as the Temple of San Luis Obispo the Old Belthemite Hospital and the main plaza.<ref name="festival">{{cite web|url=http://www.ccmc.org.mx/modules/news/article.php?storyid=5737 |title=Centro Cultural de Mexico Contemporaneo-Crean Festival de música antigua en Tlalmanalco |language=es |access-date=2009-05-06 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110722225942/http://www.ccmc.org.mx/modules/news/article.php?storyid=5737 |archive-date=2011-07-22 }}</ref>

The major annual celebrations revolve around the town's patron saints, especially Saint Louis, the Bishop and the [[Archangel Raphael]]. The feast of Saint Louis, the Bishop is on 20 August and the feast of the Archangel Raphael is 29 September.<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> They are celebrated with processions, fireworks and local dances including “Los Doce Pares de Francia”, “Marotas” and  “Chinelos.”<ref name="encmuc"/>

==The municipality==

As municipal seat, the town of Tlalmanalco has governing jurisdiction for the following communities: San Antonio Tlaltecahuacán, San Lorenzo Tlalmimilolpan, San Rafael, Santo Tomás Atzingo, Ranchería San José Zavaleta, La Ladrillera, Rancho Santa Cruz, Rancho Cuautenampa (La Quebradora), La Escondida, Rancho la Mesa, Santa María, Restaurante el Arenal (Familia Carbajal), El Brasero, Gavillero (Rancho el Gavillero), Lomas del Pedregal, Santiago (Kilómetro Cincuenta y Ocho y Medio), Fraccionamiento Valle Plateado, Villa Rincón de las Montañas, La Presa, Ejido San Lorenzo (La Cañada), Rancho de la Huerta, El Durazno, Rancho Santa Rita, Terreno San Luis (La Nopalera), Rancho la Joya (La Rosa), Vista Hermosa, Fraccionamiento Vergel de la Sierra, El Trapiche (Xacalco), Rancho Alfa y Omega, Colonia Ejidal (San Juan Atzacoaloya), El Faro, El Capulín, Tiro de las Palomas, Colonia el Magueyal Dos, Rancho Carvajal, Rancho San Luis, Rancho Fernando de la Machorra, Rancho la Encumbre, Colonia la Esperanza, Tepopotal and Fraccionamiento las Palomas.<ref name="inegi">{{cite web|url=http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |title=INEGI Census 2005 |language=es |access-date=2009-05-03 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110613183420/http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |archive-date=2011-06-13 }}</ref> Together, the municipality has a total area of {{cvt|158.76|km2}}<ref name="encmuc"/>  and a population of 43,930.<ref name="inegi"/>

Uncultivated areas are mostly pine forest, which cover about 65% of the municipality. Only 17.9% of the land is used for agriculture, mostly growing corn during the rainy season. The area also has some livestock raising. About 40% of the municipality's population is employed in industries such as paper and [[textile mill]]s as well as small workshops. There is logging for wood as well, but this is heavily managed.<ref name="encmuc"/> Tlalmanalco has been designated as a “sustainability center” by the Foundation for Sustainable Development in [[Mexico]] in cooperation with the state government and UAM. This is in response to the deterioration of the environment in this area, as well as population growth, leading to demand for more public services.<ref name="sustainable">{{cite web|url=http://www.fundacionsustentable.org/article1177-Tlalmanalco-ser%C3%A1-sede-de-centro-para-sustentabilidad.html |title=Tlalmanalco será sede de centro para sustentabilidad |access-date=2009-05-06 |url-status=usurped |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070609163002/http://www.fundacionsustentable.org/article1177-Tlalmanalco-ser%C3%A1-sede-de-centro-para-sustentabilidad.html |archive-date=June 9, 2007 }}</ref>

The major river here is called La Compañía, which has nine arroyos, one lake and seven aqueducts associated with it in the municipality. Much of the water flowing from this system comes from the Ayolotepito glacier.<ref name="encmuc"/> However, potable water availability is an issue here. In January 2008, 400 ranchers in the upper mountains of the municipality, who lack access to potable water themselves, protested and shut down a project to transport water to the lowlands and especially to the municipal seat. The protesters are demanding access to the water to be carried by the new line.<ref name="frenan">{{cite news |first=Rene |last=Ramon  |title= Frenan pobladores de Tlalmanalco trabajos de infraestructura hidráulica |url=http://www.jornada.unam.mx/2008/01/22/index.php?section=estados&article=032n2est |language=es |publisher=La Jornada |date=2008-01-22 |access-date=2016-07-29}}</ref>
[[File:MainStSanRafaelTlalMX.JPG|thumb|San Rafael]]
The most important community in the municipality outside of the seat is '''San Rafael''', located about 5&nbsp;km east of the town of Tlalmanalco at the foot of the Iztaccihuatl volcano<ref name="guia"/> The town essentially was created due to the placement of the San Rafael paper mill,<ref name="MexDesGuia"/> which reached its height of production between 1930 and 1970.<ref name="encmuc"/> Its old cinema and casino are still standing but empty, and there are a number of older houses that show the French influence that was popular in Mexico between the 19th and 20th centuries.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="MexDesGuia"/> The town is nearly surrounded by pine forest and there are many unpaved roads leading into it, most of which were cut by the paper mill.<ref name="guia"/>

The municipality has a number of elevations, including Teyotl, a 4700 meters and Tlatlachelo at 3175 meters. The latter has some [[Toltec]]-era shrines at its peak.<ref name="guia"/>
