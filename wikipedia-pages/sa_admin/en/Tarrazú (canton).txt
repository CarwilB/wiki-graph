'''Tarrazú''' is the fifth [[Cantons of Costa Rica|canton]] in the province of [[San José Province|San José]] in [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The head city of the canton is [[San Marcos, Costa Rica|San Marcos]]. It is part of [[Los Santos Zone]], together with [[Dota (canton)|Dota]] and [[León Cortés Castro (canton)|León Cortés Castro]].

==History==
The canton was established by a legislative decree of 7 August 1868.<ref name="crevoterritorial">{{cite book |last1=Hernández |first1=Hermógenes |title=Costa Rica: evolución territorial y principales censos de población 1502 - 1984 |date=1985 |publisher=Editorial Universidad Estatal a Distancia |location=San José |isbn=9977-64-243-5 |pages=164–173 |edition=1 |url=https://ccp.ucr.ac.cr/bvp/mapoteca/CostaRica/generales/atlas_censal/ |access-date=5 October 2020 |language=es |archive-date=8 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201008142102/https://ccp.ucr.ac.cr/bvp/mapoteca/CostaRica/generales/atlas_censal/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> It was part of the [[Desamparados (canton)|Desamparados]] canton by that time.

== Geography ==
[[Image:Tarrazu mountains near Quepos Costa Rica.JPG|thumb|left|Pacific Ocean in the distance, as seen from Tarrazú.]]
Tarrazú has an area of {{convert|291.27|km2}} and a mean elevation of {{convert|1471|m}}.<ref name="ignarea">{{Cite web |author=Instituto Geográfico Nacional de Costa Rica |date=20 June 2024 |title=División Territorial Administrativa, 2024 – Totales de Provincias, Cantones y Distritos de Costa Rica |trans-title=Administrative Territorial Division, 2024 – Totals of Provinces, Cantons and Districts of Costa Rica |url=https://files.snitcr.go.cr/boletines/DTA-TABLA%20POR%20PROVINCIA-CANT%C3%93N-DISTRITO%202024.pdf |language=es |format=PDF}}</ref><ref name="divadm"/>

The [[Pirris River|Pirrís River]] (also known as Parrita River) establishes much of the northern boundary of the three-pronged canton, which reaches south across the Coastal Mountain Range to its border with the canton of [[Quepos (canton)|Quepos]] in [[Puntarenas Province]].

Tarrazú is located about {{convert|70|km}} south of the capital, [[San José, Costa Rica|San José]], in a pristine valley surrounded by mountains that are part of the [[Cordillera de Talamanca|Talamanca Sierra]] in southern Costa Rica. Downtown San Marcos is {{convert|1,350|m}} above sea level but is surrounded by peaks as high as {{convert|3,000|m}} above sea level.

==Economy==
Highland coffee is the main source of income of local people. However, tourism and avocado production are of increased importance. During the months of December, January and February, population increases three-fold due to harvest time. San Marcos, the biggest town in the region, has become the center of economic activity.

More recently, the [[Costa Rican Institute of Electricity]] has invested millions of dollars in the Pirrís Hydro Dam, scheduled to start generating energy by March 2011. The Hydro Dam is now the highest structure of its kind in Central America and will be key to the economic development of the region, not only in the highlands but the coastal regions of [[Quepos (canton)|Quepos]] and [[Parrita]].

The region is deeply dependent on remittances from emigres who live in the United States, namely [[New Jersey]]. Few households in the region do not have a family member who lives and works in the United States. This outmigration has led to an influx of thousands of Panamanian laborers to help realize the annual coffee harvest.<ref>Kordick, Carmen. The Saints of Progress: Coffee, Migration, and Costa Rican National Identity. University of Alabama Press, 2019.</ref>

Tarrazú, particularly the San Lorenzo area, is felt to produce the most desirable coffee in Costa Rica.<ref>{{cite book |url= https://books.google.com/books?id=us6pBFbdb3UC&pg=PA146 |title=Labels of Origin for Food: Local Development, Global Recognition |page=146 |author=Elizabeth Barham, Bertil Sylvander|publisher=CABI |date= 24 June 2011 |access-date=7 July 2012}}</ref> ''Finca Palmilera'' coffee is grown here.<ref>[http://www.roystreetcoffee.com/2012/11/16/costa-rica-finca-palmilera/ "Costa Rica Finca Palmilera"], Roy Street Coffee and Tea. Retrieved 16 November 2012.</ref> In November 2012, it was the most expensive coffee sold in [[Starbucks]] coffee shops in the United States.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://business.time.com/2012/11/30/the-7-cup-of-starbucks-the-culmination-of-the-coffee-chains-long-term-strategy/|title=The $7 Cup of Starbucks: A Logical Extension of the Coffee Chain’s Long-Term Strategy|work=Time|author=Josh Sanburn|date=30 November 2012}}</ref>
