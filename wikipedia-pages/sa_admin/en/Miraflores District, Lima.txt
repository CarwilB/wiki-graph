'''Miraflores''' ({{IPA|es|miɾaˈfloɾes|lang}}) is a [[List of districts of Lima|district of Lima]], [[Peru]]. A residential and upscale shopping district, it is one of the wealthiest districts that make up the city of [[Lima]], located to the south of the city's [[Historic Centre of Lima|historic downtown area]], as well as one of its main tourist destinations. The area also hosts office buildings and flat-roofed multi-coloured housing.<ref name=rg />

It was first established as a district on January 2, 1857. As a result of the January 1881 [[Battle of Miraflores]] fought during the [[War of the Pacific]], Miraflores is called ''Ciudad Heroica'' ("Heroic City").

== Etymology ==
The city, founded as '''San Miguel de Miraflores''',<ref name=Hist>{{Cite web |title=Historia |url=https://www.miraflores.gob.pe/la-ciudad/historia/ |website=Municipalidad de Miraflores}}</ref> acquired its present name ({{lit|look at the flowers}}) because of the nearly year-round [[bougainvillea]] bloom, even in the mid-16th century, when it was still an [[Inca Empire|Inca]] village.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Miraflores {{!}} Location, History, Description, & Facts {{!}} Britannica |url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Miraflores |access-date=2025-04-19 |website=www.britannica.com |language=en}}</ref>

== History ==
[[File:Huaca Pucllana Miraflores Lima PE.jpg|thumb|300px|left|[[Huaca Pucllana]], Miraflores]]
Prior to the arrival of the Spanish, Miraflores was one of the locations inhabited by members of the ancient [[Lima culture]], which preceded the [[Inca Empire]]. The ''[[Huaca Pucllana]]'' (formerly Hispanicized as ''Juliana'') serves as its only remaining remnant in the district, originally much larger in size.<ref name=PDL2017>{{Cite web |title=Plan de Desarrollo Local Concertado del Distrito de Miraflores: 2017 - 2021 |url=https://www.miraflores.gob.pe/Gestorw3b/files/pdf/9708-21538-Plan%20de%20Desarrollo%20Local%20Concertado%20-%20Final.pdf |website=Municipalidad de Miraflores |page=35, 68–69}}</ref>

Once [[Viceroyalty of Peru|Spanish rule]] was established, the area became part of the district of [[Pueblo Libre District, Lima|Magdalena Vieja]].<ref name=Hist/> Established as ''San Miguel de Miraflores'', it takes its name from the [[Miraflores Charterhouse]], having become a district on {{dts|1857|01|02}} under the presidency of [[Ramón Castilla]] and containing a number of estates (Armendáriz, Balconcillo, Barboncito, La Palma, [[San Isidro District, Lima|Conde San Isidro]], [[Lince District|Lince]], Limatambo, Santa Cruz and [[Chacarilla del Estanque|Chacarilla]]), as well as the lands at Leuro and Ocharán.

Miraflores was the site of encounters between [[José de San Martín]] and [[Joaquín de la Pezuela, 1st Marquess of Viluma|Viceroy Pezuela]] that took place during the [[Peruvian War of Independence]], on {{dts|1820|09|24}}. During the [[War of the Pacific]], the district was the site of the [[Battle of Miraflores|eponymous battle]], where two thousand people died as a result and the district was sacked and burned by the [[Chilean Army]]. In 1898, it was limited to the north by the lands of the [[Surquillo]] estate, to the west by Bellavista street, to the east by the railroad tracks that reached Lima and to the south by the first blocks of [[Avenida Larco|Larco]] and Porta streets.
[[File:Ceremonia de entrega del Puente Mellizo del Villena Rey (13).jpg|thumb|Puente Villena Rey]]
As the city of Lima grew, Miraflores was absorbed into its [[Lima Metropolitan Area|metropolitan area]] during the early 20th century. Prior to the 1920s, its territory included the current districts of [[La Victoria District, Lima|La Victoria]] (1920), [[San Isidro District, Lima|San Isidro]] (1931), [[Lince District|Lince]] (1936), part of [[Santiago de Surco]] (1944), [[Surquillo]] (1949), [[San Luis District, Lima|San Luis]] (1968) and [[San Borja District|San Borja]] (1983).

During the 1940s, the ''Iglesia Matriz Virgen Milagrosa'' was built next to the district's [[Miraflores Central Park|central park]],<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.miraflores.gob.pe/la-ciudad/iglesias-y-templos/ |title=Iglesias y Templos |website=Municipalidad de Miraflores}}</ref> replacing the original San Miguel de Miraflores Church.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Miraflores: realizan misa al aire libre en los exteriores de parroquia La Virgen Milagrosa y con presencia de feligreses |date=2020-11-26 |url=https://elcomercio.pe/lima/sucesos/miraflores-realizan-misa-al-aire-libre-en-los-exteriores-de-la-parroquia-la-virgen-milagrosa-y-con-presencia-de-feligreses-covid-19-coronavirus-nndc-noticia/ |work=[[El Comercio (Peru)|El Comercio]]}}</ref>

On {{dts|1992|07|16}}, during the [[Internal Conflict in Peru|era of terrorism]], a [[Calle Tarata|street]] in the district was targeted in a [[Tarata bombing|terrorist attack]] perpetrated by the [[Shining Path]], which led to a crackdown on Peruvian insurgent groups, culminating in the group's leader being captured on September of the same year.{{cn|date=February 2025}} The [[Tarata bombing]] was one of the largest attacks in the country and occurred near [[Avenida Larco]]. A monument was constructed in the site of the attack.

== Politics ==
Miraflores is under the jurisdiction of its own [[Municipalities of Peru|district municipality]], as well as that of the [[Metropolitan Municipality of Lima]].

== Geography ==
[[File: Parque Kennedy, Lima, 2017-02-28.jpg|285px|thumb|right|[[Miraflores Central Park]] (Parque Kennedy).]]
The district has a total land area of 9.62&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. Its administrative center is located 79 meters above sea level.

=== Boundaries ===
* North: [[San Isidro District, Lima|San Isidro]] and [[Surquillo]]
* East: Surquillo and [[Santiago de Surco]]
* South: [[Barranco District|Barranco]] and Santiago de Surco
* West: Pacific Ocean

=== Climate ===
Miraflores has a marine climate, characterized by mild, humid, and comfortable conditions. Temperatures oscillate from {{convert|13|°C|°F|abbr=on}} to {{convert|18|°C|°F|abbr=on}} in winter, and from {{convert|20|°C|°F|abbr=on}} to {{convert|30|°C|°F|abbr=on}} in summer. Low clouds are frequent, especially during winter, when morning drizzles are not uncommon. Heavy rain is almost unseen.

== Ecology ==
Miraflores is a biodiverse district, with a large variety of animal species, including those off its coast, and over 200 species of plants.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Programa “Naturaliza Miraflores” |url=https://www.gob.pe/17370-programa-naturaliza-miraflores |date=2024-01-14 |website=[[Gob.pe]]}}</ref><ref name=Andina-2026>{{Cite news |title=Miraflores: científicos identifican más de 200 especies de plantas en emblemático distrito |date=2026-04-09 |url=https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-miraflores-cientificos-identifican-mas-200-especies-plantas-emblematico-distrito-1070279.aspx |work=[[Andina (news agency)|Andina]]}}</ref> As of 2026, most plant species have been introduced into the country, while only a fifth are native to Peru.<ref name=Andina-2026/> The most prevalent species are ''[[Schinus terebinthifolia]]'', ''[[Tecomaria capensis]]'', ''[[Schinus molle]]'', and ''[[Tecoma stans]]''.<ref name=Andina-2026/>

== Demographics ==
According to the [[2017 Peruvian census|2017 national census]] done by the [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática|INEI]], the district has 99,337 inhabitants and a population density of 10,326 persons per km<sup>2</sup> (26,744 persons per sq mile). Miraflores has a high [[Human Development Index]] at 0.986 and the lowest population living below the poverty line in Lima at 1.80%.<ref name="info">"[http://www.miraflores.gob.pe/_contenTempl1.php?idpadre=4951&idhijo=4972&idcontenido=5398 Informacion General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180220231010/http://www.miraflores.gob.pe/_contenTempl1.php?idpadre=4951&idhijo=4972&idcontenido=5398 |date=2018-02-20 }}" ''[[INEI]]''. Retrieved on 16 September 2017.</ref> Along with its northern neighbor, [[San Isidro District, Lima|San Isidro]], Miraflores is inhabited primarily by upper-class residents and is consistently listed as one of the most expensive districts in the country.

=== Education ===
Miraflores had 128 private schools and 12 public schools in 2010.<ref name="info"/>

== Culture ==
[[File:Develación de busto y homenaje a José Matías Delgado prócer salvadoreño en el parque Centroamérica (9).jpg|thumb|Unveiling of a bust of [[José Matías Delgado]] at Centroamérica Park.]]
The district has a pre-[[Inca]] mud-brick temple called the [[Huaca Pucllana]], one of a number of [[archeology|archeological]] sites found in Lima.<ref  name=rg>Jenkins, D., ''The Rough Guide to Peru'', fifth edition (September 2003), p. 82</ref>

=== Landmarks ===
Miraflores is home to a number of important locations, including buildings that have since been declared part of the [[cultural heritage of Peru]].<ref name=Yumpu>{{Cite book |title=Inventario del Patrimonio Edificado en el distrito de Miraflores |last=Fernández Gonzales |first=Ruth |publisher=Municipalidad de Miraflores |year=2022 |language=es |url=https://www.miraflores.gob.pe/desarrollo-urbano/inventario-del-patrimonio-edificado-en-el-distrito-de-miraflores/}}</ref>
