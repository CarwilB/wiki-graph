'''Amapala''' is a [[Municipalities of Honduras|municipality]] in the [[Honduras|Honduran]] [[Departments of Honduras|department]] of [[Valle Department|Valle]].

It is formed by [[Tiger Island|El Tigre Island]] and its satellite islets and rocks in the [[Gulf of Fonseca]]. It has an area of {{Convert|75.2|km2||abbr=on}} and a population of 2,482 as of the [[census]] of 2001 (of which 4 people were living on Isla Comandante). Thanks to a natural deep channel, and despite lacking modern infrastructure, Amapala long served as the main Honduran port in the Pacific Ocean.

==History==
The local legend has it that the name of the island on which Amapala is based is due to the fact that in the 16th century a group of pirates led by the pirate [[Francis Drake]] operated on the island. He and his men were considered by the inhabitants of the area as wild and bloody beasts, so they called the island "Cerro de El Tigre". In fact Drake probably never stayed here though some later pirates, like [[William Dampier]], did.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wallace |first=Arturo |date=2017-11-24|title=Las leyendas de Amapala, la olvidada capital de Centroamérica por la que dicen que pasaron el pirata Drake y Albert Einstein |url=https://www.bbc.com/mundo/noticias-america-latina-41588378}}</ref> The name Amapala derives from [[Nahuatl]] and means "close to amates". However, there is an alternate theory that states that it comes from the words "''ama''" (corn) and "''palha''" (hill) of the [[Honduran Lenca|Goajiquiro dialect]], and therefore would mean "hill of corn".<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Murillo Barahona |first=Douglas Javier |date=2017-05-04 |title=Construcción de un puente como vía de comunicación terrestre entre tierra firme en Coyolito y la Isla de Amapala, Honduras |url=https://tzibalnaah.unah.edu.hn/xmlui/handle/123456789/5380 |journal=Tzibalnaah |language=es}}</ref>

Amapala was founded in 1838, and its port was opened and declared free in 1868. During the 19th century it exported large quantities of gold, silver and other ores, although its progress was retarded by the delay in constructing a transcontinental railway from [[Puerto Cortés]].<ref name="EB1911">{{Cite book |last=Chisholm |first=Hugh |url=https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/007910230 |title=The Encyclopaedia Britannica: a dictionary of arts, sciences, literature and general information |publisher=Encyclopaedia Britannica |year=1911 |series=Encyclopedia Britannica |location=New York |language=en}}</ref> By the mid-1970s, Amapala was gradually replaced in importance by the port of [[San Lorenzo, Valle|San Lorenzo]] on the mainland.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Morris |first=James A. |url=https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780429048586/honduras-james-morris |title=Honduras: Caudillo Politics And Military Rulers |date=2019-09-27 |publisher=Routledge |isbn=978-0-429-04858-6 |location=New York |doi=10.4324/9780429048586}}</ref>

A description of the town in 1881 can be found in the book ''A Lady's Ride Across Spanish Honduras'' by Mary Lester (a.k.a. Mary Soltera).<ref>{{Cite book |last=Soltera |first=Maria |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=xfFRAQAAIAAJ&dq=A+Lady%27s+Ride+Across+Spanish+Honduras&pg=PA189 |title=A Lady's Ride Across Spanish Honduras |date=1884 |publisher=W. Blackwood & sons |language=en}}</ref> 

In 1895, [[El Salvador]], Honduras, and [[Nicaragua]] signed the Treaty of Amapala that established the short-lived [[Greater Republic of Central America]].<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Perry |first=Edward |date=1922 |title=Central American Union |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/2505979 |journal=The Hispanic American Historical Review |volume=5 |issue=1 |pages=30–51 |doi=10.2307/2505979 |jstor=2505979 |issn=0018-2168|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

==Demographics==
At the time of the 2013 Honduras census, Amapala municipality had a population of 12,250. Of these, 74.16% were [[Mestizo]], 22.81% [[White Latin Americans|White]], 1.55% [[Afro-Hondurans|Black or Afro-Honduran]], 0.09% [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and 1.39% others.<ref>[https://www.ine.gob.hn/V3/baseine/ Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE), bases de datos en línea]</ref>

==Economy==
The community receives funds from former residents who have moved to other countries. {{As of|2013}} most residents work as fishermen, while 300 have formal jobs.<ref>{{cite magazine|author=Mackey, Danielle Marie|url=https://newrepublic.com/article/120559/honduras-charter-cities-spearheaded-us-conservatives-libertarians|title="I've Seen All Sorts of Horrific Things in My Time.
