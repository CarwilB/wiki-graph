'''El Hatillo Municipality''' ({{langx|es|[[Municipio]] El Hatillo}}) is an [[administrative division]] of the [[Miranda State|State of Miranda]], [[Venezuela]]; along with [[Baruta Municipality|Baruta]], [[Chacao Municipality|Chacao]], [[Libertador Municipality (Venezuelan Capital District)|Libertador]] and [[Sucre Municipality, Miranda|Sucre]], it is one of the five [[municipality|municipalities]] of [[Caracas]], the [[Capital (political)|capital]] of Venezuela. It is located in the southeastern area of Caracas, and in the northwestern part of the State of Miranda.

The [[County seat|seat]] of the [[municipal government]] is [[El Hatillo Town, Miranda|El Hatillo Town]], founded in 1784 by [[Don Baltasar de León]], who was instrumental in the area's development. Although the town had its origins during the [[Spanish colonisation of the Americas|Spanish colonisation]], the municipality was not established until 1991. In 2000 – the year after a new [[Constitution of Venezuela|constitution]] was enacted in Venezuela – some of the municipality functions were delegated to a consolidated mayor's office called ''Alcaldía Mayor'', which also has some authority over the other four municipalities of Caracas.

El Hatillo has some of its [[colonial architecture]], including an 18th-century [[parish church]] and a unique [[Romanian Orthodox Church]]. The municipality also has a rich artistic culture, with at least two important musical festivals celebrated yearly, and numerous holiday celebrations reflecting the heritage of El Hatillo. The culture, the pleasant temperature, the rural landscape, and the [[gastronomy]] of the municipality have made it a place of interest for [[tourism|visitors]] to the city, and a desirable place to live.<ref name=nuevaesparta>{{cite web|author=Universidad Nueva Esparta |url=http://www.une.edu.ve/hatillo/historia.htm |title=Alcaldía del Hatillo:Historia |access-date=2006-03-28 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060428111205/http://www.une.edu.ve/hatillo/historia.htm |archive-date=2006-04-28 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref><ref name=estampasfood>{{cite journal |last=de los Ángeles Herrera |first=María |title=Redescubra los sabores de El Hatillo |journal=Estampas |pages=18–24 |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=2006-09-03 |url=http://www.eluniversal.com/estampas/anteriores/030906/encuentros.shtml |access-date=2006-12-01 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070524095903/http://www.eluniversal.com/estampas/anteriores/030906/encuentros.shtml |archive-date=2007-05-24 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref> The municipality receives a part of its income from tourism, an activity that is promoted by the government.<ref name=tourism>{{cite web|url=http://www.elhatillo.gov.ve/turismo.html |title=Turismo |year=2004 |author=Alcaldía Municipio El Hatillo |access-date=2006-03-29 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060720084240/http://www.elhatillo.gov.ve/turismo.html |archive-date=2006-07-20 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref>

Although commercial areas are growing rapidly, [[agriculture]] remains a foundation of the economy in the [[rural]] areas of the southern part of El Hatillo. The business sector remains mostly underdeveloped, causing heavy [[employee]] movement in and out of the municipality – a problem that has made the transportation infrastructure of El Hatillo very congested.<ref name=officedeficit>{{cite news |url=http://www.eluniversal.com/2006/10/31/ccs_apo_51878B.shtml |title=En contra |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=2001-10-31 |access-date=2006-11-13 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080125114417/http://www.eluniversal.com/2006/10/31/ccs_apo_51878B.shtml |archive-date=2008-01-25 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

==History==
In the 16th century, when the [[Spanish colonisation of the Americas|Spanish colonisation]] in the area began, El Hatillo was inhabited by the [[Mariches]], a tribe of [[Indigenous people of the Americas|indigenous people]] possibly related to the [[Kalina people|Kalina]] (Caribs). Cacique [[Tamanaco]] was the leader of these tribes, known for [[Resistance movement|resisting]] the Spanish colonisation. As the colonisation developed, the indigenous inhabitants were killed; by order of Caracas's founder [[Diego de Losada]], Tamanaco was also murdered.<ref name=nuevaesparta/>

[[File:Don baltasar sign.jpg|thumb|right|A plaque in front of Baltasar's house describes how he donated land to construct El Hatillo Town (click to read translation).]]
In 1752, [[Don Baltasar de León|Don Baltasar de León García]]<ref name=baltasarnote2>''Baltasar'' is sometimes spelled ''Balthasar'', ''Balthazar'' or ''Baltazar''.</ref> arrived to El Hatillo from [[Cádiz]], Spain, having just completed a prison term at ''La Carraca'', Spain, for opposing (with his father) the monopoly rules of [[Guipuzcoana Company]], which was in charge of maintaining exclusive trade between Spain and Venezuela.<ref name=baltasarinsurgency>{{cite web |author=GD Solutions C.A. |url=http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/history_en.shtml |title=Breve historia del Municipio El Hatillo |access-date=2007-01-15 |date=2002-04-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20061207102619/http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/history_en.shtml| archive-date = December 7, 2006}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> Don Baltasar founded El Hatillo Town, becoming one of the most significant contributors to its early development. Don Baltasar focused on making El Hatillo a strong, united and independent community,<ref name=buro>{{cite news|url=http://www.burodevenezuela.com/newspub/story.cfm?ID=10716 |title=El Hatillo: Un pueblo colonial anclado en la gran metrópoli |last=Lara Mendoza |first=Beatriz |publisher=Diario El Aragüeño Digital |date=2003-09-28 |access-date=2006-07-19 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928181634/http://www.burodevenezuela.com/newspub/story.cfm?ID=10716 |archive-date=September 28, 2007 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> aiming to establish the area as a distinct [[Parish (subnational entity)|parish]] from Baruta, on which El Hatillo depended. He accomplished this on June 12, 1784, when the governor and the [[bishop]] agreed to declare El Hatillo autonomous and under the direction of Don Baltasar, in front of 180 [[Canary Islands|Canary]]-descendant families;  this date is accepted as the foundation date of El Hatillo Town.<ref name=historyalcaldia>{{cite web |url=http://www.elhatillo.gov.ve/historia.html |title=Municipio El Hatillo: Historia |year=2004 |author=Alcaldía El Hatillo |access-date=2006-03-28 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060512173227/http://elhatillo.gov.ve/historia.html |archive-date=2006-05-12 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

That same year, Don Baltasar and his [[brother-in-law]] donated their properties to the town, and an engineer assisted in the urban planning, which included [[Grid plan|grid streets]] and a [[parish church]].<ref name=historyalcaldia/> The church was built to honor [[Saint Rosalia|Santa Rosalía de Palermo]], who Baltasar believed had saved him from a plague that killed his father in prison.<ref name=starosalia>{{cite web |author=GD Solutions C.A. |url=http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/starosalia_en.shtml |title=Sta. Rosalía de Palermo: valores religiosos |access-date=2006-07-15 |date=2002-04-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060503141020/http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/starosalia_en.shtml| archive-date = May 3, 2006}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> In 1803, at the age of 79, Don Baltasar was unexpectedly killed in a horse accident.<ref name=5catalogo0405>{{cite book|author=Instituto del Patrimonio Cultural |title=Catalogo del Patrimonio Cultural Venezolano 2004–2005. Municipio El Hatillo |year=2005 |publisher=Ministerio de la Cultura |location=Caracas |isbn=978-980-6448-21-6 |url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |access-date=2006-12-15 |chapter=La Creación Individual (PDF) |chapter-url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/5_La%20Creacion%20Individual_El%20Hatillo.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070515080719/http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |archive-date=May 15, 2007 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

In 1809, [[landlord]] and [[Lieutenant Colonel]] Manuel Escalona<ref name=anthem>{{cite web |author=GD Solutions C.A. |url=http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/himno_es.shtml |title=El himno de El Hatillo |access-date=2007-01-27 |date=2002-04-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070124060804/http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/himno_es.shtml| archive-date = January 24, 2007}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> achieved the separation of El Hatillo from [[Petare]], another suburb of Caracas, making it a different ''Tenientazgo de Justicia'' – a type of administrative division at the time. On April 19, 1810, Escalona enjoined the town to the movement of independence under [[Simón Bolívar]], becoming another important person in the history of the municipality.<ref name=caracasvirtual>{{cite web |url=http://www.caracasvirtual.com/elhatillo.asp |title=El Hatillo |year=2004 |author=Caracas Virtual |access-date=2006-03-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060226192228/http://www.caracasvirtual.com/elhatillo.asp <!--Added by H3llBot--> |archive-date=2006-02-26}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

Ana Francisca Pérez García, Don Baltasar's wife, was a noteworthy woman in El Hatillo, attending to community children, elders and ill citizens. She donated a considerable amount of money for the construction of a hospital in Petare after the [[1812 Caracas earthquake|1812 earthquake]]; this hospital is currently known as the Pérez de León de Petare Hospital.<ref name=caracasvirtual/>

<!-- Deleted image removed: [[File:Vistahatillo1940.jpg|thumb|left|200px|El Hatillo in the 1940s.]] -->
One of the most ambitious urbanisation projects in El Hatillo since its founding was the neighborhood called La Lagunita. In the 1950s and 1960s, La Lagunita S.A. constructed a "functional, futuristic and comfortable" residential zone. To encourage people to settle in the area, each [[land lot|parcel]] included a membership to Lagunita Country Club, which was officially opened in 1964. [[Brazil]]ian [[landscape designer]] [[Roberto Burle Marx]] contributed to this project, constructed on the 4.3 million m<sup>2</sup> [[hacienda]] that once belonged to former Venezuelan [[President of Venezuela|president]] [[Eleazar López Contreras]].<ref name=lalagunita>{{cite web |author=Concalpro Group |url=http://www.800venga.com/indexHLL_history.html |title=Breve historia de la urbanización La Lagunita |access-date=2006-12-02 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060211112620/http://www.800venga.com/indexHLL_history.html |archive-date=2006-02-11 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> La Lagunita has since become a wealthy neighborhood of El Hatillo.

Although El Hatillo has been independent from Petare since 1809, it later became part of Sucre Municipality, where Petare is located. On November 19, 1991, Miranda's Legislative Assembly gave El Hatillo full autonomy, making it an independent municipality; this decision was issued in ''Gaceta Oficial'' on January 17, 1992.<ref name=gaceta>{{cite web|url=http://www.ine.gov.ve/division/dpt.asp?sql=select+*+from+operatv+WHERE+CodigoAux+%3D+%27150000%27&ScrollAction=Pagina+3&Entid=150000 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071009070814/http://www.ine.gov.ve/division/dpt.asp?sql=select%2B%2A%2Bfrom%2Boperatv%2BWHERE%2BCodigoAux%2B%3D%2B%27150000%27&ScrollAction=Pagina%2B3&Entid=150000 |url-status=dead |archive-date=2007-10-09 |title=División Político Territorial de Venezuela, 2003 |year=2003 |author=Instituto Nacional de Estadística |access-date=2006-07-19 }}   {{in lang|es}}</ref> In 1993, Mercedes Hernández de Silva was elected the first [[mayor]] of El Hatillo.<ref name=historyalcaldia/> Since 2000, the ''Alcaldía Mayor'' manages some of the functions of the municipality.<ref name=gaceta2000>{{cite web |url=http://www.tsj.gov.ve/gaceta/Marzo/080300/080300-36906-00.html |title=Gaceta Oficial N° 36,906 |date=2000-03-08 |author=Tribunal Supremo de Justicia |access-date=2006-07-19 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060415030651/http://www.tsj.gov.ve/gaceta/marzo/080300/080300-36906-00.html |archive-date=2006-04-15 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

The municipality was the target of airstrikes by the [[United States Air Force]] during the [[2026 United States intervention in Venezuela]]. One of the strikes [[Death of Yohana Rodríguez|killed a Colombian woman]] who lived with her daughter on a rural house. The woman, Yohana Rodríguez, was one of the two civilian casualties during the attacks.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Hernández Bonilla |first1=Juan Miguel |title=La trágica historia de Yohana Rodríguez, una colombiana que murió en los bombardeos de Trump en Venezuela |url=https://elpais.com/america-colombia/2026-01-05/la-tragica-historia-de-yohana-rodriguez-una-colombiana-que-murio-en-los-bombardeos-de-trump-en-venezuela.html |access-date=13 January 2026 |work=[[El País]] |date=5 January 2026 |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Pardo |first1=Jhoan |title=Ella es Yohana Rodríguez, la colombiana que habría fallecido en bombardeos en Venezuela para capturar a Maduro |url=https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/01/05/ella-es-yohana-rodriguez-la-colombiana-que-habria-fallecido-en-bombardeos-en-venezuela-para-capturar-a-maduro/ |access-date=13 January 2026 |work=[[Infobae]] |date=5 January 2026 |language=es}}</ref>

==Geography==
[[File:Hatillosatellite.png|thumb|200px|left|Simulated-color [[Landsat 7]] satellite image of El Hatillo and surrounding areas.]]  El Hatillo Municipality lies at the southeast area of the city of Caracas and at the northwest corner of the State of Miranda; it is one of the state's 21 municipalities. El Hatillo is also within the jurisdiction of the ''Alcaldía Mayor'', which has power over three adjacent municipalities of Miranda, and over [[Libertador Municipality (Venezuelan Capital District)|Libertador Municipality]] in the [[Venezuelan Capital District|Capital District]]. These five municipalities make up the city of Caracas. El Hatillo has a land size of {{convert|114|km2|sqmi}}<ref name=geography>{{cite web |url=http://www.elhatillo.gov.ve/geografia.html |title=Municipio El Hatillo: Geografía |year=2004 |author=Alcaldía El Hatillo |access-date=2006-03-29 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060512173241/http://elhatillo.gov.ve/geografia.html |archive-date=2006-05-12 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> – the third largest municipality in the capital.

The municipality's natural southern boundary is the Turgua range, spanning east to west and separating El Hatillo from the Baruta and Paz Castillo municipalities. Parallel to Turgua in the north is the Sabaneta range; the Prepo stream runs between the two ranges. North of the Sabaneta range, the Prepo stream feeds into the Tusmare stream, which ends in the Guaire river.<ref name=geography/><ref name=aves>{{cite book |last=Blanco Dávila |first=Alberto |title=El Hatillo. Destino Ecoturístico para observadores de aves |publisher=Fonpromitur}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>
[[File:Elhatillomap.png|thumb|200px|right|[[Topographic map]] of El Hatillo.]]

La Guairita stream flows into the Guaire in northeastern El Hatillo. The Guaire river is the eastern limit of the municipality, separating it from Sucre and Paz Castillo in the southeastern sector of El Hatillo. La Guairita serves as the northern boundary between El Hatillo and the municipalities of Baruta and Sucre. Limiting Baruta to the west, the boundaries of the municipality follow [[Picacho de El Volcán|El Volcán]], Pariaguán, La Mata and other peaks until they meet Turgua range in southeastern El Hatillo.<ref name=geography/><ref name=aves/>

The tallest [[mountain|peak]] in El Hatillo is [[Picacho de El Volcán]] (Spanish for "Peak of the Volcano"), at 1,490&nbsp;meters (4,888&nbsp;ft) above sea level, from where radio, television and telecommunication antennas serve Caracas. Despite its name, the mountain has had no recorded volcanic history. Other significant mountains in El Hatillo are Gavilán at {{convert|1148|m|ft}}, Topo de El Paují at {{convert|1245|m|ft}} and Topo de Piedras Pintadas at {{convert|1196|m|ft}}.<ref name=geography/>

===Environment===
[[Image:Tiendas en Calle La Paz - El Hatillo 2013 001.JPG|thumb|Shops into the colonial houses]]
El Hatillo, at a higher altitude than the neighboring municipalities of Caracas, has slightly cooler weather than nearby downtown Caracas. The average temperature is between 21 and 24 degrees Celsius (70–75&nbsp;°F). At the highest elevations, the temperature can decrease to 18&nbsp;°C (64&nbsp;°F) and the atmosphere may have constant [[fog]]. The mean [[precipitation (meteorology)|precipitation]] is 997.3&nbsp;millimeters a year (39&nbsp;inches); annual values can range from 800 to 1,500&nbsp;millimeters (32&nbsp;in to 59&nbsp;in). The average [[humidity]] index is 75%; similar to the rest of Venezuela, the rainy season is May through November, while all other months are considered the dry season. The winds are north alizé [[trade winds]].<ref name=aves/><ref name=2catalogo0405>{{cite book|author=Instituto del Patrimonio Cultural |title=Catalogo del Patrimonio Cultural Venezolano 2004–2005. Municipio El Hatillo |year=2005 |publisher=Ministerio de la Cultura |location=Caracas |isbn=978-980-6448-21-6 |url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |access-date=2006-12-15 |chapter=Mapas de Ubicación (PDF) |chapter-url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/2_Mapas_El%20Hatillo1.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070515080719/http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |archive-date=May 15, 2007 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

Concentrated near [[water bodies]], the [[vegetation]] in most of El Hatillo is dense [[forest]]s, occupying around 30% of the surface. Smaller plants like [[shrub]]s take around 9% and [[herb]]s occupy a similar percentage. In 1972, the forests of El Hatillo were declared a protected zone of the metropolitan area of Caracas.<ref name=aves/>

The municipality is home to a wide range of [[bird]] [[species]], with more than two hundred registered, including [[American Kestrel|sparrowhawks]], [[eagle]]s, [[falcon]]s, and [[owl]]s. [[Birdwatching]] in the area is promoted by the authorities of Miranda, who have also supported conservation of these species.<ref name=aves/>

In the 2001 National Institute of Statistics [[census]], El Hatillo Municipality had 54,226 inhabitants,<ref name=census>{{cite web |author=Instituto Nacional de Estadística |url=http://www.ine.gov.ve/censo/fichascenso/tiro.asp?cod_entidad=15 |title=Primeros Resultados XIII Censo General de Población y Vivienda |access-date=2006-09-16 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060901003846/http://www.ine.gov.ve/censo/fichascenso/tiro.asp?cod_entidad=15 |archive-date=2006-09-01 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref> but demographics show a rapidly rising population. ===Neighborhoods===
Although there are no defined limits for the [[neighborhood]]s of El Hatillo, the government website divides the municipality into [[urban area|urban]] and [[rural]]. Concentrated in the northern region of the municipality, the urban neighborhoods are El Hatillo Town, El Calvario, La Lagunita, Alto Hatillo, La Boyera, Las Marías, Oripoto, Los Pomelos, Los Naranjos, Los Geranios, La Cabaña, Cerro Verde, Llano Verde, Colinas, Vista El Valle, Los Olivos, and El Cigarral. The rural localities of the municipality are located in southern El Hatillo; these are La Unión, Corralito, Turgua, La Hoyadita, Sabaneta, La Mata, Caicaguana, and Altos de Halcón.<ref name=geography/>

==Economy==
{{update-section|date=November 2020}}
[[File:Paseo el hatillo.jpg|thumb|right|300px|Paseo El Hatillo [[shopping mall]].]]
The economy of El Hatillo Municipality consists of three sectors:  the [[Commerce|commercial]] sector, which has been growing along with the population increase and is primarily represented by shopping malls and retail stores around the urban areas most of which sell typical crafts of the country; [[agricultural]], in the southern half of the municipality and existing since the founding of El Hatillo; and  [[tourism]], which contributes significantly to El Hatillo's income and is promoted by the government.<ref name=2catalogo0405/>

El Hatillo is an accessible day visit destination for people from Caracas; the municipality is only {{convert|15|km|mi|abbr=on}} southeast of downtown Caracas but in the mountains removed from the congested Caracas valley;<ref name=LP73>Dydynski (2004), p. 73.</ref> thus, development has focused on day tourism. The central town square – Plaza Bolívar – and its surroundings are well maintained, and the municipal government offers bus trips around the narrow streets for viewing the colonial architecture of the town.<ref name=tourism/> Handcrafted [[souvenir]]s and products are popular purchases, offered at local artisan shops, and there are numerous restaurants.<ref name=LP58>Dydynski (2004), p. 58.</ref> There are at least three cultural centers in the municipality that attract tourists and residents to music festivals and art expositions.

To support the increasing population, numerous [[shopping malls]] have been built in the municipality. Neighborhoods like La Lagunita, Los Naranjos and El Hatillo Town now offer large scale shopping malls with multiplex [[movie theaters]]. Since the 1980s, the older typical houses of El Hatillo Town have been converted to shops and restaurants, while preserving their colonial architecture.<ref name=shophouses>{{cite web |url=http://www.une.edu.ve/hatillo/recreacion.htm |title=Recreación |author=Universidad Nueva Esparta |access-date=2006-12-14 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070107063642/http://www.une.edu.ve/hatillo/recreacion.htm |archive-date=2007-01-07 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

Employment possibilities within El Hatillo – a [[Commuter town|bedroom community]] of Caracas – are reduced; businesses in the municipality are almost strictly commercial, and the economy has not expanded in other directions. Office space underdevelopment has resulted from a lack of land for large scale office construction, making it costly to locate large offices or businesses in the area. Those seeking employment in offices or larger businesses must look outside of El Hatillo, contributing to the high traffic to, from, and in El Hatillo.<ref name=officedeficit/>

==Law and government==
[[File:Logoalcaldiaelhatillo.gif|thumb|left|200px|[[Seal (emblem)|Seal]] of ''Alcaldía El Hatillo'', the mayor's office.]]
[[File:El Hatillo City Hall.jpg|thumb|left|200px|El Hatillo City Hall]]
Venezuelan law specifies that municipal governments have four main functions: executive, legislative, comptroller, and planning. The executive function is managed by the [[mayor]], who is in charge of representing the municipality's administration. The legislative branch is represented by the Municipal Council, composed of seven [[councillors]], charged with the deliberation of new decrees and local laws. The comptroller tasks are managed by the municipal [[comptroller]]'s office, which oversees [[accountancy]]. Finally, planning is represented by the Local Public Planning Council, which manages development projects for the municipality.<ref name=authorities>{{cite web |url=http://www.comisionlegislacionelhatillo.org/podermun.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080421085521/http://www.comisionlegislacionelhatillo.org/podermun.htm|title=Autoridades Municipales |year=2006 |author=Comisión de Legislación. Municipio El Hatillo. Estado Miranda |access-date=2006-11-21|archive-date=2008-04-21}} {{in lang|es}}</ref><ref name=podermunicipal>{{cite web |url=http://www.gobiernoenlinea.gob.ve/venezuela/estructura3.html |title=Poder Público Municipal |author=Gobierno Bolivariano de Venezuela |access-date=2006-11-21|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716090433/http://www.gobiernoenlinea.gob.ve/venezuela/estructura3.html|archive-date=2011-07-16}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

El Hatillo has had five mayors through 2014. Mercedes Hernández de Silva was the first mayor of the municipality, serving from 1993 until 1996. Succeeding her, Flora Aranguen was Mayor from 1996 until 2000. That same year, [[Alfredo Catalán]] was elected mayor and reelected in 2004. On November 23, 2008 Myriam Do Nascimento was elected mayor.<ref name=historyalcaldia/> She served in that capacity until 2013, when David Smolansky succeeded her as mayor.

The 2007 president of the Municipal Council is Leandro Pereira, supported by the [[political party]] [[Justice First]]. All but one of the seven councillors belong to political parties opposed to [[presidents of Venezuela|President]] [[Hugo Chávez]]'s administration.<ref name=councillors>{{cite web |url=http://www.comisionlegislacionelhatillo.org/concejales.htm |title=Concejales |year=2006 |author=Comisión de Legislación. Municipio El Hatillo. Estado Miranda |access-date=2006-11-21 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070519025245/http://www.comisionlegislacionelhatillo.org/concejales.htm |archive-date=2007-05-19 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref> There is also a Legislative Commission, presided over by councillor Salvador Pirrone in 2007. The commission's job is to assist the municipality in legal matters, such as the creation of new laws and decrees.

On March 8, 2000 – the year after a new constitution was introduced in Venezuela – it was [[decree]]d that the [[Metropolitan District of Caracas]] would be created, and that some of the powers of El Hatillo Municipality would be delegated to the ''Alcaldía Mayor'', which would also govern the Baruta, Libertador, Sucre and Chacao municipalities.<ref name=gaceta2000/> Each of the five municipalities is divided into parishes; El Hatillo has only one, the Santa Rosalía de Palermo Parish,<ref name=geography/> sometimes called Santa Rosalía de El Hatillo Parish or simply El Hatillo Parish.

In December 2006, as a part of a [[constitutional reform]], Chávez proposed a reorganisation of the municipal powers.<ref name=constitunionalreform>{{cite news |url=http://buscador.eluniversal.com/2006/12/23/en_pol_art_20A816893.shtml |title=The Debate on the Constitutional Reform in Venezuela |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |last=Aguiar |first=Asdrubal |date=2006-12-23 |access-date=2007-02-06 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080125095849/http://buscador.eluniversal.com/2006/12/23/en_pol_art_20A816893.shtml |archive-date=2008-01-25 }}</ref> Chávez mentioned his reform plans again in his January 2007 presidential inauguration, suggesting a new form of subdivision—communal cities—in which mayors and municipalities would be replaced by communal powers.<ref name=communalpowers>{{cite news |url=http://commentisfree.guardian.co.uk/francisco_rodriguez/2007/01/francisco_rodriguez_on_chavez.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070121041829/http://commentisfree.guardian.co.uk/francisco_rodriguez/2007/01/francisco_rodriguez_on_chavez.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=January 21, 2007 |title=Should egalitarians support Chávez? |publisher=[[Guardian.co.uk|Comment is free]] |date=2007-01-12 |last=Rodríguez |first=Francisco |access-date=2007-02-06}}</ref>

==Culture==
The most significant icon in the culture of El Hatillo is [[Saint Rosalia|Santa Rosalía de Palermo]]. The church adjacent to the plaza in the center block of El Hatillo Town is named after this [[saint]], and the only parish in the municipality also carries her name. The community is largely [[Roman Catholic Church|Catholic]]; local shops carry many religious handcrafted products, and the municipality is the site of the [[Rose of Lima|Santa Rosa de Lima]] [[Seminary]], formerly San José Seminary.<ref name=4catalogo0405/> In El Hatillo – and throughout Venezuela – images of [[Jesus]] and [[Blessed Virgin Mary|Mary]] are part of the art and culture.

Don Baltasar de León and his wife, Ana Francisca, are remembered for founding and developing El Hatillo. Manuel Escalona is recognised for including El Hatillo in the 19th century independence movement; as in the rest of Venezuela, [[Simón Bolívar]] is [[hero|considered]] a [[Libertadores|hero]].

===Heritage===
[[File:Santa rosalia sculpture.jpg|thumb|200px|left|Sculpture of ''Santa Rosalía de Palermo'', inside the church named after her. Brought to El Hatillo from Spain by Don Baltasar.<ref name=5catalogo0405/>]]
Santa Rosalía de Palermo – born in [[Palermo]], Italy – is the [[patron]] [[saint]] of El Hatillo. Rosalía was recognised in 1624 when her remains were discovered in a cave, brought to the Cathedral of Palermo, and displayed through the streets of Palermo during a plague. Within three days, the plague ended; Rosalía was credited with saving many from the plague and proclaimed patron saint of the city.<ref>{{cite web|author=New Advent |url=https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13184a.htm |title=St. Rosalia |access-date=2006-12-15 |year=2006}}</ref><ref>Ferlita, Kenneth C. (1997) [http://www.ferlita.com/ssq/story.htm Santa Rosalia.] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303193539/http://www.ferlita.com/ssq/story.htm |date=March 3, 2016 }}  Retrieved on 2006-12-15.</ref>

Years later, El Hatillo's founder also believed that Santa Rosalía had protected him from an infection. During the Guipuzcoana scandal in Venezuela, Baltasar's father, Juan Francisco de León, and his sons were held prisoners in [[Cádiz]]. Juan Francisco died as a consequence of [[smallpox]], but Don Baltasar completed his years in prison and then moved to El Hatillo. Baltasar brought the legacy of Santa Rosalía de Palermo to El Hatillo, believing she protected him from the [[infectious disease|pestilence]] that killed his father in Cádiz.<ref name=starosalia/>

Part of El Hatillo's culture has grown around Santa Rosalía; she is believed to be the one who takes care of the people and protects El Hatillo from any [[pandemic]] that could hit the area. Don Baltasar's most evident inclusion of Rosalía into El Hatillo's culture occurred at least twice:  first in 1776, when El Calvario chapel was built and dedicated to the Saint; and then in 1784, when a bigger [[parish church]] named ''Iglesia Santa Rosalía de Palermo'' was constructed.<ref name=caracasvirtualrosalia>{{cite web |author=Caracas Virtual |url=http://www.caracasvirtual.com/subsecciones.asp?sec=1&bot=1&pos=13 |title=Santa Rosalía de Palermo |access-date=2006-12-02|year=2004 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061006090931/http://www.caracasvirtual.com/subsecciones.asp?sec=1&amp;bot=1&amp;pos=13 <!--Added by H3llBot--> |archive-date=2006-10-06}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

===Regional celebrations===
In addition to the nationwide activities celebrating [[Christmas]], the [[New Year]], [[Carnival]], and [[Easter]], El Hatillo has a number of celebrations unique to the region.<ref name=7catalogo0405>{{cite book|author=Instituto del Patrimonio Cultural |title=Catalogo del Patrimonio Cultural Venezolano 2004–2005. Municipio El Hatillo |year=2005 |publisher=Ministerio de la Cultura |location=Caracas |isbn=978-980-6448-21-6 |url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |access-date=2006-12-15 |chapter=Las Manifestaciones Colectivas (PDF) |chapter-url=http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/7_Las%20Manifestaciones%20Colectivas_El%20Hatillo.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070515080719/http://www.ipc.gov.ve/censo/cat_miranda/cat_miranda_hatillo.html |archive-date=May 15, 2007 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> Since the 1766 founding of El Hatillo, a week-long festival honoring Santa Rosalía de Palermo (Spanish: ''Fiestas Patronales en honor a Santa Rosalía de Palermo'') is held in September featuring parades, [[Mass (liturgy)|Catholic masses]], and traditional games, concluding with the traditional release of balloons accompanied by fireworks. On [[Maundy Thursday|Holy Thursday]], an image of the crucified  [[Christ]] is decorated with flowers and paraded around El Hatillo's Plaza Bolívar in the [[Jesus Christ]] [[Procession]]. Since 1938, Carnival has been celebrated in El Hatillo with dancing, parades, and the election of a Carnival Queen in Plaza Bolívar. The founding of El Hatillo is commemorated on June 12 with organised activities including traditional games, mass, and balloons. A tradition having religious and agricultural significance has been celebrated every May since the beginning of the twentieth century. The third Sunday of May is the festival of ''Dama antañona'', in which residents pay homage to the women of El Hatillo, with typical food and gifts.<ref name=7catalogo0405/>

===Art===
[[File:Bolivar sq stairs.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Bolívar Square, the heart of El Hatillo.]]
The Cultural and Social Center El Hatillo, El Hatillo Art Center, and El Hatillo Atheneum are the local centers of artistic activity. In 2006, [[Dave Samuels]] inaugurated the annual International Music Festival of El Hatillo at the El Hatillo Art Center; Samuels was followed by [[Simón Díaz]], [[Steve Smith (musician)|Steve Smith]], [[Serenata Guayanesa]], [[Mike Stern]] and other notable musicians.<ref name=musicanalitica>{{cite web |author=Analítica |url=http://www.analitica.com/va/entretenimiento/ocioccs/9477430.asp |title=La música está de moda en El Hatillo |date=2006-03-21 |access-date=2006-07-18 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061019020459/http://www.analitica.com/va/entretenimiento/ocioccs/9477430.asp |archive-date=2006-10-19 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> Since 1999, the El Hatillo Jazz Festival has attracted visitors to the municipality to hear national and foreign [[jazz]] artists.<ref name=jazzfestival>{{cite news |url=http://www.globovision.com/news.php?nid=12519 |title=Este viernes comienza el Jazz Festival El Hatillo 2005 |publisher=Globovisión |date=2005-10-26 |access-date=2006-07-18 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060127101111/http://globovision.com/news.php?nid=12519 |archive-date=2006-01-27 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref><ref name=8thjazzfestival>{{cite news |url=http://www.diariolavoz.net/seccion.asp?pid=18&sid=1755&notid=200836&fecha=10/14/2006 |title=Destacados músicos reeditan éxito del Festival Jazz y gastronomia |publisher=La Voz |date=2006-10-14 |access-date=2006-12-01 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928180021/http://www.diariolavoz.net/seccion.asp?pid=18&sid=1755&notid=200836&fecha=10%2F14%2F2006 |archive-date=2007-09-28 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

El Hatillo's art culture is rich with handcrafted products. [[Pottery]] is a common [[souvenir]] for tourists, and there are many artisans devoted to ceramics and pottery in the municipality. The Turgua Group is an [[artist collective]] of almost twenty potters and [[blacksmiths]], founded in 1992 by Guillermo Cuellar, an internationally known potter.<ref>Cornell College (Dec. 20, 2002). [http://www.cornellcollege.edu/news_center/press_releases/nr2002/cuellar_ceramics.shtml Cornell hosts exhibition by Venezuelan potter, alumnus Guillermo Cuellar.] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100615024134/http://www.cornellcollege.edu/news_center/press_releases/nr2002/cuellar_ceramics.shtml |date=June 15, 2010 }} Press Release Archive, 2002–2003. Retrieved on 2006-12-15</ref><ref name=guillermocuellar>{{cite web |author=Akar Design |url=http://www.akardesign.com/creators/moreinfo.asp?iCreatorID=57 |title=Grupo Turgua |year=2006 |access-date=2006-12-01 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930084845/http://www.akardesign.com/creators/moreinfo.asp?iCreatorID=57 |archive-date=2007-09-30 }}</ref> The group has two exhibitions a year, which have expanded from pottery exposition to [[jewelry]], [[photography]], [[woodwork]], [[drawing]] and [[weaving]].<ref name=5catalogo0405/><ref name=grupoturgua>{{cite web |author=MiPunto.com |url=http://www.mipunto.com/temas/4to_trimestre03/grupo_turgua.html |title=Grupo Turgua |year=2004 |access-date=2006-12-01}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

In May 2005, the local government collaborated with the Japanese Embassy to organise Japan Cultural Week, an exposition held in the Art Center featuring [[bonsai]], [[origami]], [[kimono]], [[martial arts]], [[anime]] and other manifestations of the [[Japanese culture]]. The event offered free workshops for learning these Japanese arts.<ref name=japanweek>{{cite news|author=Cadena Global |url=http://www.cadenaglobal.com/Default.asp?pgm=Detail&Not=89277&Sec=%201 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080123090132/http://www.cadenaglobal.com/Default.asp?pgm=Detail&Not=89277&Sec=%201 |url-status=dead |archive-date=2008-01-23 |title=Semana cultural de Japón en El Hatillo |date=2005-05-27 |access-date=2006-12-15 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> Continuing cultural promotion in the municipality, the ''III Salón de Fotografía El Hatillo'' – a photography contest for children, [[amateur]] and professional photographers – was organised in October 2005.<ref name=photocontest>{{cite web |author=Analítica |url=http://www.analitica.com/va/arte/actualidad/8352018.asp |title=III Salón de Fotografía El Hatillo |date=2005-09-26 |access-date=2006-07-18 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20051110012639/http://analitica.com/va/arte/actualidad/8352018.asp |archive-date=2005-11-10 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

===Cuisine===
[[File:Churros el hatillo.JPG|thumb|left|200px|[[Churros]] with [[chocolate]].]]
The cuisine industry in El Hatillo has grown along with the commercial development of the municipality. A September 2006 article in ''Estampas'' – a weekly Venezuelan [[magazine]] – described the [[culinary art]]s of El Hatillo, noting that El Hatillo offers the usual Venezuelan table, as well as new gastronomic developments. There are also typical [[cachapa]] restaurants and [[cafés]], as well as other restaurants with a fusion of foreign and national food. El Hatillo also offers many varieties of [[confectionery]], such as [[churros]], [[pastry]] and [[ice cream]].<ref name=estampasfood/> There are a variety of other restaurants in El Hatillo, offering such diverse cuisine as German and [[Thailand|Thai]] food.<ref name=LP85>Dydynski (2004), p. 85.</ref>

===Sports===
Lagunita Country Club is one of the most important sports facilities in the municipality. The club offers tennis and swimming, but it is best known for its golf course, the home of the 1974 [[WGC-World Cup]]. Designed by [[Dick Wilson (golf course architect)|Dick Wilson]], the club began with temporary headquarters in 1959, opening officially in 1964. Lagunita Country Club played an important role in the development of La Lagunita neighborhood – an ambitious urban project, which has become one of the wealthiest areas of Caracas.<ref name=lalagunita/><ref name=lagunitacc>{{cite web|author=Tekware|url=http://www.worldgolf.com/courses/southamerica.html|title=Golf Courses of South America|access-date=2006-12-02}}</ref>

Hiparión is another club located in El Hatillo; according to the Venezuelan Census of Cultural Heritage, this [[equestrianism|equestrian]] facility from the 1930s was originally used for horse trips, but it later became a place for the training and caring of horses. The Club Hiparión is internationally known for its equestrian training.<ref name=5catalogo0405/>

Located at the highest point of the Municipality lies El Volcan, a small mountain of about 1500 meters from sea level. This mountain has a Downhill course that has about 500 meters of vertical drop, it is used by hundreds of riders a day during dry and wet weather, mostly on weekends. The course is open to the public and riding is neither specifically allowed nor prohibited by law. The trails are also used by hikers all week long. Shuttles are about 10 Venezuelan bolivars per trip, they run from the parking lot of a Farmatodo drug store in La Boyera, up to the summit using public avenues and paved roads, taking from 15 minutes to 30 minutes depending on traffic on the area. The course apart from being used mostly for recreational purpose, also has been used for irregularly scheduled downhill races due to the lack of organisation in the riders community.

===Tourism and recreation===
The hub of activity in El Hatillo Town is Bolívar Plaza (Spanish: ''Plaza Bolívar''), a garden square encompassing the central block in the town of El Hatillo. Constructed in 1785, the Plaza was originally called ''Plaza Mayor'' or ''Plaza del Mercado''. In 1911, a [[bust (sculpture)|bust]] honoring Manuel Escalona was placed in the square, which was renamed in his honor. In 1952, the bust was replaced with a statue of Simón Bolívar, and the plaza was again renamed after the Venezuelan hero.<ref name=4catalogo0405/> Across from the Bolívar Square is the 18th century Santa Rosalía de Palermo Church, which was declared a National Historic Monument in 1960.<ref name=caracasvirtual/>
[[File:Simon bolivar elhatillo.jpg|thumb|left|200px|The statue of Simón Bolívar, erected in 1952, replaced the one of Manuel Escalona.]]
Between El Hatillo and La Lagunita is the smaller Manuel Escalona Plaza (Spanish: ''Plazoleta Manuel Escalona''), another urban monument displaying the bust of Escalona that formerly occupied Bolívar Square. Sucre Plaza (Spanish: ''Plaza Sucre'') – graced since 1915 with a [[ceiba]] tree at its center – is in the southern part of town; this was historically where people tied their mules while frequenting The Four Corners, and it is also known as ''Plaza La Ceiba''. The Four Corners (Spanish: ''Las Cuatro Esquinas'') was a convenient social gathering spot in El Hatillo, comprising a general store, hardware shop, gambling place and bar.<ref name=gdpatrimonios>{{cite web |url=http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/valuespatrimony_es.shtml |title=Patrimonios de El Hatillo |year=2002 |author=GD Solutions C.A. |access-date=2006-05-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060502165801/http://www.elhatillo.com.ve/values/valuespatrimony_es.shtml| archive-date = May 2, 2006}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

La Lagunita is the site of the [[San Constantino and Santa Elena Romanian Orthodox Church]]. The building is an architectural work from the 16th century, brought from Romania, made completely from [[oak]] and [[fir]] woods, and detailed with more than 40,000 individually placed and [[Stone carving|carved]] tiles. It is one of only 15 churches of its type remaining in the world, and one of only two outside of Romania, the other being in Switzerland.<ref name=4catalogo0405/><ref name=variedades>{{cite news |author=Rusé Martín Galano |title=El Hatillo y Ávila Mágica |publisher=Variedades |pages=58–61 |date=March 2006}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

For children, the Caicaguana [[hacienda]] in La Lagunita houses the Expanzoo, where visitors can see and touch exotic animals. The zoo is recognised for offering unique employment opportunities; the workers are from families with few resources, and the staff include the [[mentally ill]].<ref name=expanzoo>{{cite web |author=Couret, Angela |url=http://www.pasoapaso.com.ve/motiva/motiva100.htm |title=Expanzoo ... Un sueño que ya es realidad |publisher=Fundación Paso a Paso |date=July 2003 |access-date=2006-07-20| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20050307053439/http://www.pasoapaso.com.ve/motiva/motiva100.htm| archive-date = March 7, 2005}}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> The Baby Zoo is another place for children to interact with animals; visitors can feed and touch the animals, ride horses and rent the location for special events.<ref name=variedades/> More interaction with nature can be experienced by visiting the Morro la Guairita park in [[El Cafetal]] – commonly known as the Indian Caves (Spanish: ''Cuevas del Indio'') – a system of 22 natural openings in the mountain, and the only place in Caracas where [[rock climbing]] is permitted. Guided tours are available, and views of [[Cerro El Ávila|El Ávila]] can be enjoyed while ascending the park.<ref name=variedades/>

==Transportation==
[[File:Hatillo-street.JPG|thumb|left|200px|A traditional street with colonial houses.]]
The mountainous terrain and geographic features of El Hatillo have made it difficult to extend the [[Caracas Metro]] to southeast Caracas, so the main transportation methods in the municipality are private vehicles and road [[public transportation]]. An extension of the Metro – [[List of Caracas Metro stations#Line 5 (Magenta)|Line 5]] – has been proposed, but construction has not been initiated as of 2007; phase 2 of [[List of Caracas Metro stations#Line 4 (Red)|Line 4]] is still under construction. [[Urban planning]] in the municipality has been unorganised; news archives show that at least since 1998, neighbors have been complaining about the dense traffic caused by new residential and commercial construction, yet new or enhanced alternative roads to resolve the traffic problems have not been completed. A south [[beltway]] suggested 25 years ago has not been constructed due to its high cost.<ref name=traffic1998>{{cite news |author=López V., Liza |url=http://buscador.eluniversal.com/1998/03/20/ccs_art_20401AA.shtml |title=El sureste seguirá atrapado en una vía |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=1998-03-20 |access-date=2006-09-16 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080125095845/http://buscador.eluniversal.com/1998/03/20/ccs_art_20401AA.shtml |archive-date=2008-01-25 }} {{in lang|es}}</ref> However, as of January 2006, a new route that will connect La Lagunita with Macaracuay – a neighborhood in northeast Caracas – is under construction and is planned to be completed in 2010; according to Mayor Catalán, 23% of El Hatillo's inhabitants will eventually use this transit way. Its cost was estimated in early 2006 as [[US$]]19,572,000.<ref name=newroute>{{cite news |author=de Ornelas, Elizabeth |url=http://buscador.eluniversal.com/2006/01/19/ccs_art_19443A.shtml |title=El Hatillo tendrá otra salida |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=2006-01-19 |access-date=2006-09-16 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070503051552/http://buscador.eluniversal.com/2006/01/19/ccs_art_19443A.shtml |archive-date=2007-05-03 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>

Local solutions – such as the proposed Metro extension line and the road connecting La Lagunita and Macaracuay – may improve the [[traffic congestion]] around El Hatillo, but the traffic issue affects all of Caracas. It is estimated that one million vehicles transit Caracas daily, causing a collapse of the transportation network.<ref name=trafficcongestion>{{cite news |last=Hernández |first=Jorge |author2=Marisol DeCarli |url=http://economia.eluniversal.com/2006/11/06/ccs_art_60042.shtml |title=Un millón de carros saturan Caracas |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=2006-11-06 |access-date=2006-11-13 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080125092415/http://economia.eluniversal.com/2006/11/06/ccs_art_60042.shtml |archive-date=2008-01-25 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref> Automobiles travel at an average speed of 15&nbsp;km/h (9&nbsp;mph) on the streets and highways of Caracas.<ref name=trafficcongestion/> There are numerous factors contributing to the traffic problem in Caracas. According to the Venezuelan Society of Transportation Engineers, a city should allocate 20% of its public area to transportation; in Caracas, less than 12% is allocated.<ref name=trafficcongestion/> In 2004, fifty thousand new vehicles were sold in Caracas. In 2005, sixty thousand more were sold, and as of November, 2006, seventy thousand more had been sold. In five years, 250 thousand more cars are circulating in Caracas on roadways that have not increased proportionally to the increase in the number of cars.<ref name=trafficcongestion/> Further, public transportation is not fully reliable; an average trip in the city using mass transit takes around ninety minutes.<ref name=trafficnumbers>{{cite news |url=http://economia.eluniversal.com/2006/11/06/ccs_apo_60050.shtml |title=Cifras que hablan del caos vehicular |publisher=[[El Universal (Caracas)|El Universal]] |date=2006-11-06 |access-date=2006-11-13 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080125092410/http://economia.eluniversal.com/2006/11/06/ccs_apo_60050.shtml |archive-date=2008-01-25 }}  {{in lang|es}}</ref>
