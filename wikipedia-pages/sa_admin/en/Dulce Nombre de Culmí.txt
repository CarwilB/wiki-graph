'''Dulce Nombre de Culmí''' ({{IPA|es|ˈdulse ˈnombɾe ðe kulˈmi}}) is a [[Municipalities of Honduras|municipality]] in the northeastern region of the [[Honduras|Honduran]] [[Departments of Honduras|department]] of [[Olancho department|Olancho]]. It lies approximately 17 miles (27 km.) southeast of [[San Esteban, Olancho|San Esteban]] and about 27 miles (43 km.) northeast of [[Catacamas]]. The [[Rivers of Honduras|Ríos]] Guampú (or Wampú) and Culmí pass through this municipality.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ordenamientoterritorial.hn/images/creacion_municipios/olancho/dulce%20nombre%20de%20culmi.pdf|title=Honduran Data|website=Datos de organización territorial de Honduras}}{{Dead link|date=July 2019 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==Demographics==
At the time of the 2013 Honduras census, Dulce Nombre de Culmí municipality had a population of 29,947. Of these, 91.29% were [[Mestizo]], 8.16% [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] (7.09% [[Pech people|Pech]]), 0.32% [[White Latin Americans|White]], 0.16% [[Afro-Hondurans|Black or Afro-Honduran]] and 0.07% others.<ref>[https://www.ine.gob.hn/V3/baseine/ Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE), bases de datos en línea]</ref>

==Pech people==
This area of Olancho is the home of one of the seven indigenous peoples living in Honduras, the [[Pech people|Pech]], formerly known as "Payas". As of early 2005, the Pech population had been reduced to 3,800. The [[Paya language|Pech language]] is a member of the [[Chibchan]] family of languages, and, although it is still spoken by older people, it is in danger of extinction in the relatively near future. Despite modern influences, the Pech still preserve their simple way of living and traditions.
