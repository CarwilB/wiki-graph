'''Tlaquiltenango''' is a city in the [[Mexican state]] of [[Morelos]]. It is {{convert|151.6|km|mi}} south of Mexico city and {{convert|60.1|km|mi}} southeast of [[Cuernavaca]], the state capital via [[Mexican Federal Highway 95D]].
[[File:Bugambilia.jpg|thumb|200x200px]]<!--- 18° 37 44”N. and 90°09 37”W. --->

The city serves as the municipal seat for the surrounding municipality, with which it shares a name. The toponym ''Tlaquiltenango ''comes from a [[Nahuatl]] name and means "place of whitewashed walls". 
The municipality reported 33,844 inhabitants in the year 2015 census.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/ |title=Número de habitantes |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160507000252/http://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/ |archive-date=2016-05-07 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

==History==
===Prehispanic history===
There are two pre-Hispanic archaeological sites in Tlaquiltenango: ''Chimalacatlan'' and ''Huaxtla''. Chamalacatlan was built on the top of the hill of "El Venado"; it had 33 terraces and an equal number of piles of cut stone. There is also a small cave that was used for ceremonies. From the top of the hill, you can see Lake Tequesquitengo, [[Xochicalco]], and parts of the state of [[Guerrero]]. The site is almost unique among [[Mesoamerica]]n ruins in that the walls and platform were constructed of megaliths rather than the smaller rocks usually found in such structures. A small ceremonial ballcourt is also uncharacteristic of the [[Olmecs]]. The ruins were described by Fernando de Alva Ixtlixochitl in the early 17th century, although the modern discovery by Father Lorenzo Castro in the late 19th century was more significant. Castro reported his find to Bishop [[Francisco Plancarte y Navarrete]], who speculated Chimalacatlan was none other than the mythological [[Tamoanchan]], the cradle of Mesoamerican civilization.<ref>{{citation |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/blogs/turismo/noticias/76330-un-viaje-r%C3%A1pido-y-maravilloso-viaje-a-chimalacatl%C3%A1n.html |title=Un viaje rápido y maravilloso a Chimalacatlán|date=15 August 2015|accessdate=24 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=A quick and wonderful trip to Chimalacatlán|newspaper=La Union Turistico|location=Cuernavaca}}</ref>
 
Huaxtla was built in what is the center of the community by that name today, at the top of a horseshoe-shaped hill. The ruins are similar to those of Chimalacatlan. It is believed that the inhabitants of these sites were from [[Tepexpan]], who migrated south in search of better food and a better climate.

===Conquest and colonialism===
Tlaquiltenango was included in the towns belonging to the marquisate of the Valley of Oaxtepec, awarded to the conquistador [[Hernán Cortés]]. Cortes established a hacienda for the breeding of fine horses that would be destined for his army; for its surveillance, he ordered the construction of a circular stone tower with a height of close to {{convert|40|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us}}.<ref name=gob>{{cite web|url=http://morelos.gob.mx/?q=tlaquiltenango|title=Tlaquiltenango| accessdate= 22 December 2018|language=Spanish|trans-title=Tlaquiltenango|publisher=Gobierno de Morelos 2018-2024}}</ref>

The convent-fortress ''Santo Domingo de Guzmán'' was initiated by the order of the [[Franciscans]] and finished by the [[Dominican Order|Dominicans]] in the year 1540, one the oldest of the American continent. The convent is not very well-maintained, and there are cracks in the walls, and interior frescos are in poor condition. The clock was installed in December, 1898.<ref name=gob /> In 1909, Mauricio de la Arena and Father Agapito Mateo Menes discovered a prehispanic codex in one corner; this is at the [[National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico)|National Museum of Anthropology]] today.<ref name=inafed>{{cite web|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17025a.html|title=Estado de Morelos:Tlaquiltenango|publisher=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de Mexico|accessdate=23 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=State of Morelos: Tlaquiltenango}}</ref>

===Independence period===
Tlaquiltenango became a city of the [[State of Mexico]] in 1825 under the [[1824 Constitution of Mexico]]. It included not only present-day Tlaquiltenango, but also the municipalities of [[Jojutla]] and [[Zacatepec]], and stretched southeast to the [[Cuautla River]].{{citation needed|date=April 2024}} It became a municipality of Morelos by decree of [[Governor of Morelos|governor Carlos Pacheco]] on 25 September 1884.<ref name=gob />

The [[2017 Puebla earthquake|2017 earthquake]] did considerable damage in Tlaquitenango. Two children and two women were killed, while a woman from ''Valle de Vázquez'' was killed in [[Jojutla]]. The earthquake left a large crack in the earth that split two houses apart and destroyed 183 others and severely damaged 365 other homes in Colonia Celino Manzanares next to the [[Yautepec River]]. The 16th-century church was severely damaged, as was the 17th-century bridge in Colonia Manzares, 60 stone fences collapsed, and the municipal hall was damaged.<ref>{{citation|newspaper=La Jornada|location=Mexico City|url=https://www.jornada.com.mx/2017/10/01/politica/018n1pol#|title=Grieta de 50 metros parte casas en Morelos|date=1 October 2017|accessdate=26 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Crack of 50 meters splits houses in Morelos}}</ref><ref>{{citation|newspaper=La Union de Morelos|location=Cuernavaca|title=Revelan daños graves en Tlaquiltenango por el sismo|date=21 September 2017|last=Torres|first=Evaristo|language=Spanish|trans-title=Severe damage in Tlaquiltenango by earthquake}}</ref> The Secreatary of Health reported that 396 homes were destroyed and 510 were damaged.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=García-Carrera |first1=Jesús Salvador |last2=Mena-Hernández |first2=Ulises |last3=Bermúdez-Alarcón |first3=Francisco Javier |title=El terremoto 19S en Morelos: la experiencia operativa del INEEL en la evaluación del riesgo estructural |url=http://saludpublica.mx/index.php/spm/article/view/9408/11478 |journal=Salud Pública de México |accessdate=27 May 2020 |pages=65–82 |language=es |doi=10.21149/9408 |date=1 March 2018|volume=60 |doi-access=free }}</ref>

Jorge Maldonado Ortiz of ''Juntos Haremos Historia'' ([[Morena, Madhya Pradesh|Morena]] Together we will make history coalition) was elected Presidente Municipal (mayor) on 1 July 2018.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://impepac.mx/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Candidatos-electos-2018.pdf |title=Candidatos electos 2018 |website=impepac.mx |access-date=14 December 2018}}</ref> José Almanza Alcaine, a former alderman and the 2018 candidate of [[Citizens' Movement (Mexico)|Movimiento Ciudadano]] for municipal president, was gunned down on [[2019 in Mexico|January 27, 2019]]. His two companions were also shot, and one died.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://laverdadnoticias.com/crimen/Asesinan-a-Jose-Almanza-ex-candidato-a-la-Alcaldia-de-Tlaquiltenango-Morelos-20190127-0068.html|title=Asesinan a José Almanza, ex candidato a la Alcaldía de Tlaquiltenango, Morelos|publisher=La Verdad Noticias|date=27 January 2019|accessdate=24 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=José Almanza, former candidate for the Mayor of Tlaquiltenango, Morelos, assassinated}}</ref>

In response to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]], Tlaquiltenango blocked the entrances to the community. The town reports two confirmed cases and one death as of 27 April 2020. Schools and many businesses were closed from mid March until 1 June.<ref>{{cite web |title=Ahora colocan retenes en Tlaquiltenango – Nodo Noticias |url=https://www.nodonoticias.com.mx/2020/04/12/ahora-colocan-reten-en-tlaquiltenango/ |accessdate=23 Apr 2020 |language=es|trans-title=Now they put blockades in Tlaquiltenango}} {{in lang|es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Redacción |first1=La |title=Situación actual del coronavirus Covid-19 en Morelos |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/avances/noticias/159372-situacion-actual-del-coronavirus-covid-19-en-morelos.html |website=www.launion.com.mx |accessdate=29 Apr 2020 |language=es-es|trans-title=Present situation of coronavirus COVID-19 in Morelos}}</ref> On 2 July, Tlaquiltenango reported eleven infections and one death from the virus; the reopening of the state was pushed back until at least 13 June.<ref>{{cite web |title=Coronavirus en Morelos {{!}} Diario de Morelos |url=https://www.diariodemorelos.com/noticias/coronavirus |website=www.diariodemorelos.com |accessdate=4 June 2020 |language=es}}</ref> Tlaqiltenango reported 75 cases, 51 recuperations, and 22 deaths from the virus as of 31 August.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Preciado |first1=Tlaulli |title=En Morelos, cinco mil 319 casos confirmados acumulados de covid-19 y mil 27 decesos |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/avances/noticias/166780-en-morelos-cinco-mil-319-casos-confirmados-acumulados-de-covid-19-y-mil-27-decesos.html |website=La Unión |accessdate=1 September 2020 |language=es-es}}</ref> One hundred twenty/one cases were reported on 27 December 2020.<ref>{{cite news |title=Llega Morelos a 1 mil 600 muertes por COVID19 |url=https://www.diariodemorelos.com/noticias/llega-morelos-1-mil-600-muertes-por-covid19 |access-date=28 December 2020 |work=diariodemorelos.com |publisher=Diario de Morelos |date=27 December 2020 |language=es}}</ref>

==Communities==

The average education level is 7 years.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nuestro-mexico.com/Morelos/Tlaquiltenango/|title=Tlaquiltenango|language=Spanish|accessdate=23 April 2019|publisher=Nuestro Mexico}}</ref> Women have a fertility rate of 2.58 children. 12.61% of the population come from outside the state of Morelos. 67.7% are Roman Catholic, and 49.7% of the adults are economically active.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://mexico.pueblosamerica.com/morelos/tlaquiltenango/|title=Tlaquiltenango |publisher=Pueblos America |language=Spanish |accessdate=24 April 2019}}</ref>

==Culture==
===Points of Interest===
The 16th-century church and monastery of [[Saint Dominic|Santo Domingo]], the haciendas of ''Las Bóvedas'' and ''Los Hornos'', and the concrete tower in El Rollo waterpark are the main historical constructions.<ref name=inafed />

There are two waterparks in the municipality. ''El Rollo'' is located in Gabriel Tepepa and is one of the best waterparks in Latin America. It has 40 pools and 18 water slides, including one with a {{convert|25|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us|adj=on}} height.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.elrollo.com.mx/web/balnearios-en-morelos/#home|title=El Rollo Parque Acuatico|publisher=Balnearios de Morelos|accessdate=23 April 2019}}</ref> ''Paraiso Aventura Las Huertas'' is a natural waterpark with a spring, camping area, cabins, picnic area, and pools.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://paraisoaventura.mx/#home|title=Las Huertas: UN BALNEARIO TOTALMENTE NATURAL|publisher=Paraiso Aventura Las Huertas|accessdate=23 April 2019}}</ref>

The ''Museo Comunitario Rubén Jaramillo'' (Ruben Jaramillo Community Museum) in Tlaquiltenango is a small museum where paintings of revolutionary characters are exhibited, as well as books from the era of the [[Mexican Revolution]], as well as pieces and pre-Hispanic tools. [[Rubén Jaramillo]] (1900-1962) was a native of Tlaquiltenango and a peasant and labor leader during the 1940s and 1950s who, along with his family, was assassinated by the [[Mexican Army]] in [[1962 in Mexico|1962]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ilam.org/index.php/es/museo?id=2370 |title=Museo Comunitario Rubén Jaramillo|publisher=Fundacion ILAM|accessdate=24 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Ruben Jaramillo Community Museum}}</ref>

====Church and monastery of Santo Domingo====
Construction of the church and former monastery of Santo Domingo was begun by the [[Franciscans]] between 1535 and 1540, and it was completed by the [[Dominican Order|Dominicans]]. It is considered one of the most beautiful 16th-century monasteries in Morelos.<ref name=moro>{{cite book| last = Moro| first =Agustín| title =Catálogo gráfico de los Conventos del S. XVI en Morelos | publisher =PUBLI JVS | volume =1 | date =1993 | location =Cuernavaca | pages =68–72 | language =Spanish}}</ref>

The church is very large and rectangular. The main entrance is on the west side, and there is a side entrance on the south that is very similar to that of the [[Cuernavaca Cathedral]]. The [[nave]] and presbytery are separated by a triumphal arch supported by large, circular The columns. The western entrance to the church is simple and austere; above the access arch there is a stone triangle with a small window. There is a clock that was added in 1898 above the window.<ref name=moro />

The base of the bell tower is connected to the main façade of the church. It is proportionately tall and consists of two ornamented sections for bells that contrast with the general austerity of the church itself.<ref name=moro />

There are large pink, stone buttresses on the south side of the church and a carved, Franciscan-style cross above the main entrance. The interior of the church is simple in the Dominican style. The monastery cloister is high with a small central patio; the cells for the monks are simple. The atrium is huge; the ''capillas pozas'' for the instruction of unbaptized Indigenous and the two large entrances are still standing.<ref name=moro />

===Fiestas, dances, and traditions===
[[Presentation of Jesus at the Temple|Candlemas Day]] is celebrated on 2 February in Tlaquiltenango. There is a fair, ''jaripeo'' (bulls), and [[Chinelo]] and Tecuanes dances. In the latter, dancers wear old clothes, a mask, and a hat while they carry a dissected animal.

Tlayehualco celebrates the appearance of the [[Our Lady of Guadalupe]] on 12 December.<ref name=inafed />

===Cuisine===
White pork [[pozole]], chito, goat and pork [[barbecue]], fish in [[mixiote]], in tamales and in [[clemole]] seasoned with wild plum and [[tamarind]] are typical foods in the area.<ref name=inafed />

==Geography==
===Location and area===
Tlaquiltenango is geographically located between parallels {{coord|18|37|44|N|90|09|37|W}} at an altitude of {{convert|911|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us}} above sea level.

To the north are the municipalities of [[Tlaltizapán]], [[Ciudad Ayala|Ayala]] and [[Tepalcingo]]; to the south are the States of Guerrero and Puebla; to the west are the municipalities of [[Zacatepec de Hidalgo|Zacatepec]], [[Jojutla]], and [[Puente de Ixtla]]; while Tepalcingo is to the east.

Tlaquiltenango has a surface area of {{convert|544|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} which represents 11.73% of the total of the State of [[Morelos]].
<ref name=inafed />

===Climate===
The wet season in Tlaquiltenango (late May to late October) is overcast, the dry season (late October to late May) is partly cloudy, and it is hot year round. Over the course of the year, the temperature typically varies from {{convert|56|to|95|F|C}}. The hot season lasts for two months, from 21 March to 23 May, with an average daily high temperature above {{convert|93|°F|°C|abbr=on}} The cool season lasts for seven months, from 25 June to 1 February, with an average daily high temperature below {{convert|88|°F|°C|abbr=on}}. Average annual rainfall is {{convert|35.8|in|mm}}.{{fact|date=February 2026}}

24 February, is the clearest day of the year, and 14 September, is the cloudiest day of the year. The windier part of the year lasts for five months, from December to May, with average wind speeds of more than {{convert|4.9|mph|km/h}}. The wind is mostly from the west in March, from the south from April through June, and from the east from July through February.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://weatherspark.com/y/5425/Average-Weather-in-Tlaquiltenango-Mexico-Year-Round|title=Average Weather in Tlaquiltenango Mexico|publisher=Weather Spark|accessdate=24 April 2019}}</ref>

===Relief and water resources===
Dominant heights in the municipality of Tlaquiltenango are the Santa María hill, the Guajolote hill, the Huautla hill, {{convert|1,642|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us}}, the Palo Verde hill, the black ground of the Ciénega, and the Limón. Along the boundaries of the State of Puebla and Municipality of Tepalcingo, are Tetillas, Cerro Picacho del Burial, Temazcales, and Cueva (Cave) de San Martín. Hilly areas cover 44% of Tlaquiltenango, mostly in the center and south. 38% of the land is semi-flat, mostly in the center and south. Plains cover 18% of the total surface, mostly in the northwest.<ref name=inafed />

Water resources in Tlaquiltenango include the [[Amacuzac River]], which flows from west to south; the Cuautla River, which flows from north to south joining the Amacuzac River west of Nexpa, and the [[Yautepec River]]. This last flows northwest, joining the Apatlaco River in [[Jojutla]]. There are also smaller, intermittent channels that descend from the mountains during the rainy season. In addition, there are springs in El Rollo, Nexpa, Los Elotes, Valle de Vázquez, and La Huertas. Finally, there are 25 wells—21 for drinking water and four used for irrigation.<ref name=inafed />

===Flora and fauna===
Flora consists mainly of low [[deciduous]] forests in a warm climate, [[ceiba]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://etermagazine.com/sin-categoria/la-ceiba-arbol-sagrado-de-america/|title=La Ceiba: Árbol Sagrado De América|work=Eter Magazine|date=22 November 2019|accessdate=18 February 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=La Ceiba: Sacred Tree Of America}}</ref> and ''[[bougainvillea]]''. Wild animals include white-tailed deer, boar of collar, raccoon, badger, skunk, armadillo, hare, common rabbit, coyote, wildcat, weasel, [[cacomixtle]], [[tlacuache]] (a member of the raccoon family), and bats, flagged bird, [[chachalaca]], magpie copetona, vulture, aura (buzzard), crow, owl, and songbirds and ornamental birds.<ref name=inafed />

====Sierra de Huautla====
{{main|Sierra de Huautla}}
Established in [[2006 in Mexico|2006]], the ''Sierra de Huautla Biosphere Reserve'' (REBIOSH) covers {{convert|59,031|ha|acre|abbr=off}} in the [[Balsas River|Balsas River Basin]] of the municipalities of Tlaquiltenango, [[Amacuzac]], [[Tepalcingo]], [[Jojutla]], and [[Puente de Ixtla]]. Its rough topology varies from {{convert|700|to|2,240|m|ft|sp=us}} above sea level in the Balsas Basin and constitutes a rich reservoir of endemic species to Mexico. There is a broad range of ecosystem, including low deciduous forest, gallery vegetation, and pine and oak forests. 939 species of plants, 44 species of butterflies, 71 species of mammals, 208 species of birds, 53 species of reptiles, 18 species of amphibians, and 14 species of fish have been noted.<ref name=unesco>{{cite web|url=http://www.unesco.org/new/en/natural-sciences/environment/ecological-sciences/biosphere-reserves/latin-america-and-the-caribbean/mexico/sierra-de-huautla/|title=Sierra de Huautla|publisher=UNESCO Ecological Sciences for Sustainable Development|accessdate=25 April 2019}}</ref> Among the species of animals are [[jaguar]]s,<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.uaem.mx/comunicacion-y-medios/comunicacion-universitaria/boletines/logra-uaem-el-primer-registro-para-morelos-de-un-jaguar|title=Logra UAEM el primer registro para Morelos de un jaguar|publisher=UAEM|date=1 November 2016|accessdate=25 April 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=UAEM achieves the first record for Morelos of a jaguar}}</ref> [[Baronia|short-horned Baronia butterfly]], [[Mexican beaded lizard|beaded lizard]], [[military macaw]], [[Rufous-backed thrush|rufous-backed robin]], [[Balsas screech owl]], [[Pileated flycatcher]], [[Cougar|mountain lion]], [[ocelot]], [[margay]], [[bobcat]], and [[jaguarundi]].<ref name=unesco />

===Natural resources and land tenure===
Rivers and land are the most important natural resources in Tlaquiltenango. [[Sugarcane]], rice, onions, [[sorghum]], tomatoes, corn, and beans are the most important crops. Cattle, pigs, goats, sheep, horses, and poultry are raised.<ref name=inafed />

The municipality has an approximate area of {{convert|581.77|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}. {{convert|5,738|ha|acre|abbr=off}} are for agricultural use, {{convert|8,319|ha|acre|abbr=off}} are for livestock, and {{convert|47,293|ha|acre|abbr=off}} are for forests. Land tenure can be divided into {{convert|51,303|ha|acre|abbr=off}} of [[Ejido|ejidal]] property and {{convert|1,334|ha|acre|abbr=off}} private property.<ref name=inafed />
