'''Ciales''' ({{IPA|es|ˈsjales}}, {{IPA|es|ˈsjaleʔ|local}}) is a [[Ciales barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]], located on the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Central Mountain Range]], northwest of [[Orocovis, Puerto Rico|Orocovis]]; south of [[Florida, Puerto Rico|Florida]] and [[Manatí, Puerto Rico|Manatí]]; east of [[Utuado, Puerto Rico|Utuado]] and [[Jayuya, Puerto Rico|Jayuya]]; and west of [[Morovis, Puerto Rico|Morovis]]. Ciales is spread over eight barrios and [[Ciales barrio-pueblo|Ciales Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

== Toponym ==
Sources diverge on the origin of the Ciales name. Nineteenth-century historian [[Cayetano Coll y Toste]] stated that it was named as such by then-governor Gonzalo de Aróstegui Herrera in honor of [[Luis de Lacy|General Luis de Lacy]], who had gone against [[Ferdinand VII of Spain|Ferdinand VII's]] absolutist wishes.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last=Mari Mut|first=José Puerto Rico]]|pages=68–69|language=es|trans-title=The Towns of Puerto Rico and the Churches in Their Squares|chapter=Ciales: Tierra del café|trans-chapter=Ciales: Land of Other sources, such as Manuel Álvarez Nazario and Luis Hernández Aquino, put forward the theory that it comes from the plural of ''cibales'', plural form of ''ciba'', meaning "stony place" or "place of stones" in [[Taíno language|Taíno]], which "had undergone loss of the intervocalic -b- and the addition of the Spanish suffix referring to place -al." Lisa Cathleen Green-Douglass, who carried out a study of toponymics in Puerto Rico and compared both theories, believed the latter to be most plausible since Coll y Toste, per Green-Douglass, must have defined an anagram as a reversal of syllables and the resulting "Cial" or "Cyal" would have to then be made plural.<ref name=":1" />

Yet others believe it originates from the [[Spanish language|Spanish]] ''sillar'' (meaning "carved stones") in reference to the stones carved by the [[Río Grande de Manatí|Río Grande de Manatí's]] currents.<ref name=":0" />

==History==
[[File:Manatí Bridge at Mata de Plátano in Ciales, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|left| [[Manatí Bridge at Mata de Plátano]], in Ciales]]
Until its founding on June 24, 1820, by Isidro Rodríguez,<ref name="Nuestro PR" /> it was part of  the neighboring Manatí municipality, a process that took four years to achieve.<ref name=":0" />

On 13 August 1898, after the armistice ending the [[Spanish–American War]] was signed, Ciales was one of three towns that held uprisings. Led by Ventura Casellas, between three hundred and four hundred individuals proclaimed the Republic of Puerto Rico. However, it has not been determined without a shadow of a doubt whether it was a clear [[Independence movement in Puerto Rico|independence-supporting event]] or a defense of [[Captaincy General of Puerto Rico|Spanish rule]].<ref>{{Cite book|last=Acosta Cruz|first=María|url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131360732|title=Dream Nation: Puerto Rican Culture and the Fictions of Independence|publisher=[[Rutgers University Press]]|year=2014|isbn=9780813565484|pages=136|chapter=Chapter 4: Dream History, Dream Nation|access-date=2021-10-13|url-access=subscription|archive-date=29 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211029173808/https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131360732/|id={{ProQuest|2131360732}} |url-status=live}}</ref> Edwin Karli Padilla Aponte calls it an "alleged revolutionary uprising" since he finds no official historical record for it, even though it appears in a vignette in the ''Pueblos Hispanos'' monthly written by a Gabriel Aracelis, a possible pseudonym for [[Juan Antonio Corretjer]]. The column describes the battle, mentions the participants by name and connects it to the [[Grito de Lares]] by identifying a Pedro González as a grandson of a Manuel González who allegedly fought in the 1868 revolt, establishing a continuity between both events. [[Paul G. Miller]], [[Secretary of Education of Puerto Rico#Education Commissioners|Education Commissioner]] between 1915 and 1921, considered this to be caused by the Seditious Parties (''Partidas Sediciosas''), gangs of bandits that raided Spaniards' homes in the late-nineteenth century, an idea that Corretjer refuted.<ref>{{Cite book|last=Padilla Aponte|first=Edwin Karli|url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131073873|title=Recovering the U. S. Hispanic Literary Heritage, Volume 6|publisher=[[Arte Público Press]]|year=2006|isbn=9781611922677|editor-last=Castañeda|editor-first=Antonia I.|pages=98, 105|language=es|chapter=La "construcción" de una nación puertorriqueña en la prensa escrita|trans-chapter=The "construction" of a Puerto Rican Nation in the Written Press|access-date=2021-10-13|editor-last2=Meléndez|editor-first2=A. Gabriel|url-access=subscription|archive-date=29 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211029175313/https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131073873/|id={{ProQuest|2131073873}} |url-status=live}}</ref>

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Ciales was 18,115.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=cZ9QAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA161|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=161|language=es|access-date=15 February 2019|archive-date=14 August 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230814162715/https://books.google.com/books?id=cZ9QAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA161|url-status=live}}</ref>

The first [[truss bridge]] erected in Puerto Rico, after the signing of the [[Treaty of Paris (1898)|Treaty of Paris]], is in Ciales. It is over the [[Río Grande de Manatí]] and is on the list of [[National Register of Historic Places listings in Puerto Rico|National Register of Historic Places]].

[[Hurricane Maria]] struck Puerto Rico on September 20, 2017, its [[Eye (cyclone)|eye]] passing through northeastern Ciales,<ref>{{Cite book|last=Lugo|first=Ariel E.|url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/2159895415|title=Social-Ecological-Technological Effects of Hurricane María on Puerto Rico: Planning for Resilience under Extreme Events|publisher=[[Springer Publishing]]|year=2018|isbn=9783030023874|pages=3|chapter=Chapter 1: Introduction|author-link=Ariel Lugo|access-date=2021-10-13|url-access=subscription|archive-date=29 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211029173813/https://www.proquest.com/docview/2159895415/|id={{ProQuest|2159895415}} |url-status=live}}</ref> leaving all municipalities without power for months. Ciales received 19.23 inches of rain which caused landslides. An estimated 3,000 homes in Ciales were completely destroyed by Hurricane María.<ref>{{cite news| title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. Lluvia y vientos destructores acabaron con 3,000 hogares en Ciales| trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget. Destructive wind and rain finished 3,000 homes in Ciales| newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13| url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/ciales/| language=es| access-date=2021-09-19| archive-date=20 September 2021| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210920212611/https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/ciales/| url-status=live}}</ref> Three months after the hurricane struck, engineers were hoping to have electrical service established, at least for the Ciales barrio-pueblo (downtown) area.<ref>{{cite news | title=La AEE restablece el servicio en municipios del sur y centro de la isla | newspaper=El Nuevo Dia | date=23 December 2017 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/laaeerestableceelservicioenmunicipiosdelsurycentrodelaisla-2384689/ | language=es | access-date=25 June 2020 | archive-date=24 December 2017 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171224000953/https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/nota/laaeerestableceelservicioenmunicipiosdelsurycentrodelaisla-2384689/ | url-status=live }}</ref> The following December, the [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]] announced the opening of a disaster recovery center in Ciales to attend the home and business owners, as well as tenants, affected by the hurricane.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2017-12-17|title=Abren Centro de Recuperación por Desastre de FEMA en Ciales|trans-title=FEMA Disaster Recovery Center Opens in Ciales|url=https://www.fema.gov/es/press-release/20201016/fema-disaster-recovery-center-now-open-ciales|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004222812/https://www.fema.gov/es/press-release/20201016/fema-disaster-recovery-center-now-open-ciales|archive-date=4 October 2021|access-date=2021-10-04|website=[[Federal Emergency Management Agency]]|language=es}}</ref>

===Energy consortium===
An Energy Consortium was signed in late February, 2019 by [[Villalba, Puerto Rico|Villalba]], [[Orocovis]], [[Morovis]], Ciales and [[Barranquitas]] municipalities. The consortium is the first of its kind for the island. It is intended to have municipalities work together to safeguard and create resilient, and efficient energy networks, with backups for their communities. This is part of the hurricane preparedness plan of these municipalities, which were hit particularly hard by [[Hurricane Maria]] on September 20, 2017.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://industrialespr.org/communications/news/|title=Asociación de Industriales de Puerto Rico|first=Priscilla|last=Vazquez|publisher=Industriales Puerto Rico|access-date=2019-03-16|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190312030317/http://industrialespr.org/communications/news/|archive-date=2019-03-12|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Geography==
[[File:Homes and mountainside in Ciales, Puerto Rico.JPG|thumb|right|View of homes and mountainside of an area of Ciales from {{lang|es|Paseo Lineal Juan Antonio Corretjera}} lookout point]]
The northern part of the municipality is located in the [[Northern Karst (Puerto Rico)|Northern Karst]] zone of Puerto Rico while the southern part is in the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]]. The highest point in the municipality is [[Cerro Rosa]] at 4,143 feet (1,262&nbsp;m), itself the third highest point in Puerto Rico. Ciales is home to a forest reserve called [[Toro Negro State Forest|Toro Negro Forest Reserve]] and a number of rivers including: [[Río Cialitos]], [[Río Grande de Manatí]], [[Río Toro Negro]], [[Río Yunes]],<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/ciales-municipality/|title=Ciales Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190327090110/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/ciales-municipality/|archive-date=2019-03-27|url-status=live}}</ref> Pozas, and Barbas.<ref name="flyer" /> Over 40% of its territory falls within protected areas, ranking as the 6th municipality with the largest portion of protected territory overall.<ref>{{Cite journal|last1=Catro-Prieto|first1=Jessica|last2=Gould|first2=William A.|last3=Ortiz-Maldonado|first3=Coralys|last4=Soto-Bayó|first4=Sandra|last5=Llerandi-Román|first5=Iván|last6=Gatzambide-Arandes|first6=Soledad|last7=Quiñones|first7=Maya|last8=Cañón|first8=Marcela|last9=Jacobs|first9=Kasey R.|date=2019-10-03|title=A Comprehensive Inventory of Protected Areas and other Land Conservation Mechanisms in Puerto Rico|url=https://data.fs.usda.gov/research/pubs/iitf/iitf_gtr_50_eng.pdf#page=34|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210118213458/https://data.fs.usda.gov/research/pubs/iitf/iitf_gtr_50_eng.pdf#page=34|archive-date=18 January 2021|access-date=2021-10-04|website=[[United States Forest Service]]|publisher=[[International Institute of Tropical Forestry]]|page=67|format=PDF|doi=10.2737/IITF-GTR-50}}</ref> Consequentially, the destruction of 508 native planted trees at the Finca Don Ingenio in the Toro Negro Forest Reserve in August 2021 was caused for an uproar. The trees, which included [[ceiba]] and [[Thespesia grandiflora|maga]] specimens, had been planted as part of the [[Hurricane Maria|Hurricane María]] recovery by the Puerto Rico Conservation Trust's ''Para La Naturaleza'' program.<ref>{{Cite news|date=2021-09-02|title=Denuncian el corte de 508 árboles nativos en área protegida en Ciales|language=es|trans-title=Cutting of 508 Native Trees in a Protected Area in Ciales Denounced|work=[[EFE]]|url=https://www.efe.com/efe/usa/puerto-rico/denuncian-el-corte-de-508-arboles-nativos-en-area-protegida-ciales/50000110-4621036|access-date=2021-10-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210902140607/https://www.efe.com/efe/usa/puerto-rico/denuncian-el-corte-de-508-arboles-nativos-en-area-protegida-ciales/50000110-4621036|archive-date=2 September 2021|url-status=live}}</ref>

As part of the karst region, there are many caves, such as Archillas Cave, located in [[Jaguas, Ciales, Puerto Rico|Jaguas Ventana]], named after the family that owns it. Since Ciales has a saying "to graduate as a Cialeño, you have to go up to the Archillas,"{{efn|{{langx|es|para graduarse de cialeño hay que subir a las Archillas.}}|group=lower-alpha|name=Romanization}} then-mayor [[Luis Maldonado (politician)|Luis R. Maldonado Rodriguez]] attempted to acquire it. The cave has been associated with the Arcaicos, though it also holds some [[Taíno]] petroglyphs. The cave system was first studied by [[Alphonse Pinart|Alphonse L. Pinart]] in 1890. Since then several investigations have been carried out that have aided in the identification of [[silex]] as the main material used by the Taíno for their carving tools and the discovery of [[Taino archaeology|ceramic fragments]], as well as the theory that the caves were used for rituals, such as [[Cohoba|cojoba-induced ceremonies]].<ref name=":2">{{Cite news|last=Kuilan Torres|first=Sandra J.|date=2009-03-27|title=Tesoro en el corazón de Ciales|language=es|trans-title=Treasure in the Heart of Ciales|work=[[El Nuevo Día]]|url=https://www.cienciapr.org/es/external-news/tesoro-en-el-corazon-de-ciales|access-date=2021-10-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200918121106/https://www.cienciapr.org/es/external-news/tesoro-en-el-corazon-de-ciales|archive-date=18 September 2020|via=[[Ciencia Puerto Rico]]|url-status=live}}</ref> During his research in the early 1900s, [[Jesse Walter Fewkes]] identified the Ciales' caves as some those occupied by the Taíno<ref>{{Cite book|last1=Fewkes|first1=Jesse Walter|url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131779235|title=The Aborigines of Puerto Rico and Neighboring Islands|last2=Curet|first2=L. ==Culture==

===Sports===
Ciales is the home town of [[Juan "Pachín" Vicens]] – Puerto Rico's national basketball star, named Best Player in the World at the 1959 World Basketball Championship, Santiago de Chile (a.k.a., Juan "Pachín" Vicens, "Astro del Balón", "El Jeep"; younger brother of Puerto Rico's National Poet, Nimia Vicens, who also hailed from Ciales). Their middle brother, [[Enrique A. Vicéns|Enrique "Coco" Vicens]], a former Puerto Rico Senator, was a track and field athlete in his own right.

===Coat of arms===
The coat of arms consists of a gold shield with a lion standing on its rear legs and silver-plated nails grasping a silver coiled parchment between its front claws. The lion also shows a red tongue. Above the lion in the superior part of the shield are located three heraldic roses arranged horizontally with red petals and green leaves. A golden crown of three towers rests on the shield. The three towers are united by walls, simulating masonry blocks. The shield is surrounded by a crown of coffee tree branches with their berries, all in natural colors.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

* Cohoba City ({{Langx|es|Ciudad de la cojoba}}) for the discover of instruments used by the Taíno in the [[Cohoba|Cohoba ritual]], whereby they inhaled the hallucinogenic powder extracted from the [[cojoba]] seeds.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2" />
* Town of the Brave ({{Lang|es|Pueblo de los valerosos}}) for the high concentration of [[Independence movement in Puerto Rico|independence supports]], such as Juan Antonio Corretjer, some of which participated in the [[Grito de Lares]].<ref name=":0" />
* Cradle of Poets ({{Lang|es|Cuna de poetas}}) due to it being the birthplace of several poets, such as Juan Antonio Corretjer<ref name=":0" /> and Nimia Vicéns,<ref name=":3">{{Cite news|last=Tió|first=Elsa|date=2020-08-12|title=Nimia Vicéns: Corazón de la patria con semillas silvestres en sus versos|language=es|trans-title=Nimia Vicéns: Heart of the country with wild seeds in her verses|work=El Adoquín Times|url=https://eladoquintimes.com/2020/08/12/nimia-vicens-corazon-de-la-patria-con-semillas-silvestres-en-sus-versos/|access-date=2021-10-08|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210204025120/https://eladoquintimes.com/2020/08/12/nimia-vicens-corazon-de-la-patria-con-semillas-silvestres-en-sus-versos/|archive-date=2021-02-04}}</ref> both considered Puerto Rico's [[national poet]]s.<ref name=":3" /><ref name=":4">{{Cite web|last=García|first=Marta Yazmín|date=2008-11-07|title=Alabanza al poeta nacional|trans-title=Praise to the national poet|url=https://www.uprm.edu/news/articles/as2008168.html|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150910084201/https://www.uprm.edu/news/articles/as2008168.html|archive-date=2015-09-10|access-date=2021-10-08|website=[[University of Puerto Rico at Mayagüez]]|language=es}}</ref>
* Central Cordillera Gate ({{Lang|es|Puerta de la Cordillera Central}}) owing to its geographic location on the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]] mountainous range.<ref name="flyer">{{cite book | title=Ciales – Puerta de la Cordillera Central – Folleto informativo| publisher= Gobierno Municipal de Ciales, Puerto Rico|pages=3–4}}</ref>
* Switzerland of Puerto Rico ({{Lang|es|Suiza de Puerto Rico}}) on account of "its similarity to European valleys with abundant vegetation," and the [[Swiss chalet style|wooden houses]] that are located in them.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Cruz Ríos|first=Melissa M.|date=2021-01-27|title=See You in Ciales...|work=The Weekly Journal|url=https://www.theweeklyjournal.com/lifestyle/see-you-in-ciales/article_f8abf834-6013-11eb-830c-47f9ba85842c.html|access-date=2021-10-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210202172618/https://www.theweeklyjournal.com/lifestyle/see-you-in-ciales/article_f8abf834-6013-11eb-830c-47f9ba85842c.html|archive-date=2 February 2021|url-status=live}}</ref>
