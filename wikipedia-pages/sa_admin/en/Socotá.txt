'''Socotá''' is a town and municipality in the [[Valderrama Province]], part of the [[Colombia]]n [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]]. The municipality is situated in the [[Cordillera Oriental (Colombia)|Eastern Ranges]] of the Colombian [[Andes]]. The urban centre is at an altitude of {{convert|2443|m|ft}} at a distance of {{convert|134|km|mi}} from the department capital [[Tunja]]. It borders [[Jericó, Boyacá|Jericó]] and [[Sativanorte]] in the north, [[Pisba]] and [[Mongua]] in the south, [[Chita, Boyacá|Chita]] and [[Támara, Casanare|Támara]], [[Casanare Department|Casanare]] in the east and [[Sativasur]], [[Tasco, Boyacá|Tasco]], [[Socha]] and [[Gámeza]] in the west.<ref name=websiteSocota>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.socota-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Socotá]</ref>

== Etymology ==
The name Socotá comes from [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]] and means either "Land of the Sun and farmfields" or "Good harvest".<ref name=TuTiempo>{{in lang|es}} [https://tierra.tutiempo.net/Colombia/Socota-CO061601.html Etymology Socotá]</ref>

== History ==
Before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]], Socotá was the northeasternmost part of the loose [[Muisca Confederation]]. It was ruled either by the ''[[iraca]]'' of [[Sogamoso|Sugamuxi]] or by the ''[[Tundama]]'' based in [[Duitama|Tundama]].<ref name=TuTiempo/>

Modern Socotá was founded on January 19, 1602 by Gonzalo Sanchez de Flandes.<ref name=websiteSocota/>

== Economy ==
Main economic activity of Socotá is [[livestock]] farming and on a minor scale [[coal]] [[mining]].<ref name=websiteSocota/>
