'''Acacoyagua Municipality''' is a [[Municipalities of Chiapas|municipality]] in the [[List of states in Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Chiapas]] in southern [[Mexico]]. As of 2020, the municipality had a total population of 17,994,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Acacoyagua (Municipality, Mexico) - Population Statistics, Charts, Map and Location |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/mexico/admin/chiapas/07001__acacoyagua/ |access-date=2024-02-21 |website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref><ref name=SEDESOL>{{cite web|title=Acacoyagua|url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=07&mun=001|work=Catálogo de Localidades|publisher=[[Secretaría de Desarrollo Social]] (SEDESOL)|access-date=23 April 2014|archive-date=19 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200919111750/http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=07&mun=001|url-status=dead}}</ref> up from 14,189 as of 2005.

==History==
The area is notable for being the place where the first organized [[Japanese Mexican|Japanese immigrants]] settled in Mexico. In 1897, thirty five initial colonists led by [[Enomoto Takeaki]] arrived to work on coffee farms, making Mexico the first [[Latin American]] country to receive Japanese immigrants.<ref name="MOFA">{{cite web | author=Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan | title=Japan-Mexico foreign relations | year=2012| url=http://www.mofa.go.jp/region/latin/mexico/data.html | publisher=MOFA |  access-date=October 22, 2014}}</ref> Although the coffee endeavor failed, the colonists began farming vegetables and flowers. Over the following 30 years, six more "waves" of migrants followed. As most of the migrants were men, marriage with local women and conversion to Roman Catholicism was common.<ref name="Kleist">{{cite web|last1=Kleist|first1=Trina|title=Japanese-Mexicans work to preserve their heritage|url=http://www.deseretnews.com/article/604844/Japanese-Mexicans-work-to-preserve-their-heritage.html?pg=all|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160816000116/http://www.deseretnews.com/article/604844/Japanese-Mexicans-work-to-preserve-their-heritage.html?pg=all|url-status=dead|archive-date=August 16, 2016|publisher=Deseret News|access-date=12 August 2016}}</ref>

[[Nikkei people|Nikkei]] have become [[Municipal president (Mexico)|Municipal President]], the first being Juvencio Nishizama Cruz in 1954 and the latest being José Martín Nomura Hernandez in 2005.<ref name="Garc’a">{{cite book|author=Jerry García| title=[[Looking Like the Enemy: Japanese Mexicans, the Mexican State, and US Hegemony, 1897-1945]] |date= 27 February 2014|publisher=University of Arizona Press |isbn=978-0816530250
|page=[https://books.google.com/books?id=1oLfAgAAQBAJ&dq=acacoyagua&pg=PA196 196]}}</ref> The main garden in town has Japanese influences and there is a monument commemorating the Japanese migrants. 

In May 1997, [[Prince Akishino]], Ambassador Teresuke Terada and members of the Japanese Navy visited the town to mark the hundred year anniversary of the arrival of the first colonists.<ref name="Kleist" />

In the early 2000s, Acacoyagua Municipality, attracted renewed interest from Japanese cultural institutions due to its small but historically rooted Japanese community.<ref name="Tanaka2004">Tanaka, H. "Nihonjin en Chiapas: La historia olvidada del sur mexicano." ''Revista de Estudios Asiático-Latinoamericanos'', Vol. 8 (2004): 55–63.</ref> In 2003, the Japanese hip-hop group [[Kick the Can Crew]] performed an outdoor concert in Acacoyagua following their appearance at the ''Festival Internacional de Sonido Urbano'' in [[Mexico City|Ciudad de México]].<ref name="Fujiwara2003">Fujiwara, K. "Del DF a Chiapas: el viaje inesperado de Kick the Can Crew." ''Sonido Global'', No. 27 (2003): 22–25.</ref> The visit was later cited as an early example of trans-Pacific cultural exchange between Japan's hip-hop scene and southern Mexico's emerging youth movements.<ref name="Mendoza2007">Mendoza, R. "Ritmos cruzados: Hip-hop japonés y latinoamericano, 1995–2005." ''Cuadernos de Cultura Contemporánea'', Vol. 5 (2007): 88–90.</ref>

==Demographics==
As of 2010, the town of Acacoyagua had a population of 7,515.<ref name=SEDESOL/> Other than the town of Acacoyagua, the municipality had 152 localities, the largest of which (with 2010 populations in parentheses) was: [[Hidalgo, Chiapas|Hidalgo]] (1,209), classified as rural.<ref name=SEDESOL/>
