'''Chiconcuac de Juárez''', typically referred to simply as Chiconcuac, is a town and ''[[municipio]]'' ([[municipalities of Mexico|municipality]]) in the [[state of Mexico]], approximately 10 kilometers north of [[Texcoco, State of Mexico|Texcoco de Mora]]. The name Chiconcuac derives from the Aztec word ''Chicome Coatl'', “Seven snakes”, which was a date on the [[Aztec calendar]].<ref name="EncMuc">{{cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15030a.htm |title=Enciclopedia de Municipios de Mexico Estado de Mexico Chiconcuac |accessdate=2008-03-07 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927205353/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15030a.htm |archivedate=2007-09-27 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==The city==

Chiconcuac is principally famous for its large ''[[tianguis]]'', or public market, which has 2,400 permanent vendors, and attracts up to 6,000 more semi-permanent and itinerant vendors on weekends. The large food court in the Chiconcuac market is widely famous for preparing ''[[barbacoa]] de borrego'' ([[Lamb and mutton|lamb]] [[barbecue]]), and other specialties of [[Mexican cuisine]].

===History===

The area which is now all one city, was a cluster of settlements of mostly [[Chichimeca]]s and [[Tlailotlaque]]s.

The area was conquered by the Spanish in 1597, with land here granted to Hernando Núñez where he established the [[Hacienda]] de Santa Cruz de Prado Alegre, better known as the  “Arojo”.  The area was then evangelized by the [[Franciscans]]. Because of its proximity to [[Mexico City]], the city suffered battles during the [[Mexican War of Independence]], the [[Second French intervention in Mexico]] and the War of "[[La Reforma]]".<ref name="EncMuc"/>

In 1980, [[mammoth]] bones were discovered in a neighborhood called Calxiacatl.<ref name="EncMuc"/>

==The municipality==

As municipal seat, Chiconcuac de Juárez has jurisdiction over only one other community, which is called Ejido San Cristóbal.<ref name="Pueblos">{{cite web |url=http://mexico.pueblosamerica.com/mexico/chiconcuac/ |title=Pueblos America Mexico México Chiconcuac |accessdate= 2008-03-07 }}</ref>

The municipality borders with [[San Salvador Atenco|Atenco]], [[Chiautla, State of Mexico|Chiautla]] and [[Texcoco, State of Mexico|Texcoco]].<ref name="EncMuc"/>
