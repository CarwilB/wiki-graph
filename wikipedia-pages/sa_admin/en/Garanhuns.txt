'''Garanhuns''' ({{IPA|pt-br|garɐˈɲus}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[Agreste]] region of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Pernambuco]], located 230 kilometers from the state capital, [[Recife]].<ref name="Distância"/> It covers an area of {{formatnum:458.552|km²}} km² and belongs to the Caruaru Intermediate Geographic Region, serving as the principal and most populous municipality in the Garanhuns Immediate Geographic Region. According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) in [[2022 Brazilian Census|2022 Census]], Garanhuns had an estimated population of approximately 142,506 inhabitants, making it the ninth most populous municipality in Pernambuco, the third most populous in the state's interior, and the second most populous in the Pernambucan Agreste region.<ref name="IBGE" /><ref name="IBGE_Pop_2016">{{cite web|url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/cidades-e-estados/pe/garanhuns.html|title=POPULAÇÃO|publisher=IBGE|access-date=2 May 2025}}</ref>

Originally, the lands of Garanhuns were inhabited by the [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous]] [[Kiriri people|Cariri]] people. During the 17h century, [[White Brazilians|white colonists]] and [[African diaspora in the Americas|enslaved Africans]] escaping Dutch domination in [[Dutch Brazil]] established communities in the region's [[Caatinga moist-forest enclaves]], establishing scattered [[village]]s. On 29 September 1658, ''[[Mestre de camp]]o'' Nicolau Aranha Pacheco, Captain Cosmo de Brito Cação, Antonio Fernandes Aranha, and Ambrósio Aranha de Farias received a land grant of approximately 20 [[League (unit)|leagues]] from the acting governor, [[André Vidal de Negreiros]]. This grant included two plots: one in the fields of Garanhuns and another in Panema. That same year, the Garcia Farm was established in the area now encompassing the municipal seat.<ref name="História">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/pernambuco/garanhuns.pdf|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6Ni6nSZWd?url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/pernambuco/garanhuns.pdf|archive-date=2014-02-27|title=Histórico do município de Garanhuns|author=IBGE|access-date=27 February 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns is the most diversified hub in the southern Agreste, serving as a center for 32 municipalities and supporting a surrounding population of over one million people. It is a regional leader in healthcare and education services. The municipality's vibrant [[commerce]] and service offerings make [[tourism]] a significant driver of employment, income, and development, supported by a robust network of service providers and hotels. Each July, Garanhuns hosts the Garanhuns Winter Festival, attracting thousands of tourists from around the world.<ref name="Características">{{cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/perfil-da-cidade|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6Ni5Ld5vI?url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/perfil-da-cidade|archive-date=2014-02-27|title=Perfil da cidade|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|access-date=27 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="Turismo">{{cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/espaco-turista/potencial-turistico|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6Ni7GUFOv?url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/espaco-turista/potencial-turistico|archive-date=2014-02-27|title=Potencial turístico|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|access-date=27 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
[[File:Garanhuns-pórtico.jpg|thumb|left|Portico at the entrance to Garanhuns]]
The origin of the [[toponym]] Garanhuns is highly debated, with multiple interpretations. Lexicographer José de Almeida Maciel, in his book "Issues of Municipal Toponymy," suggests the name derives from ''guirá-nhum'', meaning "black birds," referring to an indigenous group in the area. According to Professor João de Deus Oliveira Dias (cited in the Brazilian Territorial Documentation by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]]), the term comes from the C''airu'' people, a branch of the C''ariri'' or Q''uiriri'', derived from ''Guiranhu'' or ''Unhannhum'', combining ''guirá'' or ''[[Scarlet ibis|guará]]'' (a red-legged aquatic bird) and ''anhu'' or ''anhum'' (''[[Ani (bird)|anum]]'', a black bird), species that inhabited the valley of the [[Mundaú River]] near its [[River source|source]], where the original village was located. Conversely, lexicographer Sebastião de Vasconcelos Galvão, in his "Chorographic, Historical, and Statistical Dictionary of Pernambuco," posits that the name is of indigenous origin, meaning "site of ''guarás'' and ''anuns''" (''guarás'' being a species of wild dog and ''anuns'' a bird considered an ill omen).<ref name="A">{{cite book|last=Fonseca|first=Homero|title=Pernambucânia: o que há nos nomes das nossas cidades|location=Recife|publisher=Cepe|year=2009}}</ref><ref name="B">{{cite book|last=Galvão|first=Sebastião de V.|title=Dicionário Corográfico, Histórico e Estatístico de Pernambuco|location=Recife|publisher=Cepe|year=2006|volume=1}}</ref><ref name="C">{{cite book|last=Navarro|first=E. A.|title=[[Dicionário de Tupi Antigo|Dicionário de tupi antigo: a língua indígena clássica do Brasil]]|location=São Paulo|publisher=Global|year=2013|page=560}}</ref>

Writer Ademilson Antônio Macedo, in his "Dictionary of Names, Origins, and Meanings of Brazilian Municipalities," translates the name as "man of the field." According to writer Mário Melo, the toponym is a [[corruption (linguistics)|corruption]] of ''guirá-nhum'', evolving into ''guará-nhum'', meaning "black individual," referring to the ''[[quilombo]]'' in the mountains. He notes, "... even today, the [[Fulniô|Carijó]] Indians of [[Águas Belas]] know Garanhuns as ''claiô'' in their [[Yatê language]]: ''claí'' (white), ''iô'' (not): not white, dark, black. It should also be noted that the Garanhuns mountain range was named after the ''Garanhuns'' tribe, which was of ''Cariri'' origin and inhabited the area."<ref name="A"/><ref name="B"/>

[[Tupinology|Tupinologist]] [[Eduardo de Almeida Navarro|Eduardo Navarro]] asserts in his ''[[Dictionary of Old Tupi]]'' (2013) that the toponym originates from [[Old Tupi language|Old Tupi]], combining ''agûará'' + ''nhũ'', meaning "[[grassland]] (''nhũ'') of [[maned wolf|maned wolves]] (''agûará'')".<ref name="C"/>

== History ==
[[File:Igreja de Santo Antonio - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|left|Saint Anthony Cathedral, in the center of Garanhuns]]
[[File:Garanhuns (PE).tif|thumb|left|Garanhuns, August 1961. [[National Archives of Brazil|National Archives]]]]
European settlement of the lands now comprising Garanhuns began in the 17th century. [[White Brazilians|White colonists]] and [[African diaspora in the Americas|enslaved Africans]] escaping Dutch domination in [[Dutch Brazil]] established communities in the region's [[Caatinga moist-forest enclaves]], establishing scattered [[village]]s. On 29 September 1658, ''[[Mestre de camp]]o'' Nicolau Aranha Pacheco, Captain Cosmo de Brito Cação, Antonio Fernandes Aranha, and Ambrósio Aranha de Farias received a land grant of approximately 20 [[League (unit)|leagues]] from the acting governor, [[André Vidal de Negreiros]]. This grant included two plots: one in the fields of Garanhuns and another in Panema. That same year, the Garcia Farm was established in the area now encompassing the municipal seat. Evidence suggests the farm was thriving until around 1670, when it was destroyed during conflicts with ''[[quilombolas]]'' from Curica, in Alto do Magano, thereafter known as Tapera do Garcia, later simplified to Tapera.<ref name="D">{{cite book|title=Calendário Oficial de Datas Históricas dos Municípios de Pernambuco|publisher=Agência CONDEPE/FIDEM|year=2006|volume=3}}</ref> "Tapera" is an Old Tupi word meaning "extinct, destroyed village," now meaning "ruined house, abandoned house" in modern Portuguese.<ref>{{cite book|last=Navarro|first=E. A.|title=[[Dicionário de Tupi Antigo|Dicionário de Tupi Antigoː a Língua Indígena Clássica do Brasil]]|location=São Paulo|publisher=Global|year=2013|page=462}}</ref>

The [[Palmares (quilombo)|Palmares War]] (second half of the 17th century) significantly hindered the region's progress, as farms faced constant threats of territorial reclamation by resistant ''[[quilombola]]'' communities, forcing white, privileged, and slaveholding populations to abandon them. After the war ended in 1696, the region developed, and in 1699, a [[royal charter]] established judicial districts in various parishes in the hinterland, including Garanhuns, which became the seat of the "Ararobá Sertão Captaincy," encompassing the area between [[Pesqueira, Pernambuco|Cimbres]] and [[Serra Talhada|Pajeú das Flores]].<ref name="D"/>

In 1704, the Tapera do Garcia was purchased by [[Colonel]] Manuel Pereira de Azevedo. Following his death, it was managed by his widow, Dona Simoa Gomes de Azevedo. In 1756, she donated a half-league square of land to the Confraternity of Souls of the Garanhuns Parish, where the city later developed. With the creation of the Village of Cimbres in 1762 and its establishment the following year, Garanhuns lost its status as the captaincy seat, becoming merely the seat of the Santo Antônio de Garanhuns parish. In 1796, the local chapel ceased to be a [[curacy]] and became the seat of a [[deanery]]; on 15 August 1800, the parish of Santo Antônio of the Garanhuns settlement was established.<ref>{{cite book|title=ENCICLOPÉDIA DOS MUNICÍPIOS BRASILEIROS|location=Rio de Janeiro|publisher=IBGE|year=1958|volume=18}}</ref>
[[File:Seminário São José - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|left|São José Seminary]]
By the late 19th century, the construction of a railway connecting Garanhuns to the capital of [[Pernambuco]] spurred significant economic and population growth, driven by the [[service industry]]. The reduced travel time between coastal and inland cities led to a rapid increase in hotels and guesthouses, boosting [[rural tourism]]. In the 1990s, the establishment of the city's winter festival solidified tourism as the region's primary income source.<ref name="Ferrovia">{{cite web|url=http://jconline.ne10.uol.com.br/canal/economia/pernambuco/noticia/2017/07/19/frio-impulsiona-turismo-de-garanhuns-e-triunfo-296179.php|title=Frio impulsiona turismo de Garanhuns e Triunfo|date=19 July 2017|access-date=9 May 2018}}</ref>

Until early 2014, the municipality celebrated its emancipation on 4 February. This date was revised following research by the president of the Garanhuns Historical and Geographical Institute at the [[Torre do Tombo National Archive|Museu do Tombo]] in [[Portugal]]. While searching for documents related to the attack by [[Domingos Jorge Velho]] on the city, the president discovered a Royal Charter signed by [[John IV of Portugal|John IV]], elevating Garanhuns to village status. This finding led to compliance with a constitutional law limiting municipalities to four holidays. Consequently, the emancipation day is now celebrated as a significant date, never falling on a weekday.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/02/aniversario-de-garanhuns-e-mudado-apos-descoberta-de-texto-portugues.html|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140818001051/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/02/aniversario-de-garanhuns-e-mudado-apos-descoberta-de-texto-portugues.html|archive-date=18 August 2014|title=Aniversário de Garanhuns é mudado após descoberta de texto português|author=G1 Pernambuco|date=4 February 2014|access-date=17 August 2014}}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Garanhuns e localidades próximas.svg|thumb|left|Garanhuns and neighboring municipalities]]
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], Garanhuns spans {{formatnum:458.55|square kilometers}} km², with {{formatnum:7.11|km²}} km² forming the urban area and {{formatnum:451.44|km²}} km² comprising the rural zone.<ref name="Território_2">{{cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1076&Cod=3|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6NDHLt2CN?url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1076&Cod=3|archive-date=7 February 2014|title=Áreas dos municípios - 2010|author=BDE|access-date=25 February 2014}}</ref> It is located at 08°53'25" [[Latitude|south latitude]] and 36°29'34" [[Longitude|west longitude]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.geografos.com.br/cidades-pernambuco/garanhuns.php|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OdutU43I?url=http://www.geografos.com.br/cidades-pernambuco/garanhuns.php|archive-date=2014-04-06|title=Coordenadas geográficas de Garanhuns|author=Geógrafos|access-date=6 April 2014}}</ref> approximately 230&nbsp;km from the [[Recife|state capital]].<ref name="Distância"/> Its neighboring municipalities are [[Capoeiras]] and [[Jucati]] to the north; [[Lagoa do Ouro]] and [[Correntes, Pernambuco|Correntes]] to the south; [[São João, Pernambuco|São João]] and [[Palmeirina]] to the east; and [[Caetés, Pernambuco|Caetés]], [[Saloá]], [[Paranatama]], [[Brejão]], and [[Terezinha, Pernambuco|Terezinha]] to the west.<ref name="Ambiente">{{cite web|url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/rehi/atlas/pernambuco/relatorios/GARA064.pdf|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6Ni606Wmm?url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/rehi/atlas/pernambuco/relatorios/GARA064.pdf|archive-date=2014-02-27|title=Diagnóstico do município de Garanhuns|author=Companhia Pernambucana de Recursos Hídricos (CPRH)|access-date=27 February 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns lies within the ''Reworked Surfaces'' geo-environmental unit, characterized by heavily eroded terrain with deeply incised valleys.<ref name="Ambiente"/> Situated on the [[Borborema Plateau]], the municipality is surrounded by the Antas, Columinho, Ipiranga, Magano, Monte Sinai, Quilombo, and Triunfo hills.<ref name="Garanhns">{{cite web|url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-garanhuns.shtml|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OdveOTaE?url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-garanhuns.shtml|archive-date=6 April 2014|title=Garanhuns|author=Folha Online|access-date=6 April 2014}}</ref> Its altitude is 841 meters above [[sea level]] at the city's birthplace, though some areas, such as Monte Magano ({{formatnum:1030|m}} m), exceed {{formatnum:1000}} meters.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/espaco-turista/um-pouco-de-garanhuns|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OdvSEq7m?url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/espaco-turista/um-pouco-de-garanhuns|archive-date=2014-04-06|title=Um pouco de Garanhuns|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|access-date=6 April 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns is part of the [[Mundaú River]] basin, with its main [[watercourse]]s being the [[Mundaú River|Mundaú]], [[Canhoto River|Canhoto]], and [[Inhumas River|Inhaúma]] rivers, alongside streams such as São Pedro, São Vicente, Mimosinho, Seco, Mocambo, Repartição, Imbé, Mochila, Pacheco, das Pedras, Baixa da Lama, Estrondo, da Laje, do Dunga, Periperi, and Timbó. All watercourses are intermittent, following a [[dendritic drainage]] pattern. The most significant reservoirs are the Mundaú, with a capacity of {{formatnum:1968600|m³}} m³, and the Garanhuns public reservoir.<ref name="Ambiente"/>

Garanhuns has a [[tropical savanna climate]], featuring a dry summer and wet winter, categorized as ''As'' in the [[Köppen climate classification]] (bordering on a [[Mediterranean climate]], type ''Csa'').<ref name="Ferrovia"/> Due to its elevation, temperatures are mild and cooler than in most of the region throughout the year. The average annual temperature is {{formatnum:21|°C}} °C,<ref name="normais">{{cite web|url=https://portal.inmet.gov.br/normais|title=Normais climatológicas do Brasil|author=INMET|access-date=23 January 2023}}</ref> dropping to {{formatnum:15|°C}} °C or lower during the coldest months,<ref name="Ferrovia"/> which are also typically the rainiest. The annual [[precipitation]] index is approximately 890&nbsp;mm, with relatively high [[humidity]] year-round. The average annual [[sunshine duration|sunshine]] is {{formatnum:2330}} hours, peaking between October and December.<ref name="normais"/>

According to data from the [[weather station]] of the [[National Institute of Meteorology]] (INMET) in Garanhuns, since December 1963, the absolute minimum temperature recorded was {{formatnum:9|°C}} °C on 22 July 1999, and the historical maximum reached {{formatnum:35.3|°C}} °C on 5 September 1993. The highest 24-hour precipitation accumulation was {{formatnum:114|mm}} mm on 27 December 2002, followed by {{formatnum:100.9|mm}} mm on 25 March 2005, and {{formatnum:100.8|mm}} mm on 2 July 2022.<ref name="bdmep.inmet.gov.br"/><ref name="INMET-A322-Garanhuns-PE">{{cite web|url=https://tempo.inmet.gov.br/TabelaEstacoes/A322|title=Estação: GARANHUNS (A322)|author=INMET|access-date=17 July 2020}}</ref>

=== Ecology and environment ===
<ref>{{cite book|last=Mouret|first=Geraldo|title=Blog do Mouret: compromisso com a notícia|location=Garanhuns|year=2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns is home to two predominant types of native vegetation: the [[Atlantic Forest]] and the [[Caatinga]]. To mitigate the environmental impact of urbanization, local authorities, particularly the municipal government, have implemented policies to create green spaces and areas for leisure, social interaction, and contemplation to enhance the quality of life for residents. The city features three municipal parks: Euclides Dourado Park, Ruber van der Linden Park, and the Mundaú River Springs Natural Park. The first two are classified as sustainable use units, while the latter is designated for full conservation.<ref>{{cite book|last=Vieira|first=Alfredo|title=Garanhuns do meu tempo: memória|location=Recife|publisher=FIAM. Centro de Estudos de História Municipal (Coleção Tempo Municipal, 18)|year=1997}}</ref>

[[File:Parque Ruber Van Der Linden - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|left|Ruber van der Linden Municipal Park]]

Euclides Dourado Municipal Park, also known as the "Eucalyptus Park," is the primary sustainable use unit and the first park established in Garanhuns. Originally an 8-hectare eucalyptus grove, it was transformed into a municipal park and zoo during the administration of Mayor Euclides Dourado in the late 1920s, making it the fifth oldest park in Brazil. Designed and built by Euclides Dourado and completed by his son, Luiz Souto Dourado, the park was renamed in honor of its creator in 1943 after his death. Located a short distance from downtown Garanhuns in the Heliópolis neighborhood, the park is primarily composed of century-old eucalyptus trees, a significant natural asset of the municipality. Over the years, it has been enhanced with recreational, sports, and children's facilities. It hosts one of Pernambuco's most important artistic and cultural events every year: the Garanhuns Winter Festival.<ref>{{cite book|last=Cavalcanti|first=Alfredo Leite|title=História de Garanhuns|location=Recife|publisher=FIAM/Centro de Estudos de História Municipal|year=1983}}; GARANHUNS. Decreto Lei Nº 023/2011.</ref>

Ruber van der Linden Municipal Park is named after a distinguished Garanhuns resident, an electrical engineer who developed and implemented the city's water and electricity supply plan, serving as the general manager of the utility company. Established by Mayor Luiz da Silva Guerra, the park transformed the Pau-Pombo site—a scenic area with large trees and a spring—into a public visitation space. It has been improved over time to cater to residents and tourists seeking leisure and entertainment.<ref name="Ecologia">{{cite web|url=http://www.revistadialogos.com.br/dialogos_9/carlos_guedes.pdf|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OduB6um8?url=http://www.revistadialogos.com.br/dialogos_9/carlos_guedes.pdf|archive-date=2014-04-06|title=Os Impactos Ambientais nos Parques Urbanos de Garanhuns - PE|author=Revista Diálogo|access-date=4 April 2014}}</ref>

[[File:Parque Euclides Dourado (Parque dos Eucaliptos) - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|Euclides Dourado Park (Eucalyptus Park)]]

The Mundaú Springs Natural Municipal Park is located in the Várzea neighborhood, between urban and rural areas. The park hosts a municipal nursery project that produces fruit, tree, and ornamental seedlings. Spanning 34 hectares, it preserves 65 species of flora and 66 species of fauna, as cataloged by professionals from the former Agriculture and Environment Secretariat and the Municipal Environmental Defense Council.<ref name="Ecologia"/>

In the northern and western parts of the municipality, where the Caatinga predominates, the vegetation consists of hyperxerophytic species, including shrubs with twisted branches and deep roots. Common species include [[cactus]], caroá, [[aroeira]], angico, juazeiro, [[mandacaru]], and Deering's tree cactus. Contrary to common perceptions, the Caatinga fauna is diverse, hosting species such as the [[gray brocket]], [[Brazilian guinea pig]], [[opossum]], [[cane toad]], [[agouti]], [[Six-banded armadillo|yellow armadillo]], [[Spix's macaw]], [[picazuro pigeon]], and [[Callithrix jacchus|common marmoset]].<ref name="Ambiente"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/caatinga.htm|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6NJUnpK4l?url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/caatinga.htm|archive-date=2014-02-11|title=Vegetação - Caatinga|author=Sua Pesquisa|access-date=11 February 2014}}</ref>

In the southern and eastern regions, dominated by the Atlantic Forest, the vegetation comprises medium to large trees, forming dense, closed forests. This biome is highly biodiverse, with large trees creating a shaded, humid [[microclimate]]. Common species include [[Arecaceae|palms]], [[bromeliad]]s, [[begonia]]s, [[orchid]]s, [[liana]]s, [[Bryophyta sensu stricto|mosses]], [[Caesalpinia echinata|brazilwood]], [[jacaranda]], [[Aspidosperma spruceanum|''peroba'']], ''[[Cariniana legalis|jequitibá-rosa]]'', [[Cedrus|cedar]], [[andira]], [[pineapple]], and [[fig tree]]s. Many animal species in this biome, such as the [[golden lion tamarin]], [[howler monkey]], [[giant anteater]], [[giant armadillo]], [[glaucous macaw]], and [[jaguar]], are endangered.<ref name="Ambiente"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/mata_atlantica.htm|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6NDJCYAGf?url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/mata_atlantica.htm|archive-date=2014-02-07|title=Vegetação da Mata Atlântica|author=Sua Pesquisa|access-date=11 February 2014}}</ref>

According to the 2010 census by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], Garanhuns had a population of {{formatnum:129408}} inhabitants, with {{formatnum:68432}} females (52.88%) and {{formatnum:60976}} males (47.12%). Of these, {{formatnum:115356}} lived in the urban area (89.14%), and {{formatnum:14052}} resided in the rural zone (10.86%). The urbanization rate was 89.14%.<ref name="Atlas">{{cite web|url=http://www.atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/pt/perfil/garanhuns_pe|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140412171420/http://www.atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/pt/perfil/garanhuns_pe|archive-date=12 April 2014|title=Perfil - Garanhuns, PE|author=Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano|date=2013|publisher=[[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP)|access-date=12 April 2014}}</ref> The 2014 IBGE population estimate recorded {{formatnum:136057}} inhabitants, making Garanhuns the ninth largest municipality in Pernambuco, with a [[population density]] of {{formatnum:287.97|inhabitants/km²}} inhabitants/km².<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2016"/>

=== Poverty and Inequality ===
[[File:Garanhuns - Pernambuco - Brasil(2).jpg|thumb|240px|Street in the Heliópolis neighborhood]]

In 2000, 43.3% of the population lived with a per capita income below 140 [[Brazilian real|reais]], a figure that decreased to approximately 28.3% by 2010. Despite this reduction, about {{formatnum:36143}} people remain in poverty. In 2010, 71.7% of residents were above the poverty and indigence line, 20.6% were at the line, and 22.8% were below it.<ref name="ODM">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415190729/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Acabar com a Fome e a Miséria|author=Portal ODM|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures regional inequality, was 0.599 in 2010, with values closer to 0.00 indicating less inequality and closer to 1.00 indicating greater inequality.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415191013/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Perfil municipal de Garanhuns|author=Portal ODM|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref> The share of the poorest 20% in the municipality's wealth decreased from 2.7% in 1991 to 2.4% in 2010, indicating rising inequality. The richest 20% accounted for 62.3% of the municipal wealth in 2010, 26 times higher than the poorest 20%.<ref name="ODM"/>

Despite its moderate population, Garanhuns has communities and degraded areas lacking access to basic services such as potable water, sewage collection and treatment, and paved streets. Although irregular housing exists, the 2010 IBGE census did not identify any subnormal agglomerates, as their number is small and does not meet all criteria.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/7-qualidade-de-vida-e-respeito-ao-meio-ambiente/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415191212/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/7-qualidade-de-vida-e-respeito-ao-meio-ambiente/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Garantir qualidade de vida e respeito ao Meio Ambiente|author=Portal ODM|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

In early 2013, the ''[[Central Única das Favelas]]'' (CUFA) began operating in Garanhuns following meetings between project representatives and municipal officials. Partnerships were established with the municipal government, particularly with the Secretariats of Social Assistance, Human Rights, Health, Education, and the Sports Directorate.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/central-unica-das-favelas-comeca-a-funcionar-em-garanhuns/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415212301/http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/central-unica-das-favelas-comeca-a-funcionar-em-garanhuns/|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Central Única de Favelas começa a funcionar em Garanhuns|author=Blog do Mário Flávio|date=29 January 2013 |access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

=== Religion ===

According to the 2010 census by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, most Garanhuns residents identify as [[Catholicism|Catholic]] (76.8%). [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]] is less common, with 1.01% adherence, while [[Protestantism|evangelical]] denominations are prevalent, collectively representing 16.4%. Followers of [[African diaspora religions]], such as [[Umbanda]] (0.01%), are minimal, with no recorded adherents of [[Candomblé]]. [[Eastern religions]], including [[Buddhism]] (0.01%), [[Hinduism]] (0.02%), and [[Islam]], showed negligible or no presence. Practitioners of [[Western esotericism|esoteric traditions]] accounted for 0.1%. Those identifying as [[Irreligion|non-religious]] comprised 4.0%, [[Atheism|atheists]] and [[Agnosticism|agnostics]] 0.14% and 0.1%, respectively, and those with undetermined religion or multiple affiliations were 0.15%.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=13&i=P&c=2094|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415195654/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&i=P&nome=on&qtu8=137&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=2094&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&poc133=2&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&nivt=0&orc86=3&orp=5&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&poc86=1&opc133=2&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&orc133=4&qtu2=5&qtu15=3&sev=93&opc86=1&sec133=0&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&opn13=0&sec86=0&ascendente=on&sep=38559&orn=1&qtu7=36&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Garanhuns&OpcCara=44&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&opn7=0&decm=99|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Censo demográfico 2010: resultados da amostra - religião|author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA)|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

According to the organization of the [[Catholic Church]], Garanhuns is part of the Ecclesiastical Province of Olinda and Recife, headquartered in [[Olinda]], within the [[Greater Recife|Recife Metropolitan Region]], under the [[Archdiocese of Olinda and Recife]]. The [[Diocese of Garanhuns]] was proposed by [[Monsignor]] Afonso Pequeno and endorsed by the then-[[archbishop]] of Olinda and Recife, Dom Sebastião Leme da Silveira Cintra. It was officially established by [[Pope Benedict XV]] through the bull "''Archidioecesís Olindensis et Recifensis''" on 2 August 1918, when it was separated from the archdiocese of the capital. The same bull created the Dioceses of [[Nazaré da Mata]] and [[Pesqueira, Pernambuco|Pesqueira]]. The diocese encompasses 26 Pernambucan municipalities, including two in the Forest Zone ([[Quipapá]] and [[São Benedito do Sul]]) and 24 in the [[Agreste]], including [[Bom Conselho]], [[São Bento do Una]], and [[Lajedo, Pernambuco|Lajedo]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diocesegaranhuns.org/novo/paroquias.php|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415203630/http://www.diocesegaranhuns.org/novo/historico.php|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Histórico da Diocese de Garanhuns|author=Diocese de Garanhuns|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

The community comprises six [[parish]]es: Nossa Senhora do Perpétuo Socorro, Santa Teresa do Menino Jesus, Santo Antônio (Cathedral), São Sebastião, Sagrada Família, and Sagrado Coração de Jesus.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diocesegaranhuns.org/novo/paroquias.php|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415203841/http://www.diocesegaranhuns.org/novo/paroquias.php|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Paróquias da Diocese de Garanhuns|author=Diocese de Garanhuns|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

;Protestant Churches

Garanhuns has a significant number of evangelical churches across various denominations. The [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembly of God]] is the most prominent, with 3.8% of the population, followed by the [[Presbyterian Church of Brazil|Presbyterian Church]] (established in 1900) at 1.60%, and the [[Christian Congregation in Brazil]] at 1.48%. Other notable evangelical churches include the [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]] (0.96%), Maranatha Christian Church (0.15%), [[Foursquare Church]] (0.08%), [[Brazil for Christ Pentecostal Church]] (0.06%), [[God Is Love Pentecostal Church]], and House of Blessing Church (0.05% each). According to the IBGE, evangelicals are further categorized as ''Mission Evangelicals'' (4.0%) and ''Pentecostal'' (7.6%). Followers of evangelical churches without a specific denomination accounted for 4.6%.<ref name="Religião"/>

Other Christian minorities include [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (0.34%) and members of [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (0.26%). Adherents of [[Spiritualism (movement)|spiritualism]] represented 0.01%.<ref name="Religião"/>

== Political and administrative organization ==
The [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] of Garanhuns is governed by a political-administrative structure comprising the [[Executive (government)|Executive Branch]], led by a mayor elected by universal suffrage, who is directly supported by appointed municipal secretaries, and the [[Legislature|Legislative Branch]], embodied by the Garanhuns Municipal Chamber, a [[collegial body]] of 17 councilors also elected by universal suffrage.<ref>{{cite book|last=Meirelles|first=Hely Lopes|title=Direito municipal brasileiro|edition=18|location=São Paulo|publisher=Malheiros|year=2017}}</ref>
[[File:Garanhuns-Prefeitura-Palácio-Celso-Galvão.jpg|thumb|right|''Celso Galvão Palace'', seat of the municipal government]]

The municipality operates under an organic law, promulgated on 4 April 1990, effective from the same date.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.leg.br/index.php/lei-organica|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415220010/http://www.garanhuns.pe.leg.br/index.php/lei-organica|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Lei Orgânica de Garanhuns|author=Câmara Municipal de Garanhuns|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref> It is the seat of the ''[[Comarca]]'' of Garanhuns.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tjpe.jus.br/falecomjud/Comarcas/consultacomarca.asp|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415215443/http://www.tjpe.jus.br/falecomjud/Comarcas/consultacomarca.asp|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Consulta de Comarcas|author=Tribunal de Justiça de Pernambuco|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref> According to the Pernambuco Regional Electoral Court (TRE-PE), in 2020, Garanhuns had 90,841 registered voters, making it the tenth largest electoral district in Pernambuco.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=920&Cod=3|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140415215819/http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=920&Cod=3|archive-date=15 April 2014|title=Eleitores por município|author=BDE|access-date=15 April 2014}}</ref>

== Economy ==
[[File:Floral clock - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brazil.jpg|thumb|270px|Tourism significantly drives Garanhuns' economy during certain periods. Pictured: the Flower Clock]]

According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, Garanhuns' [[Gross Domestic Product]] (GDP) is the 413th largest in Brazil and the 11th largest in Pernambuco. In 2011, the GDP was R$ {{formatnum:1201225}} thousand, with R$ {{formatnum:149558}} thousand in taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices. The per capita GDP was R$ {{formatnum:9218.71}}.<ref name="PIB_2011">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417191359/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Produto Interno Bruto dos municípios - 2011|author=IBGE|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref>

In 2010, 62.02% of the population aged 18 or older was [[Workforce|economically active]], with an unemployment rate of 10.86%.<ref name="Atlas"/> The 2011 Central Business Register reported approximately {{formatnum:2607}} local units and {{formatnum:2469}} active companies, including commercial establishments. About {{formatnum:19059}} workers were employed, with {{formatnum:16325}} being salaried. Salaries and other remunerations totaled {{formatnum:227577}} thousand reais, with an average monthly salary of 2.0 minimum wages.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=115&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cestatisticas-do-cadastro-central-de-empresas-2011|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417191601/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=115&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cestatisticas-do-cadastro-central-de-empresas-2011|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Cadastro Central de Empresas - 2012|author=IBGE|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref> In 2010, 72.78% of households earned less than one minimum wage per resident, 17.77% earned one or more, 2.84% earned between three and five, 2.35% earned over five, and 4.24% had no income.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=4&i=P&c=3261|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417193652/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3261&i=P&opc386=2&nome=on&qtu8=137&digt102=&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=3261&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&qtu11=658&nivt=0&orp=4&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&orc386=3&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&qtu2=5&opn10=0&qtu15=3&sev=96&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&qtu102=14208&opn13=0&poc386=2&sep=26272&orn=1&digt10=&opn11=0&qtu7=36&sec386=9680&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Garanhuns&opn102=0&OpcCara=44&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&qtu10=10282&digt11=&opn7=0&decm=99|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Tabela 3261 - Domicílios particulares permanentes, por classes de rendimento nominal mensal domiciliar per capita|author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA)|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref>

=== Primary sector ===
The primary sector is the least significant in Garanhuns' economy, contributing only R$ {{formatnum:24628}} thousand to the total wealth generated.<ref name="PIB_2011"/> About 14.47% of the workforce is employed in this sector. The 2012 livestock census reported {{formatnum:29200}} cattle, 510 goats, 168 donkeys, {{formatnum:1000}} horses, 150 mules, {{formatnum:7600}} sheep, and {{formatnum:2550}} pigs. The municipality also had {{formatnum:980700}} poultry (roosters, hens, chickens, and chicks), {{formatnum:106600}} laying hens producing {{formatnum:1360}} eggs, and {{formatnum:5600}} cows milked, yielding {{formatnum:7056}} liters of milk. Additionally, 200&nbsp;kg of honey was produced.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=121&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cpecuaria-2012|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417191933/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=121&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cpecuaria-2012|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Pecuária - 2012|author=IBGE|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns is notable for its artisanal, semi-artisanal, and industrial production of dairy products. Its dairy basin supplies industries producing cheese, mozzarella, butter, ricotta, chocolate milk, yogurt, and sweets. [[Parmalat]] is the main company served, with its unit supplying the North and Northeast regions with products such as [[UHT milk]], [[pasteurized milk]], [[flavored milk]], [[condensed milk]], [[cream]], and [[powdered milk]], supported by 192 producers from the southern Pernambucan [[Agreste]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.eventosufrpe.com.br/jepex2009/cd/resumos/R1002-2.pdf|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OuOOZRkC?url=http://www.eventosufrpe.com.br/jepex2009/cd/resumos/R1002-2.pdf|archive-date=2014-04-17|title=Perfil dos produtores de leite do município de Garanhuns|author=UFRPE|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref><ref name="JC_Leite">{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/sites/semfronteiras/garanhuns.htm|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417192349/http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/sites/semfronteiras/garanhuns.htm|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Contrastes no pólo leiteiro|author=Jornal do Commercio|date=7 August 2006|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/sites/semfronteiras/iframe_garanhuns2.htm|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417192554/http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/sites/semfronteiras/iframe_garanhuns2.htm|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Contrastes no pólo leiteiro (parte 2)|author=Jornal do Commercio|date=7 August 2006|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref>

=== Secondary sector ===
In 2011, the industrial sector was the second-largest contributor to Garanhuns' wealth, generating approximately R$ {{formatnum:233712}} thousand of the GDP.<ref name="PIB_2011"/> In 2010, 0.11% of the workforce over 18 was employed in the extractive industry, and 7.11% in the manufacturing industry.<ref name="Atlas"/> As of early 2014, the city lacked a designated industrial district, hindering new industrial development. Notable industries include a [[Unilever]] factory,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.unilever.com.br/aboutus/unilever_no_brasil/anos90/anos90_linkrelacionado1.aspx|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417193252/http://www.unilever.com.br/aboutus/unilever_no_brasil/anos90/anos90_linkrelacionado1.aspx|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=Foto da fábrica de Garanhuns, no Estado de Pernambuco – 2006|author=Unilever Brasil|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref> and food-focused industries, particularly dairy, such as Parmalat and Bom Gosto, both part of the LBR group.<ref name="JC_Leite"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lbr-lacteosbrasil.com.br/marcas|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140417193104/http://www.lbr-lacteosbrasil.com.br/marcas|archive-date=17 April 2014|title=LBR|author=IBGE|date=17 April 2014|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns is home to an Expanded Logistics Business Industrial Cell (CIELA) in the COHAB 3 neighborhood, which includes small industries that generate significant employment and contribute to the city's growth.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/vereador-quer-distrito-industrial-para-garanhuns/|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140417193513/http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/vereador-quer-distrito-industrial-para-garanhuns/|archivedate=17 April 2014|title=Vereador quer Distrito Industrial para Garanhuns|author=Blog do Mário Flávio|date=26 December 2013|accessdate=17 April 2014}}</ref>

=== Tertiary sector ===
[[File:Avenida Rui Barbosa, Heliópolis - Garanhuns - Pernambuco - Brasil(4).jpg|thumb|left|Commercial advertisements in the city]]
In 2010, according to data from the United Nations Development Programme's Atlas, 6.59% of employed individuals over 18 years old worked in the construction sector, 1.30% in public utilities, 21.27% in commerce, and 43.64% in the service sector.<ref name="Atlas"/> In absolute numbers, in 2012, approximately {{formatnum:6577}} people were employed in commerce, and a total of {{formatnum:4856}} were engaged in service activities.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/ArquivosPerfilMunicipal/GARANHUNS.pdf|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6OvqS85J9?url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/ArquivosPerfilMunicipal/GARANHUNS.pdf|archivedate=18 April 2014|title=Perfil municipal de Garanhuns|author=BDE|date=19 April 2014|accessdate=19 April 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> According to IBGE's Regional Accounts for 2011, the tertiary sector was the largest contributor to the municipality's wealth, accounting for 75.44% of Garanhuns' economy, with a gross value of R$ {{formatnum:793327}} thousand.<ref name="PIB_2011"/>

Garanhuns' commerce is highly diverse, a key characteristic of its centralizing role in the southern [[Agreste]] region of Pernambuco.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.famegpe.com.br/cidade.aspx?page=2|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140420002437/http://www.famegpe.com.br/cidade.aspx?page=2|archivedate=20 April 2014|title=Garanhuns - Institucional|author=Fameg|date=19 April 2014|accessdate=19 April 2014}}</ref> Its commercial hub is served by a wide variety of national and international retail chains, including stores such as [[Casas Bahia]], [[Cacau Show]], [[Subway (restaurant)|Subway]], [[Lojas Americanas]], [[Magazine Luiza]], Pague Menos, Todo Dia, Don Pastello, Assaí Atacadista, Clube Melissa, Damyller, among others.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.comerciodegaranhuns.com/comercio-a-z/|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140420002712/http://www.comerciodegaranhuns.com/comercio-a-z/|archivedate=20 April 2014|title=Comércio de A-Z|author=Comércio de Garanhuns|date=19 April 2014|accessdate=19 April 2014}}</ref> In 1876, the city saw the founding of the largest construction materials retailer, Ferreira Costa Home Center, established by Portuguese immigrant João Ferreira da Costa.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ferreiracosta.com/quem-somos/historico.aspx|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140420003005/http://www.ferreiracosta.com/quem-somos/historico.aspx|archivedate=20 April 2014|title=Quem somos|author=Ferreira Costa Home Center|date=19 April 2014|accessdate=19 April 2014}}</ref>

[[File:Bairro de Heliópolis - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.JPG|thumb|View of Garanhuns from the 9th Military Police Battalion]]
Garanhuns has a small shopping center, the Rui Barbosa Mall, offering a wide range of stores and dining options. It is located on Rui Barbosa Avenue.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/11/18/shopping-de-garanhuns-e-duplicacao-da-br-432-so-em-2014/|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140420003247/http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/11/18/shopping-de-garanhuns-e-duplicacao-da-br-432-so-em-2014/|archivedate=20 April 2014|title=Shopping de Garanhuns e duplicação da BR-423 só em 2014|author=Rádio Jornal Pernambuco|date=18 November 2013|accessdate=19 April 2014}}</ref>

== Infrastructure ==
=== Healthcare ===
[[File:Eduardo Campos no Hospital Regional Dom Moura.jpg|thumb|left|Dom Moura Regional Hospital]]
In 2009, the municipality had 75 healthcare facilities, including hospitals, emergency rooms, health centers, and dental services, with a total of 32 private and 42 public establishments. Of these, 524 inpatient beds were recorded, with 164 in the public sector and 360 in the private sector.<ref name="Saúde">{{Cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260600&idtema=5&search=pernambuco%7Cgaranhuns%7Cservicos-de-saude-2009|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6LcNDUQa3?url=http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-Municipios-2010.aspx|archivedate=4 December 2013|title=Serviço de Saúde - 2009|author=IBGE|accessdate=27 February 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2012, approximately 98.5% of children under one year old had up-to-date vaccination records. In the same year, the municipality recorded 5,458 live births, with an infant mortality rate of 17.0 deaths per thousand live births.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/4-reduzir-a-mortalidade-infantil/BRA002026045/garanhuns---pe|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140423225546/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/4-reduzir-a-mortalidade-infantil/BRA002026045/garanhuns---pe|archivedate=23 April 2014|title=Reduzir a mortalidade infantil|author=Portal ODM|date=23 April 2014|accessdate=23 April 2014}}</ref> In 2010, 6.34% of women aged 10 to 17 had children, with 0.65% aged 10 to 14 and 5.69% aged 14 to 17, and the activity rate in this age group was 5.96%.<ref name="Atlas"/>

The number of children under two years old weighed by the [[Programa Saúde da Família|Family Health Program]] was 84.9%, with 1.2% of them classified as [[Malnutrition|malnourished]].<ref name="ODM"/> In 2010, Garanhuns' Human Development Index (HDI) for longevity was 0.795, considered medium.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/> According to [[Ministry of Health (Brazil)|Ministry of Health]] data, between 1991 and 2011, 665 [[HIV/AIDS|AIDS]] cases were reported, with 228 cases diagnosed in women and 437 in men. Between 2001 and 2011, 13,007 cases of mosquito-borne diseases were recorded, including 5 cases of [[malaria]], 105 cases of [[leishmaniasis]], and no cases of [[yellow fever]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/6-combater-a-aids-a-malaria-e-outras-doencas/BRA002026045/garanhuns---pe|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140423224001/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/6-combater-a-aids-a-malaria-e-outras-doencas/BRA002026045/garanhuns---pe|archivedate=23 April 2014|title=Combater a AIDS, malária e outras doenças|author=Portal ODM|date=23 April 2014|accessdate=23 April 2014}}</ref>

The city is equipped with the following facilities: a Hemotherapy Care Center; a Psychosocial Care Center; a Family Health Support Center; Primary Healthcare Units; Specialized Clinics (two public and five private); Isolated Clinics (all private); Specialized Hospitals; five General Hospitals (public, philanthropic, and private); Polyclinics (philanthropic); Health Centers and Health Secretariat; Diagnostic and Therapy Support Service Units (public and private); and a Mobile Terrestrial Unit. Among its main hospitals are the Dom Moura Regional Hospital, Santa Terezinha Municipal Hospital, and Palmira Sales Children's Hospital.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.famegpe.com.br/App_Themes/fameg/arquivos/PMS-PLANO_MUNICIPAL_DE_SAUDE-GARANHUNS_2010_2013.pdf|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6P3dihYSO?url=http://www.famegpe.com.br/App_Themes/fameg/arquivos/PMS-PLANO_MUNICIPAL_DE_SAUDE-GARANHUNS_2010_2013.pdf|archivedate=23 April 2014|title=Plano Municipal de Saúde|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|date=23 April 2014|accessdate=23 April 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Education ===

[[File:Avenida Rui Barbosa, Heliópolis - Garanhuns - Pernambuco - Brasil(3).jpg|thumb|220px|São José School, in the Heliópolis neighborhood]]
In the education sector, the score obtained in the Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) by Garanhuns' public schools in 2011 was 3.9 for the early years (5th grade) and 3.5 for the final years (3rd year of high school). The index ranges from 0 to 10, with 4.0 being the national average for early years in Brazilian schools.<ref name="ODM_2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/2-educacao-basica-de-qualidade-para-todos/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140531004317/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/2-educacao-basica-de-qualidade-para-todos/BRA002026067/garanhuns---pe|archivedate=31 May 2014|title=2 - educação básica de qualidade para todos|author=Portal ODM|date=2012|accessdate=31 May 2014}}</ref> The Human Development Index (HDI) for education in 2010 was 0.556.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

In 2010, 3.15% of children aged 6 to 14 were not enrolled in [[elementary school]]. The proportion of youths aged 15 to 17 with completed elementary education was 43.0%, and the literacy rate among youths and adolescents aged 15 to 24 was 94.9%. The age-grade distortion, where students are older than the recommended age for their grade, affected 21.4% of early-year students, 33.9% of final-year students, and reached 38.4% in high school. The percentage of residents over 18 with completed elementary education was 46.62%, and among those aged 18 to 20, it was 27.84%. In 2010, Garanhuns had an expected 9.26 years of schooling.<ref name="Atlas"/>

=== Public safety and crime ===
[[File:Fórum - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|220px|left|Ministro Eraldo Gueiros Leite Courthouse]]
As is common across much of [[Pernambuco]], crime rates in Garanhuns are high.<ref name="Índice"/> In 2012, the rate of Lethal and Intentional Violent Crimes (CVLI) per {{formatnum:100000}} inhabitants was 30.36, ranking Garanhuns 83rd among the most violent municipalities in the state.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1028&Cod=3|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NDy1otXq?url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1028&Cod=3|archivedate=8 February 2014|title=Número de vítimas e taxa de criminalidade violenta letal e intencional|author=Agência Condepe/Fidem|date=2012|accessdate=15 February 2014}}</ref> In 2008, 10 suicides were reported, with a rate of 7.7 suicides per {{formatnum:100000}} inhabitants, placing Garanhuns 618th in the national ranking and 15th in the state ranking.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls|title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de suicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais|author=Sangari|date=2011|accessdate=15 February 2014|format=xls|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/5wqdgmkkY?url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls|archivedate=1 March 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> Regarding workplace accidents reported in 2008, approximately 46 cases were recorded, with a rate of 35.3 per hundred thousand inhabitants, ranking 349th nationally and fourth in the state.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls|title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de Óbitos Ac.Transporte nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais|author=Sangari|date=2011|accessdate=15 February 2014|format=xls|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/5wqdiQILu?url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls|archivedate=1 March 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Pact for Life program was established by the Pernambuco State Government in 2007, when the state had one of the highest rates of Lethal and Intentional Violent Crimes (CVLI) per hundred thousand inhabitants in the country.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.pactopelavida.pe.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/PPV-6-ANOS.pdf|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NDyKDStM?url=http://www.pactopelavida.pe.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/PPV-6-ANOS.pdf|archivedate=8 February 2014|title=Seis anos de Pacto pela Vida|author=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|date=2013|accessdate=16 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2007, the state recorded a CVLI rate of 55.0 per 100,000 inhabitants. Following the creation of the Pact for Life program, which aims to reduce high homicide rates, a set of measures was agreed upon in coordination with society, the Judiciary, the Public Prosecutor's Office, the Legislative Assembly, municipalities, and the federal government to reduce violent deaths.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.pactopelavida.pe.gov.br/pacto-pela-vida/|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NQuBPRvl?url=http://www.pactopelavida.pe.gov.br/pacto-pela-vida/|archivedate=16 February 2014|title=Pacto pela Vida|author=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|date=16 February 2014|accessdate=16 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

[[File:9º Batalhão da Polícia Militar - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|220px|9th Military Police Battalion]]

In April 2013, the CVLI rate had dropped to 35.0, representing a reduction of 20.0 deaths in the index. In Garanhuns, the homicide rate per hundred thousand inhabitants in 2007 was 13.6,<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-12029.pdf|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NQuecY7v?url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-12029.pdf|archivedate=16 February 2014|title=Boletim Trimestral da Conjuntura Criminal em Pernambuco - 1° trimestre de 2008|author=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|date=2013|accessdate=16 February 2014}}</ref> while in the second quarter of 2013, the recorded number was 6.78 homicides.<ref name="Índice">{{Cite web|url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-46001.pdf|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NQul4JIL?url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-46001.pdf|archivedate=16 February 2014|title=Boletim Trimestral da Conjuntura Criminal em Pernambuco - 2° trimestre de 2013|author=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|date=2013|accessdate=16 February 2014}}</ref>

Inaugurated in 2008, the Garanhuns Public Prison has a capacity for 60 male inmates but operates with approximately 250 prisoners.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://oglobo.globo.com/brasil/presidio-de-garanhuns-em-pernambuco-registra-mais-uma-tentativa-de-fuga-3162724|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140607020827/http://oglobo.globo.com/brasil/presidio-de-garanhuns-em-pernambuco-registra-mais-uma-tentativa-de-fuga-3162724|archivedate=7 June 2014|title=Presídio de Garanhuns, em Pernambuco, registra mais uma tentativa de fuga|author=O Globo|date=2009|accessdate=6 June 2014}}</ref>

In early 2012, Garanhuns gained international notoriety due to a heinous crime committed in the municipality. Professor Jorge Beltrão Negromonte da Silveira, then 50 years old, and Bruna Cristina Oliveira da Silva, 25, were arrested on charges of murdering two women and engaging in cannibalism with the victims' bodies. The case gained notoriety when it was revealed that Isabel Cristina Pires da Silveira, a pastry vendor, had filled her pies with human flesh. The perpetrators became known as "[[Garanhuns cannibals]]."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://noticias.uol.com.br/cotidiano/ultimas-noticias/2012/04/13/acusada-de-canibalismo-vendia-empadas-com-carne-humana-em-garanhuns-pe-diz-policia.htm|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140607020139/http://noticias.uol.com.br/cotidiano/ultimas-noticias/2012/04/13/acusada-de-canibalismo-vendia-empadas-com-carne-humana-em-garanhuns-pe-diz-policia.htm|archivedate=7 June 2014|title=Acusada de canibalismo vendia empadas com carne humana em Garanhuns (PE), diz polícia|author=Uol Notícias - Cotidiano|date=13 April 2009|accessdate=6 June 2014}}</ref>

=== Housing, services, and communications ===
[[File:Rádio Jornal - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|220px|left|Garanhuns Radio News]]

According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, in 2010, the municipality had approximately {{formatnum:37368}} permanent private households, comprising {{formatnum:35291}} [[house]]s, {{formatnum:1520}} [[apartment]]s, 425 houses in villages or condominiums, and 62 rooming houses or [[tenement]]s. Of the total private households, {{formatnum:26777}} were owned, with {{formatnum:25924}} fully paid, 83 in the process of acquisition, and {{formatnum:7795}} [[Renting|rented]]. 445 households were provided by an employer, {{formatnum:2255}} were provided in other ways, and 96 were occupied in other ways. Most of the municipality has access to treated water, urban [[waste management|cleaning services]], [[wastewater|sewage treatment]], [[electricity]], [[landline telephone|fixed-line telephony]], and [[mobile phone|mobile telephony]]. In the same year the survey was conducted, {{formatnum:31155}} residences had access to the general treated water distribution network; {{formatnum:37153}} had access to electricity supplied by the distributor; {{formatnum:33292}} received garbage collection services; and {{formatnum:35140}} households had exclusive-use bathrooms.<ref name="Domicílios">{{Cite web|url=http://cod.ibge.gov.br/N2T5|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140610160653/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?codmun=260600&idtema=67|archivedate=10 June 2014|title=Censo Demográfico 2010: Resultados do universo - características da população e dos domicílios|author=IBGE|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref>

The company responsible for potable water distribution and wastewater treatment in Garanhuns and throughout Pernambuco is the Pernambuco Sanitation Company (Compesa). In October 2013, Garanhuns received approximately R${{formatnum:100000000}} in investments from the [[Programa de Aceleração do Crescimento|Growth Acceleration Program]] (PAC 2) for the expansion of the sewage collection and treatment system, benefiting over 50,000 people, including the expansion of pumping stations and treatment plants.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/10/garanhuns-foi-beneficiada-com-r-100.html|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140610161013/http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/10/garanhuns-foi-beneficiada-com-r-100.html|archivedate=10 June 2014|title=Garanhuns foi beneficiada com R$ 100 milhões de reais para investimentos em saneamento básico|author=VEC Garanhuns|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref>

The company responsible for supplying electricity to Garanhuns and all 184 municipalities in Pernambuco, as well as the municipality of [[Pedras de Fogo]] in [[Paraíba]], is the Pernambuco Energy Company (Celpe).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.celpe.com.br/Pages/A%20Celpe/quem-somos.aspx|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140610161151/http://www.celpe.com.br/Pages/A%20Celpe/quem-somos.aspx|archivedate=10 June 2014|title=Quem Somos|author=Celpe|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref> Approximately {{formatnum:99.8|%}} of Garanhuns' households receive electricity supply services.<ref name="Domicílios"/> Internet access services, including both dial-up and [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]), are offered by various paid and free providers. Mobile and fixed-line telephone services are offered by several operators. The [[direct distance dialing|DDD]] code for Garanhuns is 087, and the [[postal code]] ranges from 55290-001 to 55299-999, according to the [[Correios]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.correios.com.br/|title=CEP de cidades brasileiras|author=[[Empresa Brasileira de Correios e Telégrafos|Correios]]|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref>

Several channels are available in the [[Very High Frequency|VHF]] and [[Ultra High Frequency|UHF]] bands, with most broadcasters based in [[Caruaru]] (the regional hub). The municipality receives signals from [[TV Jornal Interior]] (affiliated with [[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]) and [[TV Asa Branca]], affiliated with [[TV Globo]] in the [[Agreste]] of Pernambuco, which also provides a digital signal.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.portalbsd.com.br/novo/terrestres_channels.php?channels=965|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140610162045/http://www.portalbsd.com.br/novo/terrestres_channels.php?channels=965|archivedate=10 June 2014|title=Listando canais de Garanhuns - PE|author=Portal BSD|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref> Garanhuns has several daily newspapers in circulation, some of which are among the most traditional in the state, such as [[Diário de Pernambuco]] and Jornal do Commercio, in addition to ''Correio Sete Colinas'' and ''Jornal Cidade''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.netpapers.com/jornais-por-cidade/garanhuns|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140610162131/http://www.netpapers.com/jornais-por-cidade/garanhuns|archivedate=10 June 2014|title=Jornais de Garanhuns|author=Netpapers|accessdate=10 June 2014}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===
;Air transport
The [[Zumbi dos Palmares International Airport]] in [[Maceió]], the capital of the neighboring state, is the closest airport to Garanhuns. However, the most commonly used airport for arrivals and departures is the [[Recife/Guararapes–Gilberto Freyre International Airport]], also known as "Guararapes Airport" or "Gilberto Freyre Airport," located in the Imbiribeira neighborhood in the southern zone of [[Recife]], 230 kilometers from central Garanhuns, with [[BR-232 (Brazil highway)|BR-232]] as the main access route. The Recife airport, serving most municipalities in the eastern part of the state, including the Forest Zone and [[Agreste]] regions, boasts the best infrastructure, the longest runway, the largest physical space, and the most advanced technology in the North/Northeast region.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.aeroportorecife.com/|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NUOsPEuv?url=http://www.aeroportorecife.com/|archivedate=18 February 2014|title=Aeroporto de Recife|author=Aeroporto Recife|date=2014|accessdate=17 February 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> Recife's airport is considered the most efficient in Brazil and the second most efficient in [[South America]], behind only the [[José Joaquín de Olmedo International Airport]] in [[Guayaquil]], Ecuador's largest city.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.worldairportawards.com/awards_2012/rgnal_samerica.htm|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NUOIa5jt?url=http://www.worldairportawards.com/awards_2012/rgnal_samerica.htm|archivedate=18 February 2014|title=Airline travellers rate Guayaquil Airport as the Best Regional Airport in South America|author=World Airport Awards|date=2014|accessdate=18 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Garanhuns also has a regional airport that serves only chartered and small to medium-sized aircraft. The 1,254-meter-long asphalt runway has 16/34 runway headings and an elevation of 863 meters above [[sea level]]. In 2012, President [[Dilma Rousseff]] announced a package of measures for the aviation sector, with the Northeast region receiving the majority of these funds. The [[Garanhuns Airport]] was included, along with most state airports, for investments and the construction of new terminals, though by early 2014, no plans had been finalized.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.liberdade.com.br/noticias.php?id=18790|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6NURj7NX5?url=http://www.liberdade.com.br/noticias.php?id=18790|archivedate=18 February 2014|title=Aeroporto de Caruaru receberá investimentos do Governo Federal|author=Liberdade Sistema de Comunicação|date=2012|accessdate=18 February 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

;Rail and metro transport
[[File:Centro Cultural - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|220px|Alfredo Leite Cavalcanti Cultural Center, located between Dom Moura, Tiradentes, and Guadalajara Squares]]
The Garanhuns railway station was part of the southern trunk line, connecting the city to the state capital. In 1894, after the construction of the Southern Pernambuco State Highway, which linked the Paquevira station to the state of [[Alagoas]], the city's station became a branch line. The line from Recife passed through the municipalities of [[São João, Pernambuco|São João]], [[Angelim]], and [[Canhotinho]], the latter receiving a station in 1885, two and a half years before the railway was extended to Garanhuns. The railway and station were inaugurated on September 28, 1887, with a grand celebration in the city, likened to the ancient Olympic Games.<ref name="Ferrovia2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.nacaocultural.org.br/salvar.php?c=7531&f=1|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6QHCHTfQ2?url=https://www.google.com.br/url?sa=t&rct=j&q=&esrc=s&source=web&cd=8&cad=rja&uact=8&ved=0CEMQFjAH&url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.nacaocultural.org.br%2Fsalvar.php%3Fc%3D7531%26f%3D1&ei=PqmZU-q6GvLhsATB_oHwBQ&usg=AFQjCNHNt40r6pTGuxsgaLpUQOlOKQKnpQ&sig2=1evuI78sUsZL3lw|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=Estação Ferroviária de Garanhuns: Arquitetura inglesa no Agreste pernambucano|author=Fundarpe|date=April 2009|accessdate=17 August 2014}}</ref>

The construction of the railway spurred rapid growth and development in commerce, export companies, and offices. It also increased the number of markets and diversified the products offered. The city's hotel sector received significant investments during this period, as improved access brought more tourists to Garanhuns.<ref name="Ferrovia"/> The station and railway were decommissioned in 1971, when then-mayor Luiz Souto Dourado announced their transformation into a cultural center. The Alfredo Leite Cavalcanti Cultural Center was inaugurated on March 27, 1971. In 1979, the construction of the Luiz Souto Dourado Theater was announced, which includes several exhibition halls.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/pernambuco/garanhuns.htm|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612132754/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/pernambuco/garanhuns.htm|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=Estação Ferroviária de Garanhuns|author=Estações Ferroviárias|date=2013|accessdate=11 June 2014}}</ref>

;Road transport
The Garanhuns passenger bus terminal is located in the Heliópolis neighborhood and is adapted to accommodate people with disabilities and/or reduced mobility. Its basic structure includes male and female restrooms, located near the luggage storage and ticket sales area. It also has a 24-hour taxi stand and four public telephones near the restrooms. The main bus companies operating there are Cruzeiro, Guanabara, Itapemirim, Gontijo, and Progresso.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.socicam.com.br/terminais/terminais_rodoviarios.php?ID=322|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612203333/http://www.socicam.com.br/terminais/terminais_rodoviarios.php?ID=322|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=Pernambuco - Terminal Rodoviário de Garanhuns|author=Socicam|date=2014|accessdate=12 June 2014}}</ref>

[[File:Avenida Rui Barbosa, Heliópolis - Garanhuns - Pernambuco - Brasil(2).jpg|thumb|In Garanhuns, twenty streets and avenues had LED lighting by early 2014.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://ne10.uol.com.br/canal/interior/agreste/noticia/2014/03/20/vinte-localidades-de-garanhuns-contempladas-com-iluminacao-de-led-477263.php|title=Vinte localidades de Garanhuns contempladas com iluminação de LED|publisher=NE10|accessdate=25 October 2016}}</ref>]]

Garanhuns is well-positioned in terms of road infrastructure, connecting the municipality to neighboring cities and the state capital, as well as other cities and capitals in the Northeast. Its main connections are via [[BR-424 (Brazil highway)|BR-424]], PE-177, and [[BR-423 (Brazil highway)|BR-423]]. The first begins in [[Arcoverde]] (in the backlands of Pernambuco) and ends in the Metropolitan Region of Maceió in Alagoas (with a section still planned by DNIT).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www2.transportes.gov.br/bit/02-rodo/3-loc-rodo/loc-rodo/br-424/gbr-424.htm|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612203726/http://www2.transportes.gov.br/bit/02-rodo/3-loc-rodo/loc-rodo/br-424/gbr-424.htm|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=BR-424|author=Ministério dos Transportes|date=2014|accessdate=12 June 2014}}</ref> The second, PE-177, starts in Garanhuns and ends in [[Quipapá]] (in the forest region of Pernambuco).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.der.pe.gov.br/web/der/rodovias-do-estado|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612203855/http://www.der.pe.gov.br/web/der/rodovias-do-estado|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=Rodovias do Estado|author=Departamento de estradas de rodagem de Pernambuco|date=2014|accessdate=12 June 2014}}</ref> The most significant access, BR-423, begins in [[Caruaru]] (BR-232), [[São Caetano, Pernambuco|São Caetano]], and crosses the entire Garanhuns Microregion, ending in [[Paulo Afonso]], Bahia, with plans to extend the highway to [[Juazeiro]] in Bahia's backlands.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www2.transportes.gov.br/bit/02-rodo/3-loc-rodo/loc-rodo/br-423/gbr-423.htm|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612204054/http://www2.transportes.gov.br/bit/02-rodo/3-loc-rodo/loc-rodo/br-423/gbr-423.htm|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=BR-423|author=Ministério dos Transportes|date=2014|accessdate=12 June 2014}}</ref> In late 2013, it was announced that a call for bids for the widening of 80 kilometers between the municipalities of São Caetano and Garanhuns would be published in July 2014. The project was scheduled for completion in June 2016.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/11/duplicacao-da-br-423-de-sao-caetano.html|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140612204316/http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/11/duplicacao-da-br-423-de-sao-caetano.html|archivedate=12 June 2014|title=Duplicação da BR-423 de São Caetano a Garanhuns está prevista para julho de 2014|author=Vec Garanhuns|date=16 November 2013|accessdate=12 June 2014}}</ref>

;Urban transport
The Municipal Traffic and Transport Authority (AMTT) is the municipal agency responsible for planning, regulating, controlling, and overseeing traffic, as well as organizing, executing, supervising, evaluating, and managing public transportation services, citizen and municipal property safety, social defense actions, and permanent civil defense measures against natural, anthropogenic, and mixed disasters.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/2013-04-06-13-17-17|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140626224629/http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/2013-04-06-13-17-17|archivedate=26 June 2014|title=Autarquia Municipal de Trânsito e Transporte|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|date=6 April 2013|accessdate=26 June 2014}}</ref> The city has several companies responsible for urban transportation, connecting the outskirts to the city center. Two of the main companies providing this service are Viação Padre Cícero and Coletivo São Cristóvão. According to [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] data, in 2012, the municipality's vehicle fleet consisted of {{formatnum:38034}} vehicles, including {{formatnum:18494}} cars, {{formatnum:1698}} trucks, 340 tractor trucks, {{formatnum:2588}} pickups, 646 vans, 244 minibuses, {{formatnum:11067}} motorcycles, {{formatnum:1758}} scooters, 104 buses, no wheeled tractors, and 957 other types of vehicles.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://cod.ibge.gov.br/9HUY|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140626225011/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?codmun=260600&idtema=110|archivedate=26 June 2014|title=Frota - 2012|author=IBGE|date=26 June 2014|accessdate=26 June 2014}}</ref>

== Culture ==
The Garanhuns Municipal Culture Secretariat oversees the city's cultural sector and is responsible for planning and implementing cultural policies in all areas. The Secretariat's primary goal is to support, preserve, and promote the elements that comprise the city's cultural identity, while also encouraging tourism through the events it organizes.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/cultura|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140703164230/http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/cultura|archivedate=3 July 2014|title=Cultura|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|date=3 July 2014|accessdate=3 July 2014}}</ref> The Municipal Tourism Secretariat establishes guidelines for tourism-driven economic development. It plans, organizes, and executes tourism-related activities in collaboration with other secretariats and public and private institutions.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/2013-04-06-02-40-08|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140703164327/http://www.garanhuns.pe.gov.br/pmg/index.php/autarquias-e-orgaos/2013-04-06-02-40-08|archivedate=3 July 2014|title=Turismo|author=Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|date=3 July 2014|accessdate=3 July 2014}}</ref>

Among Garanhuns' most illustrious natives is singer, composer, and instrumentalist José Domingos de Morais, popularly known as [[Dominguinhos]], who began his career at the age of six, playing the [[pandeiro]] alongside his brothers Moraes (on accordion) and Valdomiro (on [[zabumba]]) in the trio "Três Pinguins." The trio performed at markets and hotel entrances until, in 1948, singer [[Luiz Gonzaga]] saw one of their performances and promised Dominguinhos an accordion if he ever moved to [[Rio de Janeiro (state)|Rio de Janeiro]]. In 1954, his family relocated to Rio, where Dominguinhos sought out Gonzaga to fulfill his promise. With the gifted accordion, he formed the ''Trio Nordestino'', performing at circuses and dance events in Rio's interior cities. His success led to collaborations with Gonzaga in shows and recordings, marking the peak of his artistic career. His songs have been performed by renowned Brazilian artists such as [[Maria Bethânia]], [[Gal Costa]], [[Caetano Veloso]], [[Elba Ramalho]], [[Raimundo Fagner]], and others. He also collaborated with artists such as Nando Cordel, [[Gilberto Gil]], and [[Chico Buarque]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar./index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=234:dominguinhos&catid=39:letra-d&Itemid=1|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140703164945/http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar./index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=234:dominguinhos&catid=39:letra-d&Itemid=1|archivedate=3 July 2014|title=Dominguinhos|author=Fundação Joaquim Nabuco|date=3 July 2014|accessdate=3 July 2014}}</ref>

=== Performing arts and events ===
[[File:Praça Dom Moura - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|230px|Dom Moura Square]]
The city has few spaces dedicated to the performing arts, with the Luiz Souto Dourado Theater, located in the Alfredo Leite Cultural Center, being the largest and oldest venue.<ref name="Ferrovia"/> However, other spaces serve as stages for artistic events organized by public and philanthropic entities, such as the Colunata Space, Luiz Jardim Space, SESC Garanhuns Activity Center, and various squares and parks throughout the city.<ref name="FSAC">{{Cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/10/garanhuns-no-agreste-sedia-iv-festival-sesc-de-artes-cenicas.html|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140715235751/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/10/garanhuns-no-agreste-sedia-iv-festival-sesc-de-artes-cenicas.html|archivedate=15 July 2014|title=Garanhuns, no Agreste, sedia IV Festival Sesc de Artes Cênicas|author=G1|date=12 October 2013|accessdate=15 July 2014}}</ref>

Annually, the [[Serviço Social do Comércio|Social Service of Commerce]] (SESC) holds the SESC Performing Arts Festival in Garanhuns, featuring a variety of dance, theater, video-dance, performances, and educational activities. The fourth edition in 2013 was marked by the decentralization of cultural hubs, extending to other municipalities in the southern [[Agreste]] of Pernambuco, such as [[Saloá]], [[Jupi]], and [[Canhotinho]]. Performances also take place during the Garanhuns Winter Festival.<ref name="FSAC"/>

Since 2004, the Garanhuns Jazz Festival has been held annually, created as an alternative for tourists seeking to spend the carnival period in the Agreste of Pernambuco. The festival features performances by Pernambuco and Brazilian musicians, as well as international bands and artists. In its 7th edition, the event attracted approximately 30,000 people to the city, generating around R$2.5 million for the local economy. It is estimated to create over 800 direct jobs and 500 indirect jobs, boosting the city's hotel network, which consists of 22 establishments and reaches up to 100% occupancy during this period.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhunsjazz.com.br/edicao2014/o_festival.php|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6R64AkExe?url=http://www.garanhunsjazz.com.br/edicao2014/o_festival.php|archivedate=16 July 2014|title=O Festival|author=Festival de Jazz de Garanhuns|date=15 July 2014|accessdate=15 July 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Attractions ===
;Garanhuns Winter Festival
[[File:Vista parcial de Garanhuns, PE.jpg|thumb|230px|Partial view of Garanhuns]]

The Garanhuns Winter Festival is the main event held in the municipality and the largest arts and culture festival in [[Pernambuco]], attracting over 500,000 visitors annually and ranking as the second most important winter attraction in Pernambuco, after [[Caruaru]]'s [[Festa Junina|June festivals]]. Held from July 17 to 26 since 1990, the festival brings together music, cinema, performing arts, popular culture, and other forms of expression, boosting Garanhuns' hotel sector. It offers tourists everything from major concerts to processions of Pernambuco and Brazilian popular culture, from theatrical performances to poetry recitals at open markets, encompassing a wide range of cultural diversity. In addition to promoting culture, the festival implements various diffusion and cultural training initiatives. These initiatives solidify the festival's role as the culmination of public policies developed by the Pernambuco government. The festival is organized by the Culture Secretariat, Fundarpe, and other supporting institutions.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/pagina/festival-de-inverno-de-garanhuns/o-festival-2/|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140722012013/http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/pagina/festival-de-inverno-de-garanhuns/o-festival-2/|archivedate=22 July 2014|title=O Festival|author=Portal Cultura PE|date=21 July 2014|accessdate=21 July 2014}}</ref>

The city's seventeen cultural hubs attract thousands of tourists, who come to see renowned artists and emerging talents. One of the city's main squares, Mestre Dominguinhos Square (Guadalajara Esplanade), serves as a stage for widely followed artists across Brazil, featuring performers such as [[Vanessa da Mata]], [[Luiz Caldas]], [[Zé Ramalho]], [[Fábio Jr.|Fábio Júnior]], [[Alceu Valença]], [[Angela Maria]], [[Otto (singer)|Otto]], [[Marcelo Jeneci]], and bands such as [[Titãs]] and [[Nação Zumbi]]. The daytime program offers visitors circus performances, literary showcases, cinema and photography exhibits, popular art presentations, craft exhibitions, visual arts, design, and fashion, as well as workshops and cultural debates.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/pagina/festival-de-inverno-de-garanhuns/polos-do-fig-2014/|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140722012141/http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/pagina/festival-de-inverno-de-garanhuns/polos-do-fig-2014/|archivedate=22 July 2014|title=O Festival|author=Portal Cultura PE|date=21 July 2014|accessdate=21 July 2014}}</ref>

;Garanhuns Jazz Festival
Created in 2007, the Garanhuns Jazz Festival (FJG) emerged as an alternative during the carnival period. Held concurrently with traditional [[Carnival block|street block]] celebrations in cities such as [[Recife]] and [[Olinda]], the event offers visitors various shows on decentralized stages, musical workshops, and classes, providing comfort and flexible schedules. During the festival, approximately 30,000 visitors come to the city, injecting around R$2.5 million into the local economy. It is estimated to generate about 800 direct and 500 indirect jobs.<ref name="FJG">{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhunsjazz.com.br/edicao2014/o_festival.php|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140731173553/http://www.garanhunsjazz.com.br/edicao2014/o_festival.php|archivedate=31 July 2014|title=O Festival|author=Festival de Jazz de Garanhuns|date=31 July 2014|accessdate=31 July 2014}}</ref>

Garanhuns' hotel network, consisting of 22 establishments including hotels and guesthouses, reaches up to 100% occupancy during the event. In addition to the hotel network, the Association of Parents and Friends of the Exceptional (APAE) offers low-cost hostel-style accommodations. The festival's infrastructure is designed to ensure spectator comfort and safety. The show areas feature covered seating, special lighting, and free tables and chairs. There are also mini food courts comprising the city's busiest bars and restaurants.<ref name="FJG"/>

The FJG is considered one of the largest [[jazz]] and [[blues]] festivals in Brazil, earning praise from bands and critics worldwide. In 2008 and 2009, the festival was a pioneer, winning the II Mestre Salustiano Award, granted by the Pernambuco government to notable initiatives that promote tourism in the state's municipalities.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.jornalvanguarda.com.br/v2/?pagina=noticias&id=5673|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140731173749/http://www.jornalvanguarda.com.br/v2/?pagina=noticias&id=5673|archivedate=31 July 2014|title=Garanhuns Jazz Festival conquista Prêmio Mestre Salustiano|author=Festival de Jazz de Garanhuns|date=13 March 2010|accessdate=31 July 2014}}</ref>

;Viva Dominguinhos Festival
Created in 2014, the Viva Dominguinhos Festival is typically held at the end of April each year, sometimes extending into early May, always from Thursday to Saturday. The event honors singer and composer [[Dominguinhos]], who was born and buried in Garanhuns, and features performances by national and local artists.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Prefeitura de Garanhuns divulga datas do Viva Dominguinhos 2019|url=https://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2019/01/04/prefeitura-de-garanhuns-divulga-datas-do-viva-dominguinhos-2019.ghtml|work=G1|date=4 January 2019 |accessdate=24 March 2019|language=pt-br}}</ref> It is organized in partnership between the Tourism and Culture Secretariat and the Garanhuns Municipal Government. The event includes major [[Música popular brasileira|Brazilian Popular Music]] artists performing at the Mestre Dominguinhos Square Stage, as well as shows and accordion circles at the Colunata Space, along with workshops.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Governo Municipal divulga atrações do II Festival Viva Dominguinhos « Prefeitura Municipal de Garanhuns|url=https://garanhuns.pe.gov.br/governo-municipal-divulga-atracoes-do-ii-festival-viva-dominguinhos/|work=garanhuns.pe.gov.br|date=31 March 2015 |accessdate=24 March 2019}}</ref> The estimated attendance for Friday and Saturday nights is 60,000 people.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Público de 60 mil pessoas em Garanhuns no Viva Dominguinhos|url=http://jconline.ne10.uol.com.br/canal/cultura/musica/noticia/2018/04/21/publico-de-60-mil-pessoas-em-garanhuns-no-viva-dominguinhos-336342.php|work=jconline.ne10.uol.com.br|date=21 April 2018 |accessdate=24 March 2019|language=pt-br}}</ref> It is another major attraction in the city.

;Architecture and monuments
[[File:Cristo do Magano - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil.jpg|thumb|230px|left|Christ of Magano]]
[[File:BRUNO LIMA CASTELO DE JOÃO CAPÃO GARANHUNS PE.jpg|thumb|230px|João Capão Castle]]
[[File:Seminário São José - Garanhuns, Pernambuco, Brasil(2).jpg|thumb|230px|São José Seminary]]
Garanhuns is an important destination for religious tourists, featuring Christian sculptures and churches with rich architectural details. A notable example is the replica of the [[Stations of the Cross]], built at Alto do Columinho, consisting of 15 illustrated stations along a path leading to a sanctuary formed by two perpendicular walls with an image of the crucified Jesus Christ at the center. From this elevated site, visitors can enjoy a panoramic view of the city. Among the seven hills surrounding Garanhuns is the Christ of Magano, a sculpture depicting the [[Crucifixion of Jesus|crucifixion of Christ]], built by Renato Pantaleão in 1954. Situated at the highest point of the municipality, at an elevation of 1,030 metres above sea level, it is considered to be the highest Christian image in Brazil in relation to sea level.<ref name="Monumentos">{{Cite web|url=http://www.garanhunspalace.com.br/cidade.php|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140803231623/http://www.garanhunspalace.com.br/cidade.php|archivedate=3 August 2014|title=A cidade|author=Garanhuns Palace Hotel|date=2 August 2014|accessdate=2 August 2014}}</ref>

Construction of the Nossa Senhora do Perpétuo Socorro Church began in 1957 and was completed on December 8, 1962, with its architectural design crafted by engineers Albert Reithler and Pierre Reithler, supported by thousands of anonymous Garanhuns residents who donated construction materials and labor. The church's design was further refined after its completion. Located in the city center, the São José Seminary currently serves as a residence for seminarians of the Diocesan Congregation. Built in 1926 with rich architectural features, the seminary strictly adheres to its scheduled masses. Also in the city center is the São Bento Monastery, constructed in the style of ancient [[Benedictines|Benedictine]] monasteries, featuring notable elements such as stained-glass windows and a panel depicting the Apocalypse. The monastery also houses a factory producing communion wafers and artisanal products.<ref name="Monumentos"/>

Begun in 1914, the [[Schoenstatt Apostolic Movement]] was founded by young Christians during World War I. Men, women, youths, couples, and priests joined the movement, united by the goal of contributing to the religious and moral renewal of the world. In 2002, the Mãe Rainha Sanctuary was built in Garanhuns and completed in 2004. It is a popular destination for religious visitors worldwide and is managed by the Schoenstatt Sisters of Mary. Another notable structure is the João Capão Castle, built to fulfill its owner's childhood dream.<ref name="Monumentos"/>

== Sports ==
Garanhuns holds the second-highest sporting significance in the Pernambuco Agreste, behind only Caruaru. Despite having few football clubs, notable ones include the Associação Garanhuense de Atletismo (AGA) and the [[Sete de Setembro Esporte Clube]], which competed in and won a title in the [[Campeonato Pernambucano Série A2]] in 1995.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://esporte.hsw.uol.com.br/sete-de-setembro-pe.htm|title=Campeonato Pernambucano - Sete de Setembro|date=9 August 2014|accessdate=9 August 2014|website=How Stuff Works|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140809124457/http://esporte.hsw.uol.com.br/sete-de-setembro-pe.htm|archivedate=9 August 2014}}</ref>

The Marco Antônio Maciel Stadium, also known as "The Giant of the Agreste," is the municipality's main and largest sports venue, named in honor of Senator [[Marco Maciel]], who served as a state and federal deputy, governor of [[Pernambuco]], and vice-president of Brazil during both terms of [[Fernando Henrique Cardoso]]. The stadium gained national recognition in 2003 when it hosted a [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série B|Série B]] match between [[Sport Club do Recife|Sport]] and [[Sociedade Esportiva Palmeiras|Palmeiras]]. Located in the Indiano neighborhood, the stadium can accommodate {{formatnum:7000}} people, with {{formatnum:1500}} covered reserved seats.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://esportes.terra.com.br/futebol/noticias/0,,OI217948-EI1946,00-Palmeiras+vence+Sport+de+virada+e+volta+a+elite.html|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140809132001/http://esportes.terra.com.br/futebol/noticias/0,,OI217948-EI1946,00-Palmeiras+vence+Sport+de+virada+e+volta+a+elite.html|archivedate=9 August 2014|title=Palmeiras vence Sport de virada e volta à elite|author=Terra|date=22 November 2003|accessdate=9 August 2014}}</ref>

Every year, [[TV Asa Branca]], a [[Rede Globo]] affiliate in the region, and its partners organize the TV Asa Branca Futsal Cup. The [[Serviço Social do Comércio|SESC]] gymnasium, near the Sulanca Fair grounds in Caruaru, hosts matches involving futsal teams from key cities in the Forest Zone, Agreste, and the backlands, such as [[Palmares, Pernambuco|Palmares]], [[Catende]], [[Bezerros]], [[Santa Cruz do Capibaribe]], [[Belo Jardim]], [[Caruaru]], [[Lajedo, Pernambuco|Lajedo]], [[Arcoverde]], and [[Serra Talhada]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://globoesporte.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/11/arcoverde-sagrou-se-bicampeao-da-8-copa-tv-asa-branca-de-futsal-2013.html|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6Nc4mniYE?url=http://globoesporte.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/11/arcoverde-sagrou-se-bicampeao-da-8-copa-tv-asa-branca-de-futsal-2013.html|archivedate=24 February 2014|title=Arcoverde sagrou-se bicampeão da 8ª Copa TV Asa Branca de Futsal 2013|author=globoesporte.com|date=30 November 2013|accessdate=23 February 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Holidays ==
In Garanhuns, there are four municipal [[holidays]], as per Law Project No. 41/2013 by Mayor Izaías Régis, which abolished the municipal emancipation day, previously celebrated on March 10, and established "Garanhuns Day," observed on the second Sunday of March.<ref name="Feriado">{{Cite web|url=http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/11/camara-aprova-projeto-que-extingue-o.html|title=Câmara aprova projeto que extingue o feriado do aniversário de Garanhuns|date=13 November 2013|accessdate=16 August 2014|website=VEC Garanhuns|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140816100310/http://www.vecgaranhuns.com/2013/11/camara-aprova-projeto-que-extingue-o.html|archivedate=16 August 2014}}</ref> Additionally, there are eight national holidays, one state holiday, and several optional holidays. The municipal holidays are [[Good Friday]] in April; the day of the patron saint [[Anthony of Padua|Saint Anthony]], on June 13; the day of [[John the Apostle|Saint John]], on June 24; and [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]].<ref name="Feriado"/> "Garanhuns Day" is celebrated on the second Sunday of March, replacing the emancipation date.<ref name="Feriado"/> On March 2, the State Holiday of Pernambuco is observed optionally, commemorating the separatist movement of the [[Pernambucan revolt]] of 1817.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.pe.gov.br/conheca/datas/|title=Pontos Facultativos, Feriados, Festas|date=13 November 2013|accessdate=16 August 2014|publisher=Governo de Pernambuco|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140816100730/http://www.pe.gov.br/conheca/datas/|archivedate=16 August 2014}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Barbosa|first=Virginia|url=http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=240:data-magna-do-estado-de-pernambuco&catid=39:letra-d&Itemid=183|title=Data Magna do Estado de Pernambuco|date=18 March 2010|accessdate=16 August 2014|publisher=Fundação Joaquim Nabuco|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140816100852/http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=240:data-magna-do-estado-de-pernambuco&catid=39:letra-d&Itemid=183|archivedate=16 August 2014}}</ref>
