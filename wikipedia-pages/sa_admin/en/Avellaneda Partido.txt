'''Avellaneda''' is a [[Partidos of Buenos Aires|partido]] in [[Buenos Aires Province]], [[Argentina]]. It has an area of <span style="white-space:nowrap">55.17&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>&nbsp;(21.3&nbsp;sq&nbsp;mi)</span> and a population of 663,953 in 2001. Its [[administrative seat]] is the city of [[Avellaneda]].

The partido is located in the [[Greater Buenos Aires]] urban area, separated from the city of [[Buenos Aires]] by the [[Matanza River]], popularly known as ''Riachuelo''. The Bartolomé Mitre is the main avenue of the district, connecting with the main federal city through two bridges, the [[Pueyrredón Bridge]] to [[Barracas, Buenos Aires|Barracas]] and the [[New Pueyrredón Bridge]], directly to the [[9 de Julio Avenue]]. The [[Nicolás Avellaneda Bridge]] also connects the ''Isla Maciel'' (in [[Dock Sud]]) with [[La Boca]] neighbourhood.

==Name==
The Partido was known as ''Barracas al Sud'' (Southern Barracks), until it was renamed in honor of [[Nicolás Avellaneda]] in 1904.

Between Dock Sud, Sarandí, Villa Domínico and Wilde there is an area called [[Área Reserva Cinturón Ecológico|Reserva Cinturón Ecológico]], ("Environmental Belt Rerserve Area") which is a part of a network of waste dumps, which after reaching capacity are planted with greenery.

==Sport==
Avellaneda is home to two of the most famous football clubs in Argentina: [[Club Atlético Independiente|Independiente]] and [[Racing Club de Avellaneda|Racing]].

The partido is also home to several other football teams including [[Arsenal de Sarandí]] of the [[Primera Division Argentina]], and lower league teams [[Club Atlético San Telmo|San Telmo]], [[Sportivo Dock Sud|Dock Sud]] and [[Victoriano Arenas]].
