'''Izamal Municipality''' (In the [[Yucatec Maya language|Yucatec Maya]] Language: “dew of heaven”)<ref name="Dew of Heaven">{{cite web|title=Izamal Tourism|url=http://yucatanall.com/turismo/yucatan-english-version/izamal|website=Yucatan All|publisher=Yucatan All|access-date=9 June 2015|location=Yucatan, Mexico|language=es|date=2010}}</ref> is a [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Yucatán]] containing 275.92&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> of land and located roughly 67&nbsp;km east of the city of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]].<ref name="municipios"/>

==History==
Izamal is believed to have been founded in the late Pre-classic period (750 to 200 A.C.), by Zamná, priest of the god Itzamná. Most of the construction on the site dates to the Proto-classic period (200 B.C. to 200 a.c.), the Early Classic (200 to 600 A.C.) And Late Classic (600 to 800 A.C.) periods. When [[Chichen Itzá]] rose, during the Final Classic period (800 to 1000 A.C.), Izamal was partially abandoned.<ref name=UNESCO>{{cite web|title=Historical city of Izamal (Izamal, Mayan continuity in an Historical City)|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5394/|website=UNESCO|publisher=UNESCO|access-date=9 June 2015|date=15 October 2008}}</ref>

After the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán|conquest]] the area became part of the [[encomienda]] system, which was implemented between 1543 and 1549.<ref name="municipios"/>  Construction of the convent of San Antonio de Padua began in 1553 by fray [[Diego de Landa]],<ref name=UNESCO /> who later instituted an inquisition called the ''auto de fé'' against the Maya at [[Maní, Yucatán|Maní]] and burned the [[Maya codices]].<ref name="Timmer">{{Citation |last=Timmer |first=David E. |title=Providence and Perdition: Fray Diego de Landa Justifies His Inquisition against the Yucatecan Maya |journal=Church History |year=1997 |pages=477–483 |doi=10.2307/3169452 |jstor=3169452 }}.</ref> Landa and the encomendero, Francisco Hernández, engaged in a three-year legal battle beginning in 1556 to determine if the church, or the Spanish authorities had jurisdiction of the area. Hernández finally broke under the strain, agreed to submit to Landa's authority, and died shortly after his release from prison in 1561,<ref name="Landa/Hernandez">{{cite book|last1=Karttunen|first1=Frances|title=Between worlds : interpreters, guides, and survivors|date=1994|publisher=Rutgers Univ. Press|location=New Brunswick, NJ|isbn=0-8135-2030-4|pages=[https://archive.org/details/betweenworldsint00kartrich/page/96 96]–97|url=https://archive.org/details/betweenworldsint00kartrich|url-access=registration|access-date=9 June 2015}}</ref> the same year work on the convent was completed.<ref name=UNESCO /> Juan Cueva Santillán was the encomendero at Izamal in 1577 and filed a report on his duties there in 1581.<ref name=encomendero>{{cite book|editor-last1=Perry|editor-first1=Richard D. |title=Exploring Yucatan: A Traveler's Anthology|date=2001|publisher=Espadaña Press|location=Santa Barbara, Calif.|isbn=0-9620811-4-0|page=135|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=jKvRAJ8TBGIC&pg=PA135|access-date=9 June 2015}}</ref>

Yucatán declared its independence from the Spanish Crown in 1821 and on 25 October 1823, Izamal acquired the status of village and was made headquarters of the Coastal Region. In 1841, it was elevated to the status of city. During the land redistribution policies of the early 20th century, Izamal was declared a pueblo on 13 August 1923, but regained its status as a city in 1981.<ref name="municipios"/>

==Governance==
The municipal president is elected for a three-year term. The town council has nine councilpersons, who serve as Secretary of Culture; Public Services; communities; special events; sports; artisans; decorations; ecology and disability services.<ref name=governance>{{cite web|title=Izamal|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|website=inafed|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=4 June 2015|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es|archive-date=4 July 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220704100544/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Municipal Council administers the business of the municipality. It is responsible for budgeting and expenditures and producing all required reports for all branches of the municipal administration. Annually it determines educational standards for schools.<ref name=governance />

The Police Commissioners ensure public order and safety. They are tasked with enforcing regulations, distributing materials and administering rulings of general compliance issued by the council.<ref name=governance />

==Communities==
The head of the municipality is [[Izamal]], Yucatán. There are thirty communities in the municipality. The most important are Citilcum, Kimbilá, Sitilpech and Xanabá. Other minor population centers are Balantún, Becal, Catzín, Chan-Kin, Chichihuá, Chichi-Uh, Chixé, Choyoh, Chumul, Cuauhtémoc, Ebulá, Kanan, Kantoyehen, Kauán, Mulsay, Sacalá, Sacnicté, Hacienda Sac-Nicté, Sahaltún, San José Tzucacab, San Luis, San Pedro, Santa María, Tebic, finca Tecoh, Tziló, Tulinche, X'Tohil and Yokdzonot. ==Local festivals==
Every year on 3 April is a fiesta for San Ildefonso; from 15 to 25 August, the celebration of the town's patron saint, Our Lady of Izamal is held; from 19 to 28 October processions, gatherings and local folk dances are held in honor of the Santo Cristo de Citilpech; and from 1 to 12 December the Immaculate Conception is celebrated.<ref name=municipios />

[[Kots Kaal Pato]] ([[English language|English]]: Strangle the Duck) is a controversial event practiced among some [[Mayan people|Mayan]] youth in the village of Citilcum. In the event, ducks, bound to high wooden crossbeams by their feet, are mutilated in a ceremony-like atmosphere. It has attracted condemnation from [[animal rights]] activists in the region, with several Mexican environmental authorities filing an [[animal cruelty]] complaint in regards to the practice.<ref>{{cite news|title=Mexican Environmental Authorities file complaint against duck-killing ceremony|publisher=[[The Yucatan Times]]|url=http://www.theyucatantimes.com/2015/05/mexican-environmental-authorities-file-complaint-against-duck-killing-ceremony/|date=May 21, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015|archive-date=May 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150523105438/http://www.theyucatantimes.com/2015/05/mexican-environmental-authorities-file-complaint-against-duck-killing-ceremony/|url-status=dead}}</ref>
