'''Gran Chaco''' is a [[Provinces of Bolivia|province]] in the eastern part of [[Bolivia]], located in the [[Departments of Bolivia|department of]] [[Tarija Department|Tarija]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Tholen |first=Paul M. |url=https://www.fao.org/4/ar847s/ar847s.pdf |title=Complejo de tierra en la provincia Gran Chaco. Una determinación de cualidades de la tierra a nivel de reconocimiento. |last2=Villagra A. |first2=Oscar |date= |publisher=[[Food and Agriculture Organization]] |year=1990 |pages=5-6 |language=es}}</ref> The province voted to become an autonomous region on December 6, 2009.

==Location==
''Gran Chaco'' province is one of six provinces in the Tarija Department.<ref name=":0" /> It is located between 21° 00' and 22° 17' [[Latitude|south]] and between 62° 16' and 64° 18' [[Longitude|west]].<ref name=":0" />

The province borders [[Chuquisaca Department]] in the north, [[Burnet O'Connor Province]] in the north-west, [[Aniceto Arce Province]] in the south-west, [[Argentina]] in the south, and [[Paraguay]] in the east.

The province extends over 200&nbsp;km from north to south, and 200&nbsp;km from east to west.

==Population==
The main [[language]] of the province is [[Spanish language|Spanish]], spoken by 98.4%, while 11.5% of the population speak [[Quechua language|Quechua]], 3.0% speak [[Aymara language|Aymara]], and 2.7% [[Guarani language|Guaraní]].

The population increased from 74,612 inhabitants (1992 [[census]]) to 116,318 (2001 census), an increase of 55.9%. 41.9% of the population are younger than 15 years old.

50.7% of the population have no access to [[Electricity generation|electricity]], 41.1% have no [[Tap water|sanitary facilities]] (1992).

19.1% of the population are employed in [[Primary sector of industry|agriculture]], 1.4% in [[mining]], 8.3% in [[Secondary sector of industry|industry]], 71.2% in [[Tertiary sector of industry|general services]] (2001).

86.8% of the population are [[Roman Catholicism in Bolivia|Catholics]], 9.4% are [[Protestantism in South America|Protestants]] (1992).
