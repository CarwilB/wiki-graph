'''Villa de Zaachila''' is a town  and [[municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]], Mexico, {{convert|6|km|abbr=on}} from [[Oaxaca City]].<ref name=oaxacainfo>{{cite web|url=http://www.oaxacainfo.com/oaxaca/zaachila.htm|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20021221201847/http://www.oaxacainfo.com/oaxaca/zaachila.htm|url-status=usurped|archive-date=December 21, 2002|title=Zaachila: 14th century Oaxacan city has many unexplored gems1|work=Oaxaca Travel Guide|access-date=2007-08-10}}</ref> 
It is part of the [[Zaachila District, Oaxaca|Zaachila District]] in the west of the [[Valles Centrales de Oaxaca|Valles Centrales Region]].<ref name="encmuc">{{Cite web |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20565a.htm |title=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México Estado de Oaxaca – Villa de Zaáchila |publisher=INAFED |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=March 17, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070523100017/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20565a.htm |archive-date=May 23, 2007 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all }}</ref>  In the pre-Hispanic era, it was the main city-state for the Valley of Oaxaca after the fall of [[Monte Albán]], and the Zaachila Zapotecs were the prominent political force for much of the Valley of Oaxaca when the Spanish arrived.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="guevea">{{cite journal |last1= Oudijk |first1=Michel |last2= Jansen |first2=Maarten  |date=Spring 2000 |title= Changing history in the Lienzos de Guevea and Santo Domingo Petapa |journal=Ethnohistory |volume=47 |issue=2 |pages=281–332 |doi=10.1215/00141801-47-2-281|hdl=1887/12346 |hdl-access=free }}</ref>  Since then, it has been mostly quiet, but political unrest has been prominent since 2006 and the municipality [[Dual power|has two parallel governments]].<ref name="fantasma">{{cite news |title= En Zaachila viven con el fantasma de la confrontación |first= Francisco  |last= Reséndiz |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=December 25, 2006 |language=es |trans-title=In Zaachila, they live with the ghost of confrontation  }}</ref>

Zaachila is famous for its Thursday open air markets, which spreads over much of the center of town and has been a tradition since pre-Hispanic times. It is home to a mostly unexplored archeological site and is known for a dance called the Danza de los Zancudos, or Dance of the Stilts.<ref name="zancudos">{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/notas/6049-La-Danza-de-los-Zancudos-en-la-villa-de-Zaachila-%28Oaxaca%29 |title=La Danza de los Zancudos en la villa de Zaachila (Oaxaca) |first=Nicolás |last=Triedo |date=December 2000 |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=The Dance of the Stilts in the village of Zaachila |access-date=March 17, 2010 |archive-date=October 7, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091007171712/http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/notas/6049-La-Danza-de-los-Zancudos-en-la-villa-de-Zaachila-(Oaxaca) |url-status=dead }}</ref> 
The official name is Villa de Zaachila (Town of Zaachila). The name Zaachila is from the [[Zapotec languages|Zapotec language]] and means "large leaf of the purslane ([[Portulaca oleracea]])".<ref name="encmuc"/>

==History==
After the fall of Monte Alban, the Valley of Oaxaca was divided into a number of city-states. They shared a religious center, [[Mitla]], where the deities of the underworld were venerated. Late in the 13th century, the ruler of the Zaachila city-state began to grow in power to dominate much of the surrounding valley.<ref name="hanns24">{{cite book |title= Ancient Americas : A Brief History & Guide to Research |last= Prem |first= Hanns J  |year=1997 |publisher= University of Utah Press |location= Salt Lake City, UT, USA |isbn= 978-0-87480-536-9 |page=24 }}</ref>  Starting from around 1400, there are five known rulers of the "Zaachila yoo" or "house of Zaachila."<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="guevea"/> These rulers are named  Zaachila I, Zaachila II and Zaachila III, Cosijoeza and Coijopij. The names of the first three probably come from the fact that these lords are depicted in codices without names or at least without legible names. The last two were alive during the Spanish Conquest and the last, Coijopij, is depicted in the Lienzo de Guevea in Spanish dress on a Spanish style throne.<ref name="guevea"/>
[[File:Zaachila nuttall.png|thumb|left|150px|The [[Mixtec]] place sign for Zaachila (''Ñuhu Tocuisi'' in Mixtec), from the [[Codex Zouche-Nuttall]]]]
The territory of these Zapotecs was considered vulnerable since the [[Mixtec]]s’ eastern border was nearby.  Another threat was the [[Aztec]]s, who had  made incursions trying to control the trade route to the [[Isthmus of Tehuantepec|Isthmus]].The city of Zaachila became key to defending Zapotec lands.<ref name="guerras">{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/notas/4457-Guerras-y-alianzas-ind%EDgenas-a-la-llegada-de-los-espa%F1oles |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130219035322/http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/notas/4457-Guerras-y-alianzas-ind%EDgenas-a-la-llegada-de-los-espa%F1oles |url-status=dead |archive-date=2013-02-19 |title=Guerras y alianzas indígenas a la llegada de los españoles |first=Nelly M. |last=Robles García |date=October 2000 |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Wars and alliances with the arrival of the Spanish |access-date=March 17, 2010 }}</ref>  Despite these threats, Zaachila extended its power over a large area, so that the entire valley came to be known by the city’s name.<ref name="terracieno333">{{cite book |title= Mixtecs of Colonial Oaxaca : Nudzahui History, Sixteenth Through Eighteenth Centuries. |last= Terraciano |first= Kevin |year=2002 |publisher= Stanford University Press |location= Palo Alto, CA, USA |isbn= 978-0-8047-5104-9 |page=333 }}</ref>  The history of the Zapotecs and Mixtecs is one of war, strategy and alliances.<ref name="guerras"/> Most alliances between the Zaachila Zapotec and the Mixtec involved the common concern of keeping the Aztecs out of Oaxaca.<ref name="hamnett54">{{cite book |title= Concise History of Mexico. |url= https://archive.org/details/concisehistoryof0000hamn |url-access= registration |last= Hamnett |first= Brian R |year=1999 |publisher= Cambridge University Press |location= Port Chester, NY, USA |isbn= 978-0-521-58916-1 |page=[https://archive.org/details/concisehistoryof0000hamn/page/54 54] }}</ref> Zaachila II defeated the [[Mixe people|Mixe]]s and other peoples on the road to Tehuantepec but was in relative peace with the Mixtecs. Zaachila III fought the Mixtecs and the Aztecs, but lost the capital of Zaachila, moving to Tehuantepec.<ref name="guerras"/>

The last two rulers of the Zaachila Zapotecs were [[Cosijoeza]] and [[Cosijopii]], who were father and son. Aztec pressure forced Cosijoeza to allow trade passage and the establishment of an Aztec military outpost in what is now the city of Oaxaca.<ref name="hamnett54"/>  Cosijopii allied with the Aztecs and Mixtecs against the Spanish, regaining the lost city of Zaachila, but ultimately was unable to resist the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|Conquest]].<ref name="guerras"/> Both Cosijoeza and Cosijopii were alive during the Conquest and both were baptized by the Spanish as Don Carlos Cosijoeza and Don Juan Cortez respectively. They continued to control the key town of [[Tehuantepec]] after the Conquest.<ref name="guevea"/>

Since the [[2006 Oaxaca protests]], Zaachila has had two parallel governments, which have confronted each other numerous times.<ref name="pedradas">{{cite news |title= Enfrentamiento a pedradas y cohetones en Zaachila |url= http://www.notihuatulcopuertoescondido.com/?p=649 |archive-url= https://archive.today/20110714170339/http://www.notihuatulcopuertoescondido.com/?p=649 |url-status= usurped |archive-date= July 14, 2011 |newspaper=Noticias Huatulco/Puerto Escondido |location=Puerto Escondido, Oaxaca |date=June 20, 2008 |access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Confrontation with rocks and rockets in Zaachila  }}</ref> In June 2006, [[APPO]] took over the municipal palace, being one of the first captured by the dissidents during the unrest in Oaxaca state. This act eventually evolved into the creation of a parallel government, a "people’s municipality,"  headed by Miguel Angel Hernandez Vazquez.<ref name="policias">{{cite news |title= Retiene a policías APPO en Zaachila |first= Virgilio  |last=Sanchez |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=October 7, 2006 |language=es |trans-title=Police held by APPO in Zaachila  }}</ref>  This council rejected the government of then municipal president Jose Coronel Martinez and still rejects the "constitutional" government of Noé Pérez Martinez, accusing them of repression, abuse of authority, nepotism and other charges.<ref name="fantasma"/><ref name="policias"/>  The town's support is divided between the two governments and each government has control of different aspects of the town. The constitutional government has control of police and other services. The people's government has control of business taxation, which raises about 6,000 pesos per month to provide services such as trash removal. However, the streets are dirty and there is no police presence in the ten neighborhoods of the town. Citizens’ patrols have been organized but nighttime is still dangerous. From its inception, the "people’s government" has received international support from countries such as [[Venezuela]], France, Spain, Germany, Canada and [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="fantasma"/>  In 2007 the state legislature recognized the "closure of powers" of the constitutional government but has not recognized the APPO supported government.<ref name="aprueban">{{cite news |title= Aprueban la desaparición de poderes en Zaachila |url= http://www.jornada.unam.mx/2007/02/16/index.php?section=estados&article=041n4est |agency=Notimex |newspaper=La Jornada |location=Mexico City |date=February 16, 2007 |access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Approve closure of powers in Zaachila }}</ref>

There have been a number of confrontations between supporters of the two governments since 2006.<ref name="pedradas"/> The largest occurred in 2008, when there was a confrontation between APPO and supporters of municipal president Noé Pérez Martínez. The governor of Oaxaca, Ulises Ruiz Ortiz was scheduled to appear in the town on a tour but opponents of the visit began to block streets. Supporters of Perez Martinez countered, and the father of the municipal president was accused of firing a shot towards the crowd.<ref name="pedradas"/> However, most confrontation between the two factions has come in the form of graffiti and political posters.<ref name="fantasma"/>

Another legacy of the events of 2006 is Radio Zaachila ([[XHZAA-FM]] 96.3). It is one of eight stations that began to illegally operate around that time. It has been supported by the Section 22 of the [[Sindicato Nacional de Trabajadores de la Educación]] (Mexico's teachers’ union).<ref name="conceden">{{cite news |title=Conceden permiso a Zaachila Radio |first=Yadira |last=Sosa |url=http://www.imparcialenlinea.com/?mod=leer&id=113372&sec=primera&titulo=Conceden_permiso_a_Zaachila_Radio |newspaper=El Imparcial |location=Oaxaca, Oaxaca |date=February 23, 2010 |access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Concede permit to Zaachila Radio |archive-date=July 13, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110713022346/http://www.imparcialenlinea.com/?mod=leer&id=113372&sec=primera&titulo=Conceden_permiso_a_Zaachila_Radio |url-status=dead }}</ref>  In 2008, the homes of Melesio Melchor Angeles and Jorge Aragon Martinez were shot at, with the attack against Melchor Angeles occurring at 2:30 in the morning while he and his family were inside.<ref name="comunicador">{{cite news |title= Acusan atentado contra comunicador |first= Virgilio  |last=Sanchez |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=July 29, 2008 |language=es |trans-title=Accusation of terrorism against spokesman  }}</ref><ref name="balean">{{cite news |title= Balean a dos integrantes de Radio Zaachila, Oaxaca |url= http://impreso.milenio.com/node/8084940 |newspaper=Milenio |location=Mexico City |date=November 18, 2008 |access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Two member of Radio Zaachila shot at }}</ref>  Both had been accused by municipal authorities of being involved in the altercation that prevented the visit of the governor.<ref name="balean"/> [[Cofetel]] awarded an operating permit to Zaachila Radio in February 2010 and efforts are underway to get permits for the other stations.<ref name="conceden"/>

Author [[Peter Gelderloos]] considers the current governmental situation to be a partial example of a functioning [[Anarchism|anarchist]] society.<ref>{{Cite book|title=Anarchy Works|last=Gelderloos|first=Peter}}</ref>

==The town==
[[File:Reloj plaza zaachila.jpg|thumb|Main plaza with clock]]
Zaachila has considerable cultural wealth with native music, dance, gastronomy and market days filled with flavors and colors, whose traditions have been preserved for generations.<ref name="zancudos"/>  Many houses in this area still have wood stoves and/or a small fireplace in which food is cooked in the embers.<ref name="dinero">{{cite news |title= Con dinero y sin dinero |first= Ivan  |last= Rendon |newspaper=El Norte |location=Monterrey, Mexico |date=May 26, 1997 |page= 8 |language=es |trans-title=With money and without money  }}</ref> [[Zaachila Zapotec]] is spoken in the town.<ref>{{e25|ztx|Zaachila Zapotec}}</ref>

The town is formed by ten neighborhoods called San Pedro, San Pablo, San José, San Sebastián, San Jacinto, Del Carmen, Soledad, La Purísima, La Virgen and Del Niño. Each has its own patron saint and corresponding festival. These festivals are coordinated each year by a formal committee which includes a president, secretary and other positions.<ref name="zancudos"/> Major celebrations celebrated by the whole town include [[Holy Week]] with [[passion play]]s, processions and other religious events, and [[Guelaguetza]], with a staging of the story of [[Donaji]]. At weddings, it is traditional to send a musical band to fetch the bride and accompany her to the church.<ref name="encmuc"/> Writer Gerardo Melchor Calvo has written about the stories and customs of the municipality, based mostly on local oral tradition. The book is called ''Historia de un pueblo. Relatos y costumbres de Zaachila''. Some of the stories include "La Matlacihua", "El Perro Negro", "La Procesión" and "La Marrana." There are also descriptions of local events such as the yearly horse races on 24 July.<ref name="recupera">{{cite press release |title= Recupera en cuentos la tradición oral de Villa de Zaachila, Oaxaca |publisher=Office of the President of Mexico |date=February 3, 2009 |url= http://www.presidencia.gob.mx/prensa/ultimasnoticias/?contenido=41939 |language=es |trans-title=Recooperating oral tradition of Villa de Zaachila, Oaxaca |access-date=March 17, 2010 }}</ref> 
The center of town has a large plaza with a kiosk, which is surrounded by the municipal palace, archeological site and the church, called "Nuestra Señora de la Natividad" (Our Lady of the Nativity).<ref name="zancudos"/><ref name="MexDesOax">{{cite journal |last= Quintanar Hinojosa |first=Beatriz  |date=August 2007 |title=Joyas ocultas de los valles centrales |journal=Guía México Desconocido: Oaxaca |volume= 137  |pages=88  |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx }}</ref> The church is notable for a number of colonial-era ''santos'', statues of saints, some of them executed in polychrome and still in beautiful condition.<ref>[https://archive.today/20120722012936/http://www.aug.edu/augusta/santos/zaachila/index.html Santos in Oaxaca's Ancient Churches: Santa María de la Natividad Zaachila.] Retrieved 2012-04-17.</ref> Zaachila has a [[Traditional fixed markets in Mexico|stationary municipal market]] in which can be found regional foods such as [[barbacoa]], "[[chichilo]],"[[mole (sauce)|mole verde]], turkey in mole with rice, [[carnitas]] and empanadas, served with [[tepache]], [[atole]], [[mezcal]] and hot chocolate; however, most shopping is done on Thursday, the town's market day.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Just outside the town is its railroad station, built in the early 20th century when it was the Ferrocarril Agrícola de Oaxaca (Agricultural Railroad of Oaxaca). It is the oldest rail line in the Valley of Oaxaca, which was ceded to the Ferrocarril Mexicano del Sur in 1918.<ref name="ferrocarril">{{cite web |url= http://sic.conaculta.gob.mx/ficha.php?table=fnme&table_id=129&estado_id=20 |title= Zaachila |publisher=CONACULTA |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=March 17, 2010 }}</ref>

===The Día de Plaza===
[[File:ThursMarketZaachila.JPG|thumb|Scene from the Thursday market]]
The Thursday Día de Plaza ([[tianguis]]), or outdoor market, is Zaachila's weekly main event, which attracts people from various parts of the Valley of Oaxaca, many of whom are Zapotec-speaking peoples from the rural areas. The stalls spread for blocks below the archeological site and the main church. This market tradition dates back to pre-Hispanic times and has changed very little since then.<ref name="zancudos"/><ref name="moon">{{cite web |url=http://www.moon.com/destinations/oaxaca/oaxaca-valley/south-crafts-route/zaachila/zaachila-market |title= Zaachila Market |publisher=Moon Handbooks |access-date=March 17, 2010}}</ref>  This market is divided into three sections. The first is called by the Zapotec name "Logueguindan," which means "plaza of the people of the hills,"<ref name="dinero"/> and is mostly dedicated to firewood and charcoal for use during the week. This market is located at the entrance to the town, and much of this is from the ocote tree which has a distinctive odor.<ref name="zancudos"/><ref name="frommers">{{cite web |url= http://www.frommers.com/destinations/oaxacacity/0930024212.html  |title= South of Monte Albán: Arrazola, Cuilapan & Zaachila |publisher=Frommers |access-date=March 17, 2010}}</ref>  This firewood is obtained from the surrounding hills by native Zapotecs and sold by women, with many of the transactions occurring in the Zapotec language.  In addition to firewood, they may offer fruits and vegetables as well. The wood market also sells farming implements such as yokes for oxen. To bring the wood home, there is a service where blue carts pulled by donkeys or horses carry purchases. Although the market has existed for many years, only recently has it been formally organized with an administrator.<ref name="dinero"/>

The second section is dedicated to the sale of animals such as goats, pigs, sheep, cattle and others. The third section is on the main square proper with a variety of products from the region such as foodstuffs and local prepared foods such as "capirotadas," snacks made with chickpeas, nut cakes, sweet breads, candies such as tamarind balls and more.<ref name="zancudos"/> Other highlights include the scarlet-red pitacaya fruit and a foamy tan drink called "tejate," which is a corn based drink flavored with chocolate, rose petals or ground [[Pouteria sapota|mamey]] seeds, sweetened with brown sugar juice and honey.<ref name="moon"/> Baskets and pottery are sold for household use.<ref name="frommers"/>

===Archeological site===
[[File:Zaachila Panorama.JPG|thumb|View of the only excavated portion of the site]]
The [[Zaachila|archeological site of Zaachila]] is next to the Church of Nuestra Señora de la Natividad, the remains of the pre-Hispanic center of Zaachila, which was the local capital when the Spanish arrived. At that time, it was considered to be a second [[Tenochtitlan]], as it sat on an island in the middle of a lake, which was called Roaló.<ref name="zancudos"/> The site consists of mounds that define a ceremonial plaza. The extension of the site is not fully known because many of the mounds, plazas and other constructions are currently covered by modern structures, which have destroyed or modified mounds and other artifacts.<ref name="sicgob">{{cite web |url= http://www.sic.gob.mx/ficha.php?table=zona_arqueologica&table_id=78&estado_id=20 |title= Zaachila |publisher=CONACULTA |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=March 17, 2010 }}</ref>  There is also a large unexplored pyramid. Although largely un-excavated, two tombs were discovered at the site in 1962.<ref name=oaxacainfo/> These tombs are thought to belong to important [[Mixtec]] persons,<ref name="MexDesOax"/> as they contained a number of interesting grave goods. The first of these tombs has an entrance decorated with feline heads and inside were found seven figures molded from stucco, six placed in pairs on the side walls of the main chamber. In the antechamber, there are depictions of owls and people along with the date of Five Flower.<ref name="encmuc"/> The objects found in these tombs are now on display at the [[National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico)|National Museum of Anthropology]] in Mexico City.<ref name="frommers"/>

===Dance of the Zancudos===
The Dance of the Zancudos originated in the San Pedro neighborhood over 100 years ago and is one of the few places the dance can still be found. The origin of the Dance of the Zancudos is in a promise the inhabitants of Zaachila made to Saint Peter. The story behind it states that an old man encountered an apparition of [[Saint Peter]] who asked the community build a church in his name.  The apparition appeared each day until it was captured by the local people and enclosed in a small church. Guards were posted at the entrance with rifles and [[machete]]s. However, the next day, it was discovered that the saint was no longer there, and he appeared again to the old man who had originally seen him, and indicated that God had sent him to protect the village. Eight days remained until the feast of Saint Peter, and the saint asked that the villagers form pairs on stilts to dance. On the feast day, the men danced on stilts while the women surrounded them with candles and incense. The dancers arrived at the foot of a hill where an image of the saint was found, which was brought to the San Pedro neighborhood where a church was constructed for it.<ref name="zancudos"/>

José Mendoza is the captain of the Zancudo group of the San Pedro neighborhood, and has spent about forty years recruiting dancers and training them. This includes teaching how to make the stilts made from ocote wood. These stilts measure two meters long and have a "foot" fastened by wire and rope. Recruited dancers are boys from 12 to 14 years of age and are trained for two months after they make a pledge to Saint Peter. At first, the boys balance themselves with safety ropes on their stilts. Only men are permitted to dance, with some wearing women's masks. Performances can extend for hours, during which small gifts are distributed among the spectators from baskets. The dancers are accompanied by musical groups, "chinas Oaxaqueñas" and "monos de calenda." These dancers perform the strenuous performance on the feast day of Saint Peter as well as at the Guelaguetza of Oaxaca and other dance festivals in Mexico.<ref name="zancudos"/>

==The municipality==
[[File:ValleyZaachila1.JPG|thumb|Overlooking the area ESE of the Zaachila archeological site]]
As municipal seat, the town of Villa de Zaachila is the local governing authority for 25 other named communities,<ref name="INEGI">{{cite web|url=http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |title=INEGI Census 2005 |language=es |access-date=March 17, 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130406130712/http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |archive-date=April 6, 2013 }}</ref> covering an area of 54.86&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name="encmuc"/>  Just under half of the municipal population of 28,003 lives in the town proper,<ref name="INEGI"/> and about 2,317 people speak an indigenous language. The municipality borders the municipalities of [[San Raymundo Jalpan]], [[San Pedro la Reforma]], [[Hacienda de Tlanichico y Noriega]], [[Trinidad Zaachila]], [[San Pablo la Raya]] and [[Manteón]].<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipal seat is surrounded by low mountains, ten of which are the most prominent. The two main rivers are the Verde and the Atoya. It has a temperate climate with most rain falling in June and July. Wild flora that can be found here includes pines, oaks, willows and other trees, as well as medicinal plants. Wildlife includes [[deer]], [[fox]]es, [[coyote]]s, [[opossum]]s, [[rabbit]]s as well as a wide variety of birds and reptiles.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Much of the economy of the municipality is based on small-scale farming and livestock raising as well as commerce. Agriculture occupies the majority of the land but only 15% of the people. Crops grown here include fruit trees such as apples, peaches, pears, [[tejocote]]s, oranges and limes,  grains such as corn and various vegetables such as beans and [[nopal]]s.<ref name="encmuc"/> Most of the crops are irrigated with groundwater which is found close to the surface.<ref name="zancudos"/> Mining and industries employ 27% while commerce, tourism and services employs about 55%.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipality has had problems with the illegal trafficking of land, the victims of which are having problems obtaining basic services. One area particularly affected is a zone called Zaachila Segundo. Here about 700 lots, with a value of 50,000 pesos each, are in question. The problem extends from the current rapid growth of the town, whose population is expected to grow by 240% by 2030. About 18,000 inhabitants  of the municipality do not have sufficient water, electricity or drainage services.<ref name="victima">{{cite news |title= Zaachila Segundo, víctima del tráfico de terrenos |first= Elizabeth |last= Mendoza |url= http://www.cronicaoaxaca.info/informaciongeneral/85-zaachila-segundo-victima-del-trafico-de-terrenos.html |newspaper=Cronica Oaxaca |location=Oaxaca, Oaxaca |date=October 19, 2009|access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Zaachila Segundo, victim of land trafficking  }}</ref>

A more serious problem for the municipality is the [[open dump]] that was established about thirty years ago to receive waste from the city of Oaxaca and 18 other municipalities. The dump contains about 650 tons of trash and has space for more, but it is located in a geologically vulnerable zone, and since its establishment, many neighborhoods have been built near it. This means that a number of communities are having health problems due to contaminated ground water and airborne bacteria.<ref name="basurero">{{cite news |title= Basurero de Zaachila provoca daños irreversibles a colonos |first=Carina |last=Garcia |url= http://www.diariodespertar.com.mx/zona-m/8624-Basurero-Zaachila-provoca-daos-irreversibles-colonos.html |newspaper=Diario Despertar  |date=April 16, 2009 |access-date=March 17, 2010 |language=es |trans-title=Zaachila dump provokes irreversible damage to neighbors }}</ref>

This is the only dump for the entire Oaxaca city area.  It began illegally but was legalized in 1986. However, by this time, there had already been a number of complaints from the neighborhoods of Vicente Guerrero and Emiliano Zapata near the site. The problems with the dump were bad enough that even those who made money from sorting through the garbage asked for its closure.<ref name="davidv">{{cite book |title= Environmental Justice in Latin America : Problems, Promise, and Practice. |editor1-first= David V.  |editor1-last= Carruthers |year=2008 |publisher= MIT Press |location= Cambridge, MA, USA |isbn= 978-0-262-53300-3 |pages=124–125}}</ref> The need to build the landfill was announced in 2000.<ref name="basurero"/> However, the dump still continues to operate the way it always has, with no processing of the waste and minimal efforts in containing pollution.<ref name="davidv"/>
