'''Bruzual''' is one of the 14 municipalities of the state of [[Yaracuy]], [[Venezuela]]. The municipality is located in southwestern Yaracuy, occupying an area of 417&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> with a big majority of its 84,876 inhabitants, residing in the [[municipal seat]] of [[Chivacoa]]. The municipality was named in 1877 to honour Venezuelan liberal military officer [[Manuel Ezequiel Bruzual]]. Today, Bruzual is known for its agriculture, most notably holding the state's highest concentration of [[legume]] production. Additionally, the municipality contains the [[Cerro María Lionza Natural Monument]], officially certified as Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Nation.

==Name==
The Bruzual Municipality was named in 1877 to honor [[Manuel Ezequiel Bruzual]], a prominent historical figure celebrated as a hero of both the [[Venezuelan War of Independence]] and the [[Federal War]]. Bruzual initially served in the [[Spanish army]] before switching sides to fight for independence under Generals [[Santiago Mariño]] and [[Carlos Soublette]] and later fought for the [[Great Liberal Party of Venezuela|Liberal faction]] in the Federal War, served as Minister of War and Navy, and became provisional [[President of Venezuela]] in 1868 before being overthrown by the ''Revolución Azul'' and dying from battle wounds in [[Curaçao]]. Today, his memory is honored in the municipality by a local tribute [[bust (sculpture)|bust]] located in the center of Plaza Bruzual, directly facing the [[Municipal Council]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://albaciudad.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Yaracuy-Bruzual.pdf|title=Muncipio Bruzual|trans-title=Bruzual Municipality|language=es|website=Alba Ciudad 96.3 FM|publisher={{ill|Ministry of Culture (Venezuela)|es|Ministerio del Poder Popular para la Cultura (Venezuela)|lt=Ministry of Culture}}|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref>

==Economy==
The Bruzual Municipality is known in the state of [[Yaracuy]] as having the highest concentration of [[legumes]] production, with 61% of the beans and 15.7% of the [[black turtle bean|black beans]]. Also, the municipality is known as a grower of crops such as [[coffee bean|coffee]], [[sugarcane]], [[avocado]], [[sweet potato]], and [[bell pepper]].<ref name="OQHernández">{{cite web|url=https://bibliofep.fundacionempresaspolar.org/media/1378336/gv_t7_c58_p416_489_yaracuy_lres_single_preview.pdf|title=Estado Yaracuy|trans-title=Yaracuy State|language=es|first=Octaviano Quiroz|last=Hernández|website=GeoVenezuela|publisher=[[Empresas Polar|Fundación Empresas Polar]]|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the [[2011 Venezuelan census]], The population of the Bruzual Municipality was 74,345 people, accounting for 11.58% of the total population of the state of [[Yaracuy]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/22__yaracuy/|title=Yaracuy (State in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref> It is the third most populated municipality and together with [[San Felipe Municipality, Yaracuy|San Felipe]], [[Peña Municipality|Peña]], and [[Nirgua Municipality|Nirgua]] they compose 55.71% of the population of Yaracuy.<ref name="OQHernández"/> A big majority of the population (75.5%) resides in [[Chivacoa]], the [[municipal seat]] of the municipality.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/yaracuy/2203__bruzual/|title=Urban Localities in Bruzual (Yaracuy, Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=25 June 2017|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref>

By June 2019, official projections from the [[Statistics National Institute (Venezuela)|Venezuelan Statistics National Institute]] estimated the population of Bruzual as 84,876 people, representing an [[annual growth rate]] of 1.7% since 2011 and showing a population density of 203.5 inhabitants/km<sup>2</sup>. However, these projections do not account for the impact of emigration linked to the country's recent economic and political circumstances.<ref name="CityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/yaracuy/2203__bruzual/|title=Bruzual (Municipality in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref>

The [[gender]] distribution of the population showed an even balance with 34,980 men (50.2%) and 34,752 women (49.8%). The [[age distribution]] showed that the largest segment of the population was aged 15 to 64, comprising 65.4% of the people. Younger people aged 0 to 14 made up 29% of the population, while those aged 65 and older accounted for the remaining 5.6%. The municipality is highly urbanized, with 89.8% of the inhabitants (62,641) living in [[urban centers]] compared to  10.2% (7,091) in [[rural areas]].<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Ethnically, the municipality identified as predominantly [[Mestizo]] (58.3%) and [[White people]] (36.6%). Minority groups included 4% [[Afro-Venezuelans]] and 1.1% belonging to other ethnic groups, with 31 individuals identifying as [[indigenous peoples|indigenous]]. The [[literacy rate]] was 94.2%, with 3,306 inhabitants of Bruzual not able to read or write.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

==Culture==
The [[Cerro María Lionza Natural Monument]] is located in the Bruzual Municipality, near [[Chivacoa]]. It serves as a major tourist and religious centre for the [[María Lionza]] movement.<ref name="OQHernández"/> The monument was officially recognised by the Venezuelan government, with the [[Ministry of Culture (Venezuela)|Ministry of Culture]] formally certifying the Cult of María Lionza as Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Nation.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://elorientaldemonagas.com/culto-a-maria-lionza-es-oficialmente-patrimonio-cultural-inmaterial-de-la-nacion/|title=Culto a María Lionza es oficialmente Patrimonio Cultural Inmaterial de la Nación|trans-title=The Cult of María Lionza is Officially an Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Nation|language=es|first=Jesús|last=Rodríguez|website=[[El Oriental De Monagas]]|date=13 October 2025|access-date=31 May 2026}}</ref>
