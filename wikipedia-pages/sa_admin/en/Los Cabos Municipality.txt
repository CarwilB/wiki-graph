'''Los Cabos''' ({{IPA|es|los ˈkaβos}}) is a [[municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] located at the southern tip of [[Mexico]]'s [[Baja California Peninsula]], in the [[Mexican state|state]] of [[Baja California Sur]]. It encompasses the two towns of [[Cabo San Lucas]] and [[San José del Cabo]] (the municipal seat) linked by the thirty-two-km [[Los Cabos Corridor|Resort Corridor]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite news|url=http://www.latimes.com/world/mexico-americas/la-fg-mexico-los-cabos-violence-20170901-htmlstory.html|title=Los Cabos is no longer a haven from Mexico's bloodshed|work=Los Angeles Times|access-date=2018-01-20|language=en-US|issn=0458-3035|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180120032513/http://www.latimes.com/world/mexico-americas/la-fg-mexico-los-cabos-violence-20170901-htmlstory.html|archive-date=2018-01-20|url-status=live}}</ref>

The area was remote and rural until the latter 20th century, when the Mexican government began to develop Cabo San Lucas for tourism, which then spread east to the municipal seat. The main draw is the climate and geography, where desert meets the sea, along with [[sport fishing]], resorts and golf. This tourism is by far the main economic activity with over two million visitors per year. Over 1 million visit from the United States.

Although San José del Cabo is the seat of government for the municipality of Los Cabos, it is smaller than Cabo San Lucas. San José's  growth is now rivaling that of the more famous resort area.<ref name=encmuc>{{cite web |title=Los Cabos |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=INAFED |location=Mexico |year=2010 |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM03bajacaliforniasur/municipios/03008a.html |access-date=July 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402181509/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM03bajacaliforniasur/municipios/03008a.html |archive-date=April 2, 2015 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=page182>{{cite book  |author= John Howells and Don Merwin  |title= Choose Mexico for retirement  |publisher= The Globe Pequot Press  |location= Guilford, CT  |year= 2007  |isbn= 9780762743926  |page= [https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780762743926/page/182 182]  |url-access= registration  |url= https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780762743926/page/182  }}</ref>

This growth has been regulated to outside of the town centre, especially to the south where the beaches are, leaving the historic town centre quiet and relatively unchanged.<ref name=page182/><ref name=fodors>{{cite web |title=San José del Cabo |publisher=Fodors |url=http://www.fodors.com/world/mexico-and-central-america/mexico/los-cabos/san-jose-del-cabo/ |access-date=July 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140728112600/http://www.fodors.com/world/mexico-and-central-america/mexico/los-cabos/san-jose-del-cabo/ |archive-date=July 28, 2014 |url-status=live }}</ref> There are still [[cobblestone]] streets, [[adobe]] houses, [[jacaranda]] trees and a central square in front of a church that dates from the 18th century, where people gather in the evening when it is cooler.<ref name=page182/><ref name=fodors/><ref name=jnoel>{{cite news |author=Josh Noel |title=Contrasting Cabos Take your pick of party or peace at the tip of Mexico's Baja California |newspaper=Chicago Tribune |url=http://articles.chicagotribune.com/2013-10-29/travel/ct-trav-1103-cabo-mexico-20131029_1_cabo-wabo-cabo-san-lucas-van-halen |date=October 29, 2013 |access-date=July 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140724081220/http://articles.chicagotribune.com/2013-10-29/travel/ct-trav-1103-cabo-mexico-20131029_1_cabo-wabo-cabo-san-lucas-van-halen |archive-date=July 24, 2014 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

A number of the large houses in the center date from the 19th century, and most of these have been converted into restaurants, art galleries and shops selling everything from fine handcrafts, silver, local gemstones and souvenirs.<ref name=page182/><ref name=fodors/><ref name=skang>{{cite news |author= Stephanie Kang  |title= Off the Beaten Track / San Jose del Cabo |newspaper= Wall Street Journal, Eastern edition  |location=New York|date= February 28, 2006  |page=D7 }}</ref> The art scene in the town is well-developed because of tourism and people with vacation homes. These shops carry [[Luxury goods#Art history|high end paintings]] and sculptures from traditional Mexican, Mexican contemporary and international artisans and artists.<ref name=trwilliamson>{{cite news |author=Teresa Rodriguez Williamson |title=Mexico is Ready for Primetime Part II |newspaper=Huffington Post |url=http://www.huffingtonpost.com/teresa-rodriguez-williamson/mexico-is-ready-for-prime_b_211853.html |date=June 6, 2009 |access-date=July 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140728235226/http://www.huffingtonpost.com/teresa-rodriguez-williamson/mexico-is-ready-for-prime_b_211853.html |archive-date=July 28, 2014 |url-status=live }}</ref>

During the high season from October to May, these galleries stay open late into the night.<ref name=jnoel/> The town has resisted the addition of large shopping malls and chain stores.<ref name=trwilliamson/> There is also some [[American colonial architecture|colonial era architecture]] as well, but this style has more in common with colonial towns to the north into the United States rather than the centre and south of Mexico.<ref name=diazmunoz>{{cite news |author=Ricardo Diazmunoz and Maryell Ortiz de Zarate |title= Encuentros con Mexico/ Sol, sol y sol (II)|newspaper= Reforma  |location=Mexico City|date= March 12, 2000 |page=2  }}</ref>

The main example of colonial architecture is the town's parish church. It was part of the [[Misión Estero de las Palmas de San José del Cabo Añuití|Estero de las Palmas de San José del Cabo Mission]], founded in 1730.<ref name=trwilliamson/><ref name=mexdes>{{cite web |title= Los Cabos: donde se unen los mares |date= 20 May 2010 |publisher= Mexico Desconocido magazine |location= Mexico City |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/los-cabos.html |access-date= July 23, 2014 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20140729002610/http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/los-cabos.html |archive-date= July 29, 2014 |url-status= live }}</ref> The facade is marked with a tile mural depicting the martyrdom of founder Nicolás Tamaral, killed by the local [[Pericúes|Pericu people]].<ref name=diazmunoz/><ref name=mexdes/> The patron saint of the town is [[Saint Joseph]], whose feast day is celebrated here on 19 March. Another important occasion is the feast of the [[Our Lady of the Pillar]] on 12 October. Occasions like these are marked with traditional dance in dress styles known as "Flor de Pitaya" and the "La Cuera."<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mexdes/>

Other important landmarks in the town include the municipal hall (''palacio municipal''), which dates from 1981 and the cultural centre or ''Casa de Cultura'', housed in a 19th-century building.<ref name=diazmunoz/><ref name=mexdes/>

The tourist area of the town is the area between the town proper and the shoreline. This area has a nine-hole golf course and a line of hotels and resorts facing the ocean,<ref name=fodors/> which served over 900,000 hotel guests in 2011.<ref name=ranking>{{cite web|title=Ranking of World Tourism|url=http://www.siimt.com/work/sites/siimt/resources/LocalContent/2014/10/Numeralia_Junio2012.pdf|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220224223337/http://www.siimt.com/work/sites/siimt/resources/LocalContent/2014/10/Numeralia_Junio2012.pdf|url-status=dead|archive-date=February 24, 2022|publisher=Consejo de Promoción Turística de México|access-date=3 September 2012|page=2|language=es|year=2011}}</ref>

==The municipality==

===Overview===
[[File:AR1 ALFONSO REYES (22777087612).jpg|thumb|250px|[[List of Mexican autopistas|Autopista]] San José del Cabo–Cabo San Lucas]]
San Jose del Cabo is the seat and the government for the communities found in a 3,451.51&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> area, located in the extreme south of the state of Baja California Sur. It is connected to the capital of [[La Paz, Baja California Sur|La Paz]] via the [[Mexican Federal Highway 1|Transpeninsular Highway]].<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=secturbcs>{{cite web |title=Municipio de Los Cabos |publisher=Secretaría de Turismo, Baja California Sur |location=Baja California Sur |url=http://secturbcs.gob.mx/destinos/los-cabos/ |access-date=July 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140728221341/http://secturbcs.gob.mx/destinos/los-cabos/ |archive-date=July 28, 2014 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The municipality borders that of La Paz to the north, with the Pacific Ocean and [[Gulf of California]] surrounding it in the other directions. The municipal government consists of a municipal president, a [[syndic]] and fourteen representatives called regidors.<ref name=encmuc/>

The main areas of the municipality are the seat, Cabo San Lucas, and the tourist corridor along the coast between them. Although San Jose del Cabo is the government, Cabo San Lucas has a higher population and its natural arch at Land's End is the symbol for the municipality.<ref name=mexdes/><ref name=secturbcs/><ref name=lcampuzano>{{cite news |author= Lily Campuzano |title= Los Cabos: Con sabor a mar y desierto |newspaper= Reforma  |location=Mexico City|date= July 12, 1998 |page=10 }}</ref> Outside of the two main cities, other important communities include Colonia del Sol, Las Veredas, Colonia Los Congrejos, San José Viego and La Ribera.<ref name=encmuc/>

The municipality is one of the most important tourist destinations in Mexico, for its fishing, beaches, and resorts.<ref name=secturbcs/>  Historical landmarks are relatively few but include the municipal hall, the Casa de Cultura in San Jose del Cabo, the Faro Viejo and the San Jose del Cabo and [[Misión Santiago de Los Coras|Santiago de las Coras]] missions. The latter was founded in 1721 by [[Ignacio Maria Napoli|Ignacio María Napoli]].<ref name=encmuc/>

===Cabo San Lucas and the tourist corridor===
[[File:CaboSanLucasPort07.JPG|thumb|View of the port of Cabo San Lucas]]
The city of Cabo San Lucas, about {{convert|32|km|mi|abbr=on}} to the west of San Jose del Cabo, is far more commercial. Unlike most Mexican towns, Cabo San Lucas has no main plaza or large cathedral. Instead, it is centered around the marina and entertainment district.<ref name=jnoel/> Up until the latter 20th century, the area was a small fishing village when tourist infrastructure begun. Despite its success, high rise construction has been kept limited, focusing on resorts and sand-top restaurants in the beach area.<ref name=jnoel/><ref name=skang/>

The main attractions are fishing, nightlife and whale watching.<ref name=jnoel/><ref name=skang/> It is a place for vacation, where most visitors stay at all-inclusive resorts. The two main events during the year are Spring Break and [[Sammy Hagar]]'s birthday which happens on the second weekend in October as his birthday is 13 October 1947. The latter focuses on his [[Cabo Wabo]] bar and restaurant in the city.<ref name=jnoel/>

The success of Cabo San Lucas and San Jose del Cabo has created a tourist corridor along the coastal highway between the cities. While there has been some development of resorts, hotels, and golf courses, there are still smaller isolated and undeveloped beaches.<ref name=jnoel/>

==Socioeconomics==
[[File:RoyalSolarisCabo03.JPG|thumb|Royal Solaris resort in San Jose del Cabo]]
By far the main economic activity for the municipality is tourism, focused on a shoreline corridor between Cabo San Lucas and San Jose del Cabo.<ref name=jnoel/><ref name=lcampuzano/> Most visitors come from the United States (especially California) and Canada, followed by Mexico, and many visitors return year after year.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mexdes/><ref name=economicogob>{{cite web |title= Barometro Turístico |publisher= Municipality of Los Cabos |location= Baja California Sur |url= http://www.loscabos.gob.mx/ieconomica.html |access-date= July 23, 2014 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20140804195830/http://www.loscabos.gob.mx/ieconomica.html |archive-date= August 4, 2014 |url-status= dead }}</ref> Visitors can get by in Cabo San Lucas purely in English and use U.S. dollars.<ref name=jnoel/>

Most of the about two million visitors a year arrive by plane to the [[Los Cabos International Airport|Los Cabos airport]], but the Cabo San Lucas marina also has facilities for cruise ships. In 2012, the area has hotel occupancy of just over 60% with 248 cruise ships visiting.<ref name=secturbcs/><ref name=economicogob/> The area's high end resorts have also attracted notable names such as [[Bruce Willis]], [[Kelly Preston]] and [[John Travolta]],<ref name=ptrevino>{{cite news |author= Pepe Treviño |title= Paraíso en el desierto|newspaper= Economista  |location=Mexico City|date= August 9, 2007 }}</ref> and San Jose del Cabo also has a notable expatriate population, mostly retirees who have economic influence.<ref name=page182/>

The main draw for most visitors has been the environment, where the desert meets the sea, best symbolized by [[El Arco de Cabo San Lucas|El Arco]], a natural stone arch over the ocean in Cabo San Lucas where the Pacific Ocean and the Gulf of California meet.<ref name=mexdes/><ref name=ptrevino/> The natural features have led to ecotourism such as boat tours to El Arco, tours of the San Jose Estuary and the coral reefs of [[Cabo Pulmo National Park|Cabo Pulmo]], and whale watching from January to March, when the animals arrive to breed.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=lcampuzano/><ref name=ptrevino/>

Sports fishing is a major and the longest established draw as there are about 800 species of fish in the waters off the coast.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=lcampuzano/> High season for this activity is in the summer, the season for [[marlin]], although fishing for various other species extends all year. There are certain exotic species that are off limits due to conservation concerns and others are "catch-and-release" only.<ref name=mexdes/>

There are four main golf courses in the municipality, designed by the likes of [[Jack Nicklaus]] and [[Pete Dye]].<ref name=lcampuzano/> Other activities for tourists include snorkeling, diving, dune-buggy rides, camel rides, zip-lining, rides on water-propelled jet packs,<ref name=jnoel/> surfing (especially at the Acapulquito Beach), jet-ski riding, kayaking, sailing, horseback riding, ATV riding, hang gliding, mountain biking, camping, tennis and rappelling.<ref name=diazmunoz/><ref name=ptrevino/> Off the beaten path, there is the town of [[Miraflores, Baja California Sur|Miraflores]] (for its leather crafts), the Santiago fossil museum, the traditional towns of Caduaño, San Antonio and San Bartolo and a glass blowing factory.<ref name=encmuc/>

Los Cabos hosts a culinary event called Ritmos, Colores y Sabores, which attracts chefs from the United States and Europe.<ref name=cpoblete>{{cite news |author= Claudio Poblete |title= Una celebración con sazón |newspaper= Reforma  |location=Mexico City|date= December 21, 2007 |page=3 }}</ref> The gastronomy of the region is based on seafood, which includes clams, marlin, snails, tuna and shark. A locally produced liquor is Damiana, sweet and flavored with a local herb, said to be an [[aphrodisiac]].<ref name=mexdes/>

The main export of the municipality is the production of salt. There are also limited mineral deposits, especially in the Capuano and Mezquite areas such as limestone and granite.<ref name=encmuc/>

The small town of Miraflores is known for its leatherwork, especially saddles and other gear for horseback riding. Jewelry and decorations made with shells are produced in San José del Cabo, Cabo San Lucas and Santa Rosa.<ref name=encmuc/>

Despite the development, 28.5% live in poverty and 5.6% live in extreme poverty; 18.1% live in substandard housing and 27.1% need food assistance.<ref name=sedesol>{{cite web |title= Los Cabos BCS |work= Informe Anual de la Situación de Pobreza y Rezado Social |publisher= SEDESOL |location= Mexico |url= http://www.sedesol.gob.mx/work/models/SEDESOL/Informes_pobreza/2014/Municipios/Baja_California_Sur/Baja_California_Sur_008.pdf |access-date= July 23, 2014 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20140226080230/http://www.sedesol.gob.mx/work/models/SEDESOL/Informes_pobreza/2014/Municipios/Baja_California_Sur/Baja_California_Sur_008.pdf |archive-date= February 26, 2014 |url-status= live }}</ref> There are 313 schools from the primary to high school level. 9.3 years of schooling for those over 15. There are two vocational schools and twelve centers for adult education. There are no schools specifically targeting an indigenous population.<ref name=sedesol/>

==Geography==
[[File:RanchoPuntaSanCristobal29.JPG|thumb|The desert and sea at Rancho Punta San Cristobal, Los Cabos]]
The municipality of Los Cabos is at the southern end of the Baja California peninsula, where desert meets the sea and the Gulf of Mexico meets the Pacific Ocean.<ref name=jnoel/><ref name=lcampuzano/> The area was originally underwater, evidenced by the many fossils of marine animals, which are up to 25 million years old.<ref name=ciudadgob>{{cite web |title= Los Cabos |publisher= Municipality of Los Cabos |location= Baja California Sur |url= http://www.loscabos.gob.mx/historia.html |access-date= July 23, 2014 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20140804092844/http://www.loscabos.gob.mx/historia.html |archive-date= August 4, 2014 |url-status= dead }}</ref> The basement rock underlying Los Cabos formed even earlier, approximately 115 million years ago.<ref name="fletcher2000">{{cite journal |last1=Fletcher |first1=John |last2=Kohn |first2=Barry |last3=Foster |first3=David |last4=Gleadow |first4=Andrew |title=Heterogeneous Neogene cooling and exhumation of the Los Cabos block, southern Baja California: Evidence from fission-track thermochronology |journal=Geology |date=2000 |volume=28 |issue=2 |pages=107–110 |doi=10.1130/0091-7613(2000)28<107:HNCAEO>2.0.CO;2 |bibcode=2000Geo....28..107F }}</ref>

The municipality has an average altitude of forty meters above sea level. There are three main terrain types, mountain terrain, semi flat areas and flat areas. The mountains consist of the Sierra de la Laguna and the Sierra de San Lázaro, both formed of volcanic rock, covering about fifteen percent of the total territory with peaks between 400 and 1000 meters. The semi flat areas are located between the coast and the mountain ranges, mostly of sedimentary rock and account for sixty percent of the territory. The flat areas are along the coast, beaches and alluvial plains, which account for twenty five percent of the territory.<ref name=encmuc/>

One of the main natural resources is the beaches. Major beaches include Los Frailes, Buena Vista, Agua Caliente, Cabo San Lucas, Puerto Chileno and Punta Colorada, often promoted together as the Costa de Oro (Golden Coast).<ref name=encmuc/> One very popular beach in Cabo San Lucas is Lover's Beach, which is surrounded by dramatic rock shapes.<ref name=jnoel/> The sea experiences lows of {{convert|72|–|73|F|C}} in winter, and highs of {{convert|77|–|84|F|C}} during the summer months.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.myweather2.com/City-Town/Mexico/San-Jose-Del-Cabo/climate-profile.aspx?month=12 |title=December Climate History for San Jose Del Cabo &#124; Local &#124; Mexico |publisher=Myweather2.com |access-date=2018-06-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180621015919/http://www.myweather2.com/City-Town/Mexico/San-Jose-Del-Cabo/climate-profile.aspx?month=12 |archive-date=2018-06-21 |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[File:BeachResortsSanJoseCabo11.JPG|thumb|Beach at San Jose del Cabo]]
As it is on the edge of the desert that covers most of the Baja California peninsula, it is one of the sunniest locations in the world, with an average of 320 days of sunshine per year.<ref name=ciudadgob/><ref name=diazmunoz/> The climate is characterized as hot and dry to the north of the town of San José del Cabo, hot and semi moist in San José and along the southern coast and temperate and dry in the highest elevations, because of cyclones that hit this area. Average annual temperature is 24C with the coolest month being January. There is a rainy season in the summer, with most rain in September.<ref name=ciudadgob/><ref name=encmuc/>  The rain and terrain make for a variety of micro climates, including areas with a climate similar to the Mediterranean.<ref name=ciudadgob/> Areas between 0 and 400 meters are desert and semi-desert, with many areas having deep sand deposits. Higher elevations get more water and can have pine forests.<ref name=encmuc/>

The main surface water of the municipality is the [[Río San José (Mexico)|Río San José]] or San José River, which runs north to south, mostly during the rainy season. Other streams run only during rains and include Santiago, Miraflores, Caduaño and Las Palmas.<ref name=encmuc/> There are subterranean deposits of water such as the Santiago and San José del Cabo, but the lack of water is the main impediment to human development in the area.<ref name=encmuc/>

[[File:EsteroSanJose18.JPG|thumb|A view of the San Jose Estuary]]
The Río San José stops just shy of the ocean, with a one km long sand bar creating an [[estuary]], the third largest in Mexico.<ref name=elestero>{{cite news |title= Rescatarán el estero de San José del Cabo, oasis protegido |newspaper= NOTIMEX  |location=Mexico City|date= May 11, 2008  }}</ref> This pooling of brackish water has created an oasis in the surrounding Sarcocaule desert. The Río San José flows largely underground for {{convert|40|mi|km}} from its origin in the [[Sierra de la Laguna]] (Laguna Mountains), although its Miramonte River tributary adds almost an additional {{convert|10|mi|km}}. Its tributaries flow down the eastern side of the sierra and include Santa Rosa, Santa Lázaro, San Miguel, San Ignacio (at La Palma), Caduaño, Miraflores and San Bernard.<ref>{{cite journal |title= Explorations in the Cape Region of Baja California |author=Gustav Eisen |journal=Journal of the American Geographical Society of New York |volume=29 |number=3 |year=1897 |pages=271–280 |jstor=197262 |doi=10.2307/197262 }}</ref>

The river used to flow above ground until the beginning of the 20th century due to anthropogenic causes.<ref>{{cite journal |title=Floristic Composition of the San José del Cabo Oasis, Baja California Sur, México |author1=José Luis León de la Luz |author2=Raymundo Domínguez Cadena |author3=Miguel Domínguez León |author4=José Juan Pérez Navarro |journal=SIDA, Contributions to Botany |volume=17 |number=3 |date=September 1997 |pages=599–614 |jstor=41967252 }}</ref> For more than 250 years the Río San José has furnished drinking and irrigation water for the town of San Jose del Cabo, beginning as a source of fresh water for Spanish [[galleon]]s traveling back from the [[Philippines]].<ref name="britannica">Encyclopædia Britannica, Second edition, 1778, Edinburgh, page 1580. Scan of page can be found at http://www.hyzercreek.com/britannica.htm {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141012232217/http://www.hyzercreek.com/britannica.htm |date=2014-10-12 }}</ref> Over the sand bar from the estuary is a bay referred to by early Spanish explorers, including Sebastian Vizcaino, as the Bahía de San Bernabé or Bay of San Bernabé, and now as the Bay of San José del Cabo.<ref>{{cite book|title=Peter Masten Dunne|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=LeZLxC3_iaUC&pg=PA4|publisher=University of California Press|pages=4–|id=GGKEY:FK94TH92Q55}}</ref>

The estuary is home to both native and migratory birds and aquatic species, 250 species of tropical birds alone.<ref name=diazmunoz/><ref name=elestero/>  Most of the migratory species use the area as a stopover on their way to southern Mexico, Central American and South America. It also acts as a nursery to many juvenile and larval stage species. It was declared a state environmental reserve, but pollution and excessive water extraction has caused it to degenerate, leading to a complaint by Greenpeace.<ref name=elestero/> One effort to improve the water situation is the creation of new water treatment plants in the 2000s.<ref name=inaugura>{{cite news |title= Inaugura planta de tratamiento de agua "La Sonoreña" Por Héctor Alvarez Fernández. Enviado San José del Cabo,... [Derived headline] |newspaper= NOTIMEX  |location=Mexico City|date= June 15, 2010  }}</ref>

Vegetation varies mostly by altitude and soil type and how much moisture the area receives. However, almost all species are those adapted to desert and semi-desert zones.<ref name=encmuc/> The highest elevations have pine forests.<ref name=ciudadgob/> Wildlife is varied and includes mammals such as badgers, skunks, coyotes, foxes, pumas and other wild cats, deer, raccoons, rabbits, bats and various rodents. Bird species include quail, doves, cardinals, woodpeckers, swallows and marine species such as pelicans and seagulls. There are over 850 species of aquatic animals off the coast such as marlin, [[sailfish]], [[swordfish]], tuna, dorado and whales. Many species and subspecies of both plants and animals are endemic only to Baja California.<ref name=encmuc/>

==History==
The indigenous [[Pericu]] names for San Jose del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas were Añiñi and Yenecami, respectively, with the current names given by the colonizing Spanish.<ref name=encmuc/>  The name of San José was given by Nicolás Tamaral in honor of José de la Fuente Peña y Castrejón, the Marquis of Villa Puente who sponsored the mission. The appendix of "de Los Cabos" is to distinguish it from San José de Comondú as well as its proximity to Cabo San Lucas. San Jose was also known as San Barnabé, as the nearby bay was named this. Pirate [[Thomas Cavendish]] called Cabo San Lucas "Safe Port" as he hid there from Spanish authorities. The seal for the municipality of Los Cabos (referring to the two cities) was approved by the state government in 1981.<ref name=encmuc/>

When the Spanish arrived the main indigenous group in the area was the Pericus, a hunter-gather culture with [[Stone Age]] tools. It is possible that these people arrived to the region with a more evolved culture which later simplified to adapt to the harsh conditions. Evidence of this includes the region's cave paintings as the peoples found by the Jesuits did not have an artistic tradition. The Pericu people and culture were distinct from other indigenous groups, first by being taller and second by being polygamous in a tribal organization. Their diet consisted of local seeds and fruits, as well as fish, reptiles, and small mammals. Men were in charge of large game hunting of deer.<ref name=ciudadgob/> 
[[File:TileMuralSanJosedelCaboChurch03.JPG|thumb|Tile mural on the San Jose del Cabo church depicting the martyrdom of Father Nicolas Tamaral]]
[[Hernán Cortés]] himself arrived here in 1535, and named the Gulf of California the Sea of Cortés (Mar de Cortés) the name still used for it in Spanish.<ref name=ciudadgob/><ref name=lcampuzano/> The harsh conditions impeded colonization by the Spanish, which did not begin in earnest until 1730, when Father José Echeverría and Father Nicolás Tamaral founded a mission in what is now San Jose del Cabo in 1730.<ref name=jnoel/><ref name=diazmunoz/> This date is considered the founding of the town, although a second ceremonial found took place in 1822, when it was declared a town of the Baja California territory.<ref name=encmuc/>

Diseases brought by Europeans devastated indigenous groups here,<ref name=ciudadgob/> and in 1768 more missionaries arrived.<ref name=encmuc/> While colonization was slow, the area was important as a way station for the [[Manila Galleon]] and other ships, which stopped here for fresh water, as well as fruits and vegetables.<ref name=ciudadgob/><ref name="britannica"/>

However, its remoteness also made it a place for pirates to hide.<ref name=lcampuzano/>  The first pirate in the area was [[Francis Drake]] in 1578, followed shortly after by Thomas Cavendish, both after the treasures from Spain's Asia trade. One major attack was that on the Santa Ana Galleon, whose looting caused the Spanish colonial government to explore and map the area around Cabo San Lucas at the very beginning of the 17th century. There were recommendations to establish a mission here, but this was rejected in favor of [[Loreto, Baja California Sur|Loreto]]. This left Cabo San Lucas as a strategic hideout for English pirates until the 18th century. In 1709, pirate [[Woodes Rogers]] attacked the [[Spanish ship Nuestra Señora de la Encarnación y Desengaño|Nuestra Señora de la Encarnación y Desengaño]]. Hiding nearly a month in Cabo San Lucas, he also mapped the area and wrote detailed descriptions. He was followed by [[George Shelvocke]] in 1721, who later published the oldest known drawings of the Pericues.<ref name=ciudadgob/>

After [[Mexican War of Independence|Independence]], the Baja Peninsula was part of the California province, but Cabo San Lucas was named head of a municipality. Its remoteness kept the area out of active participation of most of Mexico's 19th and early 20th century tumultuous history.<ref name=ciudadgob/>  One exception was the [[Mexican American War]]. Resistance to U.S. forces was organized in the small community of Santa Anita, near San José, headed by José Matías Moreno, Vicente Mejia and José Antonio Mijares, who was in charge of the marina at Cabo San Lucas. One of the main streets in the town is now named after Mijares, who died defending the town.<ref name=encmuc/>

The major political players during the Mexican Revolution were Manuel Gonzalez and Pedro Orozco, along with Félix Ortega. In 1915, Ildefonso Green Ceseña, head of forces loyal to [[Venustiano Carranza]], drove out those of [[Francisco Villa]] out of the southern part of the peninsula.<ref name=encmuc/>

Some development of the area began after the [[Mexican Revolution]] with a lighthouse at [[Cabo Falso]] as early as 1905, just southwest of Cabo San Lucas, in part to remind U.S. ships in the waters here that the territory remained Mexican. Today, it is known as the "Faro Viejo" (Old Lighthouse) and is a historic monument. In 1917, a U.S. company began tuna fishing operations here and had a floating processing plant. In 1927, the Compañía de Productos Marinas based its operations in Cabo San Lucas and helped develop the port to make it open to tourism later. In the 1920s, the first road connecting San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas was begun but not completely finished until 1970.<ref name=ciudadgob/>

For the most part, the area remained rural and undeveloped until the latter 20th century, when the federal agency Fonatur began to develop a tourism industry here.<ref name=diazmunoz/> Development began with Cabo San Lucas for vacationers, but then spread to San Jose del Cabo, but with a different direction with more [[art galleries]] and promotion of its traditional Mexican character.<ref name=jnoel/>

The current municipality of Los Cabos was created in 1981, separated from the municipality of La Paz, with the seat at San Jose del Cabo. The town had previously been a municipal seat of a municipality of the same name in 1917 but lost this in a political reorganization in 1972.<ref name=encmuc/>

Mayor Armida de Jesús Castro Guzmán quit [[Morena (political party)|MORENA]] on March 1, 2021, because of sexism by the party's state leader, Alberto Rentería Santana, and because he did not support her reelection.<ref>{{cite news |last1=León |first1=Raymundo |title=Alcaldesa de Los Cabos deja Morena; denuncia violencia política de género |url=https://www.jornada.com.mx/notas/2021/03/01/estados/alcaldesa-de-los-cabos-deja-morena-denuncia-violencia-politica-de-genero/ |access-date=March 1, 2021 |work=jornada.com.mx |publisher=La Jornada |date=March 1, 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

As of March 1, 2021, the municipality reported 7,796 recoveries, 209 active cases, and 404 deaths from the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Coronavirus – Baja California Sur Situation Report – Información de Coronavirus |url=https://coronavirus.bcs.gob.mx/english/ |website=coronavirus.bcs.gob.mx |publisher=Gobierno de Baja California Sur |access-date=March 1, 2021 |language=es}}</ref>{{Update inline|date=April 2024}}
