'''Jesús María''' ({{IPA|es|xeˈsus maˈɾia|lang}}; {{lit|[[Jesus]]-[[Mary, mother of Jesus|Mary]]}}) is a [[List of districts of Lima|district of Lima]], [[Peru]]. Located to the south of the city's [[Historic Centre of Lima|historic centre]], the area was originally populated by [[Hacienda|agricultural estates]], after which it began a period of urbanisation during the early 20th century, becoming a [[Urbanisation (Peru)|neighbourhood]] of Lima until its establishment as a district of its own in 1963.

Jesús María is an [[upper class]],<ref name=INEI-2020>INEI, [https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/publicaciones_digitales/Est/Lib1744/libro.pdf "Planos estratificados de Lima Metropolitana a nivel de manzanas 2020: Según ingreso per cápita del hogar"]</ref> high-density district and it usually ranks in the top districts with the best quality of life in Peru with a [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) of 0.8372 (2019), lower only than the districts of [[La Molina District|La Molina]] and [[Lince District|Lince]]. It is bordered by the districts of [[Pueblo Libre District, Lima|Pueblo Libre]] and [[Breña District]] on the west, [[Lima District|downtown Lima]] on the north and east, [[Lince District]] on the southeast, and [[San Isidro District, Lima|San Isidro]] and [[Magdalena del Mar]] on the south.

== Etymology ==
According to historian Fernando Flores Zúñiga, the name originates from the [[Church of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph|monastic community of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph]], made up of Capuchin nuns who arrived in [[Lima]] from [[Madrid]] and took up residence in the area starting in 1718, making the place their headquarters. Sometime in the 18th century, the Capuchin nuns of the community of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph took possession of the agricultural lands in the area, which acquired the name over time, although the reference to [[Saint Joseph]] was dropped.<ref name=Orrego-2010>{{Cite web |title=El distrito de Jesús María |url=http://blog.pucp.edu.pe/blog/juanluisorrego/2010/02/01/el-distrito-de-jesus-maria/ |last=Orrego Penagos |first=Juan Luis |date=2010-02-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210109170214/http://blog.pucp.edu.pe/blog/juanluisorrego/2010/02/01/el-distrito-de-jesus-maria/ |archive-date=2021-01-09 |website=[[Blog PUCP]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Exposición histórica: El distrito de Jesús María |date=2021-12-02 |url=https://bausate.edu.pe/exposicion-historica-el-distrito-de-jesus-maria/ |work=Universidad Jaime Bausate y Meza}}</ref>

== History ==
In 1440, the area was under the influence of both the [[Kuraka|chieftanship]]s of Guatca,<ref>{{Cite book |title=Lima prehispánica |last=Agurto Calvo |first=Santiago |publisher=[[Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos]] |year=1984 |url=http://sisbib.unmsm.edu.pe/Exposiciones/FundLima/Publicaciones/Lima_prehispanica.htm}}</ref> Maranga and the kingdom of Cuismancu, two of the oldest settlements of the [[Rímac River|Rímac]] Valley.<ref name=Municipalidad>{{Cite web |title=Historia del distrito |url=https://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/distrito |website=Municipalidad de Jesús María}}</ref> Both societies developed a complex network of ceremonial and administrative centres, shown by the number of [[huaca]]s in the area. Of these, one of the most prominent was the Huaca Universidad (also known as Huaca Santa Beatriz or Huaca Grande), located near the [[National University of San Marcos]]' [[Museum of Natural History, Lima|Museum of Natural History]] and bordered by Rebagliati, Cueto, Coronel Zegarra and Montero streets, as well as [[Arenales Avenue|Arenales]] and [[Salaverry Avenue|Salaverry]] avenues. It was gradually destroyed between 1935 and 1950 and replaced by [[Edgardo Rebagliati Martins National Hospital]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=Excavando 28 de Julio |last=Torres del Pino |first=Gonzalo |date=2015-04-19 |url=https://elcomercio.pe/lima/excavando-28-julio-gonzalo-torres-pino-353950-noticia |work=[[El Comercio (Peru)|El Comercio]] |author-link=Gonzalo Torres (actor)}}</ref>

Another important site was the Huaca Los Patricios, located between the present-day Estados Unidos and Cáceres streets in the district, in the area that now belongs to the Los Patricios passage and the zone adjacent to Parque 13 de Diciembre. This archaeological monument was destroyed around 1943 as a consequence of the area's urbanisation process. Similarly, the Huaca Palermo was located in the area of ​​the current intersection of Horacio Urteaga and General Santa Cruz avenues, the site of the district's former Palermo cinema. This huaca was also destroyed around 1943, making way for the urban infrastructure that no longer exists; the Aires de Palermo residential building now stands on its site. Finally, the Huaca Matalechuza (also known as Matalechucita) was located on the 19th block of Salaverry Avenue, near its intersection with the [[Avenida del Ejército]], in an area that corresponded to the former Matamula Forest (also called Matalechuza Forest). This site was linked to the Huatica valley and was destroyed in 1951 to make way for the current green area of ​​[[Heroes of Independence Park]].

=== Spanish period ===
In the aftermath of the [[Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire]], the area formed part of estates Matalechuzas and Matalechucitas, the latter of which was smaller in size, and the lands known as Jesús María, which together totaled approximately 240 [[hectare]]s.<ref name=Orrego-2010/> Under the [[Viceroyalty of Peru|Spanish]], the area was irrigated by the now-vanished Huatica River, and became generally known as Matalechuzas. The Huatica passed through what eventually became [[La Victoria District, Lima|La Victoria]], and its main subsidiary canal coursed down what is today [[Salaverry Avenue]] and, ultimately, reached the sea at Marbella in [[Magdalena del Mar]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=El río Huatica |date=2009-07-06 |url=http://suqanqa.lamula.pe/2009/07/06/el-rio-huatica |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120303082915/http://suqanqa.lamula.pe/2009/07/06/el-rio-huatica |archive-date=2012-03-03 |access-date=2012-05-05 |work=Suqanqa (La Mula)}}</ref>

One theory about the origin of Matalechuzas' name suggests that, from the 16th century onward, there was a gradual elimination of the owls that inhabited the area. According to Spanish chroniclers, this bird was highly valued as an oracle in pre-Hispanic times, and several huacas (sacred sites) dedicated to its worship were associated with it, which were also gradually destroyed. Over time, the territory was transformed into farmland, primarily dedicated to sugarcane and fruit orchards. Thus, Matalechuzas became the most important plantation in the area, owned by Martín de Ituráin around the 17th century.<ref name=Orrego-2010/>

Regarding Matalechucitas, this was a smaller orchard or farm known to have existed since the 17th century. The orchard had several owners until [[Manuel Ignacio de Vivanco]] finally took possession of it. Decades later, Reynaldo de Vivanco put it up for sale. On the other hand, the lands called Jesús María were a farm located next to the [[Santa Beatriz]] area.<ref name=Orrego-2010/>

=== Republican period ===
[[File:José Carlos Mariátegui durante un paseo de recreo en el bosque de Matamula o "matalechuzas".jpg|thumb|left|Matamula (or Matalechuzas) forest in 1929. Pictured are [[José Carlos Mariátegui]] and acquaintances.]]
Following independence, Matalechuzas came to be owned by Manuel Ignacio de Vivanco. Following his death, the estate was inherited by his son, Reynaldo de Vivanco. He would later die [[Battle of San Juan and Chorrillos|fighting at the Morro Solar]] during the [[War of the Pacific]]. Subsequently, the Olavegoya family acquired the land, and at the beginning of the 20th century, they began the urban expansion of the former estate.<ref name=Orrego-2010/>
[[File:Jesús María Lima Map 1927.jpg|thumb|right|Map of Jesús María (then part of Lima District) in 1927.]]
What would later become the district's area maintained an agricultural character until the beginning of the 1920s, its territory belonging to the districts of [[Lima District|Lima]], [[Pueblo Libre District, Lima|Pueblo Libre]], and [[Magdalena del Mar]]. Under the [[History of Peru (1919–1930)|presidency]] of [[Augusto B. Leguía]], the [[National Agrarian University|National School of Agriculture and Veterinary Science]] developed a project to urbanise the area, which proposed some thirty-two city blocks between what would later become Arenales and Francisco Javier Mariátegui avenues.<ref name=Orrego-2010/> In 1930, local residents organised a housing association, creating a committee supporting the creation of a district the following year, chaired by Jacob Alavedra and Enrique Mafuelo Cáceres, a prominent resident at the time.<ref name=Municipalidad/><ref name="jm-muni2012">{{Cite web |title=Tu distrito |url=http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/index.php/tu-distrito-index |date=2012-09-12 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130429152708/http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/index.php/tu-distrito-index |archive-date=2013-04-29 |access-date=2013-04-04 |website=Municipalidad de Jesús María}}</ref>

The inauguration of Salaverry Avenue in the 1940s granted the area more autonomy and, at the same time, the [[El Campo de Marte|Campo de Marte]]—a park that replaced [[Hipódromo de Santa Beatriz|Santa Beatriz Hippodrome]]—began to take shape as a green space and aesthetically pleasing feature for the emerging residential area.<ref name=Municipalidad/> Several important figures settled in the area, developing residential estates in different styles, including [[Moorish Revival architecture|Neo-Moorish]], [[Spanish Colonial Revival architecture|Spanish Revival]], and styles used in the United States. In addition to these buildings, gardens, [[Balconies of Lima|balconies]] and decorated windows were also built to accompany them.<ref name=Orrego-2010/> At that time, several members of the [[Japanese Peruvians|Japanese immigrant community in Peru]] settled in the area, bringing commerce and economic dynamism, as they were the ones who opened the first businesses in the area, thus giving the future district a commercial tone that it maintains to this day.<ref name=UAZ>{{Cite book |title=Between Menace and Model Citizen: Lima's Japanese-Peruvians, 1936-1963 |last=DuMontier |first=Benjamin John |publisher=[[University of Arizona]] |year=2018 |pages=90–95 |hdl=10150/628046 |language=es |url=http://hdl.handle.net/10150/628046}}</ref> The community would be targeted during [[World War II]] due to the [[anti-Japanese sentiment]] of the time, notably in the 1940 race riots that coincided with [[1940 Lima earthquake|an earthquake]].<ref name=UAZ/>

The 1950s saw the construction of government buildings at Salaverry avenue, including the [[Ministry of Labour and Promotion of Employment|Ministry of Labour]], [[Ministry of Health (Peru)|Ministry of Health]], and the [[Edgardo Rebagliati Martins National Hospital|Employee Hospital]]. The inauguration of San Felipe avenue would complete the local urban landscape, along with the houses and residences on Estados Unidos, Inca Rípac, and Sánchez Cerro streets.<ref name=Orrego-2010/>

=== History as a district ===
The [[Peruvian Congress]] approved Law No. 1,4763 of December 27, 1955,<ref name="L14763">[https://www.deperu.com/legislacion/ley-14763-pdf.html ''Ley 14763. Creando el Distrito de Jesús María, en la Provincia de Lima'' (13 de diciembre de 1963)]. (Retrieved 10 August 2018)</ref> which elevated Jesús María to a district, but it was not put into effect.<ref name=Municipalidad/> It was ratified by President [[Fernando Belaúnde Terry]] on December 17, 1963, formally creating the district.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Plan de desarrollo concertado, ajustado al 2015. Resumén ejécutivo |publisher=Municipalidad de Jesús María |year=2010 |url=http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/pdf/presupuesto_participativo/Presupuesto_Participativo_2010/RESUMEN_EJECUTIVO_PDC_-_ALCALDIA.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303214901/http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/pdf/presupuesto_participativo/Presupuesto_Participativo_2010/RESUMEN_EJECUTIVO_PDC_-_ALCALDIA.pdf |archive-date=2016-03-03}}</ref> Its first mayor, José Benavides Muñoz, was elected in November 1966<ref name="jm-muni2012" />

In the 1960s and 1970s, the newly created district acquired a more heterogeneous profile with the emergence of more popular and small middle-class areas.<ref name=Municipalidad/> The [[Hipódromo de San Felipe|Hippodrome of San Felipe]], which succeeded that of Santa Beatriz, was demolished to make way for a [[Residencial San Felipe|residential complex of the same name]]. It was commissioned by President Fernando Belaúnde and designed by a team headed in part by architects [[Henri Ciriani|Enrique Ciriani]], Jorge Páez, Jorge Bernuy, and Nikita Smirnoff Bracamonte, designed to combine a high residential density with ample green space, and shopping, banking, and [[San Felipe Cooperative School|schooling]].<ref>Interview with architect Enrique Ciriani, [https://arquitecturaperuana.blogspot.com/2009/06/entrevista-enrique-ciriani-peru-21.html "Entrevista a Enrique Ciriani sobre la Residencial San Felipe. Perú 21."] in [http://arquitecturaperuana.blogspot.com Bitacora|arquitectura peruana] blog, 17 June 2009. (Entry retrieved 22 May 2012)</ref><ref>[https://divagarquitectura.blogspot.com/2012/05/conjunto-residencial-san-felipe.html Conjunto Residencial San Felipe] in [http://divagarquitectura.blogspot.com Divagaciones y Arquitectura blog], 16 May 2012 (Entry retrieved 22 May 2012)</ref> and is widely considered the cornerstone of Jesús María's middle-class identity.<ref name="Pereyra">Pereyra, Omar. ''Contemporary Middle Class in Latin America: A study of San Felipe''. Lexington Books, 2015. {{ISBN|978-0-7391-9106-4}}.</ref><ref>Sinesio López Jímenez, "El Zorro de Abajo. Residencial San Felipe" in ''La República'', 14 March 2008. Available [http://www.larepublica.pe/14-03-2008/el-zorro-de-abajo-residencial-san-felipe online].</ref><ref>Rafo León, "Megabarbaridad en Jesús María" in ''SOMOS'' magazine, March 2008.</ref>

Today, Jesús María is an upper class, highly residential district, home to 75,359 people. The district usually ranks in the top four districts with the best quality of life in Lima.<ref name="expat">[http://www.expatperu.com/jesus-maria.html "The District of Jesus Maria"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140708011050/http://www.expatperu.com/jesus-maria.html |date=2014-07-08 }}, at [http://www.expatperu.com Expat Peru] website. (Entry accessed 6 June 2014)</ref> The district's central location and proximity to [[San Isidro District, Lima|San Isidro]] and [[Miraflores District, Lima|Miraflores]], have also contributed to it becoming an increasingly popular residential option.<ref name="expat" /> Jesús María is experiencing a housing and construction boom, and many ''casonas'' (older large, luxury homes) are being replaced with high-rise apartments and condominiums.<ref>''Urbania'', [https://urbania.pe/blog/noticia/jesus-maria-continua-actividad-inmobiliaria/ "Jesús María continuará como zona de alta actividad inmobiliaria"] (Published 16 January 2015; retrieved 29 August 2018)</ref><ref>América TV, [https://www.americatv.com.pe/noticias/actualidad/jesus-maria-encabeza-oferta-inmobiliaria-lima-moderna-n316723 "Jesús María lidera la oferta inmobiliaria de Lima moderna"] (Published 4 April 2018; Retrieved 29 August 2018)</ref> The number of high-rise buildings being constructed has led to concerns by some residents of the district, who have voiced concerns regarding [[zoning]] and material damages.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Jesús María: vecinos de Ricardo Tizón y Bueno se oponen a construcción de edificio de 17 pisos por ruidos molestos y fisuras a otras viviendas |last=Ramírez Mendoza |first=Sebastián |date=2024-02-06 |url=https://elcomercio.pe/lima/jesus-maria-vecinos-de-ricardo-tizon-y-bueno-se-oponen-a-construccion-de-edificio-de-17-pisos-por-ruidos-molestos-y-fisuras-a-otras-viviendas-noticia/ |work=[[El Comercio (Peru)|El Comercio]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Jesús María: Denuncian que convirtieron un jirón en avenida para construir un edificio de 17 pisos |last=Giraldo |first=Clara |date=2023-06-14 |url=https://www.infobae.com/peru/2023/06/14/jesus-maria-vecinos-denuncian-que-convirtieron-un-jiron-en-avenida-para-construir-un-edificio-de-17-pisos/ |work=[[Infobae]]}}</ref> Some houses have been preserved as businesses or government installations, such as the City Hall.<ref name=Casonas/>

== Demographics ==
Jesús María is an [[upper class]],<ref name=INEI-2020/> high-density district and it usually ranks in the top districts with the best quality of life in Peru with a [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) of 0.8372 (2019), lower only than the districts of [[La Molina District|La Molina]] and [[Lince District|Lince]].

=== Notable people ===
* [[José Jerí]] (born 1986), 65th President of Peru

== Culture ==
Jesús María is home to several educational and cultural centres. Important avenues which demarcate the boundaries of the district are [[Avenida Brasil (Lima)|Brasil]], [[Avenida 28 de Julio|28 de Julio]]. [[Arenales Avenue|Arenales]], [[Salaverry Avenue|General Salaverry]], and Faustino Sánchez Carrión (commonly referred to by its old name, Pershing). Main avenues within the district are San Felipe, Gregorio Escobedo, and Francisco Javier Mariàtegui.<ref name="mapa">Municipalidad de Jesús María, [http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/images/planos/mapa_jesus_maria.pdf ''Mapa de Jesús María''] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170615051210/http://www.munijesusmaria.gob.pe/images/planos/mapa_jesus_maria.pdf |date=2017-06-15 }} (Retrieved 29 August 2018).</ref>

|Inaugurated on November 30, 1967. It is linked to the history of stained glass art in Lima's churches, a tradition championed by the Austrian artist [[Adolfo Winternitz]]. Other important medical centers within the district are the private Clínica San Felipe, and the hospitals of the National Police, the [[Central Military Hospital (Peru)|Army]] and the Air Force.<ref name="mapa" /> The [[University of the Pacific (Peru)|Universidad del Pacífico]], one of the most prestigious universities in economy, is located at Salaverry avenue.

Several renowned research institutions also have their offices in Jesus Maria, including the [[Institute of Peruvian Studies|Instituto de Estudios Peruanos]] (IEP) and the Centro Peruano de Estudios Sociales (CEPES). The Derrama Magisterial—the public school teachers' social security administration—and the [[Lima Chamber of Commerce]] are also included in the district.<ref name="mapa" /> Jesus Maria is also host to the city's [[Lima International Book Fair|yearly international Book Fair]].
