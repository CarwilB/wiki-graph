'''Huancarqui''' ({{IPA|es|waŋˈkaɾki|lang}}; [[Quechua language|Quechua]]: ''Wankarki'') is a [[Districts of Peru|district]] of [[Castilla province|Castilla]], a [[Provinces of Peru|province]] of the [[department of Arequipa]], Peru.<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=2008-04-23 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> Its capital is the [[Populated centres of Peru|populated centre]] of the same name.

== Etymology ==
The district's name, also written "Guancarqui" up until 1800, come from the [[Quecha language|Quechua]] words ''Huancar'', meaning "drum", and ''Canque'', meaning "you".<ref name=Caceres>{{Cite book |title=Huancarqui: ¡“Tierra de Brujas”! |last=Cáceres Zúñiga |first=Marlene |publisher= |year= |language=es}}</ref>

== History ==
Huancarqui is one of the oldest settlements in the province of Castilla, having been populated since [[pre-Inca cultures|pre-Inca]] times. It is presumed that the first [[Spanish Empire|Spaniards]] who arrived founded the town on Inca cemeteries. According to Spanish chronicler [[Inca Garcilaso de la Vega|Garcilaso de la Vega]], the [[Inca Empire|Incas]] that conquered the region under [[Cápac Yupanqui]] in 1000 AD made it into their local headquarters in order to be able to conquer local rival groups. The arrival of these Incas allowed for a unique development of a mixed culture descended from the [[Wari culture|Wari]], [[Tiwanaku]] and Inca cultures, as well as the [[Spanish culture]] that would arrive later.<ref name=Caceres/>

At the time the arrival of the Spanish in 1549, the area was inhabited by the Collaguas and Cabanas, two Kunti ethnic groups, as recorded by Martín López de Carvajal, a [[Conquistador]] originating from [[Extremadura, Spain|Extremadura]], as well as one of the founders of [[Arequipa]], who arrived to the area under direct orders of the Viceroy of Peru.<ref name=Caceres/> On April 19, 1813, Huancarqui's town hall was established. It was led by an ''[[Alcalde ordinario]]'', who oversaw five other people. The mayor was accompanied by a ''procurador'' and two ''regidores''.<ref name=Caceres/>

On May 3, 1955, a government law elevated Huancarqui to a [[capital (political)|capital town]] of the homonymous new district.<ref name=Caceres/> Today the town has a population of approximately 1700 people. Despite it being less developed than neighboring [[Aplao]] and Corire, it is nonetheless a well-off touristic spot among visitors.

Throughout its history, Huancarqui has had the following priests: Agustín Laso de la Vega y Soto, Pedro Velasco, Jacinto Llerena y Dávila, Manuel Dávila y Calderón, Leandro Dávila y Dávila. Pedro Chacón de Luna y Dávila, Antonio Nates y Martínez del Pino, Mariano Adrián López y Nates, Manuel Mariano Rodríguez de Olmedo y Martínez del Pino, and Alejo Rodríguez de Olmedo y Martínez del Pino, among others.<ref name=Caceres/>

== Geography ==
Huancarqui is located in the Majes Valley, where the [[Colca River]] flows through. It is located below 1 000 meters above sea level and is made up of the districts of [[Aplao]], Uraca and Huancarqui. The terrain is mostly plain and fertile, in comparison to the surrounding deserts.

The climate in the region of ''Castilla Baja'' is very hot due to its narrowness and depth, in relation to the plain where the waters have undermined its basin. Average annual temperatures range between 15º and 24&nbsp;°C. The winter rains on the coast and summer in the mountains. reach only the ends of the valley, which does not receive rain other than very scarce drizzles.

== Government ==
Huancarqui is under the jurisdiction of its own [[Municipalities of Peru|district municipality]]. The [[Catholic Church in Peru]] administers the district as part of the [[Territorial Prelature of Chuquibamba]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Jurisdicciones Eclesiásticas |url=http://www.iglesiacatolica.org.pe/cep/map_je_nondetail.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110722174033/http://www.iglesiacatolica.org.pe/cep/map_je_nondetail.htm |archive-date=2011-07-22 |website=[[Conferencia Episcopal Peruana]]}}</ref>

== Culture ==
Huancarqui is an agricultural district, known locally for its archeologic heritage, such as the many [[petroglyph]]s found, as well as a nearby dinosaur-themed park. The valley as a whole is known for its [[pisco]] production, with its many [[Winery|wineries]] being part of a gastronomic route.<ref>{{Cite news |title=El pisco de Arequipa, protagonista de ruta entre desierto y volcanes |date=2017-10-29 |url=https://rpp.pe/lima/actualidad/cronica-el-pisco-de-arequipa-protagonista-de-ruta-entre-desierto-y-volcanes-noticia-1085468?ref=rpp |work=[[RPP Noticias]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Reconocen a bodegas arequipeñas por su aporte al desarrollo del sector vitivinícola |date=2025-10-10 |url=https://www.gob.pe/institucion/regionarequipa/noticias/1262070-reconocen-a-bodegas-arequipenas-por-su-aporte-al-desarrollo-del-sector-vitivinicola |work=[[Gob.pe]]}}</ref> Fishing is also a popular activity in the district. Touristic attractions include outdoor sports, such as [[rafting]] or [[hiking]], or the local gastronomy, with many of its restaurants specializing in traditional [[Peruvian food]].

=== Religion ===
Religion is important among the local population, with [[Catholicism]] being held in high esteem by the local population. Local festivities, such as the ''Virgen de Chapi'' or [[Our Lady of Mount Carmel]] are important in the area. During [[Holy Week]], local residents tend to perform traditional activities such as the [[Via Crucis]] in the surrounding mountains, as well as traditional [[Holy mass|Mass]] services. [[Folklore|Folk legends]] are also popular with locals, who claim certain areas are haunted.

== Transport ==
Owing to its location in a valley, transport is limited to the roads linking the towns together along the valley. The town is accessed through [[Aplao District]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Bodega Vieja Herencia |url=https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/fichaInventario/index.aspx?cod_Ficha=8851 |last=Barrios Cacya |first=Claudia Isabel |date=2025-08-21 |website=[[Ministry of Foreign Trade and Tourism (Peru)|Recursos Turísticos]]}}</ref> Both public and private transport usually service these roads.
