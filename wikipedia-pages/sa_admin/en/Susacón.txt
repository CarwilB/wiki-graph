'''Susacón''' is a town and municipality in the [[Colombia]]n [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]], part of the subregion of the [[Northern Boyacá Province]]. Susacón borders to the north [[Soatá]], in the east [[Boavita]], [[La Uvita]] and [[Jericó, Boyacá|Jericó]], in the south [[Sativanorte]] and in the west the [[Santander Department|Santander]] municipality of [[Onzaga]].<ref name=website>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.susacon-boyaca.gov.co/index.shtml Official website of Susacón] - accessed 03-05-2016</ref>

== History ==
When the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish]] [[conquistador]]s led by [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] entered the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], the central highlands of the Colombian [[Andes]], they found there the [[Muisca Confederation]], the former country, home of the [[Muisca people|Muisca]].

Susacón was ruled by a ''[[cacique]]'' who was loyal to the ''cacique'' of [[Soatá]]. The ''cacique'' of Susacón took part in the bloody [[Battle of Bonza]] against the Spanish troops of De Quesada.<ref name=website/>

Although the Spanish missionaries already arrived in 1556, the village was officially founded on December 18, 1809.<ref name=website/>

Susacón means in [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]]: "ally of the ''cacique'' Susa".<ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.excelsio.net/2011/11/sabe-el-origen-del-nombre-de-su.html Etymology Susacón] - accessed 03-05-2016</ref>

== Economy ==
Main economical activities of Susacón are [[agriculture]] ([[potato]]es, [[maize]], [[barley]], [[sugar cane]], [[wheat]] and [[banana]]s), [[livestock]] farming, fishing and crafts.<ref name=website/>
