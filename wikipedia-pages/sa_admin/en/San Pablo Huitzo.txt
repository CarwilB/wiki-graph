'''San Pablo Huitzo''' (commonly referred to as '''Huitzo''') is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 63.80&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.
It is part of the [[Etla District]] in the [[Valles Centrales de Oaxaca|Valles Centrales region]]. As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 5,242.<ref name =Huitzo>{{cite web|title=San Pablo Huitzo|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20294a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=June 12, 2009|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://huitzo.webcindario.com/ |title=Home |website=huitzo.webcindario.com}}</ref>

==History==
The area comprising Huitzo was inhabited as early as 1200 [[BCE]]. By 1400 AD a [[Zapotec peoples|Zapotec]] village existed that was known by the name of ''Huijazoo'', a word that means ''Warrior Watchtower'' in the  [[Zapotec language]].<ref name=Huitzo/>  In 1522 Spanish troops arrived and founded the current city, giving it the name ''Gueixolotitlan''.  However, by 1700 the name ''Huitzo'' had been adopted by the populace.  When the first [[Roman Catholic]] monks arrived to [[evangelize]] the local population, they selected [[St. Paul the Apostle]] ''(San Pablo, in Spanish)'' as the [[patron saint]] of the village and erected a church by the same name. The church is notable for a large number of 16th-century and other colonial-era ''santos'', statues of the saints.<ref>[http://www.aug.edu/augusta/santos/huitzo/index.html Santos in Oaxaca's Ancient Churches: San Pablo Huitzo] Retrieved 2012-04-13</ref>  It was through the influence of the Catholic Church that the town arrived at its present name San Pablo Huitzo.<ref name=Huitzo/>

==Modern day==
[[File:Tomb in San Pablo Huitzo, Oaxaca.jpg|left|thumb|Post-classic era tomb in Huitzo]]
Today Huitzo is a farming community noted for growing vegetables and flowers, with almost half of its population employed by farms or other agriculture-related ventures.<ref name=Huitzo/>  Archaeological sites in the area consist of several tombs, walls, and similar Zapotec structures. Most of the sites are overgrown with vegetation, however they do attract some tourists and [[archaeologists]].<ref name=guide>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=fJ8AAqwPTRoC&q=%22Huitzo%22&pg=PA205|title=An archaeological guide to central and southern Mexico|publisher=University of Oklahoma Press|author1=Kelly, Joyce |author2=Kelly, Jerry |accessdate=2010-11-11|isbn=0-8061-3349-X|year=2001}}</ref>

== Archaeology ==
In 2025, archaeologists discovered a 1,400-year-old Zapotec tomb in San Pablo Huitzo dating to approximately A.D. 600[span_4](end_span). [span_5](start_span)The exceptionally well-preserved tomb features multicolored murals, calendrical carvings, and a large entrance sculpture of an owl whose beak opens to reveal the painted face of a Zapotec lord[span_5](end_span). [span_6](start_span)The discovery was officially announced in January 2026[span_6](end_span).<ref>{{Cite web |title=1,400-year-old Zapotec tomb discovered in Mexico features enormous owl sculpture symbolizing death |url=https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/1-400-year-old-zapotec-tomb-discovered-in-mexico-features-enormous-owl-sculpture-symbolizing-death |website=Live Science |date=2026-01-26 |access-date=2026-06-25}}</ref>
