'''Sama District''' is one of eleven [[Districts of Peru|districts]] that make up [[Tacna Province]], located in the [[department of Tacna]], under the administration of the [[Regional Government of Tacna]], in southern [[Peru]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=2008-04-23 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> In 2017, it had a population of 3,106 inhabitants and a population density of 2.89 per [[Square kilometre|km²]]. It covers a total area of 1,116&nbsp;km².<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/publicaciones_digitales/Est/Lib1564/ |title=Resultados Definitivos de los Censos Nacionales 2017 - Tacna |date=2017-10-22 |website=[[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]}}</ref>

From the hierarchical point of view of the [[Catholic Church]], it is part of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Tacna and Moquegua|Diocese of Tacna and Moquegua]] which, in turn, belongs to the [[Archdiocese of Arequipa]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.iglesiacatolica.org.pe/cep/map_je_nondetail.htm |title=Jurisdicciones eclesiáticas |website=[[Episcopal Conference of Peru]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110722174033/http://www.iglesiacatolica.org.pe/cep/map_je_nondetail.htm |archive-date=2011-07-22}}</ref>

==Etymology==
The name of the valley still generates some uncertainty. Peruvian historian {{ill|Luis Cavagnaro Orellana|es}} indicates that the word ''Sama'' is of Hispanic origin and attributes it to the [[conquistador]]s who returned from [[Captaincy General of Chile|Chile]] after accompanying [[Diego de Almagro|Almagro]]. In December 1536, a group of them, at the proposal of Diego de Rondón, applied the name in honor of an [[Asturias|Asturian]] town in the parish of [[Sama, Asturias|Sama]], head of the ''ayuntamiento'' of [[Langreo]] in the general district of [[Laviana]], in the old Province of Oviedo.

However, pre-Hispanic linguistic evidence reveals coincidences in that the valley was called ''Samaraña'' in [[Aymara language|Aymara]] and ''Samay'' in [[Quechua languages|Quechua]]. Among [[archaeologist]]s and [[ethnohistorian]]s there is consensus to establish that the term "Sama" has pre-Hispanic origins.
It derives its meaning from the concept of land of rest or rested land, alluding to the first farmers who cleared the land after cycles of rest and fertilization.<ref name=Guevara>{{Cite book |title=Afro Sama, Memorias y Testimonios |last=Guevara |publisher=Dirección Desconcentrada de Cultura de Tacna |year=2019 |language=es}}</ref>

==History==
The origins of the district are purely agricultural, with the ''Camanchacos'' being its first inhabitants, their work was concentrated on planting corn and potatoes, in addition to fishing. When the Incas arrived in [[Tacna|Takana]], groups of Quechua ''mitimaes'' remained in Sama, who entered into dispute with the original [[Aymaras]]. Over time the mitimaes specialized and along with fishing and guano extraction; They grew chili for the [[Inca]] on the Quillona farm. The Camino Real passed through the same district. The Incas also built {{ill|Sama La Antigua|es}} to serve as their main administrative centre in the valley.

Before the conquest, the valley had a small population. In 1536, Almagro left Juan de la Flor in the valley, who took possession of what is currently Sama Grande, and Fernando Albarracín Pizarro took possession of Sama Inclán. During the [[War of the Pacific]], the town was [[Occupation of Peru|occupied]] by the [[Chilean Army]] and administered as the 7th subdelegation of [[Tacna Department (Chile)|Tacna Department]], according to the limits assigned by the Chilean decrees of November 9, 1885 and May 10, 1886.<ref name=INEChile>{{Cite book |title=Chile. Historia de la división político-administrativa 1810-2000 |last=Zamudio |first=Orlando |publisher=Instituto Nacional de Estadísticas |year=2001 |location=Santiago, Chile |language=es}}</ref>

On November 28, 1920, according to Regional Law No. 14, promulgated by Peruvian President [[Augusto B. Leguía]], Sama was elevated to a district, with its first mayor being Mr. Jesús R. Chavera. This law is ratified by Regional Law No. 498.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://munidesama.gob.pe/portal/index.php/es/sama/resena-historica.html |title=Reseña Histórica |website=Municipalidad Distrital de Sama |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200110214422/http://munidesama.gob.pe/portal/index.php/es/sama/resena-historica.html |archive-date=2020-01-10}}</ref>

===Chilean administration===
Sama was administered as an [[Communes of Chile|autonomous commune]] of [[Chile]] starting on February 1, 1928, through Decree No. 8583 of January 28, which organised its territory.<ref name=INEChile/> Alongside the communes of [[Palca, Chile|Palca]]—also created by the same decree—and [[Tacna, Chile|Tacna]], it formed the municipal group of the same name between 1928 and 1929, meaning that, unlike most communes, neither town had a proper local government.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/m/norma_plana?org=&idNorma=19463 |title=DTO 8583 28-ENE-1928 |website=[[Library of the National Congress of Chile|Biblioteca Nacional del Congreso de Chile]]}}</ref>

The Chilean commune of Sama ceased its existence alongside Palca and Tacna as of August 28, 1929, when the territory of the Tacna was [[Handover of Tacna|returned to Peru]]<ref>{{Cite news |title=A 80 años de la restitución de Tacna |url=http://www.estrellaiquique.cl/prontus4_nots/site/artic/20090828/pags/20090828001002.html |date=2009-08-28 |work=La Estrella de Iquique |archive-date=2019-07-08 |access-date=2024-03-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190708033639/http://www.estrellaiquique.cl/prontus4_nots/site/artic/20090828/pags/20090828001002.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> in a ceremony held at the home of the prefect Federico Fernandini, in which an agreement was signed between the interim mayor of the Chilean province of Tacna, Gonzalo Robles, and a delegation from Peru headed by Foreign Minister [[Pedro José Rada y Gamio]]. At 4 p.m. that day, the [[Department of Tacna|Peruvian municipality of Tacna]] began its functions.<ref name=Guevara/><ref>{{Cite news|title=Tacna Province Now Back in Peru's Hands|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Nn5GAAAAIBAJ&dq=%22Tacna+Province%22+Chile&pg=PA7&article_id=6559,6855780|date=Aug 31, 1929|work=Schenectady Gazette}}</ref>

===Recent history===
In 2000, Sama had a population of 2,406 inhabitants.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www1.inei.gob.pe/biblioineipub/bancopub/Est/LIb0004/Tacna.htm |title=PERU: Estimaciones de Población por Departamentos Provincias y Distritos 1995 - 2000 |website=[[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100528030228/http://www1.inei.gob.pe/biblioineipub/bancopub/Est/LIb0004/Tacna.htm |archive-date=2010-05-28}}</ref>

It currently houses one of the most prominent valleys in the department, the [[Sama River (Peru)|Sama Valley]]. An important sugar cane and cotton production area, it focuses its activities on the cultivation of [[olive tree]]s, [[Choclo|corn]], [[chili pepper]]s, [[quinoa]], among others. It is also characterized by the breeding of [[sheep]], [[cattle]], [[goat]]s, and [[pig]]s and is currently considered the "southern cradle of the breeding of the [[Peruvian Paso]] horse."<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://munidesama.gob.pe/portal/index.php/es/sama/caballo-de-paso-sameno.html |title=Caballo de Paso Sameño |website=Municipalidad Distrital de Sama |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200110214424/http://munidesama.gob.pe/portal/index.php/es/sama/caballo-de-paso-sameno.html |archive-date=2020-01-10}}</ref>
