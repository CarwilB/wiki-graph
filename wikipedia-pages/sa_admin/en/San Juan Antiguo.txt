'''San Juan Antiguo''' is one of the 18 ''[[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrios]]'' of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico]]. San Juan Antiguo is further divided into 7 [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|subbarrios]]. San Juan Antiguo is located entirely within the [[San Juan Islet|Isleta de San Juan]], a small island off the coast of Puerto Rico where the original [[Old San Juan|colonial settlement of San Juan]] was founded. It is connected to the main island of Puerto Rico by two bridges and a causeway.

== Name ==
The name of the barrio dates to the original foundation of the city of San Juan, originally named '''Puerto Rico de San Juan Bautista''' at the moment of its foundation in 1521, [[John the Baptist|San Juan Bautista]] being the name given to the island of Puerto Rico by the earliest Spanish settlers. By the 18th century, the ambiguous and colloquial use of ''San Juan Bautista'' and ''Puerto Rico'' for both the city and the island in time led to the unofficial renaming of the island and the city as Puerto Rico and San Juan, respectively.<ref name="HistoryS.J.">{{cite web |year=2006 |title=Historia de San Juan de Puerto Rico |url=http://www.vivesanjuan.com/historia.htm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070501112503/http://www.vivesanjuan.com/historia.htm |archive-date=May 1, 2007 |access-date=2007-05-02 |publisher=Vive San Juan |language=es}}</ref> As the city itself further expanded beyond the islet into the towns of [[Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Santurce]] (merged into San Juan by the 19th century) and [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]] (merged in the 20th century),<ref name="Oxford2">{{citation |title=Puerto Rico |work=[[Oxford Art Online]]}}. Retrieved May 24, 2017</ref><ref name="FPH3">{{cite web |title=Encyclopedia of Puerto Rico |url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org |access-date=May 24, 2017 |publisher=[[Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades]] |language=en, es |oclc=234072526 |location=San Juan}}</ref> the barrio was officially named ''Antiguo'' to distinguish it from the rest of the modern city.<ref name="FPH">{{cite web |title=Encyclopedia of Puerto Rico |url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org |access-date=May 24, 2017 |publisher=[[Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades]] |language=en, es |oclc=234072526 |location=San Juan}}</ref> This naming convention of calling an urban core-''barrio'' ''Pueblo Antiguo'' or ''Pueblo Viejo'' is also seen in other municipalities' ''barrios'' such as [[Pueblo Viejo, Guaynabo, Puerto Rico|Pueblo Viejo]] in [[Guaynabo, Puerto Rico|Guaynabo]],<ref>"[[Guaynabo]] -- Encyclopædia Britannica" (with history of Puerto Rico),''[[Encyclopædia Britannica]]'', 2006, ''Britannica.com'' webpage:[http://www.britannica.com/eb/article-9038345/Guaynabo EB-Guaynabo-Puerto-Rico]:names: Caparra, the first Spanish settlement in Puerto Rico (1508).</ref> the location of the first colonial settlement in both that municipality and the island of Puerto Rico.

The name '''''San Juan Antiguo''''' (Spanish for '''Old San Juan''') refers to the geographical and political subdivision of the [[San Juan, Puerto Rico#Government|municipality of San Juan]], not to be confused with '''[[Old San Juan]]''' (''Viejo San Juan''), the [[historic district]] and tourist attraction located within the barrio itself. Historically, the names ''San Juan Antiguo'' and ''Viejo San Juan'' were often used interchangeably due to the synonymous nature of the adjectives ''antiguo'' and ''viejo'' (meaning 'old' or 'ancient'). However, the name San Juan Antiguo is almost never used today to refer to the historic district itself. The name San Juan by itself is often also used to refer to the ''barrio'' San Juan Antiguo, with many Puerto Ricans today still referring to the rest of the modern city outside of the ''isleta'' as Río Piedras.<ref name="FPH3" /><ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Una breve mirada a la historia de Río Piedras |url=https://cauce.uprrp.edu/una-breve-mirada-a-la-historia-de-rio-piedras/ |access-date=2021-12-11 |language=es}}</ref>

==History==
{{Main|Timeline of San Juan, Puerto Rico}}
[[File:San Juan - City Hall & Principal Square.jpg|left|thumb|[[San Juan City Hall]], the municipal headquarters since at least 1789.]]
The first documented inhabitants of the area now known as San Juan Antiguo were the indigenous [[Taíno|Taino]], with evidence of small fishing villages being found in [[Puerta de Tierra, San Juan|Puerta de Tierra]] ''subbarrio'' during the 19th and 20th centuries.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Historia de Puerta de Tierra (San Juan) |url=http://www.puertadetierra.info/indicehist.asp |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090601164325/http://www.puertadetierra.info/indicehist.asp |archive-date=June 1, 2009 |access-date=2022-02-12 |website=www.puertadetierra.info}}</ref> The first European settlement of the barrio began on August 8, 1511, when the settlement of [[Caparra Archaeological Site|Caparra]] was abandoned in favor of a new town planned across [[San Juan Bay|the bay]] in the southwestern corner of the [[San Juan Islet|Isleta de San Juan]]. The new settlement was officially founded on August 15, 1521. By the end of the 16th century, the town was the fully-functional administrative center for the Spanish colonization of the rest of Puerto Rico and beyond.<ref name=":1">{{cite web |year=2000 |title=San Juan National Historic Site |url=http://www.nps.gov/archive/saju/faq.html#3 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120313142618/http://www.nps.gov/pwr/404.htm#3 |archive-date=March 13, 2012 |access-date=2007-05-29 |publisher=National Park Service}}</ref> The establishment of a [[Walls of Old San Juan|well-defended port]] in the San Juan Bay also established the settlement as a key point for the Spanish military, trade and expansion into the New World.<ref name="Caparra">{{cite web |title=Historic places in Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands |url=http://www.cr.nps.gov/nR/travel/prvi/pr27.htm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070513201507/http://www.cr.nps.gov/nR/travel/prvi/pr27.htm |archive-date=May 13, 2007 |access-date=2007-05-02 |publisher=National Park Service}}</ref>[[File:Page84-1024px-Crónica de la guerra hispano-americana en Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|350px|Official Spanish map of San Juan Antiguo during the Spanish–American War]]Across its history from its foundation and by the 17th century onwards, the Spanish-founded city of '''Puerto Rico de San Juan Bautista''' was coextensive with the modern barrio of San Juan Antiguo. This continued to be the case until the annexation of the town of [[Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico#History|San Mateo de Cangrejos]] (now known as the barrio of [[Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Santurce]]) into the city in 1863.<ref name="FPH2">{{cite web |title=Encyclopedia of Puerto Rico |url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org |access-date=May 24, 2017 |publisher=[[Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades]] |language=en, es |oclc=234072526 |location=San Juan}}</ref> The district corresponding to San Juan Antiguo was often mentioned in documents of the time as ''Puerto Rico de San Juan'', ''Puerto Rico de Puerto Rico'', ''San Juan de Puerto Rico'' or ''Plaza de San Juan Bautista'' when distinguishing it from the wider "town of San Juan",<ref>{{cite web |title=Nueva España documents and transcripts, ca. 1700-ca. 1799 |url=http://archives.nypl.org/mss/2272 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160102071102/http://archives.nypl.org/mss/2272 |archive-date=January 2, 2016 |access-date=November 21, 2015 |work=New York Public Library}}</ref> which was now growing to become one of the largest towns in the island. The administrative entity of the ''barrio'' emerged after the annexation of Santurce, during the second half of the 19th century, first known as '''Barrio de San Juan'''.<ref name="Oxford">{{citation |title=Puerto Rico |work=[[Oxford Art Online]]}}. Retrieved May 24, 2017</ref>

In the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]], Puerto Rico became a territorial colony of the [[United States|United States of America]].<ref name=":1" /> According to the [[1899 Puerto Rico census|1899 census of Puerto Rico]] conducted by the [[United States Department of War|United States War Department]], the population of the barrio of San Juan Antiguo (then referred to simply as '''San Juan Pueblo''') was 33,006 inhabitants. With the implementation of new administrative practices during the 20th century, the [[Downtown|core urban barrio]] of each Puerto Rican municipality came to be officially known as ''[[Pueblos in Puerto Rico|Pueblo]]'', which was the case of the name of the barrio used from then on: ''San Juan Pueblo''. In the late 20th century, these ''Pueblo barrios'' were officially changed to ''barrio-pueblo'', to explicitly identify them for the context of the [[1990 United States census|1990]] and subsequent [[United States census|US censuses]]. In the case of the municipality of San Juan, which features multiple urban cores rather than a single center as in most other Puerto Rican municipalities, the name '''San Juan Antiguo''' was chosen instead of renaming the barrio to ''San Juan barrio-pueblo''. The name of the barrio [[Pueblo, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Pueblo]], located in the now district of [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]], serves as a reminder of that barrio's former status as the urban core of the [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico#History|former municipality of Río Piedras]] before its annexation to the modern city and municipality of San Juan in 1951.<ref name="FPH3" /> San Juan Antiguo, however, continues to serve as the administrative center for the municipality of San Juan with the [[San Juan City Hall]] and numerous other municipal and civic buildings located there.<ref name="Asamblea">{{cite web |date=25 January 2016 |title=Breve historia de la Asamblea Municipal |trans-title=Brief history of the Municipal Assembly |url=https://www.legislaturasanjuan.pr/nosotros/historia |access-date=May 24, 2017 |publisher=Legislatura Municipal de San Juan |language=es}}</ref>

== Geography ==
{{Main|San Juan Islet}}
[[File:Isleta de San Juan.tiff|thumb|[[San Juan Islet]]]]
San Juan Antiguo is located entirely within the [[San Juan Islet|Isleta de San Juan]]. Measuring 1.02 square miles (2.6 square kilometers) across, the barrio is coextensive with the ''isleta''. The total jurisdiction including water spans 2.63 square miles (6.8 square kilometers). This total area includes the isleta, several reefs and islets located around the main isleta, and portions of the [[San Juan Bay]], the [[San Antonio Channel]] (''Caño San Antonio'') and the [[Condado Lagoon]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=San Juan Puerto Rico-La Isleta |url=http://www.puertadetierra.info/mapa/isleta.htm |access-date=2022-12-05 |website=www.puertadetierra.info}}</ref> 

Despite being located in an islet, the barrio is relatively hilly and rocky, with only the eastern and southern coasts being relatively flat. Due to the continuous levels development of the fortification systems across the islet, its topography has drastically changed throughout its history. At approximately 140 feet (42 m) of elevation above sea level, the former highest point of both the ''barrio'' and the islet was located at the promontory of ''[[Promontory|El Morro]]'' at the northeastern tip of the islet, now occupied by the [[Castillo San Felipe del Morro]].<ref name="NPS 310 pgs">{{cite web |title=Old San Juan Historic District |url=https://www.nps.gov/nr/feature/weekly_features/2012/PR_12000465.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190411213703/https://www.nps.gov/nr/feature/weekly_features/2012/PR_12000465.pdf |archive-date=April 11, 2019 |access-date=31 July 2019 |website=NPS |publisher=United States Department of the Interior |location=WBM}}</ref> Another former highest point was located at the current [[Castillo San Cristóbal (San Juan)|Castillo San Cristóbal]]. Other elevations include ''Loma de los Vientos'' located near the [[Capitol of Puerto Rico]], and Las Ánimas Hill (''Loma de las Ánimas''), which at 128 feet (39 m) is the highest still-existing natural point of the islet.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Isleta High Point - Peakbagger.com |url=https://www.peakbagger.com/peak.aspx?pid=66367&utm_source=chatgpt.com |access-date=2025-07-11 |website=www.peakbagger.com}}</ref>{{AI-retrieved source|date=March 2026|checked=no}} Although several man-made ditches and drainage systems have been built throughout its history, no natural rivers, creeks or bodies of fresh water have ever existed in San Juan Antiguo.

=== Cityscape ===
The urban development of San Juan Antiguo is centered around the subbarrios of [[Catedral, Old San Juan|Catedral]], [[Mercado, Old San Juan|Mercado]], [[San Cristóbal, Old San Juan|San Cristóbal]] and [[San Francisco, Old San Juan|San Francisco]], located at the east-central portion of the islet of San Juan, particularly around Catedral, the location of the city's [[Cathedral of San Juan, Puerto Rico|main Roman Catholic church]] and [[San Juan City Hall|city hall]]. This urban core represents the original Spanish-founded settlement from around which the rest of the islet was populated. The subbarrios of [[Ballajá, Old San Juan|Ballajá]] and [[Marina, Old San Juan|Marina]] were later established as part of the development of the military barracks built to supply [[Castillo San Felipe del Morro|El Morro Castle]] and the establishment of the [[Port of San Juan|main port of the city of San Juan]], respectively. As this urban core was walled-off throughout the development of the fortification system of San Juan, various city gates were built to allow access into the town. The land-facing gate, colloquially known as ''Puerta de Tierra'' (Spanish for 'land gate'), gave way to the westward urban sprawl in the city. [[Puerta de Tierra, San Juan|This district]] today hosts the [[Capitol of Puerto Rico]],<ref>{{Cite web |date=October 16, 2019 |title=Puerta de Tierra es designada como distrito histórico [Puerta de Tierra is given historic district designation] |url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/puertadetierraesdesignadacomodistritohistorico-2523920/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191022082640/https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/puertadetierraesdesignadacomodistritohistorico-2523920/ |archive-date=October 22, 2019 |access-date=October 9, 2020 |website=El Nuevo Dia |language=es}}</ref> and is the largest and most populated in San Juan Antiguo.<ref name="2010 Census2">{{cite book |url=https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/gpo35934/cph-2-53.pdf |title=Puerto Rico: 2010 Population and housing unit counts |publisher=U.S. Dept. of Commerce, Economics and Statistics Administration, U.S. Census Bureau |year=2010 |page=76 |access-date=2019-04-04 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170220183043/https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/gpo35934/cph-2-53.pdf |archive-date=2017-02-20 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Throughout its history, San Juan Antiguo was the most densely populated area of the city of San Juan. Its population peaked at 34,637 inhabitants as recorded in the 1920 Census. This changed during the second half of the 20th century, at a time when the city's [[San Juan–Bayamón–Caguas metropolitan area|metropolitan area]] was growing substantially due to the development of then suburbs such as [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]], [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]] and [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]]. In 2020, San Juan Antiguo had a population of 6,383 inhabitants and a population density of 2,400 people per [[Square kilometre|km<sup>2</sup>]].<ref name="o613">{{cite web | title=Explore Census Data | website=Explore Census Data | url=https://data.census.gov/profile/San_Juan_Antiguo_barrio,_San_Juan_Municipio,_Puerto_Rico?g=060XX00US7212776812 | access-date=August 7, 2024}}</ref>

== Transportation ==
San Juan Antiguo is accessible via a network of roads and bridges that connect the islet to the rest of the [[San Juan–Bayamón–Caguas metropolitan area|San Juan metropolitan area]]. The San Antonio and [[Dos Hermanos Bridge|Dos Hermanos Bridges]] link the barrio to [[Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Santurce]] and the main island of Puerto Rico. Public transportation includes [[Puerto Rico Metropolitan Bus Authority|AMA buses]] and the free municipal trolley system, which circulates throughout Old San Juan and Puerta de Tierra, providing access to key landmarks and government buildings. While no [[Tren Urbano]] stations exist within San Juan Antiguo, nearby [[Sagrado Corazón station]] in Santurce offer bus connections between the barrio and the broader metro area. The narrow, historic streets and limited parking encourage walking and the use of the public trolley system, especially within the colonial core. The [[Port of San Juan]], located along the southern waterfront, serves as a major hub for cruise ships and maritime freight. The [[Cataño Ferry]] offers service to the nearby [[Cataño barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Cataño, Puerto Rico|municipality of Cataño]]. Former ferry services included connection to [[Hato Rey]] and its [[Hato Rey station|Tren Urbano station]].

== Economy and tourism ==
Between the 1950s and the 1980s, the barrio suffered from rapid social, economic and urban decline. This was evident not only in the visual urban blight but also in the physical decay of its historical buildings and fortifications. Revitalization came with the establishment of the [[San Juan National Historic Site]] and its designation, along with [[La Fortaleza]], as a [[UNESCO]] [[World Heritage Site]] in 1983.<ref name="UNESCO">{{cite web |last= |first= |date=1983 |title=La Fortaleza and San Juan National Historic Site in Puerto Rico |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/266/ |access-date=September 23, 2024 |series=[[World Heritage Sites]] |publisher=[[UNESCO]] |language=en |location=Paris |ID=#266}}</ref> Cultural, urban and economic redevelopment came shortly after as part of the incoming 500th anniversary of the [[Age of Discovery|European discovery]] and [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish conquest of Puerto Rico and the America]]. In addition to the [[List of national historic sites and historical parks of the United States|National Historic Site]], the entirety of the cultural heritage of the ''isleta'' is federally protected today, with its eastern side designated as the [[Old San Juan]] [[National Historic Landmark District]],<ref name="72001553nom">{{cite web |last=Rodriguez Morales |first=Luis M. |date=June 1971 |title=National Register of Historic Places Inventory - Nomination Form: Zona Histórica de San Juan (San Juan Historic Zone) |url=https://catalog.archives.gov/id/131518669 |access-date=September 23, 2024 |publisher=National Register of Historic Places |via=[[National Archives (United States)|National Archives]]}}</ref> and its western side designated as the [[Puerta de Tierra, San Juan#National Register of Historic Places|Puerta de Tierra Historic District]],<ref>{{Cite web |date=October 16, 2019 |title=Puerta de Tierra es designada como distrito histórico [Puerta de Tierra is given historic district designation] |url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/puertadetierraesdesignadacomodistritohistorico-2523920/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191022082640/https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/puertadetierraesdesignadacomodistritohistorico-2523920/ |archive-date=October 22, 2019 |access-date=October 9, 2020 |website=El Nuevo Dia |language=es}}</ref> all of them listed in the [[National Register of Historic Places|United States National Register of Historic Places]].<ref name="RNSZH">{{cite web |last= |first= |date=December 7, 2022 |title=Registro de Propiedades Designadas por la Junta de Planificación de Puerto Rico |trans-title=Registry of Properties Designated by the Puerto Rico Planning Board |url=https://jp.pr.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/REGISTRO-OFICIAL-DE-SITIOS-Y-ZONAS-HISTORICAS-DESIGNADAS-Rev.-DIC-2022.pdf |access-date=September 23, 2024 |website=[[Puerto Rico Planning Board]] |publisher=[[Puerto Rico Register of Historic Sites and Zones]] |page=12 |language=es |location=San Juan, Puerto Rico}}</ref>
