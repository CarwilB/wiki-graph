Noted as one of the "happiest cities in Brazil,"<ref>{{Cite web |last=Felipe |first=Mariana |date=2019-07-12 |title=As 26 cidades mais felizes do Brasil, classificadas por Estados |url=https://www.revistabula.com/24462-as-26-cidades-mais-felizes-do-brasil-classificadas-por-estados/ |access-date=2026-04-07 |website=Revista Bula |language=pt-BR}}</ref> Paraíso do Tocantins is also one of the main agricultural hubs of the [[Araguaia Valley]]. The main economic sectors in the city are commerce and services, which make up 46.42% of the local [[Gross domestic product|GDP]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics |date=2019 |title=Brazil Gross Domestic Product per Capita: North: Tocantins: Paraíso do Tocantins |url=https://www.ceicdata.com/en/brazil/sna-2008-gross-domestic-product-per-capita-by-municipality-north-tocantins/gross-domestic-product-per-capita-north-tocantins-paraso-do-tocantins |website=CEIC: Global Economic Data, Indicators, Charts & Forecasts}}</ref> Paraíso do Tocantins sits at the intersection of the [[BR-153 (Brazil highway)|BR-153]], also known as the Transbrasiliana, and the TO-080, which connects to Palmas 39 miles away.

The symbol of Paraíso do Tocantins is the [[Handroanthus chrysanthus|yellow ipê]]—a native tree of the [[Cerrado]] biome.<ref>{{Cite web |title=O CERRADO ⭣ |url=https://www.cerradodasaguas.org.br/en/o-cerrado |access-date=2026-04-07 |website=Cerrado das Águas |language=en}}</ref> The main tourist attraction is the ''Serra do Estrondo'', which is federally designated as an [[Environmental protection area (Brazil)|environmental protection area]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=PARAÍSO DO TOCANTINS, Governo Municipal |date=December 2, 2002 |title=Decreto n.º 267, de 2 de dezembro de 2002: "Cria a Árca de Proteção Ambiental (APA) da 'Serra do Estrondo' ('Serra de São Pedro'), e dá outras providências". |url=https://central.to.gov.br/download/246099 |website=Central de Conteúdo do Estado do Tocantins}}</ref> The site features a lookout point with panoramic views of the city, a chapel, an outdoor gym, a walking trail, a kiosk, and a community center.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Tocantins, Portal |title=Turismo Tocantins {{!}} Serra do Estrondo. |url=https://turismo.to.gov.br/pt/atracoes/paraiso-do-tocantins/serra-do-estrondo |website=Portal Tocantins}}</ref> 

The municipality is also an important hub of agribusiness in the region, serving as a benchmark in both agriculture—particularly in the cultivation of soybeans, sugarcane, and corn—and livestock farming, where cattle raising is a prominent feature. Consequently, the municipality hosts meatpacking plants, distribution centers, and agro-industries, as well as dealerships for machinery and implements, and retail outlets that provide support to the sector.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sodré |first=Reges |date=December 8, 2022 |title=Cidades pequenas no Tocantins: centralidades e interações espaciais na rede urbana |url=https://seer.ufu.br/index.php/caminhosdegeografia |access-date=2026-04-07 |website=Caminhos de Geografia: Universidade Federal de Uberlândia}}</ref>

== History ==
[[File:Paraiso-do-Tocantins-1977.jpg|left|thumb|Center of Paraíso do Tocantins in 1977.]]
Paraíso do Tocantins came into existence with the emergence of a settlement along the roadside of the BR-14 (currently BR-153). This settlement originated from a workers' camp established during the construction of the highway under the supervision of the contractor ''Companhia Nacional''. José Ribeiro Torres, who arrived at the site in 1958, is considered the founder of the settlement.<ref name=":0" /> 

The name ''Paraíso'' was coined by Luzia de Melo Balthazar, the wife of Adjúlio Balthazar, who was the manager of the ''Companhia Nacional''. She was reportedly enchanted by the natural beauty of the workers' camp, nestled in the ''[[cerrado]]'' at the base of Serra do Estrondo mountain range and bordered by the crystal-clear waters of the Pernada and Buritis streams.<ref>{{Cite web |title=História |url=https://www.paraisodotocantins.to.leg.br/historia/ |access-date=2026-04-07 |website=Câmara Municipal de Paraiso do Tocantins |language=pt-BR}}</ref> 

Paraíso's municipal emancipation took place a few years later, with State Law No. 4,716, dated October 23, 1963. On that occasion, the new municipality received the toponym Paraíso do Norte, having been separated from the municipality of [[Pium]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=GOVERNO DO ESTADO DE GOIÁS |date=October 23, 1963 |title=Lei nº 4.716, de 23 de outubro de 1963. Cria o município de PARAÍSO DO NORTE |url=https://legisla.casacivil.go.gov.br/api/v1/arquivos/5290 |website=Diário do Estado de Goiás}}</ref>

With the creation and establishment of the State of Tocantins, the Legislative Decree No. 001, dated January 1, 1989, changed the name from Paraíso do Norte to Paraíso do Tocantins.<ref>{{Cite web |last=TOCANTINS, Assembleia Legislativa do Estado do Tocantins - ALETO |date=January 1, 1989 |title=Decreto Legislativo nº 001, de 1º de Janeiro de 1989 |url=https://www.al.to.leg.br/arquivos/10494.pdf |website=Arquivo da ALETO}}</ref>

== Geography ==
Paraíso do Tocantins covers an area of 1,292.267 km², making it the 1,145th largest municipality in the country, the 72nd in the state, and the ninth in its immediate geographic region.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatistica |title=Paraíso do Tocantins |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/to/paraiso-do-tocantins/panorama |access-date=2026-04-07 |website=Panorama IBGE}}</ref>

=== Population ===
According to the 2022 Census, the population of Paraíso do Tocantins consists of 52,360 people, with a population density of 40.5 inhabitants per km². The municipality is the 617th most populous in Brazil, the fifth in the state, and the first in its immediate region.<ref name=":1" /> 

The [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] estimates that the municipality's had a population of 55,164 people in 2024.<ref name=":1" /> 

=== Climate ===
Paraíso do Tocantins has a tropical savannah climate (Aw) on the Köppen Climate Classification System, characterized by hot temperatures year-round with defined wet and dry seasons.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Alvares |first=Clayton Alcarde |last2=Stape |first2=José Luiz |last3=Sentelhas |first3=Paulo Cesar |last4=de Moraes Gonçalves |first4=José Leonardo |last5=Sparovek |first5=Gerd |date=2013-12-01 |title=Köppen's climate classification map for Brazil |url=https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/metz/detail/22/82078/Koppen_s_climate_classification_map_for_Brazil |journal=Meteorologische Zeitschrift |language=en |pages=711–728 |doi=10.1127/0941-2948/2013/0507}}</ref> 

==== Precipitation ====
Paraíso do Tocantins has an annual rainfall regime with accumulated totals ranging between 1,792 mm and 1,920 mm, with the highest monthly values recorded in March (between 280 and 320 mm). The rainy season lasts an average of 175 days, typically occurring between November and April.<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Batista |display-authors=etal |date=2020 |title=Elaboração das Cartas Climáticas do Estado do Tocantins |url=https://central.to.gov.br/download/213396 |website=Governo do Estado do Tocantins. Secretaria da Fazenda e do Planejamento}}</ref>

==== Temperatures ====
The average temperature in Paraíso do Tocantins is 24°C. The average minimum temperature is 18°C at the highest elevations and 20°C at the lowest points of the territory. Minimum temperatures are typically recorded during the months of June, July, and August. The annual maximum temperature ranges between 30°C and 33°C. These values are typically recorded in the month of September.<ref name=":2" />
