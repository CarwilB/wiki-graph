'''Mezcalapa''' is a [[Municipalities of Chiapas|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Chiapas]], located approximately {{convert|71|km}} northwest of the state capital of [[Tuxtla Gutiérrez]]. Its municipal seat is Raudales Malpaso.

==Geography==
The municipality of Mezcalapa is located in northwestern Chiapas on its border with the states of [[Veracruz]] and [[Tabasco]]. It borders the Chiapan municipalities of [[Ostuacán]] to the northeast, [[Tecpatán]] to the east, [[Ocozocoautla de Espinosa]] to the south, and [[Cintalapa]] to the southwest, as well as the Veracruzan municipality of [[Las Choapas]] to the northwest and the Tabascan municipality of [[Huimanguillo]] to the north. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|847.31|km2}}.<ref name=area/>

The rugged terrain of Mezcalapa has been extensively deforested and converted to pastureland.<ref name=encyclopedia>{{cite encyclopedia |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM07chiapas/municipios/07092a.html |title=Tecpatán |language=es |encyclopedia=Estado de Chiapas |series=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=[[INAFED]] |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref><ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/biblioteca/ficha.html?upc=702825292942 |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Tecpatán, Chiapas |language=es |publisher=INEGI |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref> Much of the southern part of the municipality is covered by the Malpaso or Nezahualcóyotl Reservoir, the third largest in Mexico by total capacity,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/territorio/agua/presas.aspx?tema=T |title=Presas |language=es |website=Cuéntame |publisher=INEGI |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref> created by the [[Malpaso Dam]] on the [[Grijalva River]] at Raudales Malpaso.

Mezcalapa has a tropical climate. Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|20|and(-)|28|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|1500|and(-)|3500|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
Prior to the construction of the Malpaso Dam in the early 1960s, the [[Zoque people]] were the main inhabitants of the area that is now Mezcalapa, which was then part of the municipality of Tecpatán.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Pérez Domínguez |first=Jesús David |date=June–September 2018 |title=Migración y dispersión poblacional en Tecpatán, Chiapas |journal=Revista Espacio I+D Innovación Más Desarrollo |language=es |trans-title=Migration and population dispersion in Tecpatán, Chiapas |url=https://espacioimasd.unach.mx/index.php/Inicio/article/view/155 |volume=7 |issue=17 |pages=91–102 |doi=10.31644/IMASD.17.2018.a07 |access-date=31 October 2020|doi-access=free }}</ref> The construction of the dam brought in thousands of workers from other parts of Mexico, while flooding Zoque villages in the reservoir area such as [[Quechula]]. The workers and many of the displaced Zoque lived near the dam construction site, which grew to become the settlement of Raudales Malpaso.<ref name=dynamics>{{cite journal |last1=Niño Maldonado |first1=Cinthia |last2=Kauffer |first2=Edith |date=20 July 2020 |title=Dinámicas territoriales en torno a la construcción de la presa Malpaso, Chiapas (1960-2011): construir, trabajar y permanecer |language=es |trans-title=Territorial Dynamics around the Malpaso Dam in Chiapas (1960-2011): Building, Working and Staying |url=https://revistas.ecosur.mx/sociedadyambiente/index.php/sya/article/view/2138 |journal=Sociedad y Ambiente |issue=23 |pages=1–35 |issn=2007-6576 |doi=10.31840/sya.vi23.2138 |access-date=31 October 2020|doi-access=free }}</ref> Demands to create a new municipality in the area had been made as early as 1963<ref name=dynamics/> and were finally satisfied on 23 November 2011, when the decree establishing Mezcalapa as a new municipality was gazetted.<ref name=decree>{{cite news |date=23 November 2011 |title=Decreto Número 008 |url=http://www.sgg.chiapas.gob.mx/po2006/archivos/descargas.php?f=P.O.%20337%20SEGUNDA%20SECCION.pdf |language=es |work=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Chiapas, Segunda Sección |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref> The new municipality combined the western half of Tecpatán with six communities in Ocozocoautla de Espinosa on the south shore of the Malpaso Reservoir.<ref name=dynamics/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the localities that now comprise the municipality of Mezcalapa recorded a total population of 20,950 inhabitants.<ref name=ageeml/>

There are 206 localities in the municipality, of which only the municipal seat Raudales Malpaso is designated as urban. It recorded a population of 6817 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=ageeml/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
Cattle ranching and dairy production are important economic activities in Mezcalapa.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=López Gómez |first=Irma |date=October 2013 |title=Caracterización del Sistema Silvopastoril en la Producción de Leche Orgánica, en el Grupo Malpaso, Sociedad de Producción Rural de Responsabilidad Limitada. Mezcalapa, Chiapas |type=Lic. |pages=2–3 |publisher=Intercultural University of Chiapas |language=es |url=http://www.cisc.org.mx/liderazgosjuveniles/documentos/TrabajosTerminalesUNICH/Tesis/tesis27.pdf |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref> Since the impoundment of the Malpaso Reservoir, fish farming has also become an important activity.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

The Nezahualcóyotl hydroelectric plant at Raudales Malpaso provides nearly half of the hydroelectric capacity in the entire Grijalva basin.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://premioobrascemex.com/ObrasGanadoras/dosmildieciseis_interior/infra2 |title=Central Hidroeléctrica Netzahualcóyotl |website=Premio Obras Cemex |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref>

The tolled [[Mexican Federal Highway 145D|Federal Highway 145D]] runs north–south through the eastern part of the municipality, crossing the Malpaso Reservoir on the [[Chiapas Bridge]] and linking Chiapas with southern Veracruz. [[Mexican Federal Highway 187|Federal Highway 187]] begins at Raudales Malpaso and intersects with Federal Highway 145D before continuing north into Tabasco. Another paved highway connects Raudales Malpaso with Tecpatán to the east.<ref>{{cite map |url=http://www.ceieg.chiapas.gob.mx/productos/files/MAPASMUNDC/Base_Mezcalapa.pdf |title=Municipio de Mezcalapa |year=2019 |scale=1:180,000 |publisher=CEIEG |access-date=31 October 2020}}</ref>
