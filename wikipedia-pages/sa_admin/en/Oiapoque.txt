'''Oiapoque''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|ojaˈpɔki}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the north of the state of [[Amapá]], [[Brazil]]. Its population is 27,906<ref>[https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/ap/oiapoque/panorama IBGE 2020]</ref> and its area is {{convert|22,625|km2|mi2}}.<ref name="c"/> [[Oyapock River|Oiapoque]] is also a major river in the same state, forming the international border with [[French Guiana]]. The [[Oyapock River Bridge]], connecting the village with [[Saint-Georges, French Guiana|Saint-Georges]] in French Guiana, was completed in 2011 but not opened to pedestrian or vehicle traffic until 2017.<ref name="ponte"/>

Lying on the northern coast of Brazil, Oiapoque is popularly considered the northernmost point of Brazil. The phrase ''do Oiapoque ao Chuí'' ("from Oiapoque to [[Chuí]]") means "all of Brazil."<ref name="lp"/> However, there are more northerly points in [[Roraima]] state such as the municipality of [[Uiramutã]]. Oiapoque remains the northernmost coastal city of Brazil, and the northernmost city of Amapá. It occupies more than half of the north border of the state.

==Early history==

The Oiapoque River was visited by Europeans  in the first years of the 16th century. The first European was the pirate [[Vicente Yáñez Pinzón]]. It has been called Japoc, Yapoc, Iapoco, and even Vicente Pinzón River. The name Oiapoque was officially used from 1900, when a [[Amapá Question|territorial dispute]] between Brazil and [[France]] was resolved through [[Switzerland|Swiss]] diplomatic arbitration.

==Clevelândia==
{{Main|Clevelândia do Norte}}
At the beginning of the 20th century, the village of Oiapoque hosted a political and criminal concentration camp called Clevelândia. In 1922 an agricultural outpost called the Núcleo Colonial Cleveland was transformed into a camp during the presidency of [[Artur Bernardes]] ([[Presidency of Artur Bernardes|1922–1926]]). Many Brazilian anarchist militants were sentenced to hard labour here. Of the 946 prisoners interned at Clevelândia between 1924 and 1927, 491 died. Many of the survivors returned to [[São Paulo]] and [[Rio de Janeiro]] permanently sickened with malaria.<ref name="v"/>

==Geography==
Oiapoque is bordered to the west by its long frontier with French Guiana. It is bordered to the north by the [[Atlantic Ocean]]. On its eastern and southern sides it borders the municipalities of [[Calçoene]], [[Serra do Navio]], [[Pedra Branca do Amapari]], and [[Laranjal do Jari]].<ref name="c"/>

The municipality contains 24.15% of the {{convert|2369400|ha|adj=on}} [[Amapá State Forest]], a sustainable use conservation unit established in 2006.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|FES do Amapá – ISA}}|language=pt
 |title=FES do Amapá|publisher=ISA: Instituto Socioambiental
 |url=https://uc.socioambiental.org/uc/430|access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref> The [[Cape Orange National Park]] is located on the coast of the municipality, and covers {{convert|6573|km2}} of rainforest on the coast line.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sistemas.mma.gov.br/cnuc/index.php?ido=relatorioparametrizado.exibeRelatorio&relatorioPadrao=true&idUc=169 |title=Unidade de Conservação: PARQUE NACIONAL DO CABO ORANGE|website=Ministério do Meio Ambiente|access-date=27 March 2021|language=pt}}</ref>

Oiapoque can be accessed by air via the [[Oiapoque Airport]].<ref name="market">{{cite web|url=https://www.flightmarket.com.br/pt/anuncio/aeroporto/SBOI |title=Aeroporto / OIAPOQUE|website=Flight Market|access-date=3 April 2021|language=pt}}</ref>

==Kuahí Museum==
The Kuahí Museum was opened in 2007 by the four indigenous groups living the municipality: the [[Palikur]], the [[Galibi Marwono]], the [[Galibi do Oiapoque]], and the [[Karipuna do Amapá]]. The museum contains more than 400 objects used by the Amerindians.<ref name="museum">{{cite web|url=https://amlatina.contemporaryand.com/places/kuahi-museum-of-the-indigenous-peoples-of-oiapoque/ |title=Kuahí Museum of the Indigenous Peoples of Oiapoque |website=Contemporary And|access-date=29 March 2021}}</ref> The project was conceived in the 1990s to promote and transmit the knowledge of the indigenous community. The museum is a public [[not-for-profit]] entity linked to the Amapá State Secretariat of Culture.<ref>{{cite journal|author=Lux Vidal|year=2013|title=Kuahí The Indians of the Lower Oiapoque and their museum|volume=v10n1|journal=Cultural Heritage and Museums}}</ref>
