'''San Jorge''' is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Guatemala]]n [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Zacapa Department|Zacapa]], located approximately {{convert|8|km}} southwest of the departmental capital, also called [[Zacapa]].

==Geography==
San Jorge is located in the foothills on the south side of the [[Motagua River]] valley, just south of its confluence with the [[Río Grande de Zacapa]]. It borders the Zacapan municipalities of [[Huité]] to the west, [[Estanzuela]] to the north, and [[Zacapa]] to the east, and the municipality of [[Chiquimula]], the capital of [[Chiquimula Department]], to the south. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|82.27|km2}}.<ref name=decree/>

The relief of San Jorge presents a contrast between the flat plains in the northern and western parts of the municipality, and the mountainous terrain punctuated by steep valleys in the east and south. Forests cover 41% of the municipality's land area. The municipal seat of San Jorge sits at the lowest elevation in the municipality, {{convert|210|m}} above sea level.<ref name=economics/> 

San Jorge is drained by various ''quebradas'' or streams. The longest of these is the Quebrada San Juan, which flows {{convert|24|km}} southwest to northeast from the El Gigante mountain to the Río Grande de Zacapa. Many of the ''quebradas'' flow only during the wet season – those that flow year-round have greatly diminished volumes in the dry season – and all are highly polluted by waste and pose a serious environmental issue. There is no water treatment facility in the municipality.<ref name=economics/> 

San Jorge has a tropical climate with an annual average temperature of {{convert|27|C}} and a relative humidity typically around 74%. March and April are the hottest months. As in the rest of Zacapa Department, rainfall is low and highly variable: annual precipitation is {{convert|470|mm}}, and the wet season lasts from June to October.<ref name=economics/>

==History==
The plains of San Jorge began attracting settlers in the 17th century, who raised cattle and grew corn.<ref name=economics/><ref name=history>{{cite news |date=28 January 2014 |url=https://www.prensalibre.com/guatemala/politica/zacapa-san-jorge-congreso-nuevo-municipio-0-1074492732/ |title=San Jorge, Zacapa, será el municipio 336 |language=es |newspaper=[[Prensa Libre (Guatemala)|Prensa Libre]] |accessdate=8 November 2020}}</ref> One of the first settlers was Nicolás de Peralta y Cisneros, the son of a ''[[Peninsulars|peninsular]]'' and a ''[[Criollo people|criolla]]'', who established the ''[[hacienda]]''s of San Juan and San Nicolás in the area and became its first parish priest in 1661. The area was called Llanetillos from the 18th century onwards until 1935, when [[Jorge Ubico]] approved a petition from its inhabitants to change its name to San Jorge.<ref name=economics/><ref name=history/> San Jorge built its first groundwater distribution system in 1996.<ref name=economics/>

On 28 January 2014, the [[Congress of the Republic of Guatemala]] decreed the creation of San Jorge from territory previously part of the municipality of Zacapa.<ref name=decree/> The decree was gazetted in the ''[[Diario de Centro América]]'' on 21 February 2014,<ref name=economics/> and entered into force eight days later.<ref name=decree/>

==Demographics==
In the 2018 Guatemalan Census, which had an estimated underenumeration rate of 9.0%,<ref>{{cite web |last=Brinkhoff |first=Thomas |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/cities/ |title=Guatemala |work=City Population |date=23 January 2020 |accessdate=7 November 2020}}</ref> San Jorge recorded a total population of 12,304 inhabitants.<ref name=census/> The [[National Institute of Statistics (Guatemala)|National Institute of Statistics]] subsequently estimated the population of San Jorge in 2018 to be 13,045.<ref name=population/>

==Culture==
[[Holy Week]] festivities in San Jorge include parades, the making of [[sawdust carpet]]s, and traditional cuisine and dancing. Each village in the municipality celebrates the feast day of its patron saint with dances and beauty pageants. Bullfighting, [[jaripeo]], cockfighting, and horse shows are also popular pastimes.<ref name=economics/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
The main cash crops grown in San Jorge are canteloupes and watermelons, which are exported via the port of [[Santo Tomás de Castilla|Santo Tomás]] to the United States, or sold at the Central Wholesale Market (''Central de Mayoreo'', CENMA) in [[Villa Nueva, Guatemala|Villa Nueva]] to buyers from the rest of Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras. Corn, beans, sorghum, and peppers are also produced for local consumption.<ref name=economics/>

A paved departmental road ZAC-8 connects the municipal seat of San Jorge with Central America Highway 10 just outside of the town of Zacapa. Other roads in San Jorge remain unpaved.<ref name=economics/>
