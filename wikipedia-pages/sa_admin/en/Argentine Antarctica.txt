'''Argentine Antarctica''' ({{langx|es|Antártida Argentina}} or {{Lang|es|Sector Antártico Argentino}})<ref>Pequeño Larrouse Ilustrado 1990, para la Argentina, ajustado a la cartografía oficial por el Poder Ejecutivo Nacional a través del Instituto Geográfico Militar (IGM) acorde Ley 22.963 aprobada por expediente del 5 October 1989.</ref> is an area on [[Antarctica]] claimed by [[Argentina]] as part of its national [[territory]]. It consists of the [[Antarctic Peninsula]] and a triangular section extending to the [[South Pole]], delimited by the [[25th meridian west|25° West]] and [[74th meridian west|74° West]] meridians and the [[60th parallel south|60° South]] parallel.<ref>{{cite book| last = Beck| first = Peter J. | title = The international politics of Antarctica | publisher = Routledge| year = 1986 | page = 119 | url = https://books.google.com/books?id=-4g9AAAAIAAJ&pg=PP15 | isbn =0-7099-3239-1}}</ref> This region overlaps with [[British Antarctic Territory|British]] and [[Chilean Antarctic Territory|Chilean]] claims in Antarctica. None of these claims have widespread international recognition. 

Argentina's Antarctic claim is based on its presence on a base on [[Laurie Island]] in the [[South Orkney Islands]] since 1904, along with the area's proximity to the South American continent, and is subject to the [[Antarctic Treaty]];<ref>La Antártica Chilena. p. 173. Escrito por Oscar Pinochet de la Barra. Publicado por Andrés Bello, 1976.</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=The Antarctic Treaty |url=https://media.nti.org/documents/antarctic_treaty.pdf |website=Inventory of International Nonproliferation Organizations and Regimes |access-date=19 May 2021}}</ref> however, the presence on the Antarctic mainland was established in 1951, and the official claim on the mainland was started to be formulated on 1941, with several changes and was officially declared on 1957. Administratively, Argentine Antarctica is a [[Departments of Argentina|department]] of the [[Provinces of Argentina|province]] of [[Tierra del Fuego Province, Argentina|Tierra del Fuego, Antarctica, and South Atlantic Islands]]. The provincial authorities are based in [[Ushuaia]].<ref>{{cite web |title=división política. Provincia de Tierra del Fuego, Antártida e Islas del Atlántico Sur. Provincia santa cruz. |url=http://recursosweb.tierradelfuego.gov.ar/webapps/catastro/atla/11_atlas_tdf_politico.pdf |access-date=16 December 2021 |language=es}}</ref> Argentine activities in Antarctica are coordinated by the [[Instituto Antártico Argentino]] (IAA) and [[Argentine Antarctic Program]].

The Argentine exploration of the continent started early in the 20th century. [[José María Sobral]] was the first Argentine to set foot on Antarctica in 1902, where he spent two seasons with the [[Swedish Antarctic Expedition]] of [[Otto Nordenskiöld]]. Shortly afterward, in 1904, the [[Orcadas Base|Orcadas Permanent Base]] was established. Years later, other permanent and seasonal bases were constructed. The first Argentine expedition to reach the [[South Pole]] was the 1965 [[Operación 90]]. The estimated area of Argentine Antarctica is {{convert|1461597|km2|abbr=on}}, of which {{convert|965597|km2|abbr=on}} is land. The ice of the glaciers over the territory's surface has an average thickness of 2&nbsp;kilometres (1¼ miles). Temperatures fall in a typical range from 0&nbsp;°C (32&nbsp;°F) in summer to −60&nbsp;°C (−76&nbsp;°F) in winter, although in certain points the temperature may drop to as low as −82&nbsp;°C (−115&nbsp;°F) and may rise to 18&nbsp;°C (65&nbsp;°F).<ref>{{Cite news |last=Readfearn |first=Graham |date=7 February 2020 |title=Antarctica logs hottest temperature on record with a reading of 18.3C |language=en-GB |work=The Guardian |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/feb/07/antarctica-logs-hottest-temperature-on-record-with-a-reading-of-183c |access-date=3 August 2023 |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

Time zone [[UTC-3]] is used in the area, as in Argentina. Argentina has six permanent [[Research stations in Antarctica|Antarctic stations]] and seven summer stations. According to the Argentine national census as of October 2010, Argentine Antarctica had 230 inhabitants (including 9 families and 16 children) at six permanent bases: 75 at [[Marambio Base|Marambio]], 66 at [[Esperanza Base|Esperanza]], 33 at [[Carlini Base|Carlini]], 20 at [[San Martín Base|San Martín]], 19 at [[Belgrano II]], and 17 at [[Orcadas Base|Orcadas]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=25 October 2010 |title=Censo 2010: en la Antártida viven 230 personas, 9 familias y 16 niños {{!}} Ciudadanos |url=https://www.lavoz.com.ar/ciudadanos/censo-2010-en-la-antartida-viven-230-personas-9-familias-y-16-ninos/ |access-date=4 January 2024 |website=La Voz del Interior |language=es}}</ref> Provisional results of the 2022 Argentine national census indicated 130 inhabitants for Argentine Antarctica.<ref>{{Cite web |title=INDEC: Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos de la República Argentina |url=https://www.indec.gob.ar/indec/web/Nivel4-Tema-2-41-165 |access-date=3 August 2023 |website=www.indec.gob.ar |language=es}}</ref> Residents take part in [[Elections in Antarctica|general elections]] within Tierra del Fuego Province.<ref>{{cite web |last= |first= |title=Elecciones 2021: Juntos por el Cambio arrasó en la Antártida y el Frente de Todos quedó tercero |url=https://www.infobae.com/politica/2021/11/16/elecciones-2021-juntos-por-el-cambio-arraso-en-la-antartida-y-el-frente-de-todos-quedo-tercero/ |website=infobae |date=16 November 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

== History ==

=== First expeditions ===
In 1815, [[Guillermo Brown]], an [[Ireland|Irish]]-born Argentine Marine Commodore serving in the naval forces of the [[United Provinces of the Río de la Plata]], began a campaign to intercept the [[Spain|Spanish]] fleet in the [[Pacific Ocean]]. When they were crossing [[Cape Horn]] aboard the ''Hercules'' and ''Trinidad'', strong winds pushed them to parallel 65[[25th meridian west|°]] South. Brown claimed to have sighted Antarctic land on the expedition, saying that it is the reason why Argentine cartography often calls the northernmost part of the [[Antarctic Peninsula]] ''Tierra de la Trinidad''.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Brown |first=Guillermo |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=QDtCAAAAYAAJ&dq=%22despu%C3%A9s+de+dar+vuelta+el+cabo+de+hornos%22&pg=PA18 |title=Acciones navales de la república Argentina, 1813–1828 |date=1904 |publisher=Impr. del Ministerio de Marina |language=es}}</ref><ref>Anuario de historia Argentina, Volumen 1, p. 296. Colaborador: Sociedad de Historia Argentina. Editor: Domingo Viau y ca. 1940.</ref>

On 10 June 1829, the government of the [[province of Buenos Aires]] issued a decree creating the ''Political-Military Command of the Falkland (Malvinas) Islands'' (see [[Louis Vernet]]) including the islands adjacent to Cape Horn, which is situated in the territorial waters of Argentina and the Antarctic islands.

{{Regions of Argentina}}The Argentine government joined the [[Swedish Antarctic Expedition]] on 10 October 1900. This expedition received support, and in exchange, offered the services of the [[Argentine Navy]] to deliver scientific data and zoological collections. On the way through [[Buenos Aires]], Lieutenant Jose Maria Sobral boarded the ship ''[[Antarctic (ship)|Antarctic]]'' on 21 December 1901. As no news of the expedition reached the Argentine government, it fulfilled its commitment to support the expedition by renovating the Corvette [[ARA Uruguay]]. It set out on 8 October 1903 under the command of Lieutenant [[Julián Irizar]] to find and rescue members of the [[Exploration|expedition]] who had been sheltering following the collapse of the ''Antarctic ship''.

A hut was built on [[Snow Hill Island]] in 1902. The Argentine Navy took possession of the hut in 1954 and named it Refugio Suecia. It is now an Argentine historical monument and historical site as mentioned by the [[Antarctic Treaty System|Antarctic Treaty]].<ref>Stonehouse, Bernard (2002). ''Encyclopedia of Antarctica and the Southern Oceans''. John Wiley and Sons. {{ISBN|0-471-98665-8}}</ref><ref>[http://www.ats.aq/devPH/apa/ep_protected_detail.aspx?type=1&id=115&lang=s SMH 38: Cabaña de Nordenskjöld] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923181027/http://www.ats.aq/devPH/apa/ep_protected_detail.aspx?type=1&id=115&lang=s|date=23 September 2015}} – Secretaría del Tratado Antártico (Spanish).</ref> The 1902 expedition built another hut in Hope Bay, which is also an Antarctic monument under the control of Esperanza Station.<ref>Hon. Cámara de Senadores, Comisión de Cultura, Antecedentes de Proyecto de Ley, Orden del Día Nº 590 del año 2008.</ref><ref name=atcm>{{cite web |url= http://www.ats.aq/documents/ATCM35/WW/atcm35_ww003_e.pdf|title= List of Historic Sites and Monuments approved by the ATCM yoggynhyss-date=2013-12-30 |publisher=Antarctic Treaty Secretariat |year=2012}}</ref>

=== Permanent occupation of South Orkney Islands since 1904 ===
[[File:Omond House - Laurie Island.jpg|thumb|left|290px|Omond House, [[Laurie Island]], circa 1903.]]
On 2 January 1904, Argentina acquired a weather station installed by Scotsman [[William Speirs Bruce]], in [[Laurie Island]] in the South Orkneys, where there had been a crew of six men making scientific observations. In it was a meteorological observatory, where he also worked. A post office was installed. On 22 February 1904, civilian (employee of the Argentine company official post and telegraph) Hugo Alberto Acuna noted the first time the [[flag of Argentina]] was officially raised on the Argentine Antarctic Sector. The observatory became the Orcadas Base, which is the oldest existing permanent human settlement in the Antarctic territory,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Conmemoración de Hugo Alberto Acuña y su izado de la bandera argentina en la Antártida. |url=http://www.lanueva.com/edicion_impresa/nota/22/02/2013/d2m031.prt |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130514151933/http://www.lanueva.com/edicion_impresa/nota/22/02/2013/d2m031.prt |archivedate=14 May 2013 |language=es}}</ref> an event commemorated in Argentina as the [[Day of the Argentine Antarctic]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=22 February 2023 |title=From Antarctica, President Fernández calls for 'peace and prosperity' |newspaper=[[Buenos Aires Times]] |url=https://www.batimes.com.ar/news/argentina/from-antarctica-argentinas-president-alberto-fernandez-calls-for-global-peace-and-prosperity.phtml |access-date=5 November 2023}}</ref>

The Argentine corvette ''ARA Uruguay'' returned to Antarctica in 1905 to take staff from the South Orkney and travel to Deception Island and [[Wiencke Island]] in search of [[Jean-Baptiste Charcot]], whose [[French Antarctic Expedition]] (1903–1905) was believed to be lost. Because of the Argentine collaboration with his expedition, Charcot named an insular group as [[Argentine Islands]]. One of these islands was named [[Galindez Island]] in honor of the captain of the Corvette, Ismael Galíndez, and another was named [[Uruguay Island]], in homage to the Argentine Corvette.<ref>[http://www.antarctida.kiev.ua/english/history.htm Islas Argentinas] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070607144449/http://www.antarctida.kiev.ua/english/history.htm |date=7 June 2007 }}</ref>

The Argentine Government established two meteorological observatories on [[South Georgia Island]] and [[Wandel Island]]<!-- who already had on the islands Laurie and Observatorio (near [[Isla de los Estados]])-->. Two attempted expeditions to Wandel Island failed. In June 1905, the transport ARA ''Guardia Nacional'', under the command of the Lieutenant Alfredo P. Lamas, established an observatory in [[Grytviken]] in [[Cumberland Bay]], renamed in Spanish ''Bahía Guardia Nacional''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Dirección Nacional del Antártico {{!}} Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores, Comercio Internacional y Culto |url=https://cancilleria.gob.ar/es/iniciativas/dna/ |access-date=4 January 2024 |website=cancilleria.gob.ar |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Headland |first=Robert |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Sg49AAAAIAAJ&dq=%22Erik+Nordenhaag%22&pg=PA233 |title=Chronological List of Antarctic Expeditions and Related Historical Events |date=1989 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-0-521-30903-5 |page=233 |language=en}}</ref><ref>[http://www.nzaht.org/content/library/Historic_Huts_of_the_Antarctic_from_the_Heroic_Age_B_Healand.pdf Historic Huts of the Antarctic from the Heroic Age] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130208181407/http://www.nzaht.org/content/library/Historic_Huts_of_the_Antarctic_from_the_Heroic_Age_B_Healand.pdf |date=8 February 2013 }} – Scott Polar Research Institute (2010).</ref>

On 30 March 1927, the first radiotelegraph station in Antarctica was inaugurated on the South Orkney Islands. On 15 December 1927, the General Directorate of Post and Telegraph from Argentina informed the International Bureau of the [[Universal Postal Union]] about their claims in Antarctica and other islands of the South Atlantic.

=== Creation of the National Antarctic Commission in 1940 ===
In 1939, Argentina temporarily created <!--(to attend a Norwegian invitation)-->the National Commission of the Antarctic by Decree No. 35821. On 30 April 1940, it became a permanent body by Decree No. 61852 in order to increase research in the area.<ref>{{cite book |title=MEMORIA DEL MINISTERIO DE RELACIONES EXTERIORES Y CULTO 1951–52 |publisher=Argentina |page=148 |url=https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/userfiles/ut/memoriamrec_1951-1952.pdf#:~:text=Comisi%C3%B3n%20Nacional%20del%20Ant%C3%A1rtico.%20La%20Comisi%C3%B3n%20Nacional,y%20Culto%2C%20encargado%20de%20asesorar%20al%20Poder |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=1939–1956 {{!}} Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores, Comercio Internacional y Culto |url=https://cancilleria.gob.ar/es/iniciativas/dna/historia-y-patrimonio/linea-historica/1939-1956 |website=cancilleria.gob.ar}}</ref>

On 6 November 1940, Chile established the boundaries of its Antarctic claims by decree.
{{quote|The Chilean Antarctic Territory or Antarctic Chilean Territory consists of all the lands, islands, islets, reefs, glaciers, and other known and unknown areas, as well as the respective territorial sea, within the limits of the ice cap defined by the meridians 53° and 90° west longitude from Greenwich.}}

Argentina formally protested the Chilean decree by a note dated 12 November 1940, rejecting its validity and expressing a potential claim to the same area.<ref>{{cite book|author=Donald Rothwell|title=The Polar Regions and the Development of International Law|volume=3|series=Cambridge Studies in International and Comparative Law|publisher=Cambridge University Press|year=1996|pages=58|isbn=0-521-56182-5}}</ref> In turn, the United Kingdom protested on 25 February 1941.

By the end of 1940, Argentina and Chile mutually recognized "that Chile and Argentina have indisputable sovereignty rights in the polar region known as the American Antarctica".<ref>{{Harvnp|Pinochet de la Barra|1987|p=213}}</ref>

=== Formulation of the Argentine claim to the Antarctic mainland since 1941 ===
The National Antarctic Commission establishment led to the definition of a claim on the Antarctic mainland.<ref name=Pugna>{{cite book |last1=Fontana |first1=Pablo |title=La pugna antártica, el conflicto por el sexto continente: 1939–1959 |date=2014 |publisher=Guazuvira Ediciones |location=Buenos Aires |isbn=978-987-33-5970-5 |language=es |page=108}}</ref> Argentina published a map in 1941 showing "the Antarctic Sector in which the Argentine Republic maintains rights" between 75° W and 25° W, south of 60° S, without a formal legal claim yet.<ref>{{cite book |author1=Robert D. Hayton |title=National Interests in Antarctica An Annotated Bibliography |date=1960 |publisher=United States. Antarctic Projects Office |page=22 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=YZsZAAAAMAAJ&q=Argentina%20ant%C3%A1rtida%2075%C2%B0O%2025%C2%B0%20O%201941 |quote=Argentine map published in 1940 and distributed in Oct. 1941 which includes a marginal map "del Sector Antártico sobre el cual la República Argentina mantiene derechos" [sector claimed:25° to 75° west longitude from 60th Parallel to the South Pole}}</ref> 

In January 1942, Argentina declared its Antarctic rights between the meridians at 25° (near the far east of the [[South Sandwich Islands]]) and 68° 34' W, south of 60° S, establishing the Argentine Antarctica, according to the [[theory of polar areas]].<ref>{{cite book |title=Norsk hvalfangst-tidende organ for den Norsk Hvalfangeforening |date=1957 |publisher=International Association of Whaling Companies |volume=46|page=490 |url=https://www.google.es/books/edition/Norsk_hvalfangst_tidende/0OEwr18HeOkC?hl=es&gbpv=1&bsq=%22Argentina%20claimed%22&dq=68%C2%B0%2024%2025%20Ant%C3%A1rtida%20Argentina%201942&printsec=frontcover|quote=In 1942, Argentina claimed a sector between 25° and 68 : 34 W., south of 60° S. and to the Pole. In 1946 the claim was extended to all regions be- tween 25° and 74° W., south of 60° S.}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=Todo es historia |date=1983 |page=66 |url=https://www.google.es/books/edition/Todo_es_historia/tGEkAQAAIAAJ?hl=es&gbpv=1&bsq=%22Ant%C3%A1rtida%20comprendidas%20entre%20los%20meridianos%22%20%22a%20la%20Rep%C3%BAblica%20Argentina%22&dq=68%C2%B0%2024%2025%20Ant%C3%A1rtida%20Argentina%201942&printsec=frontcover |volume=194–200 |quote=Con el propósito de reafirmar los derechos en las tierras de Antártida comprendidas entre los meridianos 25° y 68° 34 W y el Sur del paralelo 60° S}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last1=Hayton |first1=Robert D. |last2=Office |first2=United States Antarctic Projects |title=National Interests in Antarctica: An Annotated Bibliography |date=1960 |publisher=U.S. Government Printing Office |page=9 |url=https://www.google.es/books/edition/National_Interests_in_Antarctica/YZsZAAAAMAAJ?hl=es&gbpv=1&bsq=this%20note%20Argentina%20claims%20a%20sector&dq=Comisi%C3%B3n%20Nacional%20del%20Ant%C3%A1rtico%201946&printsec=frontcover |language=en |quote=Buenos Aires, 3 June 1946; in this note Argentina claims a sector South of Parallel 60 between 25° and 68° 34' long. West, and refers to her note of Sept. 1940 and subsequent notes, e. f., to the Universal Postal Union, reserving Argentine rights; pp. 55–16}}</ref>{{efn|[[Oscar Espinosa Moraga]] claims it was on the  68° 24' W meridian instead of 68° 34' W, being a direct projection from [[Punta Dúngeness]], the southernmost point of mainland Argentina.}} 

On 8 November 1942, Argentina laid claim to Antarctic land when an expedition under the command of the captain Alberto J. Oddera placed a cylinder containing a report and a flag on [[Deception Island]]. In January 1943, the crew of the [[United Kingdom|British]] ship [[MV Carnarvon Castle|HMS ''Carnarvon Castle'']] removed the evidence of the Argentine inauguration and planted the [[British flag]]. On 5 March of the same year, the Argentine vessel ARA ''1° de Mayo'' removed the British flag.<ref>{{cite web |title=EL 8 DE FEBRERO DE 1942 ARGENTINA TOMA POSESIÓN DE TERRITORIO ANTÁRTICO EN DECEPCIÓN |url=https://www.radiopolar.com/el-8-de-febrero-de-1942-argentina-toma-posesion-de-territorio-antartico-en-decepcion |website=Polar Comunicaciones |language=es-CL |date=9 February 2017}}</ref><ref>[http://www.hamilton.edu/news/more_news/display.cfm?id=3866 Passing Deception Island]</ref>

[[File:DAN-IAA logo.png|thumb|upright=0.7|left|DNA-IAA emblem]]
On 2 September 1946, Decree No. 8944 required all the national maps to show the claimed territory of the Argentine Antarctic Sector,<ref>{{cite web |title=Decreto 8944 |url=https://www.ign.gob.ar/descargas/Marco-Legal/Decreto%208944-1946.pdf |language=es |date=2 September 1946}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last1=Fraga |first1=Jorge Alberto |title=La Antartida: reserva ecológica al cumplir 30 años su tratado |date=1992 |publisher=Instituto de Publicaciones Navales, Centro Naval |isbn=978-950-9016-81-1 |page=134 |url=https://www.google.es/books/edition/La_Antartida/c5AQAAAAYAAJ?hl=es&gbpv=1&bsq=%22Comisi%C3%B3n%20Nacional%20del%20Ant%C3%A1rtico%22%201946&dq=Comisi%C3%B3n%20Nacional%20del%20Ant%C3%A1rtico%201946&printsec=frontcover |language=es}}</ref> which was extended between the meridians at 25° and 74° west longitude , south of 60° S by the National Antarctic Commission.<ref name=Pugna/><ref>{{cite journal |last1=Headland |first1=Robert |title=Review of La pugna Antártica: el conflicto por el sexto continente 1939–1959 |journal=Polar Record |date=March 2016 |volume=52 |issue=2 |pages=262–263 |doi=10.1017/S0032247415000650 |url=https://scispace.com/pdf/la-pugna-antartica-el-conflicto-por-el-sexto-continente-1939-1cz4eyupxq.pdf |quote=A ''Comisión Nacional del Antártico'' was reorganised in 1946 and a list of its nine objects is given. The western boundary of Antártida Argentina was extended and more bases established.}}</ref> 

On 18 February 1948, the Chilean President [[Gabriel Gonzalez Videla]] personally opened [[Base General Bernardo O'Higgins Riquelme]] on the [[Antarctic Peninsula]]. This was the first [[official visit]] of a [[head of state]] to Antarctica.<ref>{{cite journal |title=El itinerario del primer presidente en territorio antártico {{!}} Revista de Marina |journal=Revista Marina |date=2025 |url=https://revistamarina.cl/es/articulo/el-itinerario-del-primer-presidente-en-territorio-antartico}}</ref>

On 4 March 1948, [[Chile]] and Argentina signed a mutual agreement protecting and defending legal rights of the territorial Antarctic, mutually recognizing their claims.<ref>{{cite web |author1=Francisco Javier Lertora Pinto |title=EL SISTEMA ANTÁRTICO Y EL DERECHO DEL MAR |url=https://www.hemisfericosypolares.cl/tesis-premio-morla/TESIS_lertora_pinto_francisco_javier.pdf |publisher=UNIVERSIDAD DE CHILE |page=49 |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |author1=Pablo Rodríguez Márquez |author2=Mario L. Puig Morales |title=Chile y sus intereses en la Antártica |date=2007 |location=[[Santiago de Chile]] |isbn=978-956-8478-17-9 |url=https://anepe.cl/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/LIBRO-ANEPE-18.pdf |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |url=https://revistaei.uchile.cl/index.php/REI/article/view/15737/16207 |author1=Óscar Pinochet de la Barra |title=Negociaciones antárticas de Chile en un mundo cambiante |journal=Revista Estudios Internacionales |date=1987 |volume=20 |issue=Nº78 |page=210 |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author1=Rodrigo José Santibáñez Lehuedé |title=Antártica sudamericana: Chile y Argentina una meta en común (Primera mitad del siglo XX) |url=https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/184158/Antartica_sudamericana_Chile_y_Argentina_una_meta_en_comun_%28primera_mitad_del_siglo_XX%29.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y |publisher=Universidad de Chile |location=Santiago de Chile |language=es |date=June 2021}}</ref> There were minor agreements between both countries in 1952 and 1953.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Clarín |first1=Redacción |title=Argentina y Chile se unen para frenar a Gran Bretaña en la Antártida |url=https://www.clarin.com/ediciones-anteriores/argentina-chile-unen-frenar-gran-bretana-antartida_0_BJRZKJJJCtx.html |website=Clarín |language=es |date=18 November 2007}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Escalada |first1=Mariana |last2=Ronconi Etiquetas |first2=Agustín |title=Chile e Inglaterra excluyen a Argentina del acuerdo antártico hasta 2022 |url=https://www.eldisenso.com/politica/chile-e-inglaterra-excluyen-argentina-del-acuerdo-antartico-hasta-2022/ |website=El Disenso |language=es-AR |date=31 January 2017}}</ref>

On 7 April 1948, Decree No. 9905 placed the administration of the Argentine Antarctic Sector with the maritime Governor of the National Territory of Tierra del Fuego. On 9 June 1948, by her Decree No. 17040, the ''"Antarctic and Malvinas Division"'' was created under authority of the [[Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Worship|Argentine Ministry of Foreign Affairs]].{{Citation needed|date=June 2023}}

On 28 February 1957, Decree-Law No. 2129 repeated the claim between the meridians at 25° and 74° West and the parallel 60° South latitude. The claimed territory is overlapped in part by the British ([[20th meridian west|20°W]] to [[80th meridian west|80°W]]) and the earlier Chilean ([[53rd meridian west|53°W]] to [[90th meridian west|90°W]]) claims.

=== Presence in the Antarctic Peninsula since 1951 ===
[[File:Almirante brown antarctica (5879477835).jpg|thumb|290px|The [[Brown Station]] established 6 April 1951 is the first Argentinian base on the Antarctic mainland.]]
The first continental Argentine base in Antarctica, the [[Almirante Brown Base|Almirante Brown Naval detachment]], was opened in 1951.<ref>{{cite web |title=Estación Brown (Almirante Brown) |url=https://oceanwide-expeditions.com/es/destacados/experiencias/base-brown |website=oceanwide-expeditions.com |language=es}}</ref> The following year, the Esperanza Naval detachment (now [[Esperanza Base]]) opened. On 1 February 1952, while building the Esperanza Base at Hope Bay, the [[Hope Bay incident|first shooting war in Antarctica]] occurred when an Argentine team fired a machine gun over the heads of a civil team of British [[Falkland Islands Dependencies Survey]] workers and forced them to withdraw to their ship, the ''John Biscoe''.<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 February 1952 |title=FALKLAND ISLANDS DEPENDENCIES (HOPE BAY INCIDENT) |url=https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1952/feb/20/falkland-islands-dependencies-hope-bay |website=[[Hansard|Parliamentary Debates (Hansard)]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=1952 – Quinta Invasión Inglesa a la Antártida Argentina |url=http://www.irizar.org/invasionesinglesas42.html |website=irizar.org |language=es}}</ref>

On 17 January 1953, at [[Deception Island]], the Refugio Teniente Lasala (a hut and a tent) was opened by the staff of the Argentine ship ARA ''Chiriguano''<!--, becoming a Sergeant and a corporal of the Argentina Navy-->. On 15 February 32 [[Royal Marines]] of the British frigate HMS ''Snipe'', armed with Sten submachine guns, rifles, and tear gas, captured two Argentine sailors. The Argentine refuge and a nearby uninhabited Chilean hut were destroyed. The Argentine sailors were delivered on a British vessel to South Georgia on 18 February.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Indice General de Histarmar |url=http://www.histarmar.com.ar/HYAMNEWS/HyamNews2004/HY01-04%20Churchill.htm |access-date=4 January 2024 |website=www.histarmar.com.ar |language=es}}</ref> A British detachment stayed three months on the island while the frigate patrolled the waters until April.

On 4 May 1955, the [[United Kingdom]] filed two lawsuits against Argentina and Chile respectively, in the [[International Court of Justice]] in [[The Hague]] to declare Argentine and Chilean claims on the Antarctic and sub-Antarctic areas invalid. On 15 July 1955, the Chilean Government rejected the jurisdiction of the Court in that case, and on 1 August, the Argentine Government did the same.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.icj-cij.org/docket/index.php?p1=3&p2=3&k=cc&case=26&code=uka&p3=0 |title=Antarctica (United Kingdom v. Argentina) |access-date=3 May 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150909211103/http://www.icj-cij.org/docket/index.php?p1=3&p2=3&code=uka&case=26&k=cc&p3=0 |archive-date=9 September 2015 |url-status=dead  }}</ref>

=== Signing of the Antarctic Treaty ===
[[File:Tractor Expedición Polar Argentina 1965, Flag Day 2006, Rosario, Argentina.jpg|thumb|A caterpillar tractor (a [[Tucker Sno-Cat]]) from the first Argentine expedition that reached the [[South Pole]] in 1965.]]
[[File:Orcadas Base.jpg|thumb|left|The [[Orcadas Base]] in 1996]]
On 1 December 1959, the [[Antarctic Treaty]] was signed by Argentina, Australia, Belgium, Chile, France, Japan, New Zealand, Norway, South Africa, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States, entering into force on 23 June 1961.

In the 1960s, Argentina, with its fleet, began conducting ecological tourist cruises to Antarctica. At the same time, the Argentine state-owned [[Aerolíneas Argentinas]] began passenger flights between Ushuaia and [[Sydney]], landing at [[Marambio Base]]. Between the mid-1960s and the first half of the 1970s, Argentina launched rockets from its Antarctic bases. These rockets were designed and built in Argentina and possessed meteorological instrumentation and radiation sensors.

[[Operación 90]] was the first Argentine ground expedition to the [[South Pole]]. It was conducted in 1965 by ten soldiers of the [[Argentine Army]] under then-Colonel Jorge Edgard Leal. The operation was named for the target 90 degree South [[latitude]] point (the [[South Pole|geographic South Pole]]).<ref>[http://www.marambio.aq/index1.htm "Operación 90: La llegada al Polo Sur por vía terrestre"] by Jorge Edgard Leal {{in lang|es}}<br />(Click on '''Llegada al Polo (terrestre)''' on the column at the left)</ref>

[[File:ArgentineAntarcticEsperanza.JPG|thumb|[[Esperanza Base]] on [[Trinity Peninsula]] in 2012.]]
On 8 April 1970, the Governor of Tierra del Fuego issued Decree No. 149 creating four new departments, among them the Argentine Antarctic Sector Department.

In 1977, the Esperanza Base was chosen to house Argentine families that spent their winters in Antarctica. The first director of the Argentine Antarctic Institute, general [[Hernán Pujato]], was the forerunner of the installation of the [[Fortín Sargento Cabral]]. On 13 August 1954, he proposed that the Argentine government create homes near [[Esperanza Base]] to populate with families with a goal of strengthening Argentine rights in that part of Antarctica. After finishing the construction of the houses, the Fortín Sargento Cabral was established on 17 February 1978 with five houses.

The first documented person born in Antarctica was the Argentine [[Emilio Palma]] at Esperanza Base in 1978. His baptism in the Catholic chapel on 7 January 1978 was the first documented baptism on the continent.

On 18 December 2012, the [[Foreign & Commonwealth Office]] of the United Kingdom announced that the southern part of the British Antarctic Territory (which included a portion of Argentine Antarctica) would be named ''[[Queen Elizabeth Land]]'' in honor of [[Elizabeth II|Queen Elizabeth II]]. Argentina opposed Britain's decision to rename the area.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-20822582 |title=Argentina angry after Antarctic territory named after Queen |publisher=BBC News  |date=22 December 2012 |access-date=22 December 2012}}</ref>

In 2013, the Argentine Defense Ministry announced that [[Petrel Base]] would become a permanent base by 2015. The base will have an airport and facilities to transfer passengers and cargo.<ref>{{Cite web |title=La Radio Pública presente en la Campaña Antártica de verano |url=http://www.radionacional.com.ar/?p=44031#.VMPRxeaF28Q.facebook |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Argentina convirtiendo la Base transitoria Petrel en Antártida en permanente |url=https://es.mercopress.com/2015/04/28/argentina-convirtiendo-la-base-transitoria-petrel-en-antartida-en-permanente |access-date=4 January 2024 |website=MercoPress |language=es}}</ref>

On 27 October 2017, by law of the Legislature of the Province of Tierra del Fuego, Antarctica and South Atlantic Islands, the territorial decree 149/70 that created the department with the name of department Argentine Antarctic Sector was repealed, renaming it as department Argentine Antarctica and including within it the South Orkney Islands, which until then were part of the department South Atlantic Islands.

== Geography ==
[[File:Antártida Argentina.jpg|thumb|left|Watercraft in [[Hope Bay, Antarctica]].]]
{{see also|Geography of the British Antarctic Territory|Geography of Antarctica}}

The geographic structure of Argentine Antarctica shares some features with [[Patagonia]], located to the north. The highest peaks are located at the south of the [[Antarctic Peninsula]], which has islands and [[archipelago]]s nearby. The land is under an [[ice sheet]].

===Climate===
{{Main|Climate of Antarctica}}

The climate of the region ranges from a [[subpolar climate]] in the north to a [[polar climate]] in the south.<ref>{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161111155312/http://eviet.cancilleria.gov.ar/en/node/28732 |archive-date=11 November 2016 |url=http://eviet.cancilleria.gov.ar/en/node/28732 |title=Regiones argentinas |publisher=Embassy of Argentina in the Socialist Republic of Vietnam |language=es |access-date=11 November 2016 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The region has an extremely cold climate, with mean temperatures below {{convert|0|C|F|0}} with frost and snowfall occurring throughout the year.<ref name=obioclima>{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161111152114/http://obio.ambiente.gob.ar/ecorregiones/antartida |archive-date=11 November 2016 |url=http://obio.ambiente.gob.ar/ecorregiones/antartida |title=Antártida |publisher=Ministerio de Ambiente y Desarrollo Sustentable de la Nación |language=es |access-date=11 November 2016 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In general, there are two different climatic zones found within the region: a glacial climate in the interior and an oceanic one in the Antarctic Peninsula and adjacent islands.<ref name=obioclima/> The glacial climate found in the interior is dominated by continental ice sheets and [[glaciers]], while in the Antarctic Peninsula and its adjacent islands, the climate is characterized by very strong winds, particularly in winter.<ref name=obioclima/> In particular, the Antarctic Peninsula experiences strong cold winds and blizzards.<ref name=izaguirre>{{cite web |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161214165131/http://www.fvsa.org.ar/situacionambiental/Eco.Antartida%20e%20Islas.pdf |archive-date= 14 December 2016 |last1=Izaguirre |first1=Irina |last2=Sánchez |first2=Rodolfo |url=http://www.fvsa.org.ar/situacionambiental/Eco.Antartida%20e%20Islas.pdf |title=Situación Ambiental en La Antártida e Islas del Atlántico Sur |language=es |access-date=14 December 2016}}</ref> In the interior of the continent, the climate is colder and drier due to the higher latitude, altitude, and strong continental influences.<ref name=izaguirre/> Mean annual temperatures range from {{convert|-10|to|-20|C|F|0}} in the Antarctic Peninsula to {{convert|-30|to|-50|C|F|0}} in the interior.<ref name=izaguirre/> Temperatures are always low in the region; during the [[polar night]] in winter, temperatures drop to {{convert|-42|C|F|0}}.<ref name=obioclima/> In the warmest month, mean temperatures are usually below {{convert|0|C|F|0}}.<ref name=obioclima/> Coastal areas have mean temperatures in the warmest month at around freezing.<ref name=izaguirre/> Precipitation mainly falls as snow.<ref name=obioclima/> Due to the ice sheets and glaciers covering most of the region and the severity of the climate, the flora is sparse and limited only to coastal areas.<ref name=obioclima/>

The flag of Tierra del Fuego, which includes Argentine Antarctica, was adopted in 1999 as the result of a competition.<ref>Bordeleau, Andre G. ''Flags of the Night Sky: When Astronomy Meets National Pride''. Springer Publications: 2011. p. 118.</ref> It is a diagonal bicolor flag of sky blue and orange with a white [[albatross]] dividing the flag diagonally and the [[Southern Cross]] constellation in the blue upper half. The orange represents the fire in the province's name, while the blue represents the sky and reflects the color of the national flag.<ref>Bordeleau, Andre G. ''Flags of the Night Sky: When Astronomy Meets National Pride''. Springer Publications: 2011. p. 119.</ref>

== Argentine bases ==
[[File:Argentine Antarctica bases map.jpg|thumb|upright=1.6|Argentine bases on Antarctica (permanent bases in red).]]
[[File:Q5ARAAlmiranteIrizar.jpg|thumb|right|The icebreaker ''Almirante Irizar'', the principal supply line for Argentine bases in Antarctica since 1978.]]
Esperanza and Marambio are the largest Argentine bases, together holding 70 buildings, with a combined occupancy rate ranging from roughly 110 in winter to 250 in summer. Orcadas Base on the [[South Orkney Islands]] is the world's first Antarctic base, operating continuously since 1903. The southernmost Argentine permanent base is Belgrano II, at around 77 latitudinal degrees south. The southernmost summer base is Sobral, {{convert|1450|km|mi|0|abbr=on}} from Belgrano II.

The bases are supplied by ships as well as by [[C-130 Hercules]] and [[De Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter|DHC-6 Twin Otter]] aircraft. 

The [[Argentine Navy]] devotes resources to its annual Antarctic missions. In 2022/23, the auxiliary vessels ''Puerto Argentino'' and ''Estrecho San Carlos'' were tasked to the mission, along with the icebreaker [[ARA Almirante Irízar|''Almirante Irízar'']].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.defensa.com/argentina/armada-argentina-prepara-para-campana-antartica-2022-23 |title =La Armada Argentina se prepara para la campaña antártica 2022/23 |date=11 November 2022 |last=Piñeiro |first=Luis |website=Defensa.com}}</ref> These vessels were supported by Argentine Navy [[Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King|Sea King helicopters]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=14 March 2023 |title=El "Irízar" arribó a Ushuaia concluyendo la segunda etapa de la Campaña Antártica de Verano |url=https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/el-irizar-arribo-ushuaia-concluyendo-la-segunda-etapa-de-la-campana-antartica-de-verano-0 |website=Argentine Ministry of Defence |language=es}}</ref> 

As of mid-2023, Argentina was in discussions with Finland to build a new polar logistics vessel, the "ARC 133". The ship is to be a Polar 4 class vessel (year-round operation in thick first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions) and is envisaged for completion by the latter 2020s.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dinatale |first=Martin |date=4 May 2023 |title=Fuerzas Armadas: Argentina y Finlandia avanzan en la construcción de un buque para la Antártida |url=https://www.cronista.com/economia-politica/fuerzas-armadas-argentina-y-finlandia-avanzan-en-la-construccion-de-un-buque-para-la-antartida/ |website=El Cronista |language=es}}</ref>

Argentina's claim to the [[Antarctic Peninsula]] overlaps with the [[Antártica Chilena Province|Antarctic claims of Chile]], [[53rd meridian west|53°W]] to [[90th meridian west|90°W]], and the [[British Antarctic Territory|UK claims]], [[20th meridian west|20°W]] to [[80th meridian west|80°W]].

Currently, there are no attempts by Argentina or any other country to enforce territorial claims in Antarctica. See [[List of Antarctic territorial claims]].

None of these claims have widespread international recognition.

== Demographics ==
[[File:21.09.2011 Conectar Igualdad en la Antártida Argentina Artartida2 (6186143757).jpg|thumb|Children, teenagers and teachers of the school of Esperanza Base]]
{{see also|Demographics of Antarctica}}

In 1978, the first Antarctic baby was born in the [[Fortín Sargento Cabral]] at the Esperanza Base. He was named [[Emilio Palma]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://webecoist.com/2011/02/15/born-freezing-meet-antarcticas-first-citizen/ |title=Emilio Marcos Palma at 30 |access-date=3 May 2015 |archive-date=17 October 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111017131750/http://webecoist.com/2011/02/15/born-freezing-meet-antarcticas-first-citizen/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> María de las Nieves Delgado was the first Antarctic girl, born on 27 March 1978 at Esperanza Base. By 1980, six more children were born at the base: Rubén Eduardo de Carli (21 September 1979), Francisco Javier Sosa (21 September 1979), Silvina Analía Arnouil (14 January 1980), José Manuel Valladares Solís (24 January 1980), Lucas Daniel Posse (4 February 1980) and María Sol Cosenza (3 May 1983).<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20180614121431/http://www.marambio.aq/appaa/GACETILLA2014.pdf 60 años de Base Esperanza] – Fundación Marambio</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Espíritu Güemesiano en la Antártida |url=https://www.marambio.aq/anecdotas/espirituguemesiano.html |access-date=4 January 2024 |website=www.marambio.aq |language=es}}</ref> The base has an Argentine civil registry office where there have been births and weddings.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.argentina.gob.ar/ejercito|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20100117124815/http://www.ejercito.mil.ar/antartico/RegistroCivil.html|url-status=dead|title=Ejército Argentino|date=22 December 2017|archivedate=17 January 2010|website=Argentina.gob.ar}}</ref>

In 1991, there were 142 permanent residents, including 19 minors. These residents are families that live in Antarctica or scientists that have lived there for more than two years. There were 121 men and 21 women that lived mostly near Esperanza and other bases. As of 1998–1999, Argentine Antarctica had a winter population of 165.{{citation needed|date=May 2016}}{{needs update|reason=very outdated population info|date=May 2016}}
