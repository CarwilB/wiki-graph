'''Teúl de González Ortega Municipality''' ({{IPA|es|teˈul de ɣonˈsales oɾˈteɣa|lang|Teúl de González Ortega.ogg}}) is a town and [[Municipalities of Zacatecas|municipality]] located in the south of the Mexican state of [[Zacatecas]], between the state capital of [[Zacatecas, Zacatecas|Zacatecas]] and the city of [[Guadalajara]]. Unlike most of the rest of the state, its economy has been based on agriculture and livestock, rather than mining, and it is noted for its production of agave and mezcal. The town has been named a [[Pueblo Mágico]] to promote a tourism industry.

==Geography==
The municipality extends over {{convert|886|km2|abbr=on}}, and borders the municipalities of [[Tepechitlán]], [[Mezquital del Oro]], [[Garcia de la Cadena]] and [[Florencia de Benito Juárez]] with the state of [[Jalisco]] to the south and the state of [[Nayarit]] to the west. Outside the seat, other important communities include Ignacio Allende with 1,800 residents, Huitzila with 800 and www.milpillas.com with 500. In addition there are sixteen other communities with representation in the municipal government.<ref name=encmuc>{{cite web |title=Teúl de González Ortea |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Deleaciones de México Estado de Zacatecas |publisher=INAFED |location=Mexico |language=Spanish |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/32047a.html |accessdate=October 13, 2013 |archive-date=October 14, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131014174010/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/32047a.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

The municipality has mountains, small mesas, plain and rolling hills with an average altitude of {{convert|1940|m|sp=us}} above sea level.<ref name=encmuc/> The Cerro de Teul is marked by a large cross.<ref name=guia>{{cite journal  |title=Pueblos Mágicos Zacatecas  |journal= México Desconocido |location=Mexico City |pages=59–72 |language=Spanish |issn=1870-9397 |date=July 2013 }}</ref>
===Hydrography===
The area is part of the [[Lerma River]] basin. The municipal seat is at the juncture of two small rivers called the Rio Grande and La Aticuata. Together they form the Tlaltenango River. After joining other rivers to the Lerma, the area drains into the Pacific. Another important river in the area is the Patitos. The municipality contains one dam called the Manuel Caloca with a capacity of {{convert|5,000,000|m3|abbr=on}} and another called the Ignacio Allende with a capacity of {{convert|1,000,000|m3|abbr=on}}. The rest of the municipality has an abundance of fresh water springs which provide most of the potable water.<ref name=encmuc/>
===Climate===
The climate is between semi warm and temperate with an average annual precipitation of between {{convert|750|and|800|mm|abbr=on}} with most rain falling in the summer, especially July. The north is drier than the south.  The coldest months are December and January with lows down to {{convert|11|C}} with occasional freezing. The warmest months are May and June with highs up to {{convert|40|C}}.<ref name=encmuc/>
===Flora and fauna===
Much of the area is forested with species such as oak, holm oak, royal pine and “palo colorado”.  Fauna includes coyotes, wildcats, white-tailed deer, squirrels, rabbits, various spiders and scorpions, rattlesnakes, coral snakes, and other snakes, eagles, quail, owls, storks, ducks and various other birds.<ref name=encmuc/>

==History==
The name Teúl comes from the [[Chichimeca]] phrase teulinchan, which means “dwellings of the gods.” After [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|the Conquest]], its name became San Juan Bautista de Teúl, prepending the [[San Juan Bautista (disambiguation)|<!--[[WP:INTDAB]]-->name of its patron saint]]. In 1935, the suffix of “de Gonzaléz Ortega” was added. [[Jesús González Ortega]], a governor of Zacatecas and ally of [[Benito Juárez]], lived in the town as a boy, then returned later in life to marry.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=guia/>

Little is known of the early inhabitants of the area, but settlements probably go back as far as the first century of the Common Era, according to archeological evidence such as tombs. The [[Caxcan]]s arrived here around the middle of the 7th century, taking control of the valley from the [[Nahua peoples|Nahuas]] and the Techueshes. They remained the dominant group until the arrival of the Spanish.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=guia/>

In 1536 [[Nuño de Guzmán]] sent [[Pedro Almíndez Chirino]] to the area then followed himself shortly after. Within months the indigenous of the area were conquered and Captain Juan Delgado along with Frair Juan Pacheco founded the modern town. At that pre-Hispanic ceremonial center the friar held a mass in honor of [[John the Baptist]], who became the town’s patron saint. One probable reason for the selection of this saint by the Spanish was the area’s abundance of fresh water springs. At this mass, they baptized the area chieftain and a sister, giving them the names of Juan and Catalina. In 1574, a monastery was founded here.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=guia/>

Unlike other small towns in Zacatecas, it was never a mining town, with its economy based on livestock and the making of [[mezcal]] from agave. During the colonial period into the 19th century, much of the agriculture was organized into [[hacienda]]s, but these were dismantled with the [[Mexican Revolution]] and the [[Cristero War]], when the town itself was nearly burnt to the ground.<ref name=guia/>

The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Guadalupe was the original parish church constructed in the late 17th century.<ref name=encmuc/>

The town was formally recognized in 1714 by the Real Audiencia de la Nueva Galicia.<ref name=encmuc/>

At the time of Independence, Teul was a dependency of [[Tlaltenango de Sánchez Román Municipality|Tlaltenango]], but was separated into its own municipality in 1844. This status was reaffirmed in 1935.<ref name=encmuc/>

In 1986, the municipality adopted a seal on the occasion of the 450th anniversary of the town’s founding.<ref name=encmuc/>

==Socioeconomics and culture==
The economy of the municipality is based on agriculture and livestock. Eighty eight percent of the farmland is worked only during the rainy season, with the rest irrigated. The main crop is corn, followed by beans then sorghum and [[fava beans]]. Livestock is mostly cattle followed by pigs.<ref name=encmuc/>

Another important crop is [[agave]], which is used to make mezcal. This liquor is still made by hand, using wood barrels and pits to roast the agave hearts.<ref name=promueve>{{cite news |title=Promueve Canacozac al Teúl de González Ortega como destino turístico |publisher=NTR Noticias |location=Zacatecas |date=March 18, 2013 |language=Spanish |url=http://ntrzacatecas.com/2013/03/18/promueve-canacozac-al-teul-de-gonzalez-ortega-como-destino-turistico/ |accessdate=October 13, 2013 |archive-date=October 16, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131016074433/http://ntrzacatecas.com/2013/03/18/promueve-canacozac-al-teul-de-gonzalez-ortega-como-destino-turistico/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The municipality has seven registered distilleries making mezcal including El Caxcán, Don Aurelio Lamas and Zacatecano.<ref name=guia/> In addition to the straight mezcal, cream variations in flavors such as strawberry, piña colada, coconut, nut and coffee are produced.<ref name=promueve/>

The area remains relatively poor, with many families having one or more members living in the United States to work.<ref name=encmuc/> The state and municipality have worked to develop a tourism industry for Teúl as well, including its naming as a Pueblo Mágico.<ref name=promueve/>

This rural area maintains many of its traditional religious and secular celebrations, mostly related to local saints.<ref name=guia/> Unlike many places in Mexico, the streets are clear of street vendors, so street food is generally available only during festivals. Local specialties include gordita de horno, a sweet case baked on an oak leaf, and gorditas de arriero, a savory dish filled with refried beans flavored with [[Chili peppers|chile de arbol]]. Other popular dishes include pipian rojo, tamales with meat and nopal, tortitas de guachal (made with nopales and eggs), and chuales, a kind of baked [[tamale]] with beans and piloncillo and birria.<ref name=guia/>

Handcrafts include wooden masks, most often used for a traditional dance called Los Morenos in honor of the Holy Cross.  Another craft is ceramics, which include pots, plates, storage jars and ladles. They are made with a sand colored clay and glazed.<ref name=guia/>
