The '''Sifontes Municipality''' is one of the 11 [[Municipalities of Venezuela|municipalities]] ([[municipio]]s) that makes up the [[Venezuela]]n state of [[Bolívar (state)|Bolívar]] and, according to the 2011 census by the National Institute of Statistics of Venezuela, the municipality has a population of 50,082.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.geohive.com/cntry/venezuela_ext.aspx |title=Venezuela: administrative units, extended |access-date=2015-11-08 |archive-date=2016-03-15 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160315201648/http://www.geohive.com/cntry/venezuela_ext.aspx |url-status=dead }}</ref> The town of [[Tumeremo]] was the [[County seat|shire town]] of the Sifontes Municipality.<ref name=autogenerated1>http://www.ine.gob.ve/secciones/division/Bolivar.zip {{dead link|date=May 2018 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==Demographics==
The Sifontes Municipality, according to a 2007 population estimate by the National Institute of Statistics of Venezuela, has a population of 40,042 (up from 34,277 in 2000).  This amounts to 2.6% of the state's population.<ref>http://www.ine.gob.ve/sintesisestadistica/estados/bolivar/cuadros/Poblacion5.xls {{dead link|date=May 2018 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>  The municipality's population density is {{convert|1.64|PD/sqkm}}.<ref>http://www.ine.gob.ve/sintesisestadistica/estados/bolivar/cuadros/Poblacion4.xls {{dead link|date=May 2018 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==History==
Its first settlers were Guayan Indians and Kamaracotos, coming from the savanna of the Divina Pastora and Tupuquen located to the left margin of the river [[Yuruari]]. They fed on hunting, fishing and agriculture. Tumeremo was founded on January 26, [[1788]] under the name of "Mission of Our Lady of Bethlehem of Tumeremo" by the Capuchin monks of Catalonia, among them: Fray Mariano de Perafita, Fray Bonaventura de Carrocera and Fray Tomas de Santa Eugenia. Tumeremo means "Painted Snake", in the dialect of the first Indians who inhabited these lands.

During the Campaign of Guayana, General [[Manuel Piar]] fights against the realists to seize the missions of the kingdom of Spain. After releasing Tumeremo, the Spanish survivors were imprisoned and sentenced to death. From there, the city was a strategic site and barracks for the patriot soldiers commanded by Simón Bolívar. From 1830 many indigenous and other populations began to emerge around Tumeremo.
[[File:General Domingo Antonio Sifontes.jpg|thumb|General Domingo Sinfontes]]
In [[1894]], a group of British settlers who came from the [[British Guyana]] tried to take part of the present municipal territory, before which General Domingo Antonio Sifontes founded in March 2nd of the same year the population of [[El Dorado]] in which it established a military position, then expelled the settlers from the area. General Sifontes became a local hero in recognition of the defense of Venezuelan sovereignty. The name of the municipality takes its name in honor to the general [[Antonio Domingo Sifontes]], but the jurisdiction abbreviated its name li

In 2013, 60% of all malaria cases in Venezuela occurred in Sifontes.<ref name="veconomist" >{{cite news|author=J.R.A.|title=Why malaria is spreading in Venezuela|url=https://www.economist.com/blogs/economist-explains/2017/10/economist-explains-1|newspaper=[[The Economist]]|date=12 October 2017}}</ref>

== Fauna, flora and vegetation ==
The municipality of Sifontes lies in the [[Guianan Highlands moist forests]] ecoregion, and the major habitat type is [[tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests]].<ref name='TNCecoreg'>''The Nature Conservancy'' (2009) TNC terrestrial ecoregions, [http://maps.tnc.org/files/metadata/TerrEcos.xml] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170203181055/http://maps.tnc.org/files/metadata/TerrEcos.xml |date=2017-02-03 }}. Based on: Olson, D. M. and E. Dinerstein. 2002. The Global 200: Priority ecoregions for global conservation. (PDF file) Annals of the Missouri Botanical Garden 89:125-126.</ref>

Common birds include the Orange-winged Parrot (''[[Amazona amazonica]]''), Painted Parakeet (''[[Pyrrhura picta]]''), White Bellbird (''[[Procnias alba]]''), Helmeted Pygmy-Tyrant (''[[Lophotriccus galeatus]]''), Fiery-shouldered Parakeet (''[[Pyrrhura egregia]]''), Blue-headed Parrot (''[[Pionus menstruus]]'').<ref name='NeoMapas'>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer-Paris|first1=José Rafael|last2=Rodríguez|first2=Jon Paul|last3=Good|first3=Tatjana C.|last4=Sánchez-Mercado|first4=Ada|last5=Rodríguez-Clark|first5=Kathryn M.|last6=Rodríguez|first6=Gustavo A.|last7=Solís|first7=Ángel|title=Systematic, large‐scale national biodiversity surveys: NeoMaps as a model for tropical regions|journal= Diversity and Distributions| date=2013|volume=19|issue=2|pages=215–231|doi=10.1111/ddi.12012|s2cid=84741871 |url=https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235954117}}</ref> At least 58 species of [[dung beetles]] and 73 of [[butterflies]] have been reported in systematic surveys. Among the most common species of dung beetles are ''[[Canthon triangularis]]'', ''[[Canthon variabilis]]'', ''[[Deltochilum guildingii]]'', ''[[Eurysternus caribaeus]]'', ''[[Onthophagus rubrescens]]'', ''[[Sylvicanthon bridarollii]]'', and the emblematic ''[[Oxysternon festivum]]''.<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer‐Paris|first1=José R.|last2=Rodríguez|first2=Jon Paul|last3=Sánchez‐Mercado|first3=Ada|title=Optimización del muestreo de invertebrados tropicales: Un ejemplo con escarabajos coprófagos (Coleoptera: Scarabaeinae) en Venezuela|journal=Revista de Biología Tropical|date=2013|volume=61|issue=1|pages=89–100|doi=10.15517/rbt.v61i1.10941|url=https://revistas.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/rbt/article/download/10941/10321|doi-access=free}}</ref><ref name='IndicativeResponse'>{{cite journal|last1=Ferrer-Paris|first1=José R.|last2=Lozano|first2=Cecilia|last3=Cardozo-Urdaneta|first3=Arlene|last4=Thomas Cabianca|first4=Arianna|title=Indicative response of ''Oxysternon festivum'' Linné (Coleoptera: Scarabaidae) to vegetation condition in the basin of the Orinoco river, Venezuela |journal=Journal of Insect Conservation |date=2016 |volume=20 |issue=3 |pages=527–538 |doi=10.1007/s10841-016-9886-6|s2cid=17263106}}</ref>
