'''Porto Acre''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|ˈpoʁtu ˈakɾi}}), originally called '''Puerto Alonso''' during Bolivian rule, is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the northeast of the [[States of Brazil|Brazilian state]] of [[Acre (state)|Acre]]. 
The economy is mainly based on agriculture. As of 2020 the municipality had an estimated population of 18,824.

==Geography==

Porto Acre is in the eastern part of the state of Acre, to the north of the [[BR-364]] highway and Rio Branco. The municipal seat is on the left (west) bank of the Acre River, a tributary of the [[Purus River]] that runs through the municipality from south to north. The town can be accessed by boat along the Acre River or by land along the AC-010 highway which runs north from Rio Branco for {{convert|78|km}}.
The distance from Rio Branco as the crow flies is {{convert|51|km}}.
Porto Acre is limited to the north by the state of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], to the west and south by the municipality of [[Bujari]], to the south by Rio Branco and to the east by the municipality of [[Senador Guiomard]].{{sfn|Porto Acre – State of Acre}}

==History==

The settlement is founded in Jan 3rd, 1899 by {{ill|José Paravicini|ES}} while Acre was under [[Bolivia|Bolivian]] rule, and was named "Puerto Alonso" in honor of the then Bolivian president [[Severo Fernández|Severo Fernández Alonso Caballero]].{{Citation needed|reason=It was stated on his personal page, but after further review the cited literature there was no sign of evidence|date=November 2023}}

That year, Luis Gálvez Rodríguez de Arias expelled the Bolivians and declared the [[Independent State of Acre]].{{sfn|História de Porto Acre – Municipality}}
He renamed Puerto Alonso to Cidade do Acre and made it the headquarters of the new state. The state created a flag, minted its own currency, drafted a constitution, appointed ministers and formed an army.{{sfn|História de Porto Acre – Municipality}} However, Galves was deposed and arrested after a few months and the Bolivians regained control.{{sfn|Porto Acre - AC – Brasil Channel}}

At the start of 1902 [[José Plácido de Castro]] accepted an invitation to lead a revolt against Bolivia. He took a boat up the Acre River, ostensibly to conduct land surveys in the south but in practice carrying arms and ammunition. 
He stopped off briefly in Puerto Alonso to meet the Bolivian governor Lino Romero and evaluate his future opponent.{{sfn|Pedraja|2006|p=63}} In the south he organised the rubber tappers and other inhabitants for the revolt.{{sfn|Sergio Lamarão|Inoã Carvalho Urbinati|p=3}}
He then returned to Brazil, bypassing Puerto Alonso to avoid alerting the Bolivians.{{sfn|Pedraja|2006|p=63}}

Plácido captured [[Xapuri]] on 6 August 1902 and next day proclaimed that Acre was independent.{{sfn|Pedraja|2006|p=64}}
On 14 October 1902 the rebels captured some of the outer defences of Puerto Alonso and the armed Bolivian launch ''Rio Afua'', which they renamed the ''Independencia''.{{sfn|Scheina|2003|loc=PP30}}
Plácido de Castro surrounded the town on 8 January 1903, and launched an attack on 15 January 1903 that soon petered out.{{sfn|Pedraja|2006|p=67}}
His men started to dig zigzag trenches to the Bolivian lines. Eventually, with his forces exhausted and almost out of ammunition, Romero surrendered on 24 January 1903.{{sfn|Pedraja|2006|p=68}}

==Recent years==

The municipality of Porto Acre gained autonomy on 28 April 1992 when it was separated from the [[Rio Branco, Acre|Rio Branco]] municipality. The first mayor and councillors took office of 1 January 1993.{{sfn|Porto Acre – State of Acre}}
According to the 2010 census the municipality has a population of 14,806.
The municipality has an area of {{convert|2604.725|km2}} and a population density of 5.71 inhabitants per square kilometre.{{sfn|História de Porto Acre – Municipality}}
The local football teams include Valência Porto Acre, Porto Acre AC, and AA Águia.{{citation needed|date=November 2025}}

Porto Acre's economy is based on extraction of rubber, Brazil nut and [[wood processing]], small-scale livestock, horticultural products and general commerce. The Acre River is still used for movement of goods and people. During the dry season its fertile banks are used to grow watermelon, banana, vegetables, cassava and other food crops.{{sfn|Porto Acre – State of Acre}}
There are nine settlement projects with a total  area of {{convert|126,700|ha}}, or 44.1% of the municipal area.{{sfn|Porto Acre – State of Acre}}
