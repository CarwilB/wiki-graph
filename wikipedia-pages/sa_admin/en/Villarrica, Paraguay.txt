'''Villarrica del Espíritu Santo''' ({{IPA|es|ˌbiʎaˈrika ðel esˈpiɾitu ˈsanto}}), is a city in [[Paraguay]]. Located in the middle of the Eastern Region of Paraguay, it is the capital and most populous city of [[Guairá Department]].

Vilarrica is located to the center west of Guairá and is bordered to the east by the towns of [[Mbocayaty]], [[Yataity]], [[Colonia Independencia|Independencia]], [[Ñumí]] and [[General Eugenio A. Garay|Garay]], to the west by [[Itapé]] and [[Félix Pérez Cardozo, Paraguay|Félix Pérez]], and to the south by [[San Salvador, Paraguay|San Salvador]] and [[Borja, Paraguay|Borja]].

Founded in 1570 by [[Seville|Sevillian]] [[Ruy Diaz Melgarejo|Ruy Díaz de Melgarejo]], it is one of the oldest settlements in Paraguay. Nevertheless, the original foundation site was located within the extinct [[Spanish Empire|Spanish province]] of Guayra in what is now the state of [[Paraná (state)|Parana]] in [[Brazil]]. The residents of Villarrica were forced to relocate seven times during a timespan of more than 100 years. These relocations were mainly caused by the repeated attacks from the neighbouring [[Portuguese Empire|Portuguese]] [[bandeirantes]] but also by political and economic issues. They were finally settled in its current location near Ybytyruzu hills in 1683. As a result, the city got the nickname of "Wandering City".<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |title=La ciudad andariega |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/comercial/499-aniversario-villarrica/la-ciudad-andariega-1814163.html |language=es}}</ref>

After the end of the [[Paraguayan War]] in 1870, Villarrica experienced rapid economic and demographic growth, becoming the second most important city in Paraguay after the capital [[Asunción|Asuncion]]. The city continues to be a well-known cultural hub today.

== History ==

=== Background ===
[[File:Capitán Ruy Diaz de Melgarejo.jpg|left|thumb|Illustration of Captain Melgarejo, the founder of Villarrica.]]
The [[Treaty of Tordesillas]] signed by [[Crown of Castile|Castile]] and [[Portuguese Empire|Portugal]] set ambiguous borders between the two empires in [[South America]]. Portugal took an aggressive and expansionist policy and usually disrespected the agreements of the treaty. Meanwhile, the [[Spaniards|Spanish]] in [[Asunción|Asuncion]] decided to settle more towns to the east closer to the Portuguese border to reinforce their territorial claims.

In the 1550s, what is now Paraguay was a province of the [[Governorate of New Andalusia]] and was ruled by Captain [[Domingo Martínez de Irala|Domingo Irala]]. Under the command of Irala, the city of ''Ontiveros'' was founded to continue the conquest of the lands to the east of [[Asunción|Asuncion]] known as the ''lands of Guayrá''. In 1557, [[Ruy Diaz Melgarejo]] founded another city in Guayrá named ''Ciudad Real'' with the purpose of reducing the abuse that the Portuguese were inflicting on the [[Guaraní people|Cario Indigenous tribe]] but also due to the strategic location as a trail towards [[Colonial Brazil|Brazil]]. A couple of years later, the interim Governor Felipe de Cáceres has a personal dispute with Melgarejo and drove him out of the office of governor of the [[Guayrá|province of Guayra]]. Cáceres appointed an [[Andalusia]]n [[conquistador]] named Alonso Riquelme to replace Melgarejo. When Riquelme arrived in Ciudad Real, Melgarejo had him arrested, incarcerated and later banished to a cabin forty [[League (unit)|leagues]] away from the town.

=== Settlement ===
[[File:Igrejinha Vila Rica do Espírito Santo - Fênix, Paraná 05.jpg|left|thumb|Small chapel at the original settlement of Villarrica. Currently a state park in the town of [[Paraná (state)|Fenix]], Brazil. ]]
In the 16th century, [[Ruy Diaz Melgarejo|Melgarejo]] departed from Ciudad Real with 40 men and 53 horses towards the East where he expected to find mines of [[gold]] and [[silver]]. After arriving in the lands of an [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indian]] [[Tribal chief|chief]] named Cuaracybera (Shining-Sun), he settled a new town on May 14, 1570, and named it ''Villa Rica del Espíritu Santo'' (Wealthy village of the Holy Ghost) because of the belief that there were precious metals in the area and because the date was near the [[Catholic Church|Catholic]] festivity of the [[Holy Spirit in Christianity|Holy Ghost]].

In 1592, explorer Ruy Díaz de Guzman moved the city 100&nbsp;km east. In 1599, he moved it again, this time near the Mboteitei River, 100 leagues from Asunción. After long years of peaceful existence, the city was invaded in 1632 by the [[bandeirantes]], and after 4 years of pilgrimage the population settled near [[Mbaracayú Forest Nature Reserve|Mbaracayu hills]]. Two years later, Governor Valderrama determined to situate it in the fields of Yaru. In 1642, the population moved to [[Curuguaty]]. In 1678, the population settled close to the Tobatyry River, in a place called [[Coronel Oviedo|"Espinillo"]]. The soil was not good for agriculture, so many people decided to do a recognition expedition of the area beyond the [[Tebicuarymí River|Tebicuarymi River.]]

On the other side of the river, they found fertile land and several [[stream]]s near the Ybytyruzú hills, so they requested the Spanish Governor's authorization to move the city again. On May 25, 1682, the Governor issued the license to settle in Ybyturuzú to await the [[Monarchy of Spain|King]]'s official approval. On May 14, 1701 King [[Philip V of Spain|Philip V]] issues the [[Real cédula|Royal Order]] that approves the permanent settlement of the city in Ybyturuzú. This date became the ultimate date of foundation. The [[Franciscans|Franciscan missionaries]] helped the town's stabilization, founding the Guaraní [[Spanish missions in the Americas|Mission]] of [[Itapé]]. As a result, the city got the nickname of "Wandering City".<ref name=":0" />

=== 1600s to 20th century ===
[[File:Saliendo de Iglesia de Villarrica.jpg|left|thumb|Women coming out of the Church of Villarrica (1872) French Illustration ''Le Paraguay'']]
The [[Franciscans|Franciscan friars]] in an effort of educating the population and gaining souls to the Catholic faith ran a convent that worked as an elementary and secondary school from the 1600s until 1818 when [[José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia|Gaspar de Francia]] the dictator of Paraguay had it shut as part of his policy of retaining absolute control over the country. Schools were reopened in 1844 after Francia died only to be shut again in 1865 with the declaration of the Paraguayan War. When the war was over in 1870, Villarrica had a very small population due to the large casualties. In 1888, the railway got to Villarrica, starting rapid demographic and economic growth.

From 1892 to the beginning of the 1900s, the city received an important influx of [[Italians]], [[French people|French]], [[Spaniards]], [[Germans]], [[Ashkenazi Jews]], [[Argentines]], [[Uruguayans]] and a lesser number of [[Lebanese people|Lebanese]], [[Croats]] and [[Greeks]]. These immigrants helped develop industry, culture and education, making Villarrica the second most important city after the capital, [[Asunción|Asuncion]]. However, the two Paraguayan civil wars had a deep effect in the city, especially the so called [[Paraguayan Civil War (1947)|Revolution of 1947]] when the Paraguayan military took over and cause the migration of thousands of Paraguayans and an important part of Villarrica intellectual people spread across the country or made it to Argentina.  A similar phenomenon occurred from 1954 to 1989 with the political persecutions during the regime of [[Alfredo Stroessner]]. By the end of the 20th century Villarrica had lost its prominent position within the country to newer settlements like [[Ciudad del Este]], [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnacion]] and [[Luque]].

== Geography ==
The district of Villarrica is located 20&nbsp;km to the west of Ybytyruzu hills, 172&nbsp;km to the east of [[Asunción]], 237&nbsp;km to the north of [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnación]] and 217&nbsp;km to the west of [[Ciudad del Este]]. Within the [[Guairá Department|department of Guairá]], it is located in the central-western region at a height of about 200m above sea level. The municipal territory has roughly a shape of a triangle and has an area of 247&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

=== Geology and Orography ===
The soil is composed of [[sandstone]]s interbedded with [[shale]]s and [[Oolite|oolitic]] [[Lime (material)|calcareous formations]]. In the alluvial plains of the Tebicuarymí River, there seems to be [[Quaternary]] sediments.

The soils of the urban area are mainly sandy hills, with gentle slopes in the northern area and slightly steeper slopes in the south. Good drainage and no rockiness are also observed. Both in the urban area of Villarrica and in 14 de Mayo area (on the way to Ñumí) [[Iron|ferruginous]] [[laterite]]s can be foundin thick layers that support small topographic elevations. Due to the heavy deforestation, primitive tree species can be observed almost exclusively in areas of difficult access such as banks of streams. A great variety of grasses and palm trees grow In its terrain of undulating plains,. Grass can be natural grasslands or cultivated in order to feed cattle. The current extension of the palm groves is small but is increasing due to [[soil degradation]].

=== Hydrography ===
[[File:Karumbey2.jpg|thumb|Carumbey stream in San Miguel neighborhood|250x250px]]
Due to the particularly isolated location of Villarrica, no river reaches its municipal territory. Instead, the city is irrigated by a network of tributary [[stream]]s of the river Tebicuarymi. The type of drainage is centrifugal and good internal drainage in the urban area. There are springs that drain to the south, to a stream named Guarapo and this one drains to the Bolas Cuá stream basin.

To the north there is a stream named Bobo that serves as the border with the town of [[Mbocayaty]]. Almost parallel to the Bobo stream, there is one of its tributaries called Mitay. Mitay stream which serves as the limit between Villarrica and [[Yataity]]. To the west there is a tributary of Mitay stream named Kaundy. Kaundy is the border between Villarrica and [[Félix Pérez Cardozo, Paraguay|Félix Pérez]]. Both Kaundy and Bobo are tributaries of Mitay stream. Mitay feeds into Tebicuarymi river to the northwest.

Three streams in the urban area named Itacua, Carumbey and Ycuaangua feed into another stream named Yhu. Yhu is a tributary of Bolas Cua and its confluence is located in the hamlet of Caazapami to the southwest of the municipality. In the southeastern end of the municipal territory, in an area named 14 de Mayo, there is a stream named Remansito which is the limit between Villarrica and the town of [[Ñumí]]. The Remansito runs slightly to the southwest. The Ovie stream is located southeast of the municipality between the areas 14 de Mayo and Lemos and runs towards the southwest until it merges with the Remansito to form a new stream named Bolas Cua. The Bolas Cuá stream initially runs to the southwest where it serves as the border between Villarrica and [[San Salvador, Paraguay|San Salvador]]. However, when crossing the area  named Yhacamí (San Salvador) it takes the northwest and once again borders the territory of Villarrica (Caazapamí) where it serves as the border between Villarrica and the town of [[Borja, Paraguay|Borja]]. Bolas Cua ultimately feeds into Tebicuarymi river.

=== Native Species ===
[[File:Cachorro do mato (Cerdocyon thous).JPG|thumb|[[Crab-eating fox|Cerdocyon thous]] can be spotted in the rural areas of Villarrica. Known by the locals by its [[Guarani language|guarani]] name ''Aguara´i'']]
Native animals that can be observed within the territory of the city are the following:

* Birds: [[Great kiskadee|Pitangus sulphuratus]], [[Plush-crested jay|Cyanochorax chrysops]], [[Bare-throated bellbird|Procnias nudicollis]], [[Nyctibius griseus]], [[Crimson-crested woodpecker|Campephilus melanoleucos]], [[Brazilian merganser|Mergus octosetaceus]], [[Southern lapwing|Vanellus chilensis]] and [[Vinaceous-breasted amazon|Amazona vinacea]].
* Reptiles: [[Hydrodynastes gigas]], [[Yellow anaconda|Eunectes notaeus]] and [[Dracaena paraguayensis]]. 
* Mammals: [[Giant anteater|Myrmecophaga tridactyla]], [[Crab-eating fox|Cerdocyon thous]], [[White-eared opossum|Didelphis albiventris]] and [[Southern little yellow-eared bat|Vampyressa pusilla]].

The most vulnerable animal species are the [[giant otter]] and the [[Mantled hawk|mantle hawk]]. The apex-predator, the [[jaguar]] is currently extinct in Villarrica and western Guaira.<ref>{{Cite book |url=https://issuu.com/arquiucap/docs/atlas_guaira_compressed |title=Atlas Regional Guaira Vol.3 |year=2022 |pages=32 |language=es |trans-title=Guaira Regional Atlas Volume 3}}</ref>

As for native trees one can frequently see [[Handroanthus impetiginosus]] (pink lapacho), [[Handroanthus serratifolius]] (yellow lapacho), [[Jacaranda mimosifolia]] (blue jacaranda), [[Peltophorum dubium]] (ibirapita), [[Cordia trichotoma]] and [[Enterolobium contortisiliquum]] (pacara earpod tree).

Some native fruits include the [[Passiflora edulis|passion fruit]] (Passiflora edulis), [[Malpighia emarginata|acerola cherry]] (Malpighia emarginata), [[brazilian cherry]] (Eugenia uniflora), [[Acrocomia aculeata|macaw palm fruit]] (Acrocomia aculeata), and a fruit called ''aratiku'' ([[Annona nutans]]).

===Climate===
Villarica has a [[humid subtropical climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] ''Cfa'') bordering on a [[tropical rainforest climate]] (''Af'') and a [[tropical monsoon climate]] (''Am''). The climate, in general is benign and healthy, with an average temperature of {{convert|21|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}. In summer it can reach {{convert|38|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}, while in winter it can drop to {{convert|1|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}. The rainiest months are January and November.

== Demographics ==
The influx of [[European immigration to the Americas|European]] immigrants the city received in the late 19th century constituted a significant part of the ethnic makeup of Villarrica until the second Paraguayan Civil War in 1947. After this conflict the number of European-descended people started to shrink rapidly due to emigration to [[Asunción|Asuncion]] and [[Argentina]], decreasing birth rates among the Europeans, increasing birth-rates of the locals, and mixed-unions with the already majority population with significant [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and [[Black people|Sub-Saharian African]] ancestry. By the end of the 20th century, the racial makeup of Villarrica was virtually identical to the one of [[Paraguay]], having over 90% of racially mixed residents who prefer to speak [[Jopara]] and [[Guarani language|Guarani]] over plain [[Spanish language|Spanish]].

==Economy==

The most important economic activities of Villarrica are [[Counterfeit consumer good|commerce of Brazilian and Argentine goods]], services, and family-owned industries like sugar processing, furniture making and shoemaking. Commerce has relevance to the economy of the city because the universities attract students from other towns in [[Guairá Department|Guaira]], [[Caaguazú Department|Caaguazu]], [[Caazapá Department|Caazapa]], and [[Brazil]]. As for agriculture, it has a decreasing importance. Only 10% of the total sugar cane cultivation of Guaira comes from Villarrica.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Empresas y negocios. Villarrica |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/empresas-y-negocios/villarrica-625183.html}}</ref>

==Infrastructure==
[[File:PotreroIsla.jpg|thumb|250x250px|23km road from Villarrica to [[Itapé]] at Potrero Isla hamlet.]]
Villarrica is communicated to other towns in Paraguay mainly through [[Route 8 (Paraguay)|Route No. 8 “Blas Garay”]]. To the North of Paraguay through [[San Estanislao|Santani]] which starts at the junction of [[Route 2 (Paraguay)|Route PY02]] and [[Route 7 (Paraguay)|Route PY07]] and is paved until the district of [[Coronel Bogado]] in [[Itapúa Department]]. There is also another route that joins with Route PY07 that goes through the towns of [[Mbocayaty]], [[Natalicio Talavera, Paraguay|Natalicio]] and [[Capitán Mauricio José Troche|Troche]]. Moreover, [[Route 10 (Paraguay)|Route PY10]] communicates Villarrica with the city of [[Paraguarí|Paraguari]]. There are two paved roads that communicate Villarrica with the town of [[Itapé]]. The first one(18&nbsp;km) departs from Lomas Valentinas neighborhood and the other one (23&nbsp;km) departs from Santa Librada neighborhood. The road that communicates Villarrica directly to neighboring town of [[Félix Pérez Cardozo, Paraguay|Felix Perez]] is still a [[dirt road]].

Water service is provided by the government owned company ESSAP. Electricity is provided by the local private company CLYFSA. Villarrica is the only municipality in Paraguay that gets its electricity service from a private company.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Compañia de Luz y Fuerza SA Villarrica |url=https://clyfsa.com/about.php |language=es}}</ref> The service for Internet access is provided by the companies [[Millicom|Tigo]], [[Telecom Argentina|Personal]] and the state-owned COPACO.

Some banks that have offices in Villarrica are [[Itaú Unibanco|Itau Bank]] from Brazil, [[Banco de la Nación Argentina|Banco Nacion]] from Argentina and BNF, GNB and Continental from Paraguay. Some [[Cooperative banking|cooperative institutions]] are COOPEDUC (founded in Villarrica), COOMECIPAR (nation-wide health personnel) and Cooperativa Universitaria.

==Education==

The three main universities in Villarrica are [[Universidad Nacional de Asunción|UNA]], [[Universidad Católica "Nuestra Señora de la Asunción"|UCV]], and UNVES. UNA and UNVES are government-owned and UCV is private and run by the [[Catholic Church]]. The Medical School of the Catholic University in Villarrica is the second oldest in Paraguay after that of the UNA in Asuncion.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Proceso de creación de la Universidad Católica «Nuestra Señora de la Asunción» y de su primera sede en el interior del país, el Campus Guairá |url=https://guaira.uc.edu.py/uccg/historia/}}</ref>

Among the prominent highschools in Villarrica there are Don Bosco and Maria Auxiliadora both run by the [[Salesians of Don Bosco|Salesian order]] of the Catholic Church; Centro Educativo Internacional run by [[Nondenominational Christianity|non-denominational Christians]]; Buscio School which is private and secular and CRENT, government-owned and secular.

It has numerous educational institutions and schools, such as the National School, the Ortiz Guerreo School, Technical and Vocational National School, the Diosesano Seminar, the Agriculture Regional School, Women Professional Institute, and the Pío XII School of Arts and Crafts.

==Culture==
[[File:Porvenir.jpg|thumb|Club Porvenir Guaireño building in Villarrica|250x250px]]
Villarrica was regarded as the second most important city in Paraguay due to its dynamic cultural activity until the first half of the 20th century. The first Paraguayan [[physician]], the first Paraguayan [[poet]] and the first Paraguayan [[Neurosurgery|neurosurgeon]] all hailed from Villarrica.  Many renowned Paraguayan artists such as poet [[Manuel Ortiz Guerrero]], classical composer Diego Sanchez Haase and guitar player [[Cayo Sila Godoy]] were born and raised there. Several other Villarrica natives have been very relevant especially in [[education]], [[journalism]] and [[literature]].  In addition, many towns in Paraguay such as [[Caaguazú District|Caaguazu]], [[Mbocayaty]], [[Colonia Independencia|Independencia]] and even Union in [[San Pedro Department, Paraguay|San Pedro Department]] are said to have been settled by pioneers and families from Villarrica.

The oldest social club in Paraguay named [[Club Porvenir Guaireño]] was founded in Villarrica in 1888 and the first private electricity company named CLYFSA was also founded in the city in 1953. The second most popular political party in Paraguay, the [[Authentic Radical Liberal Party|PLRA]] was founded in Villarrica in 1886 by Villarrica-born Antonio Taboada.

Villarrica has its own [[Association football|soccer]] club named [[Guaireña F.C.]] and has competed in the First Division of Paraguayan Soccer Tournaments. Other popular sports in the city are [[Rugby football|rugby]], [[basketball]], [[cycling]] and [[tennis]].

=== Music ===
In 1992, the Asociacion Filarmonica Guaireña gives classes of [[violin]], [[viola]], [[cello]], bass, [[flute]] and [[Music theory|musical theory]]. They have also formed the Ensamble ''Lumine Antiqua'' which is the only Paraguayan band out of Asuncion that plays [[Baroque music|barroque music]]. The Department of Culture of the Municipality has its own choir named ''Sensus Aeternus'' and they organize Choir Festivals with other choirs from neighboring towns and Argentina. Privately owned institutes give classes of [[guitar]], [[piano]], [[drum]]s and [[Paraguayan harp]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=La Asociacion Filarmonica Guaireña culmina temporada |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/artes-espectaculos/la-asociacion-filarmonica-guairena-culmina-temporada-1661230.html |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Comenzaron las clases de musica en la Filamonica Guaireña |url=https://www.ultimahora.com/comenzaron-clases-musica-la-filarmonica-guairena-n885889 |language=es}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
Despite being overwhelmingly influenced by Catholicism, Villarrica presents increasing religious diversity. Villarrica is the seat of [[Pentecostalism|Iglesia Belen Pentecostal Libre del Paraguay]]. Other religious minorities present in the city include [[Baptists]], [[Jehovah's Witnesses]], and [[Mormons|Latter-Day Saints]].

Among the Catholic orders the most relevant are the [[Jesuits]], the [[Franciscans]], and the [[Salesians of Don Bosco|Salesians]].

==Tourism==
[[File:Lagunaycuapyta.jpg|thumb|250x250px|Ycua Pytá ([[Guarani language|guarani]] ''red lagoon'') in Ortiz Guerrero Park]]
One of the most visited places in Villarrica is Ortiz Guerrero Park. The park is located in the Centro neighborhood on Dr Bottrel and Coronel Oviedo streets. There is a walkway around the lagoon and the tomb of the poet [[Manuel Ortiz Guerrero]]. Animals like [[capybara]]s, [[duck]]s, [[mallard]]s, [[Neotropic cormorant|cormorants]], [[Goose|geese]], and an endangered species of rodent called [[agouti]] can be observed and fed in the park.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Avalos |first=Carlos |date=2021 |title=Lo que los carpinchos de Villarrica tienen para enseñarles a los de Argentina |url=https://www.abc.com.py/nacionales/2021/09/06/carpinchos-pobladores-dilectos-de-villarrica/ |language=es}}</ref>

Another interesting spot is Fermin Lopez Museum which is set on a house built in 1842. It keeps personal belongings of [[Paraguayan War]] heroes like Fermín López and poet [[Natalicio Talavera (poet)|Natalicio Talavera]]; arms and ammunitions from the [[Chaco War]], a collection of old [[Paraguayan guaraní|Paraguayan coins and bills]], as well as other artifacts such as indigenous weapons, old furniture, old photographs of the city and pieces of religious art.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Yubi |first=Javier |date=2009 |title=Cuna de la Cultura |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/abc-revista/cuna-de-la-cultura-53418.html |work=ABC Color |language=es}}</ref>

Other suggested activities are to attend [[Saint John's Eve|St. John´s Eve]] celebration in June at the Old Railway Station, to go for a walk or cycling at Guaira Park, attend concerts at the Cathedral or hike at the hills in Ytororó hamlet.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2023 |title=Vibrante San Juan en Villarrica |url=https://www.ultimahora.com/vibrante-san-juan-en-villarrica |language=es}}</ref>
