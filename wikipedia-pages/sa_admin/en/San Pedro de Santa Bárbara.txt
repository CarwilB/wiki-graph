'''San Pedro''', is one of the six [[Districts of Costa Rica|districts]] of the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]] canton, in the [[Heredia Province|Heredia]] province of [[Costa Rica]]. It is located {{convert|2.5|km|sp=us}} west of Santa Bárbara and is officially a part of the province of [[Heredia Province|Heredia]]. The elevation is approximately {{convert|1080|m|sp=us}} above sea-level.<ref name=muni>{{cite web|title=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara: Datos Generales|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|publisher=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|accessdate=27 April 2014|language=es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429045049/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="divadm">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |lang=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref>

==Toponymy==
Named after [[Saint Peter]].

==History==
When [[Alajuela Province]] was founded in 1782, the region of San Pedro was mentioned in the founding documents as Targúás (alternatively noted as Targuas).<ref name=RMHV>{{cite news|last=Hernández Vargas|first=Rose Marie|title=Reseña histórica de Santa Bárbara de Heredia|url=http://www.uned.ac.cr/extension/index.php/extension-en-accion/identidad-cultural/105-resena-historica-de-santa-barbara-de-heredia|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=UNED: Extensión en acción|date=6 November 2013|language=Es}}</ref> The population was fewer than one hundred residents. By 1813, the  In 1819, Juan Pablo Lara bought land that was registered in Alajuela, and San Pedro's name first appears in those documents.<ref name=centenario>{{cite book|title=Centenario del cantón Santa Bárbara de Heredia|year=1982|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/notas/seo.php/item.49/centenario-del-canton-santa-barbara-heredia.html}}{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

By 1846, San Pedro was the most populous of Santa Bárbara's current districts, with 1,000 residents. In order to pray to [[Saint Barbara|Santa Bárbara]], San Pedro's residents met with other settlers in the area at a central location close to the current site of Santa Bárbara's plaza, hence the name of the city.<ref name=centenario /> In 1852, resident Pedro Saborío suggested that a [[Catholic church]] be constructed. San Pedro was mentioned as one of the possible locations; however, because the surrounding communities all contributed, the location of the church was kept near the central meeting place in Santa Bárbara.

Between 1924 and 1928, the first running water pipes were installed in parts of San Pedro.<ref name="RHC">{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón: [http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133 Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara de Heredia, Costa Rica - Reseña Histórica del Cantón]|language=es}}</ref>

==Economy and politics==
[[File:San Pedro de Santa Bárbara.jpg|thumb|San Pedro de Santa Bárbara]]
San Pedro is largely an agricultural community whose main crop continues to be [[Coffee production in Costa Rica|coffee]]. In 1973, San Pedro was producing nearly 700,000&nbsp;kilograms of coffee per year with a population of just over 1,500 people.<ref name=centenario />

There are various commercial businesses in San Pedro, including small corner shops, restaurants, several mechanics shops, an outdoor market, and an indoor soccer field.<ref name="sbd">Santa Bárbara Digital: [http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/ Directorio Comercial : Santa Barbara de Heredia] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140521031649/http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/ |date=2014-05-21 }}, Retrieved: May 20, 2014</ref> For more than 70 years, the "Pulpería el Taconazo" ("Stingy Man's Corner Store") has been in San Pedro, making it one of the oldest corner shops in the entire canton.<ref>{{cite news|last=Morera Cabezas|first=José Manuel|title=Pulpería "EL TACONAZO"|url=http://piscuilofoto.nirudia.com/18160|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=Piscuilofoto|date=8 April 2011|language=es}}</ref> An EBAIS, [[Costa Rican Social Security Fund|Equipo Básico de Atención Integral en Salud (Basic Medical Care Center)]], is located in San Pedro, serving residents and non-residents of the district.<ref name="sbd" />

Among other goals, in 2010 the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara canton]] planned to shore up San Pedro's infrastructure.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|accessdate=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> There are plans to improve the district's water quality and distribution with new sewers and protected wells. Additionally, the canton had plans to strengthen the outdoor farmers' market and improve access to [[microbusiness]], especially for farmers and women.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

[[Colombia]]n ex-senator and presidential candidate Álvaro Leyva Durán lived in San Pedro for six years. Having fled his native Colombia, Leyva sought political asylum in Costa Rica. While in San Pedro, his assets were seized. He returned to Colombia after being absolved by the [[Supreme Court of Colombia]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Ibarra|first=Vilma|title=Álvaro Leyva habla desde Bogotá para Costa Rica|url=http://www.poder.cr/inicio/2011/07/alvaro-leyva-habla-desde-bogota-para-costa-rica/|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=Poder Costa Rica|date=June 2008|language=es}}</ref>

==Culture==
The ''Típico Copey'' is known throughout the [[San José, Costa Rica|Central Valley]] for its live music, typically held on Friday and Saturday evenings, as well as Sunday afternoons. It is named for the [[Clusia rosea|copey tree]] located on the south side of the central church.<ref>{{cite news|last=Rodriguez|first=Camilo|title=Sobrevuelo|url=http://www.tiazelmira.com/v08/2013/06/21/salvemos-los-salones-de-baile/|accessdate=27 April 2014|newspaper=Tiazelmira.com|date=21 June 2013|language=Es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429061842/http://www.tiazelmira.com/v08/2013/06/21/salvemos-los-salones-de-baile/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

San Pedro is mentioned in the poem "Poesía de Turno" ("Festival Poem") of 1900, as being a place of gathering.<ref>{{cite book|last=Molina Jiménez|first=Iván|title=Poes ía de turno: una base de datos sobre religiosidad popular en la Costa Rica de inicios del siglo XX|publisher=Presentation by author to El Museo Histórico Cultural Juan Santamaría|location=Alajuela, Costa Rica|url=http://www.historia.fcs.ucr.ac.cr/bases/poesia/p-turno.pdf|accessdate=28 April 2014|language=Es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429045514/http://www.historia.fcs.ucr.ac.cr/bases/poesia/p-turno.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> In addition, La Fundación Cultural Antidio Cabal (Antidio Cabal Cultural Foundation), which promotes the works of the Spanish poet and philosopher [[Antidio Cabal]], is headquartered in San Pedro.<ref>{{cite news|title=La Fundación Cultural Antidio Cabal|url=http://www.fundacinculturalantidiocabal.com/index.html|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=La Fundación Cultural Antidio Cabal|language=es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429052358/http://www.fundacinculturalantidiocabal.com/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Henchoz|first=Erika|title=La razón siente y el corazón piensa|url=https://www.nacion.com/archivo/razon-siente-corazon-piensa_0_1203879680.html|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=La Nacion (Costa Rica)|date=26 June 2011|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref>

San Pedro is also home to '''Pastor''', a [[Coca-Cola]] drinking [[Brahman (cattle)|Brahman cow]].<ref name=pastor>{{cite news|last1=Mora|first1=Emilia|title=Agua genera polémica en cantón de Santa Bárbara|url=http://wvw.nacion.com/ln_ee/2003/diciembre/20/pais20.html|accessdate=8 June 2014|publisher=La Nacion|date=20 December 2003|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> '''Pastor''', who is valued at over $3,000[[United States dollar|USD]], once drank 14 Coca-Cola bottles during a horse show in Alajuela.<ref name=pastor />

Every year between late June and early July, San Pedro hosts a festival in honor of Saint Peter.<ref name=mejenga>{{cite news|last=RODRÍGUEZ|first=JUAN JOSÉ|title=Gordos contra flacos: Mejenga de peso|url=http://wfnode01.nacion.com/2013-06-16/RevistaDominical/Gordos-contra-flacos--Mejenga-de-peso.aspx?subsection=Articulos|accessdate=20 May 2014|date=16 June 2013|location=SAN JOSÉ|language=Es}}</ref> The festival, which draws large crowds to the district, usually includes [[amusement ride]]s, musical concerts, horse shows, and fireworks, as well as traditional [[Catholic]] activities, such as [[Mass (liturgy)|liturgical masses]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Comienzan las fiestas patronales en Heredia|url=https://costarica.pordescubrir.com/comienzan-fiestas-patronales-heredia.html|accessdate=26 May 2014|date=25 June 2011|language=es}}</ref>

==Education==
San Pedro is home to the Elisa Soto Jiménez Elementary School. The school was one of at least four schools in 2013 where parents threatened to block access to teachers who they deemed unqualified.<ref>{{cite news|last=Torres|first=Jason|title=Padres de familia impidieron acceso a cuatro centros educativos|url=http://www.crhoy.com/padres-de-familia-impidieron-acceso-a-cuatro-centros-educativos/|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=Costa Rica Hoy|date=6 February 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> Costa Rican former President [[Laura Chinchilla]] (2010-2014) made a trip to the Elisa Soto Jiménez Elementary School in 2011.<ref>{{cite news|last=Sánchez|first=Eduardo|title=Año nuevo, buenas nuevas|url=https://soloheredia.blogspot.com/2011/01/ano-nuevo-buenas-nuevas.html|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=Solo Heredia|date=6 January 2011|language=es}}</ref>

Students from San Pedro usually attend Liceo de Santa Bárbara High School,<ref name="MEPSB">Ministerio de Educación Pública: [http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara Santa Bárbara | Ministerio de Educación Pública] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140520215916/http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara |date=2014-05-20 }}, accessdate: 5/20/2014</ref> but may also go to the high schools in [[Alajuela]]. There are several private schools within the area that students may also attend.<ref>Sector Educación Costarricense: Preescolares, Primarias, Secundarias, Universidades en Costa Rica: [http://www.actualidadeducativa.com/directorioPPS.php Actualidad Educativa - El sitio del sector educación costarricense: Preescolares, Primarias, Secundarias, Universidades en Costa Rica] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091107024535/http://www.actualidadeducativa.com/directorioPPS.php |date=2009-11-07 }}, Retrieved: 20 May 2014</ref>

==Sports==
[[File:Soccer Game (11).jpg|thumb|Soccer game in San Pedro de Santa Bárbara Plaza]]
Asociación Deportiva Fraternidad de Santa Bárbara was a San Pedro [[Football (soccer)|soccer]] club that existed in San Pedro beginning in 1947.<ref name="LADFSP">La Asociación Deportiva Fraternidad de San Pedro: [http://a-d-fraternidad-de-santa-barbara.globered.com/index.asp A.D. Fraternidad de Santa Bárbara], Retrieved: 20 May 2014</ref><ref>AD Barbareña: [http://desarrollodelftbolenelcantnbarbareo.blogspot.com/p/ad-barbarena.html AD Barbareña: Asociación Deportiva Santa Bárbara], Retrieved: 20 May 2014</ref> It disappeared in the late 1990s. During its time, Asociación Deportiva Fraternidad played in the second and third divisions of [[Costa Rican Football Federation|FEDEFUT]].<ref name="LADFSP" /> One of the first players was San Pedro's own Pedro Sánchez Soto.

San Pedro is known nationally for its "Gordos y Flacos" ("Fatties versus Skinnies") soccer game.<ref name=gordos>{{cite news|title=Gordos contra flacos: Mejenga de peso|url=http://wfnode01.nacion.com/2013-06-16/RevistaDominical/Gordos-contra-flacos--Mejenga-de-peso.aspx?subsection=Articulos|accessdate=27 April 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=21 June 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> The annual game is held at the end of San Pedro's festivals, usually in late June. The first game was organized in 1975 by local Asociación Deportiva Fraternidad supporters. Because organizers wanted to raise money for the church, they decided against spending money on uniforms. Instead, to allow people to easily identify the two opposing teams, they came up with the idea of using fat players against skinny players.<ref name=gordos /> The game brings spectators and media from around the country to San Pedro.<ref name=enfrentan>{{cite news|last=Artavia|first=Betania|title=GORDOS Y FLACOS SE ENFRENTARON EN SAN PEDRO DE SANTA BÁRBARA|url=http://anteriores.diarioextra.com/2008/julio/09/nacionales16.php|accessdate=27 April 2014|newspaper=Diario Extra|date=9 July 2008|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref>

In 2013, San Pedro hosted the Fútbol Estudiantil Categoria B ("Category B Student Football") Championship. The games were played in the Central Plaza in front of the church.<ref>{{cite news|last=Castro R.|first=Franklin|title=Escuela Julio Acosta García de Paquera a la final de la categoría B del Fútbol Estudiantil Femenino|url=http://www.miprensacr.com/escuela-julio-acosta-garcia-de-paquera-a-la-final-de-la-categoria-b-del-futbol-estudiantil-femenino|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=Mi Prensa|date=7 June 2013|language=es}}</ref>

==Geography==
San Pedro has an area of {{formatnum:2.48}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=16 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200916045223/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:1080}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

San Pedro is located between [[Alajuela]] and [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]] along Route 123 (Calle Las Americas). San Pedro is in the [[Cordillera Central (Costa Rica)|Cordillera Central]]. Quebrada La Claudia, a small river, runs through San Pedro. Like many districts in Costa Rica, San Pedro is located within a potential disaster zone because of the possibility for landslides in some areas.<ref>{{cite news|title=Decretos por declaración de zonas catastradas|url=http://www.uecatastro.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=112:decretos-por-declaracion-de-zonas-catastradas&catid=1:latest-news|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=TerranTribune Inc|date=24 September 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref>
