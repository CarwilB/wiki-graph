'''Caxias do Sul''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Sul]]. Located in the [[List of mesoregions and microregions of Rio Grande do Sul|northeast of the state]] at an elevation of 817 meters, it is the largest city in the [[Serra Gaúcha]] region, the second most populous city in Rio Grande do Sul, surpassed only by the state capital [[Porto Alegre]], and the [[List of municipalities in Brazil by population|47th largest city]] in [[Brazil]].

Throughout its history, Caxias do Sul has been known as ''Campo dos Bugres'' (until 1877), ''Colônia de Caxias'' (1877–1884), and ''Santa Teresa de Caxias'' (1884–1890).<ref name="Histórico"/> The city was established where the [[Vacaria]] Plateau begins to break into numerous valleys, intersected by small waterways, resulting in a rugged [[topography]] in its southern part. The area was inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of Brazil|indigenous]] [[Kaingang]] people since time immemorial, but they were forcibly displaced by so-called "''bugreiros''"<ref>BUENO, E. ''Brasil'': uma história. Second revised edition. São Paulo: Ática, 2003. p. 267.</ref> to make way, in the late 19th century, for the [[Empire of Brazil]]'s decision to colonize the region with a European population. Consequently, thousands of [[immigrants]], primarily [[Italian immigration to Brazil|Italians]] from the [[Veneto]] region, but also including some [[German colonization in Rio Grande do Sul|Germans]], [[French people|French]], [[Spaniards]], and [[Polish people|Poles]], crossed the sea and ascended the [[Serra Gaúcha]], exploring an area that was still almost entirely uncharted.

After an initial period filled with hardships and deprivation, the immigrants succeeded in establishing a prosperous city, with an economy initially based on the exploitation of agricultural products, particularly [[grapes]] and [[wine]], whose success is reflected in the rapid expansion of [[commerce]] and industry in the first half of the 20th century. Concurrently, the rural and ethnic roots of the community began to lose relative importance in the economic and cultural landscape as [[urbanization]] progressed, an educated urban elite emerged, and the city became more integrated with the rest of Brazil. During the first government of [[Getúlio Vargas]], a significant crisis arose between the immigrants and their early descendants and the Brazilian milieu, as nationalism was emphasized, and cultural and political expressions of foreign ethnic origin were severely repressed. After [[World War II]], the situation was pacified, and Brazilians and foreigners began to work together for the common good.

Since then, the city has grown rapidly, multiplying its population, achieving high levels of economic and human development, and developing one of the most dynamic economies in Brazil, with a presence in numerous international markets. Its culture has also internationalized, with several higher education institutions and a significant artistic and cultural life in various forms, while simultaneously facing challenges typical of rapidly growing cities, such as [[pollution]], the emergence of [[slums]], and rising [[crime]].

== History ==
{{Main|Italian immigration to Brazil|Italian immigration in Rio Grande do Sul|History of Caxias do Sul}}

=== Origins and colonization ===
Before the arrival of Italian immigrants in the 19th century, the region was inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of Brazil|indigenous]] [[Kaingang]] people, hence its former name: Campo dos Bugres. [[Tropeiro|''Tropeiros'']] also passed through the area on their journeys between the south of the state and the central region of the country. The [[Society of Jesus|Jesuits]] attempted to establish some [[reductions]], though without success.<ref name="Nome">{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=2 |title=Nome |work=Prefeitura de Caxias do Sul |publisher=Municipal Government |access-date=2010-09-25 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=2 |archive-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/portal/index.php?q=destino&cod=4&opt=&id=17&bd=&fg=3 |title=Caminho Gaúcho dos Tropeiros |publisher=Secretariat of Tourism of Rio Grande do Sul |access-date=2010-09-25 |archive-date=2012-01-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120111122704/http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/portal/index.php?q=destino&cod=4&opt=&id=17&bd=&fg=3 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

In the second half of the 19th century, due to the [[Italian unification|war of Italian unification]], Italy faced severe social and economic crises, and impoverished farmers could no longer sustain themselves. During this period, the [[Empire of Brazil|Brazilian imperial government]] decided to colonize uninhabited areas in the south of the country, encouraging immigration from [[Italy]], following the success of a similar initiative with [[Germany|German]] settlers.<ref>Trento, Angelo. [http://books.google.com/books?id=ncc7WLAXlmQC&pg=PA513&dq=historia+de+caxias+do+sul&hl=pt-BR&ei=d52fTOWdEYSdlgfu2ensAg&sa=X&oi=book_result&ct=result&resnum=3&ved=0CDYQ6AEwAg#v=onepage&q&f=false ''Do outro lado do Atlântico: um século de imigração italiana no Brasil'']. pp. 77-82</ref> The chosen area, then known as Fundos de Nova Palmira, consisted of unclaimed lands bordered by the [[Campos de Cima da Serra]] to the north and the German-colonized valley region to the south.<ref>Giron, Loraine Slomp (2006) [https://web.archive.org/web/20120630070939/http://scholar.google.com/ ''Identidade, Trabalho e Turismo'']. IV SeminTUR – Seminário de Pesquisa em Turismo do MERCOSUL. Caxias do Sul: Universidade de Caxias do Sul, 7–8 July 2006. pp. 4-5</ref>
[[File:Caxias do Sul em torno de 1876.jpg|thumb|left|The original urban core of the city around 1876]]

In 1875, the first settlers, mostly from the [[Veneto]] region, arrived after enduring a grueling month-long crossing of the [[Atlantic Ocean]] on overcrowded ships, where deaths from disease and poor conditions were common. Initially, immigrants disembarked in [[Rio de Janeiro]], where they remained in quarantine at the Immigrants' House.<ref name="Nome"/><ref>Mello, Afrânio Franco de Oliveira. ''[https://web.archive.org/web/20120111192519/http://www.ihggi.org/migracao-italiana.php Imigração Italiana]''. Instituto Histórico, Geográfico e Genealógico de Itapetininga. Access 25 Sep 2010</ref> They then traveled by steamship to the South, arriving in [[Porto Alegre]], where they were directed to the former Porto Guimarães, now the municipality of [[São Sebastião do Caí]]. From there, they ascended the serra, traversing a largely wild region, until reaching their destination: the area now known as [[Nova Milano]]. From 1876, they moved to the so-called Sede Dante, the future site of Caxias do Sul, the administrative center of the colony, the first to be demarcated in the region. They were received in a wooden shed, hence the nickname "Barracão" also given to the small colonial seat. Subsequently, they were distributed to the rural plots assigned to them by the government. A year later, approximately 2,000 settlers were already in the area.<ref name="Nome"/><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://scholar.google.com/ |title=Giron (2006), pp. 3-5 |access-date=2021-05-14 |archive-date=2012-06-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120630070939/http://scholar.google.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>Machado, Maria Abel. ''Construindo uma Cidade: História de Caxias do Sul - 1875-1950''. Caxias do Sul: Maneco, 2001. pp. 50-51</ref> On April 11, 1877, the official name of the place became Colônia Caxias, in honor of the [[Duke of Caxias]].<ref name="Nome"/>

=== Development ===
Despite some official assistance, initial conditions were extremely challenging. Families were largely isolated from one another due to the absence or poor state of roads.<ref name="Sales">Sales, Fabiana de Lima. ''O desenvolvimento econômico de Caxias do Sul na perspectiva do acervo do Museu Municipal''. Universidade de Caxias do Sul, 2006. p. 2</ref> In addition to being unfamiliar with the still-wild environment, the settlers had primitive and scarce tools, and the agricultural techniques brought from Italy were not well-suited to the local climate and soil. Until houses were built and agriculture yielded results, sustenance came from gathering, hunting, and selling felled timber. Only the efforts of each family unit ensured their survival in the early days, and as survival depended on the number of workers, families tended to be large. As a result, Colônia Caxias grew rapidly, also due to the continuous influx of new immigrants, and soon established an economy based on subsistence. The main crops were [[wheat]], [[beans]], and [[maize]], followed by [[potatoes]], [[barley]], and [[rye]]. Fruit trees such as [[chestnut]], [[quince]], [[apple]], [[pear]], [[Citrus × sinensis|orange]], and [[cherry]] trees were introduced, and [[chicken]]s, [[cow]]s, [[goat]]s, [[pig]]s, [[sheep]], and [[rabbit]]s were raised. Additionally, there was some production of [[honey]] and [[silk]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://scholar.google.com/ |title=Giron (2006), pp. 10; 12 |access-date=2021-05-14 |archive-date=2012-06-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120630070939/http://scholar.google.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref> 
[[File:Colonos-caxienses.jpg|thumb|left|An agricultural fair in the 3ª Légua, rural area of Caxias, c. 1918]]

Nevertheless, commercial and industrial development soon emerged in the urban center, primarily focused on processing and distributing surplus agricultural products. General stores and small factories, such as those specializing in tinwork, carpentry, joinery, brickmaking, goldsmithing, blacksmithing, milling, saddlery, shoemaking, and tailoring, appeared. These businesses provided the emerging colony with self-sufficiency.<ref name="Giron2006">{{Cite web |url=http://scholar.google.com/ |title=Giron (2006), p. 11 |access-date=2021-05-14 |archive-date=2012-06-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120630070939/http://scholar.google.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Industrialização">{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=5 |title=Industrialização |work=Prefeitura de Caxias do Sul |publisher=Municipal Government |access-date=2010-09-25 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=5 |archive-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=live}}</ref> The result of this activity was evident in 1881 at the first Agroindustrial Fair, the origin of the modern [[Festa da Uva]], which centralized the small rural fairs and festivals.<ref>Museu e Arquivo Histórico Municipal ''Festa da Uva: Tradição & Mudança'', in ''Boletim Memória''. Caxias do Sul: Museu e Arquivo Histórico Municipal, January 1992, s/pp</ref> By 1883, the colony had 93 commercial establishments for a population of 7,359 inhabitants.<ref name="Industrialização"/>

On April 12, 1884, the colony lost its status as an Imperial Crown Colony and was annexed to the municipality of [[São Sebastião do Caí]] as its 5th District, by which time it had a population of 10,500. Its name changed to ''Freguesia de Santa Tereza de Caxias'', defining it as an administrative unit with its own [[parish]]. On October 30, 1886, the [[Câmara municipal|Municipal Chamber]] of São Sebastião do Caí established a municipal code for the ''[[freguesia]]'' of Caxias and appointed João Muratore as its first district administrator, though de facto administration remained in the hands of imperial officials, who viewed Italians with suspicion as administrators. It was not until June 28, 1890, that Italians gained leadership positions, starting a tradition that would take time to consolidate. On that date, the State President, having emancipated the district on June 20, elevating it to an autonomous municipality, appointed the first Governing Board of Caxias, composed of Italians Angelo Chitolina, Ernesto Marsiaj, and [[Salvador Sartori]].<ref>Ponzi, Luiz Carlos. [http://umpoucodehistoria.zip.net/arch2006-02-26_2006-03-04.html ''Vila Caxias'']. Historian's page. Access 25 Sep 2010</ref><ref name="O Município">{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=6 |title=O Município |publisher=Municipal Government |access-date=2010-09-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cidade/index.php?codigo=6 |archive-date=2010-09-24 |url-status=live}}</ref> By 1895, [[telegraph line]]s crossed the village, and in 1906, the first [[telephony|telephone]] network was inaugurated.<ref name="O Município"/>

[[File:Chegada do trem em Caxias do Sul - 1910.jpg|thumb|Inauguration of the railway on June 1, 1910, the date Caxias was elevated to city status]]
[[File:OperetaDonPasticcio-1922.jpg|thumb|Scene from the operetta ''Don Pasticcio'' at the Cine Theatro Apollo, 1922]]

On June 1, 1910, Caxias was granted [[city]] status, and on the same day, the first train arrived, connecting the region to the state capital. In 1913, electric lighting was installed.<ref name="Sales"/> Several economic cycles marked the municipality's evolution throughout the century. In its early decades, the initial subsistence-based economic model predominated.<ref name="Giron2006"/> Commerce was boosted by the railway and the network of trading posts established by Germans, but Italians soon created their own channels for product distribution, generating significant capital that enabled larger-scale industrialization. Caxias do Sul became a major commercial and later industrial hub due to the production of [[wine]], [[lard]], and [[flour]], with Porto Alegre as the main distribution point. Antônio Pierucinni and [[Abramo Eberle]], wine and [[metallurgy|metallurgical]] product traders, were pioneers in opening markets in [[São Paulo]].<ref>Sales, pp. 3-4</ref> The Merchants' Association, founded in 1901, played a crucial role in the region and emerged as the most significant social force after the Intendancy and the Municipal Council. It maintained strict and efficient control over commerce, wielded significant influence with the authorities, intervened beneficially in economic crises and local infrastructure issues, and acted in the area of social assistance. Despite some internal crises and disputes with authorities, the Association led all matters related to the interests of the producing classes, even when they were purely agricultural, as all productive activities in this phase converged in commerce.<ref>Machado, Maria Abel & Herédia, Vania Beatriz Merlotti. [http://www.ub.es/geocrit/sn-94-28.htm ''Associação dos Comerciantes: Uma Forma de Organização dos Imigrantes Europeus nas Colônias Agrícolas no Sul do Brasil'']. In ''Scripta Nova''. Revista Electrónica de Geografía y Ciencias Sociales. Universidad de Barcelona. No. 94 (28), August 1, 2001.</ref>
[[File:Caxias do Sul - Praça Dante - 1933.jpg|thumb|left|The [[St. Theresa's Cathedral, Caxias do Sul|Cathedral]] overlooking [[Dante Alighieri Square]], at the laying of the cornerstone for a monument to the [[Duke of Caxias]], 1933]]

Meanwhile, the local culture, while still rooted in the traditional family organization and its rural ties and close connection to the [[Catholic Church]], began a process of refinement and secularization. With the end of the immigrant settlement phase, an urban elite emerged that was more informed, educated, and able to dedicate itself to leisure and culture in less folkloric and more cosmopolitan patterns, benefiting the general population as well.<ref>Brandalise, Ernesto A. ''Paróquia Santa Teresa - Cem Anos de Fé e História (1884 - 1984)''. Caxias do Sul: Editora da UCS (EDUCS), 1985. pp. 14; 26-33; 107-124.</ref><ref>Dacanal, José Hildebrando. ''[https://web.archive.org/web/20090612141210/http://www.esteditora.com.br/textos/imigracao.htm A Imigração e a História do Rio Grande do Sul]''. EST Publicações Online. Access 25 Sep 2010</ref> Social-recreational clubs offering cultural programs emerged, such as the [[Clube Juvenil]] and [[Recreio da Juventude]], as well as amateur sports clubs such as [[Esporte Clube Juventude]] and Grêmio Esportivo Flamengo (now [[SER Caxias]]). Public [[education]] began to be structured, and in 1917, the first municipal [[library]] was created. The first theaters and cinema halls, such as Cinema Juvenil, Cine Theatro Apollo, and Cinema Central, brought the most up-to-date film productions of the time, provided space for traveling theater companies and local amateurs, and even hosted [[opera]]tic groups.<ref>Adami, João Spadari. ''História de Caxias do Sul''. Caxias do Sul: Escola Superior de Teologia São Lourenço de Brindes, 1981. Volume III. pp. 166-167</ref> Also noteworthy is the creation in 1937 of the Tobias Barreto de Menezes Cultural Center, founded by Percy Vargas de Abreu e Lima, a prominent intellectual figure in the city, offering free evening courses in [[Humanities]] and [[Sciences]] open to the entire population, developing a range of other cultural activities, and serving as a hub for political discussion due to the [[socialism|socialist]] ideas of its founder. The city's current House of Culture bears his name.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/casadacultura/ |title=O Patrono |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080803083641/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/casadacultura/ |archive-date=2008-08-03 |publisher=House of Culture Percy Vargas de Abreu e Lima |access-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Identity construction and social crisis ===
In 1925, the 50th anniversary of [[Italian immigration in Rio Grande do Sul|Italian immigration to Rio Grande do Sul]] was celebrated, a period highly conducive to initiating public recognition of the successes already achieved and consolidated, primarily aiming to integrate the colonial elites into the state's historical panorama, until then dominated by the pastoral-latifundist representations of Portuguese descendants. In this context, the [[Festa da Uva]] became the city's most significant secular event, linking the glorification of Italian labor with the festival's potential as an important economic forum.<ref>Ribeiro, pp. 85-88.</ref> As Cleodes Ribeiro stated,

::''"If the liturgy of the Festa da Uva ritual served to proclaim the celebrants' identity, display the results of over half a century of work, and claim the status of Brazilians, its defining characteristics were expressed through the symbolic vocabulary used in the ritual. The speeches, the grape exhibitions and distributions, the triumphal procession, the vendors in their typical costumes, the songs, the banquets, the congress, and the flags decorating the streets all reflected the efforts of the festival's organizers in the process of self-representation."''<ref>Ribeiro, p. 134.</ref>
[[File:Festa-da-Uva-1932.jpg|thumb|left|Pavilions of the Festa da Uva in 1932]]

At the same time, in [[Fascist Italy]], there was growing interest in reconstructing the history of emigrants, interpreting it as a powerful civilizing contribution of the Latin race to the New World, urging Italians to defend and take pride in their ethnic origins.<ref>Machado, p. 177</ref><ref name="Maestri">Maestri, Mário. ''[https://web.archive.org/web/20030627100118/http://www.espacoacademico.com.br/025/25hmaestri.htm Os Gringos Também Amam]''. IN ''Revista Espaço Acadêmico'', No. 88, September 2008.</ref> [[Benito Mussolini]], in the prologue to the commemorative album ''Cinquantenario della Colonizzazione Italiana nello Stato del Rio Grande del Sud'', declared: ''"In the noble pride that elevates your souls as you pause to contemplate the result of long and tenacious labor, I glimpse the sign of the noblest lineage that left an indelible mark on the history of Peoples."''<ref name="Maestri"/>

This [[chauvinism|chauvinistic]] and [[racism|racist]] stance, not free from foreign manipulation, was suppressed by the government of [[Getúlio Vargas]], which adopted a nationalist development line, minimizing state autonomy and regional singularities that had formed in various regions of Brazil, including the south. At this point, the overly optimistic and confident self-image constructed by the Italians began to be dismantled, and instead of collaborators in Brazil's growth and settlement, immigrants came to be seen as potential enemies of the homeland. This process culminated with Brazil's entry into [[World War II]] alongside the [[Allies of World War II|Allies]] against the [[Axis powers]], causing a profound rupture in ties between Italy and Brazil, with significant consequences for the immigration region.<ref name="Maestri"/><ref>Ribeiro, Cleodes M. P. J. ''Festa e Identidade: Como se fez a Festa da Uva''. Caxias do Sul: UCS, 2002. pp. 137-144.</ref> Between 1941 and 1944, anti-Italian demonstrations organized by the National Defense League sought to suppress the identifying symbols of the foreign ethnicity, creating an atmosphere of terror through various public acts of aggression. At the same time, Italians and their descendants were prohibited from speaking their dialect, forming a wall of silence around them, as many still struggled to express themselves in Portuguese. Their movement required safe-conduct passes, severely hindering their interaction with Brazilians at all levels. Such repression led to self-censorship efforts by Italians and their descendants, discouraging the cultivation of memory even in the privacy of the home and halting the celebration of the Festa da Uva from 1938. Those of other foreign ethnic origins, though fewer in number, faced similar treatment.<ref>Ribeiro, pp. 137-191</ref>

=== Reconciliation and resumed growth ===
[[File:Metalurgica-Eberle.jpg|thumb|Abramo Eberle Metalworks facilities in the 1950s]]
[[File:Caxias do Sul (RS).tif|thumb|Caxias do Sul, 1970s. [[Brazilian National Archives|National Archives]].]]

Upon the resumption of the Festa da Uva in 1950, coinciding with the 75th anniversary of immigration, in a spirit of reconciliation, immigrants began to be called pioneers, indicating a reorientation in the identity to be constructed, with progressive implications that opened up to non-Italians, now considered partners in the entire civilizing process.<ref>Ribeiro, pp. 137-139; 184-186; 191.</ref> Another event of great symbolic significance was the construction of the [[National Monument to the Immigrant|Monument to the Immigrant]], inaugurated in 1954 and later designated a national monument.<ref name="Maestri"/><ref>Brandalise, pp. 65-66</ref>

The city's economic development throughout the 20th century followed a pattern similar to Brazil's, using techniques and machinery developed in industrialized countries and adapting them to local conditions. In the second half of the century, major companies established branches in [[Porto Alegre]], and the city developed significant trade in pork products, dairy, flour, timber, and the wine sector. The metallurgical industries also grew, initially leveraging artisanal work by blacksmiths, locksmiths, and tinsmiths, but by the 1950s, they adopted a modern industrial profile, primarily with capital derived from savings and the expansion of the establishments themselves. A distinguishing factor in Caxias' economic evolution was the formation of deep mutual trust within the community, the so-called social capital, enabling the organization of the economy on stronger foundations, accelerating the economic cycle, and achieving more significant results.<ref>Sales, pp. 4-5</ref> The rapid diversification of the economy was also due to progressive [[urbanization]] and the failure of the colonial family [[smallholding]] system. The successive fragmentation of rural properties among multiple heirs rendered them unable to sustain large families, leading to [[rural exodus]] and transforming many former farmers into industrial and commercial workers.<ref>Herédia, Vânia Beatriz Merlotti. ''Processo de Industrialização da Zona Colonial Italiana''. Caxias do Sul: EDUCS, 1997. pp. 166-7</ref>

Just as the first half of the 20th century represented an opening and greater integration of the city into the state and national context, the second half marked a phase of global outreach, with changes in its productive, political, and cultural profile, the beginning of penetration into foreign markets, and the consolidation of its position as one of Brazil's largest economies. The city grew rapidly during this period, increasing from a population of 54,000 in 1950 to 180,000 in 1975 and about 360,000 by 2000, bringing with it the social, cultural, economic, and environmental problems typical of Brazilian cities with high expansion rates.<ref>Giron. p. 76</ref><ref name="Zimmermann">Zimmermann, Silvia Aparecida. [http://www.ufrrj.br/cpda/static/teses/m_silvia_zimmermann_2006.pdf ''Políticas de Abastecimento Alimentar na Perspectiva da Segurança Alimentar: O Programa de Abastecimento Alimentar de Caxias do Sul'']. Rio de Janeiro: Seropédica, August 2006. pp. 11-12</ref> The city's industrial dynamism intensified from the 1970s, driven by a diversity of ventures ranging from transportation equipment, the most significant, to furniture, food products, metallurgy, clothing, footwear, and textiles, making it the second most important economic area in the state, attracting populations from other regions of Rio Grande do Sul and even other states.<ref name="Zimmermann"/> In recent years, the local economy has shown a significant shift in emphasis, with the [[tertiary sector]] gaining prominence<ref name="Giron3">{{Cite web |url=http://scholar.google.com/ |title=Giron (2006), pp. 12-13 |access-date=2021-05-14 |archive-date=2012-06-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120630070939/http://scholar.google.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref> and increasing [[information technology|computerization]] and [[automation]] in companies, infrastructure, environmental concerns, and the opening of new jobs and markets in various global contexts.<ref>Machado & Herédia, pp. 77-80</ref>

In 1994, the Northeast Rio Grande do Sul Urban Agglomeration was established, the second largest urban agglomeration in Rio Grande do Sul, notable for its population concentration and dynamic economic structure.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.scp.rs.gov.br/atlas/atlas.asp?menu=299 |title=Aglomerações Urbanas |publisher=Atlas Socioeconômico do Rio Grande do Sul |access-date=2010-09-25 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.scp.rs.gov.br/atlas/atlas.asp?menu=299 |archive-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=live}}</ref> From the fragmentation of the former Colônia Caxias emerged the current municipalities of [[Flores da Cunha]], [[Farroupilha]], and [[São Marcos]]. The municipality is also known by the nickname ''Pearl of the Colonies''.<ref name="Nome"/>

[[File:Galópolis.JPG|thumb|150px|Véu da Noiva Waterfall, from the Pinhal Stream, in Galópolis]]

Located in the physiographic region of Rio Grande do Sul known as the Upper Northeast Slope, part of the [[Serra do Mar]], the original urban core of the city was built on a peninsula-like extension of the [[Vacaria]] Plateau, an ancient [[basalt]]ic outpouring over a [[granite|granitic]] base, with a topography featuring a continuous but gentle slope from the border with [[Santa Catarina (state)|Santa Catarina]], with an average incline of about {{convert|2|m/km|ft/mi|abbr=on}}. Caxias do Sul is situated in the Taquari-Antas River Basin and the Caí River Basin, on a [[drainage divide]] between 740 and 820 meters in elevation, where the Vacaria Plateau begins to become rugged and fragment into various [[valley]]s dissected by small rivers and streams flowing south and west, tributaries of the [[Taquari River (Rio Grande do Sul)|Taquari River]], those flowing south and southeast, tributaries of the [[Caí River]], and those draining north, tributaries of the [[Rio das Antas|Antas]], [[Pelotas River|Pelotas]], and [[São Marcos River|São Marcos]] rivers.<ref name="Beretta, Pier Luigi"/>

The main watercourses in the city are the Maestra Stream (north-northeast), the Biondo Stream (northeast), the Caravaggio Stream (southwest), and the Pinhal Stream (south). This network of valleys has extensive interfluvial areas ranging from 50 to 60&nbsp;km in width. To the south, however, there are smaller, relatively branched valleys with reduced interfluvial spaces ranging from 4 to 5&nbsp;km in width. The interfluvial spaces of these southern valleys are generally at the same elevation as the Caxias Plateau, ranging between 670 and 790 meters, while the bottoms of the gorges, where red [[sandstone]]s outcrop, are typically below 200 meters in elevation.<ref name="Beretta, Pier Luigi">Beretta, Pier Luigi. [https://archive.today/20121130034650/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/monografias/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/boletimgeografico/Boletim%20Geografico%201974%20v33%20n243.pdf ''Boletim Geográfico 243'']. Rio de Janeiro: IBGE, 33(243), Nov./Dec., 1974. pp. 5-10</ref>

=== Environment ===
[[File:MatoSartori-Caxias.jpg|thumb|left|Entrance to the Mato Sartori Municipal Park]]
[[File:Araucárias em Caxias do Sul.JPG|thumb|left|Araucaria forest in the interior of Caxias do Sul]]

The municipality of Caxias do Sul is located in the [[Atlantic Forest]] [[biome]] and is characterized by [[Araucaria moist forests|mixed ombrophilous forest]] vegetation. To the east, north, and northeast, the forest is interspersed with areas of low vegetation from the Campos de Cima da Serra, occurring in shallow soils. The landscape has been significantly altered due to agricultural and industrial activities and urban expansion, resulting in a substantial reduction in araucaria presence.<ref>De Souza, pp. 3-4</ref> The less rugged the terrain, the more extensive the patches of fields, and conversely, the more dynamic the terrain forms, the denser and more continuous the araucaria forests, especially at the headwaters of the main valleys. The lower valley areas are covered by a transitional slope forest, known as the Fig Tree Forest, with greater botanical diversity. The soils, formed over the basaltic layer, are generally very fertile.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/monografias/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/boletimgeografico/Boletim%20Geografico%201974%20v33%20n243.pdf |title=Beretta, pp. 6; 10 |access-date=2012-11-30 |archive-date=2012-11-30 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121130034650/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/monografias/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/boletimgeografico/Boletim%20Geografico%201974%20v33%20n243.pdf |url-status=live}}</ref>

[http://ocaxiense.com.br/2010/04/carta-ao-comando-pedetista-nada-de-vinicius-ou-toigo/ ''3 perguntas para Adelino Teles'']. O Caxiense. Access 22 Sep 2010</ref> the 186,000 hectares around the Caxias do Sul Water Catchment Basin in the Fazenda Souza district, the largest environmental preservation area for water extraction in the Atlantic Forest,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.aguabocadaserra.com.br/?lid=2 |title=Água Boca da Serra |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.aguabocadaserra.com.br/?lid=2 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref> and the Rincão das Flores Sanitary Landfill, which dedicates most of its 275.8 hectares to preservation.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,2730227 |title=Novo aterro sanitário de Caxias do Sul deve ficar pronto até o final do ano |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=2009-11-26 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,2730227 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref> Several parks feature native flora and urbanized areas with sports and leisure facilities, including Macaquinhos Park, Cinquentenário Park, and Dr. Celeste Gobatto Park.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/meio_ambiente/texto.php?codigo=129 |title=Praças, Parques e Jardins |publisher=Secretariat of the Environment |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/meio_ambiente/texto.php?codigo=129 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref> There is also a 50-hectare [[Botanical Garden]], preserving local [[flora]] while facilitating the recovery of degraded areas through collection and reproduction.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/meio_ambiente/texto.php?codigo=99 |title=Jardim Botânico |publisher=Secretariat of the Environment |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/meio_ambiente/texto.php?codigo=99 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref>

==== Environmental issues ====
[[File:Caxias-PoluiçãoVisual.jpg|thumb|right|Deterioration of historic buildings in the city center due to heavy visual pollution]]
{{Main|Environmentalism in Rio Grande do Sul}}

One of the city's environmental problems is [[pollution]]. The waters of the Taquari River's tributaries are contaminated with [[pesticides]], domestic [[sewage]], and industrial effluents, and the city's sewage is the main contributor to the pollution of the Caí River basin.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.fepam.rs.gov.br/qualidade/guaiba.asp |title=Qualidade Ambiental |publisher=FEPAM |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-date=2022-06-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220628115240/http://www.fepam.rs.gov.br/qualidade/guaiba.asp |url-status=dead }}</ref> However, the Municipal Government has made significant investments in water depollution programs.<ref name="despoluição">{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/noticias/noticias_ler.php?codigo=13125 |title=Assinada Ordem de Início das três últimas obras do plano de despoluição dos arroios |publisher=Assessoria de Comunicação SAMAE |date=2010-06-08 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/noticias/noticias_ler.php?codigo=13125 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Noise pollution]] has shown concerning occurrences but is being addressed with special legislation.<ref>Espeiorin, Vania Marta. [http://zerohora.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&newsID=a2877346.xml&channel=13&tipo=1&section=Geral ''Câmara debate poluição sonora em Caxias do Sul''] Zero Hora, 19/04/2010 - 12h11min</ref>

[[Visual pollution]] in the downtown area is intense, affecting even listed historic buildings.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/blog/jsp/default.jsp?source=DYNAMIC,blog.BlogDataServer,getBlog&uf=1&local=1&template=3948.dwt&section=Blogs&post=250214&blog=427&coldir=1&topo=4269.dwt |title=Poluição visual logo na chegada |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=2009-11-23 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/blog/jsp/default.jsp?source=DYNAMIC,blog.BlogDataServer,getBlog&uf=1&local=1&template=3948.dwt&section=Blogs&post=250214&blog=427&coldir=1&topo=4269.dwt |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>Dal Corno, Cássia F. [http://docs.google.com/viewer?a=v&q=cache:xr3nmyxdhGgJ:www.ucs.br/ucs/tplJovensPesquisadores/pesquisa/jovenspesquisadores/trabalhos_pdf/humanas/leonardo_balen.pdf+%22polui%C3%A7%C3%A3o%22+caxias+do+sul&hl=pt-BR&pid=bl&srcid=ADGEESiDovL5SsVpzQPqLxgDGS3z0vdfKe7yB3mIFdVCSwtj_qL_FragWDluYfqQbxdTaJyN4I4EFSifCOeazoBITKU9aRWWlSc6h0_OqWX-x2zV11FZRp2_C85nfD9e46aXMznRjlBL&sig=AHIEtbSKO2vfyor0glkmcr1di6d1zwYbiA ''Poluição Visual e Descaracterização do Patrimônio Arquitetônico: um estudo de caso no centro de Caxias do Sul'']. The [[air pollution]] levels are unknown due to the deactivation of monitoring equipment since 2006.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://pioneiro.clicrbs.com.br/rs/geral/noticia/2019/01/qualidade-do-ar-em-caxias-do-sul-nao-e-avaliada-ha-12-anos-10691282.html |title=Qualidade do ar em Caxias do Sul não é avaliada há 12 anos |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=2019-01-15 |access-date=2020-01-15 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200115000000/http://pioneiro.clicrbs.com.br/rs/geral/noticia/2019/01/qualidade-do-ar-em-caxias-do-sul-nao-e-avaliada-ha-12-anos-10691282.html |archive-date=2020-01-15 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Despite legal protection for the Atlantic Forest, [[deforestation]] and [[Slash-and-burn agriculture|burning]] continue to occur in the Caxias do Sul region, often covertly, in slopes and hard-to-access areas, with exotic species such as [[pine]] and [[Acacia decurrens|black wattle]] planted to supply the timber and paper markets, leaving a belt of native vegetation as camouflage, or clearing space for crops. Another cause of deforestation is the commercial devaluation of land with significant protected forest cover, which theoretically prevents more intensive exploitation. Monitoring exists, conducted by land and air by the Caxias do Sul Environmental Patrol (Patram), linked to the [[Military Brigade of Rio Grande do Sul|Military Brigade]], but the 18 soldiers are insufficient to cover the large area under their jurisdiction, which includes 13 cities.<ref>De Toni, Nádia. [http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&section=499&action=noticiasImpressa&id=2391017&edition=11630 ''Desmatamento invisível'']. Jornal Pioneiro, 03/02/2009</ref>

In recent years, landslides and flooding have been reported, caused, respectively, by the inappropriate occupation of slopes with fragile soil cover and by soil impermeabilization and poorly planned urbanization in areas naturally prone to flooding. Sometimes posing serious threats to the population's lives, these disasters generally affect the poorer classes, driven to settle in areas unsuitable for urbanization.<ref>Argenta, Giana; Kormann, Tanice Cristina & Robaina, Luis Eduardo de Souza [http://www.geo.ufv.br/simposio/simposio/trabalhos/trabalhos_completos/eixo11/037.pdf ''Levantamento da Ocorrência de Desastres Naturais no Município de Caxias do Sul - RS''] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230818200831/https://www.geo.ufv.br/simposio/simposio/trabalhos/trabalhos_completos/eixo11/037.pdf/ |date=2023-08-18 }}. XIII Simpósio Nacional de Geografia Física Aplicada. Universidade Federal de Viçosa, 6–10/07/2009. pp. 4-11</ref>

=== Climate ===
[[File:Neve em caxias do sul 26-27 agosto 2013.jpg|thumb|[[Snow]] covering the Festa da Uva Exhibition Park, with the replica of old Caxias, on August 27, 2013]]

The [[climate]] of Caxias do Sul is [[Oceanic climate|oceanic]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Cfb]]), with relatively warm summers, relatively cold winters, and occasional [[frost]], more frequent in higher altitude and less urbanized areas in the eastern part of the city. [[Snow]] may occur in the colder months, but it is a much rarer phenomenon and, when it occurs, is generally light. However, relatively heavy snowfalls with significant accumulations have been recorded, the most recent on August 26–27, 2013<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://noticias.r7.com/cidades/caxias-do-sul-rs-registra-neve-na-noite-desta-segunda-feira-26082013 |title=Caxias do Sul (RS) registra neve na noite desta segunda-feira |access-date=2013-08-26 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130826000000/http://noticias.r7.com/cidades/caxias-do-sul-rs-registra-neve-na-noite-desta-segunda-feira-26082013 |archive-date=2013-08-26 |url-status=live}}</ref> and July 28, 2021.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/noticia/2021/07/28/cidades-do-rs-tem-neve-e-chuva-congelada-veja-imagens.ghtml |title=Neve no RS: ao menos 13 cidades registram fenômeno; veja imagens |date=2021-07-28 |website=globo.com |access-date=2021-07-29 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210729000000/https://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/noticia/2021/07/28/cidades-do-rs-tem-neve-e-chuva-congelada-veja-imagens.ghtml |archive-date=2021-07-29 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The average annual compensated temperature of the municipality is {{convert|17|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, with significant [[thermal amplitude]] annually.<ref name="NCB-INMET"/> In terms of [[precipitation]], the area receives {{convert|1800|mm|in|abbr=on}} of rainfall, which is distributed evenly throughout the year, with no [[dry season]]. Accumulated precipitation has reached {{convert|105|mm|in|abbr=on}} in twelve hours.<ref>De Aguiar, Alexandre Amaral. ''[https://web.archive.org/web/20070211164423/http://www.metsul.com/secoes/visualiza.php?cod_subsecao=28&cod_texto=309 11 de outubro de 2000: A Noite dos Tornados]''. MetSul Meteorologia, 11/10/2006 - 02:26:17</ref> Occasionally, episodes of strong [[wind]] occur, with gusts exceeding {{convert|100|km/h|mph|abbr=on}},<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/?Noticia=65404 |title=Ventos de até 120 km/h causam estragos na Serra |website=Correio do Povo |date=2009-12-02 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/?Noticia=65404 |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/brasil/noticia/2010/09/ventos-de-100-kmh-deixam-mais-de-150-mil-casas-sem-energia-no-rs.html |title=Ventos de 100 km/h deixam mais de 150 mil casas sem energia no RS |website=G1 Brasil |date=2010-09-14 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://g1.globo.com/brasil/noticia/2010/09/ventos-de-100-kmh-deixam-mais-de-150-mil-casas-sem-energia-no-rs.html |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref> and in 2009, a [[tornado]] was recorded.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.deolhonotempo.blogspot.com/2009/12/confirmado-tornado-em-caxias-do-sul-rs.html |title=Confirmado tornado em Caxias do Sul (RS) |website=De Olho no Tempo |date=2009-12-03 |access-date=2010-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.deolhonotempo.blogspot.com/2009/12/confirmado-tornado-em-caxias-do-sul-rs.html |archive-date=2010-09-22 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Demographics===
The municipality is a regional hub for migration and accounts for 40% of the population of the Northeast Rio Grande do Sul Urban Agglomeration (AUNe), with growth rates between 25% and 30% per decade. The AUNe comprises the municipalities of [[Bento Gonçalves, Rio Grande do Sul|Bento Gonçalves]], [[Carlos Barbosa]], Caxias do Sul, [[Farroupilha]], [[Flores da Cunha]], [[Garibaldi, Rio Grande do Sul|Garibaldi]], [[Monte Belo do Sul]], [[Nova Pádua]], [[Santa Tereza, Rio Grande do Sul|Santa Tereza]], and [[São Marcos]].<ref name="Flores">Flores, Ana Paula Pereira et al. ''Plano Municipal de Assistência Social 2006-2009''. Caxias do Sul: Fundação de Assistência Social, October 2005. p. 7</ref> In 2010, the AUNe had a population of 716,421 inhabitants.<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/populacao_por_municipio.shtm |title=Censo Demográfico 2010 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística |access-date=December 5, 2010|archive-date=August 13, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250813050600/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/populacao_por_municipio.shtm}}</ref>

According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), in 2022, Caxias do Sul had a population of 463,338 inhabitants.<ref name="IBGE Panorama"/> In 2010, the previous census year, the total population of the municipality was 435,564 inhabitants, with a [[population density]] of 264.89 inhabitants/km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name="IBGE Panorama"/>

In 2000, the [[life expectancy]] at birth was 74.11 years, and the [[infant mortality rate]] in 2007 was 9.04 per thousand live births.<ref name="Fundação"/> In 2000, the [[urbanization rate]] was 92.5%, with 92.5% of the population living in urban areas. The age structure showed 25.18% of the population under 15 years, 69.16% between 15 and 64 years, and the remainder over 65 years. Additionally, 97.9% had access to refrigerators, 97.3% to televisions, 59.9% to telephones, and 17.7% to computers. The [[Human Development Index]] was 0.857, ranking the city 12th among Brazilian municipalities and 4th in the state.<ref>Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano no Brasil, pp. 1-5</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], an indicator of social inequality, was 0.51, indicating high income concentration, with a poverty rate of 7.5%. Regarding family vulnerability indicators, 0.1% of girls aged 10 to 14 had children, 5.3% of youths aged 15 to 17 had children, 12.7% of children lived in families with income below half the [[minimum wage]], and 4% of families were headed by women without a spouse and with minor children. Additionally, 12.7% of families had a per capita income below half the minimum wage, and in 15.9% of families, the head had less than 4 years of education.<ref>Flores, p. 25</ref>

In 2007, the [[ethnicity|ethnic composition]] of the resident population was 88.94% [[White people|White]], 2.43% [[Black people|Black]], 7.94% ''[[Pardo]]'', 0.10% [[Asian people|Asian]], 0.24% [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|Indigenous]], and 0.36% of undeclared ethnicity.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2093&z=cd&o=7&i=P|title=Tabela 2093 - População residente por cor ou raça, sexo, situação do domicílio e grupos de idade|publisher=IBGE|date=2007|access-date=September 24, 2010|archive-date=August 13, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250813050600/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2093&z=cd&o=7&i=P}}</ref> Regarding [[religion]], 86.19% declared themselves members of the [[Roman Catholic Church]]; 1.78% adhered to [[Evangelicalism|mission-oriented evangelical]] denominations (mostly [[Lutheranism|Lutherans]], at 0.86%, followed by [[Adventism|Adventists]], at 0.54%); 6.05% were [[Pentecostalism|Pentecostals]] (predominantly belonging to the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembly of God]], at 2.48%, followed by the [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], at 1.31%); 1.40% identified as [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]], 0.38% as [[Umbanda|Umbandists]], 0.03% as followers of [[Candomblé]], 0.01% as [[Judaism|Jews]], and 0.02% as [[Islam|Muslims]], alongside a large number of other faiths in smaller fractions. == Economy ==
The municipal [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]] is estimated at 22.3 billion [[Brazilian real|reais]] (2014).<ref name="FCDL">{{Cite web |title=Caxias do Sul é a 33ª cidade com o maior PIB |url=http://pioneiro.clicrbs.com.br/rs/economia/noticia/2016/12/caxias-do-sul-e-a-33-cidade-com-o-maior-pib-revela-ibge-8753214.html |access-date=13 February 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170214000000/http://pioneiro.clicrbs.com.br/rs/economia/noticia/2016/12/caxias-do-sul-e-a-33-cidade-com-o-maior-pib-revela-ibge-8753214.html |archive-date=14 February 2017 |website=Jornal O Pioneiro |date=15 December 2016 }}</ref> The [[per capita GDP]] in 2011 was 37,700 reais.<ref name="Fundação"/> In 2007, public finances recorded budget revenues of 694,582,143.80 reais and expenditures of 645,802,901.80 reais, resulting in a surplus of 48,779,242.00 reais.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Perfil - Finanças Públicas |url=http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |access-date=20 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100921000000/http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |archive-date=21 September 2010 |website=Câmara de Indústria e Comércio de Caxias do Sul |url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Agriculture]] accounts for only 1.70% of the [[Gross Value Added]], with [[Manufacturing|industry]] contributing 40.79% and [[Tertiary sector of the economy|services]] 57.51%.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Perfil - Participação no VAB |url=http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |access-date=20 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100921000000/http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |archive-date=21 September 2010 |website=Câmara de Indústria e Comércio de Caxias do Sul |url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2007, there were 30,068 companies of all economic categories in operation.<ref name="Setores">{{Cite web |title=Perfil - Setores |url=http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |access-date=20 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100921000000/http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/perfil/ |archive-date=21 September 2010 |website=Câmara de Indústria e Comércio de Caxias do Sul |url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2005, the city had an [[Workforce|Economically Active Population]] (EAP) of approximately 150,000 workers, but only 55% were in the formal labor market. The industry sector employed the largest share, with 58.61%, two-thirds of which were in the [[metalworking]] industry. In the informal sector, small-scale industries such as knitwear, food production, clothing manufacturing, and low-tech services (repairs, domestic work, cleaning) were prominent.<ref>Flores, p. 10</ref>

In 2005, the city achieved the highest Socioeconomic Development Index (Idese) in Rio Grande do Sul for the sixth consecutive year, with a score of 0.844. It was the only municipality in the state during this period to have four of the Idese components—[[education]], [[income]], [[health]], [[sanitation]], and [[housing]]—above 0.800.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Caxias do Sul segue com o maior Índice de Desenvolvimento Socioeconômico do RS |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&section=Economia&newsID=a2189933.xml |access-date=18 September 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080919000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&section=Economia&newsID=a2189933.xml |archive-date=19 September 2008 |website=Zero Hora |date=18 September 2008}}</ref> In recent years, Caxias do Sul's economy has shown strong performance,<ref name="Perfil">{{Cite web |title=Economia de Caxias do Sul registra crescimento |url=http://www.acesseperfil.com.br/noticia.aspx?id=72 |access-date=27 June 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080628000000/http://www.acesseperfil.com.br/noticia.aspx?id=72 |archive-date=28 June 2008 |website=Perfil Imóveis |date=27 June 2008}}</ref> with 10,779 new jobs created between January and July 2010, setting a national record.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Caxias do Sul gera 10,7 mil novos empregos desde janeiro deste ano |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,3012155,Caxias-do-Sul-gera-10-7-mil-novos-empregos-desde-janeiro-deste-ano.html |access-date=20 August 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100821000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,3012155,Caxias-do-Sul-gera-10-7-mil-novos-empregos-desde-janeiro-deste-ano.html |archive-date=21 August 2010 |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=20 August 2010}}</ref>

In the first half of 2010, the local economy achieved results equivalent to Brazil's full-year projections. The 12-month cumulative index exceeded 7%, reaching 19% for the semester. In June, performance grew by 20.8% compared to the same month the previous year, with positive expectations for the following year, though possibly at a slower pace. The industry sector saw increases in working hours, purchases, sales, and payroll, while the services sector led with a 10.1% rise over 12 months and 13.4% for the year. However, the [[commerce]] sector, despite growth, faced losses, though delinquency rates decreased.<ref>Hunoff, Roberto. {{Cite web |title=Economia de Caxias do Sul cresce em 6 meses o que o Brasil projeta para 1 ano |url=http://ocaxiense.com.br/2010/08/economia-caxiense-cresce-em-6-meses-o-que-o-pais-projeta-para-1-ano/ |access-date=5 August 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100806000000/http://ocaxiense.com.br/2010/08/economia-caxiense-cresce-em-6-meses-o-que-o-pais-projeta-para-1-ano/ |archive-date=6 August 2010 |website=O Caxiense |date=5 August 2010}}</ref>

The Caxias do Sul Chamber of Industry, Commerce, and Services represents over 1,000 legal entities of micro, small, medium, and large sizes across industry, commerce, and services, making it the largest association of its kind in the state's interior.<ref>{{Cite web |title=A CIC |url=http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/institucional/apresentacao.asp |access-date=21 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100922000000/http://www.cic-caxias.com.br/institucional/apresentacao.asp |archive-date=22 September 2010 |website=Câmara de Indústria, Comércio e Serviços de Caxias do Sul |url-status=dead}}</ref> The public administration also invests in various economic development programs, including Solidarity Economy Programs, Local Productive Arrangements (APLs), Recyclers' Associations, the IT Cluster, the Fashion Cluster, the Metalworking Cluster, the Serra Gaúcha Credit Guarantee Association (AGC), the Community Credit Institution (ICC – People's Bank), and numerous projects, agreements, programs, and partnerships with various public and private entities.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Secretaria do Desenvolvimento Econômico, Trabalho e Emprego |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/desenv_economico/index.php |access-date=21 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101028235112/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/desenv_economico/index.php |archive-date=28 October 2010}}</ref>

== Infrastructure ==

=== Water, sanitation, energy, and housing ===
[[File:Estação de tratamento de água - caxias do sul.jpg|thumb|Water treatment and distribution station in the Lourdes neighborhood]]
[[File:ColetaSeletivaCaxias.jpg|thumb|Waste sorting bins]]

The city lacks large natural rivers or lakes, and water is sourced from reservoirs built to capture the flow of small streams.<ref>De Bem, Judite Sanson & Gullo, Maria Carolina R. ''Municipalização do Saneamento Básico: um estudo de caso para Caxias do Sul no período de 1996 a 2001''. VII Encontro da Sociedade Brasileira de Economia Ecológica. Fortaleza, 28 a 30 de novembro de 2007. p. 10</ref> Water supply is managed by the Municipal Water and Sewage Service (SAMAE). According to the agency, 100% of the urban population and 99.5% of the total municipal population receive water, sourced from the Faxinal, Maestra, Samuara, Dal Bó, Galópolis systems, and artesian wells. The Marrecas system is under development to meet future demand.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Apresentação |url=http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna.asp?secao_id=8 |access-date=24 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna.asp?secao_id=8 |archive-date=25 September 2010 |website=Serviço Autônomo Municipal de Água e Esgoto |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Attention to [[sewage]] is relatively recent in the city, which historically prioritized water supply.<ref name="Gullo">De Bem & Gullo, p. 13</ref> Sewage was previously collected in private septic tanks or discharged into the stormwater system.<ref name="SAMAE">{{Cite web |title=O Tratamento de Esgoto em Caxias do Sul |url=http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna_dir.asp?secao_id=26 |access-date=24 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna_dir.asp?secao_id=26 |archive-date=25 September 2010 |website=Serviço Autônomo Municipal de Água e Esgoto |url-status=dead}}</ref> A collection and treatment system began operating effectively in 1997. Until recently, only a small percentage of the population was served by sewage collection, but all collected sewage was treated.<ref name="Gullo"/> Recent progress has been notable, with a study by the Instituto Trata Brasil highlighting performance above the national average. In 2009, 79.7% of municipal households had sewage collected through a 1,345-km network, though only 13.6% of the total volume was treated.<ref>Instituto Trata Brasil. ''Benefícios Econômicos da Expansão do Saneamento Brasileiro''. Instituto Trata Brasil / Instituto Brasileiro de Economia / Fundação Getúlio Vargas. Julho de 2010, pp. 8-11</ref> SAMAE has undertaken major treatment system projects to improve this,<ref name="SAMAE"/> aiming to treat 86% of sewage by the end of 2012.<ref name="despoluição"/> Awareness campaigns are also conducted.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Setor Cultural |url=http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna_dir.asp?secao_id=256 |access-date=24 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.samaecaxias.com.br/site/interna_dir.asp?secao_id=256 |archive-date=25 September 2010 |website=Serviço Autônomo Municipal de Água e Esgoto |url-status=dead}}</ref>

[[File:Centro-CaxiasdoSul.jpg|thumb|left|City center with dense urbanization]]
[[File:Favelas-Caxias.jpg|thumb|left|The so-called ''Buraco Quente'', a [[slum]] area near the city center]]

[[Waste]] collection is managed by the Caxias do Sul Development Company (CODECA), a mixed-economy company. Waste is sorted daily in the urban center and every two days in less populated neighborhoods, covering 100% of households. Domestic waste is disposed of in a sanitary landfill authorized by the State Environmental Protection Foundation (FEPAM), where it is treated by the municipality. Nine recyclers' associations sort and sell dry waste, while industrial, hospital, and laboratory waste is the responsibility of the respective emitters.<ref>Flores, p. 18</ref>

[[Electricity]] is distributed by Rio Grande Energia (RGE), serving the north-northeast of the state. The network operates at 220V, with higher-power lines managed by CEEE.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Como ficou o Sistema Elétrico Gaúcho |url=http://www.rge-rs.com.br/SaladeImprensa/tabid/112/EntryId/484/Como-ficou-o-Sistema-Eletrico-Gaucho.aspx |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19990302000000/http://www.rge-rs.com.br/SaladeImprensa/tabid/112/EntryId/484/Como-ficou-o-Sistema-Eletrico-Gaucho.aspx |archive-date=2 March 1999 |website=Comunicação RGE |url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2003, 137,530 registered consumers were served.<ref>Flores, p. 17</ref> The city also has a [[natural gas]] supply, fueling approximately 1,800 vehicles daily.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Postos estão sem gás natural em Caxias |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&id=2305781&section=Geral&action=noticias |access-date=24 November 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081125000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&id=2305781&section=Geral&action=noticias |archive-date=25 November 2008 |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=24 November 2008}}</ref>

In 2000, there were 109,396 registered private households, with 83,677 owned (74,037 fully paid) and 17,391 rented.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Infra-Estrutura - Domicílios Particulares Permanentes e Moradores |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |access-date=25 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100926000000/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |archive-date=26 September 2010 |website=Confederação Nacional de Municípios}}</ref> As a regional hub, the city attracts large numbers of migrants that the housing infrastructure cannot accommodate, settling in [[slums]], irregular settlements, and risk zones, forming pockets of [[poverty]] with limited access to essential services such as sanitation and education, contrasting with areas that have full urban infrastructure. A survey conducted in 1994 identified 110 substandard housing units, in which 3,596 families were living in inadequate conditions. By 2005, this number was estimated to have risen to 7,000 families. The municipality maintains several housing projects, land regularization, and lot acquisition initiatives, carried out through community participation and partnerships with various governmental and non-governmental organizations.<ref>Flores, pp. 7-15</ref>

Public safety in Caxias is provided by various organizations. The Women's Coordination develops public policies to protect and combat violence against women, while the Youth Coordination performs similar work for young people, alongside other initiatives.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Secretaria de Segurança Pública e Proteção Social |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/seg_publica/index.php |access-date=22 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101105204701/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/seg_publica/index.php |archive-date=5 November 2010 |website=Secretaria de Segurança Pública e Proteção Social}}</ref> The [[Military Brigade of Rio Grande do Sul|Military Brigade]], a state force, maintains a battalion (12th BPM) in the city, divided into five companies with 445 personnel. === Education ===
[[File:ColegioCarmoCaxias2.jpg|thumb|left|La Salle Carmo School]]
[[File:Reitoria UCS 2012.jpg|thumb|left|Dean's Office of the University of Caxias do Sul]]

In terms of [[education]], the municipal school system includes 86 [[elementary schools]] and 36 early childhood education centers, with over 2,500 teachers serving [[special education]] and [[adult education]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ensino Fundamental - Apresentação |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/educacao/texto.php?codigo=1 |access-date=20 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101211231030/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/educacao/texto.php?codigo=1 |archive-date=11 December 2010 |website=Secretaria da Educação}}</ref> catering to 35,320 students in 2009. The private system had 16,633 enrolled students that year, from [[preschool]] to [[high school]] and special education, while the state system served an additional 31,494 students in basic and secondary education.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Educação - Número de Matrículas |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_matricula.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |access-date=25 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100926000000/http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_matricula.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |archive-date=26 September 2010 |website=Confederação Nacional de Municípios}}</ref> In 2000, 98.4% of children aged 7–14 attended school, and 80% of youths aged 15–17. Dropout rates in municipal and state schools between 2003 and 2005 were 6.72% and 11.8%, respectively, with failure rates of 9.01% and 25.0%. The average years of schooling in 2000 was 7.2, and the adult [[illiteracy]] rate was 4.2%.<ref>Flores, pp. 11; 24</ref> Caxias do Sul is home to several prestigious schools with long histories, founded in the early 20th century, such as [[Colégio La Salle Carmo|La Salle Carmo]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=Histórico |url=http://www.muitomaisqueumcolegio.com.br/caxias/pagina.php?id=141 |access-date=2 October 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101003000000/http://www.muitomaisqueumcolegio.com.br/caxias/pagina.php?id=141 |archive-date=3 October 2010 |website=Colégio La Salle Carmo |url-status=dead}}</ref> São José,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Histórico |url=http://www.saojosecaxias.com.br/historico.asp |access-date=2 October 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101003000000/http://www.saojosecaxias.com.br/historico.asp |archive-date=3 October 2010 |website=Colégio São José |url-status=dead}}</ref> and La Salle Caxias.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Histórico |url=http://www.muitomaisqueumcolegio.com.br/caxias/pagina.php?id=141 |access-date=2 October 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101003000000/http://www.muitomaisqueumcolegio.com.br/caxias/pagina.php?id=141 |archive-date=3 October 2010 |website=Colégio La Salle Caxias |url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Technical education]] is provided by several [[SENAI]] and SENAC units and over 20 other vocational schools.<ref name="Educação">{{Cite web |title=Educação |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/desenv_economico/programas.php?codigo=8 |access-date=20 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101029190741/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/desenv_economico/programas.php?codigo=8 |archive-date=29 October 2010 |website=Secretaria do Desenvolvimento Econômico, Trabalho e Emprego}}</ref>

There are 10 higher education institutions in the city, four offering [[distance learning]] courses, and two focused solely on [[postgraduate education]], educating a total of 36,500 students across 185 [[undergraduate]] and 189 postgraduate programs.<ref name="Andreatta">Andreatta, Graziela. {{Cite web |title=O saber multiplicado |url=http://ocaxiense.com.br/2010/04/o-saber-multiplicado/ |access-date=10 April 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100411000000/http://ocaxiense.com.br/2010/04/o-saber-multiplicado/ |archive-date=11 April 2010 |website=O Caxiense}}</ref> The [[University of Caxias do Sul]] (UCS) stands out, offering 58 undergraduate programs, 7 [[master's degree]]s, 2 [[doctorates]], and over 70 specialization courses, as well as various extension, distance learning, and technical courses. === Communications ===
[[File:RBS TV Caxias do Sul.jpg|thumb|left|Facilities of RBS TV Caxias do Sul]]

The city has a robust [[communications]] infrastructure. Local [[fixed-line telephony]] is operated by the Rio Grande Telecommunications Company (CRT). In 1999, there were 79,386 devices, 62% of which were residential. This equated to a coverage ratio of one device for every four people. [[Mobile telephony]] operates in two frequency bands: Band A (Celular CRT S/A), with approximately 28,000 phones in 1999, and Band B (Telet S/A), with about 3,200 phones, 80% of which were in Caxias do Sul. [[Telex]], data transit, message handling, interstate telephony, and executive television network services are all provided by [[Embratel]]. Postal services are provided by three urban agencies, five franchised agencies, six satellite post offices, and three collection points of the [[Correios]].<ref name="Gilmar">Santa Catarina, Gilmar. {{Cite web |title=Caxias do Sul - Dados Socieconômicos |url=http://www.al.rs.gov.br/anais/50/BKP/deputados/sartori/not%C3%ADcias/Perfil%20CAXIAS%20DO%20SUL.html |access-date=22 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100923000000/http://www.al.rs.gov.br/anais/50/BKP/deputados/sartori/not%C3%ADcias/Perfil%20CAXIAS%20DO%20SUL.html |archive-date=23 September 2010 |website=CIC de Caxias do Sul |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The city has three [[television]] stations: [[NET (telecommunications)|TV Caxias - Channel 14]], a community network offering occasional free access and fixed slots for members;<ref>{{Cite web |title=TV Caxias - Canal 14 |url=http://www.tvcaxias.com.br/?ir=noticias&id=5 |access-date=22 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100923000000/http://www.tvcaxias.com.br/?ir=noticias&id=5 |archive-date=23 September 2010 |website=TV Caxias}}</ref> [[RBS TV Caxias do Sul]], an affiliate of [[Grupo RBS|Rede Brasil Sul de Comunicações]] (RBS), retransmitting [[Rede Globo]] programming and producing local content, particularly in [[journalism]];<ref name="Gilmar"/> and UCS TV, operated by the University of Caxias do Sul, which aims to integrate the community and university with educational programming for young adults, covering local and regional issues with an academic focus while preserving local identity.<ref>{{Cite web |title=UCSTV |url=http://www.ucs.br/ucs/cetel/ucstv/ucs_tv |access-date=22 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100923000000/http://www.ucs.br/ucs/cetel/ucstv/ucs_tv |archive-date=23 September 2010 |website=UCS |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The city also has several [[radio broadcasting|radio]] stations, both [[AM radio|AM]] and [[FM radio|FM]], including Rádio Caxias, Rádio Tua Voz, Rádio Difusora Caxiense, UCS FM, Sorriso FM, and Rádio São Francisco. There are four newspapers: Pioneiro, published by RBS, Correio Riograndense, O Caxiense, and Gazeta de Caxias.<ref name="Gilmar"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=O Caxiense |url=http://ocaxiense.com.br/ |access-date=22 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100923000000/http://ocaxiense.com.br/ |archive-date=23 September 2010 |website=O Caxiense}}</ref> Major newspapers from the state capital, such as [[Zero Hora]], [[Correio do Povo]], and Jornal do Comércio, maintain operations in Caxias to strengthen ties with the state. The city's printing industry is among the most modern in Brazil, with updated infrastructure and dynamic operations.<ref name="Gilmar"/>

Caxias do Sul was also once served by rail transport through the Caxias Line of the former Viação Férrea do Rio Grande do Sul, which crosses the entire region and connects it to the city of [[Montenegro, Rio Grande do Sul|Montenegro]], where it joins the Tronco Principal Sul line. Originally, the railway linked the city to the state capital, Porto Alegre, but some of its original sections were discontinued between the 1960s and 1980s. At the Caxias do Sul Railway Station, passenger trains made their last trips in September 1978, after which the line was restricted to freight transport only. However, in 1985, the former [[Rede Ferroviária Federal]], which managed the railway at the time, reinstated passenger transport to commemorate the city's 75th anniversary, operating on the stretch between Caxias and [[Farroupilha]]. The Hungarian Train of the RFFSA operated in the region for the first time, with fully booked trains, marking the last passenger train to travel in the city, albeit for commemorative purposes.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rs_linhaspoa/caxias.htm |title=Caxias do Sul -- Estações Ferroviárias do Rio Grande do Sul |access-date=2020-09-08 |website=www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200908000000/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rs_linhaspoa/caxias.htm |archive-date=2020-09-08}}</ref>

== Culture ==
[[File:Casa de Cultura-Caxias.jpg|thumb|right|The House of Culture]]

The colonization of Caxias do Sul took place amid great sacrifices and material deprivation in a still-wild environment. With scarce government assistance, those who did not work hard faced starvation. Despite the effort, many went hungry in the challenging early days. In this adverse context, there was little time for the settlers to dedicate to cultural activities beyond their folkloric aspects, with the most prominent being religious festivals, those related to agricultural production, and domestic entertainment such as lace-making, games, and songs. Despite their diverse origins and distinct cultures, mutual support in times of hardship was crucial, creating a strong network of cooperation that formed the foundation for the rapid flourishing of a prosperous new city, which became the hub for the circulation of surplus production from surrounding family colonies. The settlers were also united by ties of religion, kinship, and ancestry, forming communities with a conservative and traditional profile in a cultural universe that was essentially pragmatic and functional but dominated, as João Carlos Tedesco described, by the ''"[[ethos]]"'' of the settler, the immigrant—foreigners uprooted from their origins but still poorly integrated into the culture of their new country. The immigrants formed a kind of cultural enclave in the region.<ref name="Tedesco, João Carlos">{{Cite book |last=Tedesco |first=João Carlos |chapter=A Família e a Carreta: as bases da vida nos primórdios da colônia |title=Etnias & carisma: polianteia em homenagem a Rovílio Costa |editor-last=Suliani |editor-first=Antônio |publisher=EDIPUCRS |year=2001 |pages=582–586 |isbn=978-85-7430-181-5 |chapter-url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Bv_x5z13fD4C&dq=%22hist%C3%B3ria+de+caxias+do+sul%22&pg=PA396}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Ribeiro |first=Cleodes Maria Piazza Julio |title=Festa e Identidade: como se fez a Festa da Uva |publisher=EDUCS |year=2002 |pages=44–47}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Machado |first=Maria Abel |title=Construindo uma Cidade: História de Caxias do Sul - 1875-1950 |publisher=Maneco |year=2001 |pages=50–62}}</ref>

In the urban core, as initial difficulties were overcome and the community rapidly prospered, it became possible to invest in more refined forms of art. Soon, the production of notable painters, religious sculptors, decorators, and photographers flourished. By the early 20th century, the city center was populated with decorated multi-story buildings, mansions, and imposing structures such as the [[St. Theresa's Cathedral, Caxias do Sul|Cathedral]], the Episcopal Palace, the Central Cinema Theater, the [[Clube Juvenil]], and various bank headquarters. Elite clubs were formed, operettas were appreciated, and the public became enthusiastic about cinema.<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Wiggers |first1=Monica Marlise |last2=Kormann |first2=Tanice Cristina |last3=Robaina |first3=Luis Eduardo de Souza |title=Evolução da ocupação urbana de Caxias do Sul e os assentamentos subnormais |journal=Anais do XVI Encontro Nacional dos Geógrafos: Crise, práxis e autonomia: Espaços de resistência e de esperanças: Espaço de diálogos e práticas |date=2010-07-25 |location=Porto Alegre |url=https://www.anaiseng.org/}}</ref><ref>{{Cite thesis |last=Nascimento |first=Roberto R. F. do |title=Campo dos Bugres-Sede Dante: a formação urbana de Caxias do Sul (1876-1884) |type=Master's dissertation |institution=PUCRS |location=Porto Alegre |year=2009}}</ref> Moreover, buoyed by their own prosperity and influenced by the [[fascism]] of [[Benito Mussolini|Mussolini]], who publicly praised them, the immigrants and their first generation of descendants developed considerable pride. Under the nationalist policies of the [[Vargas era|Vargas Era]], this ethnic pride and cultural enclave were dissolved through significant government violence and repression. Social reconciliation occurred only in the 1950s, but this forced integration led to the loss of many traditions and valuable community values.<ref name="Tedesco, João Carlos"/><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Beneduzzi |first=Luis Fernando |title=Festa da Uva e política fascista: narrativa de operosidade e resgate de italianidade |journal=Anais do XXVI Simpósio Nacional de História – ANPUH |date=2011 |location=São Paulo}}</ref>

The situation today is quite different. Caxias do Sul has opened up to cosmopolitan culture and cultivates the arts in various forms. The municipal government directly maintains or supports a wide range of departments, programs, groups, and cultural institutions, including the Percy Vargas de Abreu e Lima Cultural Center, which encompasses the Municipal Theater, the Public Library, and the Municipal Art Gallery, the Henrique Ordovás Filho Cultural Center, theater, dance, music, and literature units, and the Department of Popular Art and Culture, while also promoting various community centers. The city government also supports culture through the Culture Incentive Law (Fundoprocultura) and annually awards several prizes and trophies in various cultural fields.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/index.php |title=Apresentação |access-date=2010-09-24 |website=Secretaria da Cultura |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/index.php |archive-date=2010-09-24}}</ref> In 2006, 650,000 [[Brazilian real|reais]] were spent by the public sector on culture.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/cultura/mu_cultura_quadro.asp?iId=2&iIdMun=100143087 |title=Cultura |access-date=2010-09-25 |website=Confederação Nacional de Municípios |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.cnm.org.br/cultura/mu_cultura_quadro.asp?iId=2&iIdMun=100143087 |archive-date=2010-09-25}}</ref> In 2008, the city was named the Brazilian Capital of Culture.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.capitalbrasileiradacultura.org/cbc/?Url=FAQ#06 |title=FAQs |access-date=2010-09-25 |website=Projeto Capital Brasileira da Cultura |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100925000000/http://www.capitalbrasileiradacultura.org/cbc/?Url=FAQ#06 |archive-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Traditions and folklore ===
The decline of religion as a unifying social force, new consumption habits, profound changes in the local production system, the standardization of Brazilian education, the indifference of younger generations toward their grandparents' language and customs, the presence of a significant number of recent migrants of non-Italian descent for whom colonial traditions hold no meaning, the widespread popularity of media and entertainment such as radio, TV, and cinema, and the city's progressive opening to the world caused a rapid erosion of the ethnic and traditional characteristics of Caxias' culture, especially in the urban area, between the 1950s and 1960s.<ref name="igual">{{Cite journal |title=A cidade ficou igual a tantas outras… As diferenças? |journal=Boletim Memória |publisher=Museu e Arquivo Histórico Municipal |location=Caxias do Sul |issue=16 |date=1993}}</ref><ref name="canto">{{Cite web |last=Ribeiro |first=Cleodes M. P. J. |url=http://www.ucs.br/ucs/tplPOSLetras/posgraduacao/strictosensu/letras/professores/cleodes_ribeiro/artigo.pdf |title=O lugar do canto |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=UCS |location=Caxias do Sul |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.ucs.br/ucs/tplPOSLetras/posgraduacao/strictosensu/letras/professores/cleodes_ribeiro/artigo.pdf |archive-date=2010-08-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Maestri |first=Mário |url=http://www.esteditora.com.br/textos/silencio.htm |title=A Lei do Silêncio: história e mitos da imigração ítalo-gaúcha |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=EST Edições on line |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.esteditora.com.br/textos/silencio.htm |archive-date=2010-08-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Sensing an imminent loss of roots, starting in the mid-1970s, some local intellectuals began to focus on preserving the ancestral cultural heritage. Among them, Loraine Slomp Giron and [[João Spadari Adami]] conducted significant research that resulted in the publication of several essential books for studying local history. However, during this period, which saw a surge in literature on immigration, not only in Caxias do Sul, the history of the settlers was revisited with new, more candid, objective, and scientific interpretations, unlike the interwar generations of writers who glorified the settler. These new studies presented Caxias' history with its contradictions and conflicts, aiming to make the image of the immigrant less mythical but more real and richer.<ref name="Maestri"/><ref name="igual"/>

This same interest in the past led the municipal government to restructure and reopen the [[Municipal Museum of Caxias do Sul|Municipal Museum]] in 1975 and create the Museu Ambiência Casa de Pedra, two of the city's most important historical museums. In 1976, the [[João Spadari Adami Municipal Historical Archive|Municipal Historical Archive]] was established,<ref>{{Cite book |last=Ribeiro |first=Cleodes Maria Piazza Julio |title=Festa e Identidade: como se fez a Festa da Uva |publisher=EDUCS |year=2002 |page=202}}</ref> and in the following decade, the Department of Historical Heritage was created, initiating efforts to preserve and recover intangible traditions, artistic legacy, and Italian architectural heritage, which were rapidly disappearing. The work of these institutions, grounded in the belief that progress can coexist with the past, along with the efforts of artistic groups such as Miseri Coloni, a theater group performing in the region's characteristic dialect, ''[[Talian dialect|Talian]]'', are active forces in preserving and revitalizing important aspects of Italian tradition, reinserting them with a critical perspective into the life of an increasingly cosmopolitan city.<ref name="preservação">{{Cite journal |title=A preservação não recusa o novo |journal=Boletim Memória |publisher=Museu e Arquivo Histórico Municipal |location=Caxias do Sul |issue=4 |date=1992}}</ref><ref name="Filippon">{{Cite book |last=Filippon |first=Maria Isabel |title=A Casa do Imigrante Italiano: A Linguagem do Espaço de Habitar |publisher=UCS |location=Caxias do Sul |year=2007 |pages=50–51}}</ref><ref name="Itaqui">{{Cite web |last=Itaqui |first=José |url=http://www.esteditora.com.br/textos/ator.htm |title=O material e o imaterial no trabalho do ator: O caso Miseri Coloni |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=EST Edições On Line |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.esteditora.com.br/textos/ator.htm |archive-date=2010-08-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Casa de pedra.jpg|thumb|left|The Museu Ambiência Casa de Pedra]]

In the rural areas, traditions dating back to the early days of colonization are still found spontaneously. Among them is the ''filó'', a gathering of families who socialize in home kitchens or wine cellars. Men talk and play cards, [[bocce]], or mora, women engage in manual crafts such as [[crochet]], sewing, and making ''dressa'', a braid of corn straw used to create hats, while sharing their experiences, and children play with toys. These gatherings are always accompanied by a glass of wine, [[salami]], ''grostoli'' (a type of sweet dumplings), bread, and cheese. The group eventually starts singing, collectively singing old tunes that evoke the Italian homeland, ancestors, love, and work. During [[Christmas]], large groups travel through rural communities, visiting friends. Along the way, carrying a torch adorned with a paper star, they sing ''canções da stela'', announcing the ''new star'' symbolizing the birth of [[Jesus]]. On the feast day of the patron saint of community chapels, the ''sagra'' takes place, another ritual celebration marked by collective music and communal feasts. The figure of Nanetto Pipetta also remains alive, a literary character created in 1924 by Friar Aquiles Bernardi as an embodiment of the utopias cherished by immigrants in contrast to the harsh reality they faced in their colonizing endeavor. His adventures were published in the [[Talian dialect|''Talian'' dialect]], originally as a ''[[feuilleton]]'', in the newspaper ''Staffetta Riograndense'' and achieved immediate success. The stories were compiled into books several times, consistently selling out, with over 150,000 copies sold, including internationally. A continuation was published starting in 1990, written by various authors, also in ''Talian'', and published by the same newspaper, now called Correio Riograndense.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Costa |first=Rovílio |url=http://www.esteditora.com.br/correio/4869/4869.htm |title=80 anos de sonhos e realidade de um cidadão do mundo |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Correio Riograndense |date=2004-01-21 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.esteditora.com.br/correio/4869/4869.htm |archive-date=2010-08-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.esteditora.com.br/correio/4977/4977.htm |title=Livros ampliam influência de Nanetto |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Correio Riograndense |date=2006-03-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.esteditora.com.br/correio/4977/4977.htm |archive-date=2010-08-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref> A humorous parody of the settler's image was created by cartoonist Carlos Henrique Iotti, ''Radicci'', published in various newspapers and awarded the HQ Mix Trophy, Brazil's most prestigious award for [[comics]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.revistasamizdat.com/2008/11/entrevista-carlos-henrique-iotti.html |title=Entrevista: Carlos Henrique Iotti |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Revista Samidzat |date=2008-11-15 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://www.revistasamizdat.com/2008/11/entrevista-carlos-henrique-iotti.html |archive-date=2010-08-01}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Assis |first=Érico |url=http://omelete.com.br/quadrinhos/conheca-os-vencedores-do-21o-trofeu-hq-mix/ |title=Conheça os vencedores do 21º Troféu HQ Mix |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Omelete |date=2009-08-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100801000000/http://omelete.com.br/quadrinhos/conheca-os-vencedores-do-21o-trofeu-hq-mix/ |archive-date=2010-08-01}}</ref>

had been populated by indigenous peoples for millennia.<ref name="Vita"/> On the other hand, the massive penetration of Brazilian and international culture through various media, combined with the |last=Andrade |first=Manuel Correia de Oliveira |title=As raízes do separatismo no Brasil |publisher=UNESP |year=1998 |pages=46–47 |isbn=978-85-7139-206-9 in the city, with the ''Rincão da Lealdade'' being the oldest, while only one Italian society remains active.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/rs/rio-grande-do-sul/bom-dia-rio-grande/videos/t/edicoes/v/ctg-de-caxias-do-sul-representa-o-rs-em-festival-de-folclore-de-portugal/2033423/ |title=CTG de Caxias do Sul |archive-date=2012-07-10}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Ronsini |first=Veneza Mayora |title=Entre a capela e a caixa de abelhas: identidade cultural de gringos e gaúchos |publisher=EDIPUCRS |year=2004 An example of this is the community of Criúva's rejection of a proposed indigenous-themed park in the region, with public authorities yielding to the protests.<ref name="Vita"/>

Urban folklore stories also persist, such as that of "Martha Rocha de Caxias," a woman who identified with the [[Miss Brazil]] [[Martha Rocha]] and wore a swimsuit and sash in public squares; the tale of Garibaldi, a swindler who offered to sell public works such as viaducts;<ref name="Nostalgia">{{Cite web |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/impressa/11,2456226,499,11996,impressa.html |title=Nostalgia coletiva |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Jornal Pioneiro |date=2009-03-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090328000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/impressa/11,2456226,499,11996,impressa.html |archive-date=2009-03-28}}</ref> stories about the traditional rivalry with the neighboring city of [[Bento Gonçalves, Rio Grande do Sul|Bento Gonçalves]], the subject of many popular jokes;<ref>{{Cite book |last=Sganzerla |first=Edilio |title=300 Gargalhadas |publisher=Editora AGE Ltda |page=44 |isbn=978-85-7497-248-0 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=GdM5LGKCr5QC&dq=bento+gon%C3%A7alves+piada+rivalidade+%22caxias+do+sul%22&pg=PA44}}</ref> and various tales involving Father Giordani, a prominent community leader known for his strict morals and controversial actions.<ref name="Nostalgia"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Garavaglia |first=João |url=http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/cidade/O_Centenario_do_Padre_Giordani/ |title=Centenário do Padre Giordani |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Gazeta de Caxias |date=2010-07-12 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100719044851/http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/cidade/O_Centenario_do_Padre_Giordani/ |archive-date=2010-07-19}}</ref>

==== Festa da Uva ====
{{Main|Festa da Uva}}

[[File:Festa da Uva 1972.jpg|thumb|First live color television broadcast in Brazil at the Festa da Uva in 1972.]]

The Festa da Uva is the main festival of Caxias do Sul, dedicated to celebrating Italian colonization and reviving the community's historical traditions. Held every two years since 1931, it enlivens the entire city with a variety of events over its fifteen-day duration. At the Exhibition Park, hundreds of stalls showcase the region's typical agricultural products, highlighting grapes, while other sections display local activity in cuisine, industry, and commerce. The festival includes music shows, theater, dances, free grape distribution, thematic, artistic, and historical exhibitions, and traditional colonial games such as cheese-throwing, grape-crushing with feet, and tractor races. Wineries and cellars open to tourists, but the most iconic event is the parade of allegorical floats, which illustrate various aspects of the theme chosen for each edition. The parade features the queen and princesses of the Festa, considered true ambassadors of the city, promoting it elsewhere.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Navarro |first=Silvio |url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-caxias_do_sul-festa_da_uva.shtml |title=Caxias do Sul - Festa da Uva |access-date=2010-09-24 |website=Folha online |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924000000/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-caxias_do_sul-festa_da_uva.shtml |archive-date=2010-09-24}}</ref><ref name="Trem">{{Cite web |url=http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/O_Trem_esta_chegando/ |title=O Trem está Chegando |access-date=2025-08-13 |website=Gazeta de Caxias |date=2010-01-15 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100115000000/http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/O_Trem_esta_chegando/ |archive-date=2010-01-15 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.eventos2010.com/2010/02/01/festa-da-uva-2010-em-caxias-do-sul/ |title=Festa da Uva 2010 em Caxias do Sul |access-date=2010-09-24 |website=Eventos 2010 |date=2010-02-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924000000/http://www.eventos2010.com/2010/02/01/festa-da-uva-2010-em-caxias-do-sul/ |archive-date=2010-09-24}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.festanacionaldauva.com.br/2010/site/ |title=Festa da Uva de Caxias do Sul |access-date=2010-09-24 |website=Official page with history and full program |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924000000/http://www.festanacionaldauva.com.br/2010/site/ |archive-date=2010-09-24 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Although Italian immigrants are the protagonists of the Festa, other ethnic groups that contributed to the city's development are also represented and honored.<ref name="Trem"/>

====Handicrafts====
[[File:Cortina-renda.jpg|thumb|180px|Detail of a "filé" lace curtain, a tradition of local handicrafts]]
Handicrafts in Caxias do Sul hold a significant position due to their considerable economic, artistic, touristic, and social impact. Over 2,000 artisans are registered with the municipal government, many of whom are organized into associations. Mayor José Ivo Sartori, when opening the main events dedicated to the sector, the International Culture and Handicraft Fair "Mãos da Terra" and the Caxias Artisan Exhibition, held concurrently, emphasized the importance of integration among artisans, stating that ''"hands are the symbol of work, construction, and friendship. Handicrafts are not just a product but a way of experiencing a people's beliefs and culture. Make this fair an opportunity for good sales, exchange of experiences, and strengthening this activity as a source of income."''<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/noticias/noticias_ler.php?codigo=12514 |title=Aberta Feira Internacional de Cultura e Artesanato - Mãos da Terra e 2ª Mostra do Artesão Caxiense |access-date=April 16, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210515000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/noticias/noticias_ler.php?codigo=12514 |archive-date=May 15, 2021 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Architecture and historical heritage===
[[File:Palácio Episcopal de Caxias do Sul.jpg|thumb|180px|Canonical House, one of the city's historic buildings]]
[[File:Stangherlin-SãoMiguel.jpg|thumb|180px|Pietro Stangherlin: detail of ''Saint Michael Defeating the Demon'', late 19th to early 20th century, Municipal Museum collection]]
[[File:Arquivo Histórico-Caxias do Sul.jpg|thumb|180px|Municipal Historical Archive]]

Official interest in preserving the historical and architectural heritage of the city is relatively recent, beginning only in the mid-1970s and progressing slowly until the last decade. As a result, few buildings survived the urban modernization from the mid-20th century onward, with significant and sometimes irreplaceable losses,<ref name="preservação"/> such as the Cine Teatro Ópera, a unique example of its kind, destroyed by a fire suspected to be arson and replaced by a parking lot.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/impressa/11,2718632,499,13534,impressa.html |title=Paisagem Transitória |access-date=November 16, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/impressa/11,2718632,499,13534,impressa.html |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Frizzo |first=Odir |url=http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/colunas_e_blogs/odirfrizzo/arquivos/junho/2007/ |title=Na Semana de Caxias, o São Paulo, Foi... |access-date=June 22, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080529075444/http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/colunas_e_blogs/odirfrizzo/arquivos/junho/2007/ |archive-date=May 29, 2008}}</ref> Another example is the Casa de Pedra, a typical 19th-century colonial construction and the only building of its kind, once common, that survived in the urban area.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=42 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Museu Ambiência Casa de Pedra |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=42 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> Although converted into a museum since 1975, it was only listed as a heritage site in 2003.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/patrimonio.php?programa=10 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Patrimônio Tombado - Casa de Pedra |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/patrimonio.php?programa=10 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>

However, in recent years, the municipal government, in partnership with other institutions, has begun identifying, listing, and restoring various buildings of historical and architectural value, both in urban and rural areas, and has also started protecting intangible historical heritage.<ref name="Tombado">{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=67 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Patrimônio Tombado |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=67 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/urbal/site/projetos_noticias.php?cod_projeto=7&codigo=44 |title=Registros do Ecirs já atingem oito regiões do interior |access-date=March 21, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210515000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/urbal/site/projetos_noticias.php?cod_projeto=7&codigo=44 |archive-date=May 15, 2021 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Among the [[listed buildings|listed]] buildings in the [[eclectic architecture|eclectic]] style, built between the late 19th and early 20th centuries, are the Saldanha Bookstore, the [[João Spadari Adami Municipal Historical Archive|Carbone Hospital]], the [[Eberle Palace]], the Scotti House, the Agricultural Patronage House, and the Sassi House. The [[Chapel of the Holy Sepulchre]] is an interesting [[Gothic Revival architecture|neo-Gothic]] structure, and the historic buildings of the Abramo Eberle Metalworks are fine representatives of [[modernist architecture|modernist]] industrial construction.<ref name="Tombado"/><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/patrimonio.php |title=Bens Imóveis Tombados |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/patrimonio.php |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> Although not listed, the [[St. Theresa's Cathedral, Caxias do Sul|Caxias do Sul Cathedral]] is of great interest and importance. Built starting in 1895 in the neo-Gothic style, it features a series of German stained glass windows, side altars with statuary by local artists such as Pietro Stangherlin and [[Zambelli Family|Michelangelo Zambelli]], and a richly carved large [[high altar]], the work of Francisco Meneguzzo. The cathedral forms a complex with the Canonical House, an eclectic-style palace serving as the bishop's residence.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.brasilviagem.com/pontur/?CodAtr=66202 |title=Catedral |access-date=September 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.brasilviagem.com/pontur/?CodAtr=66202 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Zambelli |first=Irma Buffon |title=Os Filhos da Arte - Documentário Artístico de uma Família de Imigrantes |publisher=Edição da autora|location=Caxias do Sul |year=1991}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Damasceno |first=Athos |title=Artes Plásticas no Rio Grande do Sul |publisher=Editora Globo |location=Porto Alegre|year=1971}}</ref> In addition to heritage listing protection, according to the 1999 Municipal Organic Law, no building or work over 50 years old—whether public or private buildings, churches, chapels, monuments, statues, squares, or cemeteries—can be demolished without prior authorization from the Municipal Historical and Cultural Heritage Council.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/CAXIAS_POSSUI_28_BENS_TOMBADOS/ |title=Caxias possui 28 bens tombados |access-date=March 3, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/CAXIAS_POSSUI_28_BENS_TOMBADOS/ |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Other institutions linked to the Department of Memory and Cultural Heritage of the Culture Secretariat are dedicated to recovering, studying, systematizing, preserving, and disseminating relics of the past in various forms.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=27 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Apresentação |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=27 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> Among them, the following stand out:
* The [[Municipal Museum of Caxias do Sul]], focused on preserving material records of the immigration and civilizing process in the region. Housed in the former Otolini residence, it holds a large collection of tools used by early farmers, items related to various urban trades, a section of [[sacred art]], and a wide variety of other pieces. The museum is well-structured and offers a range of community-oriented activities.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=47 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Museu Municipal de Caxias do Sul |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=47 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>
* The [[João Spadari Adami Municipal Historical Archive]], established in 1976 and currently housed in the former Carbone Hospital building. It studies and preserves a variety of written and visual documentation from public and private sources.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=28 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Arquivo Histórico Municipal João Spadari Adami |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=28 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>
* The Zambelli Atelier Memorial, which preserves and displays the remnants of the sculpture studio of the important family of sacred image makers and decorators, who worked not only in the municipality but throughout the Italian colonization region.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=41 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Memorial Atelier Zambelli |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=41 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>
* The Primo Slomp Grape and Wine Museum. Focusing on one of the municipality's most characteristic productive activities, the museum was established in 2002 in the historic Forqueta Wine Cooperative building, with a varied collection of objects used in grape production and winemaking, including items related to associated activities such as [[cooperage]] and [[basketry]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=43 |title=Departamento de Memória e Patrimônio Cultural - Museu da Uva e do Vinho Primo Slomp |access-date=September 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/cultura/texto.php?codigo=43 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>
* The Caxias do Sul Railway Site, listed by the IPHAE since 2001 and dedicated to preserving the memory of the railway and trains in the city. The site includes the following structures: the Caxias do Sul Railway Station, the VFRGS (later [[RFFSA]]) railway yard, passenger platforms, the restroom building, the cargo depot, the locomotive depot, the water tank, and the Administrator's House.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.iphae.rs.gov.br/Main.php?do=BensTombadosDetalhesAc&item=14002 |title=IPHAE |access-date=September 8, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.iphae.rs.gov.br/Main.php?do=BensTombadosDetalhesAc&item=14002 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>

Under the administration of the [[Order of Friars Minor Capuchin|Order of Capuchins]], the Capuchin Museum operates, hosting temporary thematic exhibitions<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mugnol |first=Babiana |url=http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,2930170,Exposicao-no-Museu-dos-Capuchinhos-homenageia-frei-Rovilio-Costa.html |title=Exposição no Museu dos Capuchinhos homenageia frei Rovílio Costa |access-date=June 8, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.clicrbs.com.br/pioneiro/rs/plantao/10,2930170,Exposicao-no-Museu-dos-Capuchinhos-homenageia-frei-Rovilio-Costa.html |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> and housing an important collection of sacred art gathered from across the state. It also preserves other objects, such as liturgical vestments, books, paintings, photographs, manuscripts, musical instruments, agricultural tools, household items, and furniture, all related to the history of the Capuchin Province of Rio Grande do Sul.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.ucs.br/ucs/tplEncontroHistoria/extensao/agenda/eventos/cd_99/programacao_academica |title=Programação acadêmico-cultural |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.ucs.br/ucs/tplEncontroHistoria/extensao/agenda/eventos/cd_99/programacao_academica |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.capuchinhosrs.org.br/index.php?ir=PaginaPersonalizada&id_pagina=58&link=30&link_pai=45&sublink=36 |title=Museu do Capuchinhos |access-date=September 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.capuchinhosrs.org.br/index.php?ir=PaginaPersonalizada&id_pagina=58&link=30&link_pai=45&sublink=36 |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Despite the existence of municipal legislation protecting historical heritage, a series of buildings, many of them listed or inventoried, have been altered in "revitalization" or "[[retrofit]]" projects. These projects primarily aim to adapt buildings for new uses but rarely follow the rules established by international heritage charters, of which Brazil is a signatory. These charters advocate for minimal and reversible interventions and the preservation of the authenticity and documentary value of buildings. Many of these interventions have been extensive, introducing new elements and volumes while eliminating original ones, radically altering the assets. However, they have been authorized by the city's Historical Heritage Council, responsible for evaluating and approving such projects. Among the historic buildings that have undergone such "revitalizations" are the Eberle Metalworks complex, the Antunes Winery buildings, the São Victor Wine Cooperative, the Rio Grande Winery, the Caxias Pasta Factory, the Alfred Clothing factory, the Marumby factory, and the Germani Mill complex, among others.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Caon |first=Marcelo |title=Memória e cidade: o processo de preservação do patrimônio histórico edificado em Caxias do Sul 1974-1994 |publisher=Pontifícia Universidade Católica |year=2010 |pages=138–139}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Zanatta |first=Clarissa |title=Projeto Arquitetando Histórias}}</ref><ref name="Stumpp">{{Cite journal |last1=Costa |first1=Ana Elísia da |last2=Moraes |first2=Erinton Aver |last3=Stumpp |first3=Monika |title=Além de Museus: as experiências de reciclagem e requalificação de fábricas em Caxias do Sul |journal=III Seminário Projetar - O Moderno já Passado e o Passado no Moderno - reciclagem, requalificação, rearquitetura |location=Porto Alegre |year=2007}}</ref> According to Costa, Moraes, and Stumpp, most of the so-called "restoration" projects recently carried out in the city are not true restorations but rather renovations lacking theoretical grounding and/or recognition of the historical or aesthetic value of the object, disregarding international principles and prioritizing questionable practical needs and speculative interests.<ref name="Stumpp"/>

===Tourism===
[[File:Casa-de-imigrantes.jpg|thumb|Typical colonial house and winery in the rural area, part of the Caminhos da Colônia tourist route]]

In addition to the traditional Grape Festival, which has strong [[folklore|folkloric]] roots and attracts around one million visitors,<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Schleder |first=Adriana |title=Festa Nacional da Uva: uma nova identidade? |journal=XXXII Congresso Brasileiro de Ciências da Comunicação |location=Curitiba |date=September 4–7, 2009 |pages=1–10}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.tgonline.com.br/materias/festa-da-uva-2010-nos-trilhos-da-historia/ |title=Festa da Uva 2010 nos trilhos da história |access-date=September 21, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.tgonline.com.br/materias/festa-da-uva-2010-nos-trilhos-da-historia/ |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> [[tourism]] in Caxias do Sul has been relatively underexplored but has seen growing attention in recent times. In 2010, the Tourism Secretariat launched the ''Municipal Tourism Week'' project, featuring programs and tours for the public and discussions among experts.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/turismo/noticias_ler.php?codigo=14147 |title=SEMTUR apresenta a programação da 1ª Semana Municipal do Turismo |access-date=September 17, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121114211936/http://www.caxias.rs.gov.br/turismo/noticias_ler.php?codigo=14147 |archive-date=November 14, 2012}}</ref> In addition to established routes such as ''La Città'', ''Caminhos da Colônia'', ''Estrada do Imigrante'', and ''Ana Rech'', where visitors learn about the city's and immigrants’ history while enjoying traditional dishes and characteristic landscapes,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.artedoturismo.com.br/dicas_detalhes.php?id=27 |title=Caxias do Sul - Turismo |access-date=September 21, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.artedoturismo.com.br/dicas_detalhes.php?id=27 |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref> a 2008 partnership between the municipal government and [[Sebrae]]/RS identified other regions with tourism potential, including the rural districts of Fazenda Souza, Santa Lúcia do Piaí, Vila Cristina, Vila Oliva, and Vila Seca, revealing possibilities unknown even to the local population. This study led to new proposals for invigorating the sector. Between 2005 and 2008, the city received an average of 350,000 tourists per year, with projections indicating an 11% growth by December 2011.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://ecoviagem.uol.com.br/noticias/turismo/turismo-rural/caxias-do-sul-abre-as-porteiras-para-o-turismo-rural-10029.asp |title=Caxias do Sul abre as porteiras para o turismo rural |work=EcoViagem - O seu guia de viagem |access-date=August 3, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://ecoviagem.uol.com.br/noticias/turismo/turismo-rural/caxias-do-sul-abre-as-porteiras-para-o-turismo-rural-10029.asp |archive-date=August 15, 2020}}</ref>

In 2007, there were about 20 hotels and 30,000 beds across establishments of all categories, from inns and park hotels to traditional houses. In recent years, several older hotels closely tied to local history, such as the Alfred Hotel and the Real Hotel, have closed, but larger ones, such as the Blue Tree Towers, Norton Hotel, and Intercity Hotel, have entered the market.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/DIMINUI_A_DEMANDA_NOS_HOTEIS_DE_CAXIAS_DO_SUL_/ |title=Diminui a Demanda nos Hotéis de Caxias do Sul |access-date=July 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210515000000/http://www.gazetadecaxias.com.br/noticias/geral/DIMINUI_A_DEMANDA_NOS_HOTEIS_DE_CAXIAS_DO_SUL_/ |archive-date=May 15, 2021 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.caxias.tur.br/lista_estabelecimentos.php?tipo=3 |title=Onde Ficar |access-date=September 21, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.caxias.tur.br/lista_estabelecimentos.php?tipo=3 |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Other well-known attractions include the São Pelegrino Church, with a significant series of paintings by [[Aldo Locatelli]] and notable bronze doors crafted by Augusto Murer; a replica of part of the original urban core built in the Grape Festival exhibition park; the [[National Monument to the Immigrant]], a work by Antonio Caringi; the Casa de Pedra Museum; the [[St. Theresa's Cathedral, Caxias do Sul|Caxias do Sul Cathedral]]; and the [[Municipal Museum of Caxias do Sul]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/municipio/ponto_turistico.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |title=Pontos Turísticos |access-date=September 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200815000000/http://www.cnm.org.br/municipio/ponto_turistico.asp?iIdMun=100143087 |archive-date=August 15, 2020 |pages=1–2}}</ref>

===Cuisine===
[[File:Comidaitaliana-caxias.jpg|thumb|Table with typical Italian dishes: red wine, tortelli with meat sauce, salami, Parmesan cheese, and colonial bread]]

The foundation of Italian folk cuisine in Caxias do Sul includes roasted ''galeto'' (''galeto al primo canto''), soft or fried [[polenta]], and [[pasta]] dishes such as ''bigoli'' ([[spaghetti]]) and ''tortei'' (dumplings stuffed with [[pumpkin]] purée seasoned with [[nutmeg]]),<ref name="Galeto"/><ref name="gastronomia">{{Cite web |url=http://www.guiadecaxiasdosul.com/site/gastronomia.php |title=Gastronomia |access-date=September 22, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121029160917/http://www.guiadecaxiasdosul.com/site/gastronomia.php |archive-date=October 29, 2012}}</ref> but other dishes are also popular, such as ''[[agnolini]]'' soup (stuffed pasta filled with chicken meat), ''radicci'' salad with [[pancetta]] (a variety of [[chicory]] with narrow, bitter leaves seasoned with fried [[bacon]]), ''[[Canjica (dish)|canjica]]'', ''crem'' (a type of grated horseradish preserved in red [[vinegar]] and used as a meat condiment), homemade breads and biscuits, [[chicken soup]], [[risotto]], preserves and sweets, notably ''uvada'', [[salami]], ''fortaia'' ([[omelette]] with cheese or salami), and [[cheese]], along with many varieties of [[wine]].<ref name="Galeto"/><ref name="Molon">{{Cite book |last=Molon |first=Floriano |title=A Influência da Imigração Italiana na Mesa do Brasileiro |editor=Suliani, Antônio |publisher=EDIPUCRS |year=2001 |pages=459–461 |isbn=978-85-7430-181-5 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Bv_x5z13fD4C&dq=%22tradi%C3%A7%C3%B5es+italianas%22+%22caxias+do+sul%22&pg=PA395}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://oriundi.net/site/oriundi.php?menu=noticiasdet&id=11521 |title=Entrai, Encontro das Tradições Italianas, começa nesta sexta-feira, em Farroupilha |access-date=May 8, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://oriundi.net/site/oriundi.php?menu=noticiasdet&id=11521 |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> However, influenced by other ethnic groups and local conditions that necessitated cultivating crops better suited to the region's climate, the city developed a cuisine with more varied and, in many ways, unique characteristics. From the Germans, the [[potato]] was incorporated, and from the gauchos, locals adopted [[jerky]], [[barbecue]], [[beans]], [[rice]], manioc, [[sweet potato]], ''[[chimarrão]]'', ''[[cachaça]]'', native wild fruits such as ''gabiroba'', [[blackberry]], and [[Eugenia uniflora|pitanga]], and [[pine nut]] from the araucaria tree.<ref name="Galeto"/><ref name="Molon"/>

In recent times, to appeal to new clientele, traditional cuisine has also been adapted and "softened": the ''radicci'', once prized for its bitterness, is now commonly served in restaurants using younger, less bitter leaves; cheese and salami boards are enriched with varieties not part of the early immigrants’ menu, recipes are adapted using ingredients different from traditional ones, and dish presentation has been modified under the influence of international standards and tourism agency demands.<ref name="Galeto"/>

The city is known for the abundance and quality of its cuisine, served in approximately 300 restaurants and about 600 other establishments. Several specialize in traditional cuisine, others focus on burgers and steaks, and in recent years, some have diversified their menus, embracing international cuisines, particularly from the East, especially Japanese and Chinese. The most significant growth has been in pizzerias, all-you-can-eat restaurants, and delivery services. The snack sector is also expanding and diversifying, with many establishments resembling restaurants. There is also a good number of ''[[churrascaria]]s''.<ref name="gastronomia"/>

===Holidays===
In addition to the [[Public holidays in Brazil|national holidays]] established by law, the city has its own holidays: [[Good Friday]] and [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]] (floating), May 26, the day of [[Our Lady of Caravaggio]],<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.sindilojas-cs.com.br/relacoes_sindicais_feriados.php?abre=7 |title=Feriados |access-date=September 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.sindilojas-cs.com.br/relacoes_sindicais_feriados.php?abre=7 |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> and November 2, [[All Souls' Day|All Souls’ Day]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/uploads/1227711762Feriados_Munic___26.11.08.pdf |title=Feriados Municipais do Rio Grande do Sul |access-date=September 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113003326/http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/uploads/1227711762Feriados_Munic___26.11.08.pdf |archive-date=November 13, 2012 |url-status=dead |page=17}}</ref>
