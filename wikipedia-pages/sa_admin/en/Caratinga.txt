'''Caratinga''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the countryside of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]] in the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]] of [[Brazil]]. Situated in the [[Vale do Rio Doce (mesoregion)|Vale do Rio Doce]], it is part of the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area]], approximately {{convert|310|km|mi}} east of the state capital, [[Belo Horizonte]]. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|1258.479|km2|sqmi}}, with {{convert|17|km2|sqmi}} in the [[urban area]], and its population was estimated at 90,687 inhabitants in 2024.

The exploration of the region began in the 16th century with expeditions along the [[Doce River|Doce]] and Caratinga rivers in search of precious metals. However, the area was first settled in the 19th century when Domingos Fernandes Lana explored it for [[ipecac]], a valuable medicinal plant, and reported on the fertile lands and peaceful indigenous population. Subsequently, João Caetano do Nascimento, a friend of Lana, led an expedition to the region, claimed the land, and dedicated it to [[John the Baptist|Saint John]], officially founding the settlement on 24 June 1848, in honor of the saint's feast day.

Favorable agricultural conditions and its strategic location as the only urban center on the right bank of the Caratinga River spurred population growth, leading to emancipation from [[Manhuaçu]] in 1890. Despite unplanned urban expansion, development accelerated with the arrival of the [[Leopoldina Railway]] and the [[BR-116 (Brazil highway)|BR-116]] (Rio–Bahia Highway) in the 1930s and 1940s. The coffee industry flourished in the 1950s following the establishment of an office of the Brazilian Coffee Institute, positioning Caratinga as a coffee production hub. Coffee cultivation, alongside commerce, remains a primary source of income, though industry has grown since the 1980s. A new urban nucleus also emerged parallel to the original city center due to the expansion of the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area]].

Caratinga is home to significant environmental conservation areas, including the [[Feliciano Miguel Abdala Private Natural Heritage Reserve]], one of Minas Gerais' most important [[Atlantic Forest]] remnants, which shelters the [[northern muriqui]], one of the largest primates in the [[Americas]]. The reserve attracts researchers from around the world. Other notable landmarks include the Pedra Itaúna, historic farms, waterfalls, and lagoons in the rural areas, and the scenic Cesário Alvim Square, which includes the [[St. John the Baptist Cathedral, Caratinga|St. John the Baptist Cathedral]], built in 1930. Cultural traditions such as the Folia de Reis, artistic festivals, and [[Festa Junina|June festivals]] are also prominent.

== History ==
=== Exploration and colonization ===
The area now comprising Caratinga saw little colonization until the mid-19th century, except by the indigenous [[Aimoré]]s (Bugre tribe). Occasionally, the [[Botocudo]] people ventured along the Caratinga River from its confluence with the [[Doce River]] in search of food or due to weather conditions.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010">{{cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br:8080/historia |title=History of the Municipality |author=Prefeitura |date=1 July 2010 |access-date=23 April 2011 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120124032851/http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br:8080/historia |archive-date=24 January 2012}}</ref><ref name="Prefeitura_História2013">{{cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/historia/6528 |title=History |author=Prefeitura |date=7 May 2013 |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170706203314/http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/historia/6528 |archive-date=6 July 2017}}</ref> Exploration began with expeditions such as that of Spinosa in 1553, heading toward the [[São Francisco River]],<ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico">{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/painel/historico.php?lang=&codmun=311340&search=%7Ccaratinga |title=Historical Overview |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=1966 |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170706211527/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/painel/historico.php?lang=&codmun=311340&search=%7Ccaratinga |archive-date=6 July 2017}}</ref> and Sebastião Fernandes Tourinho, who explored the Caratinga River in 1573.<ref name="Monteiro_160">{{cite web|url=http://www.camara.gov.br/sileg/integras/577651.pdf |title=Caratinga, 160 Years |author=Leonardo Monteiro |date=18 June 2008 |publisher=Câmara dos Deputados |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170706210942/http://www.camara.gov.br/sileg/integras/577651.pdf |archive-date=6 July 2017}}</ref>

In the early 18th century, during the [[Colonial Brazil|colonial period]], settlers from the [[Captaincy of Espírito Santo]] ventured inland, primarily seeking [[gold]]. Notably, Pedro Bueno Cacunda, in 1733, explored the region encompassing much of present-day Caratinga, between the Itapeba River (later named Cuiethé, now Caratinga) and the Mayguassu River ([[Manhuaçu River]]), searching for gold in streams and tributaries.<ref>{{cite book |year=1924 |title=Annals of the National Library of Rio de Janeiro |url=http://docvirt.com/docreader.net/DocReader.aspx?bib=Anais_BN_WI&PagFis=18511&Pesq= |volume=46 |location=Rio de Janeiro, RJ |publisher=Bibliotheca Nacional |page=200 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170706204638/http://docvirt.com/docreader.net/DocReader.aspx?bib=Anais_BN_WI&PagFis=18511&Pesq= |archive-date=6 July 2017 |access-date=6 July 2017 |trans-title=Annals of the National Library of Rio de Janeiro |language=Portuguese}}</ref> By the late 18th century, the Estrada do Degredo was established, crossing the current municipal territory and connecting the [[Estrada Real]] in [[Ouro Preto]] to the Cuité prison (now [[Conselheiro Pena]]), serving as an alternative route to the Doce River.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://issuu.com/juliadevasconcelos/docs/cau_-_monografia_-_julia_quaresma |title=Historical and Cultural Heritage of Ipatinga - Use as a Protection Tool |author=Júlia de Vasconcelos Quaresma |date=June 2015 |publisher=Centro Universitário Católica do Leste de Minas Gerais (Unileste) |pages=16–17 |access-date=7 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707185000/https://issuu.com/juliadevasconcelos/docs/cau_-_monografia_-_julia_quaresma |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref>

In the 19th century, Domingos Fernandes Lana, from [[Araponga]], explored the region in search of [[ipecac]], a valuable medicinal plant. Around 1841, accompanied by friends, servants, slaves, and catechized indigenous people, he traversed the Caratinga River. Lana named the area's rock formations "Caratinga" due to the abundance of [[Yam (vegetable)|yam]], an edible tuber also known as ''caratinga''.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/><ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> The term derives from the [[Tupi language|Tupi]] word ''aka'ratin'ga'', meaning "white yam".<ref>{{cite book |author=Ferreira, A. B. H. |title=New Dictionary of the Portuguese Language |edition=2nd |location=Rio de Janeiro |publisher=Nova Fronteira |year=1986 |page=349 |trans-title=New Dictionary of the Portuguese Language |language=Portuguese}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |author=Ferreira, A. B. H. |title=New Dictionary of the Portuguese Language |edition=2nd |location=Rio de Janeiro |publisher=Nova Fronteira |year=1986 |page=346 |trans-title=New Dictionary of the Portuguese Language |language=Portuguese}}</ref> The expedition continued toward Cuité, near Conselheiro Pena.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/>

The fertile lands and peaceful indigenous population attracted João Caetano do Nascimento, a friend of Lana, who arrived with his elder sons and companions João da Cunha, João José, and João Antonio de Oliveira, along with their families and servants.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/><ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> On 23 June 1848, they celebrated [[John the Baptist|Saint John]]'s Day with a large bonfire, dedicating the land and founding the settlement in honor of the patron saint.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> João Caetano settled in the area now known as Serra da Jacutinga, claiming large land grants. He began clearing vegetation and preparing the land for cultivating cereals, fruits, and vegetables, as well as raising small animals and poultry, encouraging further settlement.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/><ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico"/> In June 1848, a parish was established under the ''[[comarca]]'' of Mariana.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/>

=== Emancipation and administrative development ===
[[File:Vista parcial da Igreja São João, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|São João Church, Caratinga's first parish church]]
[[File:Vista parcial da Escola Estadual Princesa Isabel, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Princesa Isabel state school, built in 1909.]]
The settlement initially developed irregularly.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> In 1867, construction began on the São João Church, now a listed cultural heritage site,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=13860 |title=Compac Members Visit Restoration Works of São João Church |author=Super Canal |date=6 June 2014 |access-date=11 July 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180711170647/http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=13860 |archive-date=11 July 2018}}</ref> with Father Maximiano João da Cruz as its first priest.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> By Provincial Law No. 2,027 of 1 December 1873, the settlement was elevated to a district under [[Manhuaçu]], named São João do Caratinga.<ref name="IBGE_História"/> A parish was created to represent the community.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diocesecaratinga.org.br/paroquia/catedral-de-sao-joao-batista/ |title=São João Batista Cathedral |author=Diocese de Caratinga |access-date=11 July 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180711171115/http://www.diocesecaratinga.org.br/paroquia/catedral-de-sao-joao-batista/ |archive-date=11 July 2018}}</ref>

The fertile lands and its position as the only urban center on the right bank of the Caratinga River sustained population growth, attracting settlers from southern regions aiming to reach the Doce River. This established Caratinga as a significant regional hub,<ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico"/> leading to its emancipation by State Decree No. 16 on 6 February 1890, under the name Caratinga, signed by Governor [[Cesário Alvim|José Cesário de Faria Alvim]].<ref name="IBGE_História"/><ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/><ref name="Prosperustec_Aeroporto">{{cite web|url=http://ong.prosperustec.com.br/mprd/wp-content/uploads/2008/10/1USMG004-1-EA-EIA-0001.pdf |title=New Ipatinga Airport: Unsustainable Development |author=Prosperustec |date=4 October 2008 |pages=176–177 |access-date=7 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707185918/http://ong.prosperustec.com.br/mprd/wp-content/uploads/2008/10/1USMG004-1-EA-EIA-0001.pdf |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref> In 1890, the population was approximately 25,000 across an area of {{convert|10572|km2|sqmi}}.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/>

Upon emancipation, Caratinga included the districts of Bom Jesus do Galho, Cuieté, Entre Folhas, Floresta, Santo Antônio do Manhuaçu, and São Francisco Vermelho, alongside the municipal seat. The municipality was officially established on 12 May 1890, confirmed by State Law No. 2 of 14 September 1891.<ref name="IBGE_História"/><ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> The first [[municipal chamber]] was sworn in on 7 March 1892, with Symphrônio Fernandes as president and municipal governor.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> The Caratinga ''comarca'' was created by Law No. 11 of 13 November 1891, enacted by Cesário Alvim, and established on 7 May 1892. It was briefly abolished in 1912 but restored on 1 December 1917.<ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico"/>

Over time, the municipality underwent several administrative changes. In 1911, it comprised Bom Jesus do Galho (emancipated in 1943), Cuieté (incorporated into [[Itanhomi]] in 1923, now [[Conselheiro Pena]]), [[Entre Folhas]] (emancipated in 1992), Floresta (incorporated into [[Itanhomi]] in 1923, now [[Alvarenga, Minas Gerais|Alvarenga]]), Imbé (emancipated in 1995 as [[Imbé de Minas]]), [[Inhapim]] (emancipated in 1938), [[Resplendor]] (incorporated into [[Aimorés]] in 1915), Santo Antônio do Manhuaçu, São Francisco do Vermelho (abolished before 1920, restored in 1924 as a district of [[Raul Soares]]), [[Tarumirim]] (incorporated into [[Itanhomi]] in 1923), and [[Vermelho Novo]] (incorporated into [[Matipó]] in 1923), alongside the municipal seat. With Inhapim's emancipation in 1938, the districts of Santo Estevão and Veadinho (created in 1923) were also detached.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

Until the end of the [[First Brazilian Republic]], in the 1930s, [[coronelism]] dominated, with two political factions, "''caranguejo''" and "''bacurau''", alternating power.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> From 1919 to 1930, the Empresa Industrial de Caratinga began producing and distributing electricity. In 1927, Agenor Ludgero Alves, a prominent political figure, signed a contract integrating Caratinga into the [[Leopoldina Railway]], inaugurated in 1930.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> The railway spurred urban development by making it easier to open roads and access other regions.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/> Initially, it provided passenger transport to [[Rio de Janeiro (city)|Rio de Janeiro]].<ref name="Estações_Caratinga"/> Between the 1930s and 1940s, Mayor Omar Coutinho cleared public debts, and the [[BR-116 (Brazil highway)|BR-116]] (Rio–Bahia Highway) enhanced commerce and development.<ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico"/> The railway ceased passenger and cargo transport in the 1970s.<ref name="Estações_Caratinga"/>

=== Urban consolidation and recent history ===
[[File:Antiga Estação de Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Old Caratinga Railway Station, built in 1930 and deactivated in 1970s.]]
The coffee industry surged in the 1950s with the establishment of a Brazilian Coffee Institute office, making Caratinga a coffee production hub.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2010"/> However, in the 1950s and 1960s, many residents migrated to the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area|Vale do Aço]] for opportunities driven by the industrial complexes of [[Aperam South America|Acesita]] in [[Timóteo (municipality)|Timóteo]] and [[Usiminas]] in [[Ipatinga]].<ref name="Monteiro_160"/> Urban expansion occurred in the 1970s under Mayor Moacyr de Mattos toward the Limoeiro neighborhood and in the 1990s under Dário da Anunciação Grossi toward the second unit of the Caratinga University Center.<ref name="Prefeitura_História2013"/> However, growth was unplanned, extending toward surrounding hills, leading to irregular land occupations and slums.<ref name="Portal_Leitura">{{cite web|url=http://www.portaldiario.info/caratinga/verconteudos.php?idnoticiasgeral=4619 |title=Joafe's Column - Dynamic Reading - 20-01 |author=Joaquim Felício |date=20 January 2011 |publisher=Portal Diário |access-date=24 April 2011 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/query?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.portaldiario.info%2Fcaratinga%2Fverconteudos.php%3Fidnoticiasgeral%3D4619&date=2011-04-24 |archive-date=24 April 2011}}</ref><ref name="PDDI3_611a613">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=611–613}}</ref>

Parallel to the original city center, a new development zone emerged near the border with Ipatinga. As urban expansion space diminished in the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area]], the [[BR-458 (Brazil highway)|BR-458]] facilitated access, prompting investors to develop subdivisions in Caratinga. In the 1980s and 1990s, Caratinga integrated into the Vale do Aço urban agglomeration, alongside [[Belo Oriente]] and [[Santana do Paraíso]].<ref name="PDDI1_43a44">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=43–44}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_100a101">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=100–101}}</ref> In January 2012, Caratinga was officially included in the Vale do Aço metropolitan area by Complementary Law 122.<ref name="Dinâmica">{{cite web|url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=94831 |title=Creation of the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Agency |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=6 January 2012 |access-date=1 March 2014 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150625194209/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=94831 |archive-date=25 June 2015 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Praça Cesário Alvim e Pedra Itaúna ao fundo, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Cesário Alvim in 2018]]

State Law No. 10,704 of 27 April 1992 led to the emancipation of the districts of Entre Folhas (created in 1890), [[Ubaporanga]] (1938), [[Santa Bárbara do Leste]] (1948), [[Santa Rita de Minas]] (1948), [[Vargem Alegre]] (1953), and [[Ipaba]] (1982). State Law No. 12,030 of 21 December 1995 emancipated Imbé de Minas (1890) and [[Piedade de Caratinga]] (1991). Since then, the municipality comprises the districts of Santo Antônio do Manhuaçu (1890), Dom Lara (1948), Santa Efigênia de Caratinga (1948), Sapucaia (1948), São Cândido (1952), São João do Jacutinga (1952), Cordeiro de Minas (1991), Patrocínio de Caratinga (1991), Santa Luzia de Caratinga (1991), and Dom Modesto (1992), alongside the municipal seat.<ref name="IBGE_História"/> Despite these changes, Caratinga remains a regional hub for cities west of the [[Serra do Caparaó]].<ref name="Monteiro_160"/>

2004 |publisher=Folha de S.Paulo |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707012045/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/cotidiano/ult95u88555.shtml |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref>

Efforts to channel urban streams and mitigate flood damage have been implemented, including river level monitoring and flood alert systems to reduce the impact of potential overflows.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=30502&t=caratinga-e-beneficiada-com-o-pac-2 |title=Caratinga Benefits from PAC 2 |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=30 December 2010 |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707014147/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=30502&t=caratinga-e-beneficiada-com-o-pac-2 |archive-date=7 July 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> After sewage fees were charged since 1999,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mpmg.mp.br/comunicacao/noticias/copasa-e-condenada-a-ressarcir-usuarios-de-caratinga.htm#.WV7oAITyveY |title=Copasa Ordered to Compensate Caratinga Users |author=Ministério Público do Estado de Minas Gerais |date=15 September 2014 |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707020846/http://www.mpmg.mp.br/comunicacao/noticias/copasa-e-condenada-a-ressarcir-usuarios-de-caratinga.htm |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref> a wastewater treatment plant began operations in 2017.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=35203 |title=Copasa Requests Provisional Operation Authorization for Caratinga ETE |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=20 January 2017 |access-date=6 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707020858/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=35203 |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref> Overall, access to basic infrastructure (piped water, sewage, waste collection, and electricity) expanded significantly between the 1990s and 2010s, though slums and a significant [[housing shortage]] persist, among the highest in the Vale do Aço metropolitan area.<ref name="PDDI3_590a598">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=590–598}}</ref>

== Geography ==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), Caratinga's area is {{convert|1258.479|km2|sqmi}}, with {{convert|17.09|km2|sqmi}} in the [[urban area]].<ref name="IBGE_Caratinga"/> Located at {{coord|19|47|24|S|42|08|20|W}}, it is {{convert|311|km|mi}} east of [[Belo Horizonte]], within the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area]] alongside 23 other cities since January 2012.<ref name="Dinâmica"/> Its neighboring municipalities are [[Inhapim]] to the northeast and north; [[Imbé de Minas]], [[Piedade de Caratinga]], [[Ubaporanga]], [[Iapu]], and [[Ipaba]] to the north; [[Santana do Paraíso]] to the northwest; [[Ipatinga]] and [[Timóteo (municipality)|Timóteo]] to the west; [[Bom Jesus do Galho]], [[Vargem Alegre]], and [[Entre Folhas]] to the southwest; [[Raul Soares]], [[Santa Rita de Minas]], and [[Santa Bárbara do Leste]] to the south; [[Simonésia]] to the southeast; and [[Ipanema, Minas Gerais|Ipanema]] to the east.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/>

According to the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] established by the IBGE in 2017,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Regional Division of Brazil |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=5 September 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170905163107/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=5 September 2017}}</ref> Caratinga belongs to the Ipatinga Intermediate Geographic Region and the Caratinga Immediate Geographic Region.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Database of Municipalities in Brazil's Immediate and Intermediate Geographic Regions |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=10 February 2018}}</ref> Previously, under the [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregion]] and [[Microregions of Brazil|microregion]] divisions, it was part of the Caratinga microregion within the Vale do Rio Doce mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=2016 Brazilian Territorial Division |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=5 September 2017}}</ref>

=== Topography and hydrography ===
[[File:Relevo montanhoso em Caratinga MG.jpg|thumb|right|Undulating terrain in the municipality]]
The predominant terrain in Caratinga is characterized by a "sea of hills," with undulating landscapes, isolated rock formations, and susceptibility to erosion and landslides.<ref name="Londrina_Percepção"/> The highest elevation is {{convert|1516|m|ft}} at Serra do Rio Preto, while the lowest is at the mouth of the Boachá stream, at {{convert|330|m|ft}}. The city center is at an elevation of {{convert|578.1|m|ft}}.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> The original urban area developed amidst undulating terrain and expanded toward surrounding hills without a proper urban plan, leading to irregular occupations and land cuts prone to landslides.<ref name="Portal_Leitura"/> The local soil features [[granite]] and [[gneiss]] complexes interspersed with [[mafic]] minerals, supporting [[latosol]]s (red-yellow soils with iron oxides), ideal for coffee cultivation.<ref name="Londrina_Percepção">{{cite web|url=https://geografiahumanista.files.wordpress.com/2009/11/nelson_percepcao.pdf |title=Perception of the Impacts of the 2003 and 2004 Floods on the Economy of the Caratinga River Basin |author=Sena Filho |date=November 2009 |publisher=Universidade Estadual de Londrina |pages=2–4 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705204628/https://geografiahumanista.files.wordpress.com/2009/11/nelson_percepcao.pdf |archive-date=5 July 2017|first=Nelson de|last2=Barros|first2=Cláudio Soares|last3=Matos|first3=Moisés Arimatéia de}}</ref>

The municipality lies within the Caratinga River basin, part of the [[Doce River Basin]].<ref name="PMS_Caratinga">{{cite web|url=http://www.pmsbfunec.com.br/Produtos/Caratinga/Caratinga/P1.pdf |title=Caratinga Municipal Sanitation Plan |author=Prefeitura |date=2015 |publisher=Planos Municipais de Saneamento Básico (PMSB) |pages=12–13 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705163227/http://www.pmsbfunec.com.br/Produtos/Caratinga/Caratinga/P1.pdf |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> The [[Doce River]] borders the municipality at its boundaries with [[Ipatinga]] and [[Santana do Paraíso]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/uf.php?lang=&coduf=31&search=minas-gerais |title=Minas Gerais - Caratinga |author=IBGE Cidades |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705163428/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/uf.php?lang=&coduf=31&search=minas-gerais |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> while the Caratinga River runs through the municipal territory and urban area, joined by smaller streams such as Salim, Sales, Santa Cruz, São João, and Seco. Urban waterways are subject to considerable levels of [[water contamination]], causing flooding, unpleasant odors, and the proliferation of insects.<ref name="IBGE_Mapa">{{cite web|url=http://bibliAoteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/SE-23-Z-D-VI.jpg |title=Sheet SE-23-Z-D-VI |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=1980 |publisher=Biblioteca IBGE |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160304002424/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/SE-23-Z-D-VI.jpg |archive-date=4 March 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/canalizacao-dos-corregos-ja-apresenta-beneficios/6933 |title=Channeling of streams already shows benefits |author=Prefeitura |date=20 November 2013 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705163306/http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/canalizacao-dos-corregos-ja-apresenta-beneficios/6933 |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> The rural area features numerous springs and streams, including the Providência, Chaves, Calixto, Macaquinho, Silvestre, Marcinos, Leite, Pastor, and São Cândido springs, as well as the Ribeirão do Boi, Macaco, Lagoa Nova, and Laje streams. The Laje stream supplies the city with water.<ref name="IBGE_Mapa"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=5866 |title=Lage or Laje |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=5 February 2015 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705163459/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=5866 |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=28849 |title=Springs "Rescue" Córrego do Lage Residents |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=31 August 2016 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705163403/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=28849 |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> Lagoons are common in the western part of the municipality.<ref name="IBGE_Mapa"/><ref name="Caratinga_Acessa"/>

=== Climate ===
[[File:Por do Sol Pedra Itauna Caratinga.jpg|thumb|left|The city at [[sunset]] viewed from Pedra Itaúna]]
Caratinga's climate ranges from mild mesothermal [[Tropical climate|tropical]] [[Tropical savanna climate|semi-humid]] to hot semi-humid ([[Köppen climate classification|Aw]]),<ref name="IBGE_Clima"/><ref name="Climatologia_CaratingaMG">{{cite web|url=https://pt.climate-data.org/location/24946/ |title=Climate: Caratinga |author=Climate-Data.org |access-date=19 June 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180619114754/https://pt.climate-data.org/location/24946/ |archive-date=19 June 2018}}</ref> with a hot semi-humid climate near the Doce River.<ref name="IBGE_Clima"/> The average annual temperature is {{convert|22|°C|°F}}, with an average rainfall of {{convert|1200|mm|in}} per year, concentrated between October and April.<ref name="NCB_1981-2010"/> The [[wet season]] coincides with warmer months, while the [[dry season]] spans milder months, with [[autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] as transitional seasons.<ref name="Climatologia_CaratingaMG"/><ref name="Weatherspark">{{cite web|url=https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30695/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Caratinga-Brasil-durante-o-ano |title=Average Weather Conditions in Caratinga |author=Weather Spark |access-date=30 August 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180830220712/https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30695/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Caratinga-Brasil-durante-o-ano |archive-date=30 August 2018}}</ref> The transition between dry and wet seasons often brings [[thunderstorm]]s, especially from late [[winter]] to spring.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://diariodecaratinga.com.br/em-doze-horas-chove-51-milimetros-em-caratinga/ |title=51 Millimeters of Rain in Twelve Hours in Caratinga |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=2 November 2017 |access-date=6 September 2020 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20200906130419/https://diariodecaratinga.com.br/em-doze-horas-chove-51-milimetros-em-caratinga/ |archive-date=6 September 2020}}</ref>

[[Fog]] occurs on cold mornings due to high humidity and low temperatures,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cptec.inpe.br/noticias/imprimir/17393 |title=Low Temperatures in MG Areas |author=Centro de Previsão de Tempo e Estudos Climáticos (CPTEC) |date=9 May 2011 |access-date=19 June 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180619114817/https://www.cptec.inpe.br/noticias/imprimir/17393 |archive-date=19 June 2018}}</ref> while [[frost]] may occur in rural areas during intense polar [[air mass]] events, such as in Santa Luzia de Caratinga in July 2019, with unofficial sub-zero temperatures.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0069931-semana-teve-o-dia-mais-frio-do-ano-na-regiao |title=The region experienced the coldest day of the year this week |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=13 July 2019 |access-date=30 August 2019 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20190830171848/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0069931-semana-teve-o-dia-mais-frio-do-ano-na-regiao |archive-date=30 August 2019}}</ref> The [[Prevailing winds|prevailing wind]] comes from the east, with an average speed of {{convert|11.1|km/h|mph}} during the windiest period (5 August to 11 January), peaking slightly in September and October. During the calmer period (April to June), speeds range from {{convert|8|to|9|km/h|mph}}.<ref name="Weatherspark"/>

According to the [[National Institute of Meteorology]]'s (INMET) conventional measurements for the periods from 1961 to 1968, 1971 to 1983, 1986 to 1987, and from January 1991 to June 2024, the lowest temperature recorded was {{convert|4.2|°C|°F}} on 1 June 1979 and 16 June 2006,<ref name="BD-INMET"/> though the absolute minimum since 1924 was {{convert|1.4|°C|°F}} on 18 July 1926.<ref name="FJP_Anuário1994">{{cite journal|author=Fundação João Pinheiro |year=1994 |title=Statistical Yearbook of Minas Gerais 1990-1993 |journal=Secretaria de Estado do Planejamento e Coordenação Geral |pages=54–55 |issn=0101-7918 |url=http://www.bibliotecadigital.mg.gov.br/consulta/verDocumento.php?iCodigo=51563 |volume=8 |location=Belo Horizonte, MG |access-date=12 March 2019 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20190312212032/http://www.bibliotecadigital.mg.gov.br/consulta/verDocumento.php?iCodigo=51563 |archive-date=12 March 2019}}</ref> The highest was {{convert|39|°C|°F}} on 7 October 2020.<ref name="BD-INMET"/> The lowest relative humidity was 22% on 12 July 1996 and 5 September 2008.<ref name="BD-INMET"/> The highest 24-hour precipitation was {{convert|164.9|mm|in}} on 2 December 2017, followed by {{convert|150|mm|in}} on 26 March 1973, {{convert|131.5|mm|in}} on 22 April 1977, {{convert|123.2|mm|in}} on 31 October 2020, and {{convert|120.7|mm|in}} on 25 January 2020.<ref name="BD-INMET"/><ref name="FJP_Anuário1994"/>

Data from INMET's [[automatic weather station]], installed on 23 May 2007, recorded a low of {{convert|6.4|°C|°F}} on 31 July 2007 and a high of {{convert|39.4|°C|°F}} on 31 October 2012, 17 October 2015, and 25 September 2023. The lowest relative humidity was 14% on multiple dates. The highest 24-hour precipitation was {{convert|126|mm|in}} on 25 January 2020, with the highest hourly precipitation of {{convert|72.4|mm|in}} between 19:00 and 20:00 on 27 February 2013. The strongest [[wind gust]] was {{convert|88.56|km/h|mph}} during a storm on 9 December 2010.<ref name="INMET-A554-Caratinga-MG"/><ref name="Gráficos-INMET-A554-Caratinga-MG"/>

=== Ecology and environment ===
[[File:Northern Muriqui 5.jpg|thumb|right|upright|A [[northern muriqui]] in the [[Feliciano Miguel Abdala Private Natural Heritage Reserve]]]]
The native [[vegetation]] belongs to the [[Atlantic Forest]] domain,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mma.gov.br/estruturas/sbf_chm_rbbio/_arquivos/relatorio_tcnico_mata_atlantica_2008_2009_72.pdf |title=Monitoring of the Atlantic Forest Biome 2008 to 2009 |author=Ministério do Meio Ambiente |page=53 |date=1 February 2012 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705205214/http://www.mma.gov.br/estruturas/sbf_chm_rbbio/_arquivos/relatorio_tcnico_mata_atlantica_2008_2009_72.pdf |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> with remnants covering {{convert|11156|ha|acre}} in 2011, or 8.9% of the municipal area.<ref name="SOSMata">{{cite web|url=http://www.sosma.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Atlas_municipio_completo2012.pdf |title=Atlas of Atlantic Forest Remnants |author=Fundação SOS Mata Atlântica |page=10 |date=20 August 2012 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140507044742/http://www.sosma.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Atlas_municipio_completo2012.pdf |archive-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> Much of the original vegetation has been cleared for pastures<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ciencia.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,reservas-particulares-chave-da-preservacao-da-mata-atlantica,20030203p72697 |title=Private Reserves, Key to Atlantic Forest Preservation |author=O Estado de S. Paulo |date=3 February 2003 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707012450/http://ciencia.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,reservas-particulares-chave-da-preservacao-da-mata-atlantica,20030203p72697 |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref> and agriculture, which continues to pressure conservation areas.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bibliotecadigital.ufmg.br/dspace/bitstream/handle/1843/BUBD-9ENJ8P/patr_cia_santos_tese_final.pdf?sequence=1 |title=Herpetofauna of the Sossego-Caratinga Corridor, Atlantic Forest of Southeast Brazil: Community Structure and Landscape Influence |author=Patrícia da Silva Santos |date=7 June 2013 |publisher=Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (UFMG) |page=5 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705210530/http://www.bibliotecadigital.ufmg.br/dspace/bitstream/handle/1843/BUBD-9ENJ8P/patr_cia_santos_tese_final.pdf?sequence=1 |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> Some areas of the municipality practice monoculture reforestation with [[eucalyptus]] to produce raw material for the Cenibra pulp mill in [[Belo Oriente]].<ref name="Cenibra_Monitoramento">{{cite web|url=https://www.cenibra.com.br/noticias/monitoramentos-evidenciam-bom-manejo-florestal-na-regiao-da-apa-caratinga/ |title=Monitoring Highlights Good Forest Management in the Caratinga APA Region |author=Cenibra |date=22 June 2015 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232436/https://www.cenibra.com.br/noticias/monitoramentos-evidenciam-bom-manejo-florestal-na-regiao-da-apa-caratinga/ |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> Caratinga has two [[Environmental protection area (Brazil)|environmental protection areas]] (APAs): the Lagoas de Caratinga APA and the Pedra Itaúna APA.<ref name="PDDI2_358a359">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=358–359}}</ref> The Lagoas de Caratinga APA is largely used for eucalyptus cultivation,<ref name="Cenibra_Monitoramento"/> while the Pedra Itaúna APA, near the urban area, suffers from occasional fires.<ref name="Diário_Incêndio">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=15069 |title=Devastated Heritage: Fire in Pedra Itaúna Affects 155 Hectares of Vegetation |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=14 October 2015 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705233435/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=15069 |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> The Caratinga Municipal Park, spanning 402 hectares, also faces degradation.<ref name="PDDI2_364">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=364}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=17269 |title=Environmental Police Identify Suspect of Illegal Burning in Córrego dos Bias |author=Raquel Borsari |date=27 August 2014 |publisher=Super Canal |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170707012614/http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=17269 |archive-date=7 July 2017}}</ref>

Caratinga is home to two [[Private natural heritage reserve (Brazil)|private natural heritage reserves]] (RPPNs): the Lagoa Silvana State Natural Heritage Reserve and the Feliciano Miguel Abdala Federal Private Natural Heritage Reserve.<ref name="PDDI2_362">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=362}}</ref> The Lagoa Silvana State Natural Heritage Reserve, near the [[Rio Doce State Park]], owned by [[Usiminas]], was established in 2000 and formalized in 2012.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.relictos.org.br/index_arquivos/Page6453.htm |title=Lagoa Silvana Private Natural Heritage Reserve Approved by IEF |author=Fundação Relictos |date=28 November 2012 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232637/http://www.relictos.org.br/index_arquivos/Page6453.htm |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> The [[Feliciano Miguel Abdala Private Natural Heritage Reserve]], purchased by Feliciano Miguel Abdala in 1942 for conservation, particularly of the [[northern muriqui]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/globo-reporter/noticia/2014/08/amor-floresta-atravessa-geracoes-e-salva-o-maior-macaco-das-americas.html |title=Love for the Forest Spans Generations and Saves the Largest Monkey in the Americas |author=Globo Repórter |date=15 August 2014 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232647/http://g1.globo.com/globo-reporter/noticia/2014/08/amor-floresta-atravessa-geracoes-e-salva-o-maior-macaco-das-americas.html |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> one of the largest primates in the [[Americas]],<ref name="Caratinga_Acessa"/> spans 957 hectares, 80% of which is preserved forest. Recognized as a private natural heritage reserve in 2001, it supports global scientific research at the Caratinga Biological Station (EBC).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/historico.htm |title=History |author=Preserve o Muriqui |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232749/http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/historico.htm |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/rppn.htm |title=Characteristics |author=Preserve o Muriqui |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232850/http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/rppn.htm |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/estacao.htm |title=Caratinga Biological Station |author=Preserve o Muriqui |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232857/http://www.preservemuriqui.org.br/estacao.htm |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> In 2015, approximately 1,000 northern muriquis remained in Brazil, with 361 in Caratinga.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/politica/blog/matheus-leitao/post/reserva-para-proteger-macaco-atrai-tambem-onca-em-minas-veja-foto-e-video.html |title=The reserve, which was established to protect monkeys, also attracts jaguars in Minas Gerais; see the photo and video |author= |date=24 June 2015 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232726/http://g1.globo.com/politica/blog/matheus-leitao/post/reserva-para-proteger-macaco-atrai-tambem-onca-em-minas-veja-foto-e-video.html |archive-date=5 July 2017|website=G1}}</ref>

Conserved areas boast notable [[biodiversity]], including the [[Geoffroy's cat]], [[jaguarundi]], [[South American coati|coati]], [[agouti]], [[tayra]], [[pale-throated sloth]], [[crab-eating raccoon]], [[greater grison]], [[Brazilian squirrel]], [[capybara]], and [[red brocket]]. Bird species include the [[dusky-legged guan]] and [[solitary tinamou]], as well as several species of [[hawk]]s.<ref name="UFMG_RPPN">{{cite web|url=http://www.icb.ufmg.br/zoo/primatas/prim17.htm |title=RPPN Feliciano Miguel Abdala (and Caratinga Biological Station – EBC) |author=Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (UFMG) |access-date=14 November 2011 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120908091841/http://www.icb.ufmg.br/zoo/primatas/prim17.htm |archive-date=8 September 2012}}</ref> In 2014, a {{convert|66424.5607|ha|acre}} area across Caratinga, Simonésia, Manhuaçu, Ipanema, Santa Bárbara do Leste, Santa Rita de Minas, and Piedade de Caratinga was designated as Minas Gerais’ first [[wildlife corridor]], linking the Feliciano Miguel Abdala Private Natural Heritage Reserve and the [[Sossego Forest Biological Station]] in Simonésia and Manhuaçu.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2015/03/19/interna_gerais,629164/primeiro-corredor-ecologico-de-minas-tera-plano-de-preservacao.shtml |title=Minas' First Ecological Corridor to Have Preservation Plan |author=Jornal Estado de Minas |date=19 March 2015 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170705232926/http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2015/03/19/interna_gerais,629164/primeiro-corredor-ecologico-de-minas-tera-plano-de-preservacao.shtml |archive-date=5 July 2017}}</ref> This corridor, encompassing 21 springs, has lost 70% of its original vegetation and remains threatened by human activity, where approximately 100 families live.<ref name="PDDI2_373">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=373}}</ref> Century-old [[Roystonea oleracea|royal palms]] in the urban center, particularly downtown, earned Caratinga the nickname "City of Palms."<ref name="Monteiro_160"/>

== Environmental issues ==
[[File:Rio Caratinga no B. Salatiel, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Areas occupied along the banks of the Caratinga River in Praça da Estação (Salatiel neighborhood)]]
Some of the main environmental problems affecting the city are [[flood]]s, which during the rainy season cause significant damage in the lower and more densely populated areas along the banks of watercourses, as well as landslides on hills and slopes. The causes of these issues are often the construction of residences in irregular zones,<ref name="Portal_Leitura" /> in addition to the disposal of garbage and sewage into streams and rivers.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=22923 |title=Obra de canalização dos córregos é adequada para escoamento das águas das chuvas, segundo Copasa |author=Raquel Borsari web|url=http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=17075 |title=Morador joga sofá no Córrego Santa Cruz |author=Raquel Borsari |date=22 August 2014 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170705233010/http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=17075}}</ref> Organizations such as the Doce River Basin Committee maintain rainfall stations and monitoring points for the water level of the Caratinga River in Caratinga and other municipalities within the river basin, alerting the population in case of potential risks.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ibioagbdoce.org.br/geral/municipios-da-bacia-do-rio-caratinga-contemplados-com-o-programa-de-convivencia-as-cheias-p31-receberao-imagens-de-satelite-de-areas-urbanas-propicias-a-ocorrencia-de-cheias/ |title=Municípios da Bacia do Rio Caratinga contemplados com o Programa de Convivência às Cheias (P31) receberão imagens de satélite de áreas urbanas propícias à ocorrência de cheias |author=IBIO AGB Doce |date=2 February 2017 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170705233033/http://www.ibioagbdoce.org.br/geral/municipios-da-bacia-do-rio-caratinga-contemplados-com-o-programa-de-convivencia-as-cheias-p31-receberao-imagens-de-satelite-de-areas-urbanas-propicias-a-ocorrencia-de-cheias/}}</ref> There is also a mapping of risk areas in the municipality, which serves as a foundation for other preventive measures.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/videos/v/defesa-civil-de-caratinga-faz-mapeamento-das-areas-de-risco-durante-periodo-chuvoso/2951188/ |title=Defesa Civil de Caratinga faz mapeamento das áreas On the other hand, during the [[dry season]] and prolonged [[Indian summer]]s in the midst of the rainy season, [[wildfire]]s are commonly reported in hills and brushlands, including within conservation units. Forest fires contribute to the destruction of native vegetation, degrade soil quality, and impair air quality,<ref name="Diário_Incêndio"/><ref name="RádioCidade_Aceiros">{{Cite web|url=http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/prefeitura-limpa-e-refaz-aceiros-no-entorno-da-itauna/ |title=Prefeitura limpa e refaz aceiros no entorno da Itaúna |author=Rádio Cidade |date=18 August 2016 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170705170443/http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/prefeitura-limpa-e-refaz-aceiros-no-entorno-da-itauna/}}</ref> which is already compromised by a high concentration of pollutants from vehicle emissions.<ref name="Prefeitura_Sustentável">{{Cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/prefeitura-apresenta-programa-cidade-sustentavel/6899 |title=Prefeitura apresenta Programa Cidade Sustentável |author=Prefeitura |date=30 October 2013 |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170705170550/http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/prefeitura-apresenta-programa-cidade-sustentavel/6899}}</ref> In the first 15 days of October 2015, Caratinga ranked seventh in Minas Gerais for the number of wildfire hotspots according to INPE, during which a three-day arson fire consumed 155 hectares of the Itaúna Environmental Protection Area. The rugged terrain hinders the Fire Department's response to such incidents.<ref name="Diário_Incêndio"/> [[Visual pollution]], meanwhile, is significant in various parts of the urban area, with a considerable presence of uncontrolled posters and advertisements affixed to walls, poles, and other open spaces.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=16121 |title=Secretaria de Meio Ambiente tenta coibir poluição visual em Caratinga |author=Raquel Borsari |date=30 June 2014 |publisher=Super Canal |access-date=5 July 2017 |archive-date=5 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170705233312/http://portal.tvsupercanal.com.br/?p=16121}}</ref>

=== Indicators and inequality ===
[[File:Crianças de escola municipal em Caratinga MG.jpg|thumb|left|Children at a municipal school in Caratinga]]
The Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Caratinga is considered high by the United Nations Development Programme ([[United Nations Development Programme|UNDP]]), with a value of 0.706 (the 1,720th highest in Brazil and the 195th highest in Minas Gerais). The city has most indicators close to the national average according to the UNDP. Considering only the education index, the value is 0.603, the longevity index is 0.836, and the income index is 0.697.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/> In the same year, there was a deficit of {{formatnum:2296}} households, the largest among the municipalities in the Vale do Aço metropolitan area in absolute numbers, while 22.7% of residences exhibited housing inadequacy.<ref name="PDDI3_590a598"/>

From 2000 to 2010, the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to half the minimum wage decreased by 44.2%. In 2010, 84.6% of the population lived above the [[poverty line]], 9.3% were at the poverty line, and 6.1% were below it.<ref name="ODM_1">{{Cite web|url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031147/caratinga---mg |title=1 - acabar com a fome e a miséria |author=Portal ODM |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-date=3 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170703170026/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031147/caratinga---mg}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.547, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{Cite web|url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031147/caratinga---mg |title=Perfil municipal |author=Portal ODM |date=2012 |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-date=3 July 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170703170042/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031147/caratinga---mg}}</ref> The share of the city's total income held by the wealthiest 20% of the population was 58.4%, or 16.8 times higher than that of the poorest 20%, which was 3.5%.<ref name="ODM_1"/>

In 2010, Caratinga had the third-highest percentage of residences in [[slum]]s among Minas Gerais municipalities, with 17.5% of its households located in areas classified as such, encompassing {{formatnum:15709}} inhabitants. That year, seven locations were identified as slums by the IBGE.<ref name="PDDI3_611a613"/><ref name="JVA_Pobreza">{{Cite web|url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94563 |title=Fabriciano: maior número de moradores em favelas |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=22 December 2011 |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-date=15 December 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121215045615/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94563 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Despite expanded access to basic infrastructure between the 1990s and 2010, the lack of public policies focused on urban growth contributes to the persistence of these issues, facilitating the emergence of irregular occupations.<ref name="PDDI3_620a622">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=620–622}}</ref>

== Subdivisions ==
[[File:Avenida Capitão João Moreira Franco em São Cândido, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Main street of the São Cândido district]]
The municipality of Caratinga is divided into ten districts, in addition to the municipal seat. These districts are: Cordeiro de Minas, Dom Lara, Dom Modesto, Patrocínio de Caratinga, Santa Efigênia de Caratinga, Santa Luzia de Caratinga, Santo Antônio do Manhuaçu, Sapucaia, São Cândido, and São João do Jacutinga.<ref name="IBGE_Sinopse">{{cite web |url=http://www.censo2010.ibge.gov.br/sinopseporsetores/?nivel=st |title=Synopsis by sectors |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=16 November 2011 |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-date=7 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210707071732/https://censo2010.ibge.gov.br/sinopseporsetores/?nivel=st |url-status=dead }}</ref> The current division results from a series of changes to Caratinga's territorial configuration throughout the 20th century, which involved the separation of several districts to create new municipalities. The most recent separation occurred on 21 December 1995, when [[Piedade de Caratinga]] and [[Imbé de Minas]] were emancipated.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

Urban development in areas distant from the municipal seat intensified from the 1980s onward, particularly in regions near the [[Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]], due to real estate saturation in the metropolitan area and easy access via the [[BR-458 (Brazil highway)|BR-458]] highway.<ref name="PDDI1_43a44"/><ref name="PDDI1_100a101"/> This led to the emergence of relatively recent subdivisions and settlements, such as the neighborhoods of Ilha do Rio Doce, Parques do Vale, and Porto Seguro.<ref name="PDDI1_196">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=196}}</ref> This region also began to attract industrial developments and leisure investments, such as clubs and [[resort]]s, primarily aimed at serving the metropolitan population.<ref name="PDDI1_185a186">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=185–186}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_208">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=208}}</ref> These factors contributed to Caratinga's integration into the urban expansion of the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region, alongside the original city seat.<ref name="PDDI1_80a81">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=80–81}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_130a132">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=130–132}}</ref>

The original urban area of Caratinga is divided into twelve official neighborhoods, as recorded by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) in 2022. These are: Anápolis, Dario Grossi, Esperança, Esplanada, Limoeiro, Nossa Senhora Aparecida, Nossa Senhora das Graças, Salatiel, Santa Cruz, Santa Zita, Santo Antônio, and Zacarias Cimini. Among these, Santa Cruz was the most populous, with {{formatnum:6815}} inhabitants in 2022.<ref name="IBGE_BairrosPop2022">{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9923.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9923/n102/3113404001,3113404004,3113404005,3113404006,3113404007,3113404008,3113404009,3113404010,3113404011,3113404012/v/allxp/p/all/c1/6795/l/v,p%2Bc1,t |title=Table 9923 - Resident population, by household situation - Neighborhood |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2022 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250101103019/https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9923.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9923/n102/3113404001,3113404004,3113404005,3113404006,3113404007,3113404008,3113404009,3113404010,3113404011,3113404012/v/allxp/p/all/c1/6795/l/v,p%2Bc1,t |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref> It is noteworthy that the original municipal seat is the area with the highest concentration of irregular settlements, such as slums.<ref name="PDDI1_232">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=232}}</ref>

== Economy ==
In the [[Gross Domestic Product]] (GDP) of Caratinga, the industrial and service sectors stand out, though [[agribusiness]] also plays a significant role, particularly [[Coffee production in Brazil|coffee production]]. According to 2021 data from the IBGE, the municipality's GDP at current prices was R$ {{formatnum:2283288.08|thousands}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021" /> {{formatnum:274854.24|thousands}} came from taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices, and the per capita GDP was R$ {{formatnum:24518.79}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/38/46996 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios - 2021 |trans-title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities - 2021 |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2021 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250101170259/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/38/46996 |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref> However, a considerable portion of the population commutes to other municipalities for work, given the easy access to industries in the [[Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]]. Among Caratinga's workforce employed in other municipalities, 28.6% work in [[Ipatinga]], 0.6% in [[Santana do Paraíso]], 0.6% in [[Timóteo (municipality)|Timóteo]], 0.5% in [[Coronel Fabriciano]], and 9.7% in other cities in the metropolitan region.<ref name="PDDI1_84e89">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=84; 89}}</ref>

In 2022, [[Salary|salaries]] and other forms of remuneration totaled {{formatnum:603017|thousands}} [[Brazilian real|reais]], with the average monthly salary in the municipality being 1.9 [[minimum wage]]s. There were {{formatnum:4467}} local units and {{formatnum:4288}} active companies.<ref name="IBGE_Empresas">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/19/143491 |title=Cadastro Central de Empresas |trans-title=Central Business Register |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2022 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250101163807/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/19/143491 |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref> According to the IBGE, in 2010, 65.58% of households survived on less than one minimum wage per month per resident ({{formatnum:17483}} households), 24.19% had between one and three minimum wages per person ({{formatnum:6449}} households), 3.46% earned between three and five minimum wages (923 households), 2.28% had monthly incomes above five minimum wages (609 households), and 4.48% had no income ({{formatnum:1194}} households).<ref name="SIDRA_Rendimento">{{cite web|url=http://www2.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=4&i=P&c=3261 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170703171201/http://www2.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3261&i=P&opc386=2&nome=on&qtu8=137&digt102=&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=3261&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&qtu11=658&qtu23=294&nivt=0&orp=4&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&orc386=3&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&qtu2=5&opn10=0&qtu15=3&sev=96&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&qtu102=14208&opn13=0&poc386=2&sep=26272&orn=1&digt10=&opn11=0&qtu7=36&opn23=0&sec386=9680&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Caratinga&opn102=0&OpcCara=104&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&qtu10=10282&digt11=&opn7=0&decm=99 |archive-date=3 July 2017 |title=Domicílios particulares permanentes, por classes de rendimento nominal mensal domiciliar per capita |trans-title=Permanent private households, by nominal monthly household income per capita classes |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2010 |access-date=3 July 2017}}</ref>

In 2021, livestock and agriculture contributed {{formatnum:104000.53|thousands}} reais to Caratinga's economy,<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021" /> while in 2010, 17.73% of the municipality's economically active population was employed in this sector.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas" /> According to the IBGE, in 2023, the municipality had {{formatnum:40858}} [[cattle]], {{formatnum:35640}} [[poultry]], {{formatnum:2226}} [[pig]]s, {{formatnum:1896}} [[sheep]], {{formatnum:1249}} [[horse]]s, 398 [[goat]]s, and 34 [[Water buffalo|buffaloes]].<ref name="Agropecuária" /> In the same year, the city produced {{formatnum:24984|thousands}} liters of milk from {{formatnum:9598}} [[Cattle|cows]], 117,000 dozens of chicken eggs from {{formatnum:9875}} hens, and {{formatnum:17500}} kilograms of [[honey]] from [[bee]]s. In [[aquaculture]], the main productions were [[tilapia]] ({{formatnum:900|kg}}), [[tambaqui]] ({{formatnum:540|kg}}), and tambacu ({{formatnum:150|kg}}).<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/18/16459 |title=Pecuária |trans-title=Livestock |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250101164017/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/18/16459 |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref>

In 2023, the largest cultivated areas for temporary crops were for [[bean]]s (900 hectares), [[Maize|corn]] (500 hectares), and [[sugarcane]] (220 hectares), in addition to [[cassava]], [[tomato]]es, [[peanut]]s, [[sweet potato]]es, [[watermelon]]s, [[rice]], [[garlic]], [[pineapple]], and [[onion]]s.<ref name="IBGE_LavouraTemporária">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/14/10193 |title=Lavoura Temporária |trans-title=Temporary Crops |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250101164737/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/14/10193 |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref> In terms of permanent crops, the highlights were [[coffee]] ({{formatnum:10000}} hectares), [[banana]]s (350 hectares), and [[Orange (fruit)|oranges]] (29 hectares), along with [[tangerine]]s, [[lemon]]s, [[avocado]]s, [[mango]]es, [[passion fruit]], [[heart of palm]], [[coconut]]s, and [[guava]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/15/11863 |title=Lavoura Permanente |trans-title=Permanent Crops |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250101164936/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/caratinga/pesquisa/15/11863 |archive-date=1 January 2025}}</ref>

Caratinga is a significant producer of mountain coffee, despite the activity experiencing a decline due to rising costs.<ref name="RevistaCafeicultura_Caratinga">{{cite web|url=http://www.revistacafeicultura.com.br/index.php?tipo=ler&mat=24350 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20131105070018/http://www.revistacafeicultura.com.br/index.php?tipo=ler&mat=24350 |archive-date=5 November 2013 |title=Caratinga lança projeto de Centro de Excelência do Café e inaugura Estação Digital |trans-title=Caratinga launches Coffee Excellence Center project and inaugurates Digital Station |author=Revista Cafeicultura |date=28 July 2009 |access-date=27 April 2011}}</ref> During the harvest season, which runs from April to September, local commerce experiences increased activity. The importance of horticultural production should also be noted, with an average output of {{formatnum:3000}} tons per month.<ref name="Prosperustec_Aeroporto"/> The city has a Central Supply Market (CEASA), where local horticultural production is sold, serving approximately 50 municipalities in the region,<ref name="Monteiro_160"/> with 51% of the products sold at Caratinga's CEASA being produced within the municipality itself.<ref name="PDDI3_781">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=781}}</ref>

=== Housing, services, and communications ===
[[File:Habitações em morros nos B. Limoeiro e Nossa Senhora Aparecida, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Houses on hills in the Limoeiro and Nossa Senhora Aparecida neighborhoods]]
In 2022, Caratinga had {{formatnum:32668}} occupied permanent private households. Of these, {{formatnum:22767}} were houses (69.69%), {{formatnum:9567}} were apartments (29.29%), 317 were houses in gated communities or condominiums (0.67%), eleven were [[tenement]]s (0.03%), and six were degraded or unfinished structures (0.02%).<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Of the total occupied households, {{formatnum:20924}} were owned (64.06%), with {{formatnum:19546}} fully paid and {{formatnum:1379}} under acquisition; {{formatnum:9593}} were [[Renting|rented]] (29.37%); {{formatnum:1935}} were loaned or provided (5.92%); and 209 were occupied through other means (0.64%).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3113404/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |title=Tabela 9930 - Domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados, por número de cômodos, condição de ocupação do domicílio e tipo do domicílio |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2022 |access-date=1 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250101170651/https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3113404/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |archive-date=1 January 2025 |language=pt}}</ref>

Water supply and sewage collection services are managed by the Minas Gerais Sanitation Company (Copasa).<ref name="G1_Lage">{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2015/10/bairros-de-caratinga-entram-em-rodizio-de-agua-por-uma-semana.html |title=Bairros de Caratinga entram em rodízio de água por uma semana |author= |date=3 October 2015 |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170703215739/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2015/10/bairros-de-caratinga-entram-em-rodizio-de-agua-por-uma-semana.html |archive-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt|website=G1}}</ref> In 2022, according to IBGE, 79.37% of households had access to the general water network as their primary supply, and 99.92% had exclusive-use bathrooms. Regarding [[sanitation]], 77.66% of households were served by the general or stormwater sewage network.<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Much of the city's water supply comes from the Lage stream and is treated at a conventional treatment plant.<ref name="G1_Lage"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=21484 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170703215747/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=21484 |archive-date=3 July 2017 |title=Caratinga é uma dádiva do Córrego do Lage |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=22 March 2016 |access-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> However, a [[sewage treatment plant]] under construction remains incomplete, and collected sewage is still discharged directly into urban watercourses.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/obras-de-captacao-e-tratamento-de-esgoto-de-caratinga-estao-paralisadas/ |title=Obras de captação e tratamento do esgoto de Caratinga estão paralisadas |author=Rádio Cidade |date=7 April 2015 |access-date=3 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170703220040/http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/obras-de-captacao-e-tratamento-de-esgoto-de-caratinga-estao-paralisadas/ |archive-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref>

Electricity is supplied by the [[CEMIG|Minas Gerais Energy Company]] (Cemig), which serves much of the state. In 2003, there were {{formatnum:26554}} consumers, with {{formatnum:60408648}} [[Watt-hour|kWh]] of energy consumed.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /> In 2010, 99.7% of households had access to the electrical network, according to IBGE.<ref name="PDDI3_582">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=582}}</ref> Regarding waste management, 88.91% of households were served by [[waste collection]] services in 2022,<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> with waste directed to a [[landfill]] located approximately {{formatnum:7.5|km}} km from downtown Caratinga, producing an average of 38 tons of waste daily in 2012.<ref name="PDDI3_438a439">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=438–439}}</ref> The municipality of Ipatinga disposed of its waste in an open-air dump in Caratinga's Cordeiro de Minas district until 1996, when it was covered. The site was closed in 2003 and designated for preservation.<ref name="PDDI2_444">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=444}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===
[[File:BR-116 sobre a Avenida Maria Catarina Cimini com a Pedra Itaúna ao fundo, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|[[BR-116]] over the Maria Catarina Cimini Avenue]]
Caratinga was once served by the now-defunct [[Leopoldina Railway]], which provided passenger transport to the city and some rural areas until the mid-1970s. Initially operational in 1931 with regular trips between Caratinga and [[Rio de Janeiro]], the railway fragmented over time, with passenger services limited to [[Ubá]] in its final years. The rail line through the municipality was dismantled in the 1980s.<ref name="Estações_Caratinga">{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efl_mg_tresrios_caratinga/caratinga.htm |title=Caratinga |author=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |date=21 February 2009 |access-date=1 May 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704010425/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efl_mg_tresrios_caratinga/caratinga.htm |archive-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2tTJ6ijoYSYC |title=Railways of Brazil in Postcards and Souvenir Albums |trans-title=Railways of Brazil in Postcards and Souvenir Albums |language=en |date=2005 |publisher=Solaris Editorial |isbn=978-85-89820-03-5 |access-date=3 July 2017}}</ref> In Minas Gerais, the decline of railways was largely due to their replacement by highways, driven by the growing vehicle fleet and the privatization of many rail lines for freight transport.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.brasilescola.com/brasil/industrializacao-regiao-sudeste.htm |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704010758/http://brasilescola.uol.com.br/brasil/industrializacao-regiao-sudeste.htm |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Industrialização da Região Sudeste |author=Eduardo de Freitas |publisher=Brasil Escola |access-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://360graus.terra.com.br/ecoturismo/default.asp?did=31234&action=geral |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140109105010/http://360graus.terra.com.br/ecoturismo/default.asp?did=31234&action=geral |archive-date=9 January 2014 |title=Estrada de ferro Leopoldina: você verá paisagens de tirar o fôlego |author= |date=2 October 2013 |access-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt|website=360 Graus}}</ref>

Although rail transport is no longer available, the nearest passenger terminal for those coming from [[Belo Horizonte|the state capital]] is the Intendente Câmara Railway Station on the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]] (EFVM), located in [[Ipatinga]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gov.br/antt/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/fugindo-da-rodovia-da-morte-pelos-trilhos-do-trem |archive-url=https://archive.today/20201015183153/https://www.gov.br/antt/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/fugindo-da-rodovia-da-morte-pelos-trilhos-do-trem |archive-date=15 October 2020 |title=Fugindo da 'rodovia da morte' pelos trilhos do trem |author=Agência Nacional de Transportes Terrestres (ANTT) |date=20 May 2014 |access-date=14 October 2020 |language=pt}}</ref> The municipality is served by [[BR-116 (Brazil highway)|BR-116]] (Rio–Bahia Highway), which runs through the urban area; [[BR-458 (Brazil highway)|BR-458]], connecting BR-116 to the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region via the northern municipal boundary; BR-474, linking Caratinga to [[Ipanema, Minas Gerais|Ipanema]], [[Aimorés]], and the state of Espírito Santo; [[List of state highways in Minas Gerais|MG-329]], providing access to [[Bom Jesus do Galho]] and [[Ponte Nova]]; and MG-425, connecting to [[Entre Folhas]]; along with secondary and connecting roads.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /><ref name="Google">[http://maps.google.com.br/ Google Maps]. Accessed 3 July 2017.</ref> The Carlos Alberto de Mattos Bus Terminal (New Bus Station) provides intermunicipal public transportation.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.doctumtv.com.br/10/2015/usuarios-da-rodoviaria-nova-de-caratinga-denunciam-falta-de-estrutura-e-seguranca/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704010828/http://www.doctumtv.com.br/10/2015/usuarios-da-rodoviaria-nova-de-caratinga-denunciam-falta-de-estrutura-e-seguranca/ |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Usuários da 'Rodoviária Nova' de Caratinga denunciam falta de estrutura e segurança |author=Doctum TV |date=30 October 2015 |access-date=3 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref>

== Culture ==
[[File:Praça Cesário Alvim com Catedral de São João Batista e Pedra Itaúna ao fundo, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Cesário Alvim Square with Saint John the Baptist Cathedral and Pedra Itaúna in the background]]
Caratinga has a municipal culture council, which serves advisory, deliberative, regulatory, and supervisory roles, and a heritage preservation council, which is advisory and deliberative, both with equal representation. Municipal legislation protects both tangible and intangible cultural heritage, managed by a dedicated municipal culture secretariat.<ref name="IBGE_Cultura">{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&posicao=6&municipio=3113404&UF=31 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231533/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&posicao=6&municipio=3113404&UF=31 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Cultura |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> For 2024, the municipality scored 1.80 out of 4 for cultural policies in the Cultural [[Goods and Services Tax|ICMS]] calculation, with a total score of 11.55, factoring in conservation, expenditure, and the number of registered or protected assets.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |title=ICMS Critério do Patrimônio Cultural |author=Instituto Estadual do Patrimônio Histórico e Artístico de Minas Gerais (IEPHA) |page=7 |access-date=15 August 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230815111757/http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |archive-date=15 August 2023 |language=pt}}</ref>

As of January 2018, the following tangible assets were designated as municipal [[cultural heritage]]: the former Caratinga Railway Station; the Agenor Ludgero Alves Mansion, known as the Casarão das Artes; the São João Church, the first parish church; Cesário Alvim Square; the [[St. John the Baptist Cathedral, Caratinga|St. John the Baptist Cathedral]]; the Episcopal Palace; Cine Brasil; Princesa Isabel State School; the Ronaldinho Calazans Bandstand in Cesário Alvim Square; Pedra Itaúna; the Desembargador Faria e Sousa Courthouse, the former Caratinga County Courthouse; Bom Será Waterfall; and the Carmelites’ Stained Glass at the Hélio Amaral Institute. Intangible heritage included the Santa Cecília Musical Band and the São João Batista Choir.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=49471 |title=Patrimônio Cultural de Caratinga |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=3 January 2018 |access-date=10 July 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180710225913/https://diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=49471 |archive-date=10 July 2018 |language=pt}}</ref>

The Folia de Reis Festival, [[Festa Junina|June festivals]], and the city's anniversary celebrations in June, featuring civic ceremonies, sports competitions, and musical performances,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/confira-a-programacao-do-aniversario-de-169-anos-de-caratinga.ghtml |title=Confira a programação do aniversário de 169 anos de Caratinga |author= |date=23 June 2017 |access-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704231027/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/confira-a-programacao-do-aniversario-de-169-anos-de-caratinga.ghtml |archive-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt|website=G1}}</ref> are prominent cultural events in the municipality. The Caratinga carnival, which once included parades of [[carnival block]]s and [[samba school]]s, ended in the 1980s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=18660 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231124/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=18660 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Vem aí: Carnaval do Amaral 2016 |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=16 January 2016 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> [[Handicraft]]s are one of the most vibrant forms of cultural expression in Caratinga. The IBGE reports that the primary handicraft activities are [[embroidery]] and the preservation of traditional cuisine.<ref name="IBGE_Cultura"/>

=== Landmarks and attractions ===
[[File:Vista parcial da Lagoa Silvana, Caratinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|View of Lagoa Silvana]]
Caratinga is part of the Muriqui Tourist Route, established in 2009 by the Minas Gerais State Tourism Secretariat to promote tourism in participating cities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |title=Circuitos Turísticos de Minas Gerais |author=Secretaria de Turismo de Minas Gerais |date=18 March 2013 |access-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141019075824/http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |archive-date=19 October 2014 |language=pt}}</ref> Key natural attractions include the [[Feliciano Miguel Abdala Private Natural Heritage Reserve]], one of Minas Gerais’ main remnants of the [[Atlantic Forest]], home to the [[northern muriqui]], the largest primate in the [[Americas]]. The reserve supports scientific research from various countries and is open for [[ecotourism]].<ref name="Caratinga_Acessa">{{cite web|url=http://www.acessa.com/turismo/arquivo/trilhas/2010/05/04-muriqui/ |title=Circuito Rota do Muriqui abriga maior primata das Américas |author=Clecius Campos |date=4 May 2010 |publisher=Acessa.com |access-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704231334/http://www.acessa.com/turismo/arquivo/trilhas/2010/05/04-muriqui/ |archive-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> Centuries-old farms and rural properties facilitate rural tourism. The municipality features several waterfalls suitable for bathing, as well as lakes, notably Lagoa Silvana and Lagoa do Piau.<ref name="Caratinga_Acessa"/> Lagoa Silvana, near Ipatinga, offers a recreational area with trails, beaches, restaurants, and [[camping]] facilities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://br381mg.com.br/turismo/ |title=Conheça os pontos turísticos das cidades por onde a BR-381/MG passa |author= |access-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704231406/http://br381mg.com.br/turismo/ |archive-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt|website=BR-381}}</ref>

Located close to the urban area, Pedra Itaúna offers scenic views and is popular for extreme sports.<ref name="Caratinga_Acessa"/> Other notable urban landmarks include the 19th-century Cesário Alvim Square and the São João Batista Cathedral, which was built around 1930, and its bandstand designed by [[Oscar Niemeyer]] in 1980;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://catedralcaratinga.com.br/a-igreja 2017 |title=A igreja |author=Catedral de Caratinga |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> the Perpetual Adoration Sanctuary, used for Catholic prayers and worship;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/santuario-de-adoracao-perpetua-abre-programacao-da-57a-semana-eucaristica/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231746/http://www.radiocidadecaratinga.com.br/site/noticia/santuario-de-adoracao-perpetua-abre-programacao-da-57a-semana-eucaristica/ |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Santuário de Adoração Perpétua abre programação da 57ª Semana Eucarística |author=Rádio Cidade |date=19 May 2016 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> the Episcopal Palace, the residence of the [[Diocese of Caratinga]]’s bishop, inaugurated on 28 April 1944;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br:8080/turismo/palacio-episcpal/palacio-episcopal/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yOU4Iqdg?url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br:8080/turismo/palacio-episcpal/palacio-episcopal/ |archive-date=3 May 2011 |title=Palácio Episcopal === Sports ===
Caratinga offers various facilities and equipment for sports activities. The municipality supports professional athletes by providing subsidies for participation in regional tournaments across multiple sports, including [[track and field]], [[cycling]], [[jiu-jitsu]], [[taekwondo]], and [[sport shooting]], with athletes achieving notable results in national and international competitions. The municipality's schools host student games, fostering sports competitions among participating institutions. There are also physical activity programs for the elderly and individuals with hypertension and diabetes.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=4584 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231925/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=4584 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Caratinga é um dos 'Melhores do Ano' no Esporte em Minas Gerais |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=8 December 2014 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/novidade-no-programa-bolsa-atleta/68621 |title=Novidade no Programa Bolsa Atleta |author=Prefeitura |date=24 July 2018 |access-date=3 December 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201206141439/https://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/novidade-no-programa-bolsa-atleta/68621 |archive-date=6 December 2020 |language=pt}}</ref> The Pedra Itaúna offers ideal conditions for [[Freeflying|free flying]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/estrada-de-acesso-para-a-pedra-itauna-passa-por-melhorias/48274 |title=Estrada de acesso para a Pedra Itaúna passa por melhorias |author=Prefeitura |date=12 April 2017 |access-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704231431/http://www.caratinga.mg.gov.br/detalhe-da-materia/info/estrada-de-acesso-para-a-pedra-itauna-passa-por-melhorias/48274 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref>

Among football clubs, the Esporte Clube Caratinga, founded in 1917 by former mayor Euclides Etiene Arreguy, is one of the city's most traditional teams.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=1768 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231835/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=1768 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Esporte Clube Caratinga comemora 97 anos |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=21 October 2014 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> The América Futebol Clube, established in 1959, achieved success in local amateur championships during the 1980s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=3685 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231901/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=3685 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Parabéns Leão |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |date=1 December 2014 |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref> The Doutor Euclides Etienne Arreguy Stadium, also known as Doutor Maninho, is the home of Esporte Clube Caratinga and has a capacity of approximately 7,000 people.<ref name="TVSuper_ECC">{{cite web|url=http://www.tvsupercanal.com.br/noticias/esporte-clube-caratinga-homenageia-socio-benemerito/?searchterm=Esporte%20Clube%20Caratinga |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yPciwbPn?url=http://www.tvsupercanal.com.br/noticias/esporte-clube-caratinga-homenageia-socio-benemerito/?searchterm=Esporte%20Clube%20Caratinga |archive-date=3 May 2011 |title=Esporte Clube Caratinga homenageia sócio benemérito |author=Super Canal |date=5 July 2010 |access-date=3 May 2011 |language=pt}}</ref>

=== Holidays ===
Caratinga observes three municipal [[public holiday|holidays]] and eight national holidays, in addition to optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the [[Feast of Corpus Christi]], celebrated on June 19 in 2025; the city's anniversary, celebrated on June 24; and the [[Feast of the Immaculate Conception]], celebrated on December 8.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=36616 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170704231931/http://www.diariodecaratinga.com.br/?p=36616 |archive-date=4 July 2017 |title=Prefeito assina decreto sobre feriados municipais e pontos facultativos |author=Jornal Diário de Caratinga |access-date=4 July 2017 |language=pt}}</ref>
