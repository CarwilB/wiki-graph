'''Juana Díaz''' ({{IPA|es|ˈxwana ˈði.as}}) is a [[Juana Díaz barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]] located on the southern coast of the island, south of [[Jayuya, Puerto Rico|Jayuya]], [[Ciales, Puerto Rico|Ciales]], [[Orocovis, Puerto Rico|Orocovis]] and [[Villalba, Puerto Rico|Villalba]]; east of [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]]; and west of [[Coamo, Puerto Rico|Coamo]] and [[Santa Isabel, Puerto Rico|Santa Isabel]] and the [[Caribbean Sea]] to the south. Juana Díaz is spread over 13 barrios and [[Juana Díaz barrio-pueblo|Juana Diaz Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[Ponce Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

Juana Díaz is known as "La Ciudad del Maví" (Maví City). ''[[Mauby|Mabi]]'' is a fermented Taíno beverage made out from the bark of the mavi tree ''[[Colubrina elliptica]]''.

==History==
Juana Díaz was founded in 1798 and was known as {{lang|es|Ciudad de Jacagua}}, in honor of the [[Taíno]] [[Cacique]] Jacaguax.<ref>{{cite news | last=Cruz Ríos | first=Melissa | title=Para reyar y turistear en Juana Díaz | newspaper=El Vocero de Puerto Rico | date=5 January 2019 | url=https://www.elvocero.com/actualidad/para-reyar-y-turistear-en-juana-d-az/article_7a615738-1071-11e9-9c38-43854ef8645b.html | language=es | access-date=27 June 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200628125952/https://www.elvocero.com/actualidad/para-reyar-y-turistear-en-juana-d-az/article_7a615738-1071-11e9-9c38-43854ef8645b.html | archive-date=28 June 2020 | url-status=live }}</ref> The civil government of this territory was established on April 25, 1798.<ref name="JDHistoria">{{cite web |last1=Rodríguez |first1=Luis E. |title=Historia |url=http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |website=juanadiaz.org |access-date=2019-01-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160222094020/http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |archive-date=2016-02-22 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In August of 1898, [[Stephen Crane]], a war correspondent for Hearst's ''New York Journal'', single-handedly took possession of the town before an American regiment arrived. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Juana Díaz was 27,896.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n268 177]|access-date=2019-01-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121115044918/https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|archive-date=2012-11-15|url-status=live}}</ref>

On September 20, 2017 [[Hurricane Maria]] struck the island of Puerto Rico. In Juana Díaz, 2,500 homes lost their roof.<ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. María dejó incomunicadas a 70 familias en Juana Díaz| trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget. 70 families are left incommunicado in Juana Díaz|newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/juana-diaz/| language=es | access-date=2022-09-11}}</ref>

==Geography==
Juana Díaz is located on the southern coast. Several rivers run through the Juana Díaz territory, among them, [[Río Inabón]] and the [[Río Jacaguas]], from which Juana Díaz takes it's nickname, "Ciudad del Jacaguas". The Guayabal dam between Juana Díaz and Villalba is located in this river. Among its main tributaries are [[Río Toa Vaca]] in Villalba, also dammed. Both [[Lake Guayabal|Guayabal]] and [[Lake Toa Vaca|Toa Vaca]] lakes are visible in the map. Lake Toa Vaca is also the main source of drinking water for Juana Díaz, Ponce and other towns.<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/juana-diaz-municipality/|title=Juana Díaz Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-date=2020-08-23|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200823020134/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/juana-diaz-municipality/|url-status=live}}</ref> The highest point in the municipality is located close to the top of [[Cerro Maravilla]].

==Economy==
[[File:Flamboyan tree, Juana Diaz, PR - panoramio.jpg|thumb|Flamboyan tree in Juana Díaz]]

===Agriculture===
In the past, Juana Díaz was a large producer of [[sugarcane]] but with industrial development the sugar cane industry disappeared. Local [[rum]]s are now produced from raw materials imported from other countries. Juana Díaz produces plantains, bananas, mangoes, and other crops that are consumed in the local markets and also exported to other countries. Cattle and pigs are also raised in local farms.

===Industry===
Juana Díaz is a producer of [[beige marble]], one of the finest marbles in the world.

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Juana Díaz celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in late August / early September. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Ramón Nonato}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref>{{cite web | title=Puerto Rico Festivales, Eventos y Actividades en Puerto Rico | website=Puerto Rico Hoteles y Paradores | url=https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | language=es | access-date=2020-07-17 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226163727/https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | archive-date=2020-02-26 | url-status=live }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Juana Díaz include:
* The Three Kings Festival in Juana Díaz is the largest in all of Puerto Rico.<ref name="nqe1r">{{cite web | title=Juana Díaz | website=Discover Puerto Rico | url=https://www.discoverpuertorico.com/regions/south/juana-diaz | access-date=June 16, 2023 | page=}}</ref> [[Epiphany (holiday)|Three Kings Festival]] is celebrated on January 6 - religious and cultural event which began in 1884 celebrating the visit of the [[Biblical Magi|Three Kings]] to baby Jesus. The main event is a parade down Comercio Street to the town's plaza where a big altar is set up for the celebration of the Holy Eucharist. The Eucharist begins with an act of the Prophets announcing the coming of the [[Messiah]]. The prophesies are followed by the sighting of three kings by the shepherds. The [[Mass in the Catholic Church|holy mass]] follows and at the end the Three Kings find and adore baby Jesus. Some people come to the festival dressed up as shepherds.<ref>{{cite web | last=Cruz Rí | first=Melissa| title=Para reyar y turistear en Juana Díaz | website=El Vocero de Puerto Rico | date=2019-01-05 | url=https://www.elvocero.com/actualidad/para-reyar-y-turistear-en-juana-d-az/article_7a615738-1071-11e9-9c38-43854ef8645b.html | language=es | access-date=2021-04-04}}</ref>
* {{lang|es|Festival del Mabí}} ([[Mauby]] Festival) - March
*[[American bullfrog|Bull Frog]] Festival - April
*[[Good Friday]] - Juana Díaz celebrates many activities during Holy Week each year. The most notable is the Good Friday procession that transits through many of the urban communities with representations of the Stations of the Cross. Thousands of juanadinos and people from other towns visit Juana Díaz on Good Friday.
*Llorensiana Week - May
*Puerto Rican Festivities - December

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
Like all municipalities in [[Puerto Rico]], Juana Díaz is administered by a mayor. The current mayor is [[Ramón Hernández Torres]], from the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] (PPD). Hernández was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2000|2000 general election]].

Part of the city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district V]], which is represented by two senators. In 2024, [[Marially González Huertas]] from the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] and [[Jamie Barlucea]], from the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]], were elected as district senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{webarchive|url=https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Ponce_V.xml |date=2024-12-30 }} on CEEPUR</ref> The other part of the city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VI]], which has been represented by [[Rafael Santos Ortiz]] and [[Wilmer Reyes Berríos]] of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] since 2024.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Guayama_VI.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{webarchive|url=https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Guayama_VI.xml |date=2024-12-30 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag of Juana Díaz is rectangular in shape, formed by two triangles whose hypotenuse extends from the upper left corner to the lower right corner. The upper triangle is white, the symbol of silver and the lower triangle is yellow or gold. At its center is the coat of arms of Juana Díaz in its natural colors.<ref name="JD_bandera">{{cite web |last1=Rodríguez |first1=Luis E. |title=Bandera oficial |url=http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |website=juanadiaz.org |access-date=11 January 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160222094020/http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |archive-date=2016-02-22 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=JUANA DIAZ | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/JUANADIAZ.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001446/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/JUANADIAZ.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The coat of arms features The Nazarene Cross. It is in the center of the coat of arms. It stands out subtly between furrows and space, symbolizing the union of two races by means of the inalienable bond of the Christian faith. The cross also symbolizes the [[Western world|western]] [[Christians|Christian]] culture.<ref name="JD_Símbolos">{{cite web |last1=Rodríguez |first1=Luis E. |title=Nuestros Símbolos |url=http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |website=juanadiaz.org |access-date=11 January 2019 |language=es |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160222094020/http://www.juanadiaz.org/inicio-es.html |archive-date=2016-02-22 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The woman represents - Mrs. Juana Díaz, the towns namesake - with her hands the woman strews thirteen grains of corn into thirteen furrows, symbolizing the seeds that germinated and were the base for the foundation and growth of our town. The woman dresses as those of her time.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

The sun symbolizes a new horizon, hope in the formation of a town. The sun within the coat of arms has thirteen rays, each represents one of the barrios of Juana Díaz.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

Thirteen knolls stand out in the coat of arms symbolizing each of the thirteen barrios or barrios of Juana Díaz. The mountains also symbolize that Juana Díaz has been one of the richest mineral towns in Puerto Rico.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

The Indians represents the natives who settled the village before the arrival of the white man. The native carries on his back thirteen sheathed arrows and a bow, and an arrow on his head. The bow and arrow represent the only effective means of defense useful for survival.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

The shackle and the whip represent Juana Díaz as the martyr of 1887 because the most heinous, repressive institution known at the time was enforced in our town, "El Componte". It was in Juana Díaz where liberal politicians and dedicated patriots were martyred because they fought against the tyranny imposed by Spanish General [[Romualdo Palacio González|Romualdo Palacios]]. Palacios was the most tyrannical and cruel of all the governors of our nation. "El Componte", a military tribunal, was used to quiet the rebel voices of those who loved law and order.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

The harp represents the music, the quill pen dipped in the ink well represents the verse, the poetry. Juana Díaz has generously given distinguished poets to Puerto Rican literature.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

Ciudad del Jacaguas is the name by which Juana Díaz was known in the past. Many people knew Juana Díaz as the city of Jacaguas, but Luis Lloréns Torres dubbed it "La Versalles de Ponce" (Ponce's Versailles).<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

Four Castles Represent that Juana Díaz reached the stature of city. Cities have five castles in their coat of arms, villages have four and smaller towns have three.<ref name="JD_Símbolos" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

==Transportation==
Among the most important roads in Juana Díaz are the Luis A. Ferré Expressway ([[Puerto Rico Highway 52|PR-52]]) which connects Juana Díaz to [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]] and the airport in just minutes and [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] in a little more than one hour. Other important roads include road [[Puerto Rico Highway 14|PR-14]] which runs through [[Juana Díaz barrio-pueblo|downtown]] Juana Díaz on its route between [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]] and [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]], road [[Puerto Rico Highway 149|PR-149]] from Juana Díaz to [[Manatí, Puerto Rico|Manatí]] running through [[Villalba, Puerto Rico|Villalba]] and the "[[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]]" and road [[Puerto Rico Highway 1|PR-1]] which goes from Ponce to [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] through the community of [[Pastillo (Juana Díaz, Puerto Rico)|Pastillo]] in the south coast of Juana Díaz.

There are 51 bridges in Juana Díaz.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Juana Díaz Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/juana-diaz/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=20 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190221224226/http://bridgereports.com/pr/juana-diaz/|archive-date=21 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Notable people==

Natives of the Juana Diaz are referred to as "Juanadinos" within Puerto Rico. Among the most notable Juanadinos are:

* [[Tito Gómez (Puerto Rican singer)|Tito Gomez]]- Salsa singer, known for his work with [[Ray Barretto]], [[La Sonora Ponceña]] and [[Grupo Niche]]
* [[Luis Lloréns Torres]]- Famous Puerto Rican poet and essayist
* [[Guillermo Jose Torres]]- Puerto Rican reporter and news anchorman
* [[Héctor Tricoche]]- Salsa singer known for his work as the lead singer for the [[Tommy Olivencia]] Orchestra
* [[Dalex]] - Reggaeton Singer
* [[José_Santiago_(1960s_pitcher)|José “Palillo” Santiago]]- former pitcher of the [[Kansas City Athletics]] and [[Boston Red Sox]]. He was the starting pitcher of Game 1 of the [[1967 World Series]], in which he also hit a home run off [[Bob Gibson]]
