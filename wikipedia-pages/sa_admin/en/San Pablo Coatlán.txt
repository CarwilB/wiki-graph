'''San Pablo Coatlán''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 195.2&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.
It is part of the [[Miahuatlán District]] in the south of the [[Sierra Sur de Oaxaca|Sierra Sur Region]].

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 3,876.<ref>{{cite web|title=San Pablo Coatlán|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20291a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=June 12, 2009}}</ref>

==History==
Coatlan was a prehispanic settlement (or group of settlements) inhabited by [[Zapotec people]]. Because there are now several towns called Coatlan, it is unclear which one corresponds to the prehispanic town described in historic documents. At any rate, Coatlan was a city-state with several subject towns. It was constantly at war against [[Tututepec]], and also went to war with [[Miahuatlán de Porfirio Díaz|Miahuatlan]] before it was conquered by [[Moctezuma II]]. Afterwards, a Mexica governor, military officers, and warriors were sent here to help defend against Tututepec. In return, Coatlan paid tribute in gold dust, [[Manta (dress)|''mantas'']], and manpower.

According to legend, sacrifice was introduced to Coatlan by a local [[cacique]] who had visited the [[La Mixteca|Mixteca Alta]] and learned from the customs there. Upon his return, he created and placed in a large cave an idol called Benelaba ("Seven Rabbit") and his wife Jonaji Belachina ("Three Deer") to be worshipped by men and women respectively.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Wauchope |first1=Robert |last2=Wiley |first2=Gordon |last3=Spores |first3=Ronald |title=Handbook of Middle American Indians, Volumes 2 and 3: Archaeology of Southern Mesoamerica |date=1965 |publisher=University of Texas Press |isbn=9781477306550 |pages=971, 974}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last1=Berdan |first1=Frances |title=Aztec imperial strategies |date=1996 |publisher=Dumbarton Oaks Research Library and Collection |location=Washington, D.C |isbn=9780884022114 |page=279}}</ref>
