'''Halachó Municipality''' ({{IPA|es|xa.laˈtʃo}}, in the [[Yucatec Maya language|Yucatec Maya]] language: “Place of the rat's [[Wattle (construction)|wattle]]”) is a [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Yucatán]] containing 671.91&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> of land and located roughly 68&nbsp;km southwest of the city of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]].<ref name="municipios"/>

==History==
The area belonged to the chieftainship of Ah-Canul the mid-fifteenth century and after the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán|conquest]] was designated as an [[encomienda]] to Gaspar Pacheco in 1549.<ref name="municipios"/> In 1644 the encomedero of Halachó was Juan Quijada Rosado, but in 1659 it had been taken over by Cristóbal de Contreras by virtue of his marriage to Francisca Dorantes Solís, who belonged to an influential family.<ref name="Kú Mis (2009)">{{cite journal|last1=Kú Mis|first1=Miguel Ángel|title=Helachó del Postclásico Tradío al Porfiriato|journal=Programa de Desarrollo Cultural Municipal|date=2009|pages=1–76|url=https://www.scribd.com/doc/86023597/Halacho#|access-date=2 June 2015|publisher=Instituto de Cultura de Yucatán|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es}}</ref>  In 1700, the encomendero was Jacinto de Almeida,<ref name="Solís Robleda (2003)">{{cite book|last1=Solís Robleda|first1=Gabriela|title=Bajo el signo de la compulsión|date=2003|publisher=M. A. Porrúa|location=México|isbn=970-701-310-9|page=174|edition=1.|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=kW_6AgKrNZEC&pg=PA174|access-date=2 June 2015|language=es}}</ref> but by 1740 the Doña Ignacia del Castillo y Solis was in possession of the encomienda Halachó.<ref name="García Bernal (1972)">{{cite book|last1=García Bernal|first1=Manuela Cristina|title=La sociedad de Yucatán (1700-1750)|date=1972|publisher=Consejo Superior de Investigaciones Cientificas|location=España|isbn=978-84-00-02030-9|page=74|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=atC-g8E9xXAC&pg=PA74|access-date=2 June 2015|language=es}}</ref> with the charge for 168 Indians. It then passed to Don Pedro Cepeda y Lira.<ref name="municipios"/>

In 1821, Yucatán was declared independent of the Spanish Crown. In 1825 the area was part of the Camino Real Bajo, with its headquarters in [[Hunucmá Municipality|Hunucmá]]. By 1875, Halachó was elevated to the category of a town, but by 1921 it was reclassified as a village. Since 1910, the town of Halachó has contained two peoples, those who descend from Cepeda and those who descend from Cuch Holoch.<ref name="municipios"/>

==Governance==
The municipal president is elected for a term of three years. The president appoints nine Councilpersons to serve on the board for three year terms, as the Secretary and councilors of villages, special events, education, culture and sports, street lighting, health and welfare, city image, market and supply, public works, public monuments, and nomenclature.<ref name=governance>{{cite web|title=Halachó|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/municipios/31020a.html|website=inafed|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=4 June 2015|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es|archive-date=22 December 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211222050133/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/municipios/31020a.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Municipal Council administers the business of the municipality. It is responsible for budgeting and expenditures and producing all required reports for all branches of the municipal administration. Annually it determines educational standards for schools.<ref name=governance />

The Police Commissioners ensure public order and safety. They are tasked with enforcing regulations, distributing materials and administering rulings of general compliance issued by the council.<ref name=governance />

==Local festivals==
Annually a festival to the ''Virgen de Santa Ana'' is held from 13 to 17 April; from 19 July to 4 August is a festival in honor of St. James the Apostle; and the local fiesta is held the last week of November each year.<ref name="municipios"/>
