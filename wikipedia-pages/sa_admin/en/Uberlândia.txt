'''Uberlândia''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|ubɛʁˈlɐ̃dʒi.ɐ}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the interior of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]], in the region known as ''[[Triângulo Mineiro]]'', [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]] of the country. With a population of {{formatnum:754954}} inhabitants, according to the 2024 population estimate by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), it is the second most populous municipality in the state.<ref name=IBGE_Pop_2024/>

Located 537 kilometers from the state capital, [[Belo Horizonte]], the Uberlândia region was inhabited by [[Caiapó]] and [[Bororo]] indigenous peoples until the arrival of the ''[[Bandeirantes|bandeirante]]'' [[Bartolomeu Bueno da Silva]] in 1632. In the late 1880s, the municipality of São Pedro de Uberabinha gained independence from [[Uberaba]], but it was not until 1929 that the city was renamed "Uberlândia." Following emancipation, the city's urban area experienced significant growth, and by the early 20th century, Uberlândia had already diversified its industrial sectors.

The [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) of the municipality is the 27th largest in [[Brazil]],<ref name = "IBGE_PIB"/> standing out in the area of [[Tertiary sector of the economy|service provision]]. In terms of tourism, the city attracts visitors with its various cultural, natural, and architectural attractions, such as the Uberlândia Municipal Market, Parque do Sabiá,<ref name="Prefeitura_ParqueSabiá"/> Victorio Siquierolli Municipal Park, Clarimundo Carneiro Square, Tubal Vilela Square, Bicota/Rosário Square,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/cidade-e-regiao/sabores-naturais-sustentam-a-sorveteria-bicota-ha-35-anos/|title=Natural flavors sustain Bicota ice cream shop for 35 years|date=29 July 2012|access-date=5 April 2017|publisher=Correio de Uberlândia|archive-url=https://archive.today/20170405123045/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/cidade-e-regiao/sabores-naturais-sustentam-a-sorveteria-bicota-ha-35-anos/|archive-date=2017-04-05}}</ref> and the renowned Rondon Pacheco Avenue.<ref name="Correio_ParqueVictorino"/>

== Etymology ==
The original name of the current city of Uberlândia was São Pedro de Uberabinha, a designation received when it was elevated to a district of [[Uberaba]] on 21 May 1852. By State Law No. 23, of 14 March 1891, it was renamed Uberabinha, coinciding with its establishment date. By State Law No. 1,128, of 19 October 1929, the city was renamed Uberlândia, a name it retains to this day.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

"Uberlândia" is a term composed of two words from different origins: "''uber''" and "''lândia''":
*"''Uber''" comes from [[Latin]] ("fertile"),<ref name="Topônimo de Uberlândia">{{cite web |url=http://www.seer.ufu.br/index.php/cdhis/article/viewFile/2251/1845 |title=Triângulo Mineiro and the construction of a regional identity of autonomy and modernity |author=Ribeiro, Ricardo Ferreira |publisher=Universidade Federal de Uberlândia (UFU) |access-date=27 January 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303165254/http://www.seer.ufu.br/index.php/cdhis/article/viewFile/2251/1845 |archive-date=2016-03-03 }}</ref> although this etymology is debated;<ref name="De onde vem o nome Uberlândia">{{cite web |url=https://nicolasteixeiracabral.com/2019/04/21/uberlandia/ |title=Where does the name Uberlândia come from }}</ref>
*"''Lândia''" comes from the [[German language|German]] ''land'' ("land").<ref name="Topônimo de Uberlândia"/>

== History ==

=== Origins ===
[[File:bandeirante.jpg|thumb|left|upright|Monument to [[Bartolomeu Bueno da Silva]], in [[Goiânia]]/[[Goiás|GO]].]]

The first European to set foot in the region of the current municipality of Uberlândia, a territory then inhabited by [[Caiapó]] and [[Bororo]] indigenous peoples, was the ''[[bandeirante]]'' [[Bartolomeu Bueno da Silva]] in 1632. The region, initially part of the [[Captaincy of São Vicente]], was incorporated into the Captaincy of Minas Gerais and [[Captaincy of São Paulo|São Paulo]] by a Royal Charter of 3 November 1709. João Pereira da Rocha (1818), after the region was explored by ''bandeirantes'', settled in the area, marking land near the Aldeia de Santana (now [[Indianópolis]]). There, he established the headquarters of the ''sesmaria'', which he named São Francisco Farm, giving rise to the municipality. He also demarcated the Letreiro Farm and Salto Farm and named a waterway he found Ribeirão São Pedro.<ref name="Prefeitura_1860">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=275 |title=Period (1680_1846) |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=20 July 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100720062558/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=275 |url-status=live }}</ref> João Pereira's arrival attracted many other families, including the Carrejos, who, in 1835, acquired part of the São Francisco Farm and nearby properties, forming settlements named Olhos d'Água, Lage, Marimbondo, and Tenda (Felisberto's). Felisberto Alves Carrijo, legally recognized as the city's founder in 1964, was a teacher educated in missionary schools. He established the first school in the municipality at his home and led Sunday rosary prayers. A settlement formed, later named Nossa Senhora do Carmo in honor of a chapel of the same name, inaugurated on 20 October 1853.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/?tp=coluna&post=10656&uid=37 |title=Large family |author=Alberto de Oliveira |date=10 August 2006 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=14 January 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140114051431/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/?tp=coluna&post=10656&uid=37 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Prefeitura_1852">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=278 |title=Period (1852_1892) |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=20 July 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100720062603/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=278 |url-status=live }}</ref>

On 11 June 1857, twelve additional [[alqueire|''alqueires'']] donated by Luís Alves Pereira's wife, Custódia Fernandes dos Santos, and other citizens were incorporated into the settlement's patrimony. This area, already inhabited by enslaved people, gave rise to the Patrimônio neighborhood in the southern zone, under Provincial Law No. 831. Exactly one month later, the [[Freguesia|Parish]] of São Pedro de Uberabinha was created. In 1861, the Chapel of Our Lady of Mount Carmel was expanded, becoming the Nossa Senhora do Carmo de São Pedro de Uberabinha Parish Church, which was demolished in 1943.<ref name="Prefeitura_1852"/>

=== Administrative and political formation ===

By Decree No. 51, of 7 June 1888, the parishes of Santa Maria and São Pedro de Uberabinha were elevated to the status of a town. Two months later, on 31 August of that year, the municipality of São Pedro de Uberabinha, now Uberlândia, was created, separating from [[Uberaba]], under Provincial Law No. 4,643. Over the years, there were several changes in district subdivisions. In the 1911 general census records, the municipality consisted of the districts of Uberabinha (seat) and Santa Maria. By State Law No. 843, of 7 September 1923, the Martinópolis district was created with land detached from the seat district. By State Decree-Law No. 1,058, of 31 December 1943, the districts of Tapuirama and Cruzeiro dos Peixotos were created. Under the same decree-law, the Santa Maria district was renamed Miraporanga, and Martinópolis was renamed Martinésia.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

On 14 March 1891, the village of São Pedro de Uberabinha was established and the first Municipal Council was inaugurated. The creation of the [[Comarca|judicial district]] of São Pedro de Uberabinha occurred under Law Eleven, of 13 November of that year. On 21 December, the Civil Court of the Uberabinha Judicial Term, Judicial District of Araguari, was established. On 7 March 1892, the first Municipal Chamber of São Pedro de Uberabinha was sworn in, with Augusto César Ferreira e Souza as the executive agent. On 22 March, the first jury session was held, presided over by Duarte Pimentel de Ulhôa, the first judge of the judicial district, a position he held from 1892 until his death on 2 January 1928.<ref name="Prefeitura_1852"/>

=== Consolidation ===
[[File:Estação Mogiana de Uberlândia MG.jpg|thumb|left|Mogiana Station in Uberlândia]]

After Uberlândia's emancipation, the city's urban area experienced significant growth. In 1897, the first secondary school in the municipality was established: the Uberabinhense School. On 7 January of the same year, the newspaper "A Reforma," the city's first newspaper, began circulation, founded and directed by Professor João Luiz da Silva.<ref name="Prefeitura_1892">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=280 |title=Period (1892-1922) |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=20 July 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100720062608/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=280 |url-status=live }}</ref> By the early 20th century, the city had diversified its industries to include a brewery, a shoe factory, a cigarette factory, a blacksmith shop, a carpentry shop, and a saddlery shop. Besides these relatively simple industries, which produced household goods, construction materials, agricultural tools, and riding equipment, there was an agropastoral industry that, though rudimentary, was regulated by the Municipal Code. The pastoral industry centered on raising [[cattle]] for local consumption and [[pigs]] for both consumption and export to other municipalities and states.<ref name="Prefeitura_Casas">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_cidade.php?id=434 |title=Houses |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=28 February 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090228135549/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_cidade.php?id=434 |url-status=live }}</ref>

On unpaved streets and avenues, wagons, carts, and oxcarts operated under regulated traffic. This legislation governed vehicle registration, passenger capacity for carts, fare prices, and animal welfare standards. Besides wagons and carts, oxcarts were among the most significant and demanded means of transport. Even with the arrival of the Mogiana Railway in Uberabinha in 1895, these transport methods retained their importance, as trade between the city and areas not served by rail relied on oxcarts for transporting goods. For small purchases, visits, and various other activities, horses were used as a means of transport.<ref name="Prefeitura_Casas"/>

=== 20th century ===
[[File:Uberlândia (MG).tif|thumb|Uberlândia, September 1969. [[Brazilian National Archives|National Archives]].]]

Until 1908, the population's social activities revolved around religious celebrations and sacraments, such as attending [[Mass (liturgy)|Mass]] on Sundays, participating in baptisms, weddings, and organizing and attending [[Kermesse (festival)|kermesses]], which helped fulfill religious duties and fostered community ties. Sunday band performances were also held. Additionally, some residents enjoyed organizing and participating in [[beauty pageant]]s. There were also gaming houses which served as social interaction spaces.<ref name="Prefeitura_Casas"/>

With the city's demographic growth, there was a need to invest in municipal infrastructure and culture. Throughout the 20th century, about ten [[movie theater]]s were established in Uberlândia. Several entertainment venues and [[theater]]s were also opened,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=282 |title=São Pedro de Uberabinha |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=2 January 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090102165202/http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_miscelanea.php?id=282 |url-status=live }}</ref> along with the Cultural Center, an institution housing the municipality's cultural collections, inaugurated in 2008. Its headquarters is located in a building constructed between 1922 and 1924, originally intended as the residence of Eduardo Marquez, mayor from 1923 to 1926.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=10&id_cg=2050 |title=Cultural Center |author=Prefeitura |access-date=20 October 2010 |archive-date=9 May 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110509004739/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=10&id_cg=2050 |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Uberlandia_Minas_Gerais.jpg|thumb|[[Aerial photography]] of the city center in 2006]]

The municipality's area is 4,115.82&nbsp;km², representing 0.7017% of the state, 0.4452% of the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]], and 0.0484% of the entire Brazilian territory.<ref name="CNM_Dados">{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |title=General data |author=Confederação Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |access-date=21 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121021124532/http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |archive-date=2012-10-21}}</ref> Of this total, 135.3492&nbsp;km² are within the urban perimeter.<ref name="Embrapa_MG">{{cite web|url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/mg.html|title=Minas Gerais|publisher=Embrapa Satellite Monitoring |access-date=21 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121021124545/http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/mg.html |archive-date=2012-10-21}}</ref>

Uberlândia's average elevation is 887 m. The highest point in the municipality is located at the headwaters of Cachoeirinha Stream, measuring 930 m. The lowest elevation is at the [[River mouth|mouth]] of the [[Uberabinha River]], at 622 m. The municipal seat is situated at an elevation of 863.18 m.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> The municipality is located within the Plateaus and Highlands domain of the [[Paraná Basin]], specifically in the Southern Plateau subunit of the Paraná Basin. It features a typical highland landscape (gently rolling over sedimentary formations, with spaced valleys). The characteristic vegetation in this region is the [[Cerrado]] and its variants. The soils are acidic and have low fertility.<ref name="Prefeitura_Integrados">{{cite web |url=https://docs.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Volume-01-2017.pdf |title=Integrated Database 2016 |author=Prefeitura |date=2009 |access-date=18 October 2010 }}</ref> Approximately 70% of Uberlândia's territory consists of rolling terrain, with the remaining 30% being flat.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/>

=== Hydrography ===
[[File:Rio Araguari visto da BR-050.JPG|thumb|left|[[Araguari River (Minas Gerais)|Araguari River]] seen from [[BR-050 (Brazil highway)|BR-050]].]]

Uberlândia is located within the [[Paranaíba River]] basin, with several sub-basins of small and medium streams playing significant roles in its configuration.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> It is drained by the [[Tijuco River]] basin (the second largest tributary of the Paranaíba River), located in the south and southwest of the municipality, with main tributaries including the Babilônia, Douradinho, and Estiva Streams, the Cabaçal River, all in rural areas, and the [[Araguari River (Minas Gerais)|Araguari River]]. The Araguari basin covers the eastern portion of the municipality. Its main tributary within the municipality is the [[Uberabinha River]], which runs through the urban perimeter. The hydroelectric potential of this river is exploited through the operation of the Nova Ponte, Miranda, and Amador Aguiar I and II hydroelectric plants.<ref name="Prefeitura_Integrados"/>

The Uberabinha River is part of the Araguari River basin and highly significant for the city. Along with its [[Tributary|tributaries]], it serves as the water source for the population. The main tributaries of the Araguari in rural areas are the Beija-Flor, Rio das Pedras, and Bom Jardim Streams, another important water source for the municipality's water supply. In the [[urban area]], the Uberabinha River has smaller tributaries, such as the Cajubá, Tabocas, São Pedro (fully channeled), Vinhedo, Lagoinha, Liso, do Salto, Guaribas, Bons Olhos, do Óleo, and Cavalo Streams, among others.<ref name="Prefeitura_Integrados"/>

=== Ecology and environment ===
[[File:Uberlândia-MG 002- 49.jpg|thumb|left|[[Cerrado]] in the city's surroundings.]]

The predominant vegetation in the municipality is the [[Cerrado]] and its variants, such as clean fields, dirty fields, [[Cerradão|''cerradões'']], floodplain forests, [[riparian forest]]s, and mesophytic forests. Only in the western part of the municipality, where altitudes range from 700 to 850 m, are the soils shallower with low [[Soil fertility|fertility]], and the predominant vegetation is semideciduous forest.<ref name="Prefeitura_Integrados"/>

The city has eleven areas protected by environmental legislation, known as Conservation Units, which include riparian forest along the banks of watercourses (rivers, streams, etc.), protecting their waters against [[siltation]] and their slopes against [[erosion]], contributing to the preservation of the [[fauna]] and [[flora]] of the Cerrado.<ref name="Prefeitura_Integrados"/>

To further environmental preservation, the city hall has organized the Environment Week annually in June since 2007, with an average participation of approximately 1,500 people. The event includes lectures, workshops, and guided visits.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=5776 |title=Environment Week programming continues |author=Prefeitura |date=7 June 2010 |access-date=21 October 2010 |archive-date=9 June 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100609041556/http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=5776 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The city of Uberlândia also features several other green spaces, such as:
[[File:Praça_Tubal_Vilela.jpg|thumb|Tubal Vilela Square]]
* '''Parque do Sabiá:''' A park/zoo managed by FUTEL (Uberlândia Tourism, Sports, and Leisure Foundation), located in the Tibery neighborhood, East Zone of the city. It is a green complex comprising a zoo with dozens of captive animal species; seven lakes forming a large lagoon; an aquarium, housing 36 aquariums with 36 different fish species; a 5,100-meter running track; and other attractions.<ref name="Prefeitura_ParqueSabiá">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=149&id=17 |title=Parque do Sabiá Complex |author=Prefeitura |access-date=18 October 2010 }}</ref>

The [[climate]] of Uberlândia is characterized as [[Tropical climate|tropical]],<ref name="vetmedunivienna">{{cite web |url=http://www0.ufu.br/catalogo_novo/idiomas/pt/cidade.htm |title=The city of Uberlândia |author=Federal University of Uberlândia (UFU) |access-date=9 March 2015 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20200903135326/http://www0.ufu.br/catalogo_novo/idiomas/pt/cidade.htm |archive-date=2020-09-03}}</ref> with reduced [[rainfall]] in [[winter]] and an annual average temperature of around {{formatnum:22}} °C.<ref name="NCB_1981-2010">{{cite web|url=https://portal.inmet.gov.br/normais|title=CLIMATOLOGICAL NORMALS OF BRAZIL|author=INMET|access-date=6 May 2018 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180506124532/https://portal.inmet.gov.br/normais |archive-date=2018-05-06}}</ref> [[Autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] are transitional seasons.<ref name="Clima">{{cite web |url=http://www.sociedadenatureza.ig.ufu.br/include/getdoc.php?id=57&article=27&mode=pdf |title=The climate in the city of Uberlândia |author=UFU |date=15 April 2004 |publisher=Sociedade Natureza.Ig |access-date=18 October 2010 |archive-date=15 October 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091015095228/http://www.sociedadenatureza.ig.ufu.br/include/getdoc.php?id=57&article=27&mode=pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> Rainfall is slightly above {{formatnum:1600}} millimeters (mm), concentrated in the [[summer]] months.<ref name="NCB_1981-2010"/> [[Precipitation]] occurs as [[rain]], with occasional [[hail]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/noticia/2013/06/temporal-causa-prejuizo-de-quase-r-10-milhoes-em-uberlandia.htmls |title=Storm causes almost R$10 million in damage in Uberlândia |author=CALFAT, Marcelo |date=29 May 2013 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |access-date=18 June 2013 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2009/01/15/34404/chuva_causa_estragos_em_uberlandia.html |title=Rain causes damage in Uberlândia |author=MATURIZO, Lorena |date=16 January 2009 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |access-date=18 October 2010 }}</ref> During the [[dry season]], the municipality often records critically low [[Humidity|relative humidity]] levels, sometimes below 20%, with levels below 30% considered a state of alert.<ref name="Clima"/>

According to data from the [[weather station]] of the [[National Institute of Meteorology]] (INMET), located at the [[Federal University of Uberlândia]] (UFU), covering the periods from 1980 to 1984 and from 1986 onward, the lowest [[temperature]] recorded in the city was {{formatnum:1}} °C on 21 July 1981, and the highest reached {{formatnum:38.5}} °C on 7 October 2020.<ref name="BDMEP-INMET"/><ref name="INMET-A507-Uberlândia-MG"/> The highest 24-hour precipitation accumulation was {{formatnum:157.8}} mm on 12 December 1986. Other significant accumulations of {{formatnum:100}} mm or more include: {{formatnum:129.8}} mm on 9 November 1999, {{formatnum:126.8}} mm on 19 January 1983, {{formatnum:116.8}} mm on 20 December 2007, {{formatnum:106.2}} mm on 15 January 2002, and {{formatnum:105.8}} mm on 1 December 1980.<ref name="Gráficos-A507"/>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Igreja_distrito_miraporanga_Uberlândia.jpg|thumb|left|Church in the center of the Miraporanga district]]

The religious diversity found in Uberlândia is comparable to the cultural diversity present in the city. Although it developed on a predominantly [[Catholicism|Catholic]] social foundation, dozens of different [[Protestantism|Protestant]] denominations can now be found. Additionally, the growth of those without religion has been notable, reaching nearly 9% of the population.<ref name="Religião"/>

Uberlândia is located in the country with the largest Catholic population in absolute numbers worldwide. The [[Catholic Church]] had its legal status recognized by the federal government in October 2009,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/mat/2009/10/08/senado-aprova-acordo-com-vaticano-767959739.asp |title=Senate approves agreement with the Vatican |author=Cristiane Agostine |publisher=O Globo |date=8 October 2009 |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124545/http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/mat/2009/10/08/senado-aprova-acordo-com-vaticano-767959739.asp |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref> although Brazil is currently an officially [[secular state]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://jus.com.br/artigos/8519/brasil-estado-laico-e-a-inconstitucionalidade-da-existencia-de-simbolos-religiosos-em-predios-publicos|title=Brazil: Secular state and the unconstitutionality of religious symbols in public buildings|last=Fernando Fonseca de Queiroz|date=October 2005|publisher=Jus Navigandi|access-date=21 June 2022 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20220621124532/https://jus.com.br/artigos/8519/brasil-estado-laico-e-a-inconstitucionalidade-da-existencia-de-simbolos-religiosos-em-predios-publicos |archive-date=2022-06-21}}</ref> The city is home to a variety of Protestant or reformed denominations, such as the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembly of God]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aduberlandia.com.br/portal/historia-da-ad-brasil |title=History of AD Uberlândia |author=Assembleia de Deus in Uberlândia |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124550/http://www.aduberlandia.com.br/portal/historia-da-ad-brasil |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref>

According to data from the [[2010 Brazilian Census|census of 2010]], conducted by the IBGE, Uberlândia's population is composed of: [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (54.58%), [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (25.16%), [[Irreligion|people without religion]] (8.36%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (7.4%), and 4.5% are divided among other religions.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/uberlandia/pesquisa/23/22107|title=Religion|access-date=16 July 2019|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190716124532/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/uberlandia/pesquisa/23/22107|archive-date=2019-07-16}}</ref> According to the 2022 Brazilian census, 48.49% of the city's population identified as Catholic, 28.54% as evangelical or Protestant, 5.98% as spiritist, 1.04% as practitioners of Umbanda or Candomblé, 0.01% as followers of traditional religions, 5.92% as followers of other religions, 9.85% as irreligious, 0.02% as other, and 0.15% as undeclared.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2025/06/06/mapa-censo-ibge-religiao.ghtml|title=MAP: What is the most popular religion in your city?|date=6 June 2025|access-date=7 June 2025|website=G1|language=pt-br|archive-url=https://archive.today/20250607124532/https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2025/06/06/mapa-censo-ibge-religiao.ghtml|archive-date=2025-06-07}}</ref>

== Politics ==
=== Administration ===
[[File:Uberlândia-MG 12.jpg|thumb|Headquarters of the Uberlândia Municipal Chamber, on João Naves de Ávila Avenue, in the Santa Mônica neighborhood, east zone of the city.]]

According to the [[Constitution of Brazil|1988 Constitution]], Uberlândia is part of a [[Federal republic|federal]] [[Presidential system|presidential]] [[constitutional republic]]. It was inspired by the [[United States|American]] model; however, the Brazilian legal system follows the [[Roman law|Roman]]-[[Germanic law|Germanic]] tradition of [[positive law]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.oas.org/juridico/mla/en/bra/en_bra-int-des-ordrjur.html |title=The Brazilian Legal System |language=English |author=Organization of American States (OAS) |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121016124532/http://www.oas.org/juridico/mla/en/bra/en_bra-int-des-ordrjur.html |archive-date=2012-10-16}}</ref> Municipal administration is carried out by the [[executive power]] and the [[legislative power]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.leismunicipais.com.br/cgi-local/orglaw.pl?city=Paul%EDnia&state=sp |title=The Municipal Public Power and the constitutional obligation to establish an internal control system |author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima |date=8 February 2006 |publisher=JusVi |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120908113231/http://www.leismunicipais.com.br/cgi-local/orglaw.pl?city=Paul%EDnia&state=sp |archive-date=2012-09-08 }}</ref>

Before 1930, municipalities were led by the presidents of municipal chambers, also called executive agents or intendants. Only after the [[Revolution of 1930|1930 Revolution]] were municipal powers separated into executive and legislative branches.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.camarabp.sp.gov.br/conteudo/camara-municipal-da-estancia-de-braganca-paulista- |title=Bragança Paulista Municipal Chamber |author=Bragança Paulista Municipal Chamber |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62vUMmKH8?url=http://www.camarabp.sp.gov.br/conteudo/camara-municipal-da-estancia-de-braganca-paulista- |archive-date=2011-11-03 }}</ref> The first intendant of the municipality was Antônio Alves dos Santos, and the first executive leader and [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]] was Lúcio Libânio. Over twenty-nine terms, 25 mayors and ten executive agents have led Uberlândia’s city hall.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_prefeitos.php |title=Mayors |author=Prefeitura |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-date=27 January 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100127064800/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_prefeitos.php |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2016, the mayor elected in the [[2016 Brazilian municipal elections|Brazilian municipal elections]] was Odelmo Leão, from the [[Progressive Party (Brazil)|Progressive Party]] (PP), with 72.05% of the valid votes.<ref name="G1_Eleições2016">{{cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/eleicoes/2016/noticia/2017/01/odelmo-leao-toma-posse-como-prefeito-de-uberlandia.html |title=Odelmo Leão takes office as mayor of Uberlândia |date=1 January 2017 |access-date=4 January 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170104124532/http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/eleicoes/2016/noticia/2017/01/odelmo-leao-toma-posse-como-prefeito-de-uberlandia.html |archive-date=2017-01-04}}</ref>

The [[legislative power]] is constituted by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of 21 councilors elected for four-year terms (in accordance with Article 29 of the [[Constitution]]<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm |author=DJI |title=Federal Constitution - CF - 1988 / Art. 29 |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/68Y1F81vD?url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm%20 |archive-date=2012-06-19 }}</ref>) and is structured as follows:<ref name="vereadores">{{cite web |url=http://apuracao.terra.com.br/2008/1turno/mg/54038/index.shtml#vereador |title=First round results (Councilors) |date=6 October 2008| access-date=16 October 2010 |author=Terra |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121016124550/http://apuracao.terra.com.br/2008/1turno/mg/54038/index.shtml#vereador |archive-date=2012-10-16}}</ref> four seats for the [[Progressistas|Progressive Party]] (PP); three seats for the [[Workers' Party (Brazil)|Workers' Party]] (PT); three seats for the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB); two seats for the [[Democratic Labour Party (Brazil)|Democratic Labour Party]] (PDT); two seats for the [[Social Christian Party (Brazil)|Social Christian Party]] (PSC); one seat for the [[Brazilian Socialist Party]] (PSB); one for the [[Liberal Party (Brazil, 2006)|Party of the Republic]] (PR); one for the [[Christian Democracy (Brazil)|Christian Social Democratic Party]] (PSDC); one for the [[Social Liberal Party (Brazil)|Social Liberal Party]] (PSL); one for the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB); one for the [[Democrats (Brazil)|Democrats]] (DEM); and one for the [[Cidadania|Popular Socialist Party]] (PPS). The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the Executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law). The municipality of Uberlândia is governed by organic laws.<ref>{{cite web |author=Prefeitura |title=Uberlândia Organic Law |url=https://leismunicipais.com.br/lei-organica-uberlandia-mg |access-date=16 October 2010 |archive-date=8 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220408040900/https://leismunicipais.com.br/lei-organica-uberlandia-mg |url-status=live }}</ref> The city is also the seat of a [[Comarca|judicial district]].<ref name="comarca">{{cite web|url=http://www.tjmg.jus.br/juridico/sf/comarcas.html|title=List of judicial districts in the State of Minas Gerais|author=Minas Gerais State Court of Justice|access-date=16 October 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100825194853/http://www.tjmg.jus.br/juridico/sf/comarcas.html|archive-date=2010-08-25}}</ref> According to the [[Regional Electoral Courts|TRE]]-[[Minas Gerais|MG]] (Minas Gerais Regional Electoral Court), the municipality had 373,744 voters in 2006.<ref name="Eleitores">{{cite report |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=partpol&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Political Representation 2006 |date=2006 |access-date=15 October 2010 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731132148/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=partpol&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archive-date=2013-07-31}}</ref>

== Subdivisions ==
[[File:Bairros_regiao_central_uberlandia.jpg|thumb|Map of neighborhoods in the central region of Uberlândia]]

When it was emancipated, Uberlândia consisted of only two districts: São Pedro de Uberabinha (seat) and Santa Maria (now Miraporanga), formalized by Provincial Law 3,643, of 31 August 1888, which declared Uberlândia's elevation to municipality status. It is currently officially subdivided into five districts: Cruzeiro dos Peixotos, Martinésia, Miraporanga, the Seat, and Tapuirama. The last district change was made under State Decree-Law 1,058, of 16 December 1943, when the districts of Tapuirama and Cruzeiro dos Peixotos were created and annexed to the municipality of Uberlândia.<ref name="IBGE_História">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/uberlandia.pdf |title=History |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |access-date=19 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121019124545/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/uberlandia.pdf |archive-date=2012-10-19}}</ref>

The municipality is also divided into five regions, referred to by the city hall as sectors: Central Region, East Zone, West Zone, North Zone, and South Zone. The city is further subdivided into approximately 160 neighborhoods.<ref name="GrandeBrasil_Uberlândia"/> However, to reduce this large number, the city hall has been implementing the "Integrated Neighborhoods" project since the 1980s, aiming to consolidate various neighborhoods through a detailed study of the current urban fabric. The goal is to reduce the number of neighborhoods to eighty.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=17&id_cg=1602 |title=Integrated Neighborhoods |author=Prefeitura |access-date=19 October 2010 |archive-date=18 July 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090718223755/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=17&id_cg=1602 |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Economy ==
[[File:Vista parcial Uberlandia MG.JPG|thumb|View of the UBT (Uberlândia Business Tower) and part of the central region of Uberlândia]]
[[File:CSUdi.jpg|thumb|Center Shopping, the largest shopping complex in Uberlândia and the entire interior of Minas Gerais]]
[[File:UdiShopping.jpg|thumb|Uberlândia Shopping, the largest mall in the south zone and the second largest in the city]]

The [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) of Uberlândia is the 27th largest in Brazil,<ref name = "IBGE_PIB"/> excelling in the area of [[Service sector|service provision]]. According to [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] data from 2008, the municipality had a GDP of 14,270,392,490 [[Brazilian real|reais]].<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Of this total, 2,003,554,000 reais are from taxes on products net of subsidies.<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pibmunic&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2007 |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124550/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pibmunic&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref> The GDP ''per capita'' is {{BRL|22926.50}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/>

Agriculture is the least significant sector of Uberlândia's economy. Of the city's total GDP, 271,271,000 reais is the gross value added by agriculture.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> According to the IBGE, in 2008, the municipality had a herd of 205,709 [[cattle]], 619,464 [[pig]]s, 6,169 [[Equidae|horses]], 762 [[goat]]s, 78 [[Bubalina|buffalo]], 319 [[donkey]]s, 541 [[mule]]s, 4,633 [[sheep]], 378 [[rabbit]]s, and 5,798,617 [[poultry]], including 2,480,000 [[chicken]]s and 3,318,617 [[rooster]]s, chicks, and pullets.<ref name="Agropecuária" /> In 2008, the city produced 48,900,000 liters of milk from 31,623 [[cattle|cows]]. It produced 50,068,000 dozen eggs and 1,400 kilograms of [[honey]] from [[bee]]s.<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2008&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Livestock 2008 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124532/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2008&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref> In terms of temporary crops, the main products are [[corn]] (140,400 tons), [[soybean]] (138,330 tons), and [[sugarcane]] (28,500 tons).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2008&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Temporary Crops 2008 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124545/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2008&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref><ref name="agricultura">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pamclo_2007&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Municipal Agricultural Production - Cereals, Legumes, and Oilseeds |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2007 |access-date=15 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121015124550/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=pamclo_2007&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref>

The industry is currently the second most significant sector for the economy of Uberlândia. {{BRL|2729956}} of the municipal GDP come from the gross value added by industry (secondary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> A significant portion of the municipal secondary sector’s contribution originates from the Guiomar de Freitas Costa Industrial District, located in the northern zone of the city. This district houses the main industries of the city, including facilities of some of Brazil’s largest companies and even [[Multinational corporation|multinationals]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.unedi.com.br/index.php?pag=noticias_ver&id=188 |title=Notícias Unedi |author=União das Empresas do Distrito Industrial de Uberlândia (UNEDI) |date=26 December 2007 |accessdate=15 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031657/http://www.unedi.com.br/index.php?pag=noticias_ver&id=188 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> such as [[Cargill]], [[Casas Bahia]], [[Algar Telecom]] (formerly CTBC), [[Monsanto]], [[Petrobras]], [[Sadia]], [[Souza Cruz]], and [[Coca-Cola]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/invista_quem_esta_aqui.php |title=Quem Está Aqui |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=15 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031658/http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/invista_quem_esta_aqui.php |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

Uberlândia is also home to [[Ambev]], which operates a distribution center in the western zone and a factory, designed to be the largest in the world, located in the rural area of the far southeastern part of the city.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/noticia/2016/02/fabrica-da-ambev-em-uberlandia-foi-projetada-para-ser-maior-do-mundo.html|title=Ambev factory in Uberlândia was designed to be the largest in the world|date=4 February 2016|accessdate=17 September 2016|publisher=G1 Triângulo Mineiro|last=Resende|first=Fernanda|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20160917123456/http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/noticia/2016/02/fabrica-da-ambev-em-uberlandia-foi-projetada-para-ser-maior-do-mundo.html|archivedate=17 September 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/cidade-e-regiao/ambev-vai-construir-fabrica-de-cerveja-de-r-550-mi-em-uberlandia/|title=Ambev to build a R$550 million beer factory in Uberlândia|date=13 September 2012|accessdate=17 September 2016|publisher=Correio de Uberlândia|last=Fernandes|first=Arthur|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20160917123457/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/cidade-e-regiao/ambev-vai-construir-fabrica-de-cerveja-de-r-550-mi-em-uberlandia/|archivedate=17 September 2016}}</ref>

{{BRL|7479038}} of the municipal GDP come from service provision (tertiary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> The tertiary sector is currently the largest contributor to Uberlândia’s GDP. According to the IBGE, in 2008, the city had 21,492 businesses and {{formatnum:339922}} workers, with {{formatnum:183888}} total employed personnel and {{formatnum:156034}} salaried employees. [[Salary|Salaries]] combined with other remunerations amounted to 2,358,463 reais, and the average monthly salary in the municipality was 2.9 [[minimum wage]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Statistics of the Central Business Register |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2008 |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031659/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

Uberlândia is home to some of the largest [[shopping mall|shopping centers]] in the Triângulo Mineiro region, such as Center Shopping and Pátio Sabiá in the Eastern Zone; Uberlândia Shopping, Pátio Vinhedos, Village Altamira, and Griff Shopping in the Southern Zone;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.griffshopping.com.br/shopping |title=The Shopping Center |author=Griff Shopping |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031700/http://www.griffshopping.com.br/shopping |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> and Pratic Shopping in the central region.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.praticshopping.com.br/pratic_center/pratic_center.php |title=Pratic Shopping is a shopping center located on the 2nd floor of the Central Terminal. |author=Pratic Shopping |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031701/http://www.praticshopping.com.br/pratic_center/pratic_center.php |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> As in the rest of the country, the peak sales period in Uberlândia is [[Christmas]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2010/10/10/48432/comercio_deve_gerar_1,7_mil_vagas_.html |title=Commerce expected to generate 1,700 jobs |author=Clarice Monteiro |date=9 October 2010 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031702/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2010/10/10/48432/comercio_deve_gerar_1,7_mil_vagas_.html |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

=== Business tourism ===
Uberlândia is part of the Triângulo Mineiro tourist circuit<ref>{{cite web |title=Circuito Turístico da Canastra |url=http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/circuitos-turisticos/lista-de-circuitos/999-circuito-turistico-triangulo-mineiro- |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130324025812/http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/circuitos-turisticos/lista-de-circuitos/999-circuito-turistico-triangulo-mineiro- |archivedate=24 March 2013 |accessdate=12 February 2013 |publisher=Secretaria de Estado de Turismo de Minas Gerais}}</ref> and stands out in the area of [[business tourism]] on a national scale and [[commerce|commercial tourism]] on a regional level. Recently, the city was ranked by the [[International Congress and Convention Association]] (ICCA), the leading organization in the international tourism and events sector, as one of the top Brazilian cities hosting international events, placing ninth. Among the top twelve cities, Uberlândia is the only non-capital city, surpassed only by major centers such as [[São Paulo]], [[Rio de Janeiro]], [[Belo Horizonte]], and [[Brasília]]. Hundreds of events from various parts of the country and even from abroad are held in the city annually.<ref name="MegaMinas_Nagócios">{{cite web |url=http://megaminas.globo.com/2010/07/10/uberlandia-se-torna-referencia-no-turismo-de-negocios |title=Uberlândia becomes a reference in business tourism |author=Mega Minas.com |date=10 July 2010 |accessdate=18 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20250707125500/http://megaminas.globo.com/2010/07/10/uberlandia-se-torna-referencia-no-turismo-de-negocios |archivedate=7 July 2025}}</ref>

A study conducted by organizations revealed that tourism is the largest generator of job opportunities worldwide, with over 200 million jobs, and holds significant importance in GDP, directly impacting approximately 50 economic sectors. Among the segments, event tourism is considered the fastest-growing globally, generating US$4 trillion annually. In Uberlândia, 17% of the companies organizing events are large-scale, and in 82% of the events, the target audience consists of company employees.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2007/06/15/28415/cdl_uberlandia_aposta_no_turismo_de.html |title=CDL Uberlândia bets on business tourism |author=Lívia Gomide |date=21 May 2008 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |accessdate=18 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031703/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2007/06/15/28415/cdl_uberlandia_aposta_no_turismo_de.html |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

== Urban structure ==
Uberlândia has a well-developed infrastructure. In 2000, the city had {{formatnum:144461}} dwellings, including [[apartment]]s, [[house]]s, and rooms. Of these, 93,233 were owned properties, with {{formatnum:69040}} fully paid (47.79%), 24,193 under acquisition (16.75%), and 37,358 [[renting|rented]] (25.86%); 13,507 properties were provided, with 2,599 by employers (1.80%) and 10,908 provided in other ways (7.55%). Another 363 were occupied in other forms (0.25%).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |title=Infrastructure - Permanent Private Dwellings and Residents |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CMN)|accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031704/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> The municipality has treated water, [[electricity]], [[sewage|wastewater]], [[municipal solid waste|urban cleaning]], [[fixed telephony|landline telephony]], and [[mobile telephony]]. In 2000, 97.68% of households were served by the [[Water supply network|general water supply network]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |title=Infrastructure - Water Supply |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CMN)|accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031705/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> 97.38% of residences had waste collection;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |title=Infrastructure - Waste Disposal |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CMN)|accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031706/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> and 96.10% of residences had sanitary sewer systems.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |title=Infrastructure - Sanitary Sewage |author=Confederação Nacional de Municípios (CMN)|accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031707/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp?iIdMun=100131826 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> In 2009, the United Nations recognized Uberlândia as the first city in Brazil with fully accessible public transportation for people with disabilities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prograd.uff.br/sensibiliza/onu-reconhece-uberl%C3%A2ndia-como-primeira-cidade-com-acessibilidade-no-brasil|title=UN recognizes Uberlândia as the first city with accessibility in Brazil|date=2009|work=prograd|publisher=prograd.uff.br|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031708/http://www.prograd.uff.br/sensibiliza/onu-reconhece-uberl%C3%A2ndia-como-primeira-cidade-com-acessibilidade-no-brasil|archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

[[File:Unidade I do Hospital do Câncer em Uberlândia.jpg|thumb|left|Cancer Hospital, at the Umuarama Campus of the [[Federal University of Uberlândia]], in the Eastern Zone of the city.]]
According to the performance index of the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|Unified Health System]] (IDSUS), in 2010, Uberlândia had a score of 5.32, the fourth worst among similar cities, below the state average (5.87) and the national average (5.46).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://portal.saude.gov.br/portal/saude/area.cfm?id_area=1080 |title=Title not yet provided (please add) |publisher=portal.saude.gov.br |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031709/http://portal.saude.gov.br/portal/saude/area.cfm?id_area=1080 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>
In 2005, the municipality had 198 health establishments, 64 private and 134 public, including hospitals, [[emergency department|emergency rooms]], [[health center]]s, and [[dentistry|dental services]]. These facilities have 944 beds for [[hospitalization]], with 420 private and 524 public.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |title=Health Services 2005 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2005 |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031710/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=317020&nomemun=Uberl%E2ndia |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> The city has twelve general hospitals, two public and ten philanthropic. In 2008, 8,160 live births were recorded, with 10.2% [[preterm birth|premature]], 88.2% delivered by [[caesarean section]], and 15.5% to mothers aged 10 to 19 (0.7% aged 10 to 14). === Education ===
[[File:UFU_CampusEdFisica.jpg|thumb|upright|Physical Education Campus of the [[Federal University of Uberlândia]]]]

Uberlândia has schools in all regions of the municipality. Due to intense urbanization, the few residents in the [[rural area]] have easy access to schools in nearby urban neighborhoods.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=13 |title=Education |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031719/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=13 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> Education in municipal schools is of a lower level than in state schools, but the city hall is conducting studies to improve municipal public education to achieve better results in the [[Basic Education Development Index|IDEB]].<ref name="IDEB_Índice">{{cite web |url=http://ideb.inep.gov.br/Site/ |title=Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) - Results and Goals |author=Federal Government |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20071009195109/http://ideb.inep.gov.br/Site/ |archivedate=9 October 2007}}</ref> In 2008, the municipality had approximately 18,493 enrollments, 1,149 teachers, and 67 schools in the public and private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> The first public school in the city was the Bueno Brandão State School, established by then-governor Júlio Bueno Brandão and authorized by decree-law no. 3200 20 June 1911. The school began operations on 1 February 1915<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/midia/documentos/cultura/arquivo_bueno_brandao.doc |title=Archival and documentary sources |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=21 November 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031720/http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/midia/documentos/cultura/arquivo_bueno_brandao.doc |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> and celebrated its centennial on 20 June 2011.

=== Crime and public safety ===
As in most medium and large Brazilian municipalities, crime is also a problem in Uberlândia. In 2006, the average [[homicide]] rate in the municipality was 18.2 per 100,000 inhabitants. The rate of [[death|fatalities]] by [[firearm]], which was 4.6 in 2002, rose to 11.7 in 2003, reaching 12.4 in 2004, 10.0 in 2005, and 12.0 in 2006.<ref name="ritla">{{cite web|url=http://www.ritla.net/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=242|title=Municipal database|author=Ritla|date=2008|work=Map of Violence in Brazilian Municipalities-2008|format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]]|accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031725/http://www.ritla.net/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=242 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> Uberlândia also ranks 128th among the 256 Brazilian cities with populations over 100,000 that had the highest number of incidents involving the use, possession, and trafficking of [[drug]]s. In 2005, 213 such incidents were recorded in the city, resulting in an average of 36.4 per 100,000 residents. According to the [[Military Police of Minas Gerais State]] (PMMG), a significant portion of the murders in the city (78%<ref name="Ktaqui_Criminalidade"/>) involve [[gang]]s competing in drug trafficking.<ref name="Correio_Assassinatos">{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2008/06/08/29814/uberlandia_sobe_no_ranking_da_viole.html |title=Uberlândia rises in the violence ranking |author=Lucas Barbosa |date=10 June 2010 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031726/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2008/06/08/29814/uberlandia_sobe_no_ranking_da_viole.html |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

To address the municipality's high crime rates, the city hall is making significant investments in public safety, together with the Military Police. Between 2006 and 2009, the region received 245 police vehicles, and 984 personnel joined the Military Police. Efforts to combat [[drug trafficking]] were also strengthened.<ref name="Correio_Assassinatos"/> These investments have shown positive results. According to Gilmar Souza de Freitas, head of the 9th Integrated Public Safety Region (Risp), in 2005, 48,951 criminal incidents were recorded in the region, which decreased to 37,152 in 2009.<ref name="Ktaqui_Criminalidade">{{cite web |url=http://ktaqui.com/tenente/news/-Seguran-a-P-blica-ter-Audi-ncia-P-blica-em-Uberl- |title=Relationship between trafficking and homicides concerns Uberlândia |author=Ktaqui |date=4 September 2010 |accessdate=16 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031727/http://ktaqui.com/tenente/news/-Seguran-a-P-blica-ter-Audi-ncia-P-blica-em-Uberl- |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

=== Services and communications ===
[[File:Novocenariomgtv.jpg|thumb|left|Former set of MGTV on [[TV Integração Uberlândia]], in 2009]]

The water supply service for the entire city is provided by the Municipal Water and Sewage Department (DMAE). Safety and quality standards are based on the guidelines of the [[Ministry of Health (Brazil)|Ministry of Health]]. The quality of Uberlândia’s water is certified through the monthly analysis of 80 parameters conducted by the Bioagri Ambiental laboratory.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/home_dmae.php?id=490 |title=Water Quality in Uberlândia |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031728/http://www3.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/home_dmae.php?id=490 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> Sewage collection is managed by the city hall itself. In 2012, the city was selected by the Trata Brasil Institute as a model for [http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/cidade-e-regiao/uberlandia-vai-ser-modelo-de-gestao-em-saneamento/ sanitation management]. In the municipality, as in the entire state of [[Minas Gerais]], the electricity supply is provided by [[CEMIG|Energy Company of Minas Gerais]] (Cemig). In 2003, there were 194,018 consumers, and 1,058,828,354 [[Watt-hour|kWh]] of energy were consumed.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /> The city also offers dial-up and [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]) [[internet]] services provided by various free and paid [[Internet service provider|ISPs]]. Mobile phone services are offered by several operators. The city is also covered by [[3G]]<ref name="Correio_3G">{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2009/06/14/38062/3g_revoluciona_o_mundo_das_telecomunicacoes.html |title=3G revolutionizes the world of telecommunications |author=Núbia Mota |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |date=15 July 2009 |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031729/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2009/06/14/38062/3g_revoluciona_o_mundo_das_telecomunicacoes.html |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> and [[4G]] mobile data connections. The area code ([[direct distance dialing|DDD]]) for Uberlândia is 034<ref name="Guiatel_DDD">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatel.com.br/info_mg3.htm |title=Minas Gerais |author=Guiatel |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031730/http://www.guiatel.com.br/info_mg3.htm |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> and the [[postal code]] ([[Código de Endereçamento Postal|CEP]]) ranges from 38.400-000 to 38.415-999.<ref name="GrandeBrasil_Uberlândia">{{cite web|url=http://www.grandebrasil.com.br/Brasil/MG_Minas_Gerais/?Cidade_Uberl%C3%A2ndia+4048 |title=Municipality of Uberlândia |author=GrandeBrasil.com |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031731/http://www.grandebrasil.com.br/Brasil/MG_Minas_Gerais/?Cidade_Uberl%C3%A2ndia+4048 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correios.com.br/|title=Postal codes of Brazilian cities|author=[[Empresa Brasileira de Correios e Telégrafos|Correios]]|accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031732/http://www.correios.com.br/ |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> On 12 January 2009, the municipality began offering [[Mobile number portability|number portability]] to the Zona da Mata region, Campo das Vertentes, and other cities with the 16 area code (interior of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]]), 41 ([[Paraná (state)|Paraná]]), 34 and 35 ([[Minas Gerais]]), and 74 ([[Bahia]]). === Transportation ===
[[File:Rpudi.jpg|thumb|Rondon Pacheco Avenue, the main arterial road of the city of Uberlândia]]
[[File:Uberlandia cesario alvim avenue.jpg|thumb|Cesário Alvim Avenue, an important thoroughfare in the city, crossing the Central Region and the Eastern Zone.]]
The bus system consists of five terminals strategically located in the main neighborhoods of the city, interconnected by express buses. In addition to express bus connections, which have no intermediate stops, the terminals are served by feeder buses that form the secondary branches of this mass transportation system.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gazetadeuberlandia.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&catid=91%3Aeditorial&id=567%3Auberlandia-122-anos&Itemid=284 |title=Uberlândia 122 years |author=Gazeta de Uberlândia |date=30 August 2010 |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031740/http://www.gazetadeuberlandia.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&catid=91%3Aeditorial&id=567%3Auberlandia-122-anos&Itemid=284 |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> The city also has thirteen bus stations located at key points along João Naves de Ávila Avenue, connecting two terminals over a ten-kilometer route (this avenue also has express buses that do not stop at the stations), such as the E131 express line and the T131 stopping line, linking the Central Terminal to the Santa Luzia Terminal. The city recently welcomed three new transportation companies—São Miguel, Sorriso de Minas, and Auto Trans—with new buses.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_transportes.php |title=SIT - Integrated Transportation System |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031741/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_transportes.php |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref> The city has twenty-six express and semi-express buses operating daily, transporting thousands of passengers through the Integrated Transportation System (SIT).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://uipi.com.br/destaques/destaque-1/2014/03/10/onibus-expressos-beneficiam-transito-e-passageiros-em-uberlandia/|title=Express buses benefit traffic and passengers in Uberlândia|last=Uipi|first=Uipi|date=10 March 2014|publisher=TV Vitoriosa|accessdate=13 January 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402152057/http://uipi.com.br/destaques/destaque-1/2014/03/10/onibus-expressos-beneficiam-transito-e-passageiros-em-uberlandia/|archivedate=2 April 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Sobre o Viaduto Engenheiro Azeli - panoramio.jpg|thumb|Section of [[BR-365 (Brazil highway)|BR-365]] in Uberlândia]]

The municipal fleet in 2010 consisted of 291,318 vehicles, including 167,795 cars, 9,795 trucks, 2,935 tractor trucks, 16,781 pickups, 873 minibuses, 77,612 motorcycles, 13,951 scooters, 1,440 buses, and 136 wheeled tractors.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=317020&idtema=128&search=minas-gerais|title=Fleet 2013 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2013 |accessdate=20 February 2015 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20150220045678/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=317020&idtema=128&search=minas-gerais|archivedate=20 February 2015}}</ref> Paved and expanded avenues, along with numerous [[traffic lights]], facilitate city [[traffic]], but the growth in the number of vehicles over the past ten years has led to increasingly slow traffic, especially in the municipal seat, where congestion on some major roads can reach 2.5&nbsp;km. Additionally, finding parking spaces in the commercial center has become challenging, causing some losses to [[commerce]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.autoescolasilvana.com.br/v1/content.asp?ContentID=361&StatusID=3 |title=Traffic feels the effects of fleet growth |author=Correio de Uberlândia |date=18 March 2009 |publisher=Auto Escola Silvana |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031742/http://www.autoescolasilvana.com.br/v1/content.asp?ContentID=361&StatusID=3 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> The Department of Traffic and Transportation (SETTRAN) is the municipal agency responsible for the city’s traffic and transportation system. It regulates and oversees the [[public transportation]] system, manages traffic, and, through its Traffic Agents, issues citations to drivers who commit traffic violations.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=22 |title=Traffic and Transportation |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031743/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=22 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> The municipality has easy access to the [[BR-050 (Brazil highway)|BR-050]] highway to [[Uberaba]] and [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]] to the south (expanded to São Paulo) and [[Araguari]], [[Catalão|Catalão/GO]], and [[Brasília|Brasília/DF]] to the north; [[BR-267 (Brazil highway)|BR-267]] to [[Porto Murtinho|Porto Murtinho/MS]]; [[BR-365]] to [[Ituiutaba]], [[Patrocínio]], [[Patos de Minas]], [[Pirapora]], and [[Montes Claros]]; [[BR-452 (Brazil highway)|BR-452]] to [[Rio Verde, Goiás|Rio Verde/GO]], [[Itumbiara|Itumbiara/GO]], [[Araxá]], and [[Belo Horizonte]]; and [[BR-497 (Brazil highway)|BR-497]] to [[Prata, Minas Gerais|Prata]], [[Campina Verde]], and [[Iturama]]. Additionally, it has access to state and national highways through paved, dual-lane secondary roads.<ref name="cidadesnet.com">{{cite web|url=http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/uberlandia.htm |title=Uberlândia - MG |author=Cidades.Net |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031744/http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/uberlandia.htm |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

The city also had two historic railway stations: Uberabinha Station (in the urban area) and Sobradinho (in the rural area), the latter designated as a Municipal Historical Heritage Site by Decree no. 10,228 31 March 2006. Currently, the only active station is the Uberlândia Railway Station (also in the urban area), used for freight trains. All stations belonged to the [[Companhia Mogiana de Estradas de Ferro|Mogiana Railway Company]], which played a significant role in the municipality’s growth in the regional and national markets. However, after the decline of [[coffee]] in the Triângulo Mineiro region and the rise of road transportation, passenger rail transport faced a collapse and strong competition, forcing companies to improve their services.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=626 |title=Sobradinho Railway Station |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031745/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=626 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> In the early 1970s, the Mogiana lines were absorbed by the São Paulo state-owned [[Ferrovia Paulista S/A|FEPASA]], including the Catalão Line and the Ômega-Araguari Branch Line, which, interconnected, cross Uberlândia and connect it to [[Araguari]] and [[Uberaba]]. Passenger rail transport ceased in the city in 1997, still under FEPASA’s management, and the following year, both lines were privatized for freight transport, currently under the concession of Ferrovia Centro-Atlântica.<ref>{{cite web |title=Uberlândia -- Railway Stations of Minas Gerais |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/mogiana_triangulo/uberlandia.htm |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200128104805/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/mogiana_triangulo/uberlandia.htm |archivedate=28 January 2020 |accessdate=22 November 2020 |website=www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Stevenson-nova -- Railway Stations of Minas Gerais |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/mogiana_triangulo/stevenson-nov.htm |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200212164349/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/mogiana_triangulo/stevenson-nov.htm |archivedate=12 February 2020 |accessdate=22 November 2020 |website=www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br}}</ref>
[[File:Gustavadas - Sala de Embarque.jpg|thumb|View from the boarding area of [[Uberlândia Airport]].]]
Uberlândia is home to the [[Uberlândia Airport|Tenente Coronel Aviador César Bombonato Airport]], commonly known as Uberlândia Airport. It is the third largest airport in [[Minas Gerais]] and the 27th in Brazil in terms of passenger traffic, with the capacity to handle over 600,000 passengers annually and accommodate larger aircraft such as the [[Airbus A320]] and [[Boeing 737]] after renovations in 2005. The airport recorded 907,169 passengers in 2011 and surpassed 1 million in 2012.<ref>{{cite web |author=INFRAERO |date=30 January 2012 |title=Airport Statistics |url=http://www.infraero.gov.br/index.php/br/estatistica-dos-aeroportos.html |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20120314133712/http://www.infraero.gov.br/index.php/br/estatistica-dos-aeroportos.html |archivedate=14 March 2012 |accessdate=30 January 2012}}</ref> The airport operates direct flights to only four Brazilian cities: [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Campinas]], [[Guarulhos]], and [[São Paulo]]. It is served by several companies, including [[LATAM Airlines Brasil|LATAM]], [[Gol Linhas Aéreas]], and [[Azul Linhas Aéreas Brasileiras|Azul Linhas Aéreas]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.infraero.gov.br/index.php/br/aeroportos/minas-gerais/aeroporto-de-uberlandia.html |title=Uberlândia Airport |author=Infraero |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20120310163437/http://www.infraero.gov.br/index.php/br/aeroportos/minas-gerais/aeroporto-de-uberlandia.html |archivedate=10 March 2012 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Portal Globo |date=14 January 2015 |title=Uberlândia will have direct flights to Recife and Porto Seguro in February |url=http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/noticia/2015/01/uberlandia-tera-voos-direto-para-recife-e-porto-seguro-em-fevereiro.html |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170212091014/http://g1.globo.com/minas-gerais/triangulo-mineiro/noticia/2015/01/uberlandia-tera-voos-direto-para-recife-e-porto-seguro-em-fevereiro.html |archivedate=12 February 2017 |accessdate=11 February 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.emsampa.com.br/wardest_uberlandia.htm |title=Uberlândia - MG |author=Emsampa |accessdate=17 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031746/http://www.emsampa.com.br/wardest_uberlandia.htm |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

== Culture and leisure ==
=== Cultural spaces ===
The city boasts a rich cultural heritage, with 19 properties listed as heritage sites.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=630 |title=Listed Properties - Identification and History |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031747/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=630 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> Among them are Tubal Vilela Square, designated as a Municipal Historical Heritage Site by Decree no. 9,676 22 November 2004, part of urban planning projects from the late 19th century aimed at building a modern city;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=632 |title=Praça Tubal Vilela |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031748/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=632 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> Nossa Senhora do Rosário Church, listed under Law no. 4,263 9 December 1985, known as the oldest religious building in Uberlândia’s urban area, with construction beginning in 1893;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=616 |title=Nossa Senhora do Rosário Church |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20250707125500/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=616 |archivedate=7 July 2025 }}</ref> and Espírito Santo do Cerrado Church, registered under Municipal Law no. 5,207 27 February 1991, designed in 1975 by architect Lina Bo Bardi at the request of Friar Egydio Parisi and Friar Fúlvio, among others.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=629 |title=Espírito Santo do Cerrado Church |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031749/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_patrimonio.php?id=629 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref>

The Uberlândia Municipal Museum is located in the city center at Clarimundo Carneiro Square. It once served as City Hall and housed the Municipal Chamber. It has been completely renovated and now hosts important artworks and cultural projects.<ref name="Prefeitura_Museu">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_museu.php |title=Uberlândia Municipal Museum |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=18 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031750/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/cidade_museu.php |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref>

=== Events ===
The city also organizes numerous festivals and events, often held in public spaces.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=10&id_cg=53 |title=Cultural Traditions of the City |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031751/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id=10&id_cg=53 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> One of the most traditional events is [[Carnival]], which originally featured a variety of music, including [[waltz]]es, [[mazurka]]s, and [[schottische]]s. The main attraction, however, was ''catira''. Today, in addition to club [[Ball (dance event)|balls]], the municipality is home to four [[samba school]]s—Tabajara, Acadêmicos do Samba, Garotos do Samba, and Unidos do Chatão—as well as the "Axé" and "Unidos de São Gabriel" [[carnival block]]s, which hold the street carnival in Uberlândia.<ref name="Prefeitura_Carnaval">{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=150&id=10 |title=Uberlândia Carnival |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031752/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=150&id=10 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> The [[Festa Junina|June Festivals]], which are celebrated throughout June in honor of three saints, are also noteworthy: [[Anthony of Padua|St. Anthony]] on the 13th, [[John the Baptist|St. John the Baptist]] on the 24th, and [[Saint Peter|St. Peter]] on the 29th. In the city, the June Festivals increasingly extend into July, known as July Festivals. Grilled foods such as [[sweet potato]] and [[cashew nut]], as well as traditional June festival foods derived mainly from [[corn]], [[peanut]], and [[cassava]], are consumed.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=151&id=10 |title=June Festival |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031753/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=151&id=10 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> Another notable tradition is the "''Congado de Uberlândia''." During the time of [[slavery]], a group of enslaved Black people gathered in the woods to sing and dance in honor of their patron saint, [[Our Lady of the Rosary]]. Around 1874, the Congado movement began in Uberlândia. Over time, they felt the need to hold the Congado Festival in the city. The Black community arrived in ox carts and gathered under a large tree, where Tubal Vilela Square now stands. They then followed a trail to the Nossa Senhora do Rosário Chapel, built with [[wattle and daub]] and [[moriche palm]], located at what is now Doutor Duarte Square, where they held the festival. This chapel was constructed around 1880.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=152&id=10 |title=Congado de Uberlândia |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031754/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=152&id=10 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref>

The Carnival, in addition to club balls, includes four samba schools and blocks that perform street Carnival in the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Carnaval"/> Another significant cultural highlight is the recent world record for the largest number of [[Caipira viola|viola]] players, achieved by the municipality on 28 October 2017, at Arena Sabiazinho, with 674 viola players performing simultaneously, reaffirming a tradition in [[sertanejo music]] inherited from great names.<ref>{{cite web |last=www.diariodeuberlandia.com.br |title=Uberlândia has the world's largest viola orchestra |url=https://diariodeuberlandia.com.br/noticia/17554/uberlandia-tem-a-maior-orquestra-de-viola-do-mundo |url-status= |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200810152114/https://diariodeuberlandia.com.br/noticia/17554/uberlandia-tem-a-maior-orquestra-de-viola-do-mundo |archivedate=10 August 2020 |accessdate=18 November 2019 |work=Diário de Uberlândia |date=24 July 2018 |language=pt-br}}</ref> The municipality also stands out in [[business tourism]] on a national scale.<ref name="MegaMinas_Nagócios"/>

=== Cuisine ===
The cuisine of Uberlândia follows the typical patterns of the [[Cerrado]] region. Notable dishes include rice with pork, ''[[galinhada]]'', [[pequi]] sauce, green papaya sauce, [[guariroba]], pumpkin sprouts, chicken with [[brown sauce]], and green corn angu, seasoned with saffron, annatto, or pepper. These are often made with natural products found in the region. Typical regional sweets include papaya wood sweets, cashew sweets, ambrosia, ''ameixa de queijo'', ''[[mocotó]]'' jelly, and others served with fresh cheese and rustic ''[[requeijão]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=157&id=10 |title=Cerrado Cuisine |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031755/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/secretaria.php?id_cg=157&id=10 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref>

=== Sports ===
[[File:Estádio Parque do Sabiá.jpg|thumb|left|[[Estádio Parque do Sabiá]], in the Virgílio Galassi Complex, Eastern Zone of Uberlândia.]]
As in much of the country, the most popular sport in Uberlândia is [[Association football|football]]. The main club in the city is [[Uberlândia Esporte Clube]], founded on 1 November 1922. It plays its matches at the [[Estádio Parque do Sabiá]], established on 27 May 1982, with a current capacity of up to 53,350 people. However, the record attendance was for a friendly match between [[Brazil national football team|Brazil]] and [[Republic of Ireland national football team|Ireland]], which ended with a score of 7-0. It was the first game held there, with an estimated audience of 75,000 people.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://esporte.ig.com.br/futebol/2010/06/25/parque+do+sabia+e+ipatingao+serao+redutos+cruzeirenses+na+reforma+do+mineirao+9523213.html |title=Parque do Sabiá and Ipatingão will be Cruzeiro strongholds during Mineirão's renovation |author=José Saecheta |date=25 June 2010 |publisher=Ig |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031756/http://esporte.ig.com.br/futebol/2010/06/25/parque+do+sabia+e+ipatingao+serao+redutos+cruzeirenses+na+reforma+do+mineirao+9523213.html |archivedate=2 January 2013}}</ref>

The municipality also excels in other sports such as basketball, volleyball, rugby, and individual sports. In [[basketball]], for instance, it has [[Unitri/Uberlândia]], a professional basketball team formed in August 1998 with the arrival of then-coach Ary Vidal. The team uses the Homero Santos Gymnasium and the Tancredo Neves Multi-Use Arena - Sabiazinho.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.basketbrasil.com.br/nacional/uberlandia-esta-de-volta-com-o-objetivo-de-resgatar-a-tradicao-do-basquete-mineiro |title=Uberlândia is back with the goal of reviving Minas Gerais' basketball tradition |author=BasketBrasil |date=23 July 2010 |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031757/http://www.basketbrasil.com.br/nacional/uberlandia-esta-de-volta-com-o-objetivo-de-resgatar-a-tradicao-do-basquete-mineiro |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> The city also has the women’s volleyball team [[Praia Clube]], which competes in the national league and plays at the Oranides Borges do Nascimento Gymnasium. The city also has [[Rugby football|rugby]] teams, notably Uberlândia Rugby Clube, founded on 28 February 2010;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rugbymania.com.br/2009/estatisticas_time08.asp?tempo=UBERL%C2NDIA%20RUGBY%20CLUBE |title=Information about the selected team |author=Rugby Mania |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031758/http://www.rugbymania.com.br/2009/estatisticas_time08.asp?tempo=UBERL%C2NDIA%20RUGBY%20CLUBE |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> [[American football]];<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2010/04/03/44297/texto.html |title=American football weekend in Uberlândia |author=Lucas Barbosa |date=3 April 2010 |publisher=Correio de Uberlândia |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031759/http://www.correiodeuberlandia.com.br/texto/2010/04/03/44297/texto.html |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> [[inline hockey]];<ref>{{cite web |url=http://uberlandiasabias.com.br/times |title=Teams |author=Uberlândia Sabiás |date=18 December 2009 |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031800/http://uberlandiasabias.com.br/times |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> [[futsal]];<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=4730 |title=Colégio Nacional/Futel team competes in the final stages of the State Handball Championship |author=Prefeitura |date=1 December 2009 |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archiveurl=https://archive.today/20130102031801/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=4730 |archivedate=2 January 2013 }}</ref> and other teams in various sports.

The city government provides various sports programs in the municipality.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=3980 |title=East Region offers free sports school and club |author=Prefeitura |date=27 August 2009 |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130827123456/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=3980 |archive-date=27 August 2013}}</ref> In the so-called sports centers, 15,000 spots were offered for children and teenagers in the disciplines of [[volleyball]], basketball, [[handball]], futsal, [[swimming]], soccer, [[karate]], [[judo]], and [[Sport of athletics|athletics]]. These sports centers promote access to activities for sports initiation and training, serving as leisure and recreation or as preparation for competitions. All centers are equipped with courts, soccer fields, locker rooms, restrooms, and other amenities conducive to community sports development. Classes are offered in volleyball, basketball, handball, futsal, swimming, soccer, karate, judo, track and field, and artistic gymnastics.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=1585 |title=City government offers 15,000 spots in sports centers |author=Prefeitura |date=7 February 2008 |accessdate=19 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130827123457/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/noticia.php?id=1585 |archive-date=27 August 2013}}</ref>

=== Holidays ===
In Uberlândia, there are four municipal [[public holiday|holidays]], eight national holidays, and three optional holidays. The municipal holidays are: [[Good Friday]], always celebrated in March or April; [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], always held on the Thursday following [[Trinity Sunday]]; the day of Our Lady of the Abbey, on 15 August; and the anniversary of the municipality’s emancipation, together with the day of [[Raymond Nonnatus|Saint Raymond]], on 31 August.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/home_calendario.php |title=2011 Calendar |author=Prefeitura |accessdate=24 January 2011 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130827123458/http://www.uberlandia.mg.gov.br/home_calendario.php |archive-date=27 August 2013}}</ref> According to federal law No. 9,093 of 12 September 1995, municipalities may have a maximum of four municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{cite web |url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |title=Carnival - Is it a holiday or not? Automatic time off may lead to contractual changes |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |accessdate=18 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130827123459/http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-date=27 August 2013}}</ref><ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaLei">{{cite web |url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/Leis/L9093.htm |title=Law No. 9,093, of 12 September 1995 |author=Presidency of the Republic |accessdate=18 October 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130827123500/http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/Leis/L9093.htm |archive-date=27 August 2013}}</ref>
