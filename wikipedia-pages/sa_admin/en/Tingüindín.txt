'''Tingüindín''' is a [[Municipalities of Michoacán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Michoacán]]. The municipality has an area of 174.24 square kilometres (0.29% of the surface of the state) and is bordered to the north by the municipalities of [[Villamar, Michoacán|Villamar]], [[Tangamandapio]] and [[Tangancícuaro]], to the east by [[Los Reyes, Michoacán|Los Reyes]],  and to the south and west by [[Tocumbo (municipality)|Tocumbo]].  The municipality had a population of 12,414 inhabitants according to the 2005 census.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |title=2005 Census |work=INEGI: Instituto Nacional de Estadística, Geografía e Informática |accessdate=2007-10-04 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110613183420/http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |archivedate=2011-06-13 }}</ref>  Its municipal seat is the city of the same name.

Before the arrival of the [[Spanish people|Spaniards]] to Tingüindín in 1530 the area was known as Tzinguitzuri or "Place of worship" by the mostly [[Purépecha people|Purepecha]] population.<ref>[http://michoacan.gob.mx/municipios/92tinguindin.htm Tingüindín de Argandar] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070829104433/http://www.michoacan.gob.mx/municipios/92tinguindin.htm |date=2007-08-29 }} ''Escudo''. Retrieved on October 4, 2007</ref>

The economy of the municipality is based mostly on avocado harvest ranching and the cultivation of maize and wheat.<ref>[http://iteso.mx/~carlosc/Tinguindin/tingundin_hist.htm Tingüindín de Argandar] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080127121000/http://iteso.mx/~carlosc/Tinguindin/tingundin_hist.htm |date=2008-01-27 }} ''Economía''. Retrieved on October 4, 2007</ref>

The principal festivity takes place on August 15 in honor of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary into Heaven. During this festivity, Tingüindín undergoes a complete transformation. The local plaza (town square) is filled with carnival rides and booths.  There are booths that sell many varieties of Mexican food. These festivities start on August 1 and end with a great celebration on August 15.
