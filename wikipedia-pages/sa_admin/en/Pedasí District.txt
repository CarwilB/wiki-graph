'''Pedasí'''  ({{IPA|es|peðaˈsi}}) is one of five [[district]]s of the [[Los Santos Province]], [[Panama]].

==History==

Human settlement in the Azuero peninsula where Pedasí is located dates back anywhere from 11,000 years<ref>Wright, S.J. and Samaniego, M.J. 2008. [http://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol13/iss2/art17/figure1.htm "Historical, Demographic, and Economic Correlates of Land-Use Change in the Republic of Panama"], ''Ecology and Society''. Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref> to 4500 years ago  based on pre-Columbian artifacts and other archaeological evidence recovered from various sites on the peninsula.<ref>Calvo, A.C. 2008. [http://stri.si.edu/cooke/PDF/sociedades_originarias.pdf Historia General de Panamá] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250119095846/https://stri.si.edu/cooke/PDF/sociedades_originarias.pdf |date=2025-01-19 }} (General History of Panama), p.10, ''Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute'' Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref>  Spanish colonizers arrived in the 16th century, largely wiping out or displacing existing indigenous populations.<ref>Calvo, A.C. 2008. [http://stri.si.edu/cooke/PDF/sociedades_originarias.pdf Historia General de Panamá] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250119095846/https://stri.si.edu/cooke/PDF/sociedades_originarias.pdf |date=2025-01-19 }} (General History of Panama), p.49, ''Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute'' Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref>

The district of Pedasí was created in 1840, when Panama was still tenuously united with [[Colombia]] (see the [[History of Panama]] for more background).  At the time, it was a division of the County of Los Santos (today the [[Los Santos Province|province of Los Santos]]), in the province of Panama (which covered all of present-day Panama, except for the provinces of [[Veraguas Province|Veraguas]] and [[Bocas del Toro Province|Bocas del Toro]]).  The population of Pedasí in 1845 was 701 inhabitants.<ref>[http://www.contraloria.gob.pa/DEC/Publicaciones/17-02/caracteristicas.pdf "Algunas Características de la División Política-Administrativa de la Republica por Provincia, Comarca Indígena y Distrito: Año 2004"](Some Details of the Political-Administrative Division of the Republic by Province, Native Territory and District: Year 2004), Panama Comptroller General. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 12 2009.</ref>

Since then, the land of the Pedasí district has been used extensively for cattle farming; the cultivation of [[sorghum]], [[maize|corn]], [[cantaloupe]], [[rice]] and [[watermelon]]; and the coastal villages support a small fishing industry.<ref>[http://www.minsa.gob.pa/minsa2008/final_newpage/documents/informacion_de_salud/publicaciones_en_linea/cuadernos/Documento%20marco%20para%20Plan%20contra%20HANTA%20.pdf Documento Marco para la Elaboración del Plan para Controlar el Sídrome Pulmonar por Hanta Virus (SPH)] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090320002643/http://www.minsa.gob.pa/minsa2008/final_newpage/documents/informacion_de_salud/publicaciones_en_linea/cuadernos/Documento%20marco%20para%20Plan%20contra%20HANTA%20.pdf |date=2009-03-20 }}(Framework Document for the Development of the Plan to Control the Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome (HPS), Panama Ministry of Health, National Health Policy Bureau, Department of Situation Analysis and Health Tendencies. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 17th 2009.</ref>

==Geography==

The Pedasí district covers an area of 378.3 square kilometers.<ref>[http://www.contraloria.gob.pa/DEC/Publicaciones/17-02/caracteristicas.pdf "Algunas Características de la División Política-Administrativa de la Republica por Provincia, Comarca Indígena y Distrito: Año 2004"](Some Details of the Political-Administrative Division of the Republic by Province, Native Territory and District: Year 2004), Panama Comptroller General. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 12 2009.</ref>  It was once home to one of Panama's few swaths of [[dry tropical forest]];<ref>Peréz, R.; Deago, J (2001)[http://biota.wordpress.com/2007/07/23/flora-arborea-del-bosque-seco-de-achiotines-pedasi-provincia-de-los-santos-panama/ "Flora Arbórea del Bosque Seco de los Terrenos del Laboratorio Achotines en la Provincia de Los Santos, Panamá"](Arboreal Flora of the Dry Tropical Forest on the Grounds of the Achotines Laboratory in the Province of Los Santos, Panama),''Biota Panama''. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 12 2009.</ref> however, the area underwent heavy deforestation to make room for cattle pastures during the [[history of Panama#Spanish colonial period|Spanish colonial era]], leaving many of the hills and lowlands denuded.   Some 70 hectares of dry tropical forest remain intact in the grounds surrounding the [[Achotines Laboratory]] in Pedasí, with another 50 hectares undergoing restoration.<ref>[http://www.iattc.org/AchotinesLab/AchotinesLaboratorySiteENG.htm "Achotines Laboratory"], Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission]. Retrieved Feb 12 2009.</ref>

==Climate==

Pedasí is part of a region known as the "[[arco seco]]", or the dry Pacific arc<ref>Wright, S.J. and Samaniego, M.J. 2008. [http://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol13/iss2/art17/figure1.htm "Historical, Demographic, and Economic Correlates of Land-Use Change in the Republic of Panama"], ''Ecology and Society''. Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref> due to the relatively little rainfall it receives during its summer (January to April) compared to the rest of Panama.<ref>http://www.feow.org/ecoregion_details.php?eco=208 {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110726052352/http://www.feow.org/ecoregion_details.php?eco=208 |date=2011-07-26 }} ''Freshwater Ecoregions of the World''. Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref> Average temperatures are 25-27 degrees Celsius year round.<ref>Wright, S.J. and Samaniego, M.J. 2008. [http://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol13/iss2/art17/figure1.htm "Historical, Demographic, and Economic Correlates of Land-Use Change in the Republic of Panama"], ''Ecology and Society''. Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref>

===Surfing===
<!-- Deleted image removed: [[File:Surfer at Playa Venao, Pedasí, Los Santos, Panama, Sept 2008.jpg|thumb|left|A surfer carves the face of a wave in the early morning during one of Playa Venao's smaller swells.|{{deletable image-caption|1=Wednesday, 29 July 2009}}]] -->

Pedasí's coast offers a variety of breaks for surfers.  The most popular of these is Playa Venao (also known as Playa Venado), at Pedasí's western end, which was chosen for the 2007 Central American Surf Championship.<ref>[http://www.surfline.com/surf-news/contest-news/costa-rica-wins-third-central-american-surf-champ-title_14411/ "Costa Rica Wins Third Central American Surf Champ Title"] ''Surfline''. Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>  Venao has a reliable south swell, with left and right breaks over a sandy bottom.  Nearby beaches Ciruelo, Madroño and Raya also offer good surf, with hollow tubes at all three under optimum conditions.  Other surfing beaches in Pedasí include El Toro, with a left and right point break; El Lagarto, with left and right beach breaks; and Los Destiladeros, with several breaks.<ref>Hanna, J. 2008 [http://www.panamainfo.com/en/surfing-and-windsurfing-panama "Surfing and Windsurfing in Panama"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081220182951/http://www.panamainfo.com/en/surfing-and-windsurfing-panama |date=2008-12-20 }}, ''PanamaInfo.com''. Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>

===Sport fishing===

As the continental shelf drops sharply relatively close to shore, Pedasí's waters are chock full of pelagic game fish,<ref>[http://www.panamafishing.net/The-Panama-Fishing-Catch.php Panama Fishing] Retrieved Feb 17 2009</ref> with year-round catch including [[amberjack]], Pacific [[sailfish]] (''Istiophorus platypterus''), [[cubera snapper]] (''Lutjanus cyanopterus''), [[roosterfish]] (''Nematistius pectoralis'') and [[grouper]], while ''dorado'' (''Coryphaena hippurus,'' commonly called mahi-mahi, or [[dolphinfish]]), both [[bigeye tuna]] (''Thunnus obesus'') and [[yellowfin tuna]] (''Thunnus albacares''), and [[wahoo]] (''Acanthocybium solandri'') are found seasonally, November through April.<ref>[http://www.pedasifishing.com Pedasi Fishing] Retrieved Feb 17 2009</ref> [[File:Yellowfin tuna at the Achiotines Laboratory in Pedasí, Panama.jpg|thumb|right|200px|A yellow-fin tuna at the Achiotines Laboratory.]]

The fishery rides deep ocean currents running close to shore — much of the fishing can be found within eight miles of the shoreline<ref>Castaneda, R. 2007 [http://www.casstours.com/central_america.htm "Panama is Happening!"] Cass Tours Retrieved Feb 17 2009</ref> — making the area ideal for light-tackle, deep-sea fishing enthusiasts.

The Pedasí district also houses an [[Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission]] (IATTC) (www.iattc.org) research laboratory, the [[Achotines Laboratory]], where visitors can take educational tours of the breeding and testing facilities, gaining a glimpse of the stages of yellowfin tuna reproduction.<ref>[http://www.iattc.org/AchotinesLab/AchotinesLaboratorySiteENG.htm "Achotines Laboratory"], Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission]. Retrieved Feb 12 2009.</ref>

===Wildlife Refuges===

====Isla Iguana Wildlife Refuge====
[[Isla Iguana Wildlife Refuge|Isla Iguana]] is an island wildlife refuge five kilometers, or a 20-minute boat ride, from Playa El Arenal.  The 52-hectare island is surrounded by an extensive coral reef, which gives it two powdery-soft white sand beaches: Playa El Cirial (252 meters long) and Playita del Faro (37 meters long).

It is also home to more than 62 bird species, and the largest nesting site in Panama for the [[magnificent frigatebird]] (''Fregata magnificens''), with a population of more than 5000.  The island also houses several reptile species, including the [[black iguana]] (''Ctenosaura similis'') for which the island is named.

The refuge also comprises a 40-hectare marine area — including migrating [[humpback whale]]s (''Megaptera novaeangliae'') between June and October — and one of the largest well-preserved coral reefs in Panama, ideal for snorkeling and scuba diving.  Visitors to the reefs can expect to see a variety of fish, rays, eels, and sea turtles.

Entrance to the refuge is $4 for Panamanian nationals, $10 for foreigners, and $2 for seniors and students.  Children under 12 enter free of charge.<ref>[http://www.islaiguana.com/english/ ''Fundación Isla Iguana'']{{Dead link|date=September 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}</ref>

====Pablo Arturio Barrios Wildlife Refuge====

The Pablo Arturio Barrios Wildlife Refuge extends 22 kilometers along Pedasí's coast from Punta Mala to the Purio River mouth.  With a total area of 30 hectares, this marine ecosystem dunes, mangroves, dry tropical forest, estuaries, and the beaches of El Rincón, Punta El Tigre, Mariabé, El Arenal, El Toro, La Garita and El Lagarto<ref>Climaco, I.E. 2006. [http://www.anam.gob.pa/areas%20protegidas/parque_pablo_artuto_barrios.htm "National Parks of Panama: Pablo Arturio Barrios Wildlife Refuge"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080804073603/http://www.anam.gob.pa/areas%20protegidas/parque_pablo_artuto_barrios.htm |date=2008-08-04 }}, National Environmental Authority. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>  The marine area facing Isla Iguana is also protected, with only [[artisanal fishing]] allowed in its waters.

The refuge is considered an important bird habitat,<ref>Wright, S.J. and Samaniego, M.J. 2008. [http://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol13/iss2/art17/figure1.htm "Historical, Demographic, and Economic Correlates of Land-Use Change in the Republic of Panama"], ''Ecology and Society''. Retrieved Feb 16 2009.</ref> home to [[egret]]s, [[heron]]s and [[cormorant]]s among others.<ref>Angehr, G. 2005. [http://www.birdlife.org/action/science/species/waterbirds/waterbirds_pdf/waterbirds_report_panama_2006.pdf Final Report: Waterbirds in Panama], ''BirdLife International''. Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>

====Isla Cañas Wildlife Refugee====

[[Isla Cañas]] is on the border of the Pedasí district, in the Tonosí district. This 832-hectare island is Panama's most important turtle-nesting site. Its 13-kilometer beach can receive hundreds of female turtles in a single night during nesting season. Five of Panama's marine turtles nest on Isla Cañas: [[olive ridley]], [[green turtle]], [[hawksbill turtle]], [[loggerhead sea turtle]] and the [[leatherback turtle]]. All of these species, with the exception of the olive ridley, are endangered or critically endangered. The island's 800 or so inhabitants have an accord with the Panamanian government to protect the nesting sites, while also harvesting the turtle eggs in an ostensibly sustainable manner.
[[File:Baby turtles neonatos isla cañas panama.jpg|thumb|Baby turtles from the nursery being released in Isla Cañas.]]
Tourism plays a significant role in the conservation of sea turtles in Isla Cañas. It is possible to see the turtles nesting from July through December. This is an evening tour<ref>{{Cite web |title=Turtle Night Tour in Isla Cañas - Libelulas Pedasi |url=https://libelulaspedasi.com/product/turtle-night-tour-in-isla-canas/ |access-date=2025-09-11 |language=en-US}}</ref> and there are more chances of seeing females laying eggs when the tide is high late afternoon since they come to shore earlier. These turtles lay 600,000 eggs during the season. Half of the eggs are kept in a turtle nursery and the other half are left out in the sand. Park rangers from Panama's Ministry of Environment and police patrol the beaches at night to protect the turtles laying and in the morning while the baby turtles are born. Turtle hatchling tours<ref>{{Cite web |title=Release of Newborn Turtles on Cañas Island - Libelulas Pedasi |url=https://libelulaspedasi.com/product/newborn-turtles-canas-island/ |access-date=2025-09-11 |language=en-US}}</ref> are done at sunrise this protects the baby turtles from predators. It is possible to see them from September through December or early January.

The island is fringed with [[mangrove]]s, and has several stands of red, white and black mangrove species, which play an important role in the life cycles of shrimp and commercial fish species.<ref>Climaco, I.E. 2006. [http://www.anam.gob.pa/areas%20protegidas/parque_isla_cana.htm "National Parks of Panama: Isla Cañas Wildlife Refuge"], National Environmental Authority. (Spanish) Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>

==Culture==
Pedasí's townspeople are laid-back, friendly, and regularly greet visitors with a smile.  Men can often be seen wearing ''cuttarras'' (traditional sandals) and the region's folkloric black and white hats; women, during festivals and carnivals, don ''[[pollera]]s'', traditional hand-stitched multi-layered dresses, some of which can take nearly a year to complete.  During local fiestas the women adorn their ''polleras'' with ornate jewelry and gold chains.<ref>[http://www.knightsbridgeinvest.com/region-Los-Santos-real-estate.html "About Los Santos Real Estate"]</ref>

===Carnival===
Carnival  is the annual high point of local life in Pedasí, which is renowned for the most exuberant celebration after Las Tablas.  The town divides itself into two factions, ''Calle Abajo'' (lower street) and ''Calle Arriba'' (upper street), with their respective queens, each trying to outdo the other with floats, music and fireworks.  At the end of the celebration, the winning queen is chosen by popular vote.<ref>[http://www.costaricapages.com/panama/blog/carnaval-in-pedasi-78 A Review of Panama Carnival] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081203133837/http://www.costaricapages.com/panama/blog/carnaval-in-pedasi-78 |date=2008-12-03 }},{{Self-published inline|date=October 2010|reason=This sources is basically a blog.}} ''Panama Travels''. Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>

Pedasí also celebrates the fiesta of ''Santa Catalina'' (St Catherine's Day) on November 25<ref>[http://201.224.42.162/buscar/festivos.php?mes=1 "November Calendar of Holidays"]{{Dead link|date=September 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}, ''Panama National Library''. Retrieved Feb 17 2009.</ref>  (''see also: [[Calendar of saints]]'').

The area's local architecture is a combination of Spanish colonial and modern day masonry block.
