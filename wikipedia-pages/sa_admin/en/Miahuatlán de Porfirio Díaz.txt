'''Miahuatlán de Porfirio Díaz''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-eastern [[Mexico]].

==Geography==
The municipality covers an area of 467.4&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> and is situated at an average elevation of 1,600 meters.

[[File:Miahuatlan 2010.jpg|thumb|left|Church in Miahuatlán de Porfirio Díaz]]
Miahuatlán is part of the [[Miahuatlán District, Oaxaca|Miahuatlán District]] in the south of the [[Sierra Sur de Oaxaca|Sierra Sur Region]].

==Demography==
As of 2005, the municipality had 6,708 households with a total population of 32,185, of whom 2,517 spoke an indigenous language.<ref name=INEC>{{cite web |title       = Miahuatlán de Porfirio Díaz |url         = http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20059a.htm |work        = Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher   = [[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |accessdate  = 12 July 2010 |url-status     = dead |archiveurl  = https://web.archive.org/web/20110718114036/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20059a.htm |archivedate = 18 July 2011 }}</ref>

==Etymology==
The name comes from the [[Nahuatl]] Miahuatlán: Miahua (ear of corn) and tlan (place or area). During the [[Aztec]] period the town was known as Miahuapan Miahuatlán, "Canal of the Corn Tassel".<ref name=miacom>{{cite web |url         = http://www.miahuatlan-oax.com.mx/historia.html |title       = Historia de Miahuatlán |work        = miahuatlan-oax.com |accessdate  = 12 July 2010 |url-status     = dead |archiveurl  = https://web.archive.org/web/20080921050149/http://www.miahuatlan-oax.com.mx/historia.html |archivedate = 21 September 2008 }}</ref>

==Education==
The city has 16 kindergartens, 12 primary schools, a technical high school, a general secondary school, and a regional university, [[Universidad de la Sierra Sur]].

==Infrastructure and media==
It has a radio station, a television station, telephone service, telegraph and a post office.<ref name=INEC/>

==History==
Before Spanish colonization, Miahuatlan was a Zapotec city-state, called ''Pelopenitza'' in Zapotec. It fought wars with Coatlan and Ozolotepec. It was a regional center of commerce, with its market offering local goods like amole, [[cochineal]], and ''[[Manta (dress)|mantas]]'' as well as salt, cotton and slaves from far away. It was apparently conquered by the [[Aztec Empire]] during the reign of [[Moctezuma II]], after which it became a provincial capital. A Mexica governor took residence and Miahuatlan paid tribute in jewels and gold dust. 

In colonial times, Miahuatlan paid tribute in honey, gold dust, ''mantas'' and turkey.<ref>{{cite book |title=Aztec imperial strategies |date=1996 |publisher=Dumbarton Oaks Research Library and Collection |location=Washington, D.C |isbn=9780884022114 |page=279}}</ref>

[[File:Francisco de P. Mendoza, Batalla de Miahuatlán, 1906..jpg|thumb|left|300px]]
The [[Battle of Miahuatlán]] took place near the town on 3 October 1866, an important military action in which the Mexican republican troops defeated a larger force of troops of the [[Second Mexican Empire]].<ref>{{cite book |page=561  |title=Wars of the Americas: a chronology of armed conflict in the New World, 1492 to the present  |author=Marley, David  |publisher=ABC-CLIO  |year=1998  |ISBN=0-87436-837-5}}</ref> The battle is celebrated in an annual holiday on the date it took place.<ref name=INEC/>

In March 1886, an area near Miahuatlán received {{convert|183|cm|0}} of snow.<ref>"[https://www.newspapers.com/image/22628964/ Untitled article]". ''The Galveston Daily News''. 24 March 1886. p. 3.</ref>
