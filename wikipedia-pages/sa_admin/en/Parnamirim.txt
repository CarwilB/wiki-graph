'''Parnamirim''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Norte]], located 17 kilometers south of the state capital, [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]]. Part of the [[Greater Natal|Natal Metropolitan Region]], it covers an area of 124 km² and had a population of {{formatnum:252716}} inhabitants according to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]], as reported by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), making it the third most populous municipality in the state, following Natal and [[Mossoró]], and the [[List of cities in Brazil by population|115th in Brazil]]. [[Conurbation|Conurbated]] with the capital,<ref name="646343,0.htm">{{cite news|url=http://www.estadao.com.br/noticias/impresso,parnamirim-emenda-com-natal-e-recebe-imigrantes-de-todo-o-rn,646343,0.htm|title=Parnamirim joins Natal and attracts immigrants from across RN|date=November 28, 2010|work=Estadão|access-date=October 27, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131205092716/http://www.estadao.com.br/noticias/impresso,parnamirim-emenda-com-natal-e-recebe-imigrantes-de-todo-o-rn,646343,0.htm|archive-date=December 5, 2013}}</ref> bordering it to the north, Parnamirim is experiencing significant economic growth, particularly in the real estate sector.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.diariodenatal.com.br/2011/01/09/cidades9_0.php|title=Parnamirim's growth raises concerns and poses challenges|author=Felipe, Maiara|date=October 9, 2011|work=Diário de Natal|access-date=October 27, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110112203650/http://www.diariodenatal.com.br/2011/01/09/cidades9_0.php|archive-date=January 12, 2011}}</ref>

Emancipated from Natal in 1958, Parnamirim is internationally recognized as the "Trampoline of Victory" due to its historical ties to [[World War II]], when it served as the site of the American airbase ''[[Parnamirim Field]]''. Its strategic global location made it a key departure point for numerous American aircraft of all types, transporting troops to the [[African front]]. The significant presence of American soldiers influenced the local population, introducing their culture, stimulating the local economy, and even participating in the social life of residents at the time.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/fotos-revelam-cotidiano-de-militares/156934|title=Photos reveal daily life of military personnel|author=Lira, Isaac|date=August 15, 2010|work=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=October 27, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170116143746/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/fotos-revelam-cotidiano-de-militares/156934|archive-date=January 16, 2017}}</ref>

Parnamirim boasts the highest [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) among municipalities in Rio Grande do Norte, with a value of 0.766. It is home to the [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]], the first rocket launch base in Brazil and [[South America]]. Tourist attractions such as the [[Cashew of Pirangi]], the beaches of Cotovelo and Pirangi do Norte, and the hosting of events and music concerts during the high season, make the city one of the primary tourist destinations in Rio Grande do Norte.

== Etymology ==
The name Parnamirim comes from the [[Tupi language|Tupi]] term “Paranã-mirim,” which means "small river," derived from ''paranã'' ("large river; sea") and ''mirĩ'' ("small").<ref>{{cite web|url=http://tupi.fflch.usp.br/node/5|title=Tupi-Portuguese Vocabulary of the Elementary Course in Old Tupi|author=Navarro, Eduardo de Almeida|access-date=June 15, 2014|archive-date=March 3, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303193419/http://tupi.fflch.usp.br/node/5|url-status=dead}}</ref> Although several rivers and streams still flow through the area corresponding to the municipality of Parnamirim, it is believed that the “Paranã-mirim” known to the Potiguar indigenous people, who inhabited the [[Captaincy of Rio Grande]] during the colonial period (17th century), referred to a watercourse that has since disappeared.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=19}}</ref>

== History ==
=== Origins ===
The first land grants in the area now comprising Parnamirim date back to the 17th century. However, until the early 20th century, much of the region remained uninhabited and covered by dense forest. The exception was the valley of the Pium River, which had been settled since the first half of the 18th century.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=27}}</ref> In 1881, the Natal-Nova Cruz Railway began operations, with its tracks passing through what is now the municipality, then part of Natal. Despite this, the area remained sparsely populated.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 28">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=28}}</ref>

In the early 20th century, Portuguese immigrant Manuel Duarte Machado, who had resided in Natal since 1903, acquired a significant portion of the land that now forms the municipality. These lands stretched from the Guarapes plantation in [[Macaíba]] to the Cajupiranga plantation, which gave rise to the current neighborhood of the same name, and included the Pitimbu plantation, intersected by the [[Pitimbu River|river of the same name]]. The Pitimbu plantation was purchased from João Duarte da Silva and his wife, Joanna Leopoldina Duarte da Silva, while the Cajupiranga plantation belonged to Francisco Pereira de Brito and Maria Honorina de Cerqueira Brito.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 28"/>

In 1927, French pilot Paul Vachet visited Natal to select a site for an [[aerodrome]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=39}}</ref> to be built by the French aviation company ''[[Compagnie Générale Aéropostale]]'' (CGA). The chosen site, approximately one thousand square meters in the Pitimbu plantation, was donated by Manuel Machado to Vachet, who transferred ownership to CGA via a deed.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 42-43">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=42–43}}</ref> Construction of the aerodrome, which coincided with the rise of [[air transport]] in Brazil, took about three months, as stipulated in the contract, beginning on July 21, 1927. The first landing occurred at 11:45 p.m. on October 14, 1927, by the ''Nungesser-et-Coli'' aircraft, which departed from [[Saint-Louis, Senegal|Saint-Louis]], in what is now [[Senegal]] (then a French colony), bound for Natal. The trip across the Atlantic, which took more than 19 hours, was captained by [[Dieudonné Costes]] and [[Joseph Le Brix]].<ref name="Peixoto, p. 42-43"/>

On September 27, 1928, the aerodrome also began receiving domestic flights, with the ''Laté-25'' aircraft landing in Natal at dusk after departing from [[São Paulo]].<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=46}}</ref> To improve access to the airfield, the Government of Rio Grande do Norte constructed a dirt road in the same year, starting at the Guarapes plantation and passing through the Natal-Nova Cruz Railway. The airfield was expanded in 1933 when Manuel Machado sold adjacent land to ''[[Air France]]'', the French state-owned company that acquired CGA.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 42-43"/> Air France operated the airfield until June 1940, when [[France]] surrendered to [[Nazi Germany]] during [[World War II]], and the aerodrome was decommissioned.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=49}}</ref>

=== World War II ===
[[File:A-Joint-US-Brazilian-Air-Base-in-South-America-142461301621.jpg|thumb|left|U.S. Army Air Forces fighter pilots disembarking at ''Parnamirim Field'' during [[World War II]]]]
In July 1941, Brazilian President [[Getúlio Vargas]] signed a mutual defense agreement allowing the [[United States]] to establish airbases in [[Northeast Brazil]], including one in Natal.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=60–61}}</ref> On March 2, 1942, Vargas enacted Decree-Law {{formatnum:4142}}, transforming the former CGA airfield into the [[Natal Air Force Base]] (BANT).<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=72}}</ref> East of BANT, the United States constructed ''Parnamirim Field'' at a cost of US$ 9.5 million.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 63-64">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=63–64}}</ref>

Approximately six thousand workers, mostly migrants from rural Rio Grande do Norte, participated in the construction of the new base.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 63-64"/> These workers rested in canvas tents set up west of the bases, forming the nucleus of a settlement that would become the modern city, as many workers established permanent residences there. The United States also contributed by building a 20-kilometer paved road connecting both BANT and ''Parnamirim Field'' to the [[Port of Natal]], replacing the old dirt road.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=65}}</ref> During the war, the Americans viewed their base as the vertex of a triangle known as the ''Trampoline of Victory'', with its other points in [[North Africa]] and [[Southern Europe]].<ref name="Peixoto, p. 71">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=71}}</ref>

In October 1946, just over a year after the end of World War II, the United States handed ''Parnamirim Field'' over to the [[Brazilian Air Force]] (FAB),<ref name="Peixoto, p. 71"/> and the Natal Air Force Base began operating a passenger terminal.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=85}}</ref> Meanwhile, the settlement south of Natal continued to grow, gaining its first health center and school in 1948. On December 23, 1948, the area was elevated to district status under State Law No. 146, authored by Deputy Antônio Soares Filho and sanctioned by Governor José Augusto Varela, with the support of Natal's mayor, Sylvio Pedroza.<ref name="Não-nomeado-xXZ4-1">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=121}}</ref> That same year, construction began on a chapel,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=125}}</ref> which was halted in 1949 due to lack of funds and resumed in 1950. The chosen patron saint was [[Our Lady of Fátima]].<ref name="Peixoto, p. 126">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=126}}</ref>

On February 9, 1949, the new district, named Parnamirim, was officially established with the opening of a judicial registry and the appointment of its first [[notary]], Otávio Gomes de Castro,<ref name="Não-nomeado-xXZ4-1"/> who also founded the local branch of the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembly of God]] on May 12, 1946.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 126"/> On November 24, 1951, BANT’s passenger station became the [[Augusto Severo International Airport]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=87}}</ref> through Federal Law {{formatnum:1473}}-A, enacted by Senate President [[Café Filho]], a native of Natal.<ref>BRASIL. [https://web.archive.org/web/20211025002610/https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/1950-1959/lei-1473-a-24-novembro-1951-361972-publicacaooriginal-1-pl.html '''Law No. 1,473-A, of November 24, 1951''']. ''Assigns a new name to the airport of the capital of Rio Grande do Norte''. [[Rio de Janeiro]], November 30, 1951.</ref> On April 1, 1952, the chapel of Our Lady of Fátima was elevated to parish status by the Bishop of Natal, Dom Marcolino Esmeraldo de Souza Dantas. The parish was inaugurated on the morning of April 26 with a mass led by BANT’s chaplain, Father João Correia de Aquino, who became the first parish priest.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 126"/>

Throughout the 1950s, the district of Parnamirim continued to develop. In 1958, [[State deputy|State Deputy]] Gastão Mariz de Faria introduced a bill in the [[Legislative Assembly of Rio Grande do Norte]] to elevate the district to municipality status. The bill was approved and became State Law {{formatnum:2325}} on December 17, 1958, signed by Governor Dinarte Mariz (Gastão’s uncle), officially creating the municipality, detached from the capital.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 135-136">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=135–136}}</ref>

=== From establishment to name change ===
The new municipality was formally established on January 10, 1959, when Deoclécio Marques de Lucena, a retired [[Brazilian Air Force]] lieutenant, was appointed as Parnamirim’s first mayor by the state governor.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=138}}</ref> The inauguration ceremony took place at the Presidente Roosevelt School and was presided over by Otávio Gomes de Castro, the local notary for a decade.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=213}}</ref> However, the new municipality did not meet the requirements of the time, as it had fewer than ten thousand inhabitants, the minimum required by the state constitution for creating new municipalities.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 135-136"/> Additionally, the local population disagreed with the municipal boundaries, as both BANT and the international airport, which had spurred the city’s growth, remained within Natal’s territory. Another territorial dispute involved the area now known as Passagem de Areia, which belonged to [[Macaíba]] and was separated from Parnamirim by the Natal-Recife Railway, as outlined in Article 2 of Law {{formatnum:2325}}:<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=137}}</ref>

{{Quote|Article 2 – The boundary line of the new municipality shall follow that of the current district, defined as follows: To the north and east, starting from the Macaíba municipality boundary, at a point 500 meters north of the Peixe Boi site, it follows this line to the intersection with the Natal-Recife Railway; from there, it follows this line to the intersection with the Parnamirim-Jiqui highway; it follows this highway in a straight line, continuing in this direction to the coastline, then along the coast until reaching the boundary of Nísia Floresta, followed by São José de Mipibu, until reaching the intersection with Macaíba, between the intersection with São José de Mipibu, to the starting point of the northern boundary.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=212}}</ref>}}

In November 1959, the first direct elections for mayor, deputy mayor, and councilors were held separately.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=145–147}}</ref> Ilson Santos de Oliveira was elected mayor, taking office on February 1, 1960, and serving until January 31, 1964. However, his running mate for deputy mayor, Raimundo Barbosa de Souza, lost to Francisco Fernandes Pimenta, a political opponent. The Municipal Council saw ten councilors elected from four different parties, half of whom were affiliated with the [[National Democratic Union (Brazil)|National Democratic Union]] (UDN), the deputy mayor’s party. Approximately {{formatnum:1200}} people voted in the election.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=148}}</ref>

[[File:Primeiro Lançamento do CLBI-01.jpg|left|thumb|[[Nike-Apache]], the first [[space rocket]] [[rocket propulsion|launched]] at the [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] (CLBI) on December 15, 1965<ref name="Peixoto, p. 83">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=83}}</ref>]]
On May 12, 1962, State Law {{formatnum:2789}}, signed by Governor [[Aluízio Alves]], partially resolved Parnamirim’s boundary disputes, except for Passagem de Areia, which remained part of Macaíba. The municipality’s area expanded to include the Taborda stream, and both the airbase and Augusto Severo Airport were transferred from Natal to Parnamirim, increasing its area to {{formatnum:92|km²}}.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=149–150}}</ref> From 1964, Parnamirim began receiving [[electricity]] from the [[Paulo Afonso Hydroelectric Complex]] in [[Bahia]], replacing the generators previously operated at BANT.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=151–152}}</ref>

On October 12, 1965, the Ministry of Aeronautics officially established the [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] (CLBI) along the RN-063 (Rota do Sol),<ref name="Peixoto, p. 83"/> which, within a decade, earned Natal the title of ''Brazil’s Space Capital''.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=84}}</ref> In 1969, the municipality adopted its first flag,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/2019-09-16-15:25:45:397.pdf|title=Law No. 159/69, of December 12, 1969|access-date=October 27, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211027141522/https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/2019-09-16-15:25:45:397.pdf|archive-date=October 27, 2021}}</ref> and on February 24, 1970, the Ministry of Aeronautics decommissioned the Natal Air Force Base, transforming it into a pilot training center.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 78">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=78}}</ref> The 1970 census recorded Parnamirim’s population at {{formatnum:14052}}, up from {{formatnum:8826}} in 1960.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 189">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=189}}</ref>

In the early 1970s, during the administration of Mayor Antenor Neves de Oliveira (February 1, 1970–January 31, 1973), the city implemented its first [[water supply]] system.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|pp=154–155}}</ref> On November 29, 1973, the FAB’s training center was redesignated as the Tactical Applications and Equipment Replenishment Center (CATRE),<ref name="Peixoto, p. 78"/> and on December 6, Parnamirim was renamed "Eduardo Gomes" in reference to the Aeronautics Minister who attended the CLBI’s inauguration. This change, supported by the military, was enacted through State Law {{formatnum:4278}}, authored by Deputy Moacyr Duarte, approved by the [[Legislative Assembly of Rio Grande do Norte]], and signed by Governor Cortez Pereira, without consulting the local population, which opposed the renaming.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=158}}</ref>

In 1974, after years of dispute, Passagem de Areia was transferred from Macaíba to Parnamirim, establishing the municipality’s current boundaries.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=156}}</ref> On June 1, 1976, the city gained a state judicial forum and held its first trial, a historic jury composed entirely of women.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 161">{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=161}}</ref> That year also saw a highly contentious election,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=162}}</ref> ultimately won by former mayor Antenor Neves, who was sworn in for a second term on February 1, 1977. His term, initially set for four years, was extended by two years due to a [[constitutional amendment]], lasting until January 31, 1983.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=165}}</ref><ref>[http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/emendas/emc_anterior1988/emc14-80.htm Constitutional Amendment No. 14, of September 9, 1980]. Amends the Title of General and Transitional Provisions, extending the terms of current Mayors, Deputy Mayors, Councilors, and Alternates until 1983, revising Article 209. [[Brasília]], September 11, 1980.</ref>

Neves was succeeded by physician Sadi Mendes Sobreira, known as "the doctor of the poor." However, during his term, he suffered a [[stroke]] and died on January 4, 1984. His deputy, Fernando Bandeira de Melo, assumed office until the term ended on December 31, 1988.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=166}}</ref> In 1987, a popular movement led by José Siqueira de Paiva, with {{formatnum:4665}} petition signatures, prompted the assembly to restore the city’s original name.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=159}}</ref> The proposal, presented by Deputy Ruy Barbosa, was approved on June 25,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=160}}</ref> but the change was only formalized on August 6, when the law was published.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 161"/>

On May 23, 1989, a municipal law created the district of Nova Parnamirim, now a neighborhood of the same name, bordering Natal and separated from the city center by the Augusto Severo Airport area, which also includes the Barreira do Inferno Launch Center.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/LEI%20(547).pdf|title=Law No. 609/1989|date=May 23, 1989|access-date=October 27, 2021|archive-date=October 28, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211028123811/https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/LEI%20(547).pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 1990, the Tactical Applications and Equipment Replenishment Center was renamed by the FAB to the Air Training Command, retaining the CATRE acronym, before reverting to the Natal Air Force Base in December 2001.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=79}}</ref> By 1991, Parnamirim became the third most populous municipality in Rio Grande do Norte, with {{formatnum:63312}} inhabitants, more than double the 1980 census figure of {{formatnum:26362}}, when it ranked tenth.<ref name="Peixoto, p. 189"/>

In 1996, the municipality established a state military police battalion,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.pm.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=8535&ACT=&PAGE=&PARM=&LBL=NOT%CDCIA|title=3rd Military Police Battalion - Parnamirim|access-date=October 27, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211027182549/http://www.pm.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=8535&ACT=&PAGE=&PARM=&LBL=NOT%CDCIA|archive-date=October 27, 2021}}</ref> and in 2000, with a population of {{formatnum:124690}}, it instituted the Parnamirim Master Plan, a foundational urban policy document.<ref name="Plano Diretor de Parnamirim">{{cite web|url=https://www.secovirn.com.br/legislacao/plano-diretor-de-parnamirim.pdf|title='''Law No. 1058/2000''': Parnamirim Master Plan|access-date=October 27, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150922005632/https://www.secovirn.com.br/legislacao/plano-diretor-de-parnamirim.pdf|archive-date=September 22, 2015}}</ref> In 2004, Nova Parnamirim was officially designated a neighborhood, incorporating Parque do Pitimbu and Parque dos Eucaliptos as housing estates.<ref name="Lei n° 1222/2004">{{cite web|url=https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/2019-10-02-10:38:16:11.pdf|title='''Law No. 1222/2004''': Amends Law No. 0803, of November 22, 1993, establishes the Nova Parnamirim neighborhood, and provides other measures|date=April 5, 2004|access-date=October 27, 2021|archive-date=October 27, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211027191108/https://parnamirim.rn.gov.br/pdf/legislacao/2019-10-02-10:38:16:11.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> Throughout the decade, Parnamirim continued its demographic expansion, extending its urban area toward neighboring municipalities [[Macaíba]] and [[São José de Mipibu]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Giesta|2016|p=79}}</ref><ref>{{Harvtxt|Giesta|2016|p=108}}</ref> reaching {{formatnum:202456}} inhabitants in 2010, a growth of over 60% from 2000, nearly five times higher than Natal’s growth of just over 12% in the same period.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Giesta|2016|p=86}}</ref>

On May 31, 2014, the [[Augusto Severo International Airport]] was decommissioned for [[civil aviation]] and [[commercial aviation]] and returned to the FAB, operating exclusively for [[military aviation]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/fab-vai-assumir-o-augusto-severo/272612|title=FAB to take over Augusto Severo|author=Lucena, Humberto|date=January 24, 2014|work=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=July 12, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221174417/http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/fab-vai-assumir-o-augusto-severo/272612|archive-date=February 21, 2014}}</ref> It was replaced by the [[Greater Natal International Airport|Governador Aluízio Alves International Airport]] in [[São Gonçalo do Amarante, Rio Grande do Norte|São Gonçalo do Amarante]].<ref>{{cite web |author=Zauli |first1=Fernanda |last2=Gibson |first2=Felipe |date=May 31, 2014 |title=Augusto Severo Airport in RN decommissioned for commercial flights |url=http://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2014/05/aeroporto-augusto-severo-no-rn-e-desativado-para-voos-comerciais.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140602213818/http://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2014/05/aeroporto-augusto-severo-no-rn-e-desativado-para-voos-comerciais.html |archive-date=June 2, 2014 |access-date=July 12, 2015}}</ref>

According to the 2017 territorial division by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), Parnamirim falls within the intermediate and immediate geographic regions of Natal.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm|title=Regional Division of Brazil|author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)|date=2017|access-date=March 29, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170919225405/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm|archive-date=September 19, 2017}}</ref> Previously, under the [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregions]] and [[Microregions of Brazil|microregions]] of Brazil framework, it was part of the Natal microregion, within the East Potiguar mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip|title=Brazilian Territorial Division 2016|author=IBGE|date=2016|access-date=March 29, 2019}}</ref> Parnamirim spans a territorial area of {{formatnum:124.006|km²}},<ref name="IBGE-Parnamirim-RN"/> accounting for 0.2348% of the state’s surface, with {{formatnum:49.4703|km²}} constituting its urban area.<ref name="Tabela 8418">{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/Tabela/8418#/k/-497573414/resultado|title=Table 8418 - Urbanized areas, vacant subdivisions, total mapped area, and subcategories|author=[[Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]] (IBGE)|year=2019|access-date=March 8, 2025}}</ref> It is part of the [[Greater Natal|Natal Metropolitan Region]] and is [[conurbation|conurbated]] with the state capital,<ref name="646343,0.htm"/> bordering it to the north. To the south, it borders [[Nísia Floresta, Rio Grande do Norte|Nísia Floresta]] and [[São José de Mipibu]], to the west with [[Macaíba]], and is bathed to the east by the [[Atlantic Ocean]],<ref name="Parnamirim">{{cite web|url=http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/idema/DOC/DOC000000000016675.PDF|title=PARNAMIRIM|date=2013|author=Instituto de Desenvolvimento Econômico e Meio Ambiente do Rio Grande do Norte (IDEMA-RN)|access-date=May 24, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170215071136/http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/idema/DOC/DOC000000000016675.PDF|archive-date=February 15, 2017}}</ref> with {{formatnum:11.9|km}} of [[coastline]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/20/aeb_2020.pdf|title=Statistical Yearbook of Brazil|author=IBGE|date=2020|access-date=September 6, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210706001835/https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/20/aeb_2020.pdf|archive-date=July 6, 2021}}</ref> comprising the beaches of Cotovelo and Pirangi do Norte.

The terrain of the municipality features [[Altitude|altitudes]] up to 100 meters and is partially situated within the Brazilian coastal plain, characterized by [[Dune|dunes]] shaped by wind action. Further inland lie the coastal tablelands, composed of [[clay]] and significantly altered by human activity. Parnamirim is located within the area of [[Rock (geology)|rocks]] from the [[Barreiras Formation|Barreiras Group]], formed during the Upper [[Tertiary period|Tertiary]] period and primarily covered by [[Conglomerate (geology)|paraconglomerates]] of [[flint]] and [[quartz]]. These rocks also form the [[Cliff|cliffs]] along the coast. The soils in Parnamirim are deep, porous, well-drained, and vary in [[Surface finish|texture]], consisting of sand (neosoils or quartz sands, covering most of the territory) or clay ([[Latosol|latosols]], specifically dystrophic red-yellow), but exhibit low [[Soil fertility|fertility]].<ref name="Parnamirim"/>

Parnamirim lies within the [[Atlantic Forest]] [[biome]], with species characterized by dense, slender [[trunk (botany)|trunks]] that are semi-evergreen, retaining [[Leaf|leaves]] for most of the year.<ref name="Parnamirim"/> Common species in the municipality include the [[Anacardium occidentale|cashew tree]], [[Curatella americana|sandpaper tree]], [[Caesalpinia echinata|Brazilwood]], [[Talisia esculenta|''pitombeira'']], and [[Lecythis pisonis|''sapucaia'']].<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|pp=26–27}}</ref> However, much of the original vegetation has been cleared due to urban expansion,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Medeiros|Petta|Duarte|2007|p=247}}</ref> through the creation of subdivisions,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Medeiros|Petta|Duarte|2007|p=252}}</ref> leaving only scattered remnants, some protected by the master plan,<ref name="Plano Diretor de Parnamirim"/> including the [[Cashew of Pirangi]], once considered the world’s largest cashew tree, and the Jiqui Lagoon. The [[fauna]] includes at least 98 species of [[Bird|birds]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|p=13}}</ref> 70 [[arthropods]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|p=25}}</ref> 17 [[mammals]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|p=17}}</ref> nine [[amphibians]],<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|p=20}}</ref> and six [[fish]].<ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2009|p=23}}</ref>

Over 80% of Parnamirim’s territory lies within the [[Drainage basin|watershed]] of the Pirangi River, with the remaining less than 20% part of the diffuse drainage eastern coastal strip.<ref name="Parnamirim"/> Two rivers flow through the municipality: the Pium River, originating at the border with [[Nísia Floresta, Rio Grande do Norte|Nísia Floresta]] and emptying into the [[Atlantic Ocean]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.piumonline.com.br/pium.html|title=Pium and Its History|author=Francisca Alves de Lima|date=2005|access-date=October 18, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100601105826/http://www.piumonline.com.br/pium.html|archive-date=June 1, 2010}}</ref> and the [[Pitimbu River]], which begins in [[Macaíba]], crosses the Natal neighborhood of Pitimbu, from which it takes its name, and flows into Parnamirim at the Jiqui Lagoon,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Duarte|Petta|Medeiros|Araújo|2004|p=2}}</ref> located in the Nova Parnamirim neighborhood and contributing to the [[water supply]] for the southern and eastern zones of Natal.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/apos-seca-lagoa-do-jiqui-transborda/250397|title=After drought, Jiqui Lagoon overflows|date=May 16, 2013|work=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=May 24, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130622051505/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/apos-seca-lagoa-do-jiqui-transborda/250397|archive-date=June 22, 2013}}</ref> The local hydrography also includes the Água Vermelha, Cajupiranga, Lamarão, Mendes, and Ponte Velha streams.<ref name="Parnamirim"/>

The [[climate]] is [[Tropical savanna climate|tropical savanna]],<ref name="Parnamirim"/> classified as [[Köppen climate classification|''As'']], with [[rain]] concentrated between March and July and an annual [[precipitation]] index exceeding {{formatnum:1500}} millimeters (mm). However, [[lightning]] occurrences are rare, with a density of only 0.219 strikes/km²/year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.inpe.br/webelat/homepage/menu/noticias/ranking.de.municipios.php|title=Municipal ranking|author=[[National Institute for Space Research]] (INPE)|publisher=Atmospheric Electricity Group (ELAT)|access-date=July 6, 2021|archive-date=April 19, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210419012828/http://inpe.br/webelat/homepage/menu/noticias/ranking.de.municipios.php|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The development of Greater Natal has drawn people to Parnamirim from other parts of Rio Grande do Norte and Brazil.<ref name="sbpcnet_crescimento">{{cite web|url=http://www.sbpcnet.org.br/livro/57ra/programas/CONF_SIMP/textos/larabarbosa.htm#_ftnref4|title=Population dynamics of the Natal Metropolitan Region|author=Barbosa, Lara de Melo|date=May 25, 2005|publisher=Sociedade Brasileira para o Progresso da Ciência|access-date=October 21, 2011}}</ref> This phenomenon can be attributed to the greater capacity of municipalities such as Parnamirim, which are located within the metropolitan region encompassing Natal, to assimilate newcomers seeking employment opportunities in the state capital.<ref name="sbpcnet_crescimento"/><ref name="estadão_imigrantes">{{cite web|url=http://www.estadao.com.br/noticias/impresso,parnamirim-emenda-com-natal-e-recebe-imigrantes-de-todo-o-rn,646343,0.htm|title=Parnamirim joins Natal and attracts immigrants from across RN|author=Estadão|date=November 28, 2010|access-date=October 21, 2011}}</ref> Many new residents settle in areas near the capital, leading to a [[conurbation]] between the two cities.<ref name="estadão_imigrantes"/>

Although [[Extremoz]], another municipality in the metropolitan region, recorded the highest percentage population growth in the state between the [[2010 Brazilian Census]] and the [[2022 Brazilian Census]] (over 150%),<ref name="valor.globo.com"/> Parnamirim saw the largest absolute growth,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/parnamirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122279|title=Resident population / Absolute variation of the resident population in 2010 (Unit: persons)|author=IBGE|date=2022|access-date=September 6, 2024}}</ref> rising from {{formatnum:202456}} inhabitants in 2010 to {{formatnum:252716}} in 2022. In both censuses, it remained the third most populous municipality in Rio Grande do Norte, surpassed only by Natal and [[Mossoró]],<ref name="valor.globo.com">{{cite web|url=https://valor.globo.com/brasil/noticia/2023/06/28/as-cidades-mais-populosas-do-rio-grande-do-norte-segundo-censo-2022.ghtml|title=The most populous cities in Rio Grande do Norte, according to the 2022 Census|author=Bartolo, Ana Beatriz|date=June 28, 2023|access-date=September 6, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230701075505/https://valor.globo.com/brasil/noticia/2023/06/28/as-cidades-mais-populosas-do-rio-grande-do-norte-segundo-censo-2022.ghtml|archive-date=July 1, 2023}}</ref> with a geometric growth rate of 1.87% per year during this period.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/parnamirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122278|title=Resident population / Geometric growth rate (Unit: %)|author=IBGE|date=2022|access-date=September 6, 2024}}</ref> The [[population density]] was {{formatnum:2037.93|inhabitants/km²}},<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/parnamirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122231|title=Territory / Population density (Unit: inhabitants per square kilometer)|author=IBGE|date=2022|access-date=September 6, 2023}}</ref> and the [[sex ratio]] was 89.26 ({{formatnum:8926}} men per {{formatnum:10000}} women),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/parnamirim/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122248|title=Resident population / Sex / Sex ratio|author=IBGE|date=2022|access-date=September 6, 2024}}</ref> with 52.84% female and 47.16% male.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9514#/n6/2403251/v/all/p/all/c2/all/c287/100362/c286/113635/d/v1000093%202/l/t+v,p+c2+c287,c286/resultado|title=Table 9514 - Resident population by sex, age, and age declaration method|author=IBGE|access-date=}}</ref> According to the same census, 46.76% of the population identified as [[White Brazilians|White]], 45.18% as [[Pardo|''Pardos'']], and 7.77% as [[Black Brazilians|Blacks]], with small minorities of [[Asian Brazilians|Asians]] (0.15%) and [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|Indigenous]] (0.14%).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9605#/n6/2403251/v/all/p/last%201/c86/all/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t/resultado|title=Table 9605 - In the 2010 census, the last to publish data on [[religion]], 62.99% of residents were [[Roman Catholic Church|Roman Catholics]], 23.73% [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]], and 2.13% [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]]. Another 8.51% reported [[Irreligion|no religion]], with other denominations accounting for 2.64%.<ref name="sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/2094">{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/2094#/n6/2403251/v/all/p/last%201/c86/0/c133/all/d/v93%200,v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t+c133/resultado|title=Table 2094 - Resident population by color or race and religion|date=2000|publisher=IBGE|access-date=July 6, 2021}}</ref> Within the [[Roman Catholic Church]], Parnamirim belongs to the [[Archdiocese of Natal]] and has [[Our Lady of Fátima]] as its patroness.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://wsantacruz.com.br/2011/05/08/paroquia-de-parnamirim-vive-a-festa-da-padroeira/|title=Parnamirim parish celebrates its patroness festival|author=Wallace|date=May 8, 2011|access-date=October 19, 2011|archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/62YXyGNag|archive-date=October 19, 2011}}</ref> Various Protestant or Reformed denominations are also present, with the [[Assembleia de Deus|Assembly of God]], [[Baptist Church]], and [[Igreja Universal do Reino de Deus|Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]] being the main ones.<ref name="sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/2094"/>

Parnamirim holds the highest Human Development Index (HDI-M) in Rio Grande do Norte. In 2010, according to the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP), its HDI was 0.766, ranking 274th in Brazil. Specifically, the longevity index was 0.825, the income index was 0.750, and the education index was 0.726.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/> In the same year, 88.2% of the population lived above the poverty line, 8.2% between the indigence and poverty lines, and 3.7% below the indigence line, with a [[Gini coefficient]], measuring [[economic inequality]], of 0.55. The wealthiest 20% contributed 59.7% of municipal income, while the poorest 20% contributed only 3%. Between 2000 and 2010, the percentage of the population with a per capita household income below 140 [[Brazilian real|reais]] fell from 27% to 11.8%, a 56.2% reduction.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/9/erradicacao-da-pobreza/BRA002024097/parnamirim---rn|title=SDG 01 Poverty eradication|access-date=July 6, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/20/reducao-de-desigualdades/BRA002024097/parnamirim---rn|title=SDG 10 Inequality reduction|access-date=July 6, 2021}}</ref>

== Politics and subdivisions ==
As a municipality, Parnamirim has two branches of government: the [[executive branch]], represented by the [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]] and their cabinet of secretaries,<ref name="Lei-orgânica"/> and the [[legislative branch]], represented by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]],<ref name="Lei-orgânica"/> composed of 18 councilors.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.camaradeparnamirim.com.br/noticias/post/tribunal-regional-eleitoral-diploma-18-vereadores-em-parnamirim|title=Regional Electoral Court certifies 18 councilors in == Economy ==
[[File:Av.Brigadeiro Everaldo Breves.jpg|thumb|left|Shops on Brigadeiro Everaldo Breves Avenue, in downtown Parnamirim]]

Parnamirim’s [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) is the third largest in the state, surpassed only by [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]] and [[Mossoró]], and the second largest in its microregion,<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> surpassed only by Natal. According to 2011 [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] data, the municipality’s GDP was R$ {{formatnum:2709922}} thousand,<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> of which {{formatnum:429357}} thousand came from net product taxes minus subsidies.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> The GDP per capita was R$ {{formatnum:13001.84}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/>

Agriculture contributes the least to Parnamirim’s economy, with {{formatnum:20982}} thousand [[Brazilian real|reais]] of the city’s GDP from the gross value added of agriculture. According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] in 2009, the municipality had a herd of {{formatnum:7580}} [[cattle]], 160 [[Equidae|horses]], {{formatnum:1590}} [[pigs]], {{formatnum:680}} [[goats]], 22 [[donkeys]], 55 [[mules]], {{formatnum:1510}} [[sheep]], and {{formatnum:411594}} [[poultry]], including {{formatnum:219471}} [[Chicken|hens]] and {{formatnum:192123}} [[roosters]], broilers, and chicks. In 2009, the city produced {{formatnum:2.603}} million liters of milk from {{formatnum:2410}} [[cows]]. It also produced {{formatnum:4326}} dozen chicken eggs and 56 thousand dozen quail eggs. In temporary agriculture, the main products are [[maize]] (86 tons), [[cassava]] ({{formatnum:1968}} tons), [[beans]] (146 tons), and [[sugarcane]] ({{formatnum:14520}} tons).<ref name="IBGE_CIDADES_PARNAMIRIM_RN">{{cite web|url=http://ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/perfil.php?lang=&codmun=240325|title=Rio Grande do Norte >> Parnamirim|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística|access-date=July 12, 2015}}</ref>

The industry is currently the second most significant sector for the municipality’s economy, contributing {{formatnum:310041}} reais to the GDP from the gross value added of the industrial sector.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Much of this comes from the Industrial District, located along the [[BR-101 (Brazil highway)|BR-101]] highway, which hosts various companies across different sectors, including the first [[Coca-Cola]] factory in Brazil. It is a mixed industrial district with small, medium, and large enterprises.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://adoroviagem.uol.com.br/cidade-parnamirim-rn|title=Parnamirim|publisher=Adoro Viagem|access-date=October 26, 2011|archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/62jA73D1e|archive-date=October 26, 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.apontador.com.br/em/rn_parnamirim/industrias/em_distrito-industrial|title=Industries in the Industrial District, Parnamirim, RN|access-date=October 26, 2011|archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/62jACzz9A|archive-date=October 26, 2011}}</ref>

The service sector generates {{formatnum:1081.317}} reais to the municipal GDP,<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> making it the largest contributor to the city’s economy. According to the IBGE, the city had 2,958 local units, 2,890 companies, and 55,994 workers in 2008. Of those workers, 29,678 were employed and 26,316 were salaried. Wages and other remunerations totaled {{formatnum:332401}} reais, with an average monthly wage of 2.4 [[minimum wages]].<ref name="IBGE_CIDADES_PARNAMIRIM_RN"/>

According to data from the Municipal Health Department, in 2008, Parnamirim had 345 health professionals residing in the municipality, including 174 [[Community health agent|community health agents]], ten [[social worker]]s, 101 [[nursing assistant]]s, 32 dental assistants, 24 [[nurse]]s, two general practitioners, and two [[nutritionist]]s. Among health professionals residing outside the municipality, there were 405 individuals, including four community health workers, 43 nursing assistants, 25 [[biochemist]]s, 52 [[dentist]]s, 65 nurses, thirteen [[physiotherapist]]s, thirty [[gynecologist]]s, six [[cardiologist]]s, five general practitioners, 46 pediatricians, six nutritionists, nineteen [[orthopedist]]s, nine [[psychologist]]s, eleven [[ophthalmologist]]s, and nine [[radiologist]]s, along with 36 others in various health professions.<ref name="Parnamirim"/>

=== Education ===
Parnamirim's network of educational institutions covers all levels, from [[basic education]] to [[secondary education]], in both public and private sectors, with a total of {{formatnum:49216}} enrollments (2020 school census), the majority in [[elementary education]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/parnamirim/pesquisa/13/78117?ano=2020 | title=School census - synopsis | author=IBGE | date=2020 | access-date=October 14, 2021}}</ref> Among the higher education institutions are Maurício de Nassau College (UNINASSAU); the Metropolitan College of Science and Technology (FAMEC), formerly União Americana College; UNIRB Parnamirim College, previously the Parnamirim College of Management and Business; the Federal Institute of Rio Grande do Norte (IFRN); and the Potiguar University (UnP).<ref>{{cite web | url=https://emec.mec.gov.br/ | title=National Registry of Courses and Higher Education Institutions | author=[[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|Ministry of Education]] | access-date=October 14, 2021}}</ref>

The education component of the Municipal Human Development Index (IDH-M) reached 0.726 in 2010, while the [[literacy rate]] for the population aged ten and older, as reported in the same year's census, was 92.4% (93.1% for women and 91.7% for men).<ref>{{cite web | url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/1383#/n6/2403251/v/all/p/all/c2/all/d/v1646%201/l/v,p+c2,t/resultado | title=Table 1383 - Literacy rate of people aged 10 or older by sex | date=2010 | publisher=IBGE | access-date=October 14, 2021}}</ref> Among neighborhoods, the highest literacy rates were in Cohabinal (97.3%) and Nova Parnamirim (96.3%), while the lowest were in Santa Tereza (85.4%) and Liberdade (85.1%).<ref name="SIDRA-1383-2403251">{{cite web | url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/1383#/n6/2403251/n102/2403251001,2403251002,2403251003,2403251004,2403251005,2403251006,2403251007,2403251008,2403251011,2403251012,2403251013,2403251014,2403251015,2403251016,2403251017/v/all/p/all/c2/all/d/v1646%201/l/v,p+c2,t/resultado | title=Table 1383 - Literacy rate of people aged 10 or older by sex | author=IBGE | date=2010 | access-date=October 14, 2021}}</ref> The expected number of years of schooling was 10.24 in 2010, surpassing the state average of 9.54 years.<ref name="sec-educacao"/>

Completion rates for elementary education (ages 15–17) and secondary education (ages 18–24) were 59.99% and 59.63%, respectively.<ref name="ODS-04">{{cite web | url=http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/13/educacao-de-qualidade/BRA002024097/parnamirim---rn | title=SDG 04 Quality Education | access-date=October 14, 2021 | archive-date=October 23, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211023192234/http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/13/educacao-de-qualidade/BRA002024097/parnamirim---rn | url-status=dead }}</ref> School attendance rates were 95.74% for children aged 5–6 and 89.15% for those aged 11–13 in the final years of elementary education. Among youths, 62.24% of those aged 15–17 had completed elementary education, and 52.5% of those aged 18–20 had completed secondary education. Among the population aged 25 and older, 65.19% had completed elementary education, 52.59% had completed secondary education, 9.86% were [[illiterate]], and 16.55% had completed [[higher education]].<ref name="sec-educacao"/> More recent data from 2014 indicated a dropout rate of 3.9% in elementary education and 10.7% in secondary education.<ref name="sec-educacao">{{cite web | url=http://atlasbrasil.org.br/perfil/municipio/240325#sec-educacao | title=Education | access-date=October 14, 2021}}</ref>

== Culture ==
The Parnamirim Culture Foundation oversees the municipality's cultural sector, aiming to plan and implement cultural policies through programs, projects, and activities that promote cultural development. Affiliated with the Mayor’s Office, it operates as part of the municipality’s indirect public administration with administrative and financial autonomy, supported by budgetary allocations, its own assets, revenue application, and contracts or agreements with other institutions.<ref name="Prefeitura_FundaçãoCultura">{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/orgao-cultura.php | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20111023074057/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/orgao-cultura.php | archivedate=October 23, 2011 | title=Parnamirim Culture Foundation | author=Prefeitura | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref> Municipal holidays include May 13 and December 17, commemorating the [[patron saint]] [[Our Lady of Fátima]] and the city’s political emancipation, respectively.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://docs.google.com/viewer?a=v&q=cache:ipksqtA-JXgJ:corregedoria.tjrn.jus.br/index.php%3Foption%3Dcom_docman%26task%3Ddoc_download%26gid%3D120%26Itemid%3D86+Feriados+dos+munic%C3%ADpios+do+RN&hl=pt-BR&gl=br&pid=bl&srcid=ADGEESiJeia9GVsXqSiSFDTdq0l-ea45rMXy3X3PpLMDN9NZih_Ci9405WeKPbAjoCI7nPYHvV-pFKfSz73OgrL4--Qebiarx4xEK0mWlHQiDivGTva7VKKCDqQlF-IIKb9guwIT8u3G&sig=AHIEtbSrXFjGXo07NxdBfpQUFbU1k6C5sQ | title=Municipal Holidays of RN Municipalities – Year 2010 | author=Association of the Public Ministry of the State of Rio Grande do Norte (AMPERN) | date=2010 | access-date=October 1, 2011}}</ref>

=== Arts ===
[[File:Asas da história.jpg|thumb|left|Performance of the show ''On the Wings of History'' in 2008]]

In Parnamirim’s theatrical scene, the Paulo Barbosa da Silva Municipal Cine-Theater stands out as one of the newest cultural venues in Rio Grande do Norte, inaugurated in September 2014 with a capacity for over 500 people.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.novojornal.jor.br/noticias/cultura/2637 | archiveurl=https://archive.today/20150712223536/http://www.novojornal.jor.br/noticias/cultura/2637 | archivedate=July 12, 2015 | title=Paulo Barbosa da Silva Cine-Theater to be inaugurated next week | work=Novo Jornal | date=September 20, 2014 | access-date=July 12, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.novojornal.jor.br/noticias/cultura/2819 | archiveurl=https://archive.today/20150712223344/http://www.novojornal.jor.br/noticias/cultura/2819 | archivedate=July 12, 2015 | title=Parnamirim Municipal Cine-Theater inaugurated | date=September 25, 2014 | access-date=July 12, 2015}}</ref> Municipal institutions also provide significant support. For instance, the Parnamirim Culture Foundation assists annually in May with celebrations for [[Our Lady of Fátima]]. In 2011, it organized the [[operetta]] ''Oratory of Our Lady of Fátima'' in honor of the city’s patron saint, alongside theatrical performances and religious shows.<ref name="Prefeitura_Cultura"/>

Since 2006, the foundation has annually organized the show ''On the Wings of History'', a theatrical production that recounts the city’s history for its residents. It is performed in various neighborhoods and districts in December, around the anniversary of political emancipation.<ref name="Prefeitura_Cultura">{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/cultura | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110928031529/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/cultura | archivedate=September 28, 2011 | title=Culture | author=Prefeitura | date=2009 | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref> The ''Conto e Encanto Reading Project'', launched in 2009, features children’s theater performances for children aged 2–6 in Parnamirim’s daycare centers.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/noticias?noticia=640 | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20111023073920/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/noticias?noticia=640 | archivedate=October 23, 2011 | title=Education promotes Theater Exhibition | author=Prefeitura | date=October 5, 2011 | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref>

[[Handicraft|Handicrafts]] are another vibrant expression of Parnamirim's cultural identity, with artisans creating distinctive works using regional [[raw material]]s and reflecting local culture and lifestyles. Various groups, including the Municipal Social Assistance Department (Semas), bring together artisans, providing spaces for crafting, exhibiting, and selling their products. These items are typically sold at fairs, exhibitions, or shops, such as the Paz de Deus Square Craft Fair, held annually in May.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/noticias?noticia=380 | archiveurl=http://www.webcitation.org/62dKUHTYM | archivedate=October 22, 2011 | title=Parnamirim artisans exhibit at Paz de Deus Square | author=Prefeitura | date=May 3, 2010 | access-date=October 22, 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/destaques?destaque=280 | archiveurl=http://www.webcitation.org/62dKWG1B5 | archivedate=October 22, 2011 | title=Parnamirim craftsmanship shines at Fiart | author=Prefeitura | date=January 27, 2010 | access-date=October 22, 2011}}</ref>

=== Attractions ===
[[File:Centro de cultura espacial e informações turísticas (CCEIT) 7.jpg|thumb|Space Culture and Tourist Information Center (CCEIT)]]

The [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] (CLBI), located along the RN-063, is a [[Brazilian Air Force]] (FAB) base for rocket launches, featuring a beach and an aerospace museum.<ref name="Prefeitura_Atrativos">{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/atracoes-turisticas | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20111102015943/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/atracoes-turisticas | archivedate=November 2, 2011 | title=Tourist Attractions | author=Prefeitura | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref> Across the highway, the Space Culture and Tourist Information Center (CCEIT), inaugurated in 2011, houses an extensive collection detailing the history of CLBI and replicas of rockets launched at the base.<ref name="barreira-do-inferno.jsp">{{cite web | url=http://portalturismo.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/barreira-do-inferno.jsp | title=Barreira do Inferno | access-date=October 27, 2021 | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180728051144/http://portalturismo.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/barreira-do-inferno.jsp | archivedate=July 28, 2018 | url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Aluízio Alves Park, opened on March 18, 2007, is part of a complex that includes a luminous fountain, restrooms, a skate park, playground, and an arena theater, as well as a small artificial river, a replica of the [[Pico do Cabugi]], and a life-sized statue of the [[Aluízio Alves|former Potiguar governor]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Atrativos"/> It also features a planetarium named after him.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/atracoes-turisticas-planetario | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110917191731/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/atracoes-turisticas-planetario | archivedate=September 17, 2011 | title=Tourist Attractions - Planetarium | author=Prefeitura | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref> Another attraction is the Municipal Public Market, a key hub for popular commerce in the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Atrativos"/>

The beaches of Cotovelo and Pirangi do Norte are among the main natural attractions. Cotovelo is known for its cliffs and significant scenic value. Pirangi do Norte is notable for the Pirangi Reefs and is home to the [[Cashew of Pirangi]], which spans 10,000 square meters and was recognized by the ''[[Guinness Book of World Records]]'' as the largest cashew tree in the world in 1994.<ref name="Prefeitura_Atrativos"/>

Among cultural attractions, the Rio Grande do Norte Livestock and Agricultural Machinery Exhibition, commonly known as "Festa do Boi," stands out. Held since 1955, when Parnamirim was still a district of Natal,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Peixoto|2003|p=180}}</ref> it is the state’s largest [[agribusiness]] event.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.nominuto.com/noticias/economia/festa-do-boi-e-aberta-e-rn-e-reclassificado-para-territorio-livre-de-medio-risco-de-aftosa/22553/ | archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107185234/http://www.nominuto.com/noticias/economia/festa-do-boi-e-aberta-e-rn-e-reclassificado-para-territorio-livre-de-medio-risco-de-aftosa/22553/ | archivedate=November 7, 2012 | title=Festa do Boi begins and RN is reclassified as a medium-risk territory for foot-and-mouth disease | author=Thyago Macedo | date=October 12, 2008 | publisher=No Minuto | access-date=October 22, 2011 | url-status=dead }}</ref> It features livestock exhibitions, contests, auctions, and extensive business activities, alongside a vibrant cultural program with performances by various artists.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.dnonline.com.br/app/noticia/divirta-se/2010/08/28/interna_divirtase,49844/index.shtml | archiveurl=http://www.webcitation.org/62dKgt0WM | archivedate=October 22, 2011 | title=Program of shows for Festa do Boi 2010 announced; check it out | author=Diário de Natal |  date=August 28, 2010 | access-date=October 22, 2011}}</ref>

== Sports ==
As in much of the country, the most popular sport in Parnamirim is [[soccer]]. A prominent club in the city is [[Potiguar Esporte Clube|Potiguar de Parnamirim]], founded on February 11, 1945.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-02-11 |title=Potiguar de Mossoró comemora 80 anos de história |trans-title=Potiguar de Mossoró celebrates 80 years of history |url=https://ge.globo.com/rn/futebol/times/potiguar-de-mossoro/noticia/2025/02/11/potiguar-de-mossoro-comemora-80-anos-de-historia.ghtml |access-date=2025-10-15 |website=G1}}</ref> The team plays its home matches at Estádio Tenente Luiz Gonzaga, commonly known as Gonzagão, which remains the city's main stadium.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-05-10 |title=Direção do Potiguar de Parnamirim tenta resgatar o clube em meio à crise |trans-title=Potiguar de Parnamirim's management attempts to rescue the club amid crisis |url=https://agorarn.com.br/esporte/direcao-do-potiguar-de-parnamirim-tenta-resgatar-o-clube-em-meio-a-crise/ |access-date=2025-10-15 |website=Agora RN}}</ref> Another notable team is the Parnamirim Sport Club, which was founded on July 14, 1985. There is also the Parnamirim Futebol Clube.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://futebolpotiguar.zip.net/arch2009-09-01_2009-09-30.html |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110216185111/http://futebolpotiguar.zip.net/arch2009-09-01_2009-09-30.html |archivedate=February 16, 2011 |title=Clubes da 1º Divisão RN 1919/2009 |publisher=Futebol Potiguar |date=September 21, 2009 |accessdate=October 22, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

The Municipal Secretariat of Tourism, Sports, and Leisure (SETEL) is the public body responsible for planning and overseeing sports activities, as well as the leisure and tourism sectors, in the municipality of Parnamirim.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/secretarias-det?secretaria=9 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110211100848/http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/secretarias-det?secretaria=9 |archive-date=11 February 2011 |title=Secretaria Municipal de Turismo, Esporte e Lazer - SETEL |author=Prefeitura |access-date=22 October 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In addition to the football clubs mentioned, the city also hosts teams in various other sports, including [[futsal]], [[Sport of athletics|athletics]], [[volleyball]], [[beach soccer]], [[dodgeball]], [[Cycle sport|cycling]], [[handball]], and [[BMX]]. Among the notable competitions, the Parnamirim School Games, held annually since 2004, stand out as a major event, bringing together over a thousand students from public and private schools to compete in a variety of sports.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.parnamirim.rn.gov.br/noticias?pg=130¬icia=479 |archive-url=http://www.webcitation.org/62dM2Mjx6 |archive-date=22 October 2011 |title=Alunos da Erivan França estão na final dos JEMPS |author=Prefeitura |date=25 August 2010 |access-date=22 October 2011}}</ref>
