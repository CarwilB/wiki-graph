'''El Chal''' is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Guatemala]]n [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Petén Department|Petén]], located approximately {{convert|50|km}} southeast of the departmental capital, [[Flores, El Petén|Flores]]. Its name may derive from [[Itzaʼ language|Itzaʼ]] ''chal'', "shawl", or ''aj-chal ja'', "clear water."<ref name=general/>

==Geography==
El Chal is located in the southern part of the [[Petén Basin]]. It borders the municipalities of [[Santa Ana, Petén|Santa Ana]] to the north, [[Dolores, Petén|Dolores]] to the east, [[Poptún]] to the south, [[Sayaxché]] to the west, and [[San Francisco, Petén|San Francisco]] to the northwest. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|957.63|km2}}.<ref name=general/>

The terrain of El Chal is slightly uneven, becoming more rugged as it approaches the [[Maya Mountains]] in the southeast. It has been significantly deforested: as of 2015, 59% of the land is without any forest cover and only 14% is covered by the original forest.<ref name=economics/> The southwestern tip of the municipality lies in the Machaquilá wildlife refuge.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.protectedplanet.net/30613 |title=Machaquilá |publisher=[[UNEP-WCMC]] |work=[[World Database on Protected Areas]] |date=November 2020 |accessdate=7 November 2020}}</ref>

El Chal lies in the watershed of the [[Pasión River]],<ref name=map>{{cite map |url=https://www.gifex.com/images/0X0/2011-11-23-15007/Watersheds_in_Guatemala_2001.jpg |title=Mapa de Cuencas Hidrográficas, República de Guatemala |date=August 2001 |scale=1:2,000,000 |publisher=Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, and Food |access-date=7 November 2020}}</ref> being drained by its tributaries such as the [[San Juan River (Guatemala)|San Juan]], [[Poxte River|Poxte]] and [[Machaquila River|Machaquila]]. These rivers are permanent but non-navigable and have a low level of pollution. The largest body of water in the municipality is Lake Oquevix, which is located on the border between El Chal and Santa Ana and covers about {{convert|2.75|km2}}. [[Tilapia]] is farmed in the lake.<ref name=economics/>

El Chal has a tropical climate with an annual average temperature of {{convert|26.6|C}} and a relative humidity generally ranging from 74% to 78%. The rainy season lasts from May to October and peaks in August. Annual precipitation is {{convert|1849|mm}}.<ref name=economics/>

==History==
The place name of El Chal is attested in records of the [[Spanish conquest of Petén]]. It was originally a stopping point where caravans travelling between Dolores and Flores could camp overnight. The first attempt at settling the area was made by the family of Ernesto Montejo and Felicita Cuoch in 1935, although they eventually returned to Santo Toribio near Dolores. The first permanent settlers were the family of Sebastián Cuz and Gertrudis Pop, who arrived in 1948 with their children. They also brought with them two workers named Gregorio Torres and Rodrigo Hernández, whose own families later joined them as well. In 1950s a road linking [[Poptún]] with Flores was built through El Chal, enabling more settlers to migrate into the area.<ref name=history>{{cite report |author=Municipality of El Chal |url=https://munielchalpeten.gob.gt/web/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/HISTORIA-DE-EL-CHAL-1.pdf |title=Historia de El Chal |language=es |accessdate=7 November 2020}}</ref>

After the [[Guatemalan Civil War]], the neighbouring communities of El Chal and [[El Quetzal]] built their first water supply between 1986 and 1987. Negotiations to bring electricity to the area began in 1989, but El Chal did not achieve electrification until 2000.<ref name=history/><ref name=economics/>

On 4 February 2014, the [[Congress of the Republic of Guatemala]] decreed the creation of El Chal from territory previously part of the municipality of Dolores.<ref name=decree>{{cite news |date=6 March 2014 |title=Decreto Número 5-2014 |url=https://munielchalpeten.gob.gt/web/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/DECRETO-5-2014-DE-CREACI%C3%93N-DEL-MUNICIPIO-DE-EL-CHAL-PET%C3%89N..pdf |language=es |newspaper=[[Diario de Centro América]] |access-date=7 November 2020}}</ref> The decree was gazetted in the ''[[Diario de Centro América]]'' on 6 March 2014.<ref name=economics/>

==Demographics==
In the 2018 Guatemalan Census, which had an estimated underenumeration rate of 9.0%,<ref>{{cite web |last=Brinkhoff |first=Thomas |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/cities/ |title=Guatemala |work=City Population |date=23 January 2020 |accessdate=7 November 2020}}</ref> El Chal recorded a total population of 13,819 inhabitants.<ref name=census/> The [[National Institute of Statistics (Guatemala)|National Institute of Statistics]] subsequently estimated the population of El Chal in 2018 as 15,095.<ref name=population/>

The largest inhabited locality in the municipality is the municipal seat, also called El Chal, with an estimated population of 2646 in 2015.<ref name=economics/>

Spanish is the first language of 89% of the population. The remainder speak [[Qʼeqchiʼ language|Qʼeqchiʼ]] but are usually bilingual in Spanish.<ref name=economics/>

Religion in El Chal is split almost evenly between Roman Catholicism (46%) and Protestantism (43%).<ref name=economics/>

==Culture==
[[File:El Chal 5.jpg|thumb|The fallen stela (Stela 5) and its associated altar at the archaeological site of [[El Chal]].]] 
The pre-Columbian archaeological site of [[El Chal]] has been protected since 1975 by [[Instituto de Antropología e Historia|IDAEH]] and is located just south of the municipal seat.<ref name=economics/> The local [[Qʼeqchiʼ]] continue to hold religious ceremonies there.<ref name=general/>

The most important festival in El Chal is the [[Saint Joseph's Day|feast of Saint Joseph]], which has been celebrated there since 1969.<ref name=general/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
El Chal's economy relies mainly on [[slash-and-burn]] agriculture. Two crops of maize and beans are harvested per year and pumpkin seeds are also cultivated on commercial scale. Other fruits and vegetables are grown on small scale.<ref name=general/><ref name=economics/> Cattle farming has developed rapidly in recent years,<ref name=general/> such that El Chal leads all municipalities in Petén in milk production, averaging 60,000 litres daily.<ref name=economics/>

The municipal seat of El Chal lies on Central American Highway 13, the main road connection between Petén and the rest of Guatemala. Other roads in the municipality remain unpaved.<ref name=economics/>
