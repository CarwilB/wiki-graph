'''Hohenau''' is a [[List of cities and towns in Paraguay|city]] and district of the [[Itapúa Department]], [[Paraguay]], and was the first of the [[Colonias Unidas]]. It was founded as a colony in 1900 by settlers of German descent, and maintains a significant population of [[German Paraguayans]]. The city has a largely agricultural economy and is known for [[Mate (drink)|yerba mate]] production. The population is 12,809 according to the 2022 census.<ref>{{Cite web |title=City Population: Check for Humans|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/checkhuman.html?url=%2Fen%2Fparaguay%2Fadmin%2F07__itap%25C3%25BAa%2F|access-date=2026-05-07|website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref>

==Name==
The name Hohenau translates to 'high meadow' in [[German language|German]], from the [[German language|German]] ''hoch'' 'high' and ''Au(e)'' 'floodplain'.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Hebblethwaite |first=Margaret |title=Paraguay |date=July 2010 |publisher=Bradt Travel Guides |isbn=978 1 84162 315 3 |edition=1st |pages=202-203}}</ref> 

==History==
[[File:Hohenau capital de los inmigrantes sign.jpg|thumb|200x200px|A welcome sign in Hohenau with the city flag, the [[flag of Paraguay]], and the [[flag of North Rhine-Westphalia]].]]
Hohenau was founded on March 14, 1900 by a group of [[German Brazilians]] including Wilhelm "Guillermo" Closs who was born in [[Dois Irmãos]] (then Baumschneis) on 31 October 1841 and established "Serra Pelada" in [[Río Grande do Sul]] before moving to [[Paraguay]].<ref>{{cite book |title= Immigrant group settlements in Paraguay: A study in the sociology of colonization|last= Fretz|first=J. Winfield |year= 1962|publisher= Bethel College |location=North Newton, KS |page= 73}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title= Rebirth of the Paraguayan Republic: the first Colorado era, 1878-1904|last1= Warren |first1= Harris Gaylord |last2= Warren |first2= Katherine F.  |year= 1985|publisher= University of Pittsburgh Press|location= Pittsburgh|page= 266}}</ref> There he met Carlos Reverchon, Hohenau's future co-founder, and together drafted plans for a massive wave of German immigrants to settle in the region.<ref>{{cite book |title= Pioniere in Argentinien, Chile, Paraguay und Venezuela: durch Bergwelt, Urwald u. Steppe erwanderte Volkskunde d. deutschsprachigen Siedler|last= Ilg|trans-title=Pioneers in Argentina, Chile, Paraguay and Venezuela: ethnology of the German settlers by journeying through the mountainsite, jungle, and steppe |first= Karl|year=1976 |publisher= Tyrolia Verlag|location= Innsbruck, Vienna, Munich|isbn=3-7022-1233-7 |page= 196}}</ref> Additional co-founders are Ambrosio and Esteban Scholler. 

Aided by the [[Austria]]n consul to Paraguay, the group persuaded the Paraguayan government to allow for the construction of a colony. In a decree dated September 12, 1898, Paraguay gave Closs and Reverchon a share of 16 square [[League (unit)|leagues]] in the then Alto Paraná, Encarnación Department. 

On March 14, 1900, the first settlers arrived from [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnación]], and the arrival of 8 more families (a total of 55 people) in August of that year set the stage for mass migration to the colony. These early families include the Dresslers, Kuschels, Fritzes, and Jachows.  

Settlers initially faced many problems, such as disease, scarcity of resources, and a lack of communication. In 1944, Hohenau's status was elevated to an official district.

German migration to Hohenau increased following [[World War I]].<ref name=":0" /> In the 1930s and 1940s, the colony began to attract [[Polish Paraguayan|Polish]], [[Ukrainian Paraguayan|Ukrainian]], and [[Russian diaspora|Russian]] settlers, and to a lesser degree [[Belgian diaspora|Belgian]] and [[French diaspora|French]]. Later, in 1958, a large wave of [[Japanese Paraguayans]] arrived and went on to found the district of [[La Paz, Paraguay|La Paz.]]

==Geography==
[[File:Spanish and German sign in Hohenau.jpg|thumb|A bilingual German and Spanish sign on the door of a store in Hohenau instructs visitors to push.]]
The city is 220 square kilometers in area. It is bordered on the north and the east by the [[Obligado]] district, on the south by the [[Trinidad, Paraguay|Trinidad]] district, and on the west by the [[Jesús, Paraguay|Jesús]], [[La Paz, Paraguay|La Paz]], and [[San Pedro del Paraná]] districts.

The area is rich in water resources, including the [[Paraná River]] and [[Capiíbary|Capi'ivary]] stream.

===Climate===
The climate is [[sub-tropical]], ideal for agriculture, with temperatures ranging from 3-4&nbsp;°C in winter to 37-38&nbsp;°C in summer.

===Demography===
Hohenau has a total population of 7,987 inhabitants.

==Economy==

The inhabitants are mainly focused on agriculture, specifically on the cultivation of [[soy]], [[cotton]], [[maize|corn]], [[manioc]], [[yerba mate]], [[Vernicia fordii|tung]], [[sorgo]], [[bean]], [[peanut]], and [[watermelon]], and the [[stock breeding]] of cows, pigs and poultry. There are also flour mills, [[yerba mate]] mills, wineries, brick factories, bakeries, [[saw-mill]]s, carpentry shops, and [[starch]] factories.

According to the General Directorate of Statistics, Polls, and Census, the Hohenau district has the second-highest living standard in Paraguay, behind the capital, [[Asunción]].{{Citation needed|date=May 2026}}

===Tourism===
A tourist attraction is the [[Alto Paraná]] Hunting and Fishing Club, where traditional dorado fishing takes place.

Hohenau can be reached by the ''Ruta Sexta Doctor Juan León Mallorquín'', a road that crosses the urban center of the city and connects it with the cities of [[Encarnación, Paraguay|Encarnación]] and [[Ciudad del Este]]. A network of local roads connects Hohenau with other places in the surrounding area.

==Neighborhoods and institutions==
Neighborhoods in the center include San José, San Blás, Cerro Corá, CONAVI, Primavera, Obrero, and Santa Lucía. Rural neighborhoods include Hohenau 1 (Puerto Hohenau), Hohenau 2 (Centro Urbano), Hohenau 3 (Campo Ángel), Hohenau 4 (Caguarené), and Hohenau 5 (Santa María).

Hohenau has a dance school, culinary arts academy, tailoring academy, barber academy, and other institutions that teach computer science and typewriting.

Medical institutions in the city include SOS Aldea de Niños, founded August 21, 1971 on land donated by the German Association of Hohenau with 17 buildings housing 160 children; the Mother and Child Hospital; the SOS Hospital with 40 beds and 2500 monthly consultations; and the Adventist Clinic that started its medical assistance service to the district and the department in 1963.
