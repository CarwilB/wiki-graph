The name Breu Branco refers to the resin of the almécega tree (''[[Protium heptaphyllum]]'').<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.breubranco.pa.gov.br/orgaos.php?id=19 |title=Dados Gerais |publisher=Breu Branco Municipality}}</ref> The village originated in 1907 with the construction of the [[Tocantins Railroad]].<ref name="VFCO">{{cite web|url=http://vfco.brazilia.jor.br/estacoes-ferroviarias/1960-norte-EF-Tocantins/Estrada-Ferro-Tocantins.shtml |title=Ferrovias da Amazônia: Estrada de Ferro Tocantins |publisher=VFCO Brazilia}}</ref> Already in 1908, the villagers joined a movement that tried to emancipate the south-east of [[Pará]], joining it with [[Goiás]].<ref name="histórico carajas">{{cite web|url=http://www.estadodocarajas.com.br/site/?page_id=20 |title=História |publisher=Portal Estado do Carajás}}</ref> The railroad brought prosperity through the trade of cashew, but was deactivated in 1973. In 1980, the village was submerged by the [[Tucuruí Dam]] and the inhabitants were relocated to a new village Breu Branco.<ref name="Eletrobrás">{{cite book|author=Eletrobrás |title=Hidreletricidade e desenvolvimento: Os impactos positivos de Belo Monte para as comunidades de seu entorno | publisher=Famma Gráfica & Editora |date=2011}}</ref> In 1991, it became a municipality.<ref name="GovPA">{{cite book|author=Government of Pará; SUDAM |title=Breu Branco |place=Belém |publisher=SEPLAN |date=1993}}</ref>

The village Moru ({{Coord|3|33|40|S|49|36|30|W|display=inline}}) on the [[Tocantins River|Tocantins]] was founded in 1793, when the [[Kingdom of Portugal|Portuguese]] transferred a group of [[Aruã people|Aruã]] there from the island [[Marajó]].<ref name="annaes">{{cite book |author-last=Almeida Pinto |author-first=A.R. |date=1906 |title=Annaes da Bibliotheca e Archivo Publico do Pará |url=https://original-ufdc.uflib.ufl.edu/AA00013075/00005/192x |volume = 5 |chapter=O bispado do Pará|location=Belém |publisher= |page=188 |isbn= |access-date=}}</ref><ref name="handbook">{{cite book |author-last=Nimuendajú |author-first=C. |chapter=The Turiwara and Aruã |chapter-url=https://etnolinguistica.wdfiles.com/local--files/hsai:vol3p193-198/vol3p193-198_turiwara.pdf |editor-last=Steward |editor-first=J.H. |date=1948 |title=Handbook of South American Indians |volume=3: The Tropical Forest Tribes |location=Washington, D.C. |publisher=Smithsonian Institution |pages=195–198 |isbn= |access-date=}}</ref>

== History ==
The emergence of the current municipality of Breu Branco is related to the construction of the Tucuruí hydroelectric plant. Existing as a village since the 1900s, it gained its current configuration in 1980, when the inhabitants of the old village were relocated. The settlement of "Breu Velho" (as the old village of Breu Branco is popularly called) was submerged by the hydroelectric lake.

Breu Velho was located between the old village of Jatobal (also submerged by the lake) and the city of Tucuruí. It was a village with approximately 400 houses built on sandy land and in the stilt style. Its residents mainly traded Brazil nuts, which supplied both the domestic and foreign markets. The production was transported mainly by the Tocantins Railroad and soon after by the Tocantins River. <ref>{{cite book|author=Eletrobrás |title=Hidreletricidade e desenvolvimento: Os impactos positivos de Belo Monte para as comunidades de seu entorno |edition=17 - Revista Sistema Eletrobras |editor1=Famma Gráfica & Editora |date=2011}}</ref>
