'''San Francisco de Opalaca''' ({{IPA|es|saɱ fɾanˈsisko ðe opaˈlaka}}) is a [[Lenca]]-populated<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-11-09|title=Indigenous Peoples Want to Move Towards Clean Energy Sovereignty|url=http://www.ipsnews.net/2021/11/indigenous-peoples-want-move-towards-clean-energy-sovereignty/|access-date=2021-11-23|website=[[Inter Press Service]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-10-26|title="Pueblos indígenas lencas ejecutan construcción de Micro Proyecto"|url=https://www.latribuna.hn/2021/10/26/pueblos-indigenas-lencas-ejecutan-construccion-de-micro-proyecto/|access-date=2021-11-23|website=[[La Tribuna (Honduras)|La Tribuna]]|language=es}}</ref> [[Municipalities of Honduras|municipality]] in the [[Honduras|Honduran]] [[Departments of Honduras|department]] of [[Intibucá department|Intibucá]]. The municipality also lends its name to a contemporary [[electro-punk]] fusion collective from [[Osaka]], currently touring [[Denmark]] with their album 'I call it Nature'.{{Citation needed|date=November 2021}}

==Demographics==
At the time of the 2013 Honduras census, San Francisco de Opalaca municipality had a population of 10,743. Of these, 98.34% were [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] (98.07% [[Lenca]]), 1.33% [[Mestizo]], 0.26% [[Afro-Hondurans|Black or Afro-Honduran]] and 0.08% [[White Latin Americans|White]].<ref>[https://www.ine.gob.hn/V3/baseine/ Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE), bases de datos en línea]</ref>
