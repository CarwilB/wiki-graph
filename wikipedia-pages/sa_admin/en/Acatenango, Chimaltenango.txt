'''Acatenango''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Chimaltenango Department|Chimaltenango department]] of [[Guatemala]]. It is in this municipality that the [[Acatenango|Acatenango volcano]] is located. The town is in the valley of the Cocoyá River.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Rio Cocoyá |url=http://www.geonames.org/maps/google_14.55_-90.917.html}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Yoc Pérez |first=Deysi Amarilis |date=May 2011 |title=Realizado en la Unidad de Planificación Geográfica y Gestión de Riesgos Upggr-Maga y en la Microcuenca del Río Cocoyá Acatenango, Chimaltenango, Guatemala |trans-title=Geographical Planning and Risk Management Analysis of the Upggr-Maga in the watershed of the Cocoyá River, Acatenango, Chimaltenango, Guatemala |series=Thesis |publisher=Universidad de San Carlos de Guatemala |url=http://www.repositorio.usac.edu.gt/6914/1/DEYSI%20AMARILIS%20YOC%20P%C3%89REZ.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180623144912/http://www.repositorio.usac.edu.gt/6914/1/DEYSI%20AMARILIS%20YOC%20P%C3%89REZ.pdf |archive-date=23 June 2018 |url-status=live |df=dmy}}</ref>

The [[Roman Catholic Church|Catholic faith]] of the town was in charge of the [[Franciscans]], who had convents and doctrines in the area covered by the modern departaments of [[Sacatepéquez]], [[Chimaltenango Department|Chimaltenango]], [[Sololá Department|Sololá]], [[Quetzaltenango Department|Quetzaltenango]], [[Totonicapán Department|Totonicapán]], [[Suchitepéquez Department|Suchitepéquez]] and [[Escuintla Department|Escuintla]].  The "Provincia del Santísimo Nombre de Jesús" (English:"Province of the most Holy Name of Jesus"), as the Franciscan area was then called, reached up to 24 convents.<ref name=anoveros891>{{harvnb|García Añoveros|1989|p=891}}</ref>  By 1700, Panajachel had a convent with three priests, in charge of ca. 1800 people, four doctrines and twelve [[cofradía]]s.<ref name=anoveros891/>

Given that Acatenango had a convent, there was daily Mass attended by cofradías leaders and their wives, who kept lighted candles during most of the ceremony.  Also daily, the Franciscans tried to have daily religious teaching for 6 year old girls and older starting at 2:00 pm and for boys of the same age starting at sunset;  the class lasted for 2 hours and consisted on memorizing the church teaching and prayers and to make some exercises with the [[catechism]] and it was run by a priest or by elder natives, called "fiscales".<ref name=anoveros896>{{harvnb|García Añoveros|1989|p=896}}</ref>  Adults attended Mass every Sunday and holiday and after mass, there were religious teachings in their own language.<ref name=anoveros896/>

[[Lent]] was a time of the year when the friars prepared the natives thoroughly, using their own language to accomplish their goals; every Friday of Lent there was a procession following the Rosary steps all the way to the Calvary temple.<ref name=anoveros897>{{harvnb|García Añoveros|1989|p=897}}</ref>

In 1754, as part of the [[Bourbon Reforms]], the Franciscans were forced to give their doctrines to the [[secular clergy]].{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=338}}

===After independence from Spain===

When the State of Guatemala was created on 11 October 1825, after independence from Spain, Acatenango was established as town, and then was raised to a municipality of the [[Chimaltenango Department|Chimaltenango District]] by a decree from 12 September 1839.<ref name=deguate>{{cite web|url=http://www.deguate.com/municipios/pages/chimaltenango/yepocapa/historia.php#.ViFFSk2FOJB|title=Historia de San Pedro Yepocapa|access-date=16 October 2015|location=Guatemala|website=De Guate|language=es}}</ref>

On 15 October 1974, tremors woke up people in the settlements surrounding the [[Volcán de Fuego]] at 2:00 AM, followed by rumbling and cinders and ashes falling from the sky, which submerged the town into darkness for a few days. President [[Kjell Eugenio Laugerud García]] gave order to evacuate people, most of whom were sorry about losing all of their belongings after the eruption; in the meantime, ash was travelling as far away s the Mexican states of [[Chiapas]] and [[Oaxaca]], to the point that Mexican authorities were on alert due to the amounts of it that they were getting.<ref name=prensa>{{cite web |title= Volcán de Fuego causa serios daños en 1974 |date=15 October 2015 |language=es |publisher=Prensa Libre |url=https://www.prensalibre.com/hemeroteca/volcan-de-fuego-causa-serios-daos-en-1974/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151015203134/http://www.prensalibre.com/hemeroteca/volcan-de-fuego-causa-daos-en-1974 |archive-date=15 October 2015 }}</ref>

San Pedro Yepocapa was one of the most impacted towns, ending under more than 1m of ash after the four days that the eruption lasted; approximately 1100 people from Morelia hacienda and annexes had to be evacuated to temporary shelters in neighboring [[Santa Lucía Cotzumalguapa]], [[Escuintla Department|Escuintla]] or [[Patulul]], [[Suchitepéquez Department|Suchitepéquez]].<ref name=prensa/>

[[Democracia Cristiana Guatemalteca]] —DCG— directors - then the main opposition party in the country after losing the presidency to Laugerud Garcia in fraudulent elections earlier that year, asked to all the mayors to help their colleagues from [[Yepocapa|San Pedro Yepocapa]] and Acatenango, which were practically destroyed by the catastrophe; those two mayor belonged to DCG.  Help began to arrive on 24 October 1974, when the eruption ceased and equipment from the Road General Direction of Guatemala arrived to the towns to clean them up, and operation that lasted three months.<ref name=prensa/>

== Acatenango volcano ==
{{Main|Acatenango}}
[[File:VolcanesdeGuatemala03.JPG|thumb|[[Acatenango|Acatenango volcano]].]]

This massive volcano complex towers more than 3,500&nbsp;metres above the [[Pacific]] coastal plain to the south and 2,000&nbsp;metres above the [[Guatemalan Highlands]] to the north. The volcano complex comprises remnants of multiple eruptive centers, which periodically have collapsed to form huge debris avalanches. The largest of these avalanches extended more than 50&nbsp;kilometres from its source and covered more than 300 square kilometres.<ref name=usgs>{{cite journal|url=http://www.geo.mtu.edu/~raman/index/Research_files/FuegoOFR.pdf|author=Vallance, J.W.|author2=Schilling, S.P.|author3=Matías, O.|author4=Rose, W.I.|author5=Howell, M.M.|title=Volcano hazards at Fuego and Acatenango, Guatemala |date=2001 |journal=USGS Open-File Report 01-431|series=Open-File Report|publisher=USGS|doi=10.3133/ofr01431|doi-access=free}}</ref> Contrary to its neighbor, Volcán de Fuego, it has been dormant for the last hundred years.
