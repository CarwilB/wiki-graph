'''Tepalcingo''' is a town in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Morelos]]. It at {{coord|18|26|N|98|18|W}}. The name [[Nahuatl]] root ''tekpa-tl'' (flint), ''tzintli'' (saves honor), ''tzinco'' (back of an individual), so in sum it means ''tekpatzinko'' "down or behind the flints".<ref name=inafed />

Tepalcingo limits to the north with [[Ciudad Ayala|Ayala]] and [[Jonacatepec]]; to the south with [[Tlaquiltenango]] and the State of [[Puebla]]; to the east with [[Axochiapan]] and Jonacatepec; to the west with Ayala and Tlaquiltenango. It is {{convert|1,160|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us}} above sea level.<ref name=inafed>{{cite web|url=http://siglo.inafed.gob.mx/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17019a.html|accessdate=Dec 21, 2018|title=Tepalcingo, Morelos|publisher=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de Mexico|language=Spanish|trans-title=Tepalcingo, Morelos}}</ref>

The city serves as the municipal seat for the surrounding municipality, with which it shares a name. It reported 27,187 inhabitants in the 2015 census.<ref>http://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/ {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220804023637/https://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/ |date=2022-08-04 }} (Dec 13, 2018)</ref>

== History ==
===Prehispanic History===
It is believed that in 1272, [[Mixtec]] women, men, and children who broke away from Iloala (present-day [[Iguala Guerrero]]) founded the town of Tepalcingo. Ruins believed to belong to the ''Pueblo Viejo'', located in the hills of Zopiloapan, west of modern Tepalcingo, have been found.

Tepalcingo was later conquered by the [[Aztecs]] in 1445, when it was subject to Huaxtepec ([[Oaxtepec]]).<ref name=inafed />

===Colonial History===
After the [[Conquest of Mexico]], Tepalcingo paid tribute directly to the Spanish crown until 1532 when it was incorporated in the lands of [[Hernán Cortés]] when it became subject to Yecapichtlan ([[Yecapixtla]]). Martín Cortés, son of the conquistador, faced an independence struggle of people who did not want to be subjects of the ''marquisate'', and from 1565 Tepalcingo was included in what is called the ''Tlalnahuac'',<ref name=inafed /> a group of fifteen towns south of Yecapixtla.<ref name=danza>{{cite web|url=http://danzadelostecuanes.blogspot.com/2017/08/el-robo-de-la-tlalnahuac.html|title=Libro: Tecuanes, tlacololeros y tlaminques
|publisher=Danza de los Tecuanes|date=August 10, 2017|accessdate=Feb 25, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Book: Tecuanes, tlacololeros and tlaminques}}</ref> Cortes gained possession of the lands in 1587, after which they passed to the Dukes of Terranova and then the Dukes of Monteleone.<ref name=inafed /> The inhabitants of the 15 villages of the Tlalnahuac were decimated by smallpox, plague, measles, and other diseases brought from Europe since they did not have defenses against so many new diseases. Thus the people of the Tlalnahuac could not organize a coordinated resistance, and for 350 years they were subjugated by the haciendas.<ref name=danza />

===Independence and 19th Century===
[[Vicente Guerrero]] abolished the marquisate in 1829; however, for many years, part of the hacienda properties would remain in the hands of descendants of Hernán and Martín Cortés. The people of Tepalcingo vainly petitioned for restitution of their lands in 1853.<ref name=inafed />

Upon independence in 1821, the modern territory of Morelos was part of the [[State of Mexico]]. In 1869 the state of Morelos was founded, and Tepalcingo, including Huautla, became a municipality on June 14, 1872. A smallpox epidemic decimated the area between 1895 and 1910; between August 1903 and January 1904, 573 of 574 children in Tepelcingo died.<ref name=inafed />

===Revolution and 20th Century===
Local residents Dionisio Gómez, Severiano Pérez, Claudio Pérez, Isabel X., Macedonio Coyote, Martín Barba, Epifanio Vázquez, Severiano Pariente, and Severiano Trejo died during the [[Mexican Revolution]].<ref name=inafed />

===21st Century===
Although the epicenter of the [[2017 Puebla earthquake|September 19, 2017 earthquake]] was in [[Axochiapan]], the municipality of Tepalcingo was among the most severely affected. Public, private, and religious buildings were seriously damaged; there was structural damage to the Sanctuary of Jesus of Nazareth, which nearly collapsed; all the other churches of the municipality were damaged as well as the municipal presidency, the market, and other buildings. Municipal President Alfredo Sánchez Vélez said that 700 homes could not be used, while others had cracks. No lives were reported lost.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/cuautla/noticias/113535-tepalcingo-y-jonacatepec-de-los-mas-devastados-por-el-temblor.html|title=Tepalcingo y Jonacatepec, de los más devastados por el temblor|publisher=La Union|date=September 22, 2017|accessdate=Feb 24, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Tepalcingo and Jonacatepec, among the most devastated by the earthquake}}</ref> The Secretary of Health reported that 486 homes were destroyed and 2,263 were damaged, more than in any other municipality.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=García-Carrera |first1=Jesús Salvador |last2=Mena-Hernández |first2=Ulises |last3=Bermúdez-Alarcón |first3=Francisco Javier |title=El terremoto 19S en Morelos: la experiencia operativa del INEEL en la evaluación del riesgo estructural |url=http://saludpublica.mx/index.php/spm/article/view/9408/11478 |journal=Salud Pública de México |accessdate=May 27, 2020 |pages=65–82 |language=es |doi=10.21149/9408 |date=1 March 2018|volume=60 |doi-access=free }}</ref>

Alfredo Sánchez Velez of [[Ecologist Green Party of Mexico|PVEM]] (Green Party) was elected Presidente Municipal (mayor) in the election of July 1, 2018.<ref>http://impepac.mx/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Candidatos-electos-2018.pdf (Dec 14, 2018)</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.milenio.com/estados/cuau-aventaja-morelos-morena-avanza-mitad-alcaldias |title=Resultados en Morelos: Elecciones 2018 |website=www.milenio.com |access-date=Dec 14, 2018}}</ref> Violence is a major concern; 400 people tried to lynch four men suspected of kidnapping a child on January 22, 2019.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.jornada.com.mx/ultimas/2019/01/22/frustran-intento-de-linchamiento-en-tepalcingo-morelos-4391.html|title=Frustran intento de linchamiento en Tepalcingo, Morelos|publisher=La Jornada|date=January 22, 2019|accessdate=Feb 25, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Lynching attempt frustrated in Tepalcingo, Morelos}}</ref>

The state of Morelos reported 209 cases and 28 deaths due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]] as of April 27, 2020; two cases were reported in Tepalcingo. Schools and many businesses were closed from mid March until June 1.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Redacción |first1=La |title=Situación actual del coronavirus Covid-19 en Morelos |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/avances/noticias/159372-situacion-actual-del-coronavirus-covid-19-en-morelos.html |website=www.launion.com.mx |accessdate=Apr 29, 2020 |language=es-es|trans-title=Present situation of coronavirus COVID-19 in Morelos}}</ref> Ninety-five cases were reported in the municipality on December 27, 2020.<ref>{{cite news |title=Llega Morelos a 1 mil 600 muertes por COVID19 |url=https://www.diariodemorelos.com/noticias/llega-morelos-1-mil-600-muertes-por-covid19 |access-date=December 28, 2020 |work=diariodemorelos.com |publisher=Diario de Morelos |date=December 27, 2020 |language=es}}</ref> Vaccinations for senior citizens (60+) are scheduled to begin on March 12, 2021.<ref>{{cite news |title=En estos 4 municipios de Morelos iniciará la vacunación COVID19 este viernes |url=https://www.diariodemorelos.com/noticias/en-estos-4-municipios-de-morelos-iniciar-la-vacunaci-n-covid19-este-viernes?fbclid=IwAR3PRRFrniEYz4uqTC8zJrI2MExnFVrt1v-VMZWxbygG7bUbm4lZ2AjTjIQ |access-date=March 11, 2021 |work=diariodemorelos.com |publisher=Diario de Morelos |date=March 11, 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

==Culture==
The oldest fair in Morelos is held in Tepelcingo<ref name=inafed /> on the third Friday of [[Lent]]. Visitors come from all over Morelos and nearby states; they enjoy [[pulque]], and traditional dances.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://blog.mapapulque.ro/event/feria-de-tepalcingo/|title=Feria de Tepalcingo 2019|publisher=MaPa Pulque|accessdate=Feb 25, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=The Tepelcingo Fair 2019}}</ref> Cattle and handicrafts, including boxes of ''Olinalá'' aromatic wood are sold. This is the fourth most important fair of Mexico and the largest popular fair in Mexico type ''Tiaquixtli''.<ref name=inafed />

There are three types of music: ''Tecuanes'', ''Tlatenquiza'', and ''Cañeros''.<ref name=inafed /> Cuisine includes green[[mole sauce|mole]] of pepita (pipián) with [[tamales]] of cenizas, red turkey mole, [[Cecina (meat)|cecina]] with cheese, cream, and green sauce with guaje; barbecue of kid and wild pigeons stewed in pipián or in green sauce.<ref name=inafed />

=== Sierra de Huautla ===
{{main|Sierra de Huautla}}
Established in 2006, the ''Sierra de Huautla Biosphere Reserve'' (REBIOSH) covers {{convert|59,031|ha|acre|abbr=off}} in the [[Balsas River|Balsas River Basin]] of the municipalities of [[Tlaquiltenango]], [[Amacuzac]], Tepalcingo, [[Jojutla]], and [[Puente de Ixtla]]. Its rough topology varies from {{convert|700|to|2,240|m|ft|sp=us}} above sea level in the Balsas Basin and constitutes a rich reservoir of endemic species to Mexico. There is a broad range of ecosystem, including low deciduous forest, gallery vegetation, and pine and oak forests. 939 species of plants, 44 species of butterflies, 71 species of mammals, 208 species of birds, 53 species of reptiles, 18 species of amphibians, and 14 species of fish have been noted.<ref name=unesco>{{cite web|url=http://www.unesco.org/new/en/natural-sciences/environment/ecological-sciences/biosphere-reserves/latin-america-and-the-caribbean/mexico/sierra-de-huautla/|title=Sierra de Huautla|publisher=UNESCO Ecological Sciences for Sustainable Development|accessdate=April 25, 2019}}</ref> Among the species of animals are [[jaguar]]s,<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.uaem.mx/comunicacion-y-medios/comunicacion-universitaria/boletines/logra-uaem-el-primer-registro-para-morelos-de-un-jaguar|title=Logra UAEM el primer registro para Morelos de un jaguar|publisher=UAEM|date=November 1, 2016|accessdate=April 25, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=UAEM achieves the first record for Morelos of a jaguar}}</ref> [[Baronia|short-horned Baronia butterfly]], [[Mexican beaded lizard|beaded lizard]], [[military macaw]], [[Rufous-backed thrush|roufus-backed robin]], [[Balsas screech owl]], [[Pileated flycatcher]], [[Cougar|mountain lion]], [[ocelot]], [[margay]], [[bobcat]], and [[jaguarundi]].<ref name=unesco />

==Tourism==
Tourism includes the Pre-Hispanic ''Pueblo Viejo'',<ref name=inafed /> ''Las Termas'' thermal water park of Atotonilco, with {{convert|29|°C|°F|abbr=on}} water and the ''Los Delfines'' water park.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.balneariosenmorelos.com.mx/index.php?q=tepalcingo|title=Balnearios en el Municipio de Tepalcingo|publisher=Balnearios Morelos|accessdate=Feb 25, 2019|language=Spanish|trans-title=Water Parks in the Municipality of Tepalcingo}}</ref> There is Ecotourism- in the ''Sierra de Huatla'', the scenic view of the ''Cerrito Tepactzin'', and the dam.<ref name=inafed /> The sanctuary of the Lord of Tepalcingo, the churches of Santa Mónica, Holy Cross, and Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe, and the parish of San Martín Obispo (feast November 11), as well as the Hacienda de San Nicolás Tolentino are also popular with tourists.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guiaturisticamexico.com/municipio.php?id_e=17&id_Municipio=00133|publisher=Guia Turistica de Mexico|title=Tepalcingo, Morelos|language=Spanish|trans-title=Tepalcingo, Morelos}}</ref>

===Shrine of the Lord of Tepalcingo===
According to legend, in the 16th century, a girl found a small [30 centimeter tall] wooden figure in the river. The figure represented Jesus Christ tied by his arms to a column. A small hermitage was built where the figure was venerated. Over the years, the sculpture was moved, first to the chapel of Santa Cruz, and then to that of San Martín. In the 18th century, a craftsman from Puebla was commissioned to make a larger sculpture of the Patron Saint, but, unlike the Lord of the Column, he was asked to change the figure to Jesus of Nazareth with a cross. The figure was brought to Tepalcingo and the men who were carrying it said, "it became heavy". A church made of yellow stone from [[Chalcatcingo]] was built in that exact place.

Agustín Pila Dreinhofer, in the book ''Arquitectura Barroca'' (UNAM, 2013), highlights the Shrine of Tepalcingo as the best example of popular baroque in New Spain. The most striking aspect of this temple is its main façade or doorway, in pink - "a true theological treatise", according to Pila Dreinhofer -, with elements, twisted columns, animals, characters and scenes from the Old and New Testaments, from the Creation of the world until the Last Supper; made in stucco, similar to a technique used in pyramids and pre-Hispanic buildings.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://confabulario.eluniversal.com.mx/historia-del-senor-de-tepalcingo-y-su-iglesia-afectada-por-el-sismo/|title=Historia del Señor de Tepalcingo y su iglesia afectada por el sismo|date=March 17, 2018|publisher=El Universal|language=Spanish|trans-title=History of the Lord of Tepalcingo and its church affected by the earthquake}}</ref>

==Communities==
Tepalcingo is divided into 20 localities; the most important are: Tepalcingo (the municipal seat), Huitchila, Ixtlilco El Chico, Ixtlilco, Ixtlilco El Grande, Los Sauces, El Tepehuaje, El Limón, Pitzotlán, El Pastor, and Zacapalco.<ref name=inafed />
