}}'''Olmué''' is a [[Chile]]an town and [[Communes of Chile|commune]] located in the [[Marga Marga Province]], [[Valparaíso Region]]. The commune spans an area of {{convert|231.8|sqkm|0|abbr=on}}.<ref name="INE"/> Olmué is located 42.8 miles northwest of [[Santiago]] and 42 kilometers east northeast of [[Valparaíso]].

The commune was created on October 14, 1893, on land that had belonged to the former [[Hacienda]] de Gulmué, owned by Mariana de Osorio, who granted part of her land to the original inhabitants who worked on the estate.

Its economy is mainly based on tourism, driven by the presence of natural landscapes, campgrounds, inns, and countryside activities. Within its territory lies [[La Campana National Park]], declared a [[World Network of Biosphere Reserves|World Biosphere Reserve]] by UNESCO.

Olmué is recognized for the [[Olmué Festival|Festival del Huaso de Olmué]], a televised music festival that promotes Chilean folk-root music and annually brings together national and international artists, along with a competition of original songs.

== Toponymy ==
The name Olmué comes from the [[Mapudungun]] words ''wülngu'' (“[[Olsynium|huilmo]]”) and ''we'' (“place, site”), meaning “place of huilmos.”<ref>{{Cite web |title=Un poco de historia |url=https://www.olmue.cl/historia.php |access-date=1 March 2026 |website=Municipality of Olmué |language=es}}</ref><ref>Wilhelm de Moesbach, E. - Botánica indígena de Chile -. Moesbach, quien propone al huilmo como origen del topónimo Olmué, usa en su gramática las palabras "huëlngu" o "wëlngu", que han sido adaptadas al alfabeto unificado como "wülngu". [Moesbach, who proposes '''''huilmo''''' as the origin of the place name ''Olmué'', uses in his grammar the words '''“huëlngu”''' or '''“wëlngu,”''' which have been adapted to the Unified Alphabet as '''“wülngu.”]'''</ref>

When adapted into Spanish, it gave rise to the name of the “Gulmué” estate, owned by Mariana de Osorio, widow of Don Alonso de Rivera y Figueroa, where the town of Olmué would later be established.

== History ==

=== Origins ===
The history of this commune dates back to around the year 1400, when troops sent by the [[Inca Empire|Inca]] [[Huayna Capac|Huayna Cápac]] arrived and established several ''[[Mitma|mitimae]]'' colonies, though they did not alter the way of life or the language of the [[Indigenous peoples in Chile|native inhabitants]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Archivo de Literatura Oral y Tradiciones Populares |title=Asuntos tïpicos de Olmué Bibliografías |url=https://www.bibliotecanacionaldigital.gob.cl/bnd/627/w3-article-346732.html |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=Biblioteca Nacional Digital |language=es}}</ref>

Years later, the Spanish conquistador [[Pedro de Valdivia]] crossed this valley leading his men toward the founding of [[Santiago]], turning its mountain ranges into the usual route between [[Valparaíso]] and Santiago—what we now know as ''La Dormida'', so called because it served as a resting place for travelers.

=== Gulmué estate ===
General Alonso de Riveros y Figueroa received, as an ''[[encomienda]]'' from the [[Monarchy of Spain|King of Spain]] through the [[Royal Governor of Chile|Governor of Chile]] [[Alonso de Sotomayor]], the lands that today correspond to the commune of Olmué, in recognition of services rendered to the Crown. On these lands, he established a country house and assigned the local [[Picunche]] people to agricultural work. After his death, his wife, Mariana de Osorio, inherited the Gulmué estate, becoming its owner.<ref name=":0" />

On May 26, 1612, Mariana de Osorio decided through a will to donate the lands to the Indigenous people who worked for her:<ref name=":0" /><blockquote>''Primeramente mando se les entregue la estancia de Gulmué, la que al presente tengo, de la cual les hago gracia i donación para mis indios, para ellos i sus mujeres, hijos i descendientes (...) Encargo la conciencia del Sr. Fiscal que lo fuere a quien mueve el mirar por el aumento i bien de estos pobres naturales i al protestar que lo fuere que no consienta que ninguno, no permitan que nadie se les entrometa en la estancia mientras no sean herederos, ni deber enajenar el ganado, ni menos la estancia para que se sustente, acudiéndoles a todos por iguales partes, fines para todos igualmente i sin su consentimiento, nadie puede sembrar ni ocupar las tierras con ganado ni sementeras para granjerías sólo para el sustento hubieren de sembrar, sea sin perjuicio de mis indios, prefiriéndoles siempre las mejores tierras (...) Les hago donación de ella, de modo que nadie pueda quitárselas ni los Ministros de su Majestad, ni enajenarlos a nadie por merced que les hago a mis herederos.''</blockquote><blockquote>“First, I order that the Gulmué estate, which I currently own, be delivered to them, which I grant and donate to my Indians, for them and their wives, children, and descendants (...) I charge the conscience of the Lord Prosecutor who may be responsible to look after the welfare and growth of these poor natives, and I declare that no one shall interfere with the estate while they are heirs to it, nor shall the livestock or the estate be alienated so that it may sustain them. They shall all benefit equally and no one may sow or occupy the lands with livestock or crops for profit—only for sustenance—without prejudice to my Indians, always granting them preference to the best lands (...) I donate it in such a way that no one may take it from them, not even the Ministers of His Majesty, nor transfer it to anyone else, as this is the grant I make to my heirs.”</blockquote>The donation of the estate was carried out almost in its entirety, except for a small portion that included a house, a mill, and an orchard intended for one of her relatives.<ref name=":1">{{Cite book |last=Venegas Espinoza |first=Fernando |title=Los herederos de Mariana Osorio. Comunidades Mestizas de Olmué : Repúblicas campesinas en los confines del Aconcagua Inferior, ss. XVII-XXI |publisher=Comunidad Agrícola-Ganadera Mariana de Osorio Olmué-Granizo |year=2009 |edition=1st}}</ref> According to historical records, Mariana de Osorio maintained a respectful relationship with the local Picunche population.<ref name=":0" /> From this transfer, a community was formed, initially composed of Indigenous people under the ''encomienda'' system, which through inheritance processes passed on to mestizo descendants. Over time, the Indigenous village disappeared, giving rise to a community that remains in existence to this day.<ref name=":1" />

=== The visit of Charles Darwin ===
On August 17, 1834, Olmué was visited by the English naturalist [[Charles Darwin]], who climbed [[Cerro La Campana]] and was left in awe upon seeing both the Andes mountain range and the Pacific Ocean from the same point.
[[File:Placa de darwin en cerro La Campana Olmue Chile.jpg|thumb|Commemorative plaque marking Charles Darwin’s ascent of Cerro La Campana]]
In his book ''[[The Voyage of the Beagle]]'', Charles Darwin describes the following:<ref>{{Cite web |title=Viaje de un naturalista alrededor del mundo |url=https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8401.html |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=[[Memoria Chilena]] |language=es}}</ref><blockquote>We climbed the immense blocks of greenstone that crown the summit of the mountain. As often happens, these crags are split and broken into considerable angular fragments. I noticed, however, a very remarkable circumstance: that the surfaces of the fractures showed every degree of freshness; one might have said that some of the blocks had been broken the day before; others, on the contrary, displayed still tender lichens, and on others very ancient mosses were growing (...) We spent the day on the summit of the mountain, and never did time seem to me so short. Chile, bounded by the Andes and the Pacific Ocean, stretched at our feet like a vast plain. The spectacle itself, along with the many reflections suggested by the view of La Campana and the parallel chains, as well as the broad valley of Quillota that cuts them at right angles.</blockquote>In commemoration of Charles Darwin’s expedition, various memorial landmarks have been installed in the commune of Olmué. In 1935, the Scientific Society of Valparaíso, together with the [[British Chileans|British colony residing in Chile]], erected a commemorative plaque at the summit of Cerro La Campana.<ref>{{Cite web |date=7 June 2019 |title=Recordando a Darwin |url=https://www.uai.cl/noticias/artesliberales/recordando-a-darwin |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=[[Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez]] |language=es}}</ref> Likewise, at one of the entrances to [[La Campana National Park]], there is a sculpture of the naturalist that recalls his passage through the area.<ref>{{Cite web |date=30 January 2020 |title=Olmué: Realizan encuesta para definir ubicación de nueva escultura de Charles Darwin |url=https://www.observador.cl/olmue-realizan-encuesta-para-definir-ubicacion-de-nueva-escultura-de-charles-darwin/ |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=[[El Observador (Chile)|El Observador]] |language=es}}</ref>

=== Foundation ===
On October 14, 1893, the President of the Republic, [[Jorge Montt|Jorge Montt Álvarez]], enacted Supreme Decree No. 199, through which the commune of Olmué was created.<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 October 1993 |title=HOMENAJE A COMUNA DE OLMUÉ, EN CONMEMORACIÓN DE SU CENTENARIO |url=https://www.bcn.cl/laborparlamentaria/participacion?idParticipacion=2070274#:~:text=El%20se%C3%B1or%20GONZALEZ%20.-.%20se%C3%B1or%20Presidente,%20Honorables,anota%20en%20su%20vida%20institucional%20la%20curiosidad |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=[[Library of the National Congress of Chile]] |language=es}}</ref> Its first mayor was Juan Crisóstomo Toledo,<ref>{{Cite web |title=OLMUÉ CELEBRÓ SUS 125 AÑOS DESTACANDO A EX ALCALDES Y VECINOS ILUSTRES |url=https://olmuenoticias.blogspot.com/2018/10/olmue-celebro-sus-125-anos-destacando.html |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=Olmué Noticias |language=es}}</ref> who held the position for nearly 27 years.

In 1927, following the restructuring of the country’s political-administrative division promoted during the government of [[Carlos Ibáñez del Campo]], Olmué was annexed to the commune of [[Limache]]. Later, thanks to efforts by local residents, town meetings, and formal petitions to the authorities, the commune regained its autonomy on January 18, 1966, when President [[Eduardo Frei Montalva]] enacted Law No. 16,422, which restored its municipality, appointing Hugo Quinteros Venegas as mayor.<ref>{{Cite web |last=[[Minstry of Interior (Chile)|Ministry of Interior]] |date=9 February 1966 |title=Ley 16422: Crea la Comuna Subdelegación de Olmué, en el Departamento de Valparaíso |url=https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=230724 |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=[[Library of the National Congress of Chile]] |language=es}}</ref>

==Demographics==
According to the 2024 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Olmué has 19,778 inhabitants (9,668 men and 10,110 women).<ref>{{Cite web |title=Resultados Censo de Población y Vivienda 2024 |url=https://ine-chile.maps.arcgis.com/apps/dashboards/f0267063ad184de1a8b5ef6e2ef3b3da |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=ine-chile.maps.arcgis.com |language=es}}</ref> Olmué accounts for 1.04% of the regional population.<ref name="INE"/>

==Administration==
[[File:Frontis Municipalidad Olmué.jpg|thumb]]
As a commune, Olmué is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a communal council, headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The current alcalde is Jorge Jil Herrera ([[Independent (politician)|Ind.]]). The communal council has the following members:<ref name="Official" />
* Patricia Arancibia Gómez ([[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]])
* Trinidad Cisternas Gutiérrez ([[Republican Party of Chile|PRCh]])
* Sebastián Guajardo Caneo ([[Independent politician|Ind.]]/[[National Renewal (Chile)|PR]])
* Diego Umaña Esteban ([[Independent politician|Ind.]]/[[People's Party (Chile)|PP]])
* Tomas Aranda Miranda ([[Christian Democratic Party (Chile)|DC]])
* Beatriz Apablaza Martínez ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]])
Olmué belongs to Electoral District No. 6 and the 7th Senatorial Constituency (Valparaíso).

It is represented in the Chamber of Deputies of the National Congress by Diego Ibáñez Cotroneo ([[Social Convergence|CS]]), Carolina Marzán Pinto ([[Party for Democracy (Chile)|PPD]]), Andrés Longton Herrera (RN), Nelson Venegas Salazar ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]]), Chiara Barchiesi Chávez (PRCh), Camila Flores Oporto (RN), Gaspar Rivas Sánchez ([[Party of the People (Chile)|PDG]]), and María Francisca Bello ([[Social Convergence|CS]]).

In the Senate, it is represented by [[Ricardo Lagos Weber]] (PPD), [[Isabel Allende (politician)|Isabel Allende Bussi]] (PS), Juan Ignacio Latorre Rivero (RD), Kenneth Pugh (RN), and Francisco Chahuán Chahuán (RN).

Olmué is represented in the Valparaíso Regional Council by the councilors of Marga Marga: María Rubilar Muñoz ([[Frente Amplio (Chilean political party)|FA]]), Emmanuel Olfos Vargas (Ind.-[[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]]), Elsa Bueno Cortés ([[Republican Party of Chile|PRCh]]), and Cristián Fuentes Duque ([[Republican Party of Chile|PRCh]]).

== Places of interest ==
[[File:Santuario del Niño Dios de Las Palmas, Olmué 20230509 08.jpg|thumb|Santuario del Niño Dios de Las Palmas, Olmué in 2023]]
Among the commune’s tourist attractions is the Chapel of La Dormida, declared a [[National monuments of Chile|National Monument]] and located in the La Dormida sector, about 17&nbsp;km from the city center.

Another important site is the Santuario del Niño Dios de Las Palmas, home to one of the commune’s most significant religious icons.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Turismo Olmué |url=https://www.olmue.cl/turismo.php |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=Municipality of Olmué |language=es}}</ref>

Also located nearby is [[La Campana National Park]], an area rich in native trees and local wildlife.

In addition, in January 2021 a route called the “Camino del Brujo” was inaugurated. It allows visitors to travel from the commune’s center to the Granizo sector, where an open-air museum highlights rural life traditions and various artistic works.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Campos |first=Javiera Saavedra |date=8 January 2021 |title=La "Ruta del Brujo": Primer circuito sustentable en Olmué |url=https://www.soychile.cl/Quillota/Sociedad/2021/01/08/688958/La-Ruta-del-Brujo-Primer-circuito-sustentable-en-Olmue.aspx |access-date=2 March 2026 |website=Soy Chile |language=es}}</ref>

== Transportation ==
[[File:Arco Bienvenida Olmué 20200101 06.jpg|thumb|Entrance to Olmué from the commune of Limache]]
The commune of Olmué is located 12&nbsp;km from [[Limache]] and 60&nbsp;km from [[Valparaíso]]. From the city of [[Santiago]], it can be reached via the La Dormida Pass (''Cuesta de La Dormida''), which connects the city with [[Tiltil]].

From the regional capital and other coastal towns (Central Coast), access is available via the Troncal Highway, which links Valparaíso with [[La Calera, Chile|La Calera]]. From there, you enter Olmué through Limache, along Eastman Avenue.

Local public transportation operates along key streets such as Eastman Avenue, Granizo Avenue, and Diego Portales Street. It also serves rural areas including Las Palmas, Quebrada de Alvarado, La Dormida, and La Vega, among others.
