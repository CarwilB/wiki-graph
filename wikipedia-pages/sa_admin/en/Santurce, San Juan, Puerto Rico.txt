'''Santurce''' ({{IPA|es-419|sanˈtuɾse}}; meaning [[Saint George]] from [[Basque language|Basque]] ''[[Santurtzi]]'') is the largest and most populated [[Barrios of San Juan, Puerto Rico|barrio]] of the [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], the capital city of [[Puerto Rico]]. With a population of 69,469 in 2020, Santurce is also one of the most [[population density|densely populated]] areas of the [[Geography of Puerto Rico|main island of Puerto Rico]] (13,257.4 persons per square mile (5,178.6/km<sup>2</sup>)) with a population larger than most municipalities of the territory.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/sanjuan.html|title=Link to Puerto Rico – San Juan|work=Proyecto Salón Hogar|language=es|access-date=November 29, 2012}}</ref>

==History==

=== 16th–18th centuries ===
[[File:Asedio Ingles.jpg|left|thumb|[[Battle of San Juan (1797)]]]]
The history of the settlement of Santurce is closely linked to that of [[Old San Juan]] due to its location as the closest entry point from the [[San Juan Islet|Islet of San Juan]] to the Puerto Rican mainland and for its location between the [[San Juan Bay]] and the Atlantic coast.<ref name="Santurce District" /> San Antonio Bridge, the first bridge connecting the islet to the main island was built across [[Condado Lagoon]] in the 1560s,<ref>{{Citation |publisher = ABC-CLIO |isbn = 1576070271 |location = Santa Barbara, California |title = Historic Cities of the Americas |author = David Marley |date = 2005 |volume=1 |chapter=Puerto Rico: San Juan |page=187 }}</ref> during a period of infrastructural and military development of San Juan that also saw the edification of its [[Walls of Old San Juan|city walls]]. [[Fortín San Antonio]] was also built during this time in order to defend the city from both northeast foreign invaders and land-based indigenous attacks.

The area that would become Santurce was first settled between the end the 16th century and throughout the 17th century by both freed and escaped slaves coming from both rural Puerto Rico and other islands throughout the [[West Indies]]. The town of '''San Mateo de Cangrejos''' ("[[Saint Matthew]]" of the Crabs) was officially founded in 1760 by [[Basque diaspora|Basque settlers]] who formally acquired the land around what are today the subbarrios of [[San Mateo (Santurce)|San Mateo]], [[Pulguero (Santurce)|Pulguero]] and [[Minillas (Santurce)|Minillas]].<ref name="UPRRP2"/> The area around what is today [[Isla Grande (Santurce)|Isla Grande]] was also developed during this time with projects such as the [[Polvorín de Miraflores|Miraflores armory]],<ref>{{cite web |author= |date= |title=Archivo Histórico y Fotográfico de Puerto Rico |url=https://www.flickr.com/photos/fredandrebecca/4839036983/in/photostream/ |accessdate=July 28, 2014 |publisher=[[Flickr]]}}</ref> established as part of military infrastructure developments by [[Alejandro O'Reilly]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Negroni |first=Héctor Andrés |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=kukMAQAAMAAJ&q=polvorin+de+miraflores+historia+militar+de+puerto+rico&pg=PA192 |title=Historia Militar de Puerto Rico |date=1992 |publisher= |isbn=8478441387 |location= |page=192 |access-date=July 28, 2014}}</ref> Santurce was captured by the British under the command of [[Ralph Abercromby]] on April 18, 1797, during the early stages of the [[Battle of San Juan (1797)|1797 siege of San Juan]], but it was later liberated on May 1.<ref name="RA">{{cite book |last1=Van Middeldyk |first1=R.A. |url=https://archive.org/details/historypuertori01middgoog |title=The History of Puerto Rico: From the Spanish Discovery to the American Occupation |date=1903 |publisher=D. Appleton and Company |editor1-last=Brumbaugh |editor1-first=Martin |pages=[https://archive.org/details/historypuertori01middgoog/page/n173 139]–141 |access-date=6 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="NPS">{{cite book |title=The Forts of Old San Juan |date=2018 |publisher=Division of Publications, National Park Service, U.S. Dept. of the Interior |isbn=9780912627625 |location=Washington, D.C. |pages=68–71}}</ref>

=== 19th century ===
[[File:Tranvía de San Juan 02.jpg|thumb|[[Rail transport in Puerto Rico#San Juan Tramway|San Juan Tramway]]|left]]
Santurce saw further urban growth during the early decades of the 19th century thanks to the establishment of the ''[[El Camino Real (Puerto Rico)|Camino Real]]'', a military road between San Juan and the town of [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]] (then called El Roble) built in 1810; this stretch of road now known as [[Avenida Juan Ponce de León|Ponce de León Avenue]] would prove to be of extreme importance in the urban history of the city of San Juan.<ref name=":02">{{Cite web |title=Historia de Puerta de Tierra (San Juan) |url=http://www.puertadetierra.info/indicehist.asp |access-date=2022-02-01 |website=www.puertadetierra.info |archive-date=June 1, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090601164325/http://www.puertadetierra.info/indicehist.asp |url-status=dead }}</ref> The town church, [[Church of San Mateo de Cangrejos of Santurce|San Mateo de Cangrejos of Santurce Parish]], would also be established during this time in 1832.<ref name="UPR">{{cite web |date=5 December 2013 |title=Parroquia San Mateo/ Santurce - Arquitectura Histórica de Puerto Rico |url=http://humanidades.uprrp.edu/ahpr/?page_id=658 |access-date=10 October 2020 |language=es}}</ref> The establishment of the telegraph network in 1858 further modernized the town,<ref name=":1">Gilberto Aponte Torres, ''San Mateo de Cangrejos: Notas para su Historia'' (1985)</ref> which by 1863 was formally annexed to the municipality of San Juan.<ref name="FPH2"/>

In 1876, an engineer from the port town of [[Santurtzi]] in Spain's autonomous [[Basque Country (autonomous community)|Basque Country]] region known as Pablo Ubarri arrived on the island to help in the construction of a railroad system and a steam tramway between San Juan and the town of Río Piedras through the center of San Mateo de Cangrejos. He was later granted the title of Count of Santurce by the Spanish Crown. With the newly acquired title and influence, the district was renamed after his title, county of Santurce (''condado de Santurce''), a decision that has caused controversy ever since. The tourist district of [[Condado (Santurce)|Condado]] (Spanish for 'county') traces its etymology to this title.<ref>Cangrejos – Santurce – Authors; Aníbal Sepúlveda, Jorge Carbonell, Centro de Investigaciones CARIMAR, Oficina Estatal de Preservación Histórica.</ref> The [[Rail transport in Puerto Rico|steamway service]] developed by the count in 1878 further helped the population of the barrio to considerably grow.<ref>Document CF408, Tranvía de la Capital a Río Piedras (Nov 16, 1898), Archivo General de Puerto Rico</ref> Other key developments during this time were the construction of a civil hospital in 1885 (today the location of the [[Museum of Art of Puerto Rico]]), the establishment of street lighting and the electric grid in 1893, the installation of phone infrastructure in 1897, and the inauguration of both railway service and the [[Carretera Central (Puerto Rico)|Carretera Central]] linking San Juan to [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]] in 1898.<ref name=":1" />

The [[Treaty of Paris (1898)|Treaty of Paris in 1898]] saw the culmination of the [[Spanish–American War|Spanish-American War]], with Puerto Rico now becoming a colony of the United States.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book |author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger |url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog |title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office |author2=Henry Gannett |author3=Walter Francis Willcox |publisher=Imprenta del gobierno |year=1900 |page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n253 162] |language=es}}</ref> In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a census of Puerto Rico, finding that the population of Santurce was 5,840.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900" />

===20th century===
[[File:Condado Beach Hotel - 1958.jpg|left|thumb|[[Condado Vanderbilt Hotel]] in 1950.]]The [[United States Army]] established [[Camp Las Casas]], in the area of [[Las Casas (Santurce)|Las Casas]] in 1904. The camp was the main training base of the ''Porto Rico Regiment of Infantry'' (on January 15, 1899, the military government changed the name of Puerto Rico to ''Porto Rico'' and on May 17, 1932, U.S. Congress changed the name back to "Puerto Rico") The ''Porto Rico Regiment of Infantry'' was a [[U.S. Army Regimental System|U.S. Army Regiment]] which was later renamed the "[[65th Infantry Regiment (United States)|65th Infantry Regiment]]". The 65th Infantry Regiment was segregated. However, a separate division called the 375th Regiment enlisted Black soldiers. The base continued in operation until 1946, when it was finally closed. [[Residencial Las Casas]] would later be developed on this location.<ref name="HMPR">"Historia Militar de Puerto Rico"; by [[Héctor Andrés Negroni]]; pg. 370; {{ISBN|84-7888-138-7}}</ref>[[File:Teatro Ambassador panorama.jpg|thumb|During its early years, the Ambassador Theatre was the place where kids went to see "Abbott and Costello" movies.]]
The district of Condado continued to rapidly grow after the construction of [[Dos Hermanos Bridge]] in 1910, connecting the district to [[San Juan Antiguo]].<ref>{{cite web |author=Carlos Toledo |date= |title=El Condado: Celebrando 100 años de distinguida historia |url=http://www.galenusrevista.com/El-Condado.html |accessdate=March 29, 2015 |publisher=Galenus}}</ref> This period of population growth brought prosperity to many of the neighborhoods of Santurce with high urbanization in [[Miramar (Santurce)|Miramar]], [[Ocean Park (Santurce)|Ocean Park]], [[Sagrado Corazón / Monteflores (Santurce)|Sagrado Corazón]] and the formal establishment of a [[La Placita de Santurce|local marketplace]], for example, also in 1910. Tourism industry in Puerto Rico is formally born during this time with the establishment of [[Condado Vanderbilt Hotel]] in 1917,<ref name="Que Pasa">Flores, Ronald. "New Hotels on the Horizon". (February/March 2009) ''¡Qué Pasa!''. [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]].</ref><ref name="Reopening1">{{cite news |author=Hansen, Kristine |date=November 13, 2012 |title=Historic Condado Vanderbilt Hotel Reopens in San Juan |url=http://www.fodors.com/news/historic-condado-vanderbilt-hotel-reopens-in-san-juan-6170.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130627194013/http://www.fodors.com/news/historic-condado-vanderbilt-hotel-reopens-in-san-juan-6170.html |archive-date=27 June 2013 |access-date=June 28, 2018 |work=Fodor's |publisher=Random House Inc}}</ref> something that would quickly prompt the opening of numerous hotels, restaurants and other businesses in the area of Condado in order to cater to the increasing number of tourists and visitors from overseas.<ref>Cangrejos - Santurce - Authors; Aníbal Sepúlveda, Jorge Carbonell, Centro de Investigaciones CARIMAR, Oficina Estatal de Preservación Histórica. (Spanish)</ref>

Between 1937 and 1948, Santurce became one of the most vibrant areas of the capital.<ref>A. Sepúlveda. Cangrejos: Historia ilustrada de su desarrollo urbano. (1987) p. 45.</ref> Numerous museums, art galleries, music venues also opened during this time. The district, notably Miramar and Sagrado Corazón, also experienced an architectural boom as vernacular ''Criollo'' style interacted with imported styles such as [[Art Deco]], [[Prairie School]] and [[Spanish Colonial Revival architecture|Spanish Revival]]. Some of these areas have been designated by the [[State Historic Preservation Office|Puerto Rico State Historic Preservation Office]] as [[Puerto Rico Register of Historic Sites and Zones|protected historic zones]] today.<ref name=":03">{{Cite web |last=GOBIERNO DE PUERTO RICO |first=JUNTA DE PLANIFICACIÓN DE PUERTO RICO |date=December 7, 2022 |title=REGISTRO DE PROPIEDADES DESIGNADAS POR LA JUNTA DE PLANIFICACIÓN DE PUERTO RICO |url=https://jp.pr.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/REGISTRO-OFICIAL-DE-SITIOS-Y-ZONAS-HISTORICAS-DESIGNADAS-Rev.-DIC-2022.pdf |website=jp.pr.gov}}</ref>[[File:EXTERIOR, GENERAL VIEW, SOUTH FRONT - William Korber House, 903 Ponce de Leon Avenue, Santurce, San Juan, San Juan Municipio, PR HABS PR,7-SAJU,37-1.tif|thumb|[[Residencia Aboy-Lompré|Korber House]], which became Sha'are Zedeck synagogue in 1954]]At its population peak in 1950, Santurce had a population of 195,007 inhabitants, making it more populated than every other municipality in the island at the time. This figure together with the annexation of Río Piedras a year after, brought the population of the municipality of San Juan to 451,658 by 1960, making it the second largest city in the Caribbean (after [[Havana]]) and the 29th largest city in the United States at the time.<ref>{{cite web |title=Population and Area (Historical Censuses) |url=http://www2.census.gov/prod2/statcomp/documents/1991-02.pdf |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080624185938/http://www2.census.gov/prod2/statcomp/documents/1991-02.pdf |archive-date=June 24, 2008 |access-date=June 20, 2008 |publisher=United States Census Bureau}}</ref> The population of the district became the most diversified at the time with large numbers of immigrant communities establishing businesses and institutions in the area. For example, the first synagogue in Puerto Rico, Sha'are Zedeck, was established here during this time in 1952 by William Korber, a wealthy Puerto Rican of [[German immigration to Puerto Rico|German]] descent.<ref name="JVL">[https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/vjw/Puerto_Rico.html The Virtual Jewish History Tour Puerto Rico], ''Jewish Virtual Library'', Retrieved November 12, 2008.</ref> Additionally, a large influx of [[History of the Jews in Cuba|Jewish Cubans]] also arrived immediately after the [[Cuban Revolution]] in 1959.<ref name="LN">{{cite web |last=Luxner |first=Larry |date=August 3, 2004 |title=Puerto Rico's Jews planting roots on an island with little Jewish history |url=http://www.luxner.com/cgi-bin/view_article.cgi?articleID=1237 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20051107203700/http://www.luxner.com/cgi-bin/view_article.cgi?articleID=1237 |archive-date=November 7, 2005 |access-date=November 12, 2008 |publisher=luxner.com}}</ref>
With the establishment of a more robust island-wide roadway infrastructure, Santurce however began to experience a rapid population decline starting in the 1960s decade as large numbers of residents began to move outward from the city and into the newly emerging suburbs of [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]], [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]], [[Guaynabo, Puerto Rico|Guaynabo]], and [[Levittown, Puerto Rico|Levittown]], for example. This period also saw a shift in business and commercialization away from Santurce, which before functioned as one of the main urban cores of the city, towards [[Hato Rey]], and its newly developed [[central business district]] that is popularly known as the [[Milla de Oro|Golden Mile]] (''la Milla de Oro''). By the end of the 20th century, Santurce had a population of only 95,000 inhabitants and, with the exceptions of districts such as Condado, Miramar and Ocean Park, was experiencing extreme [[urban decay]].<ref name="Peffer_Lonely_Planet2">{{cite book |last1=Peffer |first1=Randall |title=Lonely Planet |date=October 1, 2002 |publisher=Lonely Planet Publications |page=142}}</ref><ref name="Santurce Britannica2">{{cite web |author=Encyclopædia Britannica |title=The Contemporary City |url=http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/521311/San-Juan/252365/The-contemporary-city |access-date=July 7, 2010 |publisher=[[Encyclopædia Britannica]]}}</ref>[[File:Graffiti in Calle Cerra Santurce, Puerto Rico 04.jpg|thumb|Cerra Street (2014)]]

==Geography==
[[File:San Juan urban area from Mirador Gavillan (2025)-2.jpg|thumb|Santurce skyline from [[Mamey, Guaynabo, Puerto Rico|Mamey, Guaynabo]].]]
[[File:Couple holding hands and walking in Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|A couple walks on a street in Santurce]]
Santurce is located along the north-eastern coast of Puerto Rico. It lies south of the Atlantic Ocean, east of [[Old San Juan, Puerto Rico|Old San Juan]] and west of [[Isla Verde, Puerto Rico|Isla Verde]]. The district occupies an area of {{convert|5.24|sqmi|km2}} of land and 3.46 (8.96 km2) of water. It is surrounded by six bodies of water: [[San Juan Bay]], [[Condado Lagoon|Condado Natural Lagoon]], the Martín Peña Channel, [[Laguna San José (Puerto Rico)|San José Lagoon]], Los Corozos Lagoon, and the Atlantic Ocean with its respective beaches and estuaries.<ref name="Santurce District">{{cite web|author=Bliss, Peggy Ann|url=http://www.prdailysun.com/?page=news.article&id=1269829821|title=A walking tour of Santurce|access-date=March 29, 2010|publisher=[[Puerto Rico Daily Sun]]}}</ref>

Geographically speaking, Santurce is a [[List of peninsulas|peninsula]] connected to the Puerto Rico mainland in the east, where it borders with the [[Isla Verde, Puerto Rico|Isla Verde]] district of [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]]. It is 7.6&nbsp;km long from west to east, and up to 3.0&nbsp;km wide in the eastern part. The peninsula is bounded by the Atlantic Ocean in the north, with more than five km of beaches from the [[Condado (Santurce)|Condado]] peninsula in the west, to a point 600 m east of ''Punta Las Marías'', where it borders on the Isla Verde area, and ''[[Laguna San José (Puerto Rico)|Laguna San José]]'' and its northern embayment, ''Laguna Los Corozos'' to the east. To the south is the [[Martín Peña Channel]], which separates Santurce from the northern barrios of former municipio [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]]: [[Hato Rey Norte]], [[Hato Rey Central]], and [[Oriente, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Oriente]]. To the west is [[San Juan Bay]], where three bridges, Dos Hermanos Bridge (Ave. Ashford), G. Esteves Bridge ([[Avenida Juan Ponce de León|Ave. Ponce de León]]) and San Antonio Bridge (Ave. Fernandes Juncos) connect Santurce with ''[[Isleta de San Juan|La Isleta]]'' (small island) where [[Old San Juan, Puerto Rico|Old San Juan]] is located.
It has a total area of {{convert|8.70|sqmi|km2}} composed of {{convert|5.24|sqmi|km2}} of land and {{convert|3.46|sqmi|km2}} of water area.

The [[topography]] is mainly flat with low hills toward the central areas and swampy areas to the south along the [[Martín Peña Channel]] and to the east near the ''Laguna San José'' (San José Lagoon). The highest point is at [[Sagrado Corazón / Monteflores (Santurce)|Monteflores]] at {{convert|23|m|ft|abbr=off|sp=us}} above sea level.

Santurce is one of the top ten most-populated areas of Puerto Rico. It includes the neighborhoods of [[Miramar (Santurce)|Miramar]], [[Loíza (Santurce)|Loíza]], [[Isla Grande (Santurce)|Isla Grande]], [[Barrio Obrero (Santurce)|Barrio Obrero]], and [[Condado (Santurce)|Condado]],<ref name= "2010 Census">{{cite book|title=Puerto Rico: 2010 Population and Housing Unit Counts.pdf | url = https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/gpo35934/cph-2-53.pdf|year=2010|publisher=U.S. Dept. of Commerce, Economics and Statistics Administration, U.S. Census Bureau}}</ref> which are cultural hot spots for [[Puerto Rican art|art]], [[Music of Puerto Rico|music]], [[Puerto Rican cuisine|cuisine]], fashion, [[Tourism in Puerto Rico|hotels]], technology, multimedia, [[Cinema of Puerto Rico|film]], textile and [[Startup company|startups]].

The [[United States Census, 2010|2010 U.S. census]] recorded a total population of 81,251 people living in an area of {{convert|5.24|sqmi|km2}}. It is the most populous borough (''barrio'') in Puerto Rico and one of the most [[densely populated]] areas of San Juan, at 15,447.0 residents per square mile (6,931.2/km<sup>2</sup>).<ref name="2010 Census" />

Santurce is home to one of the largest Jewish communities in [[Puerto Rico]] and the Caribbean with over 1,500 people attending two local synagogues. Jews were officially prohibited from settling in the island through much of its history, but many managed to settle in the island as [[Crypto-Judaism|secret Jews]].<ref name="Profile"/>

Many arrived from [[History of the Jews in France|France]], the [[History of the Jews in the Netherlands|Netherlands]], [[Saint-Barthélemy]] and [[Curaçao]] after [[World War II]]. A minor portion are descendants of [[Jewish Cuban]]s who came to establishment after [[Fidel Castro]]'s [[Cuban Revolution]] of 1959. Like in many former Spanish colonies founded soon after the [[Spanish Inquisition]], there are some Puerto Ricans who are [[Crypto-Judaism|Crypto-Jews]]. Recent DNA ancestry has identified a number of [[History of the Jews in Portugal|Portuguese descendants]] who arrived in Puerto Rico after the start of the Portuguese Inquisition in 1536. These are descendants of [[Converso]] families. There are some who maintain elements of Jewish traditions, although they themselves are, or were raised as Christians.

Santurce also has a very big [[Dominican Republic immigration to Puerto Rico|Dominican community]], along with [[Cubans|Cuban]], Colombian, [[Argentines|Argentine]] and [[Chinese immigration to Puerto Rico|Chinese]] communities.

==Transportation==
Public transportation is provided by several bus lines (locally known as guaguas) operated by the [[Puerto Rico Metropolitan Bus Authority]] and circulate along the main avenues of [[Avenida Juan Ponce de León|Ponce de León]] and [[Avenida Fernández Juncos|Fernández Juncos]] among others.

In the peripheries of Santurce there is a [[rapid transit]] system called [[Tren Urbano]]. The [[Sagrado Corazón (Tren Urbano station)|Sagrado Corazón station]] is the terminus of the sole [[Tren Urbano|metro system]] line of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], located in the southeast section of the district in the neighborhood of [[Martín Peña (Santurce)|Martín Peña]].
 
Santurce is a few minutes away by car from the US territory's main airport, [[Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport]], and from San Juan's secondary commercial airport, [[Isla Grande Airport]].

==Sports==
Santurce has the most modern swimming facilities in the Caribbean and fourth in the world. It is an Olympic aquatic sports facility used to host local and international events such as the 2nd A.S.U.A Pan American Masters Swimming Championship. The [[San Juan Natatorium]] is located in Santurce's Central Park. {{citation needed|date=March 2015}}

The district also has a baseball and a basketball team both known as the [[Cangrejeros de Santurce (baseball)|
Santurce Crabbers]] (''Cangrejeros de Santurce'') because of the original name of the township. They have been part of the community for over 70 years. Both teams have enjoyed great domestic success, the baseball team is regarded{{By whom|date=June 2013}} as the ‘[[New York Yankees]] of [[Puerto Rico]]’, largely in part to the accomplishments of its legendary players, such as [[Roberto Clemente]] and [[Willie Mays]]{{Citation needed|date=June 2013}}.

==Health==
Santurce has an extensive healthcare network which includes two of the finest hospitals on the island, Ashford [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian]] Community Hospital and [[Hospital Pavia|Pavia Hospital]].

==Economy==
{{See also|Economy of Puerto Rico|Real estate in Puerto Rico}}

Santurce experienced significant economic growth following World War II. During this period the district underwent an economic revitalization. Tourism is also a key industry based on Santurce's proximity to Puerto Rico's main international airport, [[Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport]], and the smaller [[Fernando Luis Ribas Dominicci Airport]]. The concentration of hotels are primarily located in the [[Condado, Puerto Rico|Condado]] area where there are numerous luxurious hotels including [[La Concha Resort]], [[Marriott Hotel|Marriott]] and the [[Conrad Hotels|Conrad Hotel]].
