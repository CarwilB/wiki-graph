'''Jacaltenango''' is a town and municipality situated in the Western Highlands of [[Guatemala]]. It is located in a valley surrounded by the [[Sierra Madre de Chiapas|Sierra Madre Mountains]]. 
Jacaltenango serves as the municipal seat for the surrounding municipality of the same name. 
In 2002, its urban population was about 23,500<ref>{{cite web |title=2002 census |publisher= Instituto Nacional de Estadísticas |url=http://www.ine.gob.gt/ |access-date= 2008-05-11}}</ref> but at the 2018 census the town's population has decreased to 22,533.

Its economy is based mainly on agricultural products, especially [[coffee]]. Jacaltenango exports about 95% of its agricultural production. Jacaltenango has six schools: three [[elementary school|elementary]] and three [[high schools]], which include [[middle school]].

==History==

After the [[Spanish conquest of Guatemala]] in the 1520s, the "Presentación de Guatemala" Mercedarian province was formed in 1565; originally, the [[order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy]] had gotten from bishop [[Francisco Marroquín]] several doctrines in the [[Sacatepéquez]] and [[Chimaltenango Department|Chimaltenango]] valleys, close to the capital [[Santiago de los Caballeros de Guatemala]], but they traded those with friars of the [[Order of Preachers]] in exchange for the doctrines those had in the [[Sierra de los Cuchumatanes]] area.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=336}} During the first part of the 17th century they also had doctrine in four towns close to the city of Santiago, which eventually became city neighborhoods: Espíritu Santo, Santiago, San Jerónimo and San Anton —which was the capital of the Mercedarians, where they had their convent and where their [[Encomienda|comendador]] lived.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=336}}

According to bishop Juan de las Cabezas' memoir in 1613{{sfn|Pérez|1966|p=44}} and the bishop [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]] parish visit minutes from 1770,{{sfn|Cortés y Larraz|1770|p=}} the Mercedarians came to have nine doctrines, and numerous annexes, which were: [[Malacatán|Santa Ana de Malacatán]], Concepción de Huehuetenango, [[San Pedro Soloma|San Pedro de Solomá]], Nuestra Señora de la Purificación de Jacaltenango, [[Chiantla|Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria de Chiantla]], [[Cuilco|San Andrés de Cuilco]], [[Tejutla, San Marcos|Santiago de Tejutla]], [[San Pedro Sacatepéquez, San Marcos|San Pedro de Sacatepéquez]], and [[San Juan Ostuncalco|San Juan de Ostuncalco]].{{sfn|Pérez|1966|p=44-45}}

However, in 1754, due to the [[borbon reforms]] implemented by the Spanish kings, the Mercedarins - and the rest of the [[regular clergy]] for that matter - had to transfer their doctrines to the [[secular clergy]], thus losing their Jacaltaenango convent and annexed doctrines.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=338}}

Much of its population lives abroad, mainly in Indiantown, Jupiter, West Palm Beach, and Lake Worth Florida, where there is a large community of Guatemalan Mayas. Some of those who migrated to Jupiter in Palm Beach County seasonally live in Morganton in the mountains of North Carolina.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Hitchcock|first=Olivia|title=Jupiter's deep connection to one Guatemalan town|url=https://www.palmbeachpost.com/news/jupiter-deep-connection-one-guatemalan-town/hOtZyowBQGbSypAEsXW7pL/|access-date=2021-05-03|website=The Palm Beach Post|language=en|archive-date=2021-05-03|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210503155723/https://www.palmbeachpost.com/news/jupiter-deep-connection-one-guatemalan-town/hOtZyowBQGbSypAEsXW7pL/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt5hhzjt|title=A Place to Be: Brazilian, Guatemalan, and Mexican Immigrants in Florida's New Destinations|date=2009|publisher=Rutgers University Press|jstor=j.ctt5hhzjt |isbn=978-0-8135-4492-2}}</ref>
