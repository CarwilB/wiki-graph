'''Purabá''' is a [[Districts of Costa Rica|district]] of the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]] canton, in the [[Heredia Province|Heredia]] province of [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The district consists of several large neighborhoods: San Bosco (Bosconia), Marías, Purabá, Lajas, and Calle Quirós.

== History ==
Purabá was created on 2 September 1976 by Decreto Ejecutivo 6370-G. Segregated from [[Santo Domingo District, Santa Bárbara|Santo Domingo]].<ref name="dta2017" />

Like the rest of the canton, the area now known as Villa Jesús was originally occupied before the Spanish settlers came by the [[Huetar language|Huetares]], an indigenous tribe. The Huetare King, Cacique Garabito, dominated the area.<ref name=RHC>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es}}</ref>

In early 1663, settler Joseph de Sandoval Ocampo set up cattle ranches in the area, including one toward the between the present-day areas of [[San Pedro de Santa Bárbara|San Pedro]] and Purabá, which was called Tapatalanga or Anonos at the time.<ref name=RHSBH>{{cite news|last1=Hernández Vargas|first1=Rose Marie|title=Reseña histórica de Santa Bárbara de Heredia|url=http://www.uned.ac.cr/extension/index.php/extension-en-accion/identidad-cultural/105-resena-historica-de-santa-barbara-de-heredia|accessdate=5 June 2014|agency=Universidad Estatal a Distancia|publisher=Extensión en acción|date=6 November 2013|language=Es}}</ref> [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]], [[Barva]], and [[Alajuela (city)|Alajuela]], three neighboring cities, were populated and settled in the late 1700s. As trade increased between the three cities, the canton developed.<ref name=centenario>{{cite book|title=Centenario del cantón Santa Bárbara de Heredia|year=1982|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/notas/seo.php/item.49/centenario-del-canton-santa-barbara-heredia.html}}{{Dead link|date=November 2023 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

Recalling her childhood of making dolls out of plantain leaves in Purabá, former teacher María Cecilia Alfaro Víquez, born in 1940, said that the roads were made of pebbles and dirt.<ref name="eterna">{{cite news|last1=Arguedas Ramírez|first1=Krisly|title=Eterna niña...|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.print.200/eterna-nina.html|accessdate=8 June 2014|publisher=Santa Bárbara: Una Comunidad Activa|date=10 March 2012|location=Santa Bárbara de Heredia, Costa Rica|language=es}}</ref> The culture and economy of sugarcane production was so widespread that even children like Alfaro Víquez made toy sugar mills.<ref name="eterna" /> In 1949, a bridge was constructed in the district, blessed by the local Catholic priest.<ref>{{cite web|title=Construccion del Puente Zetillal 1949|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/singlelink.php?lid=79|website=Santa Bárbara: Una Comunidad Activa|accessdate=7 June 2014|language=es}}</ref>

Purabá is the newest district in Santa Bárbara, created in 1976.<ref name="centenario"/><sup>2</sup> The backbone of Purabá's economy is agricultural production, particularly [[sugarcane]] cultivation,<ref name=centenario /> even though sugarcane grows best at lower elevations.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sugarcane Climate|url=http://www.sugarcanecrops.com/climate/|publisher=Netafim's Agriculture Department|accessdate=14 June 2014}}</ref> Up until 1975, Ingenio Agroindustrial Las Marías was a buyer of agricultural products for domestic consumption, but it moved to [[Grecia, Costa Rica|Grecia]] in 1975.

==Geography==
Purabá has an area of {{formatnum:6.09}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=24 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024225203/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:1240}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

Like much of Costa Rica, the Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos (National Risk Prevention Commission) puts Purabá in a high-risk zone.<ref name=Emergencias>{{cite web|title=AMENAZAS NATURALES CANTON DE SANTA BARBARA|url=http://www.cne.go.cr/Atlas%20de%20Amenazas/STABARBARA.htm|website=Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos y Atención de Emergencias|publisher=Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos y Atención de Emergencias|accessdate=5 June 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es|archive-date=2 December 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131202062932/http://www.cne.go.cr/Atlas%20de%20Amenazas/STABARBARA.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> This is due to the district's mountainous geography, which can lead to landslides and flooding.<ref name=Emergencias /> The highest part of Purabá is on the lower slopes of the Barva Massif, also known as [[Barva Volcano]], at {{Convert|2.400|m|ft}} above sea level.<ref name=MuniDatosgen/><ref name=Turdusnig/> The lowest part, at San Juan, is at {{Convert|1.000|m|ft}} above sea level.<ref name=MuniDatosgen>[http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106&limitstart=1 Municipalidad de Santa Barbara: Datos generales] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429120734/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106&limitstart=1 |date=2014-04-29 }} Retrieved 10 July 2014</ref> The Barva volcanic massif is part of a mountain range which includes volcanic cones. So part of Purabá de Santa Bárbara is in the foothills of Barva Volcano.<ref name=Turdusnig>[http://web2.uwindsor.ca/sandoval/28_Sandoval%20y%20Fernandez%202010.pdf "Filling the gap of Turdus nigrescens ''Aves Turdidae'' distribution, first record in Barva Volcano, Costa Rica", by Luis Sandoval1 and Melania Fernández, 2009] Retrieved 10 July 2014</ref> Several large rivers run through Purabá. These include Quebrada Setillal, Rio Ciruelas, Rio Pacayas, Rio Dorita.<ref>{{cite web|title=Mapas del cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=1|website=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara 2010|publisher=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|accessdate=8 June 2014|language=es|archive-date=14 July 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140714174240/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=1|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2010, the canton outlined plans to prevent future natural disasters in the district.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|accessdate=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|archive-date=5 June 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Economy==
[[File:Flowering Sugarcane (4682277934).jpg|thumb|Sugarcane has been the backbone of Purabá's economy]]
Purabá's real estate holdings, private, public, and ecclesiastic, are assessed according to national law.<ref>{{cite news|title=Decretos por declaración de zonas catastradas|url=http://www.uecatastro.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=112:decretos-por-declaracion-de-zonas-catastradas&catid=1:latest-news|accessdate=7 June 2014|publisher=La Gaceta|date=24 September 2013|language=es}}</ref> There are various commercial businesses in Purabá, including a pharmacy, restaurants and corner stores.<ref name="sbd">{{cite web|title=Santa Bárbara Digital: [http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/ Directorio Comercial: Santa Barbara de Heredia], Retrieved: May 20, 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Láscarez|first=Carlos|title=Tenían a menor como esclava|url=http://wvw.aldia.cr/ad_ee/2012/octubre/09/sucesos3348092.html|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Al Dia|date=9 October 2012|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref>

In 2010 the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara canton]] planned to improve Purabá's environmental protections with the goal of improving tourism.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> The plans include reforestation, cleaning rivers, and setting up protected areas.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

==Public and social services==

An EBAIS, [[Costa Rican Department of Social Security|Equipo Básico de Atención Integral en Salud (Basic Medical Care Center)]], is located in the San Bosco neighborhood, serving residents and non-residents of the district.<ref>{{cite web|title=Dirección de Compra de Servicios de Salud|url=http://portal.ccss.sa.cr/portal/page/portal/Gerencia_Administrativa/DireccionComprasServiciosdeSalud/CatalogosProveedor/RegionCentralNorte/%C1rea%20de%20Salud%20Santa%20B%E1rbara|publisher=Área de Salud Santa Bárbara|accessdate=7 June 2014|language=es}}</ref> The [[Catholic Church]] serves the region through the Archdiocese of Alajuela.<ref>{{cite web|title=Parroquia Santa Bárbara (Santa Bárbara de Heredia)|url=http://www.diocesisdealajuela.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=109&Itemid=508|website=Diócesis de Alajuela|accessdate=5 June 2014|location=Alajuela, Costa Rica|date=17 October 2013|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221213211/http://www.diocesisdealajuela.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=109&Itemid=508|archivedate=21 February 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Iglesia Católica de Purabá|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/singlelink.php?lid=79|website=Santa Bárbara: Una Comunidad Activa|accessdate=7 June 2014|language=es}}</ref> In Purabá, there is a public cemetery maintained by the canton.<ref name=plan>{{cite book|title=Plan Estratégico Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara, 2010 - 2015|date=August 2009|publisher=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara y Proyecto Fortalecimiento de las Capacidades Municipales para la Planificación d el Desarrollo Humano Local en Costa Rica|edition=1|url=http://www.ifam.go.cr/PaginaIFAM/docs/PRODUCTOS%20FOMUDE%202006-2011/R3-Productos/P48%20Planes%20Estrat%C3%A9gicos%20Municipales/Plan%20Estrategico%20Municipal%20Santa%20B%C3%A1rbara.pdf|accessdate=7 June 2014|language=es}}</ref>

The district is partially electrified by the [[Instituto Costarricense de Electricidad]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Santa Bárbara, Santo Domingo (Total), Purabá y Jesús (parcial)|url=https://www.cnfl.go.cr/index.php/servicios/mejoras/26/event_details|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140607202717/https://www.cnfl.go.cr/index.php/servicios/mejoras/26/event_details|url-status=dead|archive-date=June 7, 2014|publisher=Compañía Nacional de Fuerza y Luz|accessdate=7 June 2014}}</ref> Like many areas of the country, at various times, Purabá has suffered from problems with water distribution, despite the 24 springs and wells that exist in the area.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Mora|first1=Emilia|title=Agua genera polémica en cantón de Santa Bárbara|url=http://wvw.nacion.com/ln_ee/2003/diciembre/20/pais20.html|accessdate=8 June 2014|publisher=La Nacion|date=20 December 2003|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> Because of these problems, the canton created plans to improve Purabá's water distribution before 2020 by improving delivery and building a water treatment plant.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

There are three elementary schools that serve the entire district: Escuela Don Bosco, Escuela Rodolfo Peter Sheider, and Calle Quirós.<ref name="MEPP">Ministerio de Educación Pública: [http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos?title=&u   Ministerio de Educación Pública], accessdate: 27 May 2014</ref> By 2020, the district would like to expand the gyms of Escuela Don Bosco and Escuela Rodolfo Peter Sheider, as well as add a computer lab to Escuela Rodolfo Peter Sheider.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> In addition to serving as schools, Escuela Don Bosco and Escuela Rodolfo also serve as voting centers during [[Elections in Costa Rica|general elections]] and primaries.<ref>{{cite news|title=Centros de votación Heredia|url=http://wvw.nacion.com/ln_ee/elecciones/2002/pln/convencion/heredia1.html#nal4|accessdate=8 June 2014|publisher=La Nacion|date=2002|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> Students from the district usually attend Colegio Santa Bárbara High School,<ref name="MEPSB">Ministerio de Educación Pública: [http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara Santa Bárbara | Ministerio de Educación Pública] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140520215916/http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara |date=2014-05-20 }}, accessdate: 27 May 2014</ref> but may also go to the high schools in [[Barva]] or [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]].
