'''Tefé''', known in early accounts as '''Teffé''', is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the state of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], [[northern Brazil]].

==History==
As early as 1620 the Portuguese [[Carmelites]] could already boast of converts among the [[Mura people|Muras]], those natives of the Amazon established around Lake Teffé and on the borders of the neighbouring rivers.
Tefé, also called Ega at one time, was the fourth of eight ''aldeias''<ref>{{cite journal|first=Deborah|last=de Magalhães Lima-Ayres|year=1992|title=The Social Category Caboclo.| location=Kings College}}</ref> founded by [[Carmelites|Carmelite]] missionaries between 1697 and 1751{{#tag:ref|Henry W. Bates in 1851 said the settlement had been inhabited for 170 years, which would place its founding a bit earlier, around 1680.|group=Note}} along the Solimões and Negro Rivers. In 1759 the commandant Joaquim de Mello da Povoas converted the Carmelite 
mission on Lake Tefé into a town, which he named Ega; it was elevated to a city named Tefé in 1855 (but was known as Tefé before that time). The mission, called Parauarí, was originally established on Ilha dos Veados three leagues from the location on the opposite shore of Lake Tefe, called today by the name of Nogueira. It was abandoned due to a smallpox outbreak in the early 18th century and moved across the lake, where it remains today.{{#tag:ref|Henry W. Bates gave this description: "[Ega] is built on a small tract of cleared land at the lower or eastern end of the lake...On the opposite shore of the broad expanse stands a small village, called Nogueira".|group=Note}} The survivors were taken to its present site by F. Andre da Costa in 1817.

[[Henry_Walter_Bates|Henry W. Bates]] also describes in detail the cultural interactions of the natives with those in the town from his trip in the 1850s. He describes that the natives learned [[Tupi language|Tupi]] very quickly, and despite having a very large and diverse array of languages spoken all around the Amazon, Tupi was spoken "with little corruption along the banks of the main Amazons for a distance of 2,500 miles."<ref>{{cite book | url=https://archive.org/details/naturalistonrive00bate2 | page=[https://archive.org/details/naturalistonrive00bate2/page/260 260] | quote=learn tupi. |title = The Naturalist on the River Amazons: A Record of Adventures, Habits of Animals, Sketches of Brazilian and Indian Life, and Aspects of Nature Under the Equator, During Eleven Years of Travel| publisher=J. Murray |last1 = Bates|first1 = Henry Walter|year = 1875}}</ref>

In 1910, an apostolic prefecture, the current Prelature of Teffe, was founded by priests of the Congregation of the Holy Spirit.

==Geography==

Tefé is located about 525&nbsp;km by air or 595&nbsp;km by river to the west of [[Manaus]] on the south bank of the [[Rio Solimões]] (the upper Amazon), on the lake formed by the mouth of the [[Tefé River]]. The nearest large city is [[Coari]], 192&nbsp;km SE by east of Tefé.

The municipal seat of Tefé is the largest town and commercial center of the middle Solimões region. Its population was 59,547 <ref>[https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/am/tefe/panorama IBGE 2020]</ref> evenly divided between urban and rural, and its area is {{cvt|23704|km2}}.<ref>[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] - [http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php]</ref> There are no roads into Tefé and the only access is by river boat or plane. By fast boat it is about 12 hours from [[Manaus]]. [[Azul Linhas Aereas]] and Voepass operate flights from [[Eduardo Gomes International Airport|Manaus]] to [[Tefé Airport]].

The city is the home of the [[Territorial Prelature of Tefé]]. Tefé is also the major starting point for trips into the [[Mamirauá Sustainable Development Reserve|Mamiraua Reserve]].

The municipality is in the [[Juruá-Purus moist forests]] ecoregion.<ref>{{citation |title=South America: Amazon Basin, northwestern Brazil (NT0133)
 |last=Sears|first=Robin |publisher=WWF: World Wildlife Fund
 |url=http://www.worldwildlife.org/ecoregions/nt0133|access-date=2017-03-28}}</ref>
It contains 46% of the [[Tefé National Forest]], created in 1989.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|FLONA de Tefé – ISA}}|language=pt
 |title=FLONA de Tefé|publisher=ISA: Instituto Socioambiental
 |url=https://uc.socioambiental.org/uc/6656|access-date=2016-10-19}}</ref>
The municipality contains 33.85% of the {{convert|217486|ha|adj=on}} [[Catuá-Ipixuna Extractive Reserve]], established in 2003 as the first extractive reserve in the state of Amazonas.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|RESEX Catuá-Ipixuna – ISA}}|language=pt
