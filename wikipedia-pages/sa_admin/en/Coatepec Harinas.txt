'''Coatepec Harinas''' is one of 125 [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipalities]] in the [[State of Mexico]], [[Mexico]]. The municipal seat is the town of Coatepec Harinas.  The original name is "Coauhtepetl" which means 'serpent hill' in [[Náhuatl]]. Around 1825 because of a boom in flour production, the name "Harinas" was added.<ref name="EncMuc">{{cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15021a.htm |title=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de Mexico Estado de Mexico Coatepec Harinas |accessdate=2008-03-07 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070529100111/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15021a.htm |archivedate=May 29, 2007 }}</ref>

==The town==
It is located on the southern edge of the [[Nevado de Toluca]], {{convert|35|km|0|abbr=on}} south-southeast of the city of [[Toluca]] and {{convert|27|km|0|abbr=on}} west of [[Tenancingo, State of Mexico|Tenancingo]]. The earliest town of Coauhtepetl was probably settled sometime between 650 and 750 AD. Between 1522 and 1524 the area was subjugated by [[Gonzalo de Sandoval]] and Andrés de Tapia.  Between 1560 and 1563 a new town was constructed on the site. In 1623, the first parish was constructed.<ref name="EncMuc"/>

It covers a total surface area of {{convert|35.50|km2|0|abbr=on}} and, in the year 2000 census, it reported a population of 252,555.
The altitude of the municipality varies because of its topography between 3,000 and 1,900 above sea level. The municipality borders [[Temascaltepec]], [[Zinacantepec]], [[Toluca]], [[Tenango del Valle]], [[Villa Guerrero, Mexico State|Villa Guerrero]], [[Ixtapan de la Sal]], [[Zacualpan, Mexico State|Zacualpan]], [[Almoloya de Alquisiras]] and [[Texcaltitlan]]. The current municipality was created in 1949.<ref name="EncMuc"/>

In this town the people use to cultivate some fruit trees. The main product of this region is peaches. The production of other fruits depend on the season.

==History==
As of March 17, 2021, the municipality reported 57 cases and three deaths due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Casos positivos y defunciones a COVID-19 por municipio {{!}} Secretaría de Salud |url=https://salud.edomex.gob.mx/salud/covid19_municipio |website=salud.edomex.gob.mx |access-date=March 18, 2021}}</ref>
