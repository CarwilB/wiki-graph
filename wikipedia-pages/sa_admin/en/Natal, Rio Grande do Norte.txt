'''Natal''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|naˈtaw|lang}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] and the [[Capital city|capital]] of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Norte]], located in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast Region of Brazil]]. Situated 2,227 kilometers from [[Brasília]], the federal capital, it is the second smallest Brazilian state capital in area, covering an area of approximately {{cvt|167|km2}}.  With just over 750,000 inhabitants (2022), it is the most populous municipality in its state, the eighth in the Northeast, and the 24th in Brazil. The [[Greater Natal]], composed of thirteen other municipalities in Rio Grande do Norte, has more than 1.5 million inhabitants, making it the fourth largest urban agglomeration in the Northeast and the nineteenth largest in Brazil.

Founded on [[Christmas Day]] in 1599 on the banks of the [[Potengi River]], which separates the North Zone from the others, the city was occupied by the Dutch between 1633 and 1654, during which it was named [[Dutch Brazil|New Amsterdam]]. Its growth was slow during the first three centuries of its existence. Only from the 20th century onward did Natal undergo an intense modernization process, and beginning with [[World War II]], its population began to grow at a faster pace, especially throughout the 1970s and 1980s.

Its location near the "corner of South America" caught the attention of the [[United States Department of War]], which considered Natal "one of the four most strategic points in the world." With the start of operations at the first rocket launch base in [[South America]] — the [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] in [[Parnamirim, Rio Grande do Norte|Parnamirim]] — Natal became known as the "Space Capital of Brazil." The construction of the [[Via Costeira]], a coastal highway between the [[Atlantic Ocean]] and [[Natal Dunes State Park]] that connects the beaches of Areia Preta and [[Ponta Negra]], greatly boosted local [[tourism]].

==History==
=== Background, foundation and Dutch occupation ===
[[File:Forte-reis-magos.jpg|thumb|left|[[Forte dos Reis Magos|Fort of the Three Wise Men]], landmark of the city's founding, photographed from the [[Newton Navarro Bridge]]]]
[[File:Gillis Peeters - O Forte dos Reis Magos.jpg|thumb|left|''The Fort of the Three Wise Men'', por [[Gillis Peeters]]]]
[[File:Praça André de Albuquerque, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|left|[[André de Albuquerque Square]], Natal's zero milestone<ref name="RIBEIRO, Ramon"/>]]
[[File:Igreja Matriz de Nossa Senhora da Apresentação, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|left|Parish Church of Our Lady of the Presentation, former Metropolitan Cathedral]]

The history of Natal is closely intertwined with that of the Rio Grande do Norte. The [[Captaincy of Rio Grande]] began in 1535, with the arrival of a fleet commanded by Aires da Cunha, acting on behalf of the donatary João de Barros and the [[King of Portugal]]. The goal was to colonize the region, a task made difficult by the fierce resistance of the [[Potiguara]] Indigenous people and French pirates who traded brazilwood. Thus began the historical trajectory of the area located at the "corner" of [[South America]].<ref name="ctd-669">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191129162724/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/passeio-pela-hista-ria-paisagem-e-mema-ria-afetiva/466027|title=História|access-date=19 February 2012}}</ref>

On 25 December 1597, a new squadron entered the mouth of what is now the [[Potengi River]]. The first measure taken by the expeditionaries was to secure themselves against attacks from Indigenous groups and French corsairs.<ref name="ctd-669"/> Twelve days later, on 6 January 1598, construction began on the Barra do Rio Grande Fortress (later known as the [[Forte dos Reis Magos|Fortress of the Three Wise Men]]), which was completed on 24 June of the same year.<ref name="DOC108658"/><ref>[[#Bibliography|Teixeira]], 2006, p. 47-48.</ref>

The following year, on 25 December 1599, a mass was celebrated where the Parish Church of Our Lady of the Presentation now stands, marking the foundation of Natal.<ref name="Natal_421_anos"/> The original urban perimeter was demarcated by two crosses — from the current Mothers Square (northern limit) to Santa Cruz da Bica Square (southern limit), both located in the [[Cidade Alta (Natal)|Cidade Alta]] (Upper City).<ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 98.</ref>

There is no consensus among historians regarding the true founder of the city, as historical documents about Natal's foundation were destroyed during the Dutch occupation of Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020204133019/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_2i.htm|title=Dúvidas Históricas: A Cidade do Natal|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=20 May 2011}}</ref> Some historians credit Jerônimo de Albuquerque, while others attribute the founding to Manuel de Mascarenhas Homem.<ref name="hist_rn_16g"/> However, the historian from Natal, [[Luís da Câmara Cascudo]], in his book ''História do Rio Grande do Norte'' (1955), identifies João Rodrigues Colaço as the founder of the city:<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211005004142/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/aniversa-rio-da-cidade/434032|title=Aniversário da cidade|date=25 December 2018|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

{{blockquote|''(...) Jerônimo d’Albuquerque was no longer in command in March or April 1599, and by January 9, 1600 — fifteen days after the founding of the city — Colaço was captain of the fortress and had conducted fieldwork showing his authority in the area. Thus, all evidence seems to invalidate the tradition that Jerônimo d’Albuquerque founded the city of Natal, and that honor, at least provisionally, belongs to João Rodrigues Colaço.''}}

In 1601, by decree of the [[Portuguese Crown]], the Parish of Our Lady of the Presentation was created — the first [[parish]] in Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>[[#Bibliografia|Paula]], 2019, p. 109.</ref>

In 1631, a fleet of fourteen ships departed from [[Recife]] and landed in [[Ponta Negra]], intending to conquer the Captaincy of Rio Grande, but the attempt failed.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020620172048/https://tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_4a.htm|title=O Fracasso do Primeiro Assalto|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=10 July 2013}}</ref> Only in December 1633 did the Dutch occupation truly begin, when Dutch forces arrived in Natal, wounded the captain-major Pero Mendes Gouveia, and seized the Barra do Rio Grande Fortress, renaming it Castle of Keulen.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020620172257/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_4b.htm|title=A Rendição e a Tomada da Fortaleza|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=10 July 2013}}</ref><ref name="Invasão_holandesa-RN">{{cite web|access-date=10 July 2013 |lang=pt-br |publisher=Tribuna do Norte |title=O Governo Holandês no RN |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020623112117/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_4i.htm}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> The fortress, previously built of clay, was reconstructed in masonry, and Natal was renamed New Amsterdam, in reference to the Dutch capital.

The occupation ended only in 1654, when the Dutch were expelled by the Portuguese and New Amsterdam regained its original name, Natal. In 1661, the Netherlands officially recognized Portuguese sovereignty over northeastern Brazil through the [[Treaty of The Hague (1661)|Treaty of The Hague]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 261-262.</ref> After the occupation, the Portuguese resumed control of the territory, expanding inland. However, this process was far from peaceful, as many Indigenous peoples were persecuted and decimated. Stability in the Captaincy of Rio Grande would only come at the end of the 17th century, under the governance of Bernardo Vieira de Melo.<ref name="DOC108658">{{cite web|access-date=7 October 2021 |lang=pt-br |publisher=José Augusto Foundation |title=João Rodrigues Colaço |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001192528/http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/secretaria_extraordinaria_de_cultura/DOC/DOC000000000108658.PDF}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

=== From the 18th to the 19th century ===
[[File:Igreja do Rosário dos Pretos, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|left|The Church of Our Lady of the Rosary of the Blacks, the second oldest Catholic church in the city, was listed as a historical landmark in 1987<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004145107/http://www.adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/secretaria_extraordinaria_de_cultura/DOC/DOC000000000207484.PDF|title=Bens Tombados pelo Governo do Estado|date=2018|publisher=José Augusto Foundation|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>]]
[[File:Igreja do Galo e Museu de Arte Sacra, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|left|The Church of Saint Anthony, known as "Church of the Rooster," and the Museum of Sacred Art, which contains exhibitions of various objects related to the religious art of Rio Grande do Norte<ref name="igrejas.php">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140906210146/http://turismo.natal.rn.gov.br/igrejas.php|title=Igrejas|access-date=27 January 2019}}</ref>]]

At the beginning of the 18th century, construction began on the Church of Our Lady of the Rosary of the Black People, completed in 1714. On 21 November 1753, a group of fishermen found a wooden box stranded on rocks along the right bank of the Potengi River, near the present-day Rosary Stone (''Pedra do Rosário''). Inside was a statue of [[Our Lady of the Rosary]] and a message stating that no misfortune would befall the place where the image rested.<ref name="Nossa Senhora da Apresentação"/> The fishermen informed the local parish priest, Manoel Correia Gomes, who confirmed that the statue was indeed of Our Lady of the Rosary. The image was proclaimed patroness of Natal and renamed [[Our Lady of the Presentation]], since 21 November is the date when the [[Catholic Church]] celebrates the [[Presentation of Mary]] at the Temple in [[Jerusalem]].<ref name="Nossa Senhora da Apresentação">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081120020316/http://www.arquidiocesedenatal.org.br/arquidiocese/padroeiraarq.htm|title=Nossa Senhora da Apresentação - Padroeira da Arquidiocese e da cidade do Natal|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref name="História da Arquidiocese de Natal">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140715162229/https://www.arquidiocesedenatal.org.br/especiais/historia|title=História da Arquidiocese de Natal|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> In the second half of the 18th century, construction began on the Church of Saint Anthony, the third Catholic temple in Natal, later nicknamed the "Church of the Rooster."<ref>[[#Bibliography|Hora]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 87.</ref>

In 1817, during the [[Insurrection of Pernambuco|Pernambucan Insurrection]], the Rio Grande do Norte region joined the movement under the leadership of André de Albuquerque Maranhão, a wealthy sugar mill owner. On 29 March, he seized power in the city and formed a provisional government, but it lacked popular support. After the revolutionaries defeat in [[Pernambuco]], the movement lost momentum, and André was overthrown and wounded on 25 April, imprisoned in the Fortress of the Three Wise Men, and died the next day.<ref name="hist_rn_16g">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020623112816/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_16g.htm|title=História do RN - Uma Síntese|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170321072120/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/revolua-a-o-de-1817-o-movimento-que-tomou-o-poder/374856|title=Revolução de 1817, o movimento que tomou o poder|author=RIBEIRO, Ramon|date=21 March 2017|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

On 7 September 1822, Brazil's independence from [[Portugal]] was proclaimed in [[São Paulo]], but due to the distance, the news took time to reach Rio Grande do Norte. The province, now part of the [[Empire of Brazil]], celebrated independence in Natal on 22 January 1823, when the city had about 700 inhabitants.<ref name="DOC108658"/>

In 1856, Natal inaugurated its first cemetery, the Alecrim Cemetery.<ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 64.</ref>  In 1862, the construction of the tower of the Parish Church of Our Lady of the Presentation was completed.<ref>[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2007, p. 23.</ref> On 4 August 1878, the telegraph service was inaugurated.<ref>[[#Bibliography|TRINDADE]], 2010, p. 139.</ref> On 28 September 1881, the Ribeira Railway Station was opened — the oldest train station in Rio Grande do Norte, currently operated by the Brazilian Urban Train Company.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080328233439/http://estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rgn/natal-crgn.htm |title=Natal -- Estações Ferroviárias do Estado do Rio Grande do Norte |author=GIESBRECHT, Ralph |access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250429210301/https://www.gov.br/cbtu/index.php/pt/natal/39-mapas/4079-historia-da-ferrovia-no-rn|title=História da Ferrovia no RN|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

In 1888, the Natal City Council renamed Matriz Square as [[André de Albuquerque Square]],<ref>[[#Bibliography|RIBEIRO]], 2007, p. 4.</ref> which marks the city's zero milestone,<ref name="RIBEIRO, Ramon">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191129100427/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/passeio-pela-hista-ria-paisagem-e-mema-ria-afetiva/466027|title=Passeio pela história, paisagem e memória afetiva|author=RIBEIRO, Ramon|date=29 November 2019|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> surrounded by important historical buildings. Finally, on 15 November 1889, with the fall of the monarchy and the [[Proclamation of the Republic (Brazil)|Proclamation of the Republic]], Rio Grande do Norte became a state, with Natal remaining its capital.

The second half of the 19th century was marked by the city's growth, driven by the [[cotton]] cycle, along the banks of the Potengi River, where the [[Port of Natal]] stands today.<ref name="Natal_421_anos">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201224095039/https://agorarn.com.br/ultimas/natal-cidade-do-sol-capital-potiguar-faz-421-anos-nesta-sexta/|title=Natal, Cidade do Sol: capital potiguar faz 421 anos nesta sexta-feira 25|author=NOVA; Ana Luiza Vila; FILGUEIRA, Dandarah|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

=== Early decades of the 20th century ===
The first decades of the 20th century were marked by intense modernization in the city.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 71">[[#Bibliography|EMERENCIANO]], 2007, p. 71.</ref> In 1901, the neighborhood Cidade Nova was created — corresponding today to the districts of Tirol and Petrópolis.<ref>[[#Bibliografia|EMERENCIANO]], 2007, p. 144.</ref> On 24 March 1904, the Carlos Gomes Theater ([[Alberto Maranhão Theatre]] since 1957) was inaugurated.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 151"/> In the same year, [[acetylene gas]] lighting arrived in what is now Cidade Alta. Also in 1904, about 15,000 people from the countryside, suffering from a severe drought that had persisted since 1902, migrated to the city in search of better living conditions.<ref>[[#Bibliografia|FERREIRA; DANTAS]], 2001, p. 7.</ref> Four years later, the first animal-drawn trams began operating, running from Silva Jardim Street to Priest João Maria Square.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 71"/>

On 29 December 1909, [[Pope Pius X]], through the [[papal bull]] ''Apostolicam in Singulis'', created the Diocese of Natal, separated from the Diocese of Paraíba, and the Parish Church of Our Lady of the Presentation became the [[cathedral]] of the new diocese, whose territory covered the entire state of Rio Grande do Norte. The first [[bishop]], Joaquim Antônio de Almeida, was appointed the following year and officially took office on 15 June 1911.<ref name="Arquidiocese de Natal"/> Also in 1911, several significant events occurred: the creation of the Alecrim neighborhood, the installation of the first [[telephone]] line, the introduction of electric [[trams]], and the arrival of [[Film|cinema]]. The city's first power plant was also built, replacing gas lighting.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 71"/>

In 1912, construction began on a metal bridge designed by French engineer Georges Camille Imbaul, the English Bridge (''Ponte dos Ingleses''), spanning 550 meters, with steel imported from [[England]]. It was inaugurated on 20 April 1916, boosting Natal's commerce and improving access between the capital and the countryside, which had previously been possible only by crossing the Potengi River by boat.<ref name="HORA, 2008, p. 57.">[[#Bibliografia|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 57.</ref><ref name="Ponte dos Ingleses">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160724161224/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/ponte-de-igapa-marca-um-sa-culo-na-paisagem/352964|title=Ponte de Igapó marca um século na paisagem|author=FRANÇA, Tádzio|date=23 July 2016|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> According to the 1920 census, Natal's population reached 30,696 inhabitants, almost double the 16,596 recorded in 1900.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 71"/>

On 7 September 1922, as Brazil celebrated the centennial of its independence, the Felipe Camarão Palace was inaugurated as the seat of the municipal administration (then called the intendency). The building only received its current name on 20 May 1955, in honor of [[Felipe Camarão|Antônio Felipe Camarão]], an Indigenous leader who fought against Dutch domination in Brazil. The palace housed the city hall until 1966, later restored and reopened in 1979.<ref>[[#Bibliografia|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 96.</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090421031432/https://natal.rn.gov.br/natal/ctd-670.html|title=Palácio Felipe Camarão|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001143441/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/natal-ja-teve-38-administradores/240145|title=Natal já teve 38 administradores|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|date=21 December 2012|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

[[File:Coluna Capitolina, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|left|The Capitoline Column, from [[Rome]], was donated to the city by [[Benito Mussolini]]]]

From 1927 onward, Natal began to gain importance in the history of aviation, as seaplanes started landing on the waters of the Potengi River.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010630160047/http://tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_11d.htm|title=Os Hidroaviões Aterrissam no Potengi|access-date=30 September 2012}}</ref> On 19 March 1928, Januário Cicco founded the Natal Maternity Hospital, later renamed in his honor on 12 February 1950.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004144227/https://www.gov.br/ebserh/pt-br/hospitais-universitarios/regiao-nordeste/mejc-ufrn/comunicacao/noticias/maternidade-escola-januario-cicco-completa-70-anos|title=Maternidade Escola Januário Cicco completa 70 anos|date=12 February 2020|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> In 1929, the office of ''intendente'' (municipal administrator) was officially transformed into mayor, and Omar O'Grady, who had served since November 1924, became Natal's first mayor, remaining in office until 8 October 1930, when all mayors in Rio Grande do Norte were removed during a [[Revolution of 1930|revolutionary movement]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|SANTOS]], 2009, p. 11.</ref>

On [[New Year's Day]] of 1931, the Italian ship ''Lazzaro Malocello'', commanded by Commander Carlo Alberto Coraggio, arrived in Natal carrying the Capitoline Column, a piece of [[Ancient Rome]], donated by [[Benito Mussolini]] to commemorate the first flight from Natal to [[Rome]], completed in 1928. On 6 January 1931, the city was visited by the [[Italian Air Force]], and two days later, the Capitoline Column was formally inaugurated.<ref name="hist_rn_16g" /><ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 81.</ref> On 21 October 1932, a presidential decree created the [[Port of Natal]], which began operations three days later.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221007223444/http://www.fotoimagem.srv.br/paginas/portos/rn/natal/natal.htm|title=Porto de Natal - RN|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

[[File:Quartel da salgadeira em 1935.jpg|thumb|The Salgadeira Barracks, current headquarters of the Student House, after the [[1935 Brazilian communist uprising|Communist Uprising of 1935]]]]
[[File:U.S. President Roosevelt and Brazilian President Getúlio Vargas aboard USS Humboldt (AVP-21), 1943 (25132077365).jpg|thumb|The presidents of [[President of Brazil|Brazil]], [[Getúlio Vargas]], and the [[U.S. president|United States]], [[Franklin Delano Roosevelt]], during the [[Potenji River Conference|Potengi Conference]], held aboard the [[USS Humboldt]] on 28 January 1943, with [[Harry Hopkins]], Roosevelt's advisor (left) and [[Jefferson Caffery]], [[List of ambassadors of the United States to Brazil|US ambassador to Brazil]] (right)]]

On the night of 23 November 1935, during a graduation ceremony at the Carlos Gomes Theater, the [[1935 Brazilian communist uprising|Communist Uprising]] (''Intentona Comunista'') erupted, targeting first the 21st Infantry Battalion Barracks, now the Winston Churchill State School.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO; PINHEIRO]], 2017, p. 105.</ref> Two days later, rebels seized control of the city, forming a revolutionary popular committee. Several people were killed, and banks, warehouses, and shops were looted. The uprising spread to Recife the next day and to [[Rio de Janeiro]] by 27 November, but it quickly lost strength as troops from [[Ceará]] and [[Paraíba]] arrived to suppress it.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO; PINHEIRO]], 2017, p. 112.</ref> The rebels abandoned the city and were eventually defeated in the Doctor's Mountain (''Serra do Doutor'') region, in [[Campo Redondo]], as they attempted to flee toward [[Seridó]].<ref name="hist_rn_16g"/><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020818090152/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_10g.htm|title=A Intentona Comunista: Ação Armada e o Domínio de Natal|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=3 October 2012}}</ref>

=== World War II ===
On 28 January 1943, the USS Humboldt docked at the Port of Natal, carrying [[U.S. President]] [[Franklin Delano Roosevelt]], returning from the [[Casablanca Conference]] in [[Morocco]]. On this occasion, Roosevelt met with [[Brazilian President]] [[Getúlio Vargas]]. The meeting became known as the Potengi Conference, during which both leaders discussed Brazil's entry into the war — leading to the creation of the [[Brazilian Expeditionary Force]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303200045/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/evento-celebra-70-anos-da-conferencia-do-potengi/241872|title=Evento celebra 70 anos da “Conferência do Potengi”|author=FREIRE, Gabriela|date=25 January 2013|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

With the advent of [[World War II]], Natal continued to grow rapidly, driven by the presence of Brazilian and Allied troops, especially Americans. Close relations developed between the locals and U.S. military personnel, with numerous social gatherings and dances, which introduced foreign musical styles and gradually transformed the city's cultural habits.<ref name="hist_rn_16g"/><ref name="hist_rn_11n">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010630154630/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/especial/histrn/hist_rn_11n.htm|title=Natal na Segunda Guerra Mundial: Influência Americana e Mudança dos Costumes|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=20 May 2011}}</ref><ref name="WWII">{{cite web|access-date=30 September 2012 |date=29 September 2000 |lang=pt-br |title=Histórias de Natal, da ocupação à Segunda Guerra |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010712141327/https://www.terra.com.br/turismo/roteiros/2000/09/29/006.htm}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> Natal's population grew from 54,836 in 1940 to 103,215 in 1950, nearly doubling in just one decade.<ref name="População_NatalRN">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/evolucao_da_divisao_territorial_do_brasil/evolucao_da_divisao_territorial_do_brasil_1872_2010/evolucao_da_populacao_segundo_os_municipios.pdf|title=Evolução da população, segundo os municípios|author=IBGE|access-date=16 June 2025}}</ref>

Due to its location near the "corner of South America," the [[U.S. Department of War]] considered Natal "one of the four most strategic points in the world," alongside the [[Suez Canal]] ([[Egypt]]), the [[Bosporus Strait]] ([[Turkey]]), and the [[Strait of Gibraltar]] (between [[Africa]] and [[Europe]]).<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060613211612/https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/noticias/ult338u2615.shtml|title=Natal é mais próxima da África que de SP|author=FREDERICO, Carol|date=5 May 2003|publisher=[[Folha de S.Paulo]]|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

=== From the postwar period to the 1990s ===
In 1946, during the administration of Mayor Sylvio Pedroza, the Circular Avenue — currently known as President Café Filho Avenue — was inaugurated.<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 151"/> In 1949, construction began on the Mãe Luíza Lighthouse, formally inaugurated in 1951,<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 151"/> standing 37 meters height.<ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] ''et al'', 2008, p. 44.</ref> The following year, by the papal bull ''Arduum Onus'' issued by [[Pope Pius XII]], the Diocese of Natal was elevated to the dignity of an [[Archdiocese of Natal|archdiocese]], and Bishop Marcolino Esmeraldo, who had served since 1929, became its first archbishop, remaining in office until his death in 1967.<ref name="Arquidiocese de Natal">{{cite web|access-date=7 October 2021 |lang=pt-br |title=Arquidiocese de Natal |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090715014947/http://www.arquidiocesedenatal.org.br/arquidiocese/historiaarq.htm}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

On 24 August 1954, following the suicide of Getúlio Vargas, [[Café Filho]], a native of Natal, assumed the presidency of Brazil for the following fourteen months, becoming, to this day, the only person from Rio Grande do Norte to occupy the position.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211006143857/https://historianosdetalhes.com.br/historia-do-rn/o-presidente-cafe-filho-visita-natal/|title=O Presidente Café Filho visita Natal|author=TRINDADE, Sérgio|date=28 October 2019|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211006143908/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/homenagem-a-cafa-filho/488248|title=Homenagem a Café Filho|author=LOPES, Ney|date=26 August 2020|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> In 1956, [[Djalma Maranhão]], also from Natal, was appointed mayor by Governor Dinarte Mariz, and in 1960 he won the first direct mayoral election in the city's history. Among his achievements was the "Barefoot Campaign Also Learn to Read" (''Campanha de Pé no Chão Também se Aprende a Ler''), which brought significant advances to local education. In 1964, with the establishment of the military regime in Brazil, Djalma Maranhão had his mandate revoked and was imprisoned in army barracks in the city, later being transferred to Recife and then to [[Fernando de Noronha]]. After being released by a [[habeas corpus]] from the [[Supreme Federal Court]] (STF) in December 1964, he went into exile in [[Montevideo]], [[Uruguay]], where he lived until 1971, the year of his death.<ref name="dhnet">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111110172353/http://www.dhnet.org.br/educar/penochao/apresentacao.htm|title=De Pé no Chão Também se Aprende a Ler|author=GÓES, Moacyr|year=1997|location=[[Rio de Janeiro]]|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

On 7 September 1965, the International Hotel of the Three Kings (''Hotel Internacional dos Reis Magos'') was inaugurated on Meio Beach, operating for thirty years until 1995, and remaining abandoned until its demolition in January 2020.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190424074147/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/laje-desaba-no-hotel-reis-magos/446010|title=Laje desaba no Hotel Reis Magos|date=24 April 2019|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004132549/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/hotel-reis-magos-algumas-hista-rias/469240|title=Hotel Reis Magos: algumas histórias|author=PORPINO, Luiz Antonio|date=10 January 2020|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211006160917/https://www.jolrn.com.br/2020/01/26/demolicao-do-hotel-reis-magos-e-concluida-em-natal/|title=Demolição do hotel Reis Magos é concluída em Natal|author=AMARAL, Lúcio|date=26 January 2020|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> On 12 October of the same year, the Ministry of Aeronautics officially created the first rocket launch base in South America — the [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] — located in the municipality of [[Parnamirim, Rio Grande do Norte|Parnamirim]], earning Natal the nickname "Brazil's Space Capital."<ref>[[#Bibliography|PEIXOTO]], 2003, p. 84.</ref> In 1967, the city's first master plan was drafted, and five years later the Humberto de Alencar Castelo Branco Stadium (Castelão) was inaugurated,<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 151">[[#Bibliography|EMERENCIANO]], 2007, p. 151.</ref> later renamed João Cláudio de Vasconcelos Machado Stadium (Machadão) in 1989.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001210111/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/machadao-com-os-dias-contados/179317|title=Machadão com os dias contados|date=23 April 2011|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> In May 1969, Mayor Agnelo Alves, who had governed Natal since 1966, was stripped of his political rights and imprisoned by the [[Military dictatorship in Brazil|military regime]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241219023214/https://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2015/06/morre-o-deputado-estadual-agnelo-alves-ex-prefeito-de-natal.html |title=Morre o deputado estadual Agnelo Alves, ex-prefeito de Natal |date=21 June 2015 |access-date=9 October 2025}}</ref> From that date onward, the city's mayors were appointed by the [[governor of Rio Grande do Norte]], under Institutional Act No. 3 of 1966, which imposed [[indirect elections]] for state governors and granted them the power to appoint the mayors of their capitals.<ref>BRAZIL. '''[https://web.archive.org/web/20130918032841/http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/AIT/ait-03-66.htm Ato Institucional N.º 3, de 5 de Fevereiro de 1966]'''. [[Brasília]], 7 February 1966.</ref>

[[File:Campus-em-Construção.jpg|thumb|Construction of the central campus of UFRN, in the 1970s]]

On 26 September 1970,<ref name="HORA, 2008, p. 57."/> the concrete Igapó Bridge replaced the old "English Bridge," which was to be dismantled but never completely removed; much of its metallic structure remains preserved to this day.<ref name="Ponte dos Ingleses"/> On 14 March 1971, the city gained the General Hospital and Emergency Room of Natal, which began operating only in 1973 under the name Monsignor Walfredo Gurgel Hospital.<ref name="hospital-walfredo-gurgel">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181107150949/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/veja-linha-do-tempo-sobre-o-hospital-walfredo-gurgel/246515|title=Veja linha do tempo sobre o Hospital Walfredo Gurgel|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|date=31 March 2013|access-date=27 March 2023}}</ref> In 1974, the Municipal Building Code was established,<ref name="Emerenciano, p. 152">[[#Bibliography|EMERENCIANO]], 2007, p. 152.</ref> construction began on the Ponta Negra Viaduct,<ref name="Maciel e Lima, 2014, p. 135"/> and the colonial landmark was transferred from [[Touros]] to the Fortress of the Three Wise Men.<ref>[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2020, p. 137.</ref> The following year, the road to Ponta Negra was paved — later becoming Engineer Roberto Freire Avenue — and the BR-101 highway between Natal and Parnamirim was doubled.<ref name="Maciel e Lima, 2014, p. 135">[[#Bibliography|MACIEL; LIMA]], 2014, p. 135.</ref> Also during the 1970s, the central campus of the [[Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte]] was built on a 123-hectare area of the current Lagoa Nova neighborhood.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://campusvirtual.ufrn.br/memoria/detalhes/4 |title=Biblioteca Central Zila Mamede |access-date=21 September 2024}}</ref> That decade and the following ones saw rapid population growth:<ref name="portal.iphan.gov.br">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190620221436/http://portal.iphan.gov.br/pagina/detalhes/1457/|title=História - Natal (RN)|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> between the 1960 and 1970 censuses, the population jumped from 160,253 to 264,379 inhabitants, reaching 416,898 in 1980 and 606,887 in 1991.<ref name="População_NatalRN"/>

In 1983, Natal gained its Convention Center,<ref>[[#Bibliography|TAVEIRA]], 2016, p. 200.</ref> and in the early morning of 25 February 1984, during Carnival, the "Baldo tragedy" occurred—  nineteen people were killed and twelve injured after being run over by a bus driver seeking revenge after being told he would have to work overtime.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090103012120/http://redeglobo.globo.com/Linhadireta/0,26665,GHE0-4625-220532,00.html|title=Corrida alucinada|date=2005|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> On 6 April 1984, the city hosted the ''[[Diretas Já]]'' movement, when more than sixty thousand people gathered around Gentil Ferreira Square demanding the return of direct [[presidential elections in Brazil]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211003013643/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/comicio-levou-60-mil-a-praca-gentil-ferreira/280113|title=Comício levou 60 mil à praça Gentil Ferreira|date=25 April 2014|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

[[File:Via Costeira, Natal (RN).jpg|thumb|The [[Via Costeira|Coastal Highway (RN-301)]], an expressway between the [[Atlantic Ocean]] and the [[Natal Dunes State Park]], significantly boosted local tourism starting in the 1980s.<ref name="Taveira, 2016, p. 201-202"/> Along its length are the city's four-star and five-star hotels.<ref>[[#Bibliography|LOPES NETO]], 2020, p. 48-49.</ref>]]
After being initiated in the late 1970s during Governor Tarcísio Maia's administration, the [[Via Costeira]] — officially Senator Dinarte Mariz Avenue (RN-301) — was opened to the public in 1985 by Governor [[José Agripino Maia|José Agripino]], son of Tarcísio. The road, which runs between the [[Atlantic Ocean]] and the [[Natal Dunes State Park]],<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001212044/https://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/via-costeira-ainda-desconhecida/435986|title=Via Costeira ainda desconhecida|author=SILVA, Yuno|date=15 January 2019|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref name="BARBOSA, Anderson">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201022112709/https://agorarn.com.br/ultimas/area-de-risco-para-motoristas-via-costeira-continua-sem-controle-de-velocidade/|title=Área de risco para motoristas, Via Costeira continua sem controle de velocidade|author=BARBOSA, Anderson|date=18 September 2020|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> significantly boosted local tourism,<ref name="Taveira, 2016, p. 201-202">[[#Bibliography|TAVEIRA]], 2016, p. 201-202.</ref> especially in [[Ponta Negra]], which evolved from a small fishing village into one of the city's most upscale and valued areas.<ref>[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2007, p. 46.</ref>

After two decades, Natal held direct elections for mayor again in 1985, when Garibaldi Alves Filho was elected and served between 1986 and 1989.<ref>[[#Bibliography|EVANGELISTA]], 2013, p. 89.</ref><ref>{{cite web |lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230126140033/https://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil/noticia/2007-12-12/garibaldi-alves-filho-foi-governador-do-rn-e-esta-em-seu-segundo-mandato-no-senado |title=Garibaldi Alves Filho foi governador do RN e está em seu segundo mandato no Senado |date=12 December 2007 |publisher=Agência Brasil |access-date=9 October 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240524190737/https://www12.senado.leg.br/noticias/materias/2024/05/24/verdade-eleitoral-nao-pode-ser-posta-em-duvida-afirma-garibaldi-alves-filho |title=Verdade eleitoral não pode ser posta em dúvida, afirma Garibaldi Alves Filho |date=24 May 2024 |publisher=Agência Senado |access-date=9 October 2025}}</ref> In the following election, held in 1988, [[Wilma de Faria]] became the first woman elected mayor in the city's history.<ref name="ult96u86099"/> Six days later, Archbishop Alair Vilar, at the beginning of his episcopate, inaugurated the new Metropolitan Cathedral, replacing the old Church of Our Lady of the Presentation, which had become too small for the growing number of faithful.<ref name="Arquidiocese de Natal"/>  The new cathedral received a visit from [[Pope John Paul II]] on 12 October 1991, during his third visit to Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181018063824/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/papa-dromo-reformado-por-r-8-milha-es/427546|title=Papódromo reformado por R$ 8 milhões|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|date=17 October 2018|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> That same year saw the first edition of Carnatal,<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131205201113/https://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/carnatal/2013/noticia/2013/12/carnatal-completa-22-anos-conheca-historia-da-micareta.html|title=Carnatal completa 22 anos; conheça a história da micareta|date=4 December 2013|publisher=G1 RN|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> which would later enter the [[Guinness Book]] as the world's largest street carnival.<ref name="globoesporte.globo">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131205201113/https://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/carnatal/2013/noticia/2013/12/carnatal-completa-22-anos-conheca-historia-da-micareta.html |title=Carnatal, arma potiguar para sediar a Copa |author=LAVINAS, Thiago |date=24 August 2007 |access-date=2 February 2019}}</ref> In 1996, Wilma was elected for her second term as mayor,<ref name="ult96u86099">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210303034624/https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/brasil/ult96u86099.shtml|title=Primeira mulher a governar o RN, Wilma de Faria se reelege|date=30 October 2006|publisher=[[Folha de S. Paulo]]|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> and on 16 January 1997, a state law created the [[Greater Natal]], initially composed of the capital and five neighboring municipalities.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150222163444/http://www.al.rn.gov.br/portal/_ups/legislacao//Lei%20Comp.%20152.pdf|title=Lei complementar n° 152, de 16 de janeiro de 1997|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

=== From the fourth centennial to the present day ===
[[File:Complexo viário do Quarto Centenário.jpg|left|thumb|The Senator Carlos Alberto de Sousa Road Complex, in the Lagoa Nova neighborhood, was inaugurated in 2000 during the celebrations of Natal's 400th anniversary]]
[[File:Ponteforteredinha.JPG|left|thumb|The Newton Navarro Bridge in January 2007, in the final months of its construction]]

In 1999, Natal celebrated its 400th anniversary. As part of the commemorations, the Portico of the Three Wise Men was inaugurated to welcome visitors arriving via the southern section of the BR-101 highway.<ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] et al, 2008, p. 58.</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001221751/https://www.folhadelondrina.com.br/geral/natal-ganha-portico-em-comemoracao-de-400-anos-241351.html|title=Natal ganha pórtico em comemoração de 400 anos|author=SOUZA, Juliano|date=30 December 1999|publisher=Folha de Londrina|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> On 25 February 2000, the Fourth Centennial Viaduct was opened, connecting the avenues Senator Salgado Filho and Prudente de Morais.<ref>[[Bibliography|COSTA; PENHA; NOGUEIRA; PINHEIRO]], 2021, p. 2.</ref> That same year, Ponta Negra Beach gained a four-kilometer boardwalk with kiosks along its stretch, replacing the numerous beach huts that once stood there.<ref name="Maciel e Lima, 2014, p. 135"/> Also in 2000, Wilma de Faria was re-elected for a third term, serving until April 2002, when she resigned to run for governor of Rio Grande do Norte. She won the election, becoming the state's first woman governor.<ref name="ult96u86099"/> Her vice, [[Carlos Eduardo Alves]], assumed office and was re-elected in 2004.<ref name="carlos-eduardo-pdt"/> On 27 April 2005, the [[Midway Mall]], developed by the Guararapes Group, was inaugurated, quickly becoming the largest [[shopping center]] in Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180512085053/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/midway-mall-completa-10-anos-e-espera-crescer-11/312278|title=Midway Mall completa 10 anos e espera crescer 11%|author=VASCONCELOS, Sara|date=28 April 2015|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200322095703/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/midway-mall-e-natal-shopping-fecham-as-portas-a-partir-deste-sa-bado-21/475283|title=Midway Mall e Natal Shopping fecham as portas a partir deste sábado (21)|date=20 March 2020|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201023035218/https://agorarn.com.br/geral/maior-shopping-de-natal-anuncia-possivel-reabertura-para-o-dia-28-de-julho/|title=Maior shopping de Natal anuncia possível reabertura para o dia 28 de julho|date=22 July 2020|publisher=Agora RN|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

On 21 November 2007, three years after construction began, the [[Newton Navarro Bridge]] was officially opened to traffic.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211006143854/https://nominuto.com/noticias/cidades/ponte-newton-navarro-registra-engarrafamento-no-primeiro-dia-de-liberacao-do-transito/8488/|title=Ponte Newton Navarro registra engarrafamento no primeiro dia de liberação do trânsito|date=21 November 2007|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> The new bridge connects the neighborhoods of Santos Reis and Redinha and was built as an alternative to the Igapó Bridge,<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180919115258/https://ufrn.br/imprensa/materias-especiais/13797/pesquisa-da-ufrn-aponta-que-79-dos-potiguares-nao-se-sentem-seguros-ao-transitar-na-ponte-de-igapo|title=Segundo estudo da UFRN, 79% dos potiguares não se sentem seguros ao transitar na Ponte de Igapó|author=MONTEIRO, Evelin|date=22 February 2018|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> being named after the renowned local artist Newton Navarro Bilro.<ref>[[#Bibliography|HORA]] et al, 2008, p. 65.</ref> On 31 May 2009, Natal was announced as one of the twelve host cities for the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130822115610/https://acervo.oglobo.globo.com/fatos-historicos/brasil-foi-escolhido-sede-da-copa-do-mundo-de-2014-em-outubro-de-2007-9630221|title=Brasil foi escolhido sede da Copa do Mundo de 2014 em outubro de 2007|date=20 August 2013|publisher=[[O Globo]]|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref> Later that night (into the following morning), [[Air France Flight 447]] enroute from Rio De Janeiro to Paris crashed into the Atlantic Ocean approximately 300km northeast of the city killing all 228 passengers and crew. In 2010, the Via Costeira was doubled,<ref name="BARBOSA, Anderson"/> and the city's [[Historic Center of Natal|Historic Center]] — encompassing parts of the Cidade Alta and Ribeira — was listed as a cultural heritage site by [[National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage|Brazil’s National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004224437/https://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/conselho-do-iphan-aprova-tombamento-do-centro/167270|title=Conselho do Iphan aprova tombamento do Centro|date=10 December 2010|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211004223210/https://www.panrotas.com.br/noticia-turismo/destinos/2010/12/iphan-tomba-centro-historico-de-natal-rn_63911.html|title=Iphan tomba Centro Histórico de Natal (RN)|author=ROCHA, Antônio|date=13 December 2010|access-date=7 October 2021}}</ref>

On 27 August 2012, the city witnessed the Bus Revolt after the municipal government announced a 20-cent fare increase,<ref>{{cite web |lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211006143854/http://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2012/08/grupo-protesta-contra-aumento-de-passagem-em-natal-e-interdita-br.html ==Geography==
Natal is located at {{Coord|5|46|S|35|12|W|}}, in the far east of South America. The city has a total area of {{convert|170|km²|0|abbr=out}}. Natal lies on the Atlantic Ocean, at the mouth of the [[Potengi River]].

===Climate===
Natal has a typical [[tropical climate]], specifically a [[tropical wet and dry climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification]]: ''As''), with warm to hot temperatures and high relative humidity all throughout the year. However, these conditions are relieved by a near absence of extreme temperatures and pleasant trade winds blowing from the ocean. February is the warmest month, with mean maximum of {{convert|30.7|°C|°F|1|disp=or}} and minimum of {{convert|24.6|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}, while July is the coolest with mean maximum of {{convert|28.4|°C|°F|1|disp=or}} and minimum of {{convert|21|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}. Rainfall is heavy at around {{convert|1690|mm|in|0}} per year, but there is a dry season between about September and January that is more distinct than further south on the Atlantic coast. The greatest amount of rain falls between March and July.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.destination360.com/south-america/brazil/natal.php |title=Climate of Natal |author=Destination360.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090416115439/http://www.destination360.com/south-america/brazil/natal.php |archive-date=16 April 2009 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Known as the "City of the [[Sun]]"<ref>{{cite web |url=http://hep.if.usp.br/WIN13/second-bulletin.html |title=WIN'13 – 24th International Workshop on Weak Interactions and Neutrinos |access-date=29 January 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150614030437/http://hep.if.usp.br/WIN13/second-bulletin.html |archive-date=14 June 2015 |url-status=live }}</ref> and also as "The City of the [[Dunes]]", Natal is located in the northeastern tip of [[Brazil]]. Lying about six degrees south of the equator the sun shines on for more than 3,000 hours every year. The average temperature in Natal is about {{convert|27|°C|°F|1|disp=or}}. During the summer season it normally reaches above {{convert|30|°C}} with the water at {{convert|26|°C}}.

==Economy==
{{Main|Economy of Brazil}}

With its dozens of sandy, white beaches, such as [[Ponta Negra]] and its famous [[Morro do Careca]]{{dash}}the cliff of dolphins, [[Pirangi do Norte]], [[Redinha beach|Redinha]], [[Pipa beach|Pipa]] and [[Genipabu]] with its famous fixed sand dunes and imported [[dromedaries]], tourism is the most important industry of Natal, attracting [[Brazilians]], Europeans (many from Spain, England, [[Scandinavia]], Germany, [[Portugal]], [[Italy]] and France), and [[United States|U.S. citizens]] alike. There has been an increase of 211% in foreign tourism in Natal between 2002 and 2007 (for the same time period the increase of foreign tourism in [[Brazil]] was 62,8%); This is a due to the accessibility of Natal and the appeal Brazil has for an increasing number of tourists, clearly showing that Natal is the area leading the Brazilian tourism boom. Natal is the number one area receiving international [[investment]]s in Brazil. Natal will benefit enormously from the massive future investments on the [[coastline]].

It is also a relevant administrative center for the oil industry ([[Rio Grande do Norte]] being the second largest oil-producing state in Brazil). Thus, its [[Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte]] (UFRN) is a national scientific research pole on Oil Sciences (ranging from Geophysical to Law studies), supported mostly by [[Petrobras]] financing.

The fishing industry is strong ([[shark]] [[cartilage]] being a major export to Japan) as is the cultivation of tropical fruit, especially [[mangos]], [[guava]]s, and [[cashews]] (the fruit and the nuts).

In fact, the largest cashew tree in the world is located near the coast in the neighborhood of Pirangi, south of the city center. This tree has a circumference of 500&nbsp;metres and occupies an area of 7,300&nbsp;m<sup>2</sup>, making it 70 times the size of average cashew trees.

==Main sights==
;Natal Dunes State Park
[[File:Dunas_do_Norte.jpg|thumb|[[Natal Dunes State Park]] is the second largest urban park in [[Brazil]].<ref>[http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/105583.html Dunas Park] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090408035932/http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/105583.html |date=8 April 2009 }}</ref>]]
The [[Natal Dunes State Park]] is considered the second largest urban park in [[Brazil]].<ref>[http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/105583.html Tribuna do Norte News – Dunas Park] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110706155035/http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/105583.html |date=6 July 2011 }}</ref> It includes 1,172 hectares of land, and allows observation of several vegetal and animal species, which are typical of the [[Atlantic Forest]]. The tour is along a trail, accompanied by trained guides. The park also has a jogging track, and stays open for visitors from Tuesday to Sunday.

;Museums
Museums in Natal include: Museu de Arte Sacra, Museu de Cultura Popular, Espaço Cultural Palácio Potengi, Instituto Histórico e Geográfico do Rio Grande do Norte, Pinacoteca do Rio Grande do Norte, Museum Camara Cascudo, Memorial Camara Cascudo.

;Natal Aquarium
[[File:Cavalo Marinho no Aquário Natal.jpg|thumb|left|[[Seahorses]] in the Natal Aquarium]]
The Natal [[Aquarium]] was founded and is run by a family of biologists and retired environmentalists. Besides the exhibit, the aquarium also serves as a surgery center for sea animals. The Aquarium is located between the Oceânica Avenue and the beach. The Aquarium exhibits about 60 marine species.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/tourists.html |title=Aquarium of Natal |publisher=Natal-brazil.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120224090634/http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/tourists.html |archive-date=24 February 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> There are about 30 small aquariums, where the species are put individually or in small groups of two or three species. Most species were collected at the Brazilian and [[Rio Grande do Norte]] coast. Species include several kinds of fish (including piranhas), [[sea horse]]s, [[shrimp]]s, [[lobster]]s, [[stingray]]s, [[octopus]], etc. In a refrigerated aquarium, there are a couple of [[penguin]]s.
[[File:Maior cajueiro do mundo 1.jpg|thumb|The world's largest cashew tree]]
[[File:Forte-reis-magos.jpg|thumb|[[Forte dos Reis Magos]], a [[fortress]] built in 1598, is now a museum.]]
;The Largest Cashew Tree in the World
The [[Cashew of Pirangi]] is the biggest [[cashew]] tree in the world. In 1994, it entered the [[Guinness book]] as the tree which covers the largest area, 8,400 square meters;<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/worlds-largest-cashew-tree-maior-cajueiro-do-mundo | title=The World's Largest Cashew Tree }}</ref> it is larger than a typical football pitch. Due to a genetic mutation, the branches of this tree grow outwards rather than upwards; thus when a branch touches the ground, roots are not created, rather a new [[Ramification (botany)|ramification]] starts to grow.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/tourists.html |title=The biggest cashew tree of the world |publisher=Natal-brazil.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120224090634/http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/tourists.html |archive-date=24 February 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> There is even a gazebo located in the cashew, a popular attraction among tourists.

;Forte dos Reis Magos
Forte dos Reis Magos or Fortaleza dos Reis Magos is a medieval [[fortress]] that was the first milestone of the city – founded on 25 December 1599 – on the right side of the bar of the river Potenji (today near the Ponte Newton Navarro). It received its name based on the date of commencement of its construction, 6 January 1598, at Epiphany Catholic calendar.

;Natal City Park
[[File:Mirante à espreita.JPG|thumb|left|[[Natal City Park]] tower and the forest]]
The Natal City Park Dom Nivaldo Monte, or simply the City Park is an urban park. Inaugurated on 21 July 2008, with architectural project of [[Oscar Niemeyer]]. It has a unique, monumental tower, and has a museum and restaurant.

;Festa Junina (Saint John Festival)
[[Festa Junina]] was introduced to Northeastern [[Brazil]] by the [[Portugal|Portuguese]] for whom St John's day (also celebrated as [[Midsummer]] Day in several European countries), on 24 June, is one of the oldest and most popular celebrations of the year. Differently from what happens on the European [[Midsummer]] Day, the festivities in Brazil do not take place during the [[summer solstice]] but during the tropical [[winter solstice]]. The festivities traditionally begin after 12 June, on the eve of St Anthony's day, and last until the 29th, which is Saint Peter's day. During these fifteen days, there are [[bonfires]], [[fireworks]], and folk dancing in the streets. Typical foods and beverages are served. As during Carnival, these festivities involve wearing costumes (in this case, peasant costumes), dancing, heavy drinking, and visual spectacles (fireworks display and folk dancing). Similar to what happens on [[Midsummer]] and St John's Day in Europe, [[bonfires]] are a central part of these festivities in Brazil. Festa Junina in other cities of the state, especially in the city of [[Mossoró]] which gathers thousands of people during the month of June.

;Newton Navarro bridge
[[File:TeatroAlbertoMaranhao.JPG|thumb|[[Alberto Maranhão Theatre]] (on the inside of it) is part of the Historic Center.]]

The [[Newton Navarro bridge]] is one of the highest bridges in Brazil and has over a gorgeous sunset seen from [[Potengi River]]. It makes the connection with the beaches of the north coast of the state. In addition to unburden the city traffic, the bridge became a tourist attraction because of its large size.

;Historic Center
The Natal Historic Center it comprises an area of approximately {{convert|201278|m²|2|abbr=on}}, which are included 150 buildings of various styles, including colonial architecture, neoclassical, art-deco and modernist. This area comprises the districts of Ribeira and [[Cidade Alta (Natal)|Cidade Alta]], which are the oldest neighborhoods of the city, which have characteristics of the first centuries of the history of Brazil, highlighting important aspects of Portuguese colonization.

;Barreira do Inferno
The [[Barreira do Inferno Launch Center]] is the first base to launch rockets from Brazil. With the construction of another center in Brazil, today, it only launches rockets, small and medium businesses and is open to visitation of tourists to know the rockets, but must make an appointment to visit the center.

;Parrachos de Maracajaú
[[File:Maracajaú.jpg|thumb|left|[[Maracajaú]] beach]]
The Parrachos de [[Maracajaú]] of coral reefs are the seven kilometers ({{convert|7|km|abbr=off|disp=output only}}) of beach in Maracajaú, forming natural pools. Occupy an area of {{convert|13|km|0|abbr=on}}, excellent for snorkeling in the coral diving underwater that, at low tide, touch the surface. It is one of the places in Brazil where the sea is the most crystalline and suitable for diving. The beach of Maracajaú is {{convert|60|km|0|abbr=on}} from Natal. In Maracajaú also a water park, the Ma-Noa Park.

;Genipabu
[[File:Dunas de Genipabu.jpg|thumb|right|[[Genipabu]] dunes]]
[[Genipabu]] (or Jenipabu) is a beach, a complex of dunes, a lagoon and an area of environmental protection (APA) located in Natal capital city, one of the most famous post-cards of the Brazilian state Rio Grande do Norte. It is a huge dunes and a lagoon of fresh water, with strong attraction for tourism. The region has a good infrastructure of hotels, inns, restaurants, [[beach]] [[tent]]s, tours of "buggies", rafts and dromedaries. It is played in the dunes around the lake called "esquibunda" stakeholders sit down the dunes on top of planks of wood, to dive in the waters of the lagoon. The tours of "[[Dune buggy|buggies]]" are offered in the local "with emotion" or "no emotion", according to the degree of risk, it is recommended, however, are chosen accredited professionals responsible for security not only of tourists, but the middle environment.

;Ponta Negra and Via Costeira
[[File:Morro do Careca Natal Brasil.jpg|thumb|left|[[Ponta Negra]] Beach]]
[[File:Via_Costeira,_Natal_(RN).jpg|thumb|right|[[Via Costeira]]]]
[[Ponta Negra]] is a famous beach and a neighborhood located in the Brazilian city of Natal in the state of Rio Grande do Norte. In the extreme south of the beach, is located in [[Morro do Careca]] (Bald Hill), a large dune. The extreme north of the beach, is located at Via Costeira (Coastal Way), a road in the middle of coastal dunes that connect the various beaches of the city. The beach is completely urbanized and there are several points shops, restaurants, bars and night clubs. The sand itself is lined with "tents", or huts, that serve food and drinks to beachgoers day and night. A common sight on the beach are "jangadas", simple, multicolored, local fishing boats typical of northeastern Brazil. The [[Via Costeira]] (Coastal Way) is a great avenue built next to the dunes and beaches of the city, including making the connection of several beaches in the state.[[File:Praia Pipa.jpg|thumb|[[Pipa Beach]]]]

;Pipa Beach
The [[Pipa Beach]] is located {{convert|85|km|0|abbr=off}} from Natal and completely away from urban centers. A beach Paradise that attracts thousands of tourists every year.

==Culture==

Carnival in Natal, known as "[[Carnatal]]", is one of the largest off-season carnivals in [[Brazil]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/carnatal.html |title=Carnatal |publisher=Natal-brazil.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120511031222/http://www.natal-brazil.com/entertainment/carnatal.html |archive-date=11 May 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> Carnatal takes place sometime in November or December, in the streets around the Arena das Dunas Stadium (traffic around the stadium changes radically during Carnatal days). The transit department isolates about {{convert|3|km|0|abbr=off|spell=on}} of streets,<ref>[http://www.natalriograndedonorte.com/natal-carnatal-best-off-season-carnival-party-in-brazil/ Carnatal Organization] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140814085618/http://www.natalriograndedonorte.com/natal-carnatal-best-off-season-carnival-party-in-brazil/ |date=14 August 2014 }} 12 June 2014</ref> creating a ring, along which the party takes place. During the days of party, a huge truck (called "[[trio elétrico]]"), with a [[music ensemble|band]] on the top and sound boxes all around, drives slowly along the streets. The crowd follows the trio elétrico singing, dancing, jumping to the [[sound]] of the music.

Natal is also home to the "Mada", a national festival of [[pop rock]] national, and the "Verão de Todos", a summer festival.

The patron saint of the city is [[Our Lady of Presentation]].

===Highways===
[[File:Av. Sen. Salgado Filho, Natal, Brazil.jpg|thumb|left|[[BR-101]] highway as Salgado Filho Avenue in December]]
Federal Highway [[BR-101]] is the most important access to Natal, coming from the [[Southern Brazil|South of Brazil]], through the boundary with the municipality of Parnamirim. For those who come from the State of [[Ceará]], the principle access is by the Federal Highway BR-304, through the boundary with the municipality of Macaíba, where you pick up BR-226 taking people to Natal. Leaving Natal, an important access to the southern Potiguar coast is the "[[Sun]] Route" (Rota do Sol) as RN-063 is known by, and which takes you to the [[beach]]es of Pirangi, Búzios, Tabatinga, up to the municipality of Nísia Floresta. The North Shore is accessible from the [[Newton Navarro bridge]], following the Praia do Forte to Genipabu, and the Igapó Bridge, following the district of Igapó by BR-101 to Touros, and by BR-406 to Macau.
[[File:Ponte Newton Navarro - Natal, Brazil.jpg|thumb|right|[[Newton Navarro bridge]]]]
Natal is connected to all the 167 [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipalities]] of Rio Grande do Norte and dozens of locations and Potiguar districts, through the Passenger [[Bus Terminal]] of Natal (Terminal Rodoviário de Passageiros de Natal), located in the Eastern Zone of the city. All the accesses to the countryside of the state are through the Metropolitan region of Natal, composed of the municipalities of Ceará-Mirim, Emaús, Extremoz, Macaíba, Monte Alegre, Nísia Floresta, Parnamirim e São Gonçalo do Amarante. [[Bus]]es with direct destinations to various Brazilian capitals also leave from the Passenger Bus Terminal of Natal. The cities with regular weekly schedules are: Belo Horizonte, Fortaleza, Goiânia, João Pessoa, Palmas, Recife, Salvador, São Luiz e Teresina.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.viagemdeferias.com/natal/basico/chegando.php |title=Access – Natal |publisher=Viagemdeferias.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120308215831/http://www.viagemdeferias.com/natal/basico/chegando.php |archive-date=8 March 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Bus===
Buses are the cheapest way to move around Natal. Passes or magnetic cards can be purchased beforehand, but it is easiest to pay the conductor. There are large buses, micro buses<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.clasf.com.br/transporte-alternativo-com-arrendamento-em-natal-7145108/ |title=Anúncios Classificados Grátis no Brasil &#124; CLASF |access-date=17 December 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222111001/http://www.clasf.com.br/transporte-alternativo-com-arrendamento-em-natal-7145108/ |archive-date=22 December 2015 |url-status=live }}</ref> and vans, driving along the same streets, charging the same fares.

All the main touristic spots within Natal are well served by buses. People may expect to travel standing up on the big buses (not so with micros and vans). Typical waiting time is 15 min during the peak hours, up to one hour.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.virtualtourist.com/travel/South_America/Brazil/Estado_do_Rio_Grande_do_Norte/Natal-1510077/Transportation-Natal-BR-1.html |title=Bus in Natal |publisher=Virtualtourist.com |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100501033007/http://www.virtualtourist.com/travel/South_America/Brazil/Estado_do_Rio_Grande_do_Norte/Natal-1510077/Transportation-Natal-BR-1.html |archive-date=1 May 2010 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Taxi===
There are plenty of [[taxicab|taxi]]s in the city. Taxis are white cars and have a red plate. Fares are the same, regardless of how new and comfortable the car is.
[[Fare]]s are regulated by the government. From 10:00&nbsp;pm to 6:00&nbsp;am at weekdays, and all hours at weekends (and, depending on a decree by the mayor, all times during the month of December), there is a 20% increase in fare prices,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://natal.olx.com.br/taxi-iid-4846031 |title=Natal – Taxi Transport |publisher=Natal.olx.com.br |date=7 May 2007 |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090614184630/http://natal.olx.com.br/taxi-iid-4846031 |archive-date=14 June 2009 |url-status=live }}</ref> this is indicated by a number 2 showing in the fare meter. There are several tele-taxis companies in Natal.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guiamais.com.br/Results.aspx?idi=3&ipa=16&ica=5309&ies=591&nes=RIO-GRANDE-DO-NORTE&ici=13050&nci=NATAL&txb=T%C3%A1xi |title=Taxi in Natal |publisher=Guiamais.com.br |access-date=25 June 2012}}</ref>

===Buggy===
[[File:Buggues_em_Genipabu_(5303848087).jpg|thumb|right|[[Dune buggy]]]]

===2014 FIFA World Cup===
Natal was one of the host cities of the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]], for which [[Brazil]] was the host nation. The [[Dunas Arena]] was built in place of the old [[Machadão]]<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.natalrn.com.br/estadio-machadao/|title=Estádio Machadão|date=12 November 2019|website=Natal RN|language=pt-BR|access-date=13 November 2019}}</ref> stadium.<ref>{{cite web |title=DEADLINK |work=Jornal de Hoje |url=http://www.jornaldehoje.com.br/portal/noticia.php?id=14665 |access-date=1 June 2009 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305022438/http://jornaldehoje.com.br/portal/noticia.php?id=14665 |archive-date=5 March 2016 }}</ref> It is building a new [[airport]], [[Greater Natal International Airport]], to be the largest airport in Latin America.<ref name="correiodatarde.com.br">{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-22092 |title=Aeroporto de São Gonçalo: pistas estão garantidas, mas terminal ainda não |publisher=Correiodatarde.com.br |date=25 September 2007 |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121105230326/http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-22092 |archive-date=5 November 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> It is also investing in a [[public transport]] system using the system of [[light rail]] called [[:pt:VLT de Natal|VLT de Natal]], and other projects of infrastructure.

[[David Beckham]] had announced plans for a [[David Beckham Academy|football academy]] in Natal, called the [[Beckham World of Sport]], to coincide with the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uv10.com/news/Beckham-puts-Brazil-on-the-map_18450967.php |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209084913/http://www.uv10.com/news/Beckham-puts-Brazil-on-the-map_18450967.php |url-status=dead |archive-date=9 February 2013 |title=Beckham in Natal |publisher=Uv10.com |date=1 February 2008 |access-date=25 June 2012 }}</ref>

===11th World Maxibasketball Championship===
Natal will be the first Brazilian city to host a World Maxibasketball Championship. The 11th World Maxibasketball Championship will occur in July 2011,<ref>[http://www.fimba.net/proximos_eng.htm] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090223210428/http://www.fimba.net/proximos_eng.htm|date=23 February 2009}}</ref> winning among the cities which also wanted to host the event: [[Punta del Este]] in [[Uruguay]]; [[Vancouver]] in [[Canada]] and [[Eugene, Oregon|Eugene]] in the United States.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fbbm.org/clip.php?consulta=sim&atual=Clipping&codcli=524 |title=Federaусo Brasileira de Basquetebol Master :: |publisher=FBBM |date=24 October 2007 |access-date=25 June 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120111111626/http://www.fbbm.org/clip.php?consulta=sim&atual=Clipping&codcli=524 |archive-date=11 January 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> The facilities of the modern Nélio Dias [[gym]]nasium should be used to house the event that will bring to the capital of Rio Grande do Norte about 3,500 athletes from around the world.<ref>[http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/112497.html] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110721021114/http://tribunadonorte.com.br/noticias/112497.html|date=21 July 2011}}</ref>
