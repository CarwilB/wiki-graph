'''Naranjito''' is a [[Municipality (Honduras)|municipality]] in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]], Honduras. It covers an area of {{cvt|130.5|km2}} and had a population of 11,901 inhabitants according to the 2013 national census. The economy is dependent primarily on [[agriculture]] especially [[coffee]], and [[livestock]] rearing.

== History ==
Naranjito was officially founded in 1820, and was elevated as a municipality in 1844. On 28 December 1931, the Naranjito district was created consisting of the municipalities of Protection, Atima and El Naranjito.<ref name="Edu">{{cite web|title=Municipio de Naranjito, Santa Bárbara|url=https://www.edu-honduras.info/Los-Departamentos/16-Santa-Barbara/Naranjito.html|work=Edu-Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026|lang=es}}</ref>

== Geography ==
Naranjito is located in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] in Honduras.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/|title=Administrative divisions of Honduras|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Protección]] to the south, [[Atima]] and [[San Luis, Honduras|San Luis]] to the north, [[San Nicolás, Santa Bárbara|San Nicolás]], [[Trinidad, Santa Bárbara|Trinidad]], and [[San José, Copán|San Jose]] to the west, and [[Lepaera]] to the east.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red">{{cite web|url=https://redhonduras.hn/en/geography/municipality-naranjito/|title=Municipality of Naranjito|work=Red Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|130.5|km2}} and has an average elevation of {{cvt|930|m}} above sea level.<ref name="Edu"/><ref>{{Cite web|title=Naranjito en la region de Santa Bárbara - Municipio y alcaldía de Honduras|url=https://www.municipio.hn/municipio-naranjito.html|access-date=4 August 2022|work=Municipio.hn}}</ref> It is watered by the [[Jicatuyo River]]. There are several [[hot springs]] located near the municipality.<ref name="Red"/><ref name="Edu"/>

== Demographics ==
At the time of the 2013 census, Naranjito had a total population of 11,901 inhabitants, of whom 5,948 (50.0%) were male and 5,953 (50.0%) were female.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=20}}<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/santa_b%C3%A1rbara/1614__naranjito/|title=Naranjito|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=20}} The population increased from 10,367 inhabitants in 2001 to 11,901 in 2013.<ref name="CP"/> The municipality had an urban population of 4,214 (35.4%) and a rural population of 7,687 (64.6%).<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=20}} By broad age group, 35.8% of the population were aged 0–14, 57.9% were aged 15–64, and 6.3% were aged 65 and over. The median age was 20.9 years and the mean age was 26.2 years.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}} Of the population, 0.25% were classified as [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and rest as non-indigenous.<ref name="CP"/> Among the population aged 15 and over, the illiteracy rate was 30.6%, which was higher than the departmental average of 22.2%.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}}

== Culture and economy ==
The economy of Naranjito is primarily based on [[agriculture]], especially the cultivation of [[coffee]], [[maize]], and beans. [[Livestock]] rearing and small-scale commerce also contribute to the local economy.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/> Naranjito celebrates its annual festival on 15 August in honour of its patron [[Dormition of the Mother of God|Virgin of the transit]].<ref name="Edu"/>
