'''Nanchoc District''' is one of thirteen [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of [[San Miguel Province]] in the [[Cajamarca Region]] of [[Peru]].<ref>"[[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]." [https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/]. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> In 2017, the district had an area of {{convert|359|sqkm|sqmi}} and a population of 1,368.<ref>"City Population, [http://www.citypopulation.de/en/peru/cajamarca/admin/san_miguel/061108__nanchoc/], accessed 20 November 2020</ref> The capital of the district is the town of Nanchoc which had a population of 332 in 2017.<ref>"City Population,"  [http://www.citypopulation.de/en/peru/cajamarca/san_miguel/0611080001__nanchoc/], accessed 20 November 2020.</ref>  The Nanchoc River, a tributary of the [[Zaña Valley|Zaña River]], bisects the district.

Nanchoc is located in an irrigated and cultivated valley about {{convert|1|km|mile}} wide and {{convert|60|km|mile}} from the [[Pacific Ocean]] to the west. Between the valley and the ocean is a low range of mountains and the Peruvian coastal desert. East of Nanchoc the [[Andes]] rise sharply and the greater precipitation in the Andes feeds the Nanchoc River and its tributaries, permitting irrigated agriculture to flourish in the valley.<ref>Google Earth</ref>

==History==
Nanchoc district was created 2 December 1958 by Law No. 13039 del 2, during the second government of President [[Manuel Prado Ugarteche]].<ref>Leyes Congresso", [http://www.leyes.congreso.gob.pe/Documentos/Leyes/13039.pdf]. Accessed 12 October 2019</ref> At the time the district included the population centers of Carahuasi, Bolívar, El Espino, Trigal, La Aventuraza, El Diamante, La Tambora and Tingues. Most of these communities were separated from Nanchoc district in 1989 and placed in [[Bolívar District, San Miguel|Bolivar District]].<ref>[http://www.muni-sanmiguel.gob.pe/index.php/ciudad/distritos-de-san-miguel/nanchoc Sitio web provincial] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141218223929/http://www.muni-sanmiguel.gob.pe/index.php/ciudad/distritos-de-san-miguel/nanchoc |date=2014-12-18 }}. Accessed 12 Oct 2019.</ref>

==Archaeology==
{{see also|Zaña Valley}}
On the western side of the Nanchoc river, about {{convert|3|km|mile}} from the town of Nanchoc, archaeologist [[Tom Dillehay]] found evidence of the oldest known irrigation canals in the Americas, [[radiocarbon]] dated to at least 3400 BCE and possibly as old as 4700 BCE.  The canals built by the people of Nanchoc at that time were utilized to irrigate crops such as [[peanut]]s, [[Squash (plant)|squash]], [[manioc]], and [[chenopodium|chenopods]], a relative of [[Quinoa]].<ref name="Dillehay, 2005">{{cite journal |last1=Dillehay |first1=Tom D. |last2=Eling, Jr. |first2=Herbert H. |last3=Rossen |first3=Jack |title=Preceramic irrigation canals in the Peruvian Andes |url=https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/102/47/17241.full.pdf |journal=Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America |year=2005 |volume=102 |issue=47 |pages=17241–17244 |publisher=National Academy of Science |doi=10.1073/pnas.0508583102 |pmid=16284247 |pmc=1288011 |access-date=20 November 2020 |doi-access=free |bibcode=2005PNAS..10217241D }}</ref>
