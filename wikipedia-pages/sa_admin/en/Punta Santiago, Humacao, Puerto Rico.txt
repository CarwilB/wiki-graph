'''Punta Santiago''' is a [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrio]] and fishing community located in the municipality of [[Humacao, Puerto Rico]]. Its population in 2020 was 3,716.<ref name="Pico_1969">{{cite book |last1=Picó |first1=Rafael |last2=Buitrago de Santiago |first2=Zayda |last3=Berrios |first3=Hector H. |title=Nueva geografía de Puerto Rico: física, económica, y social, por Rafael Picó. Con la colaboración de Zayda Buitrago de Santiago y Héctor H. Berrios. |url=https://archive.org/details/nuevageografad00pic/page/247|publisher=San Juan Editorial Universitaria, Universidad de Puerto Rico,1969}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=Census profile: Punta Santiago barrio, Humacao Municipio, PR |url=http://censusreporter.org/profiles/06000US7206966535-punta-santiago-barrio-humacao-municipio-pr/ |access-date=2023-08-13 |website=Census Reporter |language=en}}</ref> Located in the estuary of Humacao, Punta Santiago is famous for its fishing heritage, its iconic pier and [[Cayo Santiago]], popularly known as Monkey Island ([[Puerto Rican Spanish|Spanish]]: ''Isla de de los Monos'') after its [[Rhesus macaque]] population. The barrio is also the home of the [[Humacao Nature Reserve]] and the Punta Santiago Beach and Vacation Area.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Punta Santiago Reserva Natural de Humacao |url=https://www.discoverpuertorico.com/es/profile/punta-santiago-reserva-natural-de-humacao/7844 |access-date=2023-08-13 |website=Discover Puerto Rico |language=es}}</ref> Punta Santiago is one of the most vulnerable barrios to [[flood]]ing and complete destruction whenever [[Tropical cyclone|hurricanes]] enter Puerto Rico through the area of Humacao.

==History==
[[File:FMIB 33288 Fishing Boats and Fishermen's Homes, Punta Santiago.jpeg|left|thumb|Fishing boats (1899)]]
The fishing village of Punta Santiago was originally called '''Playa de Humacao''' as it was the closest beach to [[Humacao barrio-pueblo|Humacao Pueblo]], the historic and administrative downtown area of Humacao.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Estado Libre Asociado de Puerto Rico |first=Junta de Planificación |title=Municipio de Humacao Memoria Suplementaria al Mapa de Límites del Municipio y sus Barrios |publisher=Oficina del Gobernador |year=1955 |edition=52 |location=San Juan, Puerto Rico |pages=40–41 |language=Spanish}}</ref> The community of Punta Santiago used to be part of two former barrios of Humacao, one called Humacao Playa and the other called Mabú. By the end of the 19th century barrio Mabú had been eliminated and incorporated into barrio Playa, now renamed Punta Santiago after the geographical [[headland]] or [[Peninsula|point]] (''punta'') where the fishing village is located.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2010-09-07 |title=Municipio de Humacao - EnciclopediaPR |url=https://enciclopediapr.org/content/municipio-de-humacao/,%20https://enciclopediapr.org/content/municipio-de-humacao/ |access-date=2023-08-13 |language=en-US}}</ref>

Since December 1938, [[Cayo Santiago]], located immediately off Punta Santiago, has been the home to a population of free roaming [[Rhesus macaque]]s imported from [[India]] by the [[School of Tropical Medicine (Puerto Rico)|School of Tropical Medicine]] in [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], the [[Columbia University Vagelos College of Physicians and Surgeons|Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons]] and the [[University of Puerto Rico]].

The area where the Humacao nature reserve is located today saw a number of bunkers and defensive infrastructure built during the [[World War II|Second World War]], including a lookout post at the top of ''El Morrillo'' hill.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Geocaching |title=Geocaching - The Official Global GPS Cache Hunt Site |url=http://www.geocaching.com/ |access-date=2021-12-15 |website=www.geocaching.com |language=en}}</ref> This area which contains a number of [[mangrove]] and [[Pterocarpus]] forests was later proclaimed a wildlife refuge in 1986.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Reserva Natural Reserva Natural de Humacao — Puerto DRNA "Navega por el ambiente" |url=https://www.drna.pr.gov/historico/oficinas/oficina-de-prensa-y-comunicaciones/galeria-fotos/reserva-natural-reserva-natural-de-humacao/ |access-date=2021-12-15 |website=www.drna.pr.gov}}</ref>

=== Hurricane Maria ===
[[Hurricane Maria]] hit Puerto Rico on September 20, 2017. The [[Eye (cyclone)|hurricane eye]] entered the island close to [[Punta Yeguas]] in [[Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Yabucoa]], not far from Punta Santiago. The barrio was particularly vulnerable to the associated [[storm surge]]s. According to Father José Colón, the local Catholic priest, the hurricane caused the total flooding of Punta Santiago.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.americamagazine.org/politics-society/2017/12/27/what-hurricanes-revealed-about-puerto-rico|title=What the hurricanes revealed about Puerto Rico|date=December 27, 2017|website=America Magazine}}</ref>
{{blockquote|
"The town was flooded from all directions, from the ocean, rain and nearby rivers..."
}}Punta Santiago was one of the most impacted communities in the aftermath of the hurricane. It was the location of the viral image of an [[SOS]] message written on one of the roads of the community.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2018-07-05 |title=Quedó nuevo Punta Santiago en Humacao |url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/notas/quedo-nuevo-punta-santiago-en-humacao/ |access-date=2023-08-13 |website=Primera Hora |language=es}}</ref> The Punta Santiago Vacation Center was greatly affected by the hurricane, and it remained abandoned by 2023.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2023-04-03 |title=Centro vacacional de Punta Santiago: paraíso perdido y en el limbo |url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/gobierno-politica/notas/centro-vacacional-de-punta-santiago-paraiso-perdido-y-en-el-limbo/ |access-date=2023-08-13 |website=Primera Hora |language=spanish}}</ref><gallery mode="packed" heights="160" caption="Effects of Hurricane Maria">

==Flood zone==
In 2019, updated flood zone maps show that because of its location- where most cyclones enter the island, Humacao is one of the most vulnerable areas of Puerto Rico.<ref name="León_END_Flood_Zones">{{cite web |last1=Alvarado León |first1=Gerardo E. |title=Sobre 250,000 estructuras están en zonas inundables [More than 250,000 structures in flood zones] |url=https://jp.pr.gov/Portals/0/Noticias/Nuevo%20d%C3%ADa%20-%20Zonas%20Inundables%20%205.22.19.pdf?ver=2019-05-22-172159-483 |website=Junta de Planificación - Gobierno de Puerto Rico |publisher=El Nuevo Día |accessdate=3 July 2019 |language=es}}</ref> Humacao was working on flood mitigation plans and shared that its barrios located on the coast; [[Antón Ruíz]], Punta Santiago, [[Río Abajo, Humacao, Puerto Rico|Río Abajo]], [[Buena Vista, Humacao, Puerto Rico|Buena Vista]] and [[Candelero Abajo]] barrios, are prone to flooding and in danger of being completed destroyed by a hurricane.<ref name="Quintana_END_Mitigation">{{cite web |last1=Solano Quintana |first1=Bárbara |title=Piden participación ciudadana para revisión del plan de mitigación de Humacao |url=https://jp.pr.gov/Portals/0/Prensa/Noticias/Plan%20de%20Mitigacion-05022019040248.pdf?ver=2019-05-03-140022-567 |via=Junta de Planificación Gobierno de Puerto Rico |newspaper=El Nuevo Día |accessdate=3 July 2019 |language=es}}</ref>
