'''Monteverde'''<ref name="dta2017" /> is the twelfth [[Cantons of Costa Rica|canton]] of the [[Puntarenas Province|Puntarenas]] [[Provinces of Costa Rica|province]] of [[Costa Rica]],<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref> located in the [[Cordillera de Tilarán]] (Tilarán range). Roughly a four-hour drive from the [[Costa Rican Central Valley|Central Valley]], Monteverde is one of the country's major [[ecotourism]] destinations, with the [[Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve|Reserva Biológica Bosque Nuboso Monteverde]] (Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve) being the largest, in addition to several other natural attractions which draw considerable numbers of tourists and [[naturalists]], both from Costa Rica and abroad.

[[National Geographic Society|''National Geographic'']] has called the Monteverde Cloud Forest  "the jewel in the crown of [[cloud forest]] reserves".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://traveler.nationalgeographic.com/books-excerpts/top-ten-costa-rica-text |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090713051011/http://traveler.nationalgeographic.com/books-excerpts/top-ten-costa-rica-text |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 13, 2009 |title=Top Ten Costa Rica – National Geographic Traveler |publisher=Traveler.nationalgeographic.com }}</ref> ''[[Newsweek]]'' ranked Monteverde the world's No. 14 "place to remember before it disappears"; by popular vote in Costa Rica, Monteverde was deemed one of the seven natural wonders of the nation, along with [[Isla del Coco]], [[Volcán Arenal]], [[Cerro Chirripó]], [[Celeste River|Río Celeste]], [[Tortuguero National Park|Tortuguero]], and [[Poás Volcano|Volcán Poás]].<ref name="7 Maravillas" />

'''Santa Elena'''<!-- Bolded as this term redirects here --> is the head town in the region, serving as the area's principal tourist and visitors' hub. Together with the nearby neighborhood of Cerro Plano, and the community of Monteverde proper, visitors have diverse options for accommodations, dining, guided tours, and more when visiting the area's numerous reserves and natural attractions.<ref>{{cite web|language=es-ES|title=Viajes a Costa Rica|url=https://archive.org/details/monteverde|website=archive.org – ES}}<!-- auto-translated by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

==Toponymy==
''Monteverde'' translates directly to "green mountain", in English, a tribute to the region's wealth of natural beauty. For comparison, the word's French equivalent would be ''vermont'', or ''montanha verde'' in [[Portuguese language|Portuguese]]. Officially, the name is '''Monte Verde''' — written as two words.<ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica |date=8 March 2017 |publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional |isbn=978-9977-58-477-5 |url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref>

==Climate==
Monteverde primarily consists of [[tropical montane cloud forest]]. At an elevation of roughly {{cvt|1400|m|ft}} above sea level, the climate is never hot, as [[trade winds]] from the [[Caribbean Sea]] consistently blow across the mountains. The region's mean annual temperature is {{cvt|18|°C|°F}} 17). Annual [[precipitation]] averages around {{cvt|3000|mm|in|0}}, and ambient [[humidity]] oscillates between 74% and 97% (Nadkarni 2000: 34).<ref name="Nadkarni" />

==History==

Various [[Pre-Columbian era|pre-Columbian]] artifacts testify to the existence of early populations of [[Clovis culture|Clovis]] [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Native Americans]], who once farmed in villages in the area and hunted the forests, ca. [[30th century BC|3000 BC]]. From around [[33rd century BC|3300 BC]] to [[20th century BC|2000 BC]],  nearby tribes of the [[Arenal District, Tilarán|Arenal]] area experienced a population decline, but re-established villages in the region between 2000 and [[500 BC]]. Agriculture intensified in the centuries between 500 BC and [[300|300 CE]], with simple [[chiefdom]] societies replacing the formerly smaller, [[tribal]] settlements; some level of [[deforestation]] accompanied this rise in population, [[farm]]ing and horticulture. Early stone [[Foundation (engineering)|foundations]] dating to this period can also be found. [[Jade]], and [[Costa Rican jade tradition|creating valuable objects with the stone]], was a prized material and defining characteristic of these villages. From 300 to [[800|800 CE]], complex chiefdoms supplanted simpler chiefdoms as more intricate villages appeared; along with early modern examples of [[cemeteries]], businesses and [[public squares]], [[goldsmith]]s were seen as extremely valuable.  intertribal trade and conflict. Around 1300, a general decline in population occurred, possibly due to the [[Arenal Volcano]]'s increased activity.<ref name='Nadkarni'>Nadkarni, Nalini. Monteverde. 1. Berlin: Oxford University, 1999.</ref>{{rp|408–409}}

After the [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]] made landfall in 1502, Costa Rica endured two generations of [[warfare]]. Total [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] populations in what is now Costa Rica fell drastically, from an estimated 400,000 pre-European contact to 80,000 within less than 50 years of contact. Countless innocent people lost their lives for a multitude of reasons, from deplorable living conditions brought about by [[Slavery|enslavement]] (and harsh forced labor and servitude) to outright sadistic punishments, and even murder, of anyone who challenged the [[conquistador]]es. Many thousands more quickly succumbed to the vast array of Old World [[pathogen]]s and [[Infection|infectious diseases]] that their systems had no [[Immunity (medicine)|immunity]] against, including diseases such as [[measles]], [[influenza]], [[chickenpox]], [[bubonic plague]], [[typhus]], [[scarlet fever]], [[pneumonia]], [[syphilis]] and [[malaria]]. However, in a somewhat cruel "twist of fate", unlike neighboring [[Nicaragua]] and [[Panama]], Costa Rica did not yield considerable amounts of [[Ethnic groups in Central America|indigenous]] labor or mineral resources, and thus the region experienced [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|colonization]] at a much slower rate than many other Spanish colonies.{{Citation needed|date=April 2026}}

===Early 20th century===

During the first three decades of the 20th century, from roughly 1900–1930, [[Creole peoples|Creole]] populations arrived in small numbers to the area that is now Monteverde; many men worked within the [[Guacimal District|Guacimal]] [[Gold mining|gold mines]]; many more, still, with their wives and families, provided these miners and their greater communities with needed goods and services. Some families also settled in the lower, warmer valley of nearby San Luís.<ref name='Nadkarni'/>{{rp|353}}

===1950s and forward===
[[File:Cuaqueros cr.jpg|thumb|276x276px|Quakers in Monteverde, 1950]]
What is now considered Monteverde was founded by [[Religious Society of Friends|Quakers]] from the United States whose pacifist values led them to defy the [[Conscription in the United States|American draft]] before the [[Korean War]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Gross |first=David M. |year=2014 |title=99 Tactics of Successful Tax Resistance Campaigns |publisher=Picket Line Press |isbn=978-1490572741 |page=85}}</ref> The majority of these settlers hailed from [[Fairhope, Alabama]], and included some non-Quaker [[Pacifism in the United States|pacifists]] and [[Conscientious objection in the United States|conscientious objectors]]. The spokesman of the group was Hubert Mendenhall, a dairyman who had visited Costa Rica in 1949 as part of a farmer's tour.<ref>[http://www.monteverde.org/ Monteverde Costa Rica Nonprofit Organizations]</ref> These Quakers and pacifists chose the area for its cool climate, which would facilitate dairy farming, due to the country's [[Article 12 of the Constitution of Costa Rica|non-violent, army-free constitution]], and its friendly Costa Rican inhabitants.<ref>[http://www.cct.or.cr/reserva_monteverde/historia.php Centro Científico Tropical – Historia Monteverde] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081002213427/http://www.cct.or.cr/reserva_monteverde/historia.php |date=2008-10-02 }}</ref> The Quakers stewarded and farmed a large tract of land, part of which they eventually set aside for conservation, today the Reserva Biológica Bosque Nuboso Monteverde (Monteverde Cloud Forest Biological Reserve). This has become a major tourist attraction.

===2005 bank raid===
On March 8, 2005, a group of three armed Nicaraguan men raided and attempted to rob the Santa Elena branch of the state [[Banco Nacional de Costa Rica|National Bank]] (''Banco Nacional''). A guard killed two of the armed men. However, one assailant held bank customers hostage for 28 hours. When authorities successfully attempted to retake the bank, a senior police officer and nine civilians died, and only one of the attackers survived.<ref name="BBCNewsBank">{{cite news |title=Nine die in Costa Rica bank siege |date=2005-03-10 |publisher=BBC News |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/americas/4334659.stm |access-date=2007-08-14 }}</ref><ref name="InsideCostaRicaBank">{{cite news |title=Two Faces of Erlyn Hurtado, Dead Brothers Lying in Morgue Unclaimed |date= 2005-03-21 |publisher=Inside Costa Rica |url=http://insidecostarica.com/dailynews/2005/march/21/nac04.htm |access-date=2007-08-14 }}</ref> This event raised tensions between Nicaraguans and Costa Ricans nationwide and prompted higher security in many national banks.

===Canton creation===
As a district of [[Puntarenas (canton)|Puntarenas canton]], Monteverde had its own local government, a Municipal District Council, due to the long distance from the offices of the municipality of Puntarenas canton. As a result, its inhabitants pushed for Monteverde to become its own canton.

On 4 August 2021, the [[Legislative Assembly of Costa Rica|Legislative Assembly]] approved the file N.º 21618, which was then signed into law N.º 10019 by President [[Carlos Alvarado Quesada]] on 29 September 2021,<ref>{{cite web |title=Monteverde es oficialmente el cantón 83 de Costa Rica |url=https://www.presidencia.go.cr/comunicados/2021/09/monteverde-es-oficialmente-el-canton-83-de-costa-rica |work=Presidencia de la República de Costa Rica |accessdate=30 September 2021}}</ref> which segregates the district from Puntarenas canton to become Monteverde canton. However, due to law N.º 6068<ref>{{cite web |title=Declara invariable la División Territorial Administrativa de la República, durante los catorce meses anteriores a todas las elecciones nacionales de Presidente y Vicepresidentes y reforma Código Electoral N° 6068 |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=898&nValor3=963&strTipM=TC |work=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=30 September 2021}}</ref> the change was deferred to after 3 April 2022 as it was not possible to change the Territorial Administrative Division fourteen months before elections, which was the case due to the second round of the [[2022 Costa Rican general election]] held on that date.

The canton voted for its first municipal government and mayor in the [[2024 Costa Rican municipal elections]]. [[PRSC]] won the local election.<ref>{{Cite web | title=Municipal Election Results Costa Rica 2024 - SensorialSunsets | url=https://www.sensorialsunsets.com/en/municipal-election-results-costa-rica-2024/ | access-date=2025-10-05 | website=www.sensorialsunsets.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Díaz Zeledón |first1=Natalia |title=Monteverde: nuevo cantón prepara cambios de leyes, presupuesto, bandera y santa patrona |url=https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/monteverde-nuevo-canton-prepara-cambios-de-leyes-presupuesto-bandera-y-santa-patrona |access-date=7 August 2021 |work=Semanario Universidad |date=6 August 2021}}</ref>

===Today===
[[File:Monteverde, Costa Rica centre.JPG|right|thumb|Santa Elena, Monteverde's larger neighbor and primary provider of goods and lodging]]
In recent years, the area's rapidly increasing numbers of tourists has brought a sizable influx of Costa Ricans from other towns and cities. Now, an estimated 250,000 tourists visit Monteverde a year. Improved goods and services, including partially paved roads, have arrived in recent years. In 2007, Costa Ricans voted Monteverde one of Costa Rica's Seven Wonders, along with [[Cocos Island|Isla del Coco]], [[Tortuguero Conservation Area|Tortuguero]], [[Arenal Volcano]], [[Mount Chirripó|Cerro Chirripó]], [[Celeste River|Rio Celeste]] and [[Poás Volcano]].<ref name="7 Maravillas" />

==Education==
The area has several public grade and middle schools. These include the Escuela Santa Elena, the Escuela Cerro Plano, and two schools in San Luis. The Colegio San Rafael and Colegio Técnico Professional (also known as the Colegio Santa Elena) are responsible for the majority of secondary education in the area.

Private education is fairly robust in the Monteverde region. In 1951, the Quakers constructed a [[Monthly meeting|Quaker Meeting]] house, which served as classrooms and a general store.<ref>[http://www.mfschool.org/community/history.htm Monteverde Friends School&#91;http://www.mfschool.org&#93; – Our Community – History] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080509162315/http://www.mfschool.org/community/history.htm |date=2008-05-09 }}</ref> This became known as Monteverde Friends School (MFS) and today offers bilingual education to Pre-K through 12th grade to roughly 120 students.<ref>[http://www.mfschool.org/about/index.htm Monteverde Friends School – About MFS – Overview] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080611142634/http://www.mfschool.org/about/index.htm |date=2008-06-11 }}</ref><ref>[http://www.mfschool.org/about/whatis.htm Monteverde Friends School – About MFS – What Is MFS] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080611142139/http://www.mfschool.org/about/whatis.htm |date=2008-06-11 }}</ref>

The Cloud Forest School (CFS), or Centro de Educación Creativa, founded in 1991, is a bilingual, secular, environmentally-focused private school that enrolls local and international students from Pre-K through 6th grade.<ref>[https://cloudforestschool.org/ The Cloud Forest School]</ref> The school sits on 106 acres of cloud forest. Over 80% of students are Costa Ricans, the majority of whom receive scholarship funding. The CFS is accredited by the government of Costa Rica.

The small private Adventist school, the Escuela Adventista, is also bilingual.

While there is only one institution of higher education in Monteverde, the [[Distance State University]] (UNED), the region is home to a considerable number of local and foreign undergraduate and graduate programs. These include the Monteverde Institute, EAP, [[CIEE]], and the [[University of Georgia]] (see below). Most residents of Monteverde pursue higher education in the [[Costa Rican Central Valley|Central Valley]] at institutions such as the [[University of Costa Rica]].

Monteverde is also home to a substantial number of foreign-born scientists. A long-term resident of the town is the former lead guitarist of British [[New wave music|new wave]] band [[Japan (band)|Japan]], Robert Dean, working as a professional [[ornithology]] writer.

==Economy==

===Agriculture===

[[Agriculture in Costa Rica|Agriculture]] has historically been the area's main source of income and sustenance for both Costa Ricans and Quakers. The original [[Creole peoples|Creole]] populations relied mostly on [[subsistence agriculture]] and trade with the mining town of [[Guacimal District|Guacimal]]. Initially locals hunted [[tapirs]], [[deer]], [[paca]]s, monkeys, and birds, but diminished those populations. They turned to pigs, [[Maize|corn]], [[beans]], vegetables, fruits, herbs and livestock. In the 1950s, both Quakers and Costa Ricans produced garlic, beef, [[flax]], and [[homestead cheese]]. Quakers took advantages of the infrastructure improvements of the 1960s and exported cheese and beef to the rest of the country. However, due to [[overgrazing]], the dairy industry declined in the 1970s. This led the population to turn to coffee. By the mid-1990s, coffee farmers were receiving some of the highest prices in the world for their coffee beans. In the mid-1990s 210 families were contributing milk to the local dairy factory, with a revenue of $5.2&nbsp;million.<ref name='Nadkarni'/>{{rp|400–405}}

===Tourism===
[[Tourism in Costa Rica|Tourism]] is a growing sector in Monteverde's economy. In 1975, the region received fewer than one hundred visitors. This increased to around 50,000 in the mid-1990s. In recent years, the average has risen to 250,000 yearly tourists. Hotels, taxis, guides, and other tourist-geared services have operated since the early 1990s.<ref name='Nadkarni'/>{{rp|360}}

==Places of interest==
===Nature===
[[File:DirkvdM canopy walk.jpg|thumb|One of the many eco-tourism oriented suspension bridges in the area. (Selvatura Park)]]
[[File:2006-08-08 Brüllaffe mit Jungtier, Monteverde (Costa Rica).jpg|thumb|200px|right|A Red Howler with child near the [[Monteverde]] Cloud Forest Biological Reserve]][[File:One of the Skywalk Hanging Bridges at Sky Monteverde in Costa Rica.jpg|thumb|[[:File:One of the Skywalk Hanging Bridges at Sky Monteverde in Costa Rica.jpg|One of the Skywalk Hanging Bridges at Sky Monteverde in Costa Rica]]]]Due to the area's cloud forests and rain forests (including seven different ecological [[life zones]]),<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Bolaños |first1=R.A |last2=Watson |first2=V |title=Mapa ecológico de Costa Rica |journal=Tropical Science Center |date=1993}}</ref> Monteverde has become a major part of the Costa Rican tourist trail – despite difficult access. Readers of the country's leading newspaper ''[[La Nación (San José)|La Nación]]'' voted it one of the "7 Wonders of Costa Rica".<ref name="7 Maravillas">{{cite news |title=Seven Wonders |date=September 2007 |publisher=BBC News |url=http://www.costaricapages.com/blog/costa-rica-news/seven-wonders-of-costa-rica/182 |access-date=2007-09-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071014152348/http://www.costaricapages.com/blog/costa-rica-news/seven-wonders-of-costa-rica/182 |archive-date=2007-10-14 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Of Monteverde's quarter-million annual tourists, around 70,000 tourists visit the reserve.

The bulk of Monteverde's cloud forest can be found in the [[Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve]]. The area around the park entrance is the most visited, and features six main trails, which total 13&nbsp;km, are well-kept and easy to access. The reserve features a large network of less-accessible trails and a number of research stations, two of which house ten persons each, as well as one larger station that can house as many as forty-three persons, though these are now restricted to use by researchers only.

To the east of Monteverde lies the [[Children's Eternal Rainforest]] (El Bosque Eterno de los Niños) conservation area, a project funded by schools and children from all over the world. The Bosque Eterno is the largest preserve in the area with {{convert|22000|ha|acre|abbr=off}}. Farther north is the Reserva Santa Elena. This area attracts fewer tourists than the Monteverde Reserve, but offers a field station and views of [[Arenal Volcano]].

===Preserve===

The massive {{Convert|10500|ha|acre|adj=on}} Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve is the region's main draw, due largely to its excellent virgin and semi-virgin environment and world-class biodiversity. Scientists have determined this region to be home to the planet's largest number of [[orchids]], at 500, with 34 being recent discoveries.  Fifty-eight species of [[amphibians]], including the extinct Monteverde-endemic [[golden toad]], have been found here. This area is also a stop for ninety-one species of [[migratory birds]]. The famed [[quetzal]] resides here seasonally. Monteverde's one-hundred-and-thirty-four mammal species include representatives from both North and South America, including six species of [[marsupials]], three [[muskrats]], at least fifty-eight bats, three [[primates]], seven [[edentate]]s, two rabbits, one [[ground hog]], three species of squirrels, one species of [[spiny mouse]], at least fifteen species of long-tailed rats and mice (family Muridae), one species of [[porcupine]], one species of [[agouti]], one [[paca]], two [[canidae|canids]], five [[mustelidae|mustelids]], four species of [[Procyonidae|procyonids]], six species of [[Felidae|felines]], two species of [[wild boar|wild pigs]], two species of [[deer]], and one [[tapir]].

===UGA Costa Rica===

The [[University of Georgia]] uses a 155-acre satellite campus outside of the Monteverde region in the lower San Luis community. [[UGA Costa Rica]] operates numerous study abroad programs, ecological and forestry research, as well as [[ecotourism]] via on-campus lodging, the Ecolodge San Luis. Additionally, UGA Costa Rica is responsible for various conservation and sustainability initiatives in the San Luis Valley, namely its Carbon Offset Program and reforestation efforts in the Pájaro Campana Biological Corridor. In May 2019, ownership of the campus was transferred to [[CIEE]] (Council on International Educational Exchange), which only hosts [[University of Georgia]] and other academic groups, not tourists.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://news.co.cr/university-of-georgia-foundation-approves-sale-of-costa-rica-campus-to-nonprofit-study-abroad-organization/78906 |title=University of Georgia Approves Sale of Costa Rica Campus to Nonprofit Organization |date=2019-02-25 |website=Costa Rica Star News |language=en-US |access-date=2019-05-06}}</ref>
