'''Chuao''' is a small village located in the northern [[Venezuelan Coastal Range|coastal range mountains]] of [[Venezuela]], founded in 1660.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news|last=Lewis|first=Paul|date=2007-04-06|title=In search of the beans of Chuao|language=en-GB|work=The Guardian|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2007/apr/07/venezuela.lifeandhealth|url-status=live|access-date=2020-05-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200515174127/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2007/apr/07/venezuela.lifeandhealth|archive-date=2020-05-15|issn=0261-3077}}</ref> The village is famous for its [[cacao plantation]]s, Diablos danzantes and San Juaneras. The village is surrounded by [[mountain]]s and dense [[rainforest]]s to the south and the [[Caribbean Sea]] to the north. The nearby [[Henri Pittier National Park]] is the oldest national park in Venezuela, created in 1937. The village is only accessible by travelling via boat from nearby [[Choroní]], and is approximately 4 kilometers inland.<ref name=":0" />

== Etymology ==
Chuao's name derives from an [[Indigenous peoples in Venezuela|indigenous]] word from a local language which is related to the term water.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web|title=Aló Presidente - Empresa Campesina Chuao: ejemplo de socialismo|url=http://alopresidente.gob.ve/poder-populacho/empresa-campesina-chuao-ejemplo-de-socialismo.html|last=|first=|date=2009-12-27|language=es|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091227112708/http://alopresidente.gob.ve/poder-populacho/empresa-campesina-chuao-ejemplo-de-socialismo.html|archive-date=2009-12-27|access-date=2020-05-15}}</ref>

== History ==
While cocoa production in the site of Chuao can be traced back to 1591,<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" /> the town itself was founded in 1660.<ref name=":0" /> From 1591 onwards, the area was home to a number of [[hacienda]]s which grew cocoa.<ref name=":1" /> During colonial times, a number of slaves were imported to the village from [[Africa]] to increase cocoa production on said haciendas.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" />

== Economy ==
The village of Chuao subsists largely on [[fishing]] and [[cocoa production]].<ref name=":0" />

=== Cocoa production ===
Chuao is home to a number of cocoa plantations, dating back to 1591,<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" /> which grow [[Criollo (cocoa bean)|Criollo]] and [[Hybrid (biology)|hybrid]] varieties of cacao.{{Citation needed|date=May 2020}} Chuao beans are regarded as being high in quality and, as a result, very expensive, fetching between 9 and 13 US dollars per kilogram.<ref name=":0" /> Beans from Chuao are considered some of Venezuela's finest, along with [[Porcelana Blanca]] beans from [[Lake Maracaibo]].{{citation needed|date=October 2015}} The Chuao region is hemmed in by mountains which reduces contact with people and insects from other areas.{{Citation needed|date=May 2020}} Some experts attribute the high quality of the cacao beans of Chuao due to the intense work of the village's laborers during the pre harvest, harvest and post harvest.{{Citation needed|date=May 2020}} Annually, Chuao's cocoa production hovers around 20 tons, but has varied from approximately 17 to 25 tons in recent years.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2" /> In November 2000, the Chuao cacao beans were awarded an [[Appellation d'origine contrôlée|appellation of origin]] under the title ''Cacao de Chuao'', allowing producers to negotiate higher prices and attract investment.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wipo.int/ipadvantage/en/details.jsp?id=2618 |title=Branding Matters: The Success of Chuao Cocoa Bean |publisher=World International Property Organization |accessdate=2015-10-16}}</ref>

==== Empresa Campesina de Chuao ====
The Farmer's Company of Chuao ({{Langx|es|Empresa Campesina de Chuao}}) is the [[Worker cooperative|worker's cooperative]] which operates the cocoa plantations of Chuao.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2" /> The cooperative has 127 members, most of whom are female.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2" /><ref name=":1" />

The cooperative has sold their beans to a number of foreign firms, such as [[Valrhona]] and [[Amedei]],<ref name=":0" /> before forming an exclusive partnership with Tisano, a Venezuelan partner, in 2014. As of 2020 Chuao Trading has managed this partnership. <ref name=":2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.el-nacional.com/todo_en_domingo/Viaje-cacao-Chuao_0_830316990.html|title=Viaje al cacao de Chuao|website=www.el-nacional.com|access-date=2016-10-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161012075101/http://www.el-nacional.com/todo_en_domingo/Viaje-cacao-Chuao_0_830316990.html|archive-date=2016-10-12|url-status=dead}}</ref> The partnership sells 99% of its cocoa to foreign buyers.<ref name=":2" /> The sales outside of Venezuela work via exclusive and authorized cacao importers for the markets in US, Japan and Europe. Through this partnership, the association "Empresa Campesina de Chuao", alongside their partners Chuao Trading, Tachibana, Casa [[Franceschi]] and Silva Cacao, have implemented a Certificate of Authenticity for the chocolatiers to ensure that only the Chuao beans that were harvested in the Chuao village make it into chocolate bars that are marketed as Chuao origin. This traceability certificate assigns per sold volume and per harvest with a unique serial number to each chocolate maker.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://chocolateclass.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/the-potential-and-pitfalls-of-geographical-indications-for-cacao/|title=The Potential and Pitfalls of Geographical Indications for Cacao|last=White|first=April|date=2016-05-11|website=Chocolate Class|access-date=2016-10-11}}</ref>

The cooperative has benefitted largely from an increased global appetite for high-quality chocolate in recent years,<ref name=":0" /> which has helped its members earn a high standard of living.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /> The worker's cooperative has been touted by the Venezuelan government as an "example of socialism".<ref name=":1" /> The cooperative has invested heavily in the village, building schools and medical infrastructure.<ref name=":0" /> However, ''[[The Guardian]]'' reported that those in Chuao who are not members of the association are discontent with the power it has over village life.<ref name=":0" />

== Culture ==
[[Central Region, Venezuela|Central Venezuela]] is home to a unique tradition regarding the [[Corpus Christi (feast)|Corpus Christi]] celebration, in which young men and children partake in Los Diablos Danzantes, which depicts masked devils surrendering to a church official.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web|title=UNESCO - Venezuela's Dancing Devils of Corpus Christi|url=https://ich.unesco.org/en/lists|website=ich.unesco.org|language=en|access-date=2020-05-15}}</ref> The dancers who partake in this festival become members of a lifelong pact, known as the promise-keepers, to transmit their history and traditions to others.<ref name=":4" /> In 2012, this tradition was added to the [[UNESCO]] [[Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity]].<ref name=":4" />

The first contemporary single-origin chocolate was made from cocoa beans from the [[Chuao]] Valley. This was popularized by Italian chocolate makers [[Amedei Chocolate]], who had gained temporary exclusive use of beans from the region. Beans from the Chuao valley are particularly prized because of the limited crop,{{Sfnp|Leissle|2018|p=168}} and because mineral deposits from the local river system give the beans a unique flavor, which is considered an example of [[terroir]] in chocolate.{{Sfnp|Leissle|2018|p=168}}

The other column of the Chuao village are Las San Juaneras this is the group of ladies that take care of the beautiful church in the town square, and lead most religious celebrations especially the party of San Juan which is celebrated every year to thank San Juan for a good cacao harvest.<ref>[http://www.mintur.gob.ve/mintur/ventel/2016/06/23/costas-araguenas-reciben-a-los-fieles-sanjuaneros] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160915100617/http://www.mintur.gob.ve/mintur/ventel/2016/06/23/costas-araguenas-reciben-a-los-fieles-sanjuaneros/|date=2016-09-15}}/</ref>

The church of the Immaculate Conception was built in 1772, replacing an old chapel. It was declared a National Monument according to Official Gazette No. 26,320 dated August 2, 1960. The exterior of the church is two-body with a mixed-linear pediment and a series of double pilasters flanking the main entrance with a semicircular arch. Simple cornices define and compose the façade. The interior has a presbytery and vestry along the end wall and a bell tower on the right side. The church still has valuable colonial religious images including one of Saint Nicholas of Bari in wood and canvas.

The Pardon Cross is situated on the left side of the church courtyard, which gives on to the Calle Real and the Plaza Bolivar. In the time of slavery, if a hunted slave managed to get to the cross and kneel before it, he could not be punished, and he was absolved of blame. 

The House on the Hill dates from 1652. It was the administrator's house of the Hacienda. The principal facade is two stories high, with windows. The. upper floor is reached via the staircase on the right side. It has a simple, linear facade where the verandas have circular section columns with a Tuscan capital and base, and wooden pillars on the upper floor determine the building's original commercial use.

The Mamey Ruins is a stone construction located on El Mamey hill in natural surroundings. Its age and use goes back to the first years of the Colony, Most of the stone walls are still standing in an L-shape (50 m x 40 m). 

The Chalk Oven is located on the road between the Mamey ruins and the Caribbean Sea. This is a circular stone structure with chalk mortar and two buttresses.

The Royal road leading from El Mamey to the beach is beautifully designed and perfectly preserved. Its characteristics and size indicate that the Mamey Ruins must have been very important.

The Chuao Plantation has a cultural landscape representative of the traditional way of exploiting cocoa, one of the most important products in the economic history of South America. This has been possible because of the plantation's almost in-sable location the fact that its production has been maintained for centuries by people with deep cultural roots, and the very special variety of the criollo-type cocoa considered among the best in the world and which is in danger of extinction. 

Chuao's unique cultural landscape consists of the natural surroundings of the Henri Pittier National Park, the combination of the natural forest with the cocoa plantations, and the existence of ancient irrigation systems that make it possible to irrigate the plantation all year round. The Chuao Plantation is also a place of living culture where the native black people, descended from the slaves, have kept their traditions alive until our days. This culture is the result of the combination of their deep African roots with the Western world through the Catholic religion and today it is the oldest cultural manifestation in Venezuela. Chuao is above all a living culture, the symbiosis of African Caribbean rites and music and Catholic rites and beliefs.
