'''Arica''' ({{IPAc-en|ə|ˈ|r|i:|k|ə}} {{respell|ə|REE|kə}}; {{IPA|es|aˈɾika|lang}}) was a [[Provinces of Peru|province]] of the [[department of Tacna]], [[Peru]]. Located in the country's southernmost coast, it existed between 1823 and 1929, having been ''de facto'' occupied by Chile since 1880 and [[Arica Department|incorporated into its territory]] since 1883 in the aftermath of the [[War of the Pacific]].

Arica was, alongside [[Tacna province|Tacna]], [[Tarata province|Tarata]], [[Department of Tarapacá (Peru)|Tarapacá and Iquique]], one of five provinces [[Chilean occupation of Peru|occupied by Chile]] following the conflict. The latter two were [[Treaty of Ancón|ceded by Peru]] in 1883, Tarata was occupied until 1925, and Tacna and Arica were central to the [[Chilean–Peruvian territorial dispute|46-year territorial dispute]] that only concluded with the [[Treaty of Lima (1929)|Treaty of Lima]] in 1929, after which Tacna was [[Handover of Tacna|returned to Peru]], while Arica was formally incorporated into the Chilean province of [[Tarapacá Province|Tarapacá]].<ref>{{cite web|title=ARICA|url=https://www.welcomechile.com/arica/history.html|work=welcomechile.com|publisher=Interpatagonia|accessdate=27 March 2012}}</ref> Today, it is part of [[Arica y Parinacota Region]].

== Etymology ==
The province's name comes from the [[Kunza language|Kunza]] term ''arisca'', meaning 'new bay', or ''Ariacca'', the name of the Inca chief who used to live in what is currently the city.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Topónimos de origen autóctono |url=https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-545957.html |website=[[Memoria Chilena]]}}</ref>

== History ==
Archaeological findings indicate that Arica was inhabited by different native groups dating back 10,000 years. These people are the first known culture to mummify their dead, predating the Egyptians by 2,000 years, and their mummies have been discovered as recently as 2004 and buried as shallow as less than 1 m beneath the surface of the city of Arica.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20220519-chiles-desert-town-built-on-mummies | title=The ancient mummies older than Egypt's |author=Riumalló, Juan Francisco |date=20 May 2020 |website= BBC |access-date= 1 September 2022}}</ref> Spanish colonization in the early 16th century saw the transformation of a small town into a thriving port.<ref>{{cite web|title=Arica History|url=http://www.lonelyplanet.com/chile/northern-chile/arica/history|work=lonelyplanet.com|publisher=Lonely Planet|accessdate=27 March 2012|format=Web page|year=2011}}</ref>

=== Spanish period ===
[[Spanish people|Spaniards]] settled the land under captain Lucas Martinez de Begazo in 1541. The proportions of these are that the first made up about 66% of the population and the latter 25%.<ref name=choquemariño/> The remaining 9% were fishing-oriented people known as [[Camanchacos]].<ref name=choquemariño>{{Cite journal |title=Indios originarios y forasteros. Interacciones culturales y cambios demográficos en los Altos de Arica y Tacna (siglos XVI y XVIII) |journal=[[Estudios Atacameños]]|year=2020 |last=Choque Mariño |first=Carlos |url=https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0718-10432020000100153 |volume=64 |trans-title=Native indians and outsiders. Cultural interactions and demographic changes in the Arica and Tacna highlands (XVI and XVIII centuries) |language=es |doi=10.22199/issn.0718-1043-2020-0011}}</ref> Many of the sedentary populations are thought to have  spoken the [[Puquina language]].<ref name=choquemariño/> In 1540 the [[encomienda]] system was established in Arica and its surroundings with conquistador [[Lucas Martínez de Vegaso]] obtaining 1,638 encomienda Indians (a third of tributaries distributed).<ref name=choquemariño/> [[Pedro Pizarro]] and Pedro de la Fuente followed in numbers receiving each approximately 600 tributaries.<ref name=choquemariño/>

By 1545, Arica was the main export entrepot for Bolivian [[silver]] coming down from [[Potosí]], which then possessed the world's largest [[silver mine]]. Arica thus held a crucial role as one of the leading ports of the [[Spanish Empire]].<ref name=MemoriaChilena554>{{Cite web |title=Arica (1540-1929) |url=https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-554.html |website=[[Memoria Chilena]]}}</ref> These enviable riches made Arica the target for [[pirate]]s, [[buccaneer]]s, and [[privateer]]s, among whom [[Francis Drake]],<ref>{{cite book|last1=Drake|first1=Francis|title=The World Encompassed by Sir Francis Drake: being His Next Voyage to that to Nombre de Dios|pages=107–108}}</ref> [[Thomas Cavendish]], [[Richard Hawkins]], [[Joris van Spilbergen]], [[John Watling]], [[Baltazar de Cordes]], [[Bartholomew Sharp]],<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Thomson |first=Keith |title=Born to be hanged: the epic story of the gentlemen pirates who raided the South Seas, rescued a princess, and stole a fortune |publisher=Little, Brown and Company |year=2022 |isbn=978-0-316-70361-1 |location=New York |pages=208–223}}</ref> [[William Dampier]],<ref name=":0" /> and [[John Clipperton]] all took part in looting the city.{{citation needed|date=March 2025}} From 1540 to 1570 the population in the region of Arica shrank by 36% due to disease, death in mining or emigration to evade the harsh conditions imposed by the Spanish.<ref name=choquemariño/>

In 1570, King [[Philip II of Spain]] signed a ''[[real cédula]]'' that granted the city its own coat of arms. The port was elevated to city status, with a ''[[Cabildo (council)|cabildo]]'' of its own (composed of a mayor, an ''Alférez Real'', and other offices). He also ordered that 12 families be sent there to increase its population. 87 years later, the [[Luis Enríquez de Guzmán, 9th Count of Alba de Liste|Count of Alba de Liste]], then [[Viceroy of Peru]], ordered that the order be formalised in its corresponding book, which was done on June 4, 1657.<ref>{{Cite news |title=La Fundación de la ciudad de San Marcos de Arica y el Escudo o Emblema de Armas |last=Mondaca Raiteri |first=Hermann |date=2024-04-24 |url=https://www.elmorrocotudo.cl/noticia/cultura/la-fundacion-de-la-ciudad-de-san-marcos-de-arica-y-el-escudo-o-emblema-de-armas-foto |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250504142831/https://www.elmorrocotudo.cl/noticia/cultura/la-fundacion-de-la-ciudad-de-san-marcos-de-arica-y-el-escudo-o-emblema-de-armas-foto |archive-date=2025-05-04 |work=El Morrocotudo}}</ref>

The '''Corregimiento of San Marcos de Arica''' ({{langx|es|Corregimiento de San Marcos de Arica}}) was established by Viceroy [[Lope García de Castro]] in 1565. Its first ''[[Corregimiento|corregidor]]'' was the Portuguese Francisco Rodríguez Almeida,<ref>{{Cite book |title=Fundación de Antofagasta y su primera década |last=Cruz Larenas |first=Jorge |publisher=[[Antofagasta|Ilustre Municipalidad de Antofagasta]] |year=1966 |pages=18 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Ryp5AAAAMAAJ}}</ref> and the [[curate]]s under its jurisdiction were those of Tacna, Sama, Ilabaya and Tarapacá.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Anaco de Camilaca: uso contemporáneo de un traje prehispánico |last=Roel Mendizábal |first=Pedro |publisher=[[Ministry of Culture (Peru)|Ministerio de Cultura]] |year=2011 |isbn=9786124126017 |pages=55 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=3S9SAQAAMAAJ |last2=Borja Chávez |first2=Paola}}</ref> In 1570 the [[Francisco de Toledo]] issued a decree reorganizing indigenous labour and taxation and among other things imposing the ''[[Mit'a|mita minera]]''.<ref name=choquemariño/> In November 1604 the area was hit by an [[1604 Arica earthquake|earthquake and tsunami]]. By 1777, the corregimiento consisted of [[Ilo, Peru|Ilo]], [[Tacna]], [[Arica]], [[Iquique]], {{ill|Pabellón de Pica|es|lt=Pica}}, [[Ilabaya District|Ilabaya]], [[Tarata, Peru|Tarata]], [[Codpa]]. In 1787, the ''corregimiento'' of Arica was abolished through a ''[[real cédula]]'' issued by [[Charles III of Spain]], replaced by the [[Intendencia indiana|intendancy system]].

The '''Partido of Arica''' ({{langx|es|Partido de Arica}}) was created in 1784, with its capital in [[Arica|San Marcos de Arica]].<ref>{{Cite book |title=Retazos de la Historia de Moquegua |last=Kuong Cabello |first=Luis E. |publisher=Universidad de Moquegua |year=1982 |pages=67 |language=es |url=http://www.luiskuoncabello.com/retazos-de-moquegua.html}}</ref> It was created as one of seven ''[[Partido (region)|partidos]]'' in total created alongside the [[Intendancy of Arequipa]], a subdivision of the [[Viceroyalty of Peru]]. 

During the [[Peruvian War of Independence]], Arica was targeted by two unsuccessful military campaigns due to its importance as one of the viceroyalty's southernmost ports, called the ''Intermedios''. The [[First Intermedios campaign|first campaign]] took place from 1822 to 1823, and [[Second Intermedios campaign|the second]] took place in late 1823. Both were repelled by the [[Royal Army of Peru]]. On June 17, 1823, the troops of [[Agustín Gamarra]] and [[Andrés de Santa Cruz]] disembarked at the port.

=== Republican period ===
[[File:Departamento moquegua 1865.JPG|thumb|right|Arica (pink) within [[Department of Moquegua|Moquegua]] in 1865.]]
The '''Province of Arica''' ({{langx|es|Provincia de Arica}}) was first established by the [[Constitution of Peru]] of 1823 as a division of the [[department of Arequipa]].<ref>{{Cite book|year=1918 |title=Dicionario geográfico peruano y almanaque de "La Crónica" para 1918 |location=Lima, Peru |publisher=N. Moral |page=62|oclc=26142406}}</ref> In 1828, the capital city of the province was changed from Arica to [[Tacna]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=Law providing that the city of Tacna become the capital of the province of Arica |last=La Mar |first=José |date=1828-06-09 |url=http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1828066.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927000231/https://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1828066.pdf |archive-date=2007-09-27 |work=[[Congress of the Republic of Peru|Congreso de la República]] |author-link=José de la Mar}}</ref> In 1836, the [[port of Arica]] became subject to a special administration by the government of the [[Peru–Bolivian Confederation]]. In 1837, the province joined the established [[Department of the Littoral (Peru)|Department of the Littoral]] with its capital at Tacna.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Law providing that the provinces of Tacna and Tarapaca form a departament separate from Arequipa |last=Santa-Cruz |first=Andrés |date=1837-04-25 |url=http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1837027.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090730223707/http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1837027.pdf |archive-date=2009-07-30 |work=[[Congress of the Republic of Peru|Congreso de la República]] |author-link=Andrés de Santa Cruz}}</ref> In 1853, the province was moved to the newly established [[Moquegua Region|Department of Moquegua]], along with the provinces of Moquegua, Tacna and [[Department of Tarapacá (Peru)|Tarapacá]].

In 1855, the province was recreated in the department of Moquegua and divided into two provinces: [[Tacna province|Tacna]] and Arica. Each had their eponymous cities as political capitals. The sub-prefect resided in Arica, while the prefect continued to reside in Tacna. Each province was divided into the following districts:<ref>{{Cite news |title=Law creating again the province of Arica in the department of Moquegua |date=1855-06-25 |url=http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1855061.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090730223715/http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1855061.pdf |archive-date=2009-07-30 |work=[[Congress of the Republic of Peru|Congreso de la República]]}}</ref>

During the [[Peruvian Civil War of 1856–1858|civil war of 1856–1858]], [[Miguel Grau]] and [[Lizardo Montero]], the two second lieutenants of the ''[[Peruvian frigate Apurímac|Apurímac]]'', revolted in favour of [[Manuel Ignacio de Vivanco]]'s rebellion. The ''Apurímac'' was a anchored at the time in the port of Arica, after which it was used for a failed attack that targeted [[Callao]].

Following the decree of June 23, 1862, Monsignor {{ill|Bartolomé Herrera|es}} placed and blessed the cornerstone of the first parish church of [[Iquique]], whose iron structure was brought from Europe and installed at its main square (today Arturo Prat Square).<ref name=Diocesis>{{Cite web |title=Nuestra historia |url=https://www.iglesiadeiquique.cl/di%C3%B3cesis |website=Diócesis de Iquique}}</ref> In 1872, the parish burned down, and its tower was replaced by a [[Clock Tower (Iquique)|Clock Tower]], built in 1878 after its construction was approved the year before.

[[File:Arica after the earthquake (1868).JPG|thumb|right|The [[Morro de Arica]] overlooking the city's ruins following the [[1868 Arica earthquake|disaster of 1868]].]]
In 1868, the city of Arica was almost completely destroyed during a [[1868 Arica earthquake|high-magnitude earthquake]] that also affected its surroundings in Peru, as well as in Bolivia and Chile. In 1875, Arica was transferred to the newly established [[department of Tacna]], along with the provinces of Tacna and [[Tarata province|Tarata]]. It was divided into six districts (Arica, Belén, [[Codpa]], Livilcar, [[Lluta River|Lluta]], and [[Socoroma]]). This was the last administrative change prior to the [[War of the Pacific]], which began in 1879.

The first Peruvian territory to be occupied by the [[Chilean Army]] during the war was the port of [[Pisagua, Chile|Pisagua]], whose occupation took place on November 2 of that year. The [[Tarapacá campaign|Chilean campaign]] was successful, and led to the [[Chilean occupation of Peru|military occupation]] of the provinces of [[Department of Tarapacá (Peru)|Iquique and Tarapacá]] following the Peruvian retreat despite a military success at the [[battle of Tarapacá]].

The first Peruvian troops that left [[Tarapacá]] arrived to the city of Arica on December 17. The following year, the [[Chilean Navy]] carried out an amphibious landing at the [[Ilo, Peru|port city of Ilo]] on February 26, and the bombardment of Arica began the following day. The army continued to the south until it reached the city of [[Tacna]] on May 26, after which [[Battle of Tacna|a battle was fought]] at [[Intiorko Hill]], located on the outskirts of the city. The Chilean Army subsequently occupied the city and Bolivia withdrew from the conflict. On June 7, the Chilean advance reached Arica and, following a [[Battle of Arica|55-minute battle]], the city was conquered.

Colonel [[Francisco Bolognesi]], who was killed in action, became a war hero of Peru. Fellow soldiers [[Alfonso Ugarte]] and [[Mariano Bustamante]] are also remembered in the same manner. Lieutenant Colonel [[Roque Sáenz Peña]], a volunteer officer of the Peruvian Army, later became president of Argentina.

=== Chilean period ===
{{main|Arica Department}}
Following [[Lima campaign|another campaign]] that reached the capital, the government of [[Miguel Iglesias]] signed the [[Treaty of Ancón]] on October 20, 1883. Under the terms of the agreement, Peru ceded its department of Tarapacá, while the provinces of Tacna and Arica would be subject to Chilean control, after which a plebiscite would be held. This never came to pass.<ref>Egaña, Rafael (1900) ''The Tacna and Arica question. Historical antecedents.--Diplomatic action. Present state of the affair'' (translated from the Spanish edition by Edwin C. Reed) Barcelona Printing Office, Santiago, Chile, {{OCLC|19301902}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article238538689 |title=Chili and Peru. A money offer for Provinces |newspaper=[[The Daily Telegraph (Sydney)|The Daily Telegraph]] |issue=5144 |location=New South Wales, Australia |date=16 December 1895 |accessdate=8 December 2018 |page=5 |via=National Library of Australia}}  , ''...Senor de Pierola, the President of the Republic of Peru, has offered Chili the sum of 10,000,000 piastres (about £790,000) for the provinces of Tacna and Arica. These provinces were ceded by Peru to Chili for ten years (1883-93), at the end of which period a plebiscite was to decide their definite nationality. Owing to the troubles in Peru last year this decision was deferred...''</ref> ''De facto'', that was the end of the Peruvian province of Arica, which was incorporated as [[Arica Department|a department]] of Chile's [[Tacna Province (Chile)|Tacna Province]] and subject to a process of [[Chilenization of Tacna, Arica and Tarapacá|forced acculturation]], although the [[Chilean–Peruvian territorial dispute|territorial dispute]] was not settled until the 1929 [[Treaty of Lima (1929)|Treaty of Lima]].<ref>Jane, Lionel Cecil (1930) "The question of Tacna-Arica ..." ''Transactions of the Grotius Society'' 15: pp.&nbsp;93–119</ref>

== Politics ==
The province of Arica was administered by a municipal government that also administered Arica District. The [[Catholic Church in Peru]] administered the province as part of the [[Archdiocese of Arequipa|diocese of Arequipa]]. Following its occupation by Chile, it became part of its [[Diocese of Iquique|Apostolic Vicariate of Tarapacá]], which became independent from Arequipa in 1882.<ref name=Diocesis/>

== Geography ==
The province was located to the north of the ravine of [[Camarones River]].<ref name=Stiglich/>

=== Boundaries ===
The province was located in southern coast of Peru. It was limited to the north by [[Tacna province]], to the south by the [[Department of Tarapacá (Peru)|department of Tarapacá]], to the east by [[Bolivia]], and to the west by the [[Pacific Ocean]].

== Demographics ==
In 1913, the province had 10,420 inhabitants, of which 3,700 resided in the city of Arica.<ref name=Stiglich/> 

=== Ethnic groups ===
At the time of Spanish colonisation, the region was already multiethnic, displaying a mix of local sedentary populations and [[mitma]] settlers from the [[Altiplano]].<ref name=choquemariño/> 

In 1929, when the province was ceded to Chile, a Peruvian community remained in the province.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.grau.pe/campana-terrestre/reincorporacion-de-tacna-al-peru/ |title=Reincorporación de Tacna al Perú: 28 de agosto de 1929 |date=2017-08-01 |website=Memorias de Miguel Grau |last=Cuya Vera |first=Ricardo}}</ref>

== Economy ==
During the Spanish period, Arica was one of the main ports in the continent, and an important part of the [[silver]] trade route in the area. This period of prosperity continued into 1770, when commerce was rerouted to what became the [[Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata]]. Despite the effects on the local economy, local demand allowed the port to continue actively operating as part of the [[guano]] industry. Following Peruvian independence, the town's influence was further reduced due to its adherence to the Royalist cause, with Tacna replacing it as the local administrative centre.<ref name=MemoriaChilena554/> Under Chile's administration, the province regained some of its influence with the opening of the [[Arica–La Paz railway]] in 1913.<ref name=MemoriaChilena554/>

== Culture ==
Arica's fertile location allowed for pre-Columbian peoples to settle there long before the arrival of the Spanish in 1540.<ref name=MemoriaChilena554/> During the period of Chilean administration, the city's remaining Peruvian population was both subjected to a process of [[Chilenization of Tacna, Arica and Tarapacá|forced acculturation]], and responsible for a prolongued [[Anti-Chilean sentiment|anti-Chilean]] campaign.

=== Landmarks ===
Despite its 16th-century origins, most of the area was reconstructed following the 1868 earthquake. A number of buildings built in the following decade survived the war and have continued to exist ever since, with some eventually having been designated as [[List of National Monuments of Chile|National Monuments of Chile]].

== Transport ==
In 1856 the [[England|English]] company ''The Arica & Tacna Railway Co.'' built a [[Tacna–Arica railway|railway line]] that connected the cities of Arica and Tacna. The railway continued to operate after the war.

In 1913, a [[Arica–La Paz railway|second railway line]] was opened, which connected the province—by then under Chilean administration—with the Bolivian city of [[La Paz]].<ref name=MemoriaChilena554/>
