'''Villa de Etla''' is a town and [[municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] located in the [[States of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Oaxaca]]. It is located in the far northwestern part of the Central Valley of Oaxaca, about seventeen km from [[Oaxaca, Oaxaca|the capital]]. The town is centered on the church and former monastery of San Pedro y San Pablo, and as municipal seat functions as the local government for six other communities. Most of the town's history has been lost due to the lack of records, but oral tradition states that it was founded sometime in the 15th century. Today, the town is noted for its weekly Wednesday market, where one can find traditional merchandise such a local variety of [[Cheeses of Mexico|white cheese]], [[tamale]]s, frames for donkeys and goat [[barbacoa]] prepared in an earthen oven.

==The town==
The town of Villa de Etla is centered on the church and former monastery of San Pedro y San Pablo, with the layout of the original streets of the town marked off from it. The complex was completed in 1636 and consists of three parts, the church, the former monastery and the cloister, all constructed from green sandstone. The main portal of the church has a simple arch over which is the choir window. Over this is a pediment which contains a clock. Behind the monastery area, there are the remains of an [[Roman aqueduct|aqueduct]]. This arched construction was one of the first built in the area and was used during the colonial period to provide water to the town from the surrounding mountains. It is not known when the aqueduct was built but it was probably completed by the end of the 16th century.<ref name="rincones127">{{cite book |title=Oaxaca: Guia para descubrir los encantos del estado |trans-title=Oaxaca: Guide to discover the charms of the state |last=Akaike Garrido |first=Yuki |year=2010 |editor1-first=Victor Manuel |editor1-last=Jiménez González |publisher=Editorial Océano de Mexico, SA de CV |location=Mexico City |isbn=978-607-400-233-1 |page=127 }}</ref><ref name="oaxmex">{{cite web |url= http://www.oaxacamexico.com.mx/blog/oaxaca-mexico-6.htm |title=Turismo Arqueológico en Oaxaca: Villa de Etla |publisher=Secretaria de Turismo |location=Oaxaca |language=es |trans-title=Archeological tourism in Oaxaca: Villa de Etla |access-date=June 12, 2011 }}</ref> The church is one of a series of churches and monasteries which were established by the [[Dominican order|Dominicans]] between the [[Oaxaca, Oaxaca|city of Oaxaca]] and the [[Puebla]] border in the 16th century. Today this path is the main highway to the city of Puebla and the church is grouped with those from [[Santo Domingo Yanhuitlán|Yanhuitlán]], [[San Juan Teposcolula|Teposcolula]] and [[San Juan Bautista Coixtlahuaca|Coixtlahuaca]] .<ref name="lospasos">{{cite news |title= Tras los pasos de los dominicos |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=December 17, 2006 |page=14 |language=es |trans-title=Following the footsteps of the Dominicans }}</ref> There is one other significant church in the town but it is unknown when it was constructed.<ref name="encmuc">{{cite web |url= http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20338a.htm |title=Villa de Etla |year=2009 |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México Oaxaca |publisher=Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal and Gobierno del Estado de Oaxaca |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=June 12, 2011 }}</ref>

The other two important constructions are next to this complex, the [[Traditional fixed markets in Mexico|municipal market]], behind which is the municipal palace, or government office.<ref name="oaxmex"/> The municipal market is the center of most of the municipality's commerce, with efforts to build larger commercial stores opposed by many residents.<ref name="reclaman">{{cite news |title= Reclaman en Villa de Etla renuncia de edil aliancista  |author=Águeda Robles |url= http://www.diariodespertar.com.mx/agenda/50761-Reclaman-Villa-Etla-renuncia-edil-aliancista.html |newspaper=Diario Despertar |location=Oaxaca |date=May 31, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Demand the resignation of the Aliancista municipal president  }}</ref> The market is the center for the weekly [[tianguis]] or open-air market held on Wednesdays. The Wednesday market is part of an informal system of weekly markets that occur in most of the larger communities of the Central Valleys region.<ref name="albergan">{{cite news |title= Albergan artesanias y gastronomia |newspaper=El Norte |location=Monterrey, Mexico |date=October 21, 2001 |page=7 |language=es |trans-title=Home of handcrafts and gastronomy }}</ref> This market is best known for its locally produced variety of white cheese, but it also offers a number of kinds of traditional merchandise such as sheepskins, frames for packing donkeys, the [[green glazed pottery of Atzompa]] and [[tamale]]s.<ref name="moon">{{cite book |title= Moon Pacific Mexico: Including Mazatlan, Puerto Vallarta, Guadalajara ... |last= Whipperman |first= Bruce |date= September 28, 2007 |publisher=Moon Handbooks |page=677 |isbn= 9781566918480 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=KqeXfoJMVHUC&q=Villa+de+Etla&pg=PA677 }}</ref> Another specialty is goat [[barbacoa]] cooked in earthen ovens.<ref name="food">{{cite journal |last1=Wetherall |first1=Megan |date=June 2009 |title= destination food |journal=Arthur Frommer's Budget Travel   |volume=12 |issue=5 |pages=46–55 }}</ref>

The main festival for the municipality is in the seat in honor of [[Saint Peter|Saints Peter]] and [[Saint Paul|Paul]]. This event begins with processions along the main streets to announce the start, just before the actual feast day. Then on the day before, a very large frame loaded with fireworks, called a castillo (castle) along with smaller bull figures called toritos are set alight. On the main day, there are masses and other religious observances ending with a large public dance.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Lent is an important time in the state of Oaxaca, with many communities developing various types of celebration over these forty days. As Villa de Etla already experiences warm weather by this time, flavored drinks sold by street vendors are popular during the processions, [[mass (liturgy)|masses]] and other events held during this time. Some typical flavors served include [[horchata]], [[hibiscus]] tea, drinks made from coconuts, a fermented corn drink called [[tejate]] and one made from a sweet squash from the area called chilacayota. These are often offered to participants in the various events.<ref name="samaritana">{{cite news |title= Celebra La Villa de Etla, día de La Samaritana |url= http://www.noticiasdeoaxaca.info/2011/04/celebra-la-villa-de-etla-dia-de-la.html |newspaper=Notcias Oaxaca |location=Oaxaca |date=April 7, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Villa de Etla celebrates day of the Samaritan }}</ref>

The Viernes de Cuaresma attracts a large number of visitors from around the area. It is marked by a large fair, with about forty amusement rides for children, and musical events. There are over 100 stands selling food and other commodities. The event begins with a music band wandering the streets of the town at 9&nbsp;pm followed by the setting off of fireworks fastened to a large wooden frame called a castillo (castle). The following day is dedicated to the Christ image called the Señor de las Peñas with more music and fireworks. It ends on Sunday with dancing to popular music.<ref name="seinstalan">{{cite news |title= Se instalan Juegos Mecánicos en La Villa de Etla para la Fiesta del 5º Viernes |author=Enrique Ayala Martinez |url= http://www.noticiasdeoaxaca.info/2011/04/se-instalan-juegos-mecanicos-en-la.html |newspaper=Noticias de Oaxaca |location=Oaxaca |date=April 2, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Amusement rides set up in Villa de Etla for the Festival of the Fifth Friday}}</ref> Hundreds from Oaxaca and as far as Puebla, [[Chiapas]] and [[Mexico City]] come to honor the Señor de las Peñas to keep promises and to ask for favors. The fifth Friday of Lent is dedicated to this image. The festivities are arranged by a committee which is selected each year under Usos y Costumbres.<ref name="veneran">{{cite news |title= Veneran al Señor de las Peñas en la Villa de Etla |author=Jazmín Gómez |url= http://www.diariodespertar.com.mx/agenda/49390-Veneran-Seor-las-Peas-Villa-Etla.html?print  |newspaper=Diario Despertar |location=Oaxaca |date=April 9, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Venerate the Señor de las Peñas in Villa de Etla}}</ref>

The fourth Friday of [[Lent]], three weeks before [[Good Friday]], is the La Samaritana celebration. Large ceramic pots are placed and decorated. Some are decorated with bricks but most have imitations, and then arches of palm fronds with [[bougainvillea]] flowers are then placed. The tradition is derived from a passage in the Bible in which a Samaritan offers water from a well to Jesus.<ref name="samaritana"/>

[[Palm Sunday]] celebrations begin a day before when elaborate figures made of palm fronds are made to be blessed at mass the following day. The figures vary in size and complexity and are often decorated with crosses, pins and flowers. Those who do not make their own palm arrangements buy them from stands located in and around the main church. These are then kept for the remainder of the year to protect the home. During mass, eight young men are chosen to read different Biblical passages related to the [[Passion of Christ]]. They are dressed in white tunics, with wreaths on their head to represent Christ. Afterwards, it is traditional to go to the municipal market next door to buy provisions for that day's supper. This includes a bread called “pan Amarillo" cheese, honey, ice cream, tamales, [[tasajo]], [[tlayuda]]s, tortillas, fruits and flowers.<ref name="ramos">{{cite news |title= La fe católica del Domingo de Ramos vive en la Villa de Etla |url= http://www.diariocritico.com/mexico/2009/Abril/noticias/141203/la-fe-catolica-del-domingo-de-ramos-vive-en-la-villa-de-etla.html |newspaper=Diario Critico |location=Oaxaca |date=April 5, 2009 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=the Catholic faith of Palm Sunday lives in Villa de Etla}}</ref>

At these and other events, the most common music is produced by wind instruments and the most common dance is the jarabe, especially at weddings. The most commons foods include [[mole (sauce)|mole]] (especially amarillo, verde, and coloradito), regional sweets, tlayudas, barbacoa and fava beans, accompanying by [[tepache]], [[mezcal]] and beer.<ref name="encmuc"/>

==The municipality==
As municipal seat, the town of Villa de Etla is the local government for six other communities, but most people live in the town proper.<ref name="INEGI">{{cite web |url= http://www3.inegi.org.mx/sistemas/iter/entidad_indicador.aspx?ev=4 |title=INEGI Census 2005 |language=es |access-date=June 10, 2011 }}</ref> These communities together occupy a territory of 17.85km<sup>2</sup> and are bordered by the municipalities of [[Asunción Etla]], [[Santo Domingo Etla]], [[San Miguel Etla]] and [[Reyes Etla]]. The municipality is located in the Central Valleys region of the state about seventeen km from the capital of Oaxaca. The municipal government consists of a municipal president, an officer called a “síndico” and four secretaries called “regidors” (for taxes, education, public works and agencies/neighborhoods). These and other officials are elected under Mexico's usos y costumbres system.<ref name="encmuc"/>  
As of 2005, only 621 people out of a population of 7,637 spoke an indigenous language. Most are Catholic but about a sixth belong to another or no faith.<ref name="encmuc"/>

As of 2005, there were a total of 1691 homes almost all of which were owned by their occupants. Of these homes, 61% have running water, 95% are in areas with public lighting and 49% have public drainage. Federal Highway 190 passes through the municipality connecting it to the city of Oaxaca and Mexico City.<ref name="encmuc"/>

About seventy percent of the municipality's population is dedicated wholly or in part to agriculture, with the primary livestock being cattle. However, only eight percent are dedicated to it full-time. About thirty six percent work in industry, mining and construction. The main handcraft is basketry. About fifteen percent are dedicated to commerce, most of which is related to the sale of dairy products such as milk and cheese. Other commerce focuses on local needs, with the municipal market and the weekly market having a primary role. The total percentage dedicated to commerce, tourism and services is 53%.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipality has several preschools, primary schools along with center for study at the high school level.<ref name="encmuc"/> Villa de Etla is the head of the twenty eight schools of Scholastic Zone Number 5.<ref name="basilio">{{cite press release |title= Escuela primaria "Basilio E. Zárate" de Villa de Etla festeja su 50 aniversario |publisher=Instituto Estatal de Educación Pública de Oaxaca |date=December 13, 2007 |url= http://ieepo.info/foro/viewtopic.php?t=3260&sid=e8e4d0d245860d4133712f40047e31c4 |language=es |trans-title=Basilio E. Zarate Primary School of Villa de Etla celebrates its 50th anniversary |access-date=June 12, 2011}}</ref> Preschools include Ignacio José Allende, Jaime Torres Bodet, Leona Vicario, Niños Heroes de Chapultepec, Ovidio Decroly, Porfirio Díaz and Prodei Modulo 2. Primary schools include Basilio e Zarate, Cuauhtémoc, Iep 13 General de Division Ignacio Mejia, José María Morelos, Juan de la Barrera, Margarita Maza de Juarez, Ricardo Flores Magon and Valentin Gomez Farias. Secondary education is provided at Escuela Secundaria Technica 84. Special education schools include Centro de Atencion Multiple 26 and Unidad de Servicios de Apoyo a la Educación Regular No. 19. High school level instruction is found at the Centro de Estudios de Bachillerato 6/12. All schools and other centers of instruction are public.<ref name="eduportal">{{cite web |url= http://eduportal.com.mx/escuelas/en/oaxaca/villa-de-etla?pagina=1 |title= Escuelas en Villa de Etla, Oaxaca, México |work=Eduportal |language=es |access-date=June 12, 2011 }}</ref> 
The Basilio E. Zárate School celebrated its fiftieth anniversary in 2007. It is the largest and oldest primary school in the area, with a number of students receiving awards in the arts and academics. The school began with classes held at private homes and in makeshift constructions. The current building was eventually financed with support from local, state and federal governments. Maintenance is paid for by students’ parents. There are currently about 500 students from the first to sixth grades with a number receiving special educations services from the Unidad de Servicios Educativos Especiales del Instituto Estatal de Educación Pública de Oaxaca. It also has a social worker, a psychologist and a speech therapist. It recently installed a multimedia room.<ref name="basilio"/>

In addition to the monastery complex in the main town, an excavated archeological site is another of the municipality's monuments.<ref name="encmuc"/> The archeological zone was first occupied during the [[Monte Albán]] IIIA phase and grew during IIIB to about 2,500 people. From 700 to 450 BCE, it dominated the local area, becoming the most important site of the Valley of Etla. It was abandoned between 650–800 CE, or Monte Albán IV.<ref name="oaxmex"/>

==Geography==
The municipality is located in the Valley of Etla, which is a sub-valley located in the far northwest of the Valley of Oaxaca.<ref name="oaxmex"/> The municipality lies on a valley floor so its terrain is relatively flat with no significant elevations other than small hills. Almost all of the land in the area is used for agriculture or human habitation. The major rivers are the Asunción which flows through the community of Nativitas Etla and the Salinas River when crosses through Santo Domingo Barrio Alto and Santo Domingo Bajo Etla. It has a temperate climate.<ref name="encmuc"/>
===Flora and fauna===
Almost all of the vegetation in the municipality is secondary with decorative species such as [[geranium]]s, bougainvillea; trees such as laurel and eucalyptus, and various edible and medicinal herbs. Wildlife mostly consists of bird species such as doves and hummingbirds, various insects, along with some aquatic species such as frogs, fish and crab and reptiles such as lizards, small turtles and snakes. Mammals are all domesticated species.<ref name="encmuc"/>

==History==
The name means “town of the land of beans” mixing Spanish and [[Nahuatl]]. “Villa” is from Spanish and means “town” and “Etla” comes from Nahuatl meaning in “land of beans.” The Zapotec name for the area is Loohvana, which refers to the land's fertility.<ref name="encmuc"/>

Most of the history of this town has been lost due to the lack of records. Oral tradition states that the settlement was founded sometime in the 15th century.<ref name="encmuc"/>
The decisive [[Battle of La Carbonera]] was fought on October 18, 1866 near to Etla during the war caused by the [[Second French intervention in Mexico|French intervention in Mexico]].<ref name=marley>{{cite book

The last few years of the municipality has been marked by political instability. This has negatively impacted public works such as the repaving of roads, electrification and a non functioning water treatment plant, with delays as long as 15 years.<ref name="retomar">{{cite news |title= Impiden retomar cargo a edil en Villa de Etla |author=Luis Parra Meixueiro |url= http://www.diariodespertar.com.mx/agenda/36713-Impiden-retomar-cargo-edil-Villa-Etla.html  |newspaper=Diario Despertar |location=Oaxaca |date=June 24, 2010 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Prevent the retaking of power by the municipal president of Villa de Etla}}</ref> In 2008, Federal Highway 190 and the main entrance to town were blocked by those protesting the lack of potable water for three months in 2008. The protesters claimed they had running water irregularly only about once a week and sometimes not even that.<ref name="tresmeses">{{cite news |title= Tres meses sin agua potable en Villa de Etla; pobladores bloquean la carretera federal |author=Juan Carlos Zavala |url=http://cminoticiasoaxaca.com/Desarrollo/index.php?view=article&catid=154%3Avalles&id=554%3Atres-meses-sin-agua-potable-en-villa-de-etla-pobladores-bloquean-la-carretera-federal&option=com_content&Itemid=91|newspaper=CM Notcias |location=Oaxaca |date=September 3, 2008 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Three months without potable water in Villa de Etla, residents block federal highway }}</ref> In 2010, a group of over a hundred residents took over the municipal palace to force the state government to reinstitute Alma Delia Ramos as municipal president. A previous first takeover ended peacefully but without the desired results of the protestors.<ref name="retomar"/>  The latest protests and blockades have centered on the government of Danial Ramírez Ramírez from April to May 2011. This municipal president has been accused of [[nepotism]], putting family and friends on municipal payrolls for no work. He has also been accused of threatening the homes and lives of those who oppose him.<ref name="reclaman"/><ref name="tension">{{cite news |title= Tensión en el cabildo de la Villa de Etla  |url= http://www.lapoliciaca.com/nota-roja/tension-en-el-cabildo-de-la-villa-de-etla/ |newspaper=La Nota Roja de Oaxaca/La Policiaca |location=Oaxaca |date=May 25, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Tension in the city council of Villa de Etla }}</ref><ref name="retiran">{{cite news |title=Retirán bloqueo de la Villa de Etla  |author=Agueda Robles |url= http://www.diarioaxaca.com/capital/7-capital/12547-retiran-bloqueo-de-la-villa-de-etla- |newspaper=Diario de Oaxaca |location=Oaxaca |date=May 26, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Withdraw block of Villa de Etla }}</ref> Protestors have principally called for and received state-led investigations into the matter, in order to avoid escalation.<ref name="tension"/> There have been various incidents over these two months from the blocking of Federal Highway 190, to the blocking of all six main entrances to the town to the takeover of the municipal palace.<ref name="reclaman"/><ref name="cierre">{{cite news |title= Sigue el cierre en la Villa de Etla de seis accesos principales |author=Rebeca Luna Jimenez |url= http://www.vozdelsur.com.mx/oaxaca/noticias/oaxaca/15701-sigue-el-cierre-en-la-villa-de-etla-de-seis-accesos-principales.html |newspaper=La Voz del Sur |location=Oaxaca |date=May 26, 2011 |access-date=June 12, 2011 |language=es |trans-title=Closure of the six accesses to Villa de Etla continues }}</ref> However, there have been other demands by protestors such as the stop to the construction of a “Súper Precio” commercial center which they say would negatively affect about 300 local merchants.<ref name="reclaman"/>
