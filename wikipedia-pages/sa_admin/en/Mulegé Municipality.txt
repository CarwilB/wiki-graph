'''Mulegé''' is the northernmost [[municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] of the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[States of Mexico|state]] of [[Baja California Sur]]. It is the largest municipality by area in Mexico, with an area of 33,092.20&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> (12,777 sq mi).<ref name="landarea">{{cite web|url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/zap/datGenerales.aspx?entra=nacion&ent=03&mun=002|title=Mulegé, General Information|language=es|publisher=Unidad de Microrregiones, SEDESOL|access-date=2021-02-10}}</ref> In the 2020 Census, it had a population of 64,022 inhabitants.<ref name="census2020">{{cite web|url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/scitel/Default?ev=9|title=Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 - SCITEL|language=es|publisher=INEGI|access-date=2021-01-27}}</ref> [[Isla Natividad]] is part of the municipality.

The municipal seat is located in [[Santa Rosalía, Baja California Sur|Santa Rosalía]]. The [[El Vizcaíno Biosphere Reserve]] is located in the municipality, between the Pacific Ocean and the Gulf of California. With a landmass of over 24,930&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> (9,625 sq mi),<ref>[http://avesmx.conabio.gob.mx/verzona?tipo=anp&id=282 ANP 282 - El Vizcaíno] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304002514/http://avesmx.conabio.gob.mx/verzona?tipo=anp&id=282 |date=March 4, 2016 }}. Retrieved 01-03-2015.</ref> it is the largest wildlife refuge in Mexico and borders on the northern edge of the [[Valle de los Cirios]] Protected Area of Flora and Fauna.

==History==
[[File:San ignacio mission.jpg|thumb|left|San Ignacio Mission]]
[[Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo]] was the first explorer to navigate the coastline of Mulegé, whereas [[Sebastián Vizcaíno]] explored the inlands in 1596 on behalf of [[Gaspar de Zúñiga, 5th Count of Monterrey|Gaspar de Zúñiga]], viceroy of [[New Spain]].<ref name=Jimenez2015>{{cite book |last=Jiménez González |first=Victor Manuel |date=July 2015 |title=Baja California Sur - Los Cabos - Cabo San Lucas - Loreto - La Paz: Guía de viaje del Estado de Baja California Sur}}</ref>

The first Spanish missionary to visit the area was Father [[Juan María de Salvatierra]] in 1705. Three years later, the Jesuit missionary Juan de Basaldúa founded the [[Misión Santa Rosalía de Mulegé|Santa Rosalía de Mulegé mission]] in the area locally known as Caaman Cagaleja, which means "river between two rocks." Other important Jesuit missions in Mulegé were [[Misión San Ignacio Kadakaamán|San Ignacio]] and [[Misión Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe de Huasinapi|Guadalupe]]. In 1768, the Franciscans took over responsibility for colonial Baja California from the Jesuits.<ref name="history">{{cite web|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM03bajacaliforniasur/municipios/03002a.html|title=History of Mulegé|language=es|publisher=INAFED|access-date=2021-02-10}}</ref>

Rich copper deposits were discovered in 1868, later developed as mines. In 1872, Eiseman y Valle was incorporated as a business for the exploitation of copper. In 1885, the French mining company [[El Boleo]] began its operations and built the town of Santa Rosalía; the first copper smelting furnace was installed in 1886.<ref name="history" />

The municipality was first incorporated in 1916 as part of the [[Baja California Territory]], Southern District. In 1929, the municipal council was replaced by a municipal delegation. By 1954, the mining company "El Boleo" ceased operations, which resulted in many people moving to other parts of the country, but three years later, Fomento Minero decided to take over the company and restarted the copper exploitation work. [[Guerrero Negro]] was founded in 1957 on the Pacific Coast to supply the demand of salt in the western United States. When Baja California Sur became a state in 1972, the municipality of Mulegé was formally established.<ref name="history" />

There is an initiative to split the municipality into two, with the division along the ridge dividing the current municipality. The Pacific side, with [[Guerrero Negro]] and Villa Alberto Andrés Alvarado Arámburo would be separated from the Gulf of California side, which includes Santa Rosalía and Mulegé.<ref name="split">{{cite web|url=https://www.bcsnoticias.mx/ciudadano-revive-propuesta-en-el-congreso-para-dividir-mulege-y-crear-el-sexto-municipio-de-bcs/|title=Proposal to split Mulegé in two municipalities sent to the State Congress|language=es|publisher=BCS Noticias|access-date=2021-02-10}}</ref>

As of March 1, 2021, the municipality reported 1,188 recoveries, 46 active cases, and 109 deaths from the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Coronavirus – Baja California Sur Situation Report – Información de Coronavirus |url=https://coronavirus.bcs.gob.mx/english/ |website=coronavirus.bcs.gob.mx |publisher=Gobierno de Baja California Sur |access-date=March 1, 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

==Economy==

There are four main economic sectors in Mulegé: agriculture, mining, livestock and tourism.

===Agriculture and livestock===
Agriculture and livestock are principally concentrated in the area of Vizcaíno Valley and employs 36% of the population. There were 2,987.7 ha of crop land in the municipality.<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020>[https://biblioteca.setuesbcs.gob.mx/administrador/biblioteca/publicaciones/pdf/Estrategico_2023_Mulege.pdf 2020 Mulegé Estratégico]</ref> The main crops are [[tomatoes]], [[chili peppers]], [[onions]], [[cucumbers]], [[strawberries]] and [[figs]].<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/> The meat production comes principally from [[cattle]] (697.8 tons), [[pigs]] (117.6 tons), [[goats]] (14.9 tons) and [[sheep]] (11 tons). There is also a significant production of [[milk]] from goats and cows.<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/>

===Mining===
[[File:Salt production in Guerrero Negro 2.jpg|left|thumb|Salt production in Guerrero Negro]]
The mining sector is concentrated around the production of [[salt]] in [[Guerrero Negro]] (4.8 million tons), [[plaster]] in [[San Marcos, Baja California Sur|San Marcos Island]] (1.3 million ton) and [[copper]] in [[Santa Rosalía, Baja California Sur|Santa Rosalía]] (11 thousand tons), and employs 21.6% of the population.<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/>

===Fishing===
[[File:Estuary Scene - Mulege - Baja California Sur - Mexico - 02 (23643542419).jpg|right|thumb|Fishing boats at Mulegé beach]]
The fishing sector employs 10.6% of the population. There were 19,329 tons of fish products in 2021, of which [[fish]] represented more than 62% of the captures. Other products include [[lobsters]], [[sharks]], [[shellfish]] and [[octopus]].<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/> There also processing centers of [[lobster]], [[abalone]], [[sea snail|snail]] and [[mackerel]].<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/>

===Tourism===
[[File:Big Spy Hop, Laguna San Ignacio.jpg|left|thumb|Whale watching at San Ignacio Lagoon]]
The tourism sector has been growing in the last years. The municipality is home of two jesuit missions of the XVII century in [[Mulegé]] and [[San Ignacio, Baja California Sur|San Ignacio]], these two towns also have oasis, which are a popular sightview in the desert. [[Santa Rosalía, Baja California Sur|Santa Rosalía]] it's another spot, famous for being an historical mining town and their many buildings in French architectural style.

Ecotourism revolves around [[whale watching]] of [[gray whales]] at [[San Ignacio Lagoon]] and [[Guerrero Negro]] during the months of December to April. Mulegé also has many popular beaches, many of which are nearby Mulegé town.<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/>

[[File:Figura en el techo de la cueva "El Ratón".JPG|right|thumb|Cave paintings at El Ratón Cave]]
Mulegé also contains the most famous [[rock art]] sites in Baja California Sur and Mexico. The best known sites are located at [[Sierra de San Francisco]], although many of them requires trips by mule of 3–5 days. There are minor sites at [[Sierra de Guadalupe cave paintings|Sierra de Guadalupe]], nearby [[Mulegé]], which can be reached by car.<ref name=ESTRATEGICOMULEGE2020/>

The municipality has 381 localities, the largest of which (with 2020 populations in parentheses) were: [[Santa Rosalía, Baja California Sur|Santa Rosalía]] (14,357 hab.), [[Guerrero Negro]] (13,596 hab.), [[Villa Alberto Andrés Alvarado Arámburo, Baja California Sur|Villa Alberto Andrés Alvarado Arámburo]] (10,897 hab.), [[Mulegé]] (3,834 hab.), Bahía Tortugas (2,367 hab.), classified as urban; and San Francisco (1,919 hab.), Bahía Asunción (1,453 hab.), Las Margaritas (1,145 hab.), and Estero de la Bocana (1,013 hab.), classified as rural.<ref name="census2020" />

Mulegé is crossed by the Carretera Transpeninsular ([[Mexican Federal Highway 1]]), which runs from [[Tijuana]] to [[Cabo San Lucas]]. Regional flights are available at the [[Palo Verde Airport]] in Santa Rosalía.
