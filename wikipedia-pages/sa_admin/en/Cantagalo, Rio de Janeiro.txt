'''Cantagalo''' ({{IPA|pt|ˌkɐ̃tɐˈɡalu}}), formerly spelled '''Cantagallo''',{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}}{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} is a city located in the east-central area of [[Rio de Janeiro State]], [[Brazil]]. The population is 20,168 (2020 est.) in an area of 749&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref>[https://archive.today/20150411203510/http://cod.ibge.gov.br/23EPR Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística].</ref> Its elevation is {{convert|391|m|sp=us|abbr=on}}.

==History==
Colonization of Cantagalo began in 1755, when [[Portugal|Portuguese]] nobleman Manoel Henriques, Duke of Terso and a clandestine [[gold]] miner, left the state of [[Minas Gerais]] in search of unexplored riches. Henriques and his group erected a settlement{{citation needed|date=June 2016}} on a small tributary of the [[Parahyba River|Parahyba]] approximately {{convert|80|mi|sp=us|0}} northwest of [[Rio de Janeiro|Rio]]{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}} and began exploring along the local rivers. It was originally known as '''{{nowrap|Sertões de Macacu}}''' after the nearby [[Macacu River]].{{citation needed|date=June 2016}}

By 1784, the settlement had grown to accommodate approximately 200 houses. This growth caught the attention of the Portuguese rulers of Brazil, who had a [[monopoly]] over gold exploration in the [[colony]]. By order of the [[Viceroy of Brazil|Viceroy]] [[Luiz de Vasconcelos e Souza]], several expeditions were sent in search of Henriques and his group. The town's current name ([[Portuguese language|Portuguese]] for "[[rooster]] crow") was inspired by the circumstances of his capture. A troop was about to return to their camp after a day of searching in vain around the woods, when a soldier heard the crowing of a rooster nearby and decided to further explore the area. One of Henriques's men was found in a clearing in the woods and, in exchange for his release, revealed the whereabouts of the rest of the group. Henriques was deported to Africa in dishonour.{{citation needed|date=June 2016}}

By 1786, the settlement's name had been officially changed from Sertões de Macacu to Cantagalo. In 1814, Cantagalo was officially recognized by [[Pedro I of Brazil|Emperor Pedro I]] as a municipality and in October 1857, was officially elevated to the category of city.{{citation needed|date=June 2016}} By the mid-19th century, the area's gold was played out and the settlement came to depend on agriculture.{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}} [[Maize|Corn]], [[coffee]], and [[sugarcane]] plantations covered several acres of highly fertile land.{{citation needed|date=June 2016}}

Before the [[First World War]], Cantagallo was considered a rich fruit- and coffee-producing district and was connected to [[Rio de Janeiro|Rio]] via a 100-mile-long [[Cantagallo Railway|eponymous railway]].{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}}

Nowadays, the city's economic activities still revolve around agriculture, with the exploration of [[granite]] and [[calcareous rock]] for the [[cement]] industry also playing a strong role. Some of the largest cement manufacturers in Brazil have facilities in Cantagalo.{{citation needed|date=June 2016}}

==Notable residents==
Financial journalist [[Jose Carlos Rodrigues]] was the son of a coffee planter of Cantagallo, born here in 1844.
Writer [[Euclides da Cunha]] was born here on January 15, 1886; a city district is named Euclidelândia in his honour.
