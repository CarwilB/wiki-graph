'''San Cristóbal de la Barranca''' is a town and [[municipalities of Jalisco|municipality]], in [[Jalisco]] in central-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 523&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

As of 2020, the municipality had a total population of 2,924.<ref>{{cite web|title=San Cristóbal de la Barranca|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14071a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=April 16, 2009|archive-date=3 June 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070603201602/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14071a.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Geography ==
=== Limits ===
San Cristóbal de la Barranca is bounded on the north by the state of [[Zacatecas]]; on the south by the municipality of [[Zapopan]]; on the east by the municipality of [[Ixtlahuacán del Río]]; and on the west by the municipality of [[Tequila, Jalisco|Tequila]].

=== Hydrography ===

The hydrological resources of the municipality are the [[Río Grande de Santiago|Santiago]], [[Juchipila River|Juchipila]], [[Cuixtla River|Cuixtla]], Mezquital del Oro and Patitos rivers; and the streams of Mojoneras, Cuates, Agua Prieta, Fresno, La Trinidad, Saucillo, Terrero, Carrizalillo, Las Pilas, Guayabo, El Limón and Cántaros.

There are also hot springs and geysers nearby, at the ''Parque Ecológico Los Hervores'' on the Rio Patitos between San Cristóbal de la Barranca and Teúl.<ref name="Pint2019">{{cite news |author1=John Pint |title=The amazing singing geyser at a natural spa in Jalisco |url=https://mexiconewsdaily.com/mexicolife/the-amazing-singing-geyser/ |accessdate=27 September 2019 |work=Mexico News Daily |date=12 July 2019}}</ref>

== History ==

Before the arrival of the Spaniards, the region of San Cristóbal de la Barranca was inhabited by native tribes allied with [[Goaxicar]], the chief of [[Xochitepec]]. In 1530, [[Cristóbal de Oñate]], a Spanish captain in the army of [[Nuño de Guzmán]], arrived here to serve as acting governor. In 1538, during the administration of [[Diego Pérez de la Torre]], governor of [[Nueva Galicia]], the native population revolted against Spanish rule. The governor personally organized the suppression of the insurgents, but his forces were defeated and he was killed when he fell from his horse.<ref name="Topete1951">{{cite book|author=Jesús Amaya Topete|title=Ameca, protofundación mexicana: historia de la propiedad del valle de Ameca, Jalisco, y circunvecindad|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=8IpnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA108|year=1951|publisher=Editorial Lumen|page=108}}</ref> In 1540, the inhabitants participated in the [[Mixton War]].{{fact|date=November 2021}}
[[File:SAN+CRISTÓBAL+DE+LA+BARRANCA.jpg|alt=San Cristobal de la Barranco temple|thumb|San Cristobal de la Barranco temple]]

From the second half of the sixteenth century to the first decades of the nineteenth century, San Cristóbal de la Barranca had a corregimiento character. In his ''Description of New Galicia'', Domingo Lázaro de Arregui refers to San Cristóbal de la Barranca in the following terms: "Under the jurisdiction of Tlacotlán, 3 leagues more or less from [[Guadalajara (Mexico)| Guadalajara]], to the north, near the road of those who go to Zacatecas and the other band of the river, is the town of San Cristóbal, in the same canyon where the river runs, which is a very deep canyon, and is the reason they call it 'de la Barranca'. It is the seat of this district and has many villages under its jurisdiction: [[Tecsistlán]], {{ill|Cópala, Jalisco|lt=Cópala|es|Cópala (Jalisco)}}, [[San Esteban (Jalisco) | San Esteban]], [[Ixcatlán, Jalisco|Ixcatlán]] and San Juan ...; these towns are of but few people ... The Indians of this jurisdiction take honey from maguey. There are very few people in this band; They are the doctrine of clergymen of the Ocotlan party. The others are administered by friars of San Francisco from the convent of Xuchipila".

These writings refer to the year 1621, the date on which the aforementioned work was carried out.

In 1825 it had a town hall and belonged to the [[Zapopan]] department of 1st canton of Guadalajara; It was integrated to this canton from this year until the suppression of that form of political-territorial organization.

In 1838 San Cristobal obtained the status of town; and in 1895 with the same category included 13 police stations.

The current town dates from 1875, the year in which a strong tremor destroyed the previous one. Produced by the [[volcano]] "[[El Ceboruco]]", at 11:30 p.m. from February 11 of the aforementioned year an underground noise was heard, a trepidatory tremor immediately arose that was repeated five minutes later, leaving the population in ruins. There the town was founded again.

The date of its beginning status as a municipality is not known, although the decree of 1 May 1886, which indicates the Territorial and Political Division of the State of [[Jalisco]], already mentions it as such.
