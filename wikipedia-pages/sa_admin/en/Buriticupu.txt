'''Buriticupu''' is a municipality in [[Maranhão]] founded in 1994.  Its population in 2020 was 72,983.

The area has suffered significant [[gully]] erosion with about 30 gullies (in April 2024), one of which is 80m deep. These are called “voçoroca” or “torn land” in the indigenous [[Tupi–Guarani languages|Tupi-Guarani]] language. At least 50 houses have been destroyed by the erosion and other residents have abandoned their homes, leaving whole neighbourhoods deserted. 

The area, part of the [[Amazon rainforest]], was once covered in trees, such as [[List of plants known as cedar|cedar]], [[Hymenaea courbaril|West Indian locust]] and [[Handroanthus|ipê]]. In the 1990s the area had over 50 sawmills working 24 hours a day which depleted the vegetation which increased run-off, resulting in rapid [[soil erosion]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Suzuki |first=Shin |date=23 April 2024 |title=The vast ravines swallowing whole neighbourhoods around the world - BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/resources/idt-61d57e25-d458-4785-8a37-3ef119a504c7 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240423012452/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/resources/idt-61d57e25-d458-4785-8a37-3ef119a504c7 |archive-date=23 April 2024 |access-date=23 April 2024 |website=News |language=en-GB}}</ref> The area has a volume of rainfall over 2,000 liters per square metre each year.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Martín |first=Gloria |date=14 November 2023 |title=Buriticupu: the city in the Brazilian Amazon that is being swallowed by the earth |url=https://www.yourweather.co.uk/news/trending/buriticupu-the-city-in-brazilian-amazon-swallowed-by-the-earth-geology.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240423042331/https://www.yourweather.co.uk/news/trending/buriticupu-the-city-in-brazilian-amazon-swallowed-by-the-earth-geology.html |archive-date=23 April 2024 |access-date=23 April 2024 |website=Yourweather.co.uk {{!}} Meteored |language=en-GB}}</ref>
