'''Huanta District''' is one of eight [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of the [[Huanta Province]] in [[Peru]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=2008-04-23 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> Its seat is [[Huanta]].

== Geography ==
One of the highest peaks of the district is [[Rasuwillka (Huanta-La Mar)|Rasuwillka]] at approximately {{convert|4800|m|ft|abbr=on}}. Other mountains are listed below:<ref>escale.minedu.gob.pe/ UGEL map Huanta Province (Ayacucho Region)</ref>

* Ch'aki Qucha
* Mataru Q'asa
* Quri Willka
* Titi Q'asa
* Yana Waqra

Some of the largest lakes of the district are Qarqarqucha, Ichmaqucha, Mituqucha, Muruqucha, Chakaqucha, San Antonio, [[Yanaqucha (Huanta)|Yanaqucha]], [[Pampaqucha (Ayacucho)|Pampaqucha]], Pisququcha, Jarjaqucha and Hatun Tiklla.

The most important river is the [[Kachimayu (Ayacucho-Huancavelica)|Kachimayu]] ("salt river") on the western border of the district. It is an affluent of the [[Mantaro River|Mantaro]] River.

== Ethnic groups ==
The people in the district are mainly [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Quechua language|Quechua]] is the language which the majority of the population (52.78%) learnt to speak in childhood, 46.89% of the residents started speaking using the [[Spanish language|Spanish]] language ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=2013-01-27 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007, Frequencias: Preguntas de Población: Idioma o lengua con el que aprendió hablar (in Spanish)</ref>
