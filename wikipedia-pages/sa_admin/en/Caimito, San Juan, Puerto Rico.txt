'''Caimito''' is one of the 18 [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrios]] of the municipality of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico]] located in the mountainous part of the municipality. With a land area of {{convert|5.41|sqmi|km2}}, Caimito is the second largest [[Barrios of San Juan, Puerto Rico|barrio]] in the [[municipality]] of [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], [[Puerto Rico]]. In [[2020 United States Census|2020]] it had 19,547 inhabitants and a population density of 4,034.2 per sq.mi. Located in the southern part of San Juan, Caimito is bordered by barrios [[Tortugo, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Tortugo]] and [[Quebrada Arenas, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Quebrada Arenas]] to the west, by [[Monacillo, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Monacillo]] to the north, by [[Cupey, San Juan, Puerto Rico|Cupey]] to the east and by the municipality of [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]] to the south.<ref name="Pico_1969">{{cite book |last1=Picó |first1=Rafael |last2=Buitrago de Santiago |first2=Zayda |last3=Berrios |first3=Hector H. |title=Nueva geografía de Puerto Rico: física, económica, y social, por Rafael Picó. ==History==
The name Caimito comes from the [[Pouteria|Caimito tree]], which is common in the region. The word ''caimito'' is possibly a [[Spanish language|Spanish]] adaptation (''-ito'' being the Spanish [[diminutive]]) of the [[Taíno language|Taino]] word ''caima'', possibly related to the Carib ''acayouman'', meaning crocodile (see [[caiman]]).<ref>{{Citation|title=caiman|date=2021-01-25|url=https://en.wiktionary.org/w/index.php?title=caiman&oldid=61654998|work=Wiktionary|language=en|access-date=2021-10-13}}</ref>

Assigned as a suburb of [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]] in 1835 by [[Pedro Tomás de Córdova]], who was [[Governor of Puerto Rico|Captain General of Puerto Rico]] from 1816 to 1836, Caimito consisted of 3,595.30 acres or land or roughly 5.16 square miles.<ref>[http://cauce.uprrp.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/rio_piedras_notas_para_su_historia.pdf Ocasio Meléndez, Marcial E. 1985. Río Piedras (Ciudad Universitaria): Notas para su historia. San Juan: Comité Historia de los Pueblos.]</ref> Caimito comprises the former barrios of Caimito Alto and Caimito Bajo, which were last documented in the 1899 census.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n251 160]| language=es}}</ref> Caimito was part of Rio Piedras until 1951 when it was merged as a barrio of the municipality of San Juan. Historian Dr. [[Fernando Picó]] [[Society of Jesus|S.J.]], wrote a book about the history and genealogy of the area, published in 1989, called ''Vivir en Caimito''.<ref>[http://www.upr.edu/biblioteca-upru/wp-content/uploads/sites/78/2019/04/Fernando-Pic%C3%B3-Bibliograf%C3%ADa.pdf Bibliografía Fernando Picó - Universidad de Puerto Rico. Selected Works.]</ref>

The neighborhood commissioner (''comisario de barrio'' was the main representative of Caimito to the Mayor of [[Río Piedras]]. By the 19th century, they were the main local representatives of an absent state. Most of them were not originally from Caimito, but instead were people who had settled there, usually businessmen and farmers. This position was not compensated properly but they garnered some power and prestige.

==Geography==
Caimito is located in the mountainous area of San Juan. The municipality's highest point, an unnamed peak measuring almost 1,030 feet (314 m) above sea level, close to the municipal border with [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]], is located in this barrio close to the Sendra Forest Park on the Morcelo sector.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Áreas naturales protegidas y bosques urbanos – Estuario|date=2 October 2019 |url=https://estuario.org/areas-naturales-protegidas-y-bosques-urbanos/|access-date=2021-10-13|language=es-ES}}</ref> Some of the most important rivers in San Juan have their source in this area too, such as the [[Piedras River (San Juan, Puerto Rico)|Piedras River]], which gives its name to [[Río Piedras, Puerto Rico|Río Piedras]] and flows into the [[Puerto Nuevo River]] and the [[San Juan Bay]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=U.S. Department of Agriculture|first=Forest Service|title=The Rio Piedras Watershed and Its Surrounding Environment|url=https://www.fs.fed.us/global/iitf/pubs/RioPiedras_FNLrvsd.pdf|url-status=live|website=fs.fed.us|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111025020403/http://www.fs.fed.us/global/iitf/pubs/RioPiedras_FNLrvsd.pdf |archive-date=2011-10-25 }}</ref>

The major routes that cross Caimito include the [[Puerto Rico Highway 199|PR-199]], PR-842 and [[Puerto Rico Highway 1|PR-1]].

It includes a fourteen-screen theater by [[Caribbean Cinemas]] with [[IMAX]], CXC and [[4DX]] screens.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Outlets at Montehiedra |url=https://montehiedratowncenter.com/}}</ref>
