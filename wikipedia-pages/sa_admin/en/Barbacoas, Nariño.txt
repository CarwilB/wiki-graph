'''Barbacoas''' is a town and municipality in [[Nariño Department]], [[Colombia]].<ref>{{GEOnet2|32FA88428CE03774E0440003BA962ED3|Barbacoas (Approved)}}, [[National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency|United States National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]]</ref> The urban centre of Barbacoas is located at an altitude of {{convert|36|m|ft}} and the municipality borders [[Magüí Payán]] in the north, Magüí Payán, [[Cumbitara]], [[Los Andes, Nariño|Los Andes]], [[La Llanada]], [[Samaniego, Nariño|Samaniego]] and [[Ricaurte, Nariño|Ricaurte]] in the east, Ricaurte and [[Ecuador]] in the south and [[Tumaco]] and [[Roberto Payán]] in the west.<ref name=websiteBarbacoas>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.barbacoas-narino.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Barbacoas] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923182217/http://www.barbacoas-narino.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml |date=2015-09-23 }}</ref>

== History ==
In the times before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]], Barbacoas was inhabited by the Barbacoa, Telembí and Iscuande tribes. Already in those ages, the town was an important source of [[gold]] for the [[indigenous peoples of Colombia|indigenous people]].<ref name=websiteBarbacoas/>

Modern Barbacoas was founded on April 6, 1600, by Francisco de Praga y Zuniga.<ref name=websiteBarbacoas/>

== Economy ==
Barbacoas is an important mining town in Nariño, producing gold, [[silver]], [[platinum]] and [[coal]].<ref name=UPMEgold>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.upme.gov.co/generadorconsultas/Consulta_Series.aspx?idModulo=4&tipoSerie=116&grupo=496& Producción de oro] - UPME</ref><ref name=UPMEsilver>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.upme.gov.co/GeneradorConsultas/Consulta_Series.aspx?idmodulo=4&tiposerie=117&grupo=502 Producción de plata]{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }} - UPME</ref><ref name=UPMEplatinum>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.upme.gov.co/GeneradorConsultas/Consulta_Series.aspx?idmodulo=4&tiposerie=118&grupo=503 Producción de platino] - UPME</ref><ref name=UPMEcoal>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.upme.gov.co/generadorconsultas/Consulta_Series.aspx?idModulo=4&tipoSerie=121&grupo=493 Producción de carbón] - UPME</ref> Other economical activity is agriculture, with [[rice]], [[avocado]]es, [[cooking banana|plantains]], [[sugarcane]] and fruits as [[banana]]s and citrus fruits. Other fruits cultivated are [[ciruelo]], [[guayaba]], [[papaya]], [[pineapple]]s, [[guanábana]], [[borojó]], [[guayabilla]], [[lulo]], [[anón]], [[guaba]], [[maracuyá]], [[guayaba brasilera]], [[zapallo]], [[coconut]], [[Theobroma cacao|cacao]] and other agricultural products as [[arracacha]], [[camote]], [[ñame]], [[achiote]], [[chillangua]], [[tomato]]es, [[Capsicum|peppers]], [[palmito]], [[chillarán]], and [[oregano]].<ref name=websiteBarbacoas/>

== Strike ==
The town is infamous for its 2011 "crossed legs" strike, where women in the town foreswore sexual activity pending action on the promised paved road to their town.<ref>{{Cite news|author=News Desk |title=Colombia: Women's sex strike gets husbands' support |date=28 June 2011 |newspaper=Global Post |url=http://www.globalpost.com/dispatches/news/regions/americas/colombia/colombian-women-sex-strike-men-hunger-strike |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120921014825/http://www.globalpost.com/dispatches/news/regions/americas/colombia/colombian-women-sex-strike-men-hunger-strike |archive-date=2012-09-21 }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|author=Montes, Euclides|title=Colombia's 'crossed legs' protest is redefining women's activism|date=1 August 2011|newspaper=The Guardian|url=https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2011/aug/01/colombia-crossed-legs-sex-strike}}</ref><ref name="Huff">{{Cite news|author=Staff|title=Colombia: Sex Strike Ends In Barbacoas|date=12 October 2011|newspaper=Huffington Post|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2011/10/12/colombia-sex-strike_n_1007778.html}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|author=Staff|title=Colombian women end 'crossed legs' abstinence protest for new road|date=17 October 2011|newspaper=The Daily Telegraph|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/worldnews/southamerica/colombia/8830666/Colombian-women-end-crossed-legs-abstinence-protest-for-new-road.html}}</ref>
