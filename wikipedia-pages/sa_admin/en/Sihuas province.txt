'''Sihuas''' ([[Quechua language|Quechua]] '''Siwas''') is one of twenty provinces of the [[Ancash Region]] in [[Peru]].This province was created by Law nº 13485, dated at January 9, 1961, when Manuel Prado was president of Peru.<ref>Back page of book "Cómo resurge una Provincia Bolivariana" {{ISBN|978-612-00-1099-0}}</ref>  It is bordered by provinces of [[Huaylas Province|Huaylas]] and [[Corongo Province|Corongo]] on the west, [[Pallasca Province|Pallasca]] on the north, [[La Libertad Region]] on the east, and [[Pomabamba Province|Pomabamba]] on the south.<ref>{{in lang|es}} Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Digital] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=2008-04-23 }}''. Retrieved June 15, 2008.</ref>

== Ethnic groups ==
The province is inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Spanish language|Spanish]] is the language which the majority of the population (62.89%) learnt to speak in childhood, 36.83% of the residents started speaking using the [[Quechua language|Quechua]] language ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=2013-01-27 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007</ref>

==Earthquake of November 10, 1946==
On November 10, 1946, a [[Moment magnitude scale|magnitude]] 7.3 [[earthquake]] struck the province.  The epicenter is estimated to have occurred at 8°20' S. 77°50' W. at a depth of 30 – 40&nbsp;km. The surface fault was observed to run about 18&nbsp;km northwest from Quiches toward Conchucos.  The quake was "the first well-observed instance of major faulting."<ref>[[Charles F. Richter]], ''Elementary Seismology'' (San Francisco, W.H. Freeman and Co. 1958) p. 768, quoted in [https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/world/events/1946_11_10 Historic Earthquakes].</ref> The fault was purely slip-dip (vertical), with an offset of as much as 3.5 meters (11&nbsp;feet).  The quake and resulting landslides resulted in a death toll estimated at 1400 - 1700,<ref>Newspaper "El Comercio" of Lima, November 1946, National Library of Peru</ref> a substantial number given the sparse population in the area.  The village of Acobamba was buried by one landslide, killing 217.
