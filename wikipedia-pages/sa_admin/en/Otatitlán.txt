'''Otatitlán''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Veracruz|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Veracruz]], in the south of the state along the border with the state of [[Oaxaca]]. The town is best known for its large black image of a crucified Christ, one of three notable images of this type. Most of the population in Otatitlán is poor and the area is dedicated to agriculture, especially sugar cane and bananas.

==The town==
The town of Otatitlán is about 180&nbsp;km south of the state capital, near the state's border with Oaxaca.<ref name=encmuc>{{cite web |title=Otatitlán |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=INAFED |year=2010 |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM30veracruz/municipios/30119a.html |access-date=May 18, 2014 |archive-date=May 19, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140519040417/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM30veracruz/municipios/30119a.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> All but a couple hundred of the municipality's population lives here.<ref name=sefiplan>{{cite web |title=Otatitlán |work=Sistema de Información Municipal Cuadernillos Municipales 2014 |publisher=Secretaria de Finanzas y Planeación del Estado de Veracruz |url= http://www.veracruz.gob.mx/finanzas/files/2013/04/Otatitlan.pdf |access-date=May 18, 2014 }}</ref> It is centered on a large plaza surrounded by wide streets. The main church is San Andrés, which contains the image of the Cristo Negro, or Black Christ that the town is noted for.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=rinden>{{cite news |title=Feligreses católicos rinden honores al Cristo negro de Otatitlán |newspaper=El Piñero de la Cuenca |location=Tuxtepec |date=May 4, 2014 |url=http://www.elpinerodelacuenca.com.mx/epc/index.php/policiaca/72871-feligreses-catolicos-rinden-honores-al-cristo-negro-de-otatitlan |access-date=May 18, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140519041800/http://www.elpinerodelacuenca.com.mx/epc/index.php/policiaca/72871-feligreses-catolicos-rinden-honores-al-cristo-negro-de-otatitlan |archive-date=May 19, 2014 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Traditional music and dance of Otatitlán include [[Son Jarocho]] and [[Danzón]]. Traditional dishes include machuco ([[Plantain (cooking)|plantain]]s fried with green chile and garlic) and tapistes, a dish with plantains, chicken, acuyo ([[Piper auritum]]), tomato and chili pepper.<ref name=encmuc/>

==The municipality==
As a municipality, the town of Otatitlán is the local government for thirty other communities, which combined have a territory of 53.46&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=sefiplan/> The municipality borders those of [[Cosamaloapan]] and [[Tlacojalpan]] with the state of Oaxaca to the south. The municipal government consists of a municipal president, a syndic and one representative for the other communities.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=sefiplan/>

The municipality is rural with a population density of 104.8 people/km<sup>2</sup>, with only the seat considered urban. Outside the seat the only communities of any size are El Peladiente (pop. 111), Jesus Urueta (pop. 90), La Conchita (pop. 72) and La Isleta (pop. 58).<ref name=sefiplan/> Only 210 people are considered to live in indigenous households, where the head speaks an indigenous language. Only 81 speak an indigenous language, of which [[Mazatecan languages|Mazateco]] dominates.<ref name=sefiplan/>

The municipality has eight schools - two preschools, four primary schools, one middle school and one high school - with an illiteracy rate of 11.8%. It also has 16&nbsp;km of federal highway.<ref name=sefiplan/>

==Black Christ of Otatitlán==
[[File:SanctuaryBlackChristOtatitlan11.JPG|thumb|The "Black Christ" of Otatitlán]]
Otatitlán is best known for a large image of a crucified Christ which is black, known as a "Cristo Negro" or Black Christ. It is one of three well known images of this type, with the others in [[Chalma, Malinalco|Chalma, State of Mexico]] and [[Esquipulas]], [[Guatemala]].<ref name=mexdes>{{cite web |title= El Cristo Negro de Otatitlán, Veracruz |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/el-cristo-negro-de-otatitlan-veracruz.html |access-date=May 18, 2014 }}</ref>

There are two stories of the origin of this particular image. One states the [[Philip II of Spain|Spanish king Philip II]] had the three Black Christ images commissioned, which then went on to their current locations.<ref name=rinden/> Another story says that the image was found on the [[Day of the Cross]] by an indigenous person at the foot of a tree in the village of Puctlanzingo, which was then claimed by the Mazatecos of the region. Later it was moved on the [[Papaloapan River]] on a raft to its current location.<ref name=rinden/><ref name=mexdes/>

According to researcher [[Carlos Navarrete Cáceres|Carlos Navarrete]], the phenomenon of "Black Christ" images follows known Aztec merchant ([[pochteca]]) routes. It is possible that these images were a substitution for the deity [[Yacatecuhtli|Yacatgecutli]], the god of commerce, which was also black.<ref name=mexdes/>

During the [[Cristero War]] in the 1930s, the image was taken by men allied with then governor of Veracruz, Adalberto Tejada, who tried unsuccessfully to burn it. Instead it was decapitated. The headless image returned to Otatitlán and a replacement was made. In 1950, the original head was found and returned, with the replacement head now kept in a glass case.<ref name=mexdes/><ref name=encmuc/>

The devotion to this image is a [[syncretism]]. It is not unusual for there to be a mass followed by a ritual cleansing by a local "brujo" (roughly witch doctor).<ref name=mexdes/> The major feast day for this image is 3 May, Day of the Cross, with events beginnings on 28 April and extending to 7 May. The event can attract up to 150,000 people from Veracruz and other parts of Mexico.<ref name=rinden/>

==Geography==
[[File:CanalOtatitlan04.JPG|thumb|irrigation canal with egrets]]
The area of the Otatitlán town is flat with a few small hills and an average altitude of ten meters above sea level. The soil is loose and susceptible to erosion. The territory is defined by the [[Papaloapan River]], with the only other river of note, the Obispo, a tributary.<ref name=encmuc/>
===Climate===
In 84% of the municipality, the climate is warm and mostly moist with rains in the summer. In the rest it is warm and very moist with very abundant rain in the summer. Average annual temperature is between 24 and 26C with the average annual rainfall varying between 1900 and 2100mm.<ref name=sefiplan/>
===Flora and fauna===
The wild vegetation here was high and medium tropical forest, but today almost all of the territory (45.9 km<sup>2</sup>) is dedicated to agriculture, 2.8 km<sup>2</sup> is covered by water and 1.4 km<sup>2</sup> is urban. Wildlife includes rabbits, [[coati]]s, [[raccoon]]s, [[Coyote|coyotes]], doves, parrots, [[egret]]s and various reptiles.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=sefiplan/>

==Socioeconomics==
[[File:BananaOtatitlan02.JPG|thumb|Banana trees in the municipality]]
The municipality is rural with 32.2% of the workforce involved in agriculture and livestock, 18.8% in industry (mostly sugar cane processing) and 48.8% in commerce and services.<ref name=sefiplan/> It is poor with 61.4% of the population under the poverty line and 12.1% living in extreme poverty.<ref name=sefiplan/> However, because of the availability of schools, medical care, running water, electricity, the area is considered to have only a medium level of socioeconomic marginalization.<ref name=sefiplan/>

Main crops are sugar cane, bananas and corn. Livestock is split between cattle and pigs, followed by domestic fowl and sheep.<ref name=sefiplan/>

==History==
The name "Otatitlán" comes from [[Nahuatl]] and means "place of the otates ([[Mexican weeping bamboo]])," given by traveling merchants when the Aztecs reached here.<ref name=encmuc/> The pre Hispanic population was [[Popoluca]], which changed to [[Mazateco]] during the colonial period. From the pre Hispanic into the colonial period, it was a trading post for the indigenous population.<ref name=encmuc/><ref name=mexdes/>

Jesuit missionaries founded the mission of '''San Ignacio Otatitlan'''.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Bancroft |first1=Hubert Howe |last2=Oak |first2=Henry Lebbeus |last3=Peatfield |first3=Joseph Joshua |last4=Nemos |first4=William |title=History of the North Mexican States and Texas |date=1884 |publisher=History Company |page=341 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IaFFAQAAMAAJ |access-date=9 May 2024 |language=en}}</ref>

The town itself was founded by the Marquis of Guadalcazar, Diego Fernández de Córdoba, dedicated to the [[Apostle Andrew]].<ref name=mexdes/> The village gained municipality status in 1831, bordering Tlacojalpan, Tuxtepec, Tsochoacan and Las Lomas.<ref name=encmuc/> Otatitlán was officially recognized as a town in 1958 and its coat of arms approved in 1985.<ref name=encmuc/>
