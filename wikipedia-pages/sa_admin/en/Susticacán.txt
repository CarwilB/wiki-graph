'''Susticacán''' (from [[Purépecha language|Purépecha]] ''Etsosticacán'', derived from ''et'', "gully"; ''sost'', "strong"; and ''can'', "place"<ref name=encyclopedia>{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/32043a.html |title=Susticacán |language=es |website=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=[[INAFED]] |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref>) is a [[Municipalities of Zacatecas|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Zacatecas]], located approximately {{convert|55|km}} west of [[Zacatecas City]], the state capital.

==Geography==
The municipality of Susticacán lies at an elevation between {{convert|2000|and(-)|3000|m}} in the {{ill|Sierra de los Cardos|es|Sierra de Cardos}} in west-central Zacatecas.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/32/32043.pdf |title=Susticacán, Zacatecas |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos |language=es |publisher=INEGI |year=2009 |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last1=Hurtado Reveles |first1=Leopoldo |last2=Burgos Hernández |first2=Mireya |last3=Vázquez Sánchez |first3=Monserrat |last4=López Acosta |first4=Juan Carlos |date=2022 |title=Contribution to the floristic knowledge of the Sierra de los Cardos, Susticacán, Zacatecas, Mexico |trans-title=Contribución al conocimiento florístico de la Sierra de los Cardos, Susticacán, Zacatecas, México |journal=Botanical Sciences |url=https://www.botanicalsciences.com.mx/index.php/botanicalSciences/article/view/2882 |volume=100 |issue=1 |pages=247–262 |language=es |access-date=3 January 2022 |doi=10.17129/botsci.2882|doi-access=free }}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Jerez de García Salinas|Jerez]] to the northwest, [[Tepetongo]] to the south, [[Monte Escobedo]] to the southwest, and [[Valparaíso, Zacatecas|Valparaíso]] to the northwest.<ref name=compendium/> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|199.6|km2}} and comprises 0.3% of the state's area.<ref name=panorama/>

The land cover in Susticacán comprises temperate forest (58%), grassland (20%), tropical forest (16%), agricultural land (4%), and built-up areas (1%).

===Hydrography===
The municipality lies in the basin of the [[Bolaños River]] in the [[Río Grande de Santiago]] system.<ref name=compendium/> It is drained by seasonal streams, the most important of these being the Susticacán which flows east into the [[Jerez River (Mexico)|Jerez River]], and the Chiquihuite which flows south into the [[Huejúcar River]].<ref name=compendium/> The Jerez and Huejúcar rivers combine at Las Liebres in Jalisco to form the [[Colotlán River]]. The municipality's largest reservoir is the Susticacán Reservoir on the Susticacán River, which has a total capacity of {{convert|4500000|m3}}.<ref>{{cite book |title=Cuadro 4.16: Capacidad total y útil de almacenamiento, y volumen anual utilizado de las presas por municipio y presa, 2016 |work=Anuario estadístico y geográfico de Zacatecas 2017 |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/anuarios_2017/702825092047.pdf |language=Spanish |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref>

===Climate===
Susticacán's climate ranges from temperate in the higher elevations of the west to semiarid in the east, with dry winters. Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|12|and(-)|18|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|500|and(-)|800|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==Etymology==
The name Susticacán is believed to be derived from the [[Purépecha language|Tarascan]] ''etsosticacán,'' meaning "place of the strong canyon." The modified Susticacán is a result of changing dialects of the time.<ref>{{cite web|title=Plan municipal de desarrollo Susticacán, 2014-206|url=http://transparenciasusticacan.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/plan-anual-de-desarrollo-1.pdf|website=H. Ayuntamiento de Susticacán|accessdate=27 April 2015}}</ref>

==History==
The first indigenous inhabitants of the region, a Zacatecos band of [[Chichimeca]], were not subdued by force from Spanish conquistadors, but rather became integrated into the government of New Spain with the establishment of the Catholic faith. The town of Santo Tomás de Etsosticacán was founded in 1562 by local indigenous peoples under the leadership of Andrés Melchor. The Spaniards originally permitted the natives to live under their own laws with Melchor as their first leader.

The original land allocation was imprecise and in 1597 the area was reduced to fewer than 800 hectares. In the 17th century, this led to the acquisition of additional land due to social organization and the collective efforts of residents, including the work of religious congregations such as the Brotherhood of the [[Assumption of Mary|Virgin of the Assumption]] (also called ''La Virgen del Rayo'').

In 1648, Juan Antonio, the indigenous steward of the Brotherhood, purchased a large tract of land the mountains and with his family founded the ranch ''Chiquihuitillo'', a community in the municipality of Susticacán known today as El Chiquihuite. In the years that followed, the boundaries of the municipality continued to expand with the formation of additional communities into the late 18th century.

The town later switched its patronage first to St. [[Peter of Alcántara]], and then in 1677 to the Virgin of the Immaculate Conception; a church dedicated to her was built in 1704.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

After Mexican independence, Susticacán became part of the ''partido'' or district of Jerez. A municipal government (''junta municipal'') was established for the town on 29 October 1833. After ''partidos'' were abolished in 1916, Susticacán became an independent municipality.<ref name=division>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Zacatecas. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/integracion/pais/divi_terri/1810-1985/zac/ZACATECAS.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=[[INEGI]] |isbn=970-13-1519-7 |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref> A dirt road connecting Susticacán to Jerez was built in 1960; Susticacán Reservoir was built from 1962 to 1965; and the municipal seat was electrified in 1968.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Administration==
The municipal government of Susticacán comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''),<ref name=encyclopedia/> and seven trustees (''regidores''), four elected by [[relative majority]] and three by [[proportional representation]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://igualdad.ine.mx/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Lineamientos-de-Paridad-Zacatecas.pdf |title=Lineamientos para el registro de candidaturas a cargos de elección popular de los partidos políticos y coaliciones |language=es |pages=12–13 |date=27 November 2017 |publisher=Instituto Electoral del Estado de Zacatecas |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref> The current president of the municipality is Fabiola Rodríguez Saldívar.<ref>{{cite web |last=Alvarado |first=Silvia |date=8 June 2021 |url=http://portico.mx/2021/06/08/quienes-ganaron-en-los-municipios-de-zacatecas |title=Va por Zacatecas gana más municipios |language=es |website=Pórtico |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Susticacán is the least populous municipality in Zacatecas. In the 2020 Mexican Census, it recorded a population of 1365 inhabitants living in 404 households.<ref name=panorama>{{cite book |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/702825198053.pdf |title=Panorama sociodemográfico de Zacatecas. Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 |pages=100–101 |language=es |publisher=INEGI |date=2021 |accessdate=3 January 2022}}</ref> The 2010 Census recorded a population of 1360 inhabitants in Susticacán.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=32&mun=043 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Susticacán |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[SEDESOL]] |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref>

There are eight inhabited localities in the municipality,<ref name=panorama/> of which only the municipal seat, also called Susticacán, is classified as urban.<ref name=catalog/> It recorded a population of 929 inhabitants in the 2020 Census.<ref name=panorama/>

==Economy==
According to the 2015 Intercensal Survey, 22% of Susticacán's workforce was employed in the primary sector, 45% in the secondary sector, 5% in commerce, and 27% in services.<ref>{{cite book |title=Cuadro 10.5: Población ocupada por municipio y su distribución porcentual según sector de actividad económica, al 15 de marzo de 2015 |work=Anuario estadístico y geográfico de Zacatecas 2017 |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/anuarios_2017/702825092047.pdf |language=Spanish |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref> Economic activities in the municipality include farming of silage corn and oats,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://coepla.zacatecas.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Susticacán.docx |title=Información estadística básica. Municipio: 043 Susticacán |language=es |publisher=Coordinación Estatal de Planeación |date=17 June 2021 |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref> and tourism.<ref>{{cite web |year=2018 |url=https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/susticacan-el-pueblito-secreto-de-zacatecas.html |title=Susticacán, el pueblito secreto de Zacatecas |language=es |website=México Desconocido |access-date=3 January 2022}}</ref>
