'''Tingambato''' is a [[Municipalities of Michoacán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Michoacán]]. Its municipal seat is the city of the same name. The municipality has an area of 188.77 square kilometres (0.32% of the surface of the state) and is bordered by the north by the municipalities of [[Nahuatzén]] and [[Erongarícuaro]], to the east by [[Pátzcuaro]] and [[Salvador Escalante]], to the south by [[Ziracuaretiro]], and to the west by [[Uruapan]]. The municipality had a population of 17,630 inhabitants according to the 2015 census.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |title=2005 Census |work=INEGI: Instituto Nacional de Estadística, Geografía e Informática |accessdate=2007-10-04 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110613183420/http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |archivedate=2011-06-13 }}</ref> Its municipal seat is the city of the same name.
==Name==
The word Tingambato is of [[Chichimeca]] origin and it means "Hill of mild climate".<ref>[http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/tingambato/index.php Municipios de Michoacán] {{Webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20071216033846/http://www.municipiosmich.gob.mx/tingambato/index.php |date=2007-12-16 }} ''Portal de los municipios: Tingambato''. Retrieved on October 4, 2007.</ref> 
==History==
The story of the region can be traced 1,300 years to the arrival of the [[Purépecha monarchy]] to the region.

The site had two period of construction. In AD 450-600 ceremonial buildings with [[Teotihuacan]]-style taluds were built. In the second stage (AD 600-950) a ball court was also built in the Teotihuacan style. One tomb contains 6 men who provide evidence of dental mutilation.<ref>Ancient Mexico and Central America</ref>
