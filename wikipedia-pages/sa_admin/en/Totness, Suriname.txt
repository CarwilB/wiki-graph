'''Totness''' is a town in [[Suriname]], located in the [[Coronie]] district, of which it is the capital. Totness is the oldest settlement in the district.<ref name="history_coronie"/>

==History==
Totness was settled by [[Scotland|Scottish]] and English colonists from 1808 onward, and is named after [[Totnes]], England.<ref>[[Tessa Leuwsha]], Reishandboek Suriname, {{ISBN|9789038924939}}, 2017</ref><ref name="history_coronie">{{Cite web|url=http://www.coronie.nl/wordpress/?page_id=21|title=Geschiedenis van Coronie|website=Coronie.nl|access-date=8 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> In 1863, the area around Totness was designated for independent agriculture.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch West-Indië - Page 441 - Landbouw|website=[[Digital Library for Dutch Literature]]|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/benj004ency01_01/benj004ency01_01.pdf|date=1916|access-date=5 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> A market and a District Commissioner's Office on the former [[plantation]] Friendship were added to the resort. In the 1940s, a road was built linking Totness with [[Paramaribo]] which is nowadays part of the [[East-West Link (Suriname)|East-West Link]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.coronie.nl/wordpress/?page_id=122|title=Totness|access-date=8 May 2020|language=nl|website=Coronie.nl}}</ref>

The Suriname-Guyana Submarine Cable System has its landing station in Totness. It connects the telecommunications networks in Suriname with those in [[Guyana]] and [[Trinidad]] and from Trinidad to the rest of the world.<ref>[http://www.subtelforum.com/issues/STF_52.pdf Submarine Telecoms Forum], Issue 52, retrieved August 1, 2010</ref> The [[Totness Airstrip]] is one of the oldest airports in Suriname, in use since 1953, when the [[Piper Cub]] (PZ-NAC) of Kappel-van Eyck named "Colibri" landed there from [[Zorg en Hoop Airport]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://issuu.com/kitpublishers/docs/flying_on_trusted_wings_nederlandse_versie/27|title=Flying on trusted wings|language=nl|website=Issuu|access-date=9 May 2020}}</ref>

Totness has been designated as a regional centre, and is planned to be upgrade with a medium sized [[hotel]] and a proper city centre.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.planningofficesuriname.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/STRUCTUUR-ANALYSE-IV.pdf|title=STRUCTUUR ANALYSE DISTRICTEN  2009-2013|website=Planning Office Suriname|access-date=22 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> The village of [[Friendship, Suriname|Friendship]] is located is on the north side of the East-West link, and Totness is to the south.

==Sports==
The [[Letitia Vriesde Sportcomplex]] is a multi-purpose stadium located in Totness. It is home to [[SVB Eerste Klasse]] club [[F.C. West United]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.worldstadiums.com/south_america/countries/suriname.shtml |title=Stadiums in Suriname |publisher=Worldstadiums.com |accessdate=6 April 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121006162654/http://www.worldstadiums.com/south_america/countries/suriname.shtml |archive-date=6 October 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Tata Colin==
{{anchor|Tata Colin}}Tata Colin (circa 1806 - 1836) was a slave on the plantation Leasowes near Totness. In 1835, he attempted a [[slave rebellion]]. His intention was to free all the slaves, but he was betrayed, taken to [[Paramaribo]] where he was tortured and tried.<ref name="ruud_mungroo">{{Cite web|url=https://dbnl.nl/tekst/mung003tata01_01/mung003tata01_01_0001.php|title=Tata Colin|first=Ruud Mungroo|date=1982|last=Digital Library for Dutch Literature|author-link=Digital Library for Dutch Literature|language=nl|access-date=8 May 2020}}</ref> Colin was taken to [[Fort Zeelandia (Paramaribo)|Fort Zeelandia]] to be hung, but died<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.spanglefish.com/slavesandhighlanders/index.asp?pageid=612872|title=Tata Colin|website=Spangle Fish|access-date=8 May 2020}}</ref><ref name="tijdschrift"/> or vanished using black magic,<ref name="ruud_mungroo"/> before his sentence was carried out. His followers were sentenced to hard labour or public corporal punishment.<ref name="tijdschrift">{{cite web|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_oso001199401_01/_oso001199401_01_0005.php?q=welgelegen%20coronie#hl17|title=OSO. Tijdschrift voor Surinaamse taalkunde, letterkunde en geschiedenis. Jaargang 13|date=1994|access-date=21 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>

A statue had been erected to Tata Colin in the central square of Totness, and the local school had been named after him.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.andasuriname.com/201cult/monu01.html|title=Monuments|access-date=17 May 2020|language=nl|website=Anda Suriname}}</ref>
