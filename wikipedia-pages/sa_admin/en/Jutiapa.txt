'''Jutiapa''' is a city and a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Jutiapa (department)|Jutiapa]] [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Guatemala]]. It is the largest city and seat of the department. It covers an area of approximately {{cvt|610.3|km2}}. As per 2023 estimates, it has a population of about 168,787 inhabitants. The municipality lies close to the border with [[El Salvador]].

==History==
Jutiapa might mean "river of jute". It might also have been derived from the Mexican word "xotiapán" which is a combination of the words xochil and apan meaning flower and river respectively. It roughly translates to "river of the flowers".<ref name="Hist"/>
 
During the Spanish occupation, Jutiapa belonged to the province of [[Chiquimula]], and it remained so till 1825. In 1839, it became part of the Mita department, which was further subdivided into the departments of Santa Rosa, [[Jalapa department|Jalapa]], and [[Jutiapa department|Jutiapa]] in 1852. It was declared as a villageon 8 May 1852 and was elevated to a city on 15 September 1878, which was ratified on 6 September 1921.<ref name="Hist">{{cite web|title=Historia del municipio de Jutiapa|work=Deguate|trans-title=History of Jutiapa municipality|url=https://departamentos.deguate.com/jutiapa/historia-del-municipio-de-jutiapa-jutiapa/|access-date=23 May 2026|language=es}}</ref>

==Geography==
Jutiapa is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] and the capital city of the eponymous [[Jutiapa Department|Jutiapa Department]] in [[Guatemala]]. It is spread over an area of {{cvt|610.3|km2}}.<ref name="Hist"/><ref name="CP">{{cite web|title=Jutiapa (Municipality, Guatemala)|work=Citypopulation.de|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/admin/jutiapa/2201__jutiapa/|access-date=24 May 2026}}</ref> It lies on the central highlands in the southeastern part of the country, about {{cvt|120|km}} from the national capital of [[Guatemala city]].<ref name="Brit">{{cite web|url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Jutiapa-Guatemala|title=Jutiapa|work=[[Britannica]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Monjas]] and [[El Progreso, Jutiapa|El Progreso]] to the north, [[Santa Catarina Mita]] to the east, [[Comapa, Jutiapa|Comapa]] and [[Jalpatagua]] to the south, and [[Quesada, Guatemala|Quesada]], Jalpatagua and [[Casillas, Guatemala|Casillas]] to the west.<ref>{{cite web|title=Geografia del municipio de Jutiapa|work=Deguate|trans-title=Geography of Jutiapa municipality|url=https://departamentos.deguate.com/jutiapa/geografia-del-municipio-de-jutiapa-jutiapa/|access-date=23 May 2026|language=es}}</ref>

Located at an elevation of {{cvt|878.11|m}} above sea level, Jutiapa has a [[tropical monsoon climate]] ([[Koppen Classification]]: Am). The district has an average annual temperature of {{cvt|27.46|C}}. Jutiapa typically receives about {{cvt|85.59|m}} of precipitation and has 135.06 rainy days (37.0% of the time) on average annually.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://weatherandclimate.com/guatemala/jutiapa|title=Jutiapa|work=Weather and Climate|access-date=1 April 2025}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Jutiapa had an estimated population of 168,787 inhabitants in 2023. The population consisted of 83,620 males and 85,167 females. About 30.0% of the population was below the age of fourteen, and 5.7% was over the age of 65 years. The entire population was classified as urban. Most of the residents (92.6%) were born in the same municipality. The city had a literacy rate of 81.5%. [[Ladino people|Ladinos]] (64%) formed the major ethnic group, with [[Xinca people|Xinca]] (34.5%) forming a significant minority. [[Spanish language|Spanish]] (98.8%) was the most spoken language.<ref name="CP"/>

==Economy==
Jutiapa is a major commercial and administrative centre in the region, and serves as the market for the rural agricultural areas. The major agricultural produce include [[maize]] and beans.<ref name="Brit"/> Local crafts include candles (both [[tallow]] and [[Paraffin wax|paraffin]] type); woven hats and other palm products; [[leather]] [[saddle|saddles]], belts and riding gear; and traditional [[Ceramic|ceramics]].<ref name="viajegt">{{cite web|url=http://www.viajeaguatemala.com/english/Jutiapa/1051016311132.htm |title=Jutiapa|work=Travel to Guatemala|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>
