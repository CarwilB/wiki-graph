'''Metztitlán''' ([[Otomi language|Otomi]]: '''Nziʼbatha''') is a town and one of the 84 [[municipalities of Hidalgo]], in central-eastern [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 814.7&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 20,123.<ref>{{cite web|title=Metztitlán|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13037a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|access-date=December 27, 2008|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070527203052/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13037a.htm|archive-date=May 27, 2007}}</ref>

In 2023, Metztitlán was designated a [[Pueblos Mágicos|Pueblo Mágico]] by the federal government, recognizing its cultural and historical importance.<ref>{{Cite web |title=These are Mexico's newest Magical Towns |url=https://www.travelweekly.com/Mexico-Travel/Insights/Mexico-names-new-Magical-Towns |access-date=2025-09-15 |website=www.travelweekly.com |language=en}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
[[File:Mapa de Metztitlán - Relaciones geográficas de Indias (1579).jpg|thumb|220px|Map of Metztitlán - Geographical relations of the Indies (1579).]]

From [[Náhuatl]] ''Metztli'' 'moon' and ''tlan'' 'place' so its meaning would be: 'Place of the moon'. The town is named after the [[Aztec religion|Aztec]] moon goddess, [[Metztli]].<ref name=gob>{{Cite web|title=Metztitlán|url=http://www.hidalgo.gob.mx/page/municipio/metztitlan|work=[[Municipalities of Hidalgo]]|access-date=March 22, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170408082353/http://www.hidalgo.gob.mx/page/municipio/metztitlan|archive-date=April 8, 2017}}</ref>

==History==

=== Pre-Columbian ===
Before the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|Spanish colonization of the region in 1519]], Metztitlán was the site of a powerful, independent [[Otomi]] state, unconquered by the [[Aztec Empire]]. According to [[Michael E. Smith (archaeologist)|Professor Michael E. Smith]], this small kingdom's independence was fortified and maintained as a result of the mountainous terrain in the valley surrounding it. Professor Smith wrote that the factors behind the state's enduring independence was due to the fact that "there were few resources of interest to the (Aztec) Empire in this area, and the final emperors may have decided that Metztitlán was not worth the effort."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.houstonculture.org/mexico/aztecs.html|title=History of Mexico - the Aztec Empire}}</ref> The lunar deity Zäna was important in this town.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Wauchope |first1=Robert |last2=Ekholm |first2=Gordon F. |last3=Bernal |first3=Ignacio |title=Handbook of Middle American Indians, Volumes 10 And 11: Archaeology of Northern Mesoamerica |date=2015 |publisher=University of Texas Press |location=Austin, TX |isbn=9781477306758 |page=444}}</ref> Metztitlan used a calendar very similar to [[Aztec calendar|that of the Aztecs]], but changing the names of a few days: ''xilotl'' "green ear of corn" instead of Aztec ''cuetzpalin'', ''tzontecomatl'' "skull" instead of ''miquiztli'', ''itlan'' "next to him" instead of ''malinalli'', ''cuixtli'' "kite (bird)" instead of ''cuauhtli'', and ''teotl itonal'' "day of divinity" instead of ''cozcacuauhtli''.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Kaufman |first1=Terrence |title=The Day-Names of the Meso-American Calendar: A Linguistic Perspective |date=1989 |page=158}}</ref>

=== 16th century ===
Fray Juan de Sevilla established the first Augustinian monastery in Metztitlan in 1538 on lower ground near Lake Metztitlan. That small mission is today known as the Old Convent ''La Communidad''<ref>{{Cite web | title=Antiguo convento La Comunidad (Metztitlán) - Wikipedia, la enciclopedia libre | url=https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiguo_convento_La_Comunidad_(Metztitl%C3%A1n) | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220626233944/https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiguo_convento_La_Comunidad_(Metztitl%C3%A1n) | access-date=2025-10-05 | archive-date=2022-06-26}}</ref> with the remains of the church, convent and a third section occupied by the town government. Due to frequent flooding, the convent was moved to a higher location in the 1540s alleged to be the site of the pre-Columbian Temple of the Moon. The new site was permanently occupied by the 1550s and the massive conventual church completed in 1557.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Perry |first1=Richard |title=Mexico's Fortress Monasteries |date=1992 |publisher=Espadana Press |location=Santa Barbara, CA |isbn=0-9620811-1-6 |page=69}}</ref>

The church "Convento de los Santos Reyes" is unique for its preservation of six colonial retalbos (altarpieces) and the only principal atarpiece retalbo to survive in the region. The main altarpiece retalbo was completed in 1700 and was the work of an indigenous artist Salvador Ocampo. It is fifty feet high and consists of five tiers (rows) divided into five calles (columns). The registers consist of 13 sculptures and reliefs and six paintings. Those of the Three Kings (Santos Reyes), the Crucifixion and Padre Eterno are reputed to have been painted by the hand of Ocampo himself.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Perry |first1=Richard |title=Mexico's Fortress Monasteries |date=1992 |publisher=Espadana Press |location=Santa Barbara, CA |isbn=0-9620811-1-6 |pages=71–73}}</ref>

=== Later history ===
In April and September 1811, two indigenous revolts occurred in the city, ending in the deaths of 1225 people. The city was elevated to municipal status in 1869.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13037a.htm|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070527203052/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13037a.htm|archive-date = 2007-05-27|title = Hidalgo.- Metztitlán}}</ref>
