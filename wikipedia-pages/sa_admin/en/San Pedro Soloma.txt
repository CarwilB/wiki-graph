'''San Pedro Soloma''' (also known as '''{{lang|kjb|Tzu'luma}}''' in [[Qʼanjobʼal language|Qʼanjobʼal]]) is a town and [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] of [[Huehuetenango Department|Huehuetenango]], a department of [[Guatemala]]. It is located in the [[Sierra de los Cuchumatanes]] at 2,300 m above [[sea level]]. The municipality covers a total area of 264&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> with elevations ranging from 1,900 m to 3,500 m.  Its population of 49,030 is spread over the town of Soloma, 19 villages and 50 smaller rural communities (''caserios'').
<ref name ="muni">{{cite web |url=http://www.sanpedrosoloma.com |title=San Pedro Soloma - Ubicación |access-date=2009-02-05 |author= |date= |publisher=}}</ref> 
 
The annual celebrations in honor of the town's [[patron saint|patron]] [[Saint Peter]] take place from June 24 to 30. Its mayan name is Tzu'luma.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.sanpedrosoloma.com/fundacion.asp|title = Fundación de Soloma}}</ref>

==History==
===Mercedarian doctrine===
{{See also|Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy}}

[[File:Coat_of_Arms_of_the_Mercedarians.svg|left|thumb|90px|<br /> [[Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy|Mercedarian]] coat of arms.]]

After the [[Spanish conquest of Guatemala]] in the 1520s, the "Presentación de Guatemala" Mercedarian province was formed in 1565; originally, the [[order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy]] had gotten from bishop [[Francisco Marroquín]] several doctrines in the [[Sacatepéquez]] and [[Chimaltenango Department|Chimaltenango]] valleys, close to the capital [[Santiago de los Caballeros de Guatemala]], but they traded those with the [[Order of Preachers]] friars in exchange for the doctrines those had in the [[Sierra de los Cuchumatanes]] area.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=336}} During the first part of the 17th century they also had doctrine in four town close to the city of Santiago, which eventually became city neighborhoods: Espíritu Santo, Santiago, San Jerónimo and San Anton —which was the capital of the Mercedarians, where they had their convent and where their [[Encomienda|comendador]] lived.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=336}}

According to bishop Juan de las Cabezas memoir in 1613{{sfn|Pérez|1966|p=44}} and the bishop [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]] parish visit minutes from 1770,{{sfn|Cortés y Larraz|1770|p=}} the Mercedarians came to have nine doctrines, and numerous annexes, which were: [[Malacatán|Santa Ana de Malacatán]], Concepción de Huehuetenango, San Pedro de Solomá, [[Jacaltenango|Nuestra Señora de la Purificación de Jacaltenango]], [[Chiantla|Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria de Chiantla]], [[Cuilco|San Andrés de Cuilco]], [[Tejutla, San Marcos|Santiago de Tejutla]], [[San Pedro Sacatepéquez, San Marcos|San Pedro de Sacatepéquez]], and [[San Juan Ostuncalco|San Juan de Ostuncalco]].{{sfn|Pérez|1966|p=44-45}}

However, in 1754, due to the [[borbon reforms]] implemented by the Spanish kings, the Mercedarins -and the rest of the [[regular clergy]] for that matter-, had to transfer their doctrines to the [[secular clergy]], thus losing their Chiantla convent and annexed doctrines.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=338}}
