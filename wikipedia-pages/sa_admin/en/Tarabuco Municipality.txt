'''Tarabuco  Municipality''' is the first [[Municipalities of Bolivia|municipal section]] of the [[Yamparáez Province]] in the [[Chuquisaca Department]], [[Bolivia]]. Its seat is [[Tarabuco]] and its population is overwhelmingly indigenous and [[rural]]. On 6 December 2009, the residents voted in favor of indigenous autonomy for the municipality, with a vote of 90.8%.<ref name=Avanzan>"Las autonomías indígenas avanzan a paso lento per seguro, entre consensos e interrogantes" ''Diálogos en Democracia'', 21 March 2010 (Supplement to ''Pulso Bolivia'').</ref>

==Demographics==
According to the [[2024 Bolivian census]] conducted by the [[National Institute of Statistics of Bolivia]], the Tarabuco Municipality had a population of 13,323 people. The municipality has experienced a consistent [[population decline]] over  two decades, decreasing from 19,554 counted in the [[2001 Bolivian census]] to 16,944 counted in the [[2012 Bolivian census]], with an annual population change decrease of -2.1% between 2012 and 2024. The municipality's area is 1,029 km² and the [[population density]] stands at 12.95 per km² as of 2024.<ref name="CityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://citypopulation.de/en/bolivia/admin/chuquisaca/010601__tarabuco/|title=Tarabuco (Municipality in Bolivia)|website=Citypopulation.de|date=14 September 2025|access-date=5 May 2026}}</ref>

The gender distribution of Tarabuco is nearly balanced, with a slight female majority. As of 2024, there are 6,731 women (50.5%) and 6,592 men (49.5%). The age distribution shows that a majority of the population are of [[working age]], with 60% (7,996 people) aged between 15 and 64. Children aged 0 to 14 make up 24.7% (3,289) of the population, while the elderly account for 15.3% (2,038).<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Tarabuco is a [[rural]] area, with a majority of the population (77.6%) residing in rural areas, while only 22.4% live in [[urban centers]]. The population identifies overwhelmingly as indigenous. In the 2024 census, a majority of residents identified as [[Quechua people|Quechua]] (51.9%), while 16.1% identified with other indigenous groups and 3% as [[Peasant#Latin American farmers|Campesino]] peasants. A segment of 28.3% reported no specific indigenous identification. There were also a few who identified as [[Aymara people|Aymara]], [[Guaraní people|Guaraní]], or [[Chiquitano people|Chiquitano]].<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/> The indigenous majority is also shown in the language spoken, with [[Quechuan languages|Quechua]] being the most widley spoken (by 75.3% of the municipality). [[Spanish language|Spanish]] is the second most spoken language with 23.1% the rest of the population speaking other languages, including a few who speak [[Aymara language|Aymara]] and [[German language|German]].<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

==Culture==
The [[Pukllay]] festival, which means "game" in [[Quechuan languages|Quechua]], is a historical celebration held annually on March 19 and 20 in the Tarabuco Municipality. Together with the [[Ayarichi]] dance, the festival represents the [[Yampara]] culture and was submitted by [[Elizabeth Salguero]], the [[Ministry of Cultures (Bolivia)|Bolivian Minister of Cultures]], to [[UNESCO]] for consideration as [[intangible cultural heritage]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.lostiempos.com/diario/actualidad/tragaluz/20110317/bolivia-postula-tres-expresiones-culturales-como-patrimonio-inmaterial_117403_233217.html|title=Bolivia postula tres expresiones culturales como patrimonio inmaterial ante la Unesco |trans-title=Bolivia submits three cultural expressions for UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage status|lang=es|newspaper=[[Los Tiempos]]|date=17 March 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140201213428/http://www.lostiempos.com/diario/actualidad/tragaluz/20110317/bolivia-postula-tres-expresiones-culturales-como-patrimonio-inmaterial_117403_233217.html|archive-date=1 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The festival also includes a recreation of the Battle of Jumbate, in which the local natives attempted to defend themselves from the [[Spaniard]] attack in March 1816.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://boliviatravelsite.com/boliviatourism.php?attraction=Tarabuco&city=Sucre|title=Tarabuco, Chuquisaca|website=[[Bolivia Travel Site]]|access-date=5 May 2026}}</ref>
