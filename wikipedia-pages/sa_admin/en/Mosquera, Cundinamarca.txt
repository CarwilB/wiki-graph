'''Mosquera''' is a municipality of [[Colombia]] in the [[Western Savanna Province]], part of the [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]]. Mosquera is close to the capital [[Bogotá]] and is part of its [[Metropolitan Area of Bogotá|metropolitan area]]. The urban centre of Mosquera is situated at an altitude of {{convert|2516|m|ft}} on the [[Bogotá savanna]]. The municipality borders the localities [[Bosa (Bogotá)|Bosa]] and [[Fontibón]] of Bogotá in the east, [[Soacha]] in the south, [[Madrid, Cundinamarca|Madrid]] and [[Funza]] in the north and [[Bojacá]] in the west.<ref name=websiteMosquera>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.mosquera-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Mosquera] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151220090018/http://www.mosquera-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml |date=2015-12-20 }}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Mosquera is named after its founder, former [[list of presidents of Colombia|president of Colombia]] [[Tomás Cipriano de Mosquera]], who in 1861 separated Mosquera from Funza.<ref name=websiteMosquera/>

== History ==
Mosquera is close to [[Lake Herrera]], a remnant lake of the large [[Pleistocene]] [[Lake Humboldt, Colombia|Lake Humboldt]] that existed on the Bogotá savanna until approximately 30,000 years [[Before Present|BP]]. The archaeological findings around Lake Herrera date to the [[Andean preceramic|preceramic]] period, predating the [[Herrera Period]], named after the lake.

The [[Muisca people|Muisca]] established their civilisation typically as of 800 AD. They were an advanced culture and performed [[Muisca agriculture|agriculture]] on the high plains of the savanna. In the Muisca times [[maize]], [[potato]]es, [[arracacha]], [[quinoa]], [[sweet potato]]es, [[yuca]], [[capsicum|peppers]], [[tomato]]es, [[bean]]s and [[pumpkin]]s were cultivated in Mosquera. The Muisca were predominantly farmers and traders and their [[Muisca economy|economy]] was based on the extraction of [[halite|salt]] from the northern parts of the Bogotá savanna. Fruits and other agricultural products growing in different climates were traded on the frequent markets of the Muisca. In Mosquera [[pineapple]]s, [[avocado]]es, [[guanabana]]s, [[Inga edulis|ice-cream beans]], [[pitahaya]]s and [[guayaba]]s were the fruits traded with warmer climates. [[Tobacco]] also came from more tropical regions while [[cotton]] was traded with the colder climate [[Lache people|Lache]].<ref name=websiteMosquera/>

Funza, of which Mosquera formed the southern part until 1861, was founded by [[conquistador]] [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] who arrived in the region on April 20, 1537.

Presently, Mosquera is a satellite town of the capital Bogotá and has grown significantly in recent years.<ref name=websiteMosquera/>

In 2022, an unusual type of [[white]] [[foam]] covered the town. Residents reported that it produced an awful smell and that it had covered large parts of the municipality. While many locals were forced to get [[inhalers]] no effects on public health were recorded.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2022-04-29 |title=Foul-smelling foam clogs up Colombian town of Mosquera |language=en-GB |work=BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-61270024 |access-date=2022-04-29}}</ref>

==Climate==
Mosquera has a [[Oceanic climate|subtropical highland climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] ''Cfb'') with cool temperatures throughout the year.
