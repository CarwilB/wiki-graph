'''Dzilam González Municipality''' (In the [[Yucatec Maya language|Yucatec Maya]] Language: "peeling" combined with the Spanish surname "González") is a [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Yucatán]] containing {{Convert|545.45|km2}} of land and located roughly {{Convert|90|km}} northeast of the city of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]].<ref name="municipios"/> It is located in the former Province of [[Ah Kin Chel Province]] to the north of [[Izamal]], about {{Convert|10|km}} inland from the coast.<ref>{{cite book|title=Contributions to American Anthropology and History|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=4HrjAAAAMAAJ|year=1952|publisher=Carnegie Institution of Washington}}</ref> It was once the port of Dzilam which has now receded inland is known as Dzilam González.<ref>{{cite book|title=Studies in the Archaeology of Coastal Yucatan and Campeche, Mexico|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=0KgRAQAAIAAJ|year=1978|publisher=Middle American Research Institute, Tulane University}}</ref> The agricultural produces of the area are corn, onions, beans and fruit.{{Sfn|Ellis|Ellis1964|p=129}}

==History==
During pre-Hispanic times, the town fell within the provinces under the chieftainship of Cheles. After the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán|conquest]] the area became part of the [[encomienda]] system. In 1544 [[Francisco de Montejo]] established a town at the site.<ref name="municipios"/>

Yucatán declared its independence from the Spanish Crown in 1821 and in 1825, the area was assigned to the Coastal region with its headquarters in [[Izamal]]. In 1825, "González" was appended to the name to differentiate this place from a port with the same name. In 1900 it was separated to form its own municipality, which included the area that is now [[Dzilam de Bravo Municipality]] until 1921, when that was separated.<ref name=municipios />

Some years back there were extensive ruins here but now there are two Mayan ruins.{{Sfn|Ellis|Ellis1964|p=129}}

==Governance==
The municipal president is elected for a three-year term. The town council has seven councilpersons, who serve as Secretary and councilors of sports, public services, heritage, public security, cemeteries and public works.<ref name=governance>{{cite web|title=Dzilam González|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|website=inafed|publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México|access-date=2 July 2015|location=Mérida, Mexico|language=es|archive-date=8 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140508064449/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM31yucatan/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Municipal Council administers the business of the municipality. It is responsible for budgeting and expenditures and producing all required reports for all branches of the municipal administration. Annually it determines educational standards for schools.<ref name=governance />

The Police Commissioners ensure public order and safety. They are tasked with enforcing regulations, distributing materials and administering rulings of general compliance issued by the council.<ref name=governance />
