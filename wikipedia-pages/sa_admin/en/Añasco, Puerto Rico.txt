'''Añasco''' ({{IPA|es|aˈɲasko}}, {{IPA|es|aˈɲaʔko|local}}), named after one of its settlers, Don Luis de Añasco, is a [[Añasco barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]] located on the west coast of the island bordering the [[Mona Passage]] to the west, north of [[Mayagüez, Puerto Rico|Mayagüez]], and [[Las Marias, Puerto Rico|Las Marias]]; south of [[Rincón, Puerto Rico|Rincón]], [[Aguada, Puerto Rico|Aguada]], and [[Moca, Puerto Rico|Moca]] and west of [[San Sebastián, Puerto Rico|San Sebastián]] and [[Las Marías, Puerto Rico|Las Marias]]. It is part of the [[Aguadilla-Isabela-San Sebastián Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

== Etymology and nicknames==
[[File:Estatua de Diego Salcedo en la plaza de recreo de Añasco, Puerto Rico.jpg|left|thumb|Statue in the main ''plaza''<nowiki/>'s fountain depicting the drowning of legendary conquistador [[Diego Salcedo (soldier)|Diego Salcedo]], a story which gives the municipality one of its many nicknames]]
The name ''Añasco'' comes from Don Luis de Añasco, former owner of the land where the [[Añasco barrio-pueblo|town]] and municipality were founded. This family name is of [[Spaniards|Spanish origin]] from the province of [[Extremadura]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Añasco familia heráldica genealogía escudo Añasco|url=https://www.heraldrysinstitute.com/lang/es/cognomi/A%C3%B1asco/idc/635503/|access-date=2021-10-07|website=Heraldrys Institute of Rome|language=es}}</ref>

Some of the municipality's nicknames include: ''La ciudad donde los dioses mueren'' ("The city where gods die") and ''Los nativos'' ("the natives") are a reference to the [[Legend of Diego Salcedo|legend of Spanish conquistador Diego Salcedo]], who according to the story was drowned by the indigenous [[Taíno]] in order to prove that the [[European colonization of the Americas|European colonizers]] were not immortal deities; and ''Pueblo del Hojaldre'' ("[[Puff pastry|Puff Pastry]] Town") after the ''[[hojaldre]]'', a type of puff pastry the municipality is famous for.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2016-11-28|title=Añasco, Puerto Rico – Where The Gods Die|url=https://boricuaonline.com/en/anasco-puerto-rico/|access-date=2021-10-07|website=BoricuaOnline.com|language=en-US}}</ref>

==History==
Although the ''Bahía de Aguada (Aguada Bay)'' in the neighboring town of [[Aguada, Puerto Rico|Aguada]], commonly known as the ''Ciudad del Descubrimiento'' ''(City of the Discovery)'', has historically been recognized as the location where [[Christopher Columbus]] first landed in [[Puerto Rico]] during his [[Voyages of Christopher Columbus#Lesser Antilles and Puerto Rico|second voyage]] on 19 November 1493, it is believed by most historians that ''Bahía de Añasco (Añasco Bay)'' near the mouth of the [[Río Grande de Añasco]] in Añasco was the place of Columbus’ first landing and stay of two days in Puerto Rico before continuing to [[La Navidad]], first European settlement in the [[Americas]], in [[Haiti]] in [[Hispaniola]].<ref>{{cite book|last= Van Middeldyk|first=R.A.|title=The history of Puerto Rico, from the Spanish discovery to the American occupation |year=1903|publisher=D. Appleton and Co.|location=NY|url=https://www.loc.gov/resource/lhbpr.08353/?sp=35&st=image |page=13-16}}</ref><ref name="Monson1986">{{cite book |last1=Morison |first1=Samuel Eliot |title=The Great Explorers: The European Discovery of America |date=1986 |publisher=Oxford University Press |isbn=978-0-19-504222-1 |pages=440, 448, 449 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JnotvLHX80gC&pg=PA448}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Controversia en Puerto Rico por el punto donde Colón llegó a la isla caribeña | website=Diario Libre| date=December 6, 2019| url= https://www.diariolibre.com/actualidad/internacional/controversia-en-puerto-rico-por-el-punto-donde-colon-llego-a-la-isla-caribena-IB15723931 | language=es | access-date=January 19, 2024}}</ref>

The town of Añasco was founded on October 18, 1733, and named after Don Luis de Añasco, a colonist from the [[Extremadura]] region of [[Spain]] who came to Puerto Rico with [[Juan Ponce de León]]. Añasco was founded by the initiative of rich landowner Don José de Santiago, who wanted to establish the villa in properties owned by Don Luis de Añasco. The property was located on the margins of the río ''[[Río Grande de Añasco|Guaorabo]]'', as the [[Taínos]] called it.<ref name="PR_Ency_Founding_Symbols">{{cite web|title=Añasco Municipality Founding History and Symbols|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/anasco-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14|website=enciclopediapr.org|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|language=en|access-date=14 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214233411/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/anasco-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14|archive-date=14 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Boquilla Creek Wildlife Reserve informational Brochure| date=2011-07-18 | url=http://www.drna.gobierno.pr/oficinas/arn/recursosvivientes/costasreservasrefugios/pmzc/publicaciones/folletos/RN%20Cano%20Boquilla.pdf?set_language=es-pr&cl=es-pr | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718212016/http://www.drna.gobierno.pr/oficinas/arn/recursosvivientes/costasreservasrefugios/pmzc/publicaciones/folletos/RN%20Cano%20Boquilla.pdf?set_language=es-pr&cl=es-pr | archive-date=2011-07-18 | url-status=dead | access-date=2021-06-20}}</ref>

[[Añasco barrio-pueblo|Añasco]] town was preceded by the first original location of the settlement of [[San Germán, Puerto Rico|San Germán]]. This settlement was attacked and destroyed several times by the Taínos and pirates before moving to the current location. In November 1511, [[Juan Ponce de León]] handed over governorship to [[Juan Cerón]], a lieutenant of the viceroy [[Diego Columbus|Diego Colon]] (son of Cristobal Colon or [[Christopher Columbus|Cristopher Columbus]]). Cerón ordered Miguel de Toro, a lieutenant of Juan Ponce de León, to create a "Christian Village" in western [[Puerto Rico|Borinquen]], calling it San Germán. This was the second attempt of foundation given in 1511 at the mouth of the Guaorabo River (present day [[Río Grande de Añasco|Rio Añasco]]), near the area known today as Añasco, Puerto Rico. This first settlement was attacked in 1528, 1538, and again in 1554. A fort to protect this town began in 1540, but its construction was suspended in 1546 when the people decided to move inland being tired of attacks. Attacks by [[Island Caribs|Carib]] Indians forced the population to move south inland to the present site of the present town of San Germán{{citation needed|date=August 2014}}.

The Añasco River is also claimed to be the site of the popular legend of the drowning of the Spaniard [[Diego Salcedo (soldier)|Diego Salcedo]] in 1511 at the hands of the [[Taínos]], proving the Spanish soldiers were not gods and igniting a revolt. It is believed that the revolt was led by the cacique "[[Cacique]] Chayoán" (Uruyoán) and suppressed by Spanish soldiers.<ref name="Iriarte 2018 p. 42">{{cite book | last=Iriarte | first=H. | title=Puerto Rico, a Unique Culture: History, People and Traditions | publisher=Balboa Press | year=2018 | isbn=978-1-9822-0597-3 | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=waVjDwAAQBAJ&pg=PT42 | access-date=October 11, 2021 | page=42}}</ref>

Many of the first settlers to the area came from the [[Canary Islands]] and the south of [[Spain]]. The [[1918 San Fermín earthquake]] destroyed Añasco's parish church, the town hall and other buildings, almost eliminating most of the historic downtown structures.

[[Hurricane Maria|Hurricane María]] on September 20, 2017, triggered numerous landslides in Añasco. In some areas of Añasco there were more than 25 landslides per square mile due to the significant amount of rainfall.<ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides map">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref> Barrio [[Playa, Añasco, Puerto Rico|Playa]] was particularly affected with about 96% of the homes under water.  Bridges in and around Añasco sustained extensive damage as a result of Hurricane María.<ref name="Maria">{{cite web | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar - Añasco |trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget - Añasco | website=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/anasco/| language=es | access-date=2021-08-24}}</ref>

==Geography==
Añasco is located in the Coastal Plains of the west, bordered by the [[Río Grande de Añasco]]. It is bordered in the north by [[Rincón, Puerto Rico|Rincón]], [[Aguada, Puerto Rico|Aguada]], and [[Moca, Puerto Rico|Moca]]; [[Mayagüez, Puerto Rico|Mayagüez]] to the south; and [[San Sebastián, Puerto Rico|San Sebastián]] and [[Las Marías, Puerto Rico|Las Marías]] to the east. The [[Mona Passage]] lies to the west of the town.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info">{{cite web|title=Añasco Municipality General Info (Location, Square Miles, Economy and Geography)|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/anasco-municipality/#1463492689874-dd12c211-136e|website=enciclopediapr.org|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|language=en|access-date=14 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214233411/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/anasco-municipality/#1463492689874-dd12c211-136e|archive-date=14 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> The Añasco terrain is mostly plain but features a series of hills and mountains like Canta Gallo (1,194 feet, or 364 meters), Gordo and Pichón (both at 1,115 feet, or 340 meters).<ref>[http://www.lat-long.com/Latitude-Longitude-1609931-Puerto_Rico-Cerro_Canta_Gallo.html Cerro Canta Gallo, Puerto Rico] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120405223418/http://www.lat-long.com/Latitude-Longitude-1609931-Puerto_Rico-Cerro_Canta_Gallo.html |date=2012-04-05 }} on Lat-Long</ref> It is also crossed by several rivers like Icaco River, Caguabo River, La Balsa, and others.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" /><ref>[http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=08112002 Municipios: Añasco] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101123062106/http://enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=08112002 |date=2010-11-23 }} on Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico</ref>

==Demographics==
Like most of the people of [[Puerto Rico]], the Añasco population originated with the [[Taíno|Taino]] Indians and then by immigrants from [[Spaniards|Spain]] that settled the central highland, most prominently the [[Andalusians|Andalusian]] and [[Canary Islanders|Canarian]] Spanish migration who formed the bulk of the [[Jíbaro (Puerto Rico)|jibaro]] or [[Criollo people|white]] peasant stock of the island.<ref>{{cite web|title=Brief History of the Canarian Migration to Spanish America|first=Miguel|last=Hernández|work=Puerto Rican Hispanic Genealogical Society|publisher=[[RootsWeb.com]]|url=http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/passengers_01.htm#Migration|access-date=2014-12-09|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418105438/http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/passengers_01.htm#Migration|archive-date=2015-04-18|url-status=live}}</ref> The Andalusian and Canarian Spaniards also influenced much of the [[Culture of Puerto Rico|Puerto Rican culture]] which explains the [[Andalusian Spanish|Southern Spanish dialect]], and the [[Spanish Colonial architecture|Spanish colonial architecture]].

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the United States conducted its first [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Añasco was 13,311.

==Tourism==
===Landmarks and places of interest===
[[File:Caguabo, Añasco, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|left|Resort in  [[Caguabo, Añasco, Puerto Rico |Caguabo]]]]
There are 18 beaches in Añasco.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/las1200playasdepuertorico-1216285/|title=Las 1,200 playas de Puerto Rico [The 1200 beaches of Puerto Rico]|date=April 14, 2017|website=Primera Hora|language=es|access-date=December 12, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191212200804/https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/las1200playasdepuertorico-1216285/|archive-date=December 12, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Tres Hermanos Beach]] is a well-known Añasco beach<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/ruta-marina/balnearios/tres-hermanos-anasco/|title=Tres Hermanos, Añasco - Ruta Marina &#124; EnciclopediaPR|access-date=2019-07-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190715011701/https://enciclopediapr.org/ruta-marina/balnearios/tres-hermanos-anasco/|archive-date=2019-07-15|url-status=live}}</ref> which remained closed for more than two years after Hurricane Maria destroyed it in September 2017. ==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Añasco celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in January. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Antonio Abad}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" /><ref>{{cite web | title=Puerto Rico Festivales, Eventos y Actividades en Puerto Rico | website=Puerto Rico Hoteles y Paradores | url=https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | language=es | access-date=2020-07-17 | archive-date=February 26, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226163727/https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | url-status=dead }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Añasco include:
* Mayuco Festival – January
* Fine Arts Festival ({{lang|es|Festival de Bellas Artes}} – January
* [[Epiphany (holiday)|Three Kings Day]] Festival in [[Ovejas, Añasco, Puerto Rico|Ovejas]] and [[Corcovada, Añasco, Puerto Rico|Corcovada]] barrios – January
* [[Anthony the Great|Saint Anthony]]'s Marathon – January
* Antique Car Fair – January
* Theater Festival – May
* Youth Festival – July
* Festival in Honor of [[Rose of Lima|Saint Rose of Lima]] – August
* Añasco Triathlon – September
* Chipe Festival – October

===Sports===
Añasco has a [[Double-A (baseball)|AA baseball]] team called the Fundadores de Añasco.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/deportes/beisbol/nota/sansebastianavanzaporprimeravezdesde2013alcarnavaldecampeones-1349163/|title=San Sebastián avanza por primera vez desde 2013 al Carnaval de Campeones|language=es|date=June 23, 2019}}</ref> It is also known for being the hometown of some [[amateur boxing]] prospects like [[Samuel Figueroa]].<ref name="boxrec Sam">{{Cite web|url=https://boxrec.com/en/proboxer/584846|title = Please login}}</ref>

==Notable people==
* [[Mariana Bracetti]] - (1825–1903) was a revolutionary leader of the Puerto Rico independence movement in the 1860s. She is attributed with having knitted the flag that was intended to be used as the national emblem of Puerto Rico in its attempt to overthrow the Spanish government on the island, and to establish the island as a sovereign republic. 
* [[Ivy Queen]] - Singer
* [[Aristides Gonzalez]] - boxer, Olympic bronze medalist in 1984
*[[Young Miko]] - rapper and singer-songwriter.

==Economy==
In 2016 the municipal government reported a budget surplus of $1.3 million nonetheless the government also indicated that the municipal debt was $13 million.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Radiografía municipal: Añasco|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/politica/videos/radiografia-municipal-anasco-201390/|access-date=2021-05-02|website=El Nuevo Día|language=es}}</ref>

===Agriculture===
[[Sugar cane]] had been cultivated in Añasco as early as the [[16th century]]. The earliest known [[sugar mill]] ("ingenios") operator around the Añasco area was Tomás de Castellón in 1523. Añasco has also been a place for fruits and [[coffee]] cultivation.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" />

==Government==
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a [[mayor]], elected every four years. The current mayor of Añasco is Kabir Solares García, of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP). He was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2020|2020 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district IV]], which is represented by two Senators. In [[Puerto Rican general election, 2024|2024]], Jeison Rosa and Karen Michelle Román Rodríguez, both from the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP), were elected as District Senators.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Mayaguez_IV.xml |title=Senatorial District Results Mayagüez IV |website=ceepur.org |date=December 31, 2024 |access-date=January 3, 2025}}</ref>

==Transportation ==
There are 22 bridges in Añasco,<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Anasco Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/anasco/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=19 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190220181422/http://bridgereports.com/pr/anasco/|archive-date=20 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> and many sustained damages on September 20, 2017, when [[Hurricane Maria|Hurricane María]] hit Puerto Rico.<ref name="Maria" />

==Education==
The [[Puerto Rico Department of Education]] operates several public schools in the municipality, including two bilingual schools, Antonio Gonzalez Suarez Regional Bilingual Elementary School and [[Sergio Ramirez de Arellano-Hostos Regional Bilingual Secondary School]], the first public bilingual schools on the island. There are also private bilingual schools in the municipality, such as Colegio de la Salle and [[MAS Integrated School]].

===Flag===
The flag colors, design and symbolism are taken from the Añasco's coat of arms, with the only exception the silver parts on the coat of arms are white on the flag.<ref name="PR_Ency_Founding_Symbols" /><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=AÑASCO | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/ANASCO.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219000335/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/ANASCO.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=16 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The green field of the shield contains a saber cross massed in black, outlined in silver, and has a gold scallop shell in each quadrant formed by a [[cross fleury]]. A gold mural crown of three towers crests the shield.<ref name="PR_Ency_Founding_Symbols" />
