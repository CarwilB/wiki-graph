'''Santa Bárbara''' is one of the six districts that make up the canton of [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]], [[Costa Rica]]. The district of Santa Bárbara is commonly referred to as ''Santa Bárbara de Heredia'', and is the seat of the municipality of Santa Bárbara, and therefore awarded the title of city. It is an important commercial and transportation hub, approximately seven miles from the provincial capital of [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]] and five miles from the provincial capital of [[Alajuela]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref>

==History==
Before the Spanish settlers came, Santa Bárbara was originally occupied by the Huetares, an indigenous tribe. The Huetare King, Cacique Garabito, dominated the area.<ref name=RHC>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|year=2010|language=es|archive-date=22 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522195138/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|url-status=dead}}</ref> When the Spanish arrived, they originally called the area Churruca or Surruco.<ref name=centenario>{{cite book|title=Centenario del cantón Santa Bárbara de Heredia|year=1982|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/notas/seo.php/item.49/centenario-del-canton-santa-barbara-heredia.html|language=es}}{{Dead link|date=November 2023 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> Heredia, Barva, and Alajuela, three neighboring cities, were populated and settled in the late 1700s. As trade increased between the three cities, Santa Bárbara  developed as a [[waypoint]]. Though still commonly referred to as Churruca or Surruco, the name for the municipality and canton of Santa Bárbara was first mentioned in documents from 1821.<ref name=centenario />

The area of the current plaza in Santa Bárbara was the approximate center of the four districts in the region and a small hermitage was built there in 1837. By 1846, 1,500 people lived with the Santa Bárbara canton.<ref name=centenario /> With a priest visiting every Sunday, [[San Pedro de Santa Bárbara]] resident Pedro Saborío suggested the construction of a [[Catholic church]] near the current plaza in 1852.<ref name=centenario /> In 1859, Monseñor Joaquín Anselmo Llorente y Lafuente, the [[Bishop (Catholic Church)|bishop]] of San José, began construction of a cathedral in its current location.

The district of Santa Bárbara was officially formed in December 1848 as a church parish, recognized by the national government. In 1855, Florentino Cortés became the first mayor of the district.<ref name=RHC3>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=2|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|year=2010|language=es|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140523011151/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=2|archivedate=23 May 2014}}</ref> In September 1889, the city of Santa Bárbara was officially founded.<ref name="RHC" /> It was first classified as a villa, but became a city as the population grew.<ref name="RHC" />

Santa Bárbara's first public school opened in 1860 with [[shoemaking|cobbler]] Tomás Redondo giving the first lessons.<ref name="RHC3" /> The first official teacher was Diego Trejos.<ref name="RHC3" />

In 1896, the first electric lighting arrived in the district. A hydroelectric plant was built along the Porrosatí River, on the border of the districts of Santa Bárbara and San Juan in 1914.<ref name="RHC3" /> In 1911, running water pipes were installed in Santa Bárbara.<ref name=centenario />

==Economy and politics==
As the center of commerce for the canton, there are many commercial businesses in the municipality. Larger businesses include a supermarket<ref>{{cite web|title=Súper Víquez|url=http://www.superviquezcr.com/quienesSomos.html|publisher=Súper Víquez|accessdate=23 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=23 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140523231029/http://www.superviquezcr.com/quienesSomos.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> and appliance store.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sucursales Golla|url=http://www.gollotienda.com/sucursales/|publisher=El gallo más gallo de Alajuela S.A.|accessdate=23 May 2014|language=es|archive-date=24 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140524022750/http://www.gollotienda.com/sucursales/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Several banks are within a few blocks of Santa Bárbara's central plaza, including Banco Popular,<ref name="banco popular">{{cite web|title=Periférica Santa Bárbara de Heredia: [https://www.popularenlinea.fi.cr/Bpop/Pie/Sucursales/Heredia/Periferica_Santa_Barbara_Heredia.htm BPDC – Periférica Santa Bárbara de Heredia], accessdate: 22 May 2014|language=es}}</ref> Grupo Mutual Alajuela, and Banco Nacional.

The municipality of Santa Bárbara is considered a [[bedroom community]].<ref name="Plan Desarrollo">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010–2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|accessdate=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Most residents go to [[Alajuela]], [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]], Belén and [[San José, Costa Rica|San José]] for work.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

Among other goals, in 2010 the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara canton]] planned to shore up Santa Bárbara 's infrastructure.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo"/> There are plans to improve the district's agricultural market, located two blocks north of the central plaza.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> There are also plans to create a "Casa de Arte y Cultura" (House of Art and Culture).<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

In 2013, the city council called on Mayor Melvin Alfaro Salas to step down.<ref name=renuncia>{{cite news|title=Concejo Municipal pide renuncia del Alcalde Melvin Alfaro Salas|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.item.210/concejo-municipal-pide-renuncia-del-alcalde-melvin-alfaro-salas.html|accessdate=23 May 2014|date=18 April 2013|location=Santa Barbara|language=es}}</ref> Citing problems with water distribution, "unacceptable" labor practices, poor budgeting, and failure to attend council meetings, each member of the council asked for Alfaro Salas' resignation.<ref name=renuncia /> Alfaro Salas did not resign. In 2014, municipal workers went on strike<ref name=paro>{{cite news|last=Solano|first=Hugo|title=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara está en paro general|url=http://www.nacion.com/nacional/municipalidades/Municipalidad-Santa-Barbara-paro-general_0_1389061203.html|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=7 January 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> Albino Vargas, president of the [[Labor unions in Costa Rica|Asociación Nacional de Empleados Públicos]] (National Association of Public Employees) joined the strike, saying that workers had been abandoned by Alfaro Salas, a charge he denied.<ref name=paro />

==Culture==
[[File:Cementerio de Santa Bárbara de Heredia.jpg|thumb|Santa Bárbara de Heredia Cemetery]]
There is a chapter of the Consejo de la Persona Joven (Council of Young People) in Santa Bárbara.<ref>{{cite news|title=Comités Cantonales de la Persona Joven|url=http://www.cpj.go.cr/comit%C3%A9s-cantonales-de-la-persona-joven|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Conseja de la Persona Joven|language=es}}</ref> The Council of Young People organizes activities and groups, such as the United Canton Band.<ref name=pe>{{cite news|title=Patrullas Ecológicas: Plan de trabajo|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.item.206/comite-cantonal-de-la-persona-joven.html|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Santa Barbara: Una Comunidad Activa|date=7 July 2012|location=Santa Barbara|language=es|archive-date=13 October 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201013010345/http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.item.206/comite-cantonal-de-la-persona-joven.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The municipality maintains two cemeteries. The cemetery on the western outskirts contain both [[tomb]]s and burial plots.<ref>{{cite news|title=Cementerio|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=49&Itemid=64|accessdate=24 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|year=2010|language=es|archive-date=19 January 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180119120053/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=49&Itemid=64|url-status=dead}}</ref> Many of the tombs have neo-classical monuments.

A popular attraction near the town is [[Ark Herb Farm]], a botanical garden with a focus on medicinal herbs.

==Education==
The elementary school in the municipality of Santa Bárbara is named after former head of state [[Juan Mora Fernández]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Don Juan Mora Fernández|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.item.44/don-juan-mora-fernandez.html|accessdate=22 May 2014|newspaper=Santa Bárbara: Una Comunidad Activa|date=25 February 2011|location=Santa Bárbara de Heredia (Costa Rica)|language=es|archive-date=22 June 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200622035249/http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.item.44/don-juan-mora-fernandez.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> It was built in 1951 on the west side of the plaza.<ref name="RHC3" /> The high school, Liceo de Santa Bárbara, was established in 1973 using the facilities at Juan Mora Fernández, but it later moved to a location 800 meters from the central plaza.<ref name="RHC3" />

The library in Santa Bárbara opened in 1981.<ref name="mcj">Ministerio de Cultura y Juventud: [http://www.mcj.go.cr/literatura/sistema_bibliotecas/biblioteca_sta_barbara.aspx MCJ] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140523230446/http://www.mcj.go.cr/literatura/sistema_bibliotecas/biblioteca_sta_barbara.aspx |date=2014-05-23 }}, Retrieved: 22 May 2014</ref> Due to budgetary reasons, it closed in 1995, but reopened in 2001 in the Salón Comunal.<ref name="mcj" />

==Sports==

===Volleyball===
[[File:2011 Equipo Voleibol de Santa Barbara.jpg|thumb|Santa Bárbara Volleyball Sports Association (ASBAVOL), 2011, before game against Universidad de Costa Rica]]
The municipality is also home to the Asociación Deportiva Barbareña de Voleibol (ASBAVOL) (Santa Bárbara Volleyball Sports Association), one of the country's most successful volleyball clubs.<ref>{{cite news|last=Luis Rodríguez C.|first=Jose|title=Atenas será el rival de Santa Bárbara en la final del voleibol femenino|url=http://www.nacion.com/deportes/otros-deportes/Atenas-Santa-Barbara-voleibol-femenino_0_1400860055.html|accessdate=16 April 2014|newspaper=La Nacion (Costa Rica)|date=7 March 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> ASBAVOL was started in 1992 by former soccer player Julio Alfaro.<ref name="ASBAVOL Historia">ASBAVOL.org: [http://www.asbavol.org/historia Historia] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522155527/http://www.asbavol.org/historia |date=2014-05-22 }}, Retrieved: 22 May 2014</ref>   Since 2005, ASBAVOL's first division association team has won the national championship five times (2005, 2007, 2008, 2009, and 2010), as well as eight gold medals in the Juegos Deportivos Nacionales (National Sporting Championship) (2002, 2004, 2007, 2008, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014).<ref name="ASBAVOL Historia" /><ref>{{cite news|last=Araya N.|first=José Fernando|title=Santa Bárbara alcanzó el tricampeonato del voleibol femenino al derrotar a Atenas|url=http://www.nacion.com/deportes/otros-deportes/Santa-Barbara-tricampeonato-derrotar-Atenas_0_1401059981.html|accessdate=22 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=8 March 2014|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> ASBAVOL's student team won the gold medal in the Juegos Deportivos Estudiantiles (Student Sporting Championship) 13 times since 1996 (1996, 1997, 1998, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, and 2013) and two times at the Juegos Deportivos Estudiantiles Centroamericanos (Central American Student Sporting Championship) in 2010 and 2013. In addition, ASBOVAL has fielded championship teams in other youth divisions.<ref name="ASBAVOL Historia" /> Players from ASBAVOL regularly play for the [[Costa Rica women's national volleyball team]]. El Liceo de Santa Bárbara's gym is the site of many volleyball games.<ref>{{cite news|last=Jiménez|first=Daniel|title=Santa Bárbara venció a la UNED en segundo juego de la final nacional de voleibol|url=http://www.nacion.com/deportes/otros-deportes/Santa-Barbara-UNED-nacional-voleibol_0_1385261685.html|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=19 December 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref>

===Soccer===

Beginning in 1943, Club Sport Barbareño represented Santa Bárbara in the first division of the national soccer league.<ref name=semillero>{{cite news|title="Club Sport Barbareño" Semillero de talentos futbolísticos.|url=http://desarrollodelftbolenelcantnbarbareo.blogspot.com/|accessdate=23 May 2014|date=16 February 2011|language=Es}}</ref> The team changed its name to Asociación Deportiva Barbareña (Bárbara Sports Association) in 1984. The team plays within the national [[Costa Rican Primera División|Costa Rican soccer hierarchy]]. Between 1962 and 1975, it was a third division team. Between 1975 and 1981, as well as 1993 and 1997, it was a second division team. Between 1997 and 2004, it was a first division team.<ref name=unafut>{{cite news|last=Coto|first=Gerardo|title=Santa Bárbara también tuvo su equipo|url=http://www.unafut.com/site/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=368:santa-barbara-tambien-tuvo-su-equipo&catid=9:noticias-historicas&Itemid=49|accessdate=23 May 2010|newspaper=UNAFUT|date=4 January 2012|location=San Jose|language=es|archive-date=5 October 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005213634/http://www.unafut.com/site/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=368:santa-barbara-tambien-tuvo-su-equipo&catid=9:noticias-historicas&Itemid=49|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2004, the team was sold and moved to [[Puntarenas]], becoming [[Puntarenas F.C.]]<ref name=unafut /> It currently plays in the third division at the Carlos Alvarado Villalobos Stadium in Santa Bárbara, which holds 1,500 spectators.

Another team in the municipality was Deportivo Machado F.C.<ref name=semillero /> Although they were rivals from the 1950s to the 1970s, Asociación Deportiva Barbareña absorbed Machado's players when the team folded in the mid-1980s.<ref name=globo>{{cite news|title=Historia e incremento del fútbol en el cantón barbareño|url=http://ad-barbarea.globered.globered.com/categoria.asp?idcat=21|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=AD Barbareña|date=17  November 2010|language=es|archive-date=24 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140524004010/http://ad-barbarea.globered.globered.com/categoria.asp?idcat=21|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Players such as  Abdulio Jiménez Araya,<ref>{{cite news|last=Arguedas Ramírez|first=Krisly|title=Fui un buen jugador|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/notas.print.194/fui-un-buen-jugador.html|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Santa Bárbara: Una Comunidad Activa|date=26 March 2012|location=Santa Bárbara|language=Es}}</ref> and goalie [[Carlos Alvarado Villalobos|Carlos "El Aguilucho" Alvarado]] played with Asociación Deportiva Barbareña and Deportivo Machado F.C.<ref>{{cite news|title=Carlos Alvarado|url=http://lda.cr/carlos-alvarado/|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Revista Oficial de Socios de LDA “En Rojo y Negro”|date=June 2003|location=Alajuela, Costa Rica|language=Es}}</ref>

==Geography==
Santa Bárbara has an area of {{formatnum:1.27}} km²<ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=24 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024225203/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:1140}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

The municipality is a part of the [[Greater Metropolitan Area (Costa Rica)|Greater Metropolitan Area]].<ref name=OUGAM>{{cite news|title=La Gran Área Metropolitana|url=http://ougam.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/la-gam|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Observatorio Urbano de la Gran Área Metropolitana (OUGAM)|year=2014|location=San Jose|language=Es|archive-date=8 December 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151208093741/http://ougam.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/la-gam|url-status=dead}}</ref> The district of Santa Bárbara is the smallest district of the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara canton]].<ref name=DG>{{cite news|title=Datos Generales|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|year=2010|language=es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429045049/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|url-status=dead}}</ref> With respect to the other districts, Santa Bárbara is the most central.

Santa Bárbara is in the [[Cordillera Central (Costa Rica)|Cordillera Central]].<ref name=mapa>{{cite news|title=Mapa General del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=147&Itemid=147Municipalidad|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|year=2010|language=es}}</ref> It is a potential disaster zone, like many other areas of Costa Rica.<ref>{{cite news|title=Decretos por declaración de zonas catastradas|url=http://www.uecatastro.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=112:decretos-por-declaracion-de-zonas-catastradas&catid=1:latest-news|accessdate=28 April 2014|newspaper=TerranTribune Inc|date=24 September 2013|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> The elevation is approximately 1,140m&nbsp;meters above sea-level, within [[Cordillera Central (Costa Rica)|Cordillera Central]] .<ref name=muni>{{cite web|title=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara: Datos Generales|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|publisher=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|accessdate=27 April 2014|language=es|archive-date=29 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140429045049/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=106|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Potrerillos River runs through the northwest side of the district while the Quebrada Cruz is the border between the Santa Bárbara and the district of [[San Juan de Santa Bárbara]].<ref name=mapa />

== Transportation ==
=== Road transportation ===
The district is covered by the following road routes:
*[[File:CR RNS 123.svg|25px]] [[National Route 123 (Costa Rica)|National Route 123]]
*[[File:CR RNS 127.svg|25px]] [[National Route 127 (Costa Rica)|National Route 127]]
*[[File:CR RNS 128.svg|25px]] [[National Route 128 (Costa Rica)|National Route 128]]

Most of the majors routes in the canton originate in Santa Bárbara's central plaza.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> Route 123 (Calle Las Americas) passes along the southern side of the plaza, beginning in [[San Juan de Santa Bárbara]], going west toward [[San Pedro de Santa Bárbara]] and [[Alajuela]]. Route 127 goes from Santa Bárbara toward Zetillal and San Bosco.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> In addition, Calle Guapichelines starts in the north of the city.
