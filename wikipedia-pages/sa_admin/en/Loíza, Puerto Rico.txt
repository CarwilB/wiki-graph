'''Loíza''' ({{IPA|es-419|loˈisa|pron}}) is a [[Loíza barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] on the northeastern coast of [[Puerto Rico]], located east of [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]], west of [[Río Grande, Puerto Rico|Río Grande]], and north of [[Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|Canóvanas]]. An outer municipality within the [[San Juan–Bayamón–Caguas metropolitan area#Municipalities|San Juan metropolitan area]], it is spread over 5 barrios and the downtown area and administrative center of [[Loíza barrio-pueblo|Loíza Pueblo]]. Loíza is renowned for its rich [[Afro–Puerto Ricans|Afro-Puerto Rican]] culture and heritage. The vendors of Puerto Rican [[street food]] in kiosks, the unrestricted beaches, and the [[Casuarina|Australian pine]] and [[Mangrove forest|mangrove]] [[Piñones State Forest|state forest]] in the district of ''Piñones'' in the barrio of [[Torrecilla Baja]] are popular destinations among domestic and foreign tourists.

==History==
According to some sources, its name comes from a female [[cacique]], named Loaíza or Yuíza, who governed the region formerly called Haimanio, on the shores of the [[Río Grande de Loíza]]. It is said that this cacique might have married a [[mulatto]] [[conquistador]] called Pedro Mejías, but there is no evidence of this.<ref>[http://elyunque.com/loiza.htm Loíza] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111201044725/http://www.elyunque.com/loiza.htm |date=2011-12-01 }} on ElYunque</ref> Other sources point to a [[Spanish people|Spanish]] landlord named Iñigo López de Cervantes y Loayza, who owned a lot of the territory, and was renowned among governors and colonists of the time.<ref>[http://www.salonhogar.net/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/loiza.html Loíza Capital de la Tradición] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120518040211/http://www.salonhogar.net/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/loiza.html |date=2012-05-18 }} on SalonHogar.net</ref>

In 1692, Loíza was officially declared an urban area due to its population (100 houses and 1,146 residents), but it was in 1719 that the [[Spain|Spanish government]] declared it as an official town. It was founded by Gaspar de Arredondo.

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Loíza was 12,522.<ref>{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Report on the census of Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office| url=https://archive.org/details/reportoncensusof00unitiala/page/156/mode/2up |year=1900|publisher=Washington : Govt. print. off.|page=156}}</ref>

In the 1970s, an [[Aero Virgin Islands]] [[DC-3]] plane crashed in a Loíza beach, with no fatalities.

On September 20, 2017 [[Hurricane Maria]] struck Puerto Rico. ==Geography==
Loíza belongs to the geographical region called the Coastal Plains of the North. Its terrain is uniformly plain, since it doesn't exceed 100 meters above water level.<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/loiza-municipality/|title=Loíza Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org}}</ref>

According to the [[United States Census Bureau]], the city has a total area of {{convert|65.71|sqmi}}; of which {{convert|19.44|sqmi}} of it is land and {{convert|46.27|sqmi}} of it is water.

The population of the municipality was 23,693 at the [[2020 United States census|2020 census]].<ref name="census.gov"/> Consisting of 8,508 households, and 6,140 families residing in the municipality. The [[population density]] was 1,673.4 inhabitants per square mile (646.1/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 8,508 housing units at an average density of 562 per square mile (217/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 8,508 households, out of which 45.8% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 49.1% were [[Marriage|married couples]] living together, 29.7% had a female householder with no husband present, and 16.3% were non-families. 14.9% of all households were made up of individuals, and 5.3% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 3.39 and the average family size was 3.77. In the town the population was spread out, with 39.3% under the age of 19, 7.8% from 20 to 24, 27.4% from 25 to 44, 17.3% from 45 to 64, and 8.2% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 27 years. The median income for a household in the town was $8,962, and the median income for a family was $9,911. Males had a median income of $14,076 versus $12,903 for females. The [[per capita income]] for the town was $4,707. 67% of the population and 64.7% of families were below the poverty line. Out of the total population, 62.3% of those under the age of 18 and 59.5% of those 65 and older were living below the poverty line. The municipality has the highest concentration of [[Afro-Puerto Ricans]] on the island. About 4,256 residents speak [[English Language|English]] as a native language (Census 2000).<ref>{{Cite web|title=Data Center Results|url=https://apps.mla.org/cgi-shl/docstudio/docs.pl?map_data_primary|access-date=2022-04-11|website=apps.mla.org/map_data}}</ref>

==Culture==
[[File:Phoenix-Phoenix-Musical Instrument Museum-Vejigante Mask and Costume.jpg|thumb|250px|Vejigante Mask and Costume on display at the [[Musical Instrument Museum (Phoenix)|Musical Instrument Museum]] of Phoenix]] 
One of Loíza's barrios, Loíza Aldea, is famous across Puerto Rico because it has been a talent pool for dancers and artisans. A center for [[Afro–Puerto Ricans|black Puerto Rican]] music, it is said to be the traditional birthplace of the musical form known as ''[[plena]]'' along with Ponce. Each year there is a celebration in Loíza where people parade wearing {{lang|es|Máscaras de [[Vejigante]]}}, a type of mask made of [[coconut]] and brightly painted. 

Loíza is known as "{{lang|es|La Capital de la Tradición}}" (Capital of Traditions) for its [[bomba (Puerto Rico)|bomba]] music, traditional [[Taíno]] and [[African cuisine|African dishes]], folk art, and distinct culture.

===Festivals and events===
Loíza celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in March. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Patricio}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref name="J.D. 2006">{{cite web | author=J.D. | title=Loíza | website=Link To Puerto Rico.com | date=2006-05-02 | url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/loiza.html# | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18}}</ref><ref name="Kepler2000">{{cite book|author=David Ungerleider Kepler|title=Las fiestas de Santiago Apóstol en Loíza: la cultura afro-puertorriqueña ante los procesos de hibridación y globalización|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=S0AbGJPfMO4C&pg=PA73|year=2000|publisher=Isla Negra Editores|isbn=978-1-881715-64-1|pages=73–}}</ref><ref name="ElNuevoDia 2019">{{cite web | title=Guía de wikén: celebran las Fiestas Tradicionales de Loíza | website=El Nuevo Dia | date=17 July 2019 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/guiadewikencelebranlasfiestastradicionalesdeloiza-2506300/ | language=es | access-date=22 July 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190722011416/https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/nota/guiadewikencelebranlasfiestastradicionalesdeloiza-2506300/ | archive-date=22 July 2019 | url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Repeating Islands">{{cite web | title=What's On Our Nightstands: Re/visions of Santiago Apóstol | website=Repeating Islands | date=10 April 2009 | url=https://repeatingislands.com/2009/04/10/whats-on-our-nightstands-revisions-of-santiago-apostol/ | language=es | access-date=8 November 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191108114122/https://repeatingislands.com/2009/04/10/whats-on-our-nightstands-revisions-of-santiago-apostol/ | archive-date=8 November 2019 | url-status=live }}</ref>

==Government==
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
[[File:Julia Nazario Fuentes, Mayor of Loiza walking past damaged house in Loíza, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Julia Nazario Fuentes, the mayor of Loiza, is seen walking past a damaged house in Loíza, Puerto Rico after [[Hurricane Maria]] in September 2017.]]
Like all municipalities in Puerto Rico, Loíza is administered by a mayor. The current mayor is [[Julia Nazario|Julia María Nazario Fuentes]], who was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2016|2016 Puerto Rican general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VIII]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Marissa Jiménez]] and [[Héctor Joaquín Sánchez Álvarez]] were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Carolina_VIII.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{webarchive|url=https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Carolina_VIII.xml |date=2024-12-30 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
Green and gold and red with three undulating stripes - The silhouette of a bell tower in the upper  left hand  corner (Canton) of the first stripe represents religious tradition and also serves as a symbol of the [[Parroquia del Espíritu Santo y San Patricio|Church of San Patricio]] as an historical monument.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=LOIZA | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/LOIZA.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219000545/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/LOIZA.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The mounted figure of [[James the Great|Saint James the Apostle]], dominant in the shield, proclaims the devotion to the saint that the Loiceños profess, manifested in a special way during the celebration of traditional festivities every July 25. The flames are emblem of the Holy Spirit, bearer of the seven gifts, a title of the old church of Loíza. The undulating stripe represents the [[Río Grande de Loíza|Grande de Loíza River]], notable in geography, history and literature of Puerto Rico. The crown symbolizes the famous Taína Chief Yuisa, who lived in Loíza territory where she died. The [[shamrock]]s represent [[Saint Patrick]] of [[Ireland]] and patron of the population.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

==Education==
[[File:Students at Belen Blanco De Zequeira, Loiza, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Students at Belen Blanco De Zequeira, a middle school in Loiza]]
Like all other municipalities, education in Loíza is administered by the [[Department of Education of Puerto Rico]]. Loíza has several elementary schools, but only two junior high and two high schools.

==Transportation==
Service to Loiza is available from San Juan by [[Puerto Rico Metropolitan Bus Authority|AMA]] route D45. This route provides service to [[Piñones (Loíza)|Piñones]] and [[Isla Verde, Puerto Rico|Isla Verde]] and connects to the [[Tren Urbano]] at [[Sagrado Corazón station|Sagrado Corazon]] station.

There is no public transportation connecting Loiza to the eastern cities of PR, and there are few if any hotels and guest houses in Loiza itself, but there are resorts in [[Rio Grande, Puerto Rico|Rio Grande]].

There are 5 bridges in Loíza.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web|title=Loíza Bridges|url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/loiza/|website=National Bridge Inventory Data|publisher=US Dept. of Transportation|access-date=20 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190221224236/http://bridgereports.com/pr/loiza/|archive-date=21 February 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>
