'''Alotenango''' (''Alo-tenamitl-co''; translation "in the wall of the parrots")<ref name="Membreño">{{cite book|last=Membreño|first=Alberto |title=Nombres geográficos indígenas de la república de Honduras|url=https://archive.org/details/nombresgeogrfic03membgoog|year=1901|publisher=Tipografía Nacional|page=xix|chapter=Nombres Geograficos de Guatemala}}</ref> (variation: '''Atchalan''')<ref name="Moore">{{harvnb|Moore|1998|pp=415–416}}</ref> is a town and municipality in the [[Guatemala]]n department of [[Sacatepéquez Department|Sacatepéquez]].  According to the 2018 census, the town has a population of 23,358.<ref>[https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/cities/ Citypopulation.de] Population of cities & towns in Guatemala</ref> The municipality consists of four [[Ward (country subdivision)|wards]] and is situated on the [[Escuintla]] road ([[National Highway 14 (Guatemala)|National Highway 14]]).<ref name="Faubert">{{harvnb|Faubert|Soldevila|2000|p=142}}</ref> Located in a valley, Alotenango is a [[Ladino people|Ladino]] coffee center, since the times of general [[Justo Rufino Barrios]] liberal regime (1873–1885).{{sfn|Stewart|Whatmore|2002|pp=112,114}}

==History==

[[File:A glimpse of Guatemala 39-firepeak mesta.JPG|thumb|200px|left|[[Volcán de Fuego]] summit as seen from the saddle that separates it from [[volcán Acatenango]] in 1899. Photograph by [[Alfred Percival Maudslay]], who climbed the peak from Alotenango.{{sfn|Maudslay|Maudslay|1899|p=39}}]]

According to the [[Popol Vuh]], the town is mentioned as Vucuc Caquix. The community of Alotenango grew up originally 3 to 4 kilometers south of the current settlement which today is the Candelaria farm. This settlement was established before the Spanish arrived in Alotenango in 1524.<ref>{{cite web|url=
http://www.inforpressca.com/alotenango/historia.php|title=Alontenango:Historia|language=Spanish|publisher=Inforpressca|access-date=16 October 2010}}</ref> In the 1540s, bishop [[Francisco Marroquín]] split the religious coverage of the Guatemala central valley between the [[Order of Preachers]] and the [[Franciscans]], getting to the latter the Alotenango curato, among others.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=336}} Given that there was not a separation of Church and State, the curato division was transferred into the geography of the valley; thus, the Alotenango valley was delimited by the Guatemala valley – that is, [[Antigua Guatemala]] to the east, Chimaltenango valley to the north, and the [[Escuintla Department|Escuintla Province]] to the south and west.{{sfn|Juarros|1818|p=345}}

In 1881, French writer [[Eugenio Dussaussay]] climbed the volcano, then practically unexplored.{{sfn|Dussaussay|1897|p=180}} First, he needed to ask for permission to climb to Sacatepéquez governor, who gave him a letter for Alotenango major asking him for guides to help the explorer and his companion, Tadeo Trabanino.{{sfn|Dussaussay|1897|p=179-182}}  They wanted to climb the central peak, unexplored at the time, but could not find a guide and had to climb to the active cone, which had had a recent eruption in 1880.{{sfn|Dussaussay|1897|p=179}}  His guide, Rudecindo Zul, from Alotenango, was the only one to offer to help, but only to the saddle that divides Fuego from [[volcán Acatenango]], as the townspeople feared and respected the mountain too much to go beyond that point.{{sfn|Dussaussay|1897|p=179}}

Archeologist Alfred Percival Maudslay, in his book ''A Glimpse at Guatemala'', tells about his expeditions to volcán de Fuego.  The second one took place on 1 January 1892, from Alotenango,{{sfn|Maudslay|Maudslay|1899|pp=37–38}} and he described it as follows:{{sfn|Maudslay|Maudslay|1899|p=38}}

{{quote box|align=center|width=70%|"On New Year's day, 1892, I climbed up Agua again, and as it was fortunately a clear day I took a round of angles and some photographs.
During the next few days I made the acquaintance of Dr. Otto Stoll, who was then practicing medicine in Antigua, and collecting the valuable notes on the Indian languages which he has since published, and, to my great delight, I learnt that he wished to make the ascent of Fuego; so we arranged to start the very next day for the village of Alotenango. On 7 January we left that village about 7 o'clock in the morning with seven Mozos, carrying food, clothing, and my camp-bed, and rode for an hour towards the mountains, when we dismounted and sent back our mules. The first two hours' climb was not so very steep, but it was tiring work walking over the loose mould and dry leaves under the thick forest."|[[Alfred Percival Maudslay]]{{sfn|Maudslay|Maudslay|1899|p=38}}}}

==Culture==
{{See also|La Aurora International Airport}}

The municipality is home to a [[Franciscan]] [[monastery]] and a large church. A large hut has been used as a [[courthouse]]; the sandy [[plaza]] holds a Sunday market.<ref name="Moore" />

The residents, mainly [[Maya peoples]], are notable for a highly particular local culture retaining elements from their ancient [[Maya society]] past while including elements from the dominant [[Hispanic]] culture as well.<ref>{{harvnb|Moore|1998|p=426}}</ref> Most of the people are [[peasant]] [[Kaqchikel people|Cakchiquel]] who once spoke only [[Kaqchikel language|Cakchiquel]] but now mainly speak Spanish.<ref name="Moore, p. 311">{{harvnb|Moore|1998|p=311}}</ref> Other historians believe the town is inhabited by descendants of [[Nahuatl dialects|Nahua]] speaking [[Pipils]], an indigenous people who live in western [[El Salvador]].<ref name="Faubert" /> However, in the ''Titulo de Alotenango'', a 16th-century legal document, the land dispute claims were between the Cakchiquel of Alotenango and the Pipil of Escuintla.<ref name="Williams">{{harvnb|Williams|2002|p=334}}</ref>

Married couples leave their [[Patrilocal residence|patrilocal]] extended household only after several children are born.<ref name="Moore, p. 311"/> 24 June is a festival day in honor of [[Saint John the Baptist]], the town's patron saint.

==Economy==
Under [[Anacafé]], Alotenango is situated in the [[Antigua Guatemala|Antigua]] coffee region.<ref name="antiguacoffee">{{cite web|url=http://antiguacoffee.org/portal/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=17&Itemid=33|title=Antigua Coffee|work=antiguacoffee.org|access-date=16 October 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101211020658/http://antiguacoffee.org/portal/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=17&Itemid=33|archive-date=11 December 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Capetillo farm was developed in the 18th century by Spanish Royal Treasurer Juan Antonio Capetillo. Formed from different lots totaling seven caballertas, it only grew [[sugar cane]] until 1875. The farm was purchased by Jose Mariano Rodriguez in 1875, and he began coffee cultivation. Revenue generation figures for that year were 2,800 pesos, increasing to 12,000 pesos in 1878. In 1880, there were 200,000 producing coffee trees, a mill, and 25,000 pesos of revenue. Capetillo coffee was awarded the only Grand Prize for Coffee at the [[Panama–Pacific International Exposition|1915 Universal Exhibition]].{{sfn|Wagner|Von Rothkirch|2001|pp=114,135}}

==Geographic location==

The town is the starting point for the ascent of [[Volcán de Agua]] which like most other places within the department looms above to the immediate east of the town. There is also a direct ascent available for [[Volcán de Fuego]] from Alotenango.<ref name="Stewart">{{harvnb|Stewart|Whatmore|2002|p=115}}</ref> National Highway 14 connects Alotenango to the cities of [[Antigua Guatemala]] in the north and [[Escuintla]] in the south.
