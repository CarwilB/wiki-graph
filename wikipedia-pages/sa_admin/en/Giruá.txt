'''Giruá''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the northern part of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Sul]], [[Brazil]]. The population is 15,863 (2020 est.) in an area of 855.92 km<sup>2</sup>.<ref>[http://cod.ibge.gov.br/N0X Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]</ref> It is located 474&nbsp;km west of the state capital of [[Porto Alegre]], northeast of [[Alegrete]]. It also borders [[Santa Rosa, Rio Grande do Sul|Santa Rosa]] and [[Santo Ângelo]].

==Economy==

Because of its rich [[volcanic]] soil, agriculture is important in Giruá, notably [[soy]] production. Its nickname is the Capital of Productivity. Other important crops are [[maize]], [[wheat]], [[sunflower seed|sunflower]] and [[linseed]].

==History==

The area of Giruá was first inhabited by the [[Guarani people]], and in the 17th century [[Misiones Orientales|Jesuit missions]] arrived. The name Giruá comes from ''jerivá'', an indigenous word for the fruit of the ''[[Butia yatay|butia]]'' palm.
