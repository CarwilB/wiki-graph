'''Manacapuru''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[Greater Manaus|Manaus Metropolitan Region]], in the [[Administrative divisions of Brazil|state]] of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]]. It is the third most populous city in the state with {{formatnum:101883}} inhabitants, according to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]] conducted by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/am/manacapuru/panorama|title=Manacapuru|access-date=November 14, 2020|publisher=IBGE Cities|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023123812/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/am/manacapuru/panorama|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Situated on the banks of the [[Solimões River]], 93 kilometers from [[Manaus]] by road,<ref name="Maps"/> the main access to the city is via the Manoel Urbano Highway, where the [[Rio Negro Bridge]] is located, playing a crucial role in the integration and development of Greater Manaus.<ref>{{cite web|date=October 24, 2011|title=Rio Negro Bridge is a Milestone in Regional Integration|url=http://g1.globo.com/am/amazonas/noticia/2011/10/ponte-rio-negro-e-um-marco-na-integracao-da-regiao-metropolitana.html|publisher=G1 Amazonas|access-date=December 31, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023123834/http://g1.globo.com/am/amazonas/noticia/2011/10/ponte-rio-negro-e-um-marco-na-integracao-da-regiao-metropolitana.html|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

The municipality covers an area of {{formatnum:7336.579}} km<sup>2</sup>, representing 0.4705% of the Amazonas state area, 0.1903% of the [[Northern Brazil|Northern Region]], and 0.0862% of the entire [[Geography of Brazil|Brazilian territory]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100113036|title=General Data of the Municipality of Manacapuru (AM)|author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=December 15, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023123856/http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100113036|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Manacapuru has an average annual minimum temperature of 24&nbsp;°C and a maximum of 35&nbsp;°C.<ref name="Manacapuru 43"/> The vegetation, typical of the Amazon region, consists of floodplain and upland forests, with a landscape of lakes, islets, and a small hill range surrounding it.<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Silva|first1=Francisco Weliton Rocha|last2=Macedo|first2=Rodrigo Santana|last3=Souza|first3=Kleberson Worslley|last4=Guimarães|first4=Sérgio Tavares|last5=Lima|first5=Hedinaldo Narciso|last6=Teixeira|first6=Wenceslau Geraldes|last7=Neves Junior|first7=Afrânio Ferreira|date=March 1, 2013|title=Characterization and Classification of Gleysols in the Solimões River Floodplain (Manacapuru and Iranduba), Amazonas, Brazil|url=http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0100-06832013000200003|journal=Brazilian Journal of Soil Science|access-date=September 21, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023123912/http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0100-06832013000200003|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

Founded in 1786, it originated from a [[Mura people|Mura indigenous]] village, pacified in 1785, which settled on the left bank of the [[Solimões River]] in the 18th century, giving rise to the locality.<ref name="História"/> Manacapuru is nationally known as the ''Little Princess of the Solimões'', a nickname it has held since the mid-19th century.<ref>{{cite web|title=Amazonas Government Builds Concrete Bridge for Rural Community in Manacapuru|url=http://www.amazonas.am.gov.br/2018/07/governo-do-amazonas-constroi-ponte-em-concreto-para-comunidade-rural-de-manacapuru/|publisher=Government of the State of Amazonas|date=July 25, 2018|access-date=December 31, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023123934/http://www.amazonas.am.gov.br/2018/07/governo-do-amazonas-constroi-ponte-em-concreto-para-comunidade-rural-de-manacapuru/|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Many of its natural attractions are nationally recognized, as is its traditional cultural festival, the Manacapuru Ciranda Festival,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://viagem.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,rivalidade-na-festa-das-cirandas,222059|title=Rivalry at the Ciranda Festival|date=August 12, 2008|access-date=November 14, 2020|publisher=O Estado de S. Paulo|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124012/https://viagem.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,rivalidade-na-festa-das-cirandas,222059|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> making the municipality one of the most visited by tourists in the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]].<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.amazonas.am.gov.br/2018/09/festival-de-ciranda-de-manacapuru-movimenta-o-turismo-no-municipio/|title=Manacapuru Ciranda Festival Boosts Tourism in the Municipality|date=September 3, 2018|access-date=November 14, 2020|publisher=Government of the State of Amazonas|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124034/http://www.amazonas.am.gov.br/2018/09/festival-de-ciranda-de-manacapuru-movimenta-o-turismo-no-municipio/|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

Manacapuru has the third largest GDP in Amazonas, accounting for 1.35% of the state's total GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> Its [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) is 0.614, considered below the national average but medium compared to the [[List of municipalities in Amazonas by HDI|HDI of the state]], which was 0.674. In 2022, the municipality had 66 healthcare facilities (hospitals, primary healthcare units, and medical clinics).<ref name="Saúde 2009">{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2024/02/02/casas-templos-hospitais-escolas-ibge-mapeia-111-milhoes-de-enderecos-no-brasil-consulte-os-mais-comuns-na-sua-cidade.ghtml?_gl=1*d31bft*_ga*YW1wLTRseDhMbHc5RW5WQVQxM1VJYzdHZ1ExaUdrTUI4NUhxTzgwalRBdXNMRXVBTEV2VGg5OUlSbk1tX3NncDAzZDI.|title=Houses, Temples, Hospitals, Schools: IBGE Maps 111 Million Addresses in Brazil; Check the Most Common in Your City|date=February 2, 2024|access-date=March 2, 2024|author=((g1.com – source: IBGE))|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124112/https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2024/02/02/casas-templos-hospitais-escolas-ibge-mapeia-111-milhoes-de-enderecos-no-brasil-consulte-os-mais-comuns-na-sua-cidade.ghtml|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Its patron saint is [[Our Lady of Nazareth]], and the Our Lady of Nazareth Church is the city's main landmark.<ref name=":1">{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/biblioteca-catalogo.html?id=440541&view=detalhes|title=Our Lady of Nazareth Church|access-date=November 14, 2020|publisher=IBGE Library|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124134/https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/biblioteca-catalogo.html?id=440541&view=detalhes|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Manacapuru is a word of [[Indigenous peoples of Brazil|indigenous]] origin, derived from the terms ''Manacá'' and ''Puru''. ''Manacá'' is a Brazilian [[Dicotyledon|dicotyledonous plant]] belonging to the [[Solanaceae]] family. In [[Tupi-Guarani]], the word means "flower." The word "Puru" has the same origin but means "ornamented" or "variegated." Thus, in Tupi-Guarani, Manacapuru translates to ''Variegated Flower''.<ref name="Manacapuru 43"/>

Another possible translation of the municipality's name comes from the [[Mura language]], also meaning "Variegated Flower." The name Manacapuru refers to a fierce female warrior leader who ruled this nation on the left bank of the [[Solimões River]], where the city now stands.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://parintins24hs.com.br/astronomia-em-manacapuru-am-frutos-do-erea76/|title=Meaning of Manacapuru|website=parintins24hs.com.br|date=12 April 2019 |access-date=September 21, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124212/https://parintins24hs.com.br/astronomia-em-manacapuru-am-frutos-do-erea76/|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

== History ==
The [[Mura people]], the original inhabitants of the region, occupied the area of present-day Manacapuru as early as the 17th century. The Mura were known to the [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]] as warlike and hostile, which led to wars waged by Portuguese settlers starting in 1774, under the command of Matias Fernandes and the director of the Santo Antônio do Imaripi village, located in [[Japurá]], far from the region.<ref name="História"/>

Due to the significant distance from Japurá to the Mura's location, by 1785, a fishing post called Caldeirão existed on the banks of the [[Solimões River]], just below the mouth of the [[Manacapuru River]], with its production intended to supply the military garrison stationed in [[Barcelos, Amazonas|Barcelos]], then the seat of the [[Captaincies of Brazil|captaincy]]. The fishing post was managed by Sebastião Pereira de Castro.<ref name="História"/>

Sebastião Pereira de Castro reported to General Pereira Caldas a large migration of Mura people from other regions to the area. According to Castro, on September 27 of that year, a "large number of Mura people" arrived, wishing to settle in the vicinity. In response, General Pereira Caldas recommended relocating the indigenous people to the village of [[Anamã]]—later to become a municipality—or another place designated by the administrator. The chosen location for the Mura settlement was the bank of Lake Manacapuru. There, approximately 290 Mura people settled on February 15, 1786, establishing the settlement named ''Manacapuru'' after the lake.<ref name="História"/>

=== Administrative formation ===
The Parish of Our Lady of Nazareth of Manacapuru was established on August 12, 1865, by Law No. 148, with its seat in the Manacapuru village. By Law No. 83 of September 27, 1894, the municipality of Manacapuru was created, with territory separated from the municipality of [[Manaus]]. Its establishment occurred on June 16, 1895.<ref name="História"/>

The Manacapuru District was created by Law No. 354 of September 10, 1901. By Law No. 1,126 of November 5, 1921, the district was abolished, only to be reestablished the following year, in 1922, by Law No. 1,133 of February 7. The municipality was granted city status on July 16, 1932, by State Act No. 1,639.<ref name="História"/>

Under the administrative division in effect in December 1959, the municipality comprised three districts: Manacapuru, [[Beruri]], and [[Caapiranga]], all of which have since been emancipated.<ref name="História"/>

Currently, the municipality has only one district, Caviana, which also had a proposal for emancipation in 2010, along with twenty-seven other districts in the state of Amazonas.<ref name="Caviana">{{cite web|url=http://www.cbnmanaus.com.br/site/noticia.php?cod=1551|title=Deputies Visit Manacapuru District Seeking Emancipation This Monday|work=CBN Manaus|date=December 12, 2010|access-date=February 8, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124234/http://www.cbnmanaus.com.br/site/noticia.php?cod=1551|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

=== Recent history ===
Due to Manacapuru's demographic growth, currently the fourth most populous city in Amazonas and one of the largest in population in the [[Northern Brazil|Northern Region]], the municipality was included in the [[Greater Manaus|Manaus Metropolitan Region]] on December 27, 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sapl.al.am.leg.br/sapl_documentos/norma_juridica/850_texto_integral|title=Complementary Law No. 59/2007 of December 27, 2007|date=December 27, 2007|access-date=September 2, 2018|publisher=Amazonas State Legislative Assembly|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124312/https://sapl.al.am.leg.br/sapl_documentos/norma_juridica/850_texto_integral|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

It currently has an area of 7,329 square kilometers.<ref name="IBGE_Área"/> Its [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) is 0.663.

== Geography ==
[[File:City Lights of Greater Manaus.jpg|alt=|left|thumb|[[Satellite imagery|Satellite image]] at night showing Manacapuru integrated into the [[conurbation]] process of [[Greater Manaus]].]]
The municipality of Manacapuru is located on the left bank of the [[Solimões River]], at the confluence with the [[Manacapuru River]], southwest of the [[Manaus|capital of Amazonas]], 93&nbsp;km by road from it. Its geographic coordinates are 3° 18' 15" south latitude and 60° 37' 03" west longitude.<ref name="Geografia">{{cite web|url=http://animais.mfrural.com.br/estados/animais-peixes-2-228-amazonas-cidade-manacapuru.aspx|publisher=MF Rural|title=Details about the City of Manacapuru|access-date=December 24, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124334/http://animais.mfrural.com.br/estados/animais-peixes-2-228-amazonas-cidade-manacapuru.aspx|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

The territory has significant aquatic, floral, and faunal potential. It was the first municipality in Amazonas to establish a Municipal Conservation Unit System (SMUC)—the Piranha Sustainable Development Reserve—along with the Miriti Environmental Protection Area and the Paru and Calado Lakes Maintenance Area.<ref name="Manacapuru 43"/>

The vegetation is almost entirely characterized by floodplain and upland areas, heavily influenced by these environments.<ref name="Manacapuru 43"/>

=== Bordering municipalities ===
Manacapuru borders six municipalities, namely: [[Iranduba]] and [[Manaquiri]] to the east; [[Beruri]] to the south; [[Anamã]] and [[Caapiranga]] to the west; and [[Novo Airão]] to the north and northwest.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru">{{cite web|url=http://www.citybrazil.com.br/am/manacapuru/geral_detalhe.php?cat=2|title=General Data: Manacapuru Borders|work=City Brazil|date=2010|access-date=February 17, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150217190429/http://www.citybrazil.com.br/am/manacapuru/geral_detalhe.php?cat=2|archive-date=February 17, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

;Border with Iranduba
The border with Iranduba begins at the headwaters of the Açú Stream, the watershed between the [[Rio Negro (Amazon)|Negro]] and [[Manacapuru River|Manacapuru]] rivers. This watershed extends southeast, reaching the headwaters of the Anta Stream, along its midline, until it meets Lake Aracapuri. From there, the Açú Stream, separating the two municipalities, extends to Lake Ubim. 
In the northern region of the municipality, the dividing line with Iranduba is the highway, which connects [[Manaus]] to [[Novo Airão]]. In this region, the divider is also the Ariaú Paraná, a lake that flows into the [[Solimões River]].<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

;Border with Manaquiri
The border between Manacapuru and Manaquiri begins on the right bank of the [[Solimões River]], ascending along this bank to Lake Paraná do Barroso, at the westernmost part of Barroso Island. From there, the boundary is a line crossing the Paraná do Lago Grande village, reaching Lake Grande. From this lake, the divider becomes the Solimões River again, ending at Lake Manaquiri and the source of the Sucuri Stream.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

;Border with Beruri
Manacapuru's border with Beruri also begins at the source of the Sucuri Stream. After the Sucuri Stream, the territorial divider becomes the Pupunha Stream, which separates the boundaries along a midline, reaching the Acarituba, Papagaio, and Baruri lakes. From there, the territorial divider becomes the Solimões River.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

;Border with Anamã
It begins on the right bank of the Solimões River, at the westernmost part of Iauara Island, descending the river along the western bank. From there, the territorial divider between Manacapuru and Anamã becomes a midline, reaching its intersection with the parallel of 3º 30' south.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

;Border with Caapiranga
It starts at the parallel of 3º 30' south. This parallel, to the northeast, reaches the source of the Cuité Stream. Other dividers used for the territorial boundaries between Manacapuru and Caapiranga include the Cabaliana, Paraná do Anamã, Campina, Cláudio, and Piraí lakes, and the Manacapuru and Solimões rivers.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

;Border with Novo Airão
The border between the two municipalities begins at the Piraí Stream and reaches the Petrobras Station Stream. The Manacapuru River is used as the boundary between the two municipalities after the Petrobras Stream ends. Finally, the [[Rio Negro (Amazon)|Rio Negro]] marks the geographic and territorial boundary between Manacapuru and Novo Airão.<ref name="Limites de Manacapuru"/>

=== Climate ===
Manacapuru has a [[tropical rainforest climate]], prevalent throughout the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]]. The climate is moderated by high rainfall and [[trade winds]] blowing from the Atlantic Ocean. Temperature drops are common in the municipality, significantly reducing heat intensity, especially at night. There are two distinct seasons: winter, starting in December, and summer, starting in May.<ref name="Geografia"/>

Between April 1958 and December 1960, the highest recorded temperature in Manacapuru was 35.8&nbsp;°C, observed on October 15, 1958.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=1245&action=diaria&mes=10&ano=1958&ok.x=21&ok.y=6|title=Maximum Temperature in October 1958 (Bela Vista/Manacapuru – BRA)|author=INPE/CPTEC|work=BDC (Climatological Databases)|access-date=February 9, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115012345/http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=1245&action=diaria&mes=10&ano=1958&ok.x=21&ok.y=6|archive-date=January 15, 2019}}</ref> The lowest was 18.1&nbsp;°C, on July 21, 1958.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=1245&action=diaria&mes=07&ano=1958&ok.x=17&ok.y=1|title=Minimum Temperature in July 1958 (Bela Vista/Manacapuru – BRA)|author=INPE/CPTEC|work=BDC (Climatological Databases)|access-date=February 9, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115012412/http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=1245&action=diaria&mes=07&ano=1958&ok.x=17&ok.y=1|archive-date=January 15, 2019}}</ref> During this period, the highest 24-hour rainfall accumulation was 94&nbsp;mm, on December 29, 1958.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=22293&action=diaria&mes=05&ano=2005&ok.x=6&ok.y=6|title=Accumulated Precipitation in December 1958 (Bela Vista/Manacapuru – BRA)|author=INPE/CPTEC|work=BDC (Climatological Databases)|access-date=February 9, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115012456/http://bancodedados.cptec.inpe.br/climatologia/Controller?codigo=22293&action=diaria&mes=05&ano=2005&ok.x=6&ok.y=6|archive-date=January 15, 2019}}</ref>

=== Hydrography ===
Manacapuru is located within the [[Amazon River basin|Amazon hydrographic basin]]. The rivers flowing through Manacapuru are the Solimões and Manacapuru rivers, the latter giving the city its name.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.viagensmaneiras.com/viagens/manacapuru.htm|publisher=Viagens Maneiras|title=Manacapuru (AM)|access-date=December 22, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124412/http://www.viagensmaneiras.com/viagens/manacapuru.htm|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> The Solimões River originates in [[Peru]] and, upon entering Brazil in the municipality of [[Tabatinga]], is named Solimões.<ref name="rio solimões 2">{{cite web|url=http://portalamazonia.globo.com/pscript/amazoniadeaaz/artigoAZ.php?idAz=242|publisher=PortalAmazônia|title=Solimões River|date=September 6, 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115012534/http://portalamazonia.globo.com/pscript/amazoniadeaaz/artigoAZ.php?idAz=242|archive-date=January 15, 2019}}</ref>

In addition to the Solimões and Manacapuru rivers, the municipality is bathed by the [[Purus River|Purus]] and Jará rivers.<ref name="Geografia"/>

=== Fauna and flora ===
[[File:Vitregias02.jpg|thumb|right|[[Victoria amazonica]], the second-largest waterlily in the world.]]

The [[fauna]] and [[flora]] of the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]] are highly diverse, with the same tropical rainforest fauna found in various municipalities. Manacapuru is home to numerous species of plants and [[Passerine|birds]], countless [[amphibian]]s, and millions of insects.<ref name="fauna e flora de Manaus">{{cite web|url=http://www.emdiv.com.br/pt/brasil/geografia/311-fauna-e-flora-da-amazonia.html|publisher=Portal EmDiv|title=Fauna & Flora of the Amazon|date=September 6, 2010|access-date=February 7, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101215193936/http://emdiv.com.br/pt/brasil/geografia/311-fauna-e-flora-da-amazonia.html|archive-date=December 15, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Large aquatic mammals, such as the [[manatee]] and [[boto]], are found mainly in areas with minimal movement in the [[Rio Negro (Amazon)|Rio Negro]]. The municipality's hydrography is privileged, especially at the border with [[Novo Airão]], known as the "land of the manatee."<ref name="ReferenceA">Revista Eletrônica Aboré – Publication of the Superior School of Arts and Tourism – Manaus Edition 03/2007</ref> Some Amazonian trees, such as the [[Carapa guianensis|andiroba]] and [[Ceiba pentandra|kapok]], are found in some city areas, especially in untouched regions. In the urban area, such trees are scarce.<ref name="ReferenceA"/> Reptiles such as [[Testudinata|turtles]], [[caiman]]s, and [[viper]]s also inhabit the area. There are birds and fish of all species, plumages, and skins. In some areas along the rivers, ''[[Victoria amazonica]]'' is found, with circular leaves reaching over one meter in diameter.<ref name="vitória régia">{{cite web|url=http://www.canalkids.com.br/meioambiente/mundodasplantas/vitoria.htm|publisher=Canal Kids|title=Victoria regia|date=September 6, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124434/http://www.canalkids.com.br/meioambiente/mundodasplantas/vitoria.htm|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

== Demography ==

According to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]] conducted by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], the municipality's population was 101,883 inhabitants, making it the 3rd most populous in the state with a population density of 13.89 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/populacao_por_municipio.shtm|title=2010 Demographic Census|access-date=December 5, 2010|publisher=Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124512/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/populacao_por_municipio.shtm|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> According to the 2022 Brazilian Census, 51.02% of the population was male (51,978) and 48.98% was female (49,905), with a median population age of 25 years.

Manacapuru's population growth compared to the 2010 Census was 19.66%.<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=http://www.ogenial.com.br/brasil/amazonas-manacapuru|title=Manacapuru|author=O Genial|date=2010|access-date=February 1, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304094214/http://www.ogenial.com.br/brasil/amazonas-manacapuru|archive-date=March 4, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref> Over nine years, between 1991 and 2000, the population had an average annual growth rate of 2.97%, rising from {{formatnum:57173}} inhabitants in 1991 to {{formatnum:73695}} inhabitants in 2000. In contrast, the urbanization rate grew by only 2.66%, from 63% urbanization in 1991 to 64.67% in 2000.<ref name="SEPLAN">{{cite web|url=http://www.seplan.am.gov.br/arquivos/download/arqeditor/indicadores/perf_mun/Condensadov3/Conteudo/subregiao7/15-manacapuru.html|title=Municipal Profile – Manacapuru|date=2000|access-date=January 14, 2011|author=Secretariat of State for Planning and Economic Development of Amazonas (SEPLAN)|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120206071822/http://www.seplan.am.gov.br/arquivos/download/arqeditor/indicadores/perf_mun/Condensadov3/Conteudo/subregiao7/15-manacapuru.html|archive-date=February 6, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2000, the municipality's population accounted for 2.62% of the [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|state]]'s population and 0.04% of the country's population.<ref name="SEPLAN"/>

During the same period from 1991 to 2000, there was a decline in the dependency ratio. In 1991, the dependency ratio among inhabitants was very high at 107.6%. By 2000, this index dropped to 85% of inhabitants.<ref name="SEPLAN"/>

Manacapuru's Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) is considered medium by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP). In 2000, considering only education, the index was 0.761, while Brazil's was 0.849. The health index was 0.684 (Brazil's was 0.787), and the income index was 0.544 (Brazil's was 0.723).<ref name="SEPLAN"/> Compared to other Brazilian municipalities, Manacapuru has an intermediate situation, ranking 3,555th in HDI, with 3,554 municipalities (64.5%) in a better situation and 1,952 municipalities (35.5%) in a worse or similar situation.<ref name="SEPLAN"/> Compared to Amazonas municipalities, Manacapuru has a reasonable situation: it ranks 15th, with 14 municipalities (22.6%) in a better situation and 47 municipalities (77.4%) in a worse or similar situation.<ref name="SEPLAN"/> From 1991 to 2000, Manacapuru's HDI grew by 11.24%, from 0.596 in 1991 to 0.663 in 2000. Longevity contributed most to this growth, with 51.2%, followed by education, with 49.8%.<ref name="SEPLAN"/> If Manacapuru maintained this HDI growth rate, it would take 26 years to reach [[São Caetano do Sul]], the municipality with the highest HDI in Brazil (0.919), and 12 years to reach [[Manaus]], the municipality with the highest HDI in the [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|state]] (0.774).<ref name="SEPLAN"/>

=== Ethnic composition ===
The cultural, political, and economic traits inherited from the [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]], [[Spanish people|Spaniards]], and [[Dutch people|Dutch]] mark the municipality of Manacapuru. However, the significant contribution of indigenous peoples to its ethnic makeup cannot be overlooked. The indigenous peoples initiated human occupation in the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]], and their descendants, the [[caboclo|''caboclos'']], developed in close contact with the environment, adapting to regional peculiarities and opportunities offered by the forest.

In its historical formation, Manacapuru's demography results from the [[miscegenation]] of the three basic [[ethnic groups]] that make up the Brazilian population: the [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]], the [[Europeans|European]], and the [[Black people|African]], forming the region's ''mestiços'' (''caboclos''). Later, with the arrival of immigrants, especially Japanese<ref name="Japoneses">{{cite web|publisher=Japan 100|url=http://www.japao100.com.br/arquivo/quarta-raca/|title=The Fourth Race|date=2007|access-date=January 28, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124556/http://www.japao100.com.br/arquivo/quarta-raca/|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> and Jews from [[Morocco]],<ref name="Judeus">{{cite web|publisher=Morashá Magazine|url=http://www.morasha.com.br/conteudo/artigos/artigos_view.asp?a=645&p=2|title=Moroccan Jews Who Came to the Amazon|date=December 2010|access-date=January 28, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124612/http://www.morasha.com.br/conteudo/artigos/artigos_view.asp?a=645&p=2|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> a unique cultural blend was formed, characterizing the city's population, values, and way of life. The city is home to a notable Moroccan community, mostly Jewish.<ref name="Judeus"/>

According to the [[2022 Brazilian Census]] by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], the population of Manacapuru is composed of the following groups: [[Pardo Brazilians|mixed race]] (82.93%, or 84,487 people), [[White Brazilians|white]] (12.39%, or 12,620 people), [[Afro-Brazilians|black]] (3.83%, or 3,898 people), [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous]] (0.81%, or 830 people), and [[Asian Brazilians|Asian]] (0.05%, or 48 people).

=== Religion ===
Cultural diversity is evident in Manacapuru, with various religious manifestations present. Like many Brazilian municipalities, the city developed on a predominantly [[Catholicism|Catholic]] social matrix, and today, numerous [[Protestant]] denominations can be found. The growth of those identifying as [[Irreligion|non-religious]] is also notable, accounting for 6.09% of the city's religious population in 2000.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P|title=Count by Color, Race, and Religion|author=IBGE – through the Ministry of Information Support|date=2010|access-date=January 14, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124634/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Manacapuru is located in the world's most Catholic country in absolute numbers. The [[Catholic Church]] had its legal status recognized by the federal government in October 2009,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/mat/2009/10/08/senado-aprova-acordo-com-vaticano-767959739.asp|title=Senate Approves Agreement with the Vatican|author=Cristiane Agostine|publisher=O Globo|date=October 8, 2009|access-date=October 12, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124712/http://oglobo.globo.com/economia/mat/2009/10/08/senado-aprova-acordo-com-vaticano-767959739.asp|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> although Brazil is currently an officially [[secular state]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jus2.uol.com.br/doutrina/texto.asp?id=8519|title=Brazil: Secular State and the Unconstitutionality of Religious Symbols in Public Buildings|author=Fernando Fonseca de Queiroz|date=October 2005|publisher=Jus Navigandi|access-date=October 12, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091223024547/http://jus2.uol.com.br/doutrina/texto.asp?id=8519|archive-date=December 23, 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref>

According to 2000 census data from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, Manacapuru's population is composed of: [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (65.68%), [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (25.73%), [[Irreligion|non-religious]] (6.09%), [[Buddhism|Buddhists]] (0.01%), and 2.14% are divided among other religions.<ref name="Religião"/> Among Protestant denominations, the [[Assemblies of God]] (15.36%), Baptist Church (2.09%), and [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]] (1.72%) stand out.<ref name="Religião"/> Among restorationist Christian denominations, [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (1.16%) and [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (0.40%) are notable.<ref name="Religião"/> [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]], Judaism, [[Umbanda]], and [[Candomblé]] were not identified among the religious population.<ref name="Religião"/>

== Politics ==
=== Administration ===

According to the [[Constitution of Brazil|1988 Constitution]], Manacapuru is located in a republic [[Federation|federative]] [[Presidential system|presidential]] system. It was inspired by the American model, but the Brazilian legal system follows the [[Roman law|Roman]]-[[Germanic law|Germanic]] tradition of [[positive law]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.oas.org/juridico/mla/en/bra/en_bra-int-des-ordrjur.html|title=The Brazilian Legal System|language=en|author=Organization of American States (OAS)|access-date=December 23, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124734/http://www.oas.org/juridico/mla/en/bra/en_bra-int-des-ordrjur.html|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Municipal administration is carried out by the [[executive branch]] and the [[legislative branch]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.leismunicipais.com.br/cgi-local/orglaw.pl?city=Paul%EDnia&state=sp|title=The Municipal Public Authority Leading the Constitutional Obligation to Create the Internal Control System|author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima|date=February 8, 2006|publisher=JusVi|access-date=December 23, 2010|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120908113231/http://www.leismunicipais.com.br/cgi-local/orglaw.pl?city=Paul%EDnia&state=sp|archive-date=September 8, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The executive branch is represented by the mayor and a cabinet of secretaries, in accordance with the model proposed by Article 29 of the Constitution of Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm|author=DJI|title=Federal Constitution – CF – 1988 / Art. 29|access-date=December 23, 2010|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/64ioyjB1v?url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm|archive-date=January 16, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The legislative branch consists of the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of twenty-one councilors elected for four-year terms (in accordance with Article 29 of the Constitution<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm|author=DJI|title=Federal Constitution – CF – 1988 / Art. 29|access-date=February 3, 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/64ioyjB1v?url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm|archive-date=January 16, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>) and is structured as follows: seven seats for [[Republicans (Brazil)|Republicans]]; seven seats for [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|MDB]]; two seats for [[Progressistas]]; five seats for the [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|Social Democratic Party]] (PSD); and one seat each for the Liberal Party (PL) and União Brasil. The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for administration and the executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law). The municipality of Manacapuru is governed by organic laws.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.info.leigeral.com.br/mapa/html/leisEstaduais/municipal/AM/Manacapuru%20-%20AM.pdf|title=State of Amazonas – Manacapuru Municipal Prefecture|author=Municipal Official Gazette|date=October 31, 2007|access-date=December 23, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121212104803/http://www.info.leigeral.com.br/mapa/html/leisEstaduais/municipal/AM/Manacapuru%20-%20AM.pdf|archive-date=December 12, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> The ''[[comarca]]'' of Manacapuru was created in September 1901, abolished twenty years later in 1921, and reestablished in 1922.<ref name="comarca">{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.com.br/cidade/manacapuru/000159.html|title=Information about Manacapuru|author=Cidades|access-date=February 3, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124812/http://www.cidades.com.br/cidade/manacapuru/000159.html|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

The current mayor of Manacapuru is Valcileia Maciel, affiliated with MDB, who has been in office since January 1, 2025, having been elected on October 6, 2024, with over 71% of valid votes, for a term from January 1, 2025, to January 1, 2029.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/am/amazonas/eleicoes/2024/resultado-das-apuracoes/manacapuru.ghtml?_gl=1*16t3j3d*_ga*YW1wLW84RXZ0dlVrcUNqTTBNRF9WbFNKV09tVVpoTHhKZ2IzLTJxX3IwZ0N5bm9FVnBTdjFiQnR0RFloTUcxbVB4Z1o.|title=Election Results in Manacapuru (AM) – Elections 2024|access-date=April 21, 2025|website=G1|language=pt-br|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124834/https://g1.globo.com/am/amazonas/eleicoes/2024/resultado-das-apuracoes/manacapuru.ghtml|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

== Economy ==
The economy is characterized primarily by the collection of rubber and [[Brazil nut]], hunting, fishing, extensive livestock farming in natural fields, and nascent itinerant agriculture in upland areas, with notable growth in jute and black pepper cultivation in recent years. In Manacapuru, although animal and plant extractive industries are significant sources of wealth, agriculture, particularly jute cultivation, forms the economic backbone of the municipality.

=== Primary sector ===
Agriculture in Manacapuru is one of the main economic sources. The municipality is the largest national producer of [[jute]], with significant production of other crops such as [[cassava]], banana, corn, orange, [[bean]], coffee, and vegetables.<ref name="Economia">{{cite web|url=http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/241/manacapuru-am.html|title=Characteristics of Manacapuru|access-date=December 22, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124912/http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/241/manacapuru-am.html|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>Livestock and fishing also constitute strong economic activities in the municipality, with emphasis on cattle, horse, and pig farming. In 2009, 20,568 cattle, 639 buffalo, and 294 horses were recorded in the municipality.<ref name="IBGE_Pecuária">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Livestock 2009|author=Cidades@ – IBGE|date=2009|access-date=December 22, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=April 30, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> In fishing, the most common species are [[pacu]], [[sardine]], [[Curimata (fish)|curimata]], [[prochilodus]], [[brycon]], and other freshwater fish species.<ref name="Economia" />

[[Poultry farming]] also represents an economic activity for the city, with a farm dedicated to laying hens. [[Extractivism|Vegetable extractivism]] remains a significant activity for the local economy, through the exploitation of products such as rubber, [[Bactris gasipaes|''pupunha'']], and timber. There are several fish farms in the area, focused on raising Amazonian fish species.<ref name="Economia" /> In terms of fruit farming, the municipality produces [[Passion fruit (fruit)|passion fruit]], [[cupuaçu]], [[papaya]], [[pineapple]], banana, [[avocado]], orange, lemon, and [[watermelon]].<ref name="Economia" />

=== Secondary sector ===
Industrial production in the municipality is closely tied to agriculture and local extractive industries. There are industries focused on agribusiness, production of non-metallic minerals, metallurgy, mechanics, electrical materials, transportation equipment, timber, furniture, paper, rubber, leather, pharmaceuticals and veterinary products, plastics, textiles, clothing, beverages, tobacco, publishing, printing, footwear, and construction.<ref name="Economia"/>

In 2008, there were 918 registered companies in the municipality, according to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], generating approximately 4,850 direct jobs.<ref name="IBGE_Empresas">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Central Business Register Statistics 2008|author=Cidades@ – IBGE|date=2008|access-date=December 22, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=April 30, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Tertiary sector ===
The municipality maintains commercial transactions with the markets of [[Manaus]] and [[Belém]]. Among the imported products, food items, fabrics, medicines, hardware, and electrical materials are prominent.<ref name="Setor terciário">{{cite web|url=http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/241/manacapuru-am.html|title=Economic Characteristics|author=Feriastur|date=2008|access-date=February 1, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023124934/http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/241/manacapuru-am.html|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> According to 2008 data, the municipal seat has 918 commercial establishments, generating approximately {{formatnum:4850}} direct jobs with salaries of R$71,537, an average of 3.3 minimum wages.<ref name="Setor terciário 2">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Central Business Register Statistics 2008|author=Cidades@ – IBGE|date=2008|access-date=February 1, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=April 30, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Urban structure ==
=== Housing, basic infrastructure, and security ===
Manacapuru has a reasonable infrastructure. In 2000, the city had {{formatnum:13351}} [[housing|dwellings]] including [[apartments]], [[houses]], and rooms. Of these, {{formatnum:11608}} were owned properties, with {{formatnum:11545}} fully paid (86.47%), 63 in acquisition (0.47%), and 678 [[Renting|rented]] (5.08%); {{formatnum:1010}} properties were provided, with 326 by employers (2.44%) and 684 provided otherwise (5.12%). 55 were occupied in other ways (0.41%).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp|title=Infrastructure – Permanent Private Dwellings and Residents|author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=January 29, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125012/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> Some of these residences have access to treated water, electricity, [[sewage]], [[waste management|urban cleaning]], [[landline telephone|fixed telephony]], and [[mobile phone|mobile telephony]]. In 2000, 50.56% of dwellings were served by the [[Water supply network|general water supply network]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp|title=Infrastructure – Water Supply|author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=January 29, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125034/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> 57.26% of homes had garbage collection<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp|title=Infrastructure – Waste Disposal|author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=January 29, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125056/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref> and only 9.16% of residences had sanitary sewage systems.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp|title=Infrastructure – Sanitary Sewage|author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM)|access-date=January 29, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125112/http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

Between 1991 and 2000, access to consumer goods was considered low. In 1991, only 34.6% of residences had [[refrigerator]]s; 39.5% had televisions; 8.3% had telephones, and none had computers. By 2000, these figures increased: 57.5% of residences had refrigerators; 62.6% had televisions; 8.5% had telephones, and 0.9% had computers.<ref name="SEPLAN"/>

Under the [[Constitution of Brazil|1988 Brazilian Constitution]], the Manacapuru Municipal Guard, along with the Military Police, is responsible for protecting public goods, services, and facilities. Additionally, in the public interest and in exercising its police powers, it acts to prevent and suppress certain crimes, particularly against public goods and services, and can arrest offenders ''[[in flagrante delicto]]'' and bring them before a police chief, as provided by criminal procedure law.<ref name="segurança">{{cite web|url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm|publisher=Planalto|title=Federal Constitution of Brazil|date=September 14, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125134/http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

Crime in Manacapuru is on the rise, as in other Brazilian municipalities. In 2006, the [[homicide]] rate was 9.7 per 100,000 inhabitants, up from 8.8 in 2005. The rate of deaths by [[firearm]], after significant growth between 2002 and 2005, fell in 2006 to 4.5 per 100,000 inhabitants. The rate of deaths from traffic accidents, which was 1 per 100,000 inhabitants in 2002, rose to 12 per 100,000 inhabitants in 2006.<ref name="ritla">{{cite web|url=http://www.ritla.net/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=242|title=Municipal Database|author=Ritla|date=2008|work=Map of Violence in Brazilian Municipalities-2008|format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]]|access-date=January 30, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231023125156/http://www.ritla.net/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=242|archive-date=October 23, 2023}}</ref>

=== Healthcare and education ===

The municipality has twenty-four healthcare facilities, all of which are public and municipal. There are no private healthcare facilities. Of the twenty-four facilities, fifteen provide [[Dentistry|dental services]] and operate as primary healthcare units.<ref name="Saúde">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Health Services 2009|author=Cidades@ – IBGE|date=2009|access-date=January 14, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=April 30, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Manacapuru has schools in all regions of the municipality.<ref name="IBGE_Educação">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Education – Enrollments, Teachers, and School Network 2009|author=@ Cidades – IBGE|access-date=January 14, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=April 30, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2009, the municipality had approximately {{formatnum:22429}} enrollments, 727 teachers, and 171 schools in public and private networks, with 19 state schools and 150 municipal schools.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> Twenty-three municipal schools offered preschool activities.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/>
[[File:Projeto Rondon 2009 Manacapuru.JPG|thumb|right|Horticulture workshop of the Rondon Project in Manacapuru (2009).]]
In terms of higher education, the municipality has a campus of the [[Amazonas State University|University of the State of Amazonas]] (UEA), a FAMETRO college, and a medical school belonging to Afya/Itpac. Manacapuru is home to the Metropolitan Center for Higher Studies of UEA (CMESU-UEA). The Metropolitan Center offers courses in Naval Engineering, Fishing Engineering, Food Production, Fiber Production, and Archaeology at the University of the State of Amazonas, emphasizing these programs to meet the specific demands of [[Novo Airão]], a regional naval industry hub, [[Iranduba]], with significant archaeological sites, and Manacapuru, Brazil's largest producer of plant fiber.<ref name="CMESU-UEA">{{cite web|url=http://blogdafloresta.com.br/ciencia-e-tecnologia/94-manacapuru-vai-sediar-o-centro-metropolitano-de-estudos-superiores-da-uea.html|title=Manacapuru to Host the Metropolitan Center for Higher Studies of UEA|author=Blog da Floresta|access-date=January 14, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190115012612/http://blogdafloresta.com.br/ciencia-e-tecnologia/94-manacapuru-vai-sediar-o-centro-metropolitano-de-estudos-superiores-da-uea.html|archive-date=January 15, 2019}}</ref>

Manacapuru is one of the host cities of the Rondon Project, a Brazilian government initiative coordinated by the [[Ministry of Defence (Brazil)|Ministry of Defence]], in collaboration with the Secretariat of Higher Education of the [[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|Ministry of Education]].<ref name="ProjetoRondon">{{Cite web|url=http://ecoviagem.uol.com.br/noticias/ambiente/nossa-agua/rondonistas-ensinam-ribeirinhos-a-fazer-a-filtragem-artesanal-de-agua-no-amazonas-8360.asp |title=Rondonistas ensinam ribeirinhos a fazer a filtragem artesanal de água no Amazonas |author=Ecoviagem – UOL |access-date=February 9, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107135705/http://ecoviagem.uol.com.br/noticias/ambiente/nossa-agua/rondonistas-ensinam-ribeirinhos-a-fazer-a-filtragem-artesanal-de-agua-no-amazonas-8360.asp |archive-date=November 7, 2012}}</ref>

== Services and communications ==
The municipality is supplied with electricity by the [[Balbina Dam]], located in Balbina, a district of the municipality of [[Presidente Figueiredo]]. Together with the [[Samuel Hydroelectric Dam]], Balbina supplies much of the municipalities in [[Amazônia Legal|Western Amazon]]. The distribution of electricity in the municipality is provided by Amazonas Energia.<ref name="Energia">{{Cite web |url=http://www.horizontegeografico.com.br/index.php?acao=exibirMateria&materia%5Bid_materia%5D=251 |title=Água demais, água de menos – Onde tem água de sobra |author=Editora Horizonte |access-date=February 7, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107135705/http://www.horizontegeografico.com.br/index.php?acao=exibirMateria&materia%5Bid_materia%5D=251 |archive-date=November 7, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The water supply service for the entire city is managed by the Water and Sewage Service (SAAE).<ref name="SAAE">{{Cite web |url=https://www.voxtecnologia.com.br/public/diario/pdf/publicado_3377_2010-10-26_c4337338f8810546d3e7d98c62ca16c3.pdf |title=Serviço de Abastecimento de Água e Esgoto (SAAE) |author=Associação Amazonense de Municípios (ASAM) |access-date=February 7, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190101000000/https://www.voxtecnologia.com.br/public/diario/pdf/publicado_3377_2010-10-26_c4337338f8810546d3e7d98c62ca16c3.pdf |archive-date=January 1, 2019 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Sewage collection is carried out by the municipal government itself. Dial-up and broadband (ADSL) internet services are offered by various free and paid access providers. Mobile telephone services are provided by several operators. The area code ([[Direct distance dialing|DDD]]) for Manacapuru is 092, and the city's postal code ([[Código de Endereçamento Postal|CEP]]) is 99825-000.<ref name="CEP/DDD">{{Cite web|url=http://www.grandebrasil.com.br/Brasil/Cidades/?Municipio_de_Manacapuru,_estado_de_Amazonas_(AM)+241&Grupo=4|title=Município de Manacapuru – Informações |author=Grande Brasil|access-date=February 7, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107135705/http://www.grandebrasil.com.br/Brasil/Cidades/?Municipio_de_Manacapuru,_estado_de_Amazonas_(AM)+241&Grupo=4 |archive-date=November 7, 2012}}</ref>

Manacapuru receives [[free-to-air television]] signals from various Brazilian [[television channel|broadcasters]]. Among the regional broadcasters airing programs related to the municipality, notable ones include Rede Calderaro de Comunicação, which broadcasts [[TV A Crítica]]; Grupo Norte de Comunicação, which airs [[TV Norte Amazonas]], an affiliate of [[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]; and Rede Amazônica, affiliated with [[Rede Globo]].

== Transportation ==
The municipality has a strong tradition of water-based transportation due to the abundance of rivers that traverse the area. However, with the establishment of the Manaus Metropolitan Region in 2007, road transportation has gained greater significance within its territorial boundaries. Like other municipalities in Amazonas, Manacapuru is not served by railways. Public transportation in Manacapuru, as in the entire Manaus Metropolitan Region, is managed by the Greater Manaus Urban Transport Superintendence (STU-RMM). Air transportation is also used, though to a lesser extent.<ref name="Manacapuru 43" />

=== Waterways ===
[[File:Amazon boat.jpg|thumb|Boat on the [[Amazon River]]. [[River transport]] in this region is very common due to the scarcity of roads.]]
The Amazonian rivers remain, as they were during the colonial period of Brazil, the main routes of access in this region.<ref name="Hidrovias">{{Cite web|url=http://www.transportes.gov.br/index/conteudo/id/820 |title=Dados técnicos sobre as bacias hidroviárias – Hidrovia do Solimões |author=Ministério dos Transportes |access-date=February 1, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107135705/http://www.transportes.gov.br/index/conteudo/id/820 |archive-date=November 7, 2012}}</ref> River navigation is highly active in [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], home to the world's largest river by water volume, the [[Amazon River]]. Smaller rivers in the region, such as the [[Juruá River|Juruá]] and [[Purus River|Purus]], are navigable up to their headwaters. The types of watercraft used in the region range from small boats to [[ship|cruise ships]]. Sail-powered vessels handle much of the traffic to the capital, [[Manaus]], and other rural areas of the municipality. The Manacapuru River Terminal, managed by SNPH, is one of the largest in [[Northern Brazil]].<ref name="Hidrovias 2">{{Cite web |url=http://www.manausemnoticia.com/2010/06/entra-em-operacao-as-atracacoes-de.html |title=Entra em operação as atracações de barcos no Terminal Hidroviário de Manacapuru |author=Manaus em Notícia |access-date=February 1, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190101000000/http://www.manausemnoticia.com/2010/06/entra-em-operacao-as-atracacoes-de.html |archive-date=January 1, 2019 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Roads ===
The only highways in Manacapuru are AM-070, which connects Manacapuru to [[Manaus]], [[Iranduba]], and [[Manaquiri]]; and AM-254, which links Manaus to [[Novo Airão]], passing through Manacapuru.<ref name="Manacapuru 43" /> Regarding the vehicle fleet, in 2009, a total of {{formatnum:12492}} vehicles were recorded.<ref name="Frota SEPLAN">{{Cite web|url=http://seplan.am.gov.br/pagina.php?cod=115|title=Frota 2009|author=SEPLAN|access-date=February 2, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100616165046/http://www.seplan.am.gov.br/pagina.php?cod=115|archive-date=June 16, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> There are also public transportation companies that provide services to rural communities in the municipality. Access to Manaus is primarily via AM-070, where the [[Rio Negro Bridge]] is located, along with four other bridges—two over the Ariaú River and two over the Miriti River. The journey takes approximately one hour by car, and there is a bus service between the two cities with thirteen daily trips.<ref name="Ponte Rio Negro">{{Cite web |url=http://acritica.uol.com.br/manaus/Ponte-Rio-Negro-podera-ficar_0_365963528.html |title=Ponte Rio Negro pode ficar para 2011 |author=A Crítica |date=November 4, 2010 |access-date=February 1, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110602035918/http://acritica.uol.com.br/manaus/Ponte-Rio-Negro-podera-ficar_0_365963528.html |archive-date=June 2, 2011 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Culture and society ==
The culture of the municipality, like that of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], has been heavily influenced by the [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous peoples of the region]] and by various groups of immigrants and migrants, particularly [[Spaniards]], who settled there.<ref name="Cultura da Amazônia">{{Cite web|url=http://portalamazonia.globo.com/pscript/amazoniadeaaz/artigoAZ.php?idAz=270|title=Cultura da Amazônia|publisher=Portal Amazônia|access-date=February 1, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090822050039/http://portalamazonia.globo.com/pscript/amazoniadeaaz/artigoAZ.php?idAz=270|archive-date=August 22, 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref> Manacapuru has become a city with extensive cultural blending and diverse traditions.<ref name="Cultura da Amazônia" /> [[Northeastern Brazil|Northeasterners]] who migrated to the Amazon in the late 19th century and early 20th century, drawn by the [[Rubber boom]], also contributed significantly to the formation of the municipal culture.<ref name="Amazônia:Interesses e conflitos">{{Cite web |url=http://www.comciencia.br/reportagens/amazonia/amaz8.htm|title=As Várias Faces da Amazônia: Migrações, Deslocamentos e Mobilidade Social na Região Norte|publisher=Com Ciência|access-date=February 1, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107135705/http://www.comciencia.br/reportagens/amazonia/amaz8.htm |archive-date=November 7, 2012}}</ref> This has resulted in a ''[[mestiço]]'' culture in the region and the [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|state]], with a significant and enduring contribution from [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] culture.<ref name="Amazônia:Interesses e conflitos" />

Manacapuru's culture is rich in traditions and highly appreciated folk festivals. Due to the Ciranda Festival, which in recent years has attracted a large number of tourists to the city, Manacapuru has become known as the "Land of Cirandas".<ref name="Festival de Cirandas 2">{{Cite web|url=http://portalamazonia-teste.tempsite.ws/sites/ciranda/conteudo-menu.php?idM=2557|title=Manacapuru|date=August 30, 2010|access-date=February 3, 2011|author=Portal Amazônia|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100307230710/http://portalamazonia-teste.tempsite.ws/sites/ciranda/conteudo-menu.php?idM=2557|archive-date=March 7, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In music, the highlights are ciranda and [[forró]]. Manacapuru hosts a project called "Caravana da Música," which features the participation of various regional and national artists.<ref name="Música">{{Cite web|url=http://www.portaldanielamercury.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=610:daniela-mercury-acompanha-o-projeto-caravana-da-musica-em-manacapuru-am&catid=50:acoes-sociais&Itemid=125|title=Daniela Mercury acompanha o projeto Caravana da Música em Manacapuru|date=August 31, 2010|access-date=February 3, 2011|author=Portal Daniela Mercury|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190101000000/http://www.portaldanielamercury.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=610:daniela-mercury-acompanha-o-projeto-caravana-da-musica-em-manacapuru-am&catid=50:acoes-sociais&Itemid=125|archive-date=January 1, 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Ciranda Festival ===
Like [[Parintins]], Manacapuru is also known for its [[folklore|folk festival]], but instead of the characteristic ''boi-bumbá'' of Parintins, the city is renowned for its ''cirandas'', groups that perform a typical local music style and hold competitive parades. The Manacapuru Ciranda Festival is held annually at the city's Cirandódromo, located in Parque do Ingá, typically during the last weekend of August or the beginning of September.<ref name="Festival de Cirandas">{{Cite web|url=http://portalamazonia-teste.tempsite.ws/sites/ciranda/conteudo-menu.php?idM=2551|title=Histórico do Festival de Cirandas de Manacapuru|date=August 30, 2010|access-date=February 3, 2011|author=Portal Amazônia|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100307230705/http://portalamazonia-teste.tempsite.ws/sites/ciranda/conteudo-menu.php?idM=2551|archive-date=March 7, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The first competitive Ciranda Festival took place in 1997. Since then, this cultural manifestation has been recognized as a folk tradition, improving in quality and technical level with each passing year.<ref name="Festival de Cirandas" />

The festival features three groups: Flor Matizada, Guerreiros Mura, and Tradicional.<ref>{{Cite web|title=23° Festival de Cirandas de Manacapuru começa nesta sexta (30)|url=https://emtempo.com.br/cultura/170586/23-festival-de-cirandas-de-manacapuru-comeca-nesta-sexta-30|work=Em tempo|date=August 30, 2019|access-date=September 21, 2019|language=pt-br|first=Em|last=tempo|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190921000000/https://emtempo.com.br/cultura/170586/23-festival-de-cirandas-de-manacapuru-comeca-nesta-sexta-30|archive-date=September 21, 2019}}</ref> The Manacapuru Ciranda Festival is one of the largest in the state of Amazonas and in Northern Brazil, attracting between 50,000 and 90,000 tourists annually.<ref name=":0" />

=== Tourism ===
Manacapuru is one of the top [[tourism|tourist destinations]] in Amazonas, attracting a large number of visitors to its nearby beaches, lakes, and streams, which are home to several jungle lodges.<ref name="Turismo">{{Cite web|url=http://www.turismobrasil.gov.br/promocional/roteiros/Mamiraua_Projetos_Desenvolvimento_Sustentavel.html|publisher=Ministério do Turismo|title=Mamirauá e Projetos de Desenvolvimento Sustentável – AM|date=February 3, 2011|access-date=February 3, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120110223811/http://www.turismobrasil.gov.br/promocional/roteiros/Mamiraua_Projetos_Desenvolvimento_Sustentavel.html|archive-date=January 10, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Ecotourism, also known as nature tourism, draws thousands of tourists to the municipality.<ref name="Turismo" /> Among the city's natural attractions, the Manacapuru Ecological Reserve stands out, featuring typical floodplain vegetation with giant trees such as the [[Ceiba pentandra|kapok]]. It is a migratory and breeding area for birds and one of the best locations for sport fishing due to the variety of fish, including [[piranha]] and [[Osteoglossidae|bonytongue]]. The reserve includes a floating hotel and an observatory for birds, alligators, and other wildlife. It is located on the left bank of the [[Solimões River]].<ref name="Turismo 2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.manacapuru.am.gov.br/Manacapuru/Turismo.html|publisher=Prefeitura de Manacapuru|title=Turismo|date=February 3, 2011|access-date=February 3, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120729072616/http://www.manacapuru.am.gov.br/Manacapuru/Turismo.html|archive-date=July 29, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Sahu-Apé Indigenous Community is another natural attraction in the area, where indigenous spices can be found.<ref name="Turismo 2" /> Santo Afonso Island, located on the Solimões River, is also a popular natural attraction.<ref name="Turismo 2" />

The Port Pier, in the city center, is one of the main tourist attractions, serving as a historic and regional port.

The century-old Our Lady of Nazareth Church is the most iconic landmark of Manacapuru. A symbol of the city's religious and educational heritage, it was built through community collaboration and solidarity and is one of the ten parishes of the Coari Prelature.

The church underwent several renovations that improved and expanded its structure over time. Before the current temple was built, religious services were held in a modest chapel constructed in the early 19th century, located where the 16 de Julho Square now stands. Initially, the chapel was a community initiative, with locals responsible for its maintenance and religious services. The construction of the Our Lady of Nazareth Church began in 1904 and was completed in 1907. The Ventura family played a significant role in supporting the construction.

Investments in the project came from contributions by successful local merchants and through cultural fairs and festivals. Some of the construction materials were sourced from the state capital, while many bricks were purchased from the municipality of [[Coari]].

The materials used are of high quality, as evidenced by the enduring original structure. The tiles that adorned the chapel's altar for many years were imported from [[Portugal]], though they were removed during the last renovation in 2007 to improve the floor quality.

The community's spirit of collaboration was so strong that when construction materials arrived at the city's port by boat, residents—men, women, and children—cheerfully transported them to the construction site, eager to see the project completed.

The canoe-shaped altar in the church was used during a Mass celebrated by [[Pope John Paul II]] when he visited [[Manaus]] in 1980.

[[Redemptorists|Redemptorist]] priests were responsible for organizing the community that later became the Our Lady of Nazareth Parish.<ref name=":1" />

The Municipal Chamber, which housed the Executive Branch, the State Revenue Office, and the Court of Justice during the 1930s, became the permanent seat of the Manacapuru Municipal Chamber in 1976, and the building was named Antero de Rezende Palace.<ref name="Turismo 2" /> The Our Lady of Nazareth School, with construction beginning in 1946 and completed in 1951, features an architectural design inspired by European styles. It was built with funds from the mission, the Brazilian government, and contributions from the local community, which also provided labor. The Court of Justice Building, constructed in the early 1930s by Francisco do Couto Valle, was originally intended as a municipal jail and later served as a hotel. From 1991 to 1993, it housed the Municipal Health Secretariat before becoming the permanent seat of the Manacapuru Municipal Chamber's Court of Justice. Other notable buildings include the Municipal Prefecture, one of the city's oldest structures, and the [[Freemasonry]] lodge.<ref name="Turismo 2" />

=== Football ===
In football, the main club in Manacapuru is the [[Princesa do Solimões Esporte Clube]], founded on August 18, 1971.<ref name="Futebol">{{Cite web|url=http://www.arquivodeclubes.com/am/princesa.htm|publisher=Arquivo de Clubes|title=Princesa do Solimões Esporte Clube|date=February 3, 2011|access-date=February 3, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101126213303/http://www.arquivodeclubes.com/am/princesa.htm|archive-date=November 26, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> Its headquarters are located on Getúlio Vargas Avenue in the Historic Center of Manacapuru. Princesa do Solimões played its first official match in the inaugural Amazonas Football Championship in 1987, becoming the third club from the [[Amazonas inland]] to participate in the event, following Penarol from [[Itacoatiara, Amazonas|Itacoatiara]] and Olaria from [[Humaitá, Amazonas|Humaitá]].<ref name="Futebol" /> A significant talent developer, the club has produced several stars of northern football, such as Naílton, Zédvan, and Alcimar.<ref name="Futebol" /> Known as the "Tubarão do Solimões" (Solimões Shark) among Amazonas residents, the club's symbol is a shark.<ref name="Futebol" /> It won the [[Campeonato Amazonense|Amazonas Football Championship]] in 2013 but finished as runner-up six times (1995, 1997, 2014, 2015, 2016, and 2022). The club also won the Torneio Início in 1997 and 2007. It has participated, and occasionally still does, in regional and national competitions such as the [[Copa Verde]], the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série D]], the Second Division of the Brazilian Championship (1989), and the [[Copa do Brasil]].

Another Manacapuru club to reach professional football is [[Operário Esporte Clube|Operário]], founded on June 10, 1982. The club turned professional in 2010, winning the [[Campeonato Amazonense Second Division|Amazonas Championship Série B]] in its debut year and reaching Série A in less than two years.
