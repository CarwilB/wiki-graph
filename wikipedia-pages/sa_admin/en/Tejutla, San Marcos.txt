'''Tejutla''' ({{IPA|es|teˈxutla}}) is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the western highlands of [[Guatemala]], in the [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[San Marcos Department|San Marcos]]. Tejutla was officially founded on 25 July 1672, although it was already an important town within the Mam kingdom in 1524, at the time of the [[Spanish conquest of Guatemala]].<ref name="munihistory"/> It is situated high in the mountains, the so-called "cold zone", at about 2500 m altitude.

Tejutla is primarily a [[Ladino people|Ladino]] municipality in that the majority of its inhabitants speak Spanish and are not indigenous.  Tejutla has about 31,139 inhabitants,<ref name="munihistory"/> with about 6,000 living within the urban center (casco urbano).<ref>[http://www.prensalibre.com/pl/2004/julio/15/93014.html Prensa Libre] {{in lang|es}}</ref>  The municipio of Tejutla includes 67 communities, 20 of which are Aldeas and the rest are Caserios, Cantones and Colonias.  The rivers Xolobaj and Del Molino flow through the town of Tejutla.<ref>[http://www.visitguatemala.com/nuevo/destinos_municipio.asp?id=241 visitguatemala.com] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070720090707/http://www.visitguatemala.com/nuevo/destinos_municipio.asp?id=241 |date=2007-07-20 }}</ref>  The general climate in Tejutla during the winter, which runs from November through February, is about 10 degrees Celsius but temperatures can drop up to 5 degrees lower.  Temperatures during the months of May through November can range anywhere from 18 to 30 degrees Celsius. The economy in Tejutla lives on agriculture, mainly apples and potatoes, although carrots, onions, cauliflower, broccoli, cabbage, beans, lettuce, tomatoes, and peaches are also grown locally within the municipality.

==History==

===Spanish Colony===
[[File:Pedrocortesylarraz.jpg|170px|thumb|left|Bishop [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]] portrait; he arrived to Tejutla in 1770.]]

The name ''Tejutla'' is of uncertain origin, being variously attributed to the local [[Mam language]], derived from ''twui c'ukal'' meaning "upon the hill of white sand", or from the [[Nahuatl language]] of [[Tlaxcala]] in [[Mexico]], meaning "walled land" or "land of the dyers", or from the [[Chorti language]] in which it means "land of embers".<ref name="munihistory">{{cite web|title=Monografía e Historia de Tejutla |url=http://munitejutla.webnode.es/monografia-e-historia-de-tejutla-/ |publisher=Municipalidad de Tejutla |location=Guatemala |access-date=2011-08-31|language=es}}</ref>

The "Villa de Tejutla', town's official name, is an old settlement that already existed when the Spaniards arrived to the region in 1524; it was an important commercial town for the Mam people due to the weaving that was done there. However, its foundation as a colonial town was not official until 25 July 1627, when the encomenderos founded a Spanish town called ''Santiato Tenango'' next to the Indian town.  each town had its own authorities and their own business leaders.  Eventually, both towns merged and decided to call the resulting settlement "Santiago Tejutla" to remember the old names; Tenango was on the north side, and Tejutla on the South.

In 1690, Tejutla had a large area and included the modern municipalities of [[Comitancillo]], [[Ixchiguán]], [[Concepción Tutuapa]], [[Sipacapa]], [[Sibinal]], [[Tajumulco]], [[Tacaná]] and part of what is now [[San Miguel Ixtahuacán]]. According to the historical writings from ''Recordación Florida'' of [[Francisco Antonio de Fuentes y Guzmán]], Tejutla belonged to [[Quetzaltenango Department]] and it was a "prosperous land with rich weathers and comfortable forest with enough water".{{sfn|Fuentes y Guzmán|1932|p=}}

Tejutla was an important commercial and religious center; in August 1767, Joseph Domingo Hidalgo described Santiago Tejutla as "El Curato" -i.e., the focal center of commerce of all the towns that were around it− in the ''Gaceta de Guatemala'', the official newspaper of the times.  Then, in the last quarter of the 18th century, bishop Dr. [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]], who arrived from [[Cuilco]] in 1770 as part of the inspection he was doing of the Guatemalan dioceses, called Tejutla "Santiago en la Cima del Monte" (English: Santiago at the top of the hill" and reported that there were "sixty four families who lived very well" in the area.{{sfn|Cortés y Larraz|1770|p=}}

In 1754 the Mercedarians gave away their convent to the [[secular clergy]] per the Borbonic reforms that were instituted by the King of Spain, and Tejutla became a secular "curato".{{sfn|Pérez|1966|p=90}}

===After the independence from Spain===
{{See also|Rafael Carrera|Justo Rufino Barrios}}

The Central American United Provinces constitution from 11 October 1821, showed Tejutla under modern San Marcos jurisdiction for the very first time.

In 1870 Tejutla reached "Villa" category and, due to its development, its authorities requested to the [[Congress of Guatemala|House of Representatives of Guatemala]] to be named a Department capital. The department was going to have the municipalities mentioned above, along with the modern municipalities of [[Cuilco]], [[Santa Bárbara, Huehuetenango|Santa Bárbara]] and  San Gaspar, Huehuetenango, from the modern [[Huehuetenango Department]]. Besides, in those days, Motocintla, Cacahuatán and Tapachula—which would go definitively to [[Mexico]] in 1892 due to the [[Herrera-Mariscal treaty]]<ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5nFVAAAAMAAJ|title=Memoria sobre la cuestión de límites entre Guatemala y México|author=Comisión Guatemalteca de Límites con México|publisher=Tipografía Nacional|location=Guatemala|year=1900|pages=325|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal|year=1896|title=Nuestras fronteras|journal=[[La Ilustración Guatemalteca]]|url=https://archive.org/stream/ilustracionguate00guat#page/470/mode/2up|location=Guatemala|volume=I|issue=13|publisher=Síguere, Guirola y Cía|language=es}}</ref>— were under the jurisdiction of the [[Mercedarian]] convent located in Tejutla.  Furthermore, Tejutla even had House representatives of its own in those days.

But power shifted when the conservatives led by Field Marshal [[Vicente Cerna]] were defeated by the liberal forces of generals [[Miguel Garcia Granados]] and [[Justo Rufino Barrios]] −who was a [[San Lorenzo, San Marcos|San Lorenzo]] native;  once the liberals were in power, they expelled the regular clergy from Guatemala and abolished mandatory tithing for the secular clergy, thus leaving Tejutla without their main administrative and leadership support, the curato.  In fact, Barrios government confiscated monasteries, large extensions of farm land, sugar mills and Indian doctrines from the [[regular clergy|regular orders]] and then distributed it to his liberal friend and comrades, who became large landowners in the area.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Castellanos Cambranes|first=Julio|journal=500 años de lucha por la tierra. Estudios sobre propiedad rural y reforma agraria en Guatemala|title=5. Tendencias del desarrollo agrario en el siglo XIX y surgimiento de la propiedad capitalista de la tierra en Guatemala|volume=1|location=Guatemala|publisher=FLACSO|year=1992|url=http://www.flacsoandes.edu.ec/libros/digital/53099.pdf|language=es}}</ref>

===20th century===
{{See also|Guatemala Civil War}}

This remote area was hit by the [[Guatemalan Civil War|civil war]] in the early 1980s, when innocent citizens were terrorized by G-2 forces, the former [[secret police]] of Guatemala during the government of general [[Fernando Romeo Lucas García]].<ref>[http://www.tulane.edu/~libweb/RESTRICTED/CERIGUA/1991_0818.txt "Weekly briefs" at the website of Tulane University] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080905060938/http://www.tulane.edu/~libweb/RESTRICTED/CERIGUA/1991_0818.txt |date=2008-09-05 }}. [http://www.gam.org.gt/public/publi/pdf/MASACRESenGUATEMALA.pdf gam.org.gt] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070212072911/http://www.gam.org.gt/public/publi/pdf/MASACRESenGUATEMALA.pdf |date=2007-02-12 }}</ref>

===21st century===

In 2005, Tejutla suffered under the devastation of [[hurricane Stan]].

==Tourism==
{{See also|La Aurora International Airport|Tapachula International Airport}}

Although Tejutla is settled in a spectacular landscape, [[tourism]] is still very limited in this region. Every July, the city celebrates its town festival, dedicated to Santiago Apóstol, [[James, son of Zebedee|St. James]]. There is a cultural centre "casa de la cultura" in the city.
