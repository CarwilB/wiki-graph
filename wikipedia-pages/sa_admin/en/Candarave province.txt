'''Candarave''' is a [[Provinces of Peru|province]] of the [[department of Tacna]], [[Peru]]. Located in the department's northernmost border, it is the smallest of its provinces. Its capital is [[Candarave]].

== Etymology ==
Peruvian linguist [[Rodolfo Cerrón-Palomino]] speculates that the name comes from ''kanta-ra-wi'', an [[Aymara language|Aymara]] word meaning "place of many lodgings".<ref>{{Cite journal |title=La naturaleza probatoria del cambio lingüístico: a propósito de la interpretación toponímica andina |journal=[[Pontifical Catholic University of Peru|Voces del Ande: ensayos sobre onomástica andina]] |last=Cerrón Palomino |first=Rodolfo |url=https://repositorio.pucp.edu.pe/items/09fdc3f2-951d-4139-8413-e089c7a68654 |author-link=Rodolfo Cerrón-Palomino |year=2008 |doi=10.18800/9789972428562 |isbn=978-9972-42-856-2|doi-access=free |hdl=20.500.14657/174297 |hdl-access=free }}</ref>

==History==
The first inhabitants descended from the Protocollahuas, the [[Aymara people|Aymara]], and the [[Tiwanaku]] peoples. Candarave is a town that originated from Aymara-descended [[Ayllu]]s.

After [[Manco Inca]]'s uprising in 1536, Pedro Pizarro pacified the territories of [[Chucuito]], [[Moquegua]], [[Tacna]], and [[Tarapacá]]. In 1540, the encomienda system was established in favor of the conquistador Hernán Rodríguez de San Juan, who was required to pay tribute with basic products such as poultry, sheep, pigs, corn, wheat, eggs, and firewood. The ''[[Cacique|cacicazgos]]'', and the [[Archdiocese of Arequipa|Bishop of Arequipa]], {{ill|Manuel Abad Yllana|es|lt=Manuel Abad Illana}}, from Valladolid, established their boundaries in 1776.

Later, Candarave was established as a district during the [[Peruvian War of Independence|Independence period]]. By decree of June 25, 1855, it became part of the [[Tacna province|province of Tacna]]. By law of November 11, 1874, it became part of the [[Tarata province|province of Tarata]]. In 1875, Curibaya and Huanuara were separated to form the [[Curibaya District|district of Curibaya]]. During its [[Tarata Department (Chile)|Chilean administration]] (1880–1929), [[Candarave]] served as the provisional capital of the province of Tarata.

The province was created by Law No. 24887, of August 18, 1988, prior to which its territory had belonged to [[Tacna province]].

== Politics ==
The province is administered by a municipal government that also administers [[Candarave District]]. The [[Catholic Church in Peru]] administers the province as part of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Tacna and Moquegua]] since 1944.

== Demographics ==
The province is inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Aymara people|Aymara]] descent. [[Spanish language|Spanish]], however, is the language which the majority of the population (78.17%) learnt to speak in childhood, 20.94% of the residents started speaking using the [[Aymara language]] ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe]  {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=January 27, 2013 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007</ref>
