'''Tuzantla''' is a [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] in the eastern part of the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[Mexican state|state]] of [[Michoacán]]. The municipality has an area of 1,018.49 square kilometres (1.73% of the surface of the state)<ref>[http://www.e-local.gob.mx/wb2/ELOCAL/EMM_Michoacan Michoacán] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110517162648/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/wb2/ELOCAL/EMM_michoacan |date=2011-05-17 }} ''Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México''. Retrieved on October 6, 2007</ref> and is bordered to the north by the municipalities of [[Jungapeo]] and [[Ciudad Hidalgo, Michoacán|Hidalgo]], to the east by [[Juárez Municipality, Michoacán|Juárez]] and [[Susupuato]], to the south by [[Tiquicheo]] and the state of [[Guerrero]], and to the west by [[Tiquicheo]] and [[Tzitzio]]. The municipality had a population of 15,302 inhabitants according to the 2005 census.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |title=2005 Census |work=INEGI: Instituto Nacional de Estadística, Geografía e Informática |accessdate=2007-10-06 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130406130712/http://www.inegi.gob.mx/est/contenidos/espanol/sistemas/conteo2005/localidad/iter/ |archivedate=2013-04-06 }}</ref>  Its municipal seat is the city of the same name. 

In [[Pre-Columbian]] times, the area was inhabited by [[Nahuas]], but was conquered by the [[Purépecha Empire]]. Its name, ''Tuzantla'', is a word of [[Nahuatl]] origin that means "Place where gophers are abundant". Its [[Purépecha language|Purépecha]] name was either ''Cusaro''<ref>{{cite book |last1=Gerhard |first1=Peter |title=A guide to the historical geography of New Spain |date=1993 |publisher=Univ. of Oklahoma Pr |location=Norman, Okla. London |isbn=9780806125534 |page=172 |edition=Rev.}}</ref> or ''Ocumo'' (also spelled ''Hocumu'' or ''Ucumu''), the latter meaning "[[Otomi]]".<ref>{{cite book |last1=Pollard |first1=Helen Perlstein |title=Taríacuri's legacy: the prehispanic Tarascan state |date=1993 |publisher=University of Oklahoma Press |location=Norman |isbn=9780806124971 |page=104}}</ref>
