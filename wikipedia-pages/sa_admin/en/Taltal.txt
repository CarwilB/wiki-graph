'''Taltal''' is a [[Chile]]an [[Communes of Chile|commune]] and [[List of cities in Chile|city]] in [[Antofagasta Province]], [[Antofagasta Region]]. According to the 2012 census, the commune has a population of 11,132 and has an area of {{convert|20405.1|sqkm|0|abbr=on}}.<ref name="INE"/>
The commune is home to [[Paranal Observatory]] and includes the northern portion of [[Pan de Azúcar National Park]].

The commune of Taltal hosts multiple copper mines including [[Franke mine|Franke]], [[Altamira mine|Altamira]] and [[Las Luces]]; all operated and owned by [[Grupo Minero Las Cenizas]].<ref name=guiafranke>{{Cite news |title=KGHM vendió en US$25 millones su mina Franke a Minera Las Cenizas |url=https://www.guiaminera.cl/kghm-vendio-en-us25-millones-su-mina-franke-a-minera-las-cenizas/ |access-date=2025-05-18 |work=Guía Minera |language=es}}</ref><ref name=gmlcft>{{Cite web |title=Faena Taltal |url=https://www.cenizas.cl/faenas_oficinas/faena-taltal/ |access-date=2025-05-25 |website=Grupo Minero Las Cenizas |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Faena Franke |url=https://www.cenizas.cl/faenas_oficinas/faena-franke/ |access-date=2025-05-25 |website=Grupo Minero Las Cenizas |language=es}}</ref>

==Etymology ==
{{see also|Origin of the Mapuche}}
It has been claimed the etymological origin of the name is the [[Mapuche language|Mapuche]] word ''Thalthal'', which means “night bird.”<ref name="OHM1879">{{cite journal |editor-last=Vidal Gormaz |editor-first=Francisco |editor-link=Francisco Vidal Gormaz |title=Esploraciones Hidrográficas Practicadas, en las Costas de Chile por la Marina Militar de la Republic |journal=Anuario Hidrográfico de la Marina de Chile |volume=5 |publisher=Oficina Hidrográfica de Chile |location=Santiago |date=1879 |page=790 |url=http://repositorioarchivohistorico.armada.cl/handle/1/12445 |via=Repositorio Digital del Archivo y Biblioteca Histórica de la Armada}}</ref> This would make it one of the northernmost Mapuche toponyms in Chile.<ref>{{Cite journal |title=Indagaciones en torno al significado del oro en la cultura mapuche. ==History==
[[File:Atacama Desert Dispute between Bolivia and Chile (1825 - 1879).svg|250px|left|thumb|The [[Atacama border dispute]] between Bolivia and Chile (1825-1879)]]
The history of Taltal begins with the opening of the copper mine "El Cobre" by José Antonio Moreno in 1850 about 20 kilometers north of Taltal.  This brought miners and prospectors to the area. In 1855 Moreno expanded his operations to include Taltal.<ref name="Hist-1">{{Cite web|title=Taltal: Un puerto nacido del empuje |language=es |publisher=Municipalidad de Taltal |url=http://www.taltal.cl/contenido/44/historia |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130725152531/http://www.taltal.cl/contenido/44/historia |archive-date=25 July 2013|url-status=live}}</ref> The discovery of copper in 1858 on nearby Mount Cachiyuyal increased pressure on the port, which had to utilize customs officials from [[Copiapo]]. So Moreno requested official recognition of the port which was granted by decree<ref>Decreto Supremo Nº 91 signed by Chilean President [[Manuel Montt Torres]].</ref> on 12 July 1858.<ref name="Hist-1" />

However, the real boom began in 1876 with the opening of nitrate mines, in particular [[potassium nitrate]].<ref name="Hist-1" /> In 1879, the first nitrates were shipped from the area.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Caleta Oliva, Un Hito Histórico |language=es |date=23 June 2008 |publisher=Municipalidad de Taltal |url=http://www.taltal.cl/noticias/582/caleta-oliva-un-hito-historico |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150811002743/http://www.taltal.cl/noticias/582/caleta-oliva-un-hito-historico |archive-date=11 August 2015|url-status=live}}</ref> In 1882 a railway was completed from Cachinal de la Sierra and other nitrate mining sites. It was built by the "Taltal Railway Company", a British company, sold to private investors in 1954, and dismantled in 1970 due to the end of nitrate mining. At the peak of production in the 1930s, Taltal had a population of over 30,000 and was the third largest nitrate port in Chile.<ref name="Hist-1" />

On 18 June 1991, following an unusual rainfall event, a series of [[1991 Antofagasta mudflows|mudflows devastated part of the city]].<ref name=LaTercera2019>{{Cite news|title=A 28 años del aluvión de Antofagasta, estudio advierte riesgos en zona de La Chimba|url=https://www.latercera.com/que-pasa/noticia/aluvion-antofagasta-1991-la-chimba/704902/|last=Yáñez|first=Cecilia|date=June 18, 2019|access-date=June 18, 2021|work=[[La Tercera]]|language=Spanish}}</ref>

==Demographics==
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Taltal had 11,000 inhabitants (6,182 men and 4,918 women). Of these, 9,564 (86%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 1,536 (14%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 2.3% (11,148 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Taltal is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2008-2012 alcalde is Guillermo Hidalgo Ocampo.<ref name="Official"/>
 
Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Taltal is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Pedro Araya ([[Regionalist Party of the Independents|PRI]]) and Manuel Rojas  ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]]) as part of the 4th electoral district, together with [[Antofagasta]], [[Mejillones]] and [[Sierra Gorda]]. The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Jorge Soria ([[Independent (politician)|Ind.]], 2018–2026) and [[Luz Ebensperger]] ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]], 2018–2022) as part of the 2nd senatorial constituency (Antofagasta Region).

==Tourism==

* [[Llullaillaco National Park]] is an area of 268,670 ha protected by the National Forestry Corporation (CONAF). It lies in the Andes at an altitude of between 3,000 and 6,700 meters, and includes the [[Llullaillaco]] Volcano (6,739 meters), Cerro de la Pena Mountain (5260 m) and Cerro Aguas Calientes Mountain (5,060 m). The park is home to [[vicuña]]s and [[guanaco]]s, with the highest population density of these species in the region. The park is part of the protected area of the Montano Desert that lies in the [[Cordillera Domeyko]] the desert steppe and the Andean salt flats.
* Cifuncho bay, located 30&nbsp;km south of Taltal. With an extensive and beautiful beach of white sand and turquoise water, it is considered one of the five most beautiful beaches in Chile.<ref>[http://www.taltal.cl/nuestra-comuna/53/cifuncho CIFUNCHO  La Belleza Naturalmente Atractiva de Taltal] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131014202539/http://www.taltal.cl/nuestra-comuna/53/cifuncho |date=14 October 2013 }} www.taltal.cl retrieved 1 September 2013</ref>
* El Medano canyon, located 90 kilometres north of Taltal, is one of the most important sites to observe [[cave painting]]s in the northern coast of Chile. This paintings are the remains of an extinct culture called the [[Chango people]], who drew their experiences of hunting [[guanaco]]s, [[sea lion]]s and [[whale]]s. It is believed that the paintings are between 500 and 1,000 years old.
* Caleta [[Paposo]] is 54&nbsp;km north of Taltal. It was once a mining centre and was inhabited by the pre-Hispanic [[Chango people]]. Surrounded by pastures irrigated by moisture coming off the sea, the area has a very rich flora and fauna and was once a border between Chile and Bolivia in the nineteenth century.

==Notable sites ==
* [[Paranal Observatory]], located on [[Cerro Paranal]] in the [[Atacama Desert]], 110&nbsp;km north of the city and administered by the [[European Southern Observatory]]. Soon the [[communes of Chile|commune]] will also be home to the E-ELT ([[European Extremely Large Telescope]]) to be located on [[Cerro Armazones]], 20 kilometers east of Cerro Paranal. This site is not generally open to tourists; visitors are received by appointment only.<ref>https://www.eso.org/public/about-eso/travel/paranal/</ref> However, {{asof|lc=y|2025}},<ref>https://web.archive.org/web/20250811002250/https://www.eso.org/public/about-eso/visitors/paranal/</ref> weekly guided tours are given; registration in advance is required.<ref>https://www.eso.org/public/about-eso/visitors/paranal/</ref>
** The [[ESO Hotel]] at Paranal, featured in the 2008 [[James Bond|Bond]] film ''[[Quantum of Solace]]''. This facility is used by ESO scientists and engineers. It is not a commercial hotel, and the public cannot book rooms.<ref name=frank>[http://travelwithfrankgehry.blogspot.com/2008/12/hotel-on-mars.html Hotel on Mars]</ref>

==Architectural heritage==
The city of Taltal contains many houses that are registered in the Inventory of Cultural Heritage Properties in Chile. Highlights include the Alhambra Theatre (1921), the Augusto Capdeville Museum (1885), the railroad houses (1886), the Plaza Hotel (1898), houses in the downtown area, the Catholic church (which was completely devastated by a fire in January 2007 but is now rebuilt) and the Protestant church (1896).

=== Taltal wind farm ===
{{Main | Taltal wind farm}}

The company Enel Green Power has begun the construction of the new [[Taltal wind farm|Taltal Wind Farm]], which will be located in the commune of Taltal, [[Chile]], approximately 1,500 kilometers north of [[Santiago]], the [[Chile]]an capital.
The project will require a total investment of $190 million US dollars and will produce a total of 99 megawatts (MW). It will have 33 windmills, making it the biggest wind farm in Chile.
