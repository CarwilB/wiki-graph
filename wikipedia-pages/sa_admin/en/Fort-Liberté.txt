'''Fort-Liberté''' ({{IPA|fr|fɔʁ libɛʁte}}; {{langx|ht|Fòlibète}}) is a [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] and the administrative capital of the [[Nord-Est (department)|Nord-Est]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Haiti]]. It is one of the oldest cities in the country. Haiti's independence was proclaimed there on November 29, 1803.<ref name=tresure>{{Cite web|url=http://www.haitiantreasures.com/HT_fort-liberte.htm|title=Fort-Liberté: A captivating Site|access-date=2010-07-01|publisher=Haitian Treasures|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100527062805/http://www.haitiantreasures.com/HT_fort-liberte.htm|archive-date=2010-05-27}}</ref><ref name=archive>{{Cite web|url=https://archive.org/details/expositiondelv3518tous|title= Exposition de l'événement du Fort-Liberté, des causes qui l'ont produit, et analyse des pièces y relatives (1799)|year= 1799|access-date=2010-07-01|publisher=American Libraries: Archives}}</ref>

The area around Fort-Liberté was originally inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas]], and later by Spanish colonists, who founded the city of Bayaja in 1578, but abandoned it in 1605. The site was reoccupied by the [[French people|French]] in 1732 as Fort-Dauphin; it was [[Capture of Fort-Dauphin (1794)|captured]] by Spanish forces in 1794, restored to the French in 1801 and then [[Surrender of Cap Français|surrendered to the British]] on 8 September 1803, shortly before the declaration of independence. The city has undergone a succession of name changes: Bayaja (1578), Fort-Dauphin (1732), Fort St. Joseph (1804), Fort-Royal (1811) and finally Fort-Liberté (1820).<ref name=tresure/><ref name=Clammer>{{Cite book|last=Clammer|first=Paul |author2=Michael Grosberg |author3=Jens Porup|title= Dominican Republic and Haiti|pages=339, 330–333|access-date=2010-07-01|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Kjde3Fmwb7IC&q=Fort-Libert%C3%A9%2C+Haiti&pg=PA340|publisher= Lonely Planet|year=2008|isbn= 978-1-74104-292-4}}</ref><ref name=Pop>{{Cite web|url=http://population.mongabay.com/population/haiti/3725170/fort-liberte|title=Population of Fort Liberté, Haiti|access-date=2010-07-01|publisher=Mongabay.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110717220658/http://population.mongabay.com/population/haiti/3725170/fort-liberte|archive-date=2011-07-17}}</ref> The town is the [[see city]] of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Fort-Liberté]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://nwicatholic.com/index.php/2011-10-28-15-52-16/column?start=3" |title=Newsletter |website=nwicatholic.com}}</ref>

==Demographics==
As of 2015, the population of the commune of Fort-Liberté was estimated to be 34,434, of which 22,416 resided in the town itself. The number of households in the commune was 4,822, with an average of 4.5 individuals per household. The adult population (18+) made up 58% of the commune, with 42% under the age of 18.<ref name="ht2015est">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ihsi.ht/pdf/projection/Estimat_PopTotal_18ans_Menag2015.pdf|title=Population totale, population de 18 ans et plus ménages et densités estimés en 2015|date=March 2015|website=IHSI|language=fr|trans-title=Total Population, Population of 18 and over, Households and Densities Estimated in 2015|access-date=15 May 2017}}</ref> [[Haitian Creole]] is spoken in this area, with the more educated people also speaking French. The most common religious affiliations are [[Roman Catholic]] and [[Haitian Vodou]].<ref name=Biography/>

==Geography==
Fort-Liberté is part of the [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Nord-Est (department)|Nord-Est]], which borders the Dominican Republic. Nord-Est has an area of {{convert|1805|km2|mi2}} and a population of 283,800 (2002). The [[Fort-Liberté Arrondissement|arrondissement]] consists of the three communes of Fort-Liberté, [[Ferrier, Nord-Est|Ferrier]] and [[Perches, Nord-Est|Perches]]. In the colonial era, it was a major plantation area, and today it remains an important coffee-producing area. Its pine forests are heavily exploited for charcoal. In addition, several colonial-era forts, mostly in ruins, are situated here.<ref name=Clammer/>

Fort-Liberté is a natural harbour of the [[Saint-Domingue]]. It is strategically located in the centre of the bay facing the Atlantic Ocean. It was used as a naval base by the French, with four forts that "guarded the bay like beads on a string." Two of the larger forts are Fort Lachatre and Fort [[Labourque]]. They were captured by [[Toussaint Louverture]] (May 20, 1743 – April 7, 1803), the leader of the [[Haitian Revolution]], in 1793. He later proceeded to the north and conquered the Spanish.<ref name=Clammer/>

The fort is {{convert|40|nmi|km}} from [[Port-de-Paix]] (the capital of [[Nord-Ouest (department)|Nord-Ouest]]) and {{convert|290|km|mi}} from [[Port-au-Prince]] (the capital of Haiti). The average elevation of the town is about {{convert|1|m|ft}}.<ref name=Pop/><ref name=trade>{{Cite book|title= Trade promotion series, Issue 122|year=1931|page=222|access-date=2010-06-01|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=xVkjAQAAIAAJ&q=Climate+of+Fort+Liberte|publisher= Bureau of Foreign and Domestic Commerce}}</ref>

===Fort-Liberté Bay===
The coast line between Fort-Liberté Bay and Point Yaquezi is about {{convert|8|mi|km}}. It has a low sandy beach. It has  reefs with mangrove forests, and two hills (spaced at {{convert|0.5|mi|km}}) about {{convert|3.5|mi|km}} to the west of the entrance to the bay. The hills are the markers for the entrance to the port. Land locked, Fort-Liberté Bay is spread over  a length of{{convert|5 |mi|km}} in the east–west direction and has a breadth of about of {{convert|1|mi|km}}. The shallow waters that extends to {{convert|1|mi|km}} provides for adequate draft and safe anchorage conditions. The entrance to the fort is stated to be "about {{convert|1.25|mi|km}} long with not less than 15 fathoms depth of water in the fairway but is narrow and tortuous, so that a sailing vessel entering requires the wind to be well to the northward of east, and its leaving must have a commanding land breeze." The coast line from the entrance to the bay extends to {{convert|6.5|mi|km}} in an easterly direction extending to [[Manzanillo Bay]]. There is no wharf. The tides are high – spring rise is {{convert|5.75|ft|m}} and neap is {{convert|3.5|ft|m}}. Vessels anchor at the port in 12 fathoms deep water with manoeuvring space of {{convert|600|-|1200|yard|m}} on the east and northeast direction of the [[Bayon Islet]], which is in the midst of the bay. Another anchorage point with 9 fathoms depth is found to the east of the fort. The tidal current at the entrance is said to be low in the morning hours when it is the best time to enter the port.<ref name=port>{{Cite book|title= West Indies pilot, Volume 1|pages=367–369|access-date=2010-07-03|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wFsvAAAAYAAJ&q=Dimensions+of+Fort+Liberte&pg=PA368|author=United States. Hydrographic Office|publisher= G.P.O.|year=1918}}</ref>

===Fresh water resource===
Marion River empties into the bay about {{convert|1|mi|km}} to the west of the Fort-Liberté and is the source of water supply to the town.<ref name=port/>

==Climate==
The city has a pleasant climate with a cool ocean breeze and an average temperature of {{convert|86|F|C}}. [[Hispaniola]] island as a whole is subject to varying weather changes, which result in severe storms, such as hurricanes and sunshine.<ref name=Biography>{{Cite web|url=http://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/beard63/beard63.html|title=Toussaint L'Ouverture: A Biography and Autobiography: Electronic Edition|author=  John Relly,(1800&ndash;1876)|access-date=2010-07-01|publisher=Documenting the American South}}</ref>

==History==
Between 1503 and 1505, [[Nicolás de Ovando]], Spanish [[governor]] of [[Hispaniola]], founded the town of [[Puerto Real]] ("Royal Port"), which today lies around the town of Caracol, to the west of Fort-Liberté.<ref>{{cite book| last = de Saint-Méry|first = M.L.E. Moreau| title = Description topographique, physique, civile, politique et historique de la partie française de l'isle Saint-Domingue| date = 1797–1798|location = Philadelphia, Paris, Hambur |language = fr|url = https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k111191g}}</ref> However, soon this town was abandoned and the people moved to the east and in 1578 a new town was founded with the [[Taíno people|Taíno]] name of the region, '''Bayajá'''. Caracol was thought to be near the location where Santa Maria, Columbus's flagship struck a reef and sank on [[Christmas Day]] in 1492. The shipwreck was salvaged for its wood to build settlements known as [[La Navidad]], which was decimated by [[Taino Indians led by its chief Guanagarix]] after Columbus left the place. This was discovered by the American Archaeologist [[William Hodges]] while excavating at Puerto Real, a city founded at the same spot years later. Relics gathered from this site are displayed at museum [[Limbé Arrondissement|Limbe]]. However, no trace of the site is visible at the location.<ref name=Cameron/>

In 1606, the persons living in the old Spanish towns of Bayajá and Yaguana under the orders of the Spanish king, moved to the eastern part of the island, to a new town called [[Bayaguana]], combining the two old names.<ref name="moya">{{cite book| last =Moya Pons| first =Frank| title =Manual de Historia Dominicana| publisher =UCMM| year =1977| location =Santiago| pages =59| language =es}}</ref> This episode in Dominican history is now known as the [[devastations of Osorio]]. The Spaniards had founded the city of Bayaha, now known as Fort-Liberté, one of the several towns of Hispaniola. The location became the historic site of Fort-Liberté as it was built in 1731 under the orders of [[Louis XV]], King of France. Successive changes happened in the naming of the town reflecting the shift of power from Spanish to French colonization. The town was witness to the Haiti's first declaration of independence on November 29, 1803.<ref name= Coupeau>{{Cite book|last= Coupeau|first= Steeve|title= The history of Haiti|pages=16–27|access-date=2010-07-01|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=tA-XfYZFNvkC&q=Fort-Libert%C3%A9,+Haiti&pg=PA16|publisher=Greenwood Publishing Group|year= 2008|isbn= 978-0-313-34089-5}}</ref>

==Fort==
[[File:Fort Saint Joseph - Fort Liberte, Haiti (Narrow entryway).jpg|thumb|left|The fort at the edge of the city overlooking the bay]]
The fort, as such, within the city limits was constructed in 1731 at the port near the land end facing the bay, built under the directive of Louis XV, King of France, in order to defend against invasions. Fort-Liberté is on the southern shore of the bay. It is about {{convert|0.5|mi|km}} north from the city centre. The shoal in front of the fort is steep and extends to about {{convert|20|yard|m}}. Now, only the fort ruins are seen as evidence of the ingenious design of the architects who selected the most strategic point on the island to build it overlooking the turquoise blue ocean waters. However, efforts have been made during the middle of the 1990s to restore the fort and the structures within it. Pilferage has seen the loss of the cannons and the cannonballs, apart from removal of stones imported from [[Nantes]], France for pecuniary benefits without realising the gravity of the vandalism act. An issue of concern is the appearance of fissures in the fort walls, which are endangering the protection of forts from rains.<ref name=tresure/><ref name=port/>

The fort has a colonial cathedral, which is now the renovated entrance to the city. It is called the "Belle Entrée (Beautiful Gate). In the vicinity, other forts are the [[Labourque|Fort la Bouque]], the Batterie de l'Anse, the Fort Saint Charles and the Fort Saint Frédérique. Bayau Island is also another important place.<ref name=tresure/>

The Ministry of Tourism, the Ministry of Culture, the Haitian representatives and the Royal Caribbean officials have launched a project to encourage tourism to Fort-Liberté and its fort and [[Port-au-Prince]] by building facilities of hotels and other infrastructure.<ref name=tresure/>

==Economy==
The bay was the site of Caribbean's largest [[sisal]] plantation until [[nylon]] was invented.<ref name=Cameron>{{cite book|last=Cameron|first=Sarah|title= Footprint Caribbean Islands|page=[https://archive.org/details/caribbeanislands0000came_m5x4/page/409 409]|access-date=2010-07-01|url=https://archive.org/details/caribbeanislands0000came_m5x4|url-access=registration|quote=Fort-Liberté, Haiti.|publisher=Footprint Travel Guides|year= 2007|isbn=978-1-904777-97-7}}</ref> From the time of colonization, the economy of the island has been essentially agriculture centric. Plantation tillage has been the main occupation, inclucing sugar-cane, coffee, sisal, cocoa, and cotton. In 1789, the French part of the island had 793 sugar plantations, 3,117 coffee plantations, 789 cotton plantations, and 182 establishments for making rum, plus other minor factories and workshops. In 1791, investments were largely oriented towards these cultivations.<ref name=Biography/> Trade and economy of the  city and its precincts, at present are – [[coffee]], [[cacao bean|cacao]], [[honey]], [[logwood]], [[pineapple]], and sisal, which are the principal products.<ref name=trade/>

== Communal Sections ==
The commune consists of four [[communal section]]s, namely:

* [[Bayaha (communal section)|Bayaha]], rural
* [[Loiseau (communal section)|Loiseau]], urban and rural, containing the town of [[Dérac]]
* [[Haut Madeleine]], urban and rural, containing the [[Quartier de l'Acul Samedi]]
