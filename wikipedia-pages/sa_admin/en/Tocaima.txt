'''Tocaima''' ({{IPA|es|toˈkajma}}) refers to both a city and a municipality in [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]], [[Colombia]].

==City==
The city of Tocaima was founded on March 20, 1544 as '''San Dionisio de los Caballeros de Tocaima''' by the Spanish explorer [[Hernán Venegas Carrillo]]. This city is most well known for being a [[Tourism|vacation]] site during [[religious holiday]]s, especially for college students from [[Bogotá]] and other surrounding areas.{{Citation needed|date=October 2010}}

The town is crossed by the [[Pati River]], which sometimes floods the town.

===History===
Before Spanish colonization, the area was home to the [[Guacana]], an [[Amerindian]] tribe belonging to the [[Panche people|Panche]] Amerindian Nation. Tocaima was named in honor of a legendary warrior from this tribe, during the ruling period of the [[Cacique|Cacica]] Guacana.

It is believed{{By whom|date=November 2010}} that Tocaima is the only city in the [[Cundinamarca Department]] that presently has a royal title and [[coat of arms]] issued by the [[Spanish Monarchy]]. [[Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor|Charles V]] issued the royal title and coat of arms on February 7, 1549, in appreciation of the city's loyalty and fame for being a powerful and wealthy region.

In 1581, the city was completely destroyed by a devastating flood of the Pati River. President [[Juan Buenaventura de Borja y Armendia|Juan de Borja]] sent Captain [[Martin de Ocampo]] to refound the city, which he did on March 18, 1621 by constructing the [[Convent]] of San Jacinto and its contiguous chapel.

During the decolonization of Colombia from Spain in 1810, Tocaima was represented in the [[electoral college|electoral]] and [[constituent assembly|constitutional college]] by jurist [[Miguel de Tobar y Zerrato]] and Don [[Juan Salvador Rodriguez de Lago]]. The [[Cabildo (council)|Cabildo]], or colonial administrative council, was re-established that same year.

The new [[Constitution of Cundinamarca]], created in 1815, divided the nation into [[Canton (administrative division)|cantons]], which provoked a confrontation between the Tocaima Canton and the neighboring Canton of [[La Mesa, Cundinamarca|La Mesa]]. In 1816, Spain re-conquered the colonies and subsequently repressed the newly created government.

After defeating the Spanish in 1819, the colonists declared total independence from Spain and created the Republic of [[Gran Colombia]], which—led by General [[Francisco de Paula Santander|Santander]] in 1822—re-established the Tocaima Canton.
