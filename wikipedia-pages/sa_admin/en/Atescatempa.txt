'''Atescatempa''' is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Jutiapa Department|Jutiapa]] in [[Guatemala]]. It covers an area of approximately {{cvt|80.23|km2}}. As per 2023 estimates, it has a population of about 20,300 inhabitants. The municipality lies close to the border with [[El Salvador]].

==History==
Atescatempa is described as derived from [[Nawat language|Pipil language]] meaning "place on the shore of a pond". As per local legend, it was a flourishing town ruled by a chief named Atezcatl, and was engaged in conflict with the neighboring groups. The original settlement was damaged by volcanic activity from the Chingo volcano, which forced the population to move to the current location. There are various local legends associated with the destruction of the town, and associated elements.<ref name="Hist"/>

In the 19th century, Atescatempa was part of various administrative units. It was incorporated into the [[Jutiapa department|department of Jutiapa]] on 8 May 1852, when the department was officially created during Guatemala’s territorial reorganization. It was formally recognized as a municipality in 1863.<ref name="Hist">{{cite web|title=Historia del municipio de Atescatempa|work=Deguate|trans-title=History of Atescatempa municipality|url=https://departamentos.deguate.com/jutiapa/historia-del-municipio-de-atescatempa-jutiapa/|access-date=23 May 2026|language=es}}</ref>

==Geography==
Atescatempa is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Jutiapa Department|Jutiapa]] in [[Guatemala]]. It is spread over an area of {{cvt|80.23|km2}}.<ref name="CP">{{cite web|title=Atescatempa (Municipality, Guatemala)|work=Citypopulation.de|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/admin/jutiapa/2207__atescatempa/|access-date=23 May 2026}}</ref> It is situated about {{cvt|174|km}} from [[Guatemala City]]. It borders [[Asunción Mita]] to the north, [[Jerez, Jutiapa|Jerez]] to the west, and [[Yupiltepeque]] to the east. It shares land border with [[El Salvador]] to the south.<ref name="Geo">{{cite web|title=Geografía del municipio de Atescatempa, Jutiapa|work=Deguate|url=https://departamentos.deguate.com/jutiapa/geografia-del-municipio-de-atescatempa-jutiapa/|trans-title=Geography of Atescatempa municipality|access-date=23 May 2026|language=es}}</ref>

It is located at an elevation of {{cvt|700.77|m}} above sea level. The district has a tropical monsoon climate ([[Koppen Classification]]: Am). The average annual temperature is {{cvt|26.15|C}}. The district receives an average annual rainfall of {{cvt|8.15|cm}} and has 128.61 rainy days in a year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://weatherandclimate.com/guatemala/jutiapa/atescatempa|title=Atescatempa|work=Weather and Climate|access-date=1 April 2025}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Atescatempa had an estimated population of 20,300 inhabitants in 2023. The population consisted of 9,878 males and 10,422 females. About 24.3% of the population was below the age of fourteen, and 8.9% was over the age of 65 years. About 47.8% of the population was classified as rural, and the rest (52.2%) lived in urban areas. Most of the residents (88.9%) were born in the same municipality. The city had a literacy rate of 84.3%. [[Ladino people|Ladinos]] (96.0%) formed the major ethnic group, with [[Xinca people|Xinca]] (2.9%) forming a minor minority. [[Spanish language|Spanish]] (99.2%) was the most spoken language.<ref name="CP"/>
