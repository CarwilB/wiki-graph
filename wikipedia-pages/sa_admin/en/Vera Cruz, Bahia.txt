'''Vera Cruz (Bahia)''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the [[states of Brazil|state]] of [[Bahia]] in the [[Nordeste|North-East region]] of [[Brazil]]. It occupies 87% of the island of [[Itaparica Island|Itaparica]]; the remainder belongs to the municipality of [[Itaparica, Bahia|Itaparica]]. The municipality of Vera Cruz has a population of is 43,716 (2020 estimate) and covers an area of {{convert|297.537|km2|mi2}}.

The municipality is located {{convert|5.5|km|mi}} opposite of Salvador across the [[Bay of All Saints]].<ref name="i"/><ref name="PNUD_IDH_2000"/>

==History==

The island of Itaparica was home to a large population of [[Tupinambá people|Tupinambá peoples]] prior to the arrival of the Portugues. [[Caramuru|Diogo Álvaro Corrêa]] (c. 1475-1557), a Portuguese navigator also known as Caramuru, arrived on Itaparica in 1510. He met [[Catarina Paraguaçu]] (died 1586), daughter of the Tupinambá chief Taparica, and married her. Catarina travelled with Caramuru to France in 1526, was baptized in 1528, and is noted for her [[Mary (mother of Jesus)|Marian]] visions.<ref name="i"/>

Portuguese settlements in Vera Cruz were established soon after the arrival of [[Tomé de Sousa]], Brazil's first governor-general, in 1549. The [[Jesuits]] founded a small settlement in present-day Baiacu in 1560. It was called the Vila do Senhor da Vera Cruz, and featured Brazil's first hydraulic works, a dam to provide drinking water. The ruins of the Jesuit church remain on the site. Sugarcane, wheat, and cattle were introduced to the area in the same period. The wealth of the island led to attacks by English corsairs by 1597. It was invaded by the Dutch between the years 1600 and 1647.<ref name="i"/>

The Island of Itaparica was administered as part of Salvador until 1833. It was elevated to city status in 1962, and subsequently divided into two municipalities: Vera Cruz and Itaparica.<ref name="i"/>

==Historic structures==

Vera Cruz his home to numerous colonial-era structures, many dating to the 16th century. All lack federal, state, or municipal protection, with the exception of the Chapel of Saint Antony of Velásquez.
