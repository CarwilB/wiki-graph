'''Nahuatzen''' is a [[Municipalities of Michoacán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Michoacán]]. It is located approximately {{convert|75|km}} west of the state capital of [[Morelia]].

==Geography==
The municipality of Nahuatzen is located in the [[Tarascan Plateau]] west of [[Lake Pátzcuaro]] at an elevation between {{convert|2300|and(-)|3300|m}}. It borders the municipalities of [[Zacapu]] to the north, [[Erongarícuaro]] to the east, [[Tingambato]] to the south, [[Uruapan]] to the southwest, [[Paracho]] to the west, and [[Cherán]] to the northwest.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/16/16056.pdf |title=Nahuatzen, Michoacán de Ocampo |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos |language=es |publisher=INEGI |date=2009 |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|305.057|km2}}<ref name=infocard/> and comprises 0.52% of the state's area.<ref name=compendium/>

As of 2009, the land cover in Nahuatzen consists of temperate forest (47%) and grassland (6%). Another 44% of the land is used for agriculture and 2% consists of urban areas.<ref name=compendium/> The western half of Nahuatzen is in the [[Balsas River]] basin, the northeastern portion drains into the [[Lerma River]], and the southeastern portion drains into Lake Pátzcuaro.<ref name=compendium/>

Nahuatzen has a temperate climate with rain in the summer.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|12|and(-)|16|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|1000|and(-)|1500|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
[[File:Nahuatzen 03.jpg|thumb| Participants in a festival in Nahuatzen.]]
In the [[Purépecha language]], Nahuatzen means "place where it freezes". According to oral tradition, it was founded in the mid-16th century by people from the settlement of Xaracatan located about {{convert|3.5|km}} to the southeast. It is depicted in the ''[[Lienzo]] de Nahuatzen'', a cloth painting dating to the 17th or 18th century.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://mediateca.inah.gob.mx/islandora_74/islandora/object/codice%3A629 |title=Lienzo de Nahuatzen |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia|INAH]] |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last=Roskamp |first=Hans |year=2004 |title=El Lienzo de Nahuatzen: origen y territorio de una comunidad de la Sierra Tarasca, Michoacán |journal=Relaciones |language=es |url=https://colmich.repositorioinstitucional.mx/jspui/bitstream/1016/529/1/RoskampHans2004.pdf |volume=25 |pages=279–311 |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref> Nahuatzen was one of the 61 municipalities created in Michoacán in 1831.<ref name=encyclopedia/><ref>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Michoacán de Ocampo. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |pages=160–161 |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |isbn=970-13-1501-4}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Nahuatzen recorded a population of 27,174 inhabitants living in 6193 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=16&mun=056 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Nahuatzen |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref> The 2015 Intercensal Survey estimated a population of 28,074 inhabitants in Nahuatzen.<ref name=survey/>

There are 10 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> of which five are classified as urban:
* the municipal seat Nahuatzen, which recorded a population of 10,283 inhabitants in the 2010 Census;
* Comachuén, which recorded a population of 4762 inhabitants in 2010;
* Turícuaro, which recorded a population of 3388 inhabitants in 2010;
* Sevina, which recorded a population of 3344 inhabitants in 2010; and
* Arantepacua, which recorded a population of 2707 inhabitants in 2010.<ref name=catalog/>

In the 2015 Intercensal Survey, 92.86% of people in the municipality identified themselves as [[Indigenous peoples of Mexico|indigenous]].<ref name=survey/> The 2010 Census recorded 9789 [[Purépecha language|Purépecha]] speakers constituting 36% of the population in Nahuatzen.<ref name=snim/>

The lack of economic opportunities in Nahuatzen has caused many of people to find work elsewhere in Mexico and the United States. [[Sarasota, Florida]] is home to a community of people from Nahuatzen which numbers in the hundreds,<ref>{{cite news |last=Wallace |first=Hannah |date=30 September 2007 |url=https://www.sarasotamagazine.com/news-and-profiles/2007/09/on-the-road-2?day=30&month=9&year=2007 |title=On the Road |magazine=Sarasota Magazine |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref> [[Upstate New York]] is home to vegetable farm workers from Comachuén,<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Stevenson|first=Mark|title=Indigenous town survives on remittances from US|url=https://indiancountrytoday.com/news/indigenous-town-survives-on-remittances-from-us|access-date=2022-02-19|website=Indian Country Today, Associated Press|language=en}}</ref> and [[Tijuana]] has a community of Purépecha who emigrated from Arantepacua beginning in the 1980s.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=Álvarez Luis |first=Jonathan Uriel |date=2020 |title=Asentamiento y liderazgo de mujeres purépechas en Tijuana Baja California: Expresiones étnicas, de poder y agencia |type=MEC |page=34 |publisher=[[Colef|El Colegio de la Frontera Norte]] |url=https://www.colef.mx/posgrado/tesis/20181449/ |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref>

==Economy==
The main economic activities in Nahuatzen are [[Agriculture in Mexico|agriculture]], corn being the main crop, and forestry.<ref name=plan>{{cite news |author=Nahuatzen Municipal Council |url=http://congresomich.gob.mx/file/3a-7420-1.pdf |title=Plan de Desarrollo Municipal |language=es |pages=30, 32 |newspaper=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Michoacán, 3a. Secc. |publisher=Government of Michoacán |date=11 August 2020 |access-date=27 December 2020}}</ref>

[[Remittances from the United States|Remittances]] from the United States have enabled families in Comachuén to maintain [[artisan]] traditions of embroidered textiles, woodworking and construction.<ref name=":0" />
