'''Lota''' is a [[List of cities in Chile|city]] and [[Communes of Chile|commune]] located in the center of [[Chile]] on the [[:es:Golfo de Arauco|Gulf of Arauco]] (in Spanish), in the southern [[Concepción Province, Chile|Concepción Province]] of the [[Biobío Region]], {{convert|39|km}} south of Concepción, and is one of the ten cities (communes) that constitutes the [[Greater Concepción|Concepción metropolitan area]]. The city is mostly known for being the traditional centre of [[coal mining in Chile]], although mining ended in the 1990s.

==History==
{{See also|Coal mining in Chile|History of mining in Chile}}
The first Spanish settlement at this site, ''Santa Maria de Guadalupe,'' was founded by the governor [[Ángel de Peredo]] on October 12, 1662, but it did not survive long amidst the hostilities of the [[Arauco War]].{{citation needed|date=February 2017}} The modern city is linked to the [[coal mining]] industry that started in the nineteenth century. The first coal seams to be exploited were easy to work because they lay almost at ground level. Coal mining started after the arrival of [[steamship]]s at the port of [[Talcahuano]]. These steam ships, mostly from Britain, initially bought the coal very cheaply.<ref name=VivaBrito/> Industrialist [[Matías Cousiño]] begun mining operations in Lota in 1852.<ref name=Davis1990>{{cite conference |title=Breves recuerdos de algunas actividades mineras del carbón |last1=Davis |first1=Eliodoro Martín|date=1990 |publisher=Departamento de Geociencias, Facultad de Ciencias, [[Universidad de Concepción]] |book-title=Actas |pages=189–203 |location=Santiago, Chile |conference=Segundo Simposio sobre el Terciario de Chile |language=es}}</ref> Coal mining transformed Lota from being a sparsely populated [[frontier|frontier zone]] in the mid-19th century into a large industrial hub that attracted immigrants from all over Chile well into the 20th century.<ref name=VivaBrito>{{cite journal |last1=Vivallos Espinoza |first1=Carlos |last2=Brito Peña |first2=Alejandra |date=2010 |title=Inmigración y sectores populares en las minas de carbón de Lota y Coronel (Chile 1850-1900)|trans-title=Immigration and popular sectors in the coal mines of Lota and Coronel (Chile 1850-1900) |url=http://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-04622010000100005&script=sci_arttext |journal=[[Atenea]] |volume=501 |pages=73–94 |language=es }}</ref>

Lota was established officially as a town on January 5, 1875, and became a city on November 30, 1881.{{citation needed|date=February 2017}}  The name ''Lota'' is thought to be derived from the [[Mapudungun]] word ''Louta'' meaning ''small piece of land''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://etimologias.dechile.net/?Lota |title=Etimología de LOTA |website=Etimologías de Chile|access-date=January 27, 2017|quote=Uno pudiera pensar que la ciudad de Lota, en Chile, lleva ese nombre puesto que de ahí se extraía la mayor porción de carbón, pero no es así. Es todo lo contrario. La ciudad toma ese nombre del mapudungun ''louta'' (pequeña porción de tierra).|language=es}}</ref>

In 1960 miners and their families started a [[general strike]] demanding higher salaries. As protesters marched on Concepción the [[1960 Concepción earthquake]] struck the territory ending the strike.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/> During much of the 20th century the city was a stronghold of [[Communist Party of Chile|pro-Soviet communism]]. The city's coal mines were [[nationalized]] in 1971 by [[Presidency of Salvador Allende|Salvador Allende]], a move that was welcomed by miners. When Allende was overthrown and a [[Military dictatorship of Chile (1973–90)|military dictatorship]] established, Lota's political parties and powerful [[syndicate|trade union]]s were forbidden. People active in these organizations were persecuted, and in some instances killed by the military. Much of the local press was suppressed as it had links to either the trade unions or political parties.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/>

In the late 20th century Lota's coal industry began to struggle for markets as [[Metallurgical furnace|furnace]]s, the shipping industry and trains, all of which were important buyers of coal, shifted to other energy sources. There had been concerns as early as the 1920s about the vulnerability of the coal-dependent local economy.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/> One of the problems of Lota's coal industry was difficulty in [[mechanization|mechanizing]] as the coal beds were thin in nature and displaced by many [[fault (geology)|geological faults]].<ref>[http://www.mch.cl/reportajes/carbon-mantiene-su-sitial-en-chile/ Carbón mantiene su sitial en Chile]</ref> Costs also increased as [[Beam (structure)|wooden mining support]]s became more expensive, easily accessible coal beds were depleted and mining had to be done below the sea bed.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/> The mines were closed in the 1990s after demand for Lota's coal resources diminished and cheaper [[Coal mining#Colombia|Colombian coal]] arrived on the market, plunging the residents of Lota into [[poverty]].<ref name=Reyesetal2014>{{cite journal |last1=Reyes Herrera |first1=Sonia E. |last2=Rodríguez Torrent|first2=Juan Carlos|last3=Medina Hernández |first3=Patricio |date=2014 |title=El sufrimiento colectivo de una ciudad minera en declinación. El caso de Lota, Chile |url=http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0104-71832014000200010 |journal=Horizontes Antropológicos |volume=20 |issue=42 |language=es }}</ref> The end came in 1997 when [[Empresa Nacional del Carbón]] closed the mines and sold the industrial equipment. Subsequently, the mines flooded.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/>

[[Tourism in Chile|Tourism]], [[forestry in Chile|forestry]], [[fishing in Chile|artisan fishing]] and small-scale entrepreneurship have replaced mining as sources of employment, but older miners have found it difficult to adapt.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/> Despite the decline of the coal industry, the Lota community continues to identify with it.<ref name=VivaBrito/> Compared to the 1960s and 1970s the city is very much depoliticized; evidence of this is the fact some evangelical churches have more attendants than the offices of political parties.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/>

==Demographics==
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Lota spans an area of {{convert|135.8|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 49,089 inhabitants (23,944 men and 25,145 women). Of these, 48,975 (99.8%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 114 (0.2%) in [[rural area]]s. The population fell by 2.3% (1167 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/> In the last years of the 2000s the population fell below 40,000 meaning that the population had halved since the 1980s when it was about 80,000.<ref name=Reyesetal2014/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Lota is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2008-2012 alcalde was Patricio Marchant Ulloa ([[Christian Democratic Party (Chile)|PDC]]).<ref name="Munitel"/><ref name="Official"/>

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Lota has been represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Manuel Monsalve ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]]) and Iván Norambuena ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]]) as part of the 46th electoral district, (comprising Lota, [[Lebu, Chile|Lebu]], [[Arauco, Chile|Arauco]], [[Curanilahue]], [[Los Álamos]], [[Cañete, Chile|Cañete]], [[Contulmo]] and [[Tirúa]]). The commune has been represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Victor Pérez Varela (UDI) and Mariano Ruiz -Esquide Jara ([[Christian Democratic Party|PDC]]) as part of the 13th senatorial constituency (Biobío-Coast).
