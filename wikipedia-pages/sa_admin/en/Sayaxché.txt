'''Sayaxché''' ({{IPA|es|saʝaʃˈtʃe}}) is a [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[El Petén]] [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Guatemala]], on the [[Pasión River|Río La Pasión]] River. It covers an area of {{convert|3,904|sqkm|sqmi|0|disp=or}}, and had 55,578 inhabitants at the 2002 Census; the latest official estimate (as at mid-2012) was 114,781 inhabitants.

The city was founded in 1874 to provide accommodation for forest workers of Jamet Sastré logging company. It obtained municipal status in 1929.

[[El Rosario National Park]] is located just east of the town. The archaeological sites of [[Seibal|Ceibal]] and [[Dos Pilas]] are located within the municipal boundaries.

== African oil palm ==
{{See also|African Oil Palm}}
[[File:Guatemalapalma2014.JPG|200px|thumb|African oil palm plantation areas in Guatemala as of 2014.{{sfn|Solano|2015|p=6}}]]
There is a large demand within Guatemala and some of its neighbors for edible oils and fats, which would explain how the African oil palm became so prevalent in the country in detriment of other oils, and which has allowed new companies associated to large capitalists into a new investment phase that can be found particularly over some territories within the Northern Transversal Strip of Guatemala.<ref name=enfoque/> The investors are trying to turn Guatemala into one of the main palm oil exporters, in spite of the decline on its international price.  The most active region is found in [[Chisec]] and [[Cobán]], in [[Alta Verapaz Department]]; [[Ixcán]] in [[Quiché Department]], and Sayaxché, [[Petén Department]], where Palmas del Ixcán, S.A. (PALIX) is located, both with its own plantation and those of subcontractors. Another active region is that of [[Fray Bartolomé de las Casas]] and [[Chahal, Guatemala|Chahal]] in Alta Verapaz Department; El Estor and [[Livingston, Guatemala|Livingston]], [[Izabal Department]]; and [[San Luis, Petén|San Luis]], [[Petén Department|Petén]], where Naturaceites operates.<ref name=enfoque>{{harvnb|Solano|2015|p=1}}</ref>

== Ecological disaster ==
On June 6, 2015, residents around [[Pasión River|Río La Pasión]] River reported the finding of a high count of dead fish floating along the river.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cmiguate.org/palma-africana-la-farsa-de-la-responsabilidad-social-empresarial/|date=11 June 2011|title=Palma Africana: La Farsa de la Responsabilidad Social Empresarial|work=Cmiguate.org|access-date=18 June 2015|language=es}}</ref>

On June 11, 2015, Guatemala's authorities inspected [[palm oil]] extracting company "Reforestadora de Palmas de Petén, S.A." (REPSA), located in Sayaxché, and found traces [[Malathion]] in the company's tributaries leading to Río La Pasión river.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.s21.com.gt/nacionales/2015/06/11/peces-rio-pasion-fueron-envenenados|title=Hallan plaguicida en aguas del río La Pasión|date=11 June 2015|website=www.s21.com.gt|publisher=Siglo21.com.gt|access-date=2015-06-18|archive-date=2015-09-24|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924113848/http://www.s21.com.gt/nacionales/2015/06/11/peces-rio-pasion-fueron-envenenados|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Tranquilino Xojalaj, administrator of REPSA declared that heavy rain caused the treatment wells to flood into the river,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prensalibre.com/guatemala/mortandad-de-peces-abarca-105-kilometros-1|title=Mortandad de peces abarca 105 kilómetros|last1=Escobar |first1=R. |last2=Alvarado |first2=H.|last3=Alvarez |first3=C. |date=11 June 2015|publisher=Prensalibre.com|access-date=2015-06-18}}</ref> however the company is denying any involvement in the disaster,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.s21.com.gt/nacionales/2015/06/11/repsa-niega-responsabilidad-contaminacion|title=Repsa niega responsabilidad en contaminación|date=11 June 2015|publisher=Siglo21.com.gt|access-date=18 June 2015|language=es}}</ref> stating that they do not use [[Malathion]] as pesticide.

As of June 15, 2015, contamination has extended to over 105 km downstream and is affecting over 5,600 families (around 30,000 residents) from 16 communities by the riverside. These communities are dependable on the river's water supply and also for their fishing activities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prensalibre.com/guatemala/mortandad-de-peces-abarca-105-kilometros-1|title=Mortandad de peces abarca 105 kilómetros|last1=Escobar |first1=R. |last2=Alvarado |first2=H.|last3=Alvarez |first3=C. |date=11 June 2015 |publisher=Prensalibre.com|access-date=18 June 2015|language=es}}</ref>

The pollution is threatening to extend to the [[Usumacinta River]], which travels all the way to Mexico, and may continue its way towards rivers in Tabasco.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://diariodelatarde.com.mx/2015/05/06/empresa-guatemalteca-contamina-al-rio-usumacinta-en-mexico-2/|title=Empresa guatemalteca contamina al río Usumacinta en México|last1=Paredes |first1=Fredy|date=6 May 2015|publisher=Diariodelatarde.com.mx|access-date=18 June 2015|language=es}}</ref>
