'''João Pessoa''' ({{IPAc-en|'|ʒ|w|aʊ|n|_|p|ə|ˈ|s|oʊ|ə}} {{respell|ZHWOWN|_|pə|SOH|ə}}; {{IPA|pt|ʒuˈɐ̃w peˈsoɐ}}) is a port city in northeastern [[Brazil]]. It is the largest city and capital of the state of [[Paraíba]], with an estimated population of 888,679 (as of 2024). It is located on the right bank of the [[Paraíba do Norte river]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Joao Pessoa: Brazil |url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Joao-Pessoa-Brazil |publisher=[[Encyclopaedia Britannica]] |access-date=22 April 2020}}</ref>

The new "Estação Ciência, Cultura e Artes" (Science, Culture and Art Station),<ref name="ESTAÇÃO CIÊNCIA, CULTURA e ARTES">{{cite book|url=http://www.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/projetos/estacaocienciaculturaartes/|title=ESTAÇÃO CIÊNCIA, CULTURA e ARTES|publisher=E.C.C.A.|location=joão pessoa, Brazil|format=PDF|isbn=85-240-3919-1|access-date=January 10, 2008|year=2006|language=pt|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071224214719/http://www.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/projetos/estacaocienciaculturaartes/|archive-date=December 24, 2007}}</ref> located at the most eastern point of the Americas (''[[Ponta do Seixas]]''), is an educational and cultural institution as well as a national landmark. The complex, inaugurated in 2008, was created by Brazilian architect [[Oscar Niemeyer]] and is one of his final projects.

The capital of Paraíba received the title of [[City of Crafts and Folk Arts|Creative City]] by [[UNESCO]] in 2017,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/titulo-de-cidade-criativa-da-unesco-coloca-joao-pessoa-na-rota-turistica-mundial-atraves-da-arte-popular/|title=Unesco Creative City title puts João Pessoa on the world tourist route through folk art|access-date=August 29, 2018|date=November 3, 2017|publisher=City Hall of João Pessoa|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190112052115/http://www.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/titulo-de-cidade-criativa-da-unesco-coloca-joao-pessoa-na-rota-turistica-mundial-atraves-da-arte-popular/|archive-date=January 12, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> appointing João Pessoa as "Brazilian city of [[handicrafts]]".

==History==
=== Background, foundation, and early years ===
In 1534, King [[John III of Portugal]] divided the colony into hereditary captaincies, with Paraíba being subordinated to the [[Captaincy of Itamaracá]], extending from the [[Guaju River]] to the [[Goiana River]]. The Captaincy of Paraíba was created only in 1574, after the attack on Tracunhaém, a sugar mill in the [[Captaincy of Itamaracá]]. However, the new captaincy would only be effectively occupied eleven years later, after five expeditions attempted to conquer it, the first four of which ended in failure.<ref name="Raposo, Araújo, p. 157"/>

To repel invaders, on May 1, 1584, the Fort of São Filipe was built on the left bank of the [[Paraíba do Norte River|Paraíba River]], on land that today belongs to the district of Forte Velho, in [[Santa Rita, Paraíba|Santa Rita]], an area inhabited by the [[Potiguara]] Indigenous people. The fort, however, suffered constant attacks from French corsairs and the Potiguaras and was abandoned less than a year later, being set on fire in June 1585.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 139.</ref> Earlier that same year, in February, the [[Tabajara]] Indigenous people, led by Piragibe, arrived in Paraíba and settled on the left bank of the river. Initially allies, the Tabajaras soon became rivals of the Potiguaras and entered into conflict both with them and with the Portuguese colonizers.<ref name="Guimarães, 2000, p. 4">[[#Bibliography|GUIMARÃES]], 2000, p. 4.</ref>

A peace agreement between the Portuguese and the Tabajaras, represented by Piragibe, was sealed on August 5, 1585, thereby consolidating the conquest of Paraíba.<ref>[[#Bibliography|RAPOSO; ARAÚJO]], 2015, p. 154.</ref> This accord allowed the beginning of settlement in the region starting on October 31, 1585, in an area at the mouth of the Sanhauá River,<ref name="Guimarães, 2000, p. 4"/> named Porto do Capim, located in the lower part of what is now the Varadouro neighborhood.<ref>[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2015, p. 2,244.</ref> On November 4 of the same year, construction began on the city's fortress.<ref name="Moura Filha, p. 174">[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 174.</ref> Some historians consider this date to be the true foundation of ''Cidade Real de Nossa Senhora das Neves'' — rather than August 5.<ref name="Guimarães, 2000, p. 4"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/2012/08/joao-pessoa-3-capital-mais-antiga-do-brasil-completa-427-anos.html|title=João Pessoa, 3ª capital mais antiga do Brasil, completa 427 anos|date=2012-08-05|access-date=2021-10-18|language=pt-br}}</ref> This fort, often confused with the Varadouro Fort<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 176.</ref> (built only in the 1630s), lasted only a few years and by the late 1600s was already in ruins.<ref name="Moura Filha, p. 174"/>

Between 1585 and 1586, on top of a hill, a chapel dedicated to [[Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of the Snows, João Pessoa|Our Lady of the Snows]] (''Nossa Senhora das Neves'') began to be built. It quickly became the parish church, whose first vicar was Father João Vas Sallem, appointed on October 30, 1586.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 162.</ref> To connect this hill (Cidade Alta, or ''Upper City'') to the mouth of the [[Sanhauá River]] (Cidade Baixa, or ''Lower City''), a road was opened, identified by some historians as the Ladeira de São Francisco and by others as the Ladeira da Borborema.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 185.</ref> In [[1588]], the city was renamed ''Filipeia de Nossa Senhora das Neves'' in honor of King [[Philip II of Spain]] (Philip I of Portugal), who at the time ruled both [[Portugal]] and Spain under the [[Iberian Union]] (1580–1640). In 1599, after a peace agreement with the Potiguaras, Filipeia de Nossa Senhora das Neves became known as Parahyba.<ref name="Raposo, Araújo, p. 157">[[#Bibliography|RAPOSO; ARAÚJO]], 2015, p. 157.</ref>

The early years following the city's foundation were also marked by the arrival of religious orders, including the [[Franciscans]], [[Carmelites]], and [[Benedictines]]. In 1589, Friar Melchior de Santa Catarina, custodian of the Franciscans, arrived in the city to establish a mission. The colonial government offered land for the construction of a convent, which the friar approved. The project was designed by Friar Francisco dos Santos, and construction began in 1590, though it was later halted at an uncertain date between 1592 and 1596. The work resumed only in 1602 and was completed in 1608.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 169.</ref>

It is believed that the Carmelites arrived around 1591, although they began building their convent only after 1600. In 1595, Friar Damião da Fonseca, representing the Benedictines, arrived in Filipeia and requested from the captaincy's governor, Feliciano Coelho de Carvalho, a plot of land for the construction of a monastery,<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 167.</ref> which also began around 1600. The land for the monastery, located a few meters from the parish church, belonged to Vicar Sallem.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 187.</ref>

=== From the Dutch Occupation to the 18th Century ===
The first Dutch attempt to conquer the [[Captaincy of Paraíba]] occurred between December 5 and 10, 1631, but ended in failure.<ref>[[#Bibliography|VILAR]], 2014, p. 8.</ref> To prevent new invasions and ensure the captaincy's security, two forts were built: Santo Antônio and Restinga. A new attempt followed in February 1634, again without success. Only on the third attempt, on December 24, 1634, did the Dutch manage to enter the city without resistance, prompting the local population to flee to rural areas and abandon the settlement,<ref name="Moura Filha, p. 253-254">[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 253-254.</ref> which was renamed Frederica — ''Frederikstad'' in Dutch – in honor of [[Frederick Henry, Prince of Orange]].

At the time, the city had about 1,500 inhabitants and nineteen sugar mills,<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 226.</ref> most of which were seized by the invaders.<ref name="Moura Filha, p. 253-254"/> The Franciscans were expelled from their convent, which was converted into a fortress (1636).<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 359.</ref> The Portuguese only recognized Dutch rule in [[1637]], acknowledging the colony of [[Dutch Brazil|New Holland]], which extended from the [[São Francisco River]] to present-day [[Ceará]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 249.</ref>

Elias Herckmans, who governed the captaincy between 1636 and 1639, described the city as follows:<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 256.</ref>

:::''It is surrounded by forest and cannot be seen by those approaching, except when one is already within it, unless one sails up or down the river; for when arriving at the mouth or entrance of the bay called Varadouro, one can perfectly see the convent of St. Francis and some buildings on the northern side.''

[[File:Nederlands-Brazillië-es.svg|thumb|left|280px|[[Dutch Brazil|New Holland]] at its greatest extent]]
In 1645, the [[Insurrection of Pernambuco|Pernambuco Insurrection]] broke out – a revolt against Dutch domination in Brazil. Among its leaders was [[André Vidal de Negreiros]], born in the captaincy at the Engenho São João (current [[São João do Cariri]]), who took part in all stages of the movement, including both [[Battle of Guararapes (disambiguation)|Battles of Guararapes]] in [[1648]] and [[1649]]. The [[First Anglo-Dutch War]] (1652–1655) weakened Dutch power in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast]], leading the invaders to sign their surrender on January 26, 1654, leaving the captaincy in ruins. However, it was only in 1661 that the [[Netherlands]] officially recognized Portuguese sovereignty over northeastern Brazil through the [[Treaty of The Hague (1661)|Treaty of The Hague]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 261-262.</ref> With the end of the occupation, Frederica reverted to the name Parahyba do Norte.<ref name="Raposo, Araújo, p. 157"/> In 1671, the Church of Our Lady of the Snows had most of its structure demolished, except for the nave. The rebuilding of the temple, interrupted several times, lasted over a century and was completed only in the late 18th century.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 331-332.</ref>

In August 1704, the captain-major of Paraíba, Fernando de Barros e Vasconcelos, ordered the construction of the Powder House (''Casa de Pólvora'') on the Ladeira de São Francisco, which lasted until 1710.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 380-381.</ref> On September 24, 1729, the cornerstone of the Church of Our Lady of Mercy (''Igreja de Nossa Senhora das Mercês'') was laid, and it was consecrated on September 21, 1741. In 1763, the Portuguese sailor Sílvio Siqueira, while navigating off the coast of Paraíba, faced a violent storm at sea. Gathering his crew, he prayed to Our Lady of Penha, to whom he was devoted, asking for the tempest to subside. Moments later, the storm weakened, and the crew disembarked at what is now Penha Beach, where a shrine dedicated to the Virgin was built.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MELO; VENZELLA; BRAMBILLA]], 2019, p. 5.</ref> Around the same decade, circa 1767, the Church of Our Lady Mother of Men (''Nossa Senhora Mãe dos Homens'') was founded.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 382.</ref>

Still in the 18th century, the city entered a period of decline, leading to the extinction of the Captaincy of Paraíba in 1756, when it was annexed to the [[Captaincy of Pernambuco]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 403.</ref> [[Paraíba]] would only regain its status as a separate political unit in January 1799, when its autonomy was restored.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MOURA FILHA]], 2004, p. 279.</ref>

=== 19th Century ===
[[File:Ponte Rio Paraíba - panoramio.jpg|thumb|Baralho Bridge over the Sanhauá River, the first connection between the City of Parahyba and the village of Barreiras (now the neighborhoods of Baralho and São Bento, in [[Bayeux, Paraíba|Bayeux]])]]

On September 7, [[1822]], Brazil became politically independent from Portugal, and [[Paraíba]] became a province of the [[Empire of Brazil]], with the City of Parahyba as its capital. At that time, the city had about three thousand inhabitants. In the same year, the city received its first public lighting system, consisting of twenty oil lamps fueled by castor oil.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MAIA; GUTIERRES; SOARES]], 2009, p. 5.</ref> By 1828, the capital already had 5,816 residents and 2,119 houses, most made of thatch or mud walls.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MAIA]], 2011, p. 11.</ref> The following year, public lighting was reinforced, and the City of Parahyba had fifty lamps in operation.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MAIA; GUTIERRES; SOARES]], 2009, p. 6.</ref> On March 24, 1836, on the upper floor of the Provincial Assembly, the Lyceu Paraibano was founded, with its first statute approved on April 19, 1837.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2009, p. 2.</ref>

Around 1840, the Sanhauá Bridge, also known as Baralho Bridge, was built over the [[Sanhauá River]]. It was the first connection between the City of Parahyba and the village of Barreiras – the areas that later became the neighborhoods of Baralho and São Bento, giving rise to the present-day city of [[Bayeux, Paraíba|Bayeux]], which at that time belonged to [[Santa Rita, Paraíba|Santa Rita]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|RODRIGUES]], 2015, p. 37.</ref> In 1847, by imperial decree, the Captaincy of the Ports was created, responsible for the administration of ''Porto do Capim''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.marinha.mil.br/cppb/historicocppb|title=Histórico da CPPB|access-date=2022-12-09|language=pt-br}}</ref> On January 24, 1855, the city gained its first cemetery.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MAIA]], 2012, p. 5.</ref> By 1856, due to its low efficiency, the city's public lighting system was discontinued, except during festivities and in public or commercial establishments. It was only resumed in 1885, this time using kerosene lamps.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MAIA; GUTIERRES; SOARES]], 2009, p. 9.</ref>

On November 4, 1858, Provincial Law No. 13 was enacted, creating the Colégio Nossa Senhora das Neves, which was inaugurated on February 5, 1859. However, the school closed less than two years later and remained inactive until March 1895.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervopatrimonial/25.pdf|title=Antigo Colégio N. Sra. das Neves|access-date=2021-10-31|language=pt-br}}</ref> In 1859, the city of Paraíba received a visit from Emperor [[Pedro II of Brazil]] and his wife [[Teresa Cristina of the Two Sicilies|Teresa Cristina]],<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.polemicaparaiba.com.br/brasil/curiosidade-dom-pedro-ii-passou-natal-na-paraiba-no-ano-de-1859/ |title=CURIOSIDADE: Dom Pedro II passou Natal na Paraíba no ano de 1859 |date=2018-12-23 |access-date=2025-11-03 |language=pt-br}}</ref> on which occasion it received the title of "Imperial City". When Brazil's [[1872 Brazilian census|first national census]] was conducted in 1872, the city's population was recorded at 24,714 inhabitants, making it the fourth most populous locality in the Province of Paraíba – after [[Sousa, Paraíba|Sousa]] (29,726), Independencia (now [[Guarabira]], with 28,191), and [[Areia, Paraíba|Areia]] (25,549).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv25477_v5_pb.pdf|title=Recenseamento do Brazil em 1872 – Parahyba|access-date=2021-11-01|language=pt-br}}</ref>

[[Image:Teatro Santa Roza.jpg|thumb|Santa Roza Theater, the most important in Paraíba and the second oldest in the state]]
The Santa Roza Theater, the most important theater in Paraíba and the second oldest in the state, was inaugurated on November 3, 1889. Named after the provincial president Francisco da Gama Rosa, it became a landmark of local culture. Just twelve days later, the [[Proclamation of the Republic (Brazil)|Republic]] was proclaimed, transforming Paraíba from a province into a state, while the city remained its capital. Venâncio Neiva became the first state president and renamed the building ''Teatro do Estado'' (State Theatre).<ref name="antigo.paraiba.pb.gov.br">{{cite web|url=http://antigo.paraiba.pb.gov.br/index-34098.html|title=Patrimônio material do Estado, Teatro Santa Roza completa 126 anos de existência|date=2015-11-04|access-date=2021-10-31|language=pt-br}}</ref>

On April 27, 1892, the Church of Nossa Senhora das Neves became a cathedral, following the creation of the [[Diocese of Paraíba]] through the [[papal bull]] ''Ad Universas Orbis Ecclesias'' by [[Pope Leo XIII]]. The new diocese, separated from the [[Diocese of Olinda]] ([[Pernambuco]]), covered the entire territories of [[Paraíba]] and [[Rio Grande do Norte]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|NÓBREGA]], 2023, p. 45-46.</ref> It was formally established on March 4, 1894, with the installation of its first bishop, Dom Adauto Aurélio de Miranda Henriques, who also founded the Diocesan Seminary of Paraíba Imaculada Conceição and the Diocesan College (today the Marist College Pio X).<ref>[[#Bibliography|NÓBREGA]], 2023, p. 82.</ref> Dom Adauto was also responsible for reopening Our Lady of the Snows School (''Colégio Nossa Senhora das Neves'') on March 14, 1895, after more than thirty years of closure.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervopatrimonial/25.pdf|title=Antigo Colégio N. Sra. das Neves|access-date=2011-11-01|language=pt-br}}</ref>

Starting March 2, 1895, a state law abolished the municipal council and replaced it with a mayoral system, under which the mayor was appointed by the Paraíba state governor. The first person to hold the position was Jovino Limeira Dinoá, a former provincial deputy, who served until October 1900.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.geocities.ws/dinoamedeiros/JovinoDinoa.html|title=Jovino Limeira Dinoá|access-date=2023-12-27|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.maispb.com.br/226855/ex-prefeitos-de-joao-pessoa-relembram-desafios-e-abrem-bau-de-historias.html|title=Ex-prefeitos de JP abrem baú de histórias|author=LIRA, Bruno|date=2017-08-03|access-date=2023-12-27|language=pt-br}}</ref> In 1896, the city received its first animal-drawn trams, operated by Ferro Carril Parahyba.<ref name="SARMENTO et al, 2010, p. 4."/>

=== Early decades of the 20th Century ===
[[Image:Usina Cultural Energisa - João Pessoa (PB).jpg|thumb|left|250px|Old headquarters of Usina Cruz do Peixe, the first electricity supplier in the city, now home to Energisa Cultural Usine (inaugurated in 2003)]]
According to the 1900 census, the municipality had 28,793 inhabitants, a population increase of more than 50% compared to 1890 (18,645). However, Guarabira remained the most populous municipality in the state, with 40,052 inhabitants, followed by [[Campina Grande]] (38,303).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv25487.pdf|title=Synopse do Recenseamento|date=1900|access-date=2021-11-01|language=pt-br}}</ref> At that time, the urban area of Parahyba City was limited to the region between the Sanhauá River and Irerês Lagoon. In 1906, during the administration of state president Valfredo Leal, construction began on the Tambaú Railway,<ref>[[#Bibliography|HIGINO JUNIOR]], 2013, p. 21-22.</ref> the first connection between the city center and the coast. It was completed in 1911 under his successor, João Lopes Machado.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 179.</ref>

In March 1912, the Usina Cruz do Peixe began operating under the ''Empresa de Tração, Força e Luz'', supplying electric power to the city and replacing acetylene gas and kerosene lighting.<ref>[[#Bibliography|HIGINO JUNIOR]], 2013, p. 24-25.</ref> The following month, the water supply system began operating,<ref name="Oliveira, 2006, p. 63"/><ref name="BRITO; VANZELLA, 2018, p. 8"/> fed by artesian wells in the Buraquinho Forest (''Mata do Buraquinho'').<ref>[[#Bibliography|COUTINHO]], 2004, p. 54.</ref> The project had been planned since 1907,<ref name="ARAÚJO, 2012, p. 115."/> when the government purchased the land for five thousand cruzeiros.<ref name="BRITO; VANZELLA, 2018, p. 8">[[#Bibliography|BRITO; VANZELLA]], 2018, p. 8.</ref> However, both the electricity and water systems were limited to wealthier social classes.<ref name="ARAÚJO, 2012, p. 115.">[[#Bibliography|ARAÚJO]], 2012, p. 115.</ref> In 1913, the sanitary engineer [[Saturnino de Brito]], invited by Governor João Pereira de Castro Pinto, prepared a sewage system project for the city, which would only begin construction in 1922.<ref name="Almeida, 2006, p. 102">[[#Bibliography|ALMEIDA]], 2006, p. 102.</ref>

| caption3          = The Church of Our Lady of the Conception of the Military in the 1920s, before its demolition in 1929
}}

On February 6, 1914, through the papal bull ''Maius Catholicae Religionis Incrementum'', [[Pope Pius X]] elevated the Diocese of Paraíba to the status of an [[archdiocese]], making Dom Adauto its first archbishop, a position he held until his death in 1935. On February 17 of the same year, electric trams replaced the animal-drawn ones.<ref name="Oliveira, 2006, p. 63">[[#Bibliography|OLIVEIRA]], 2006, p. 63.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|COUTINHO]], 2004, p. 55.</ref> Between 1915 and 1916, the city saw the inauguration of its first hotels – Globo Hotel (1915) and Luso-Brasileiro Hotel (1916).<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 92.</ref> Around 1918, under Camilo de Holanda,<ref>[[#Bibliography|COUTINHO]], 2004, p. 62.</ref> then state president (1916–1920), construction began on a road connecting the city center to the Tambaú beach, which at that time was only a small fishing village. This road would later become Presidente Epitácio Pessoa Avenue, although it was not completed at that time.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 179-182.</ref>

In 1920, when the capital's population reached 52,990 inhabitants,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv31687.pdf|title=Recensamento de 1920|date=1926|access-date=2021-11-01|language=pt-br}}</ref> Walfredo Guedes Pereira assumed the city's leadership. His administration built the Independence Square, inaugurated on September 7, 1922, to commemorate Brazil's first centenary of independence;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervopatrimonial/135.pdf|title=Coreto, Obelisco e Praça da Independência|access-date=2021-10-08|language=pt-br}}</ref> established the Arruda Câmara Park (inaugurated on December 24, 1922);<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervopatrimonial/51.pdf|title=Parque Arruda Câmara|access-date=2021-10-08|language=pt-br}}</ref> and carried out drainage and sanitation around Irerês Lagoon. This lake was renamed Sólon de Lucena Park (''Parque Sólon de Lucena'') by municipal decree on September 27, 1924, in honor of Sólon Barbosa de Lucena, then state president.<ref>[[#Bibliography|FERNANDES]], 2018, p. 20-21.</ref> His government also oversaw the demolition of two historic churches, Mãe dos Homens and Rosário dos Pretos, both in 1923 – the former to extend what is now Monsenhor Walfredo Leal Avenue, and the latter to build Vidal de Negreiros Square, later known as ''Ponto de Cem Réis'',<ref>[[#Bibliography|MENESES]], 2014, p. 77</ref> named after the tram fare charged there.<ref name="SARMENTO et al, 2010, p. 4.">SARMENTO; CUNHA; FILHA; AZEVEDO, 2010, p. 4.</ref>

In 1926, the city's sewage system finally entered into operation,<ref name="Almeida, 2006, p. 102"/><ref>[[#Bibliography|DIAS]], 2013, p. 73.</ref> and imperial palm trees were planted around Parque Sólon de Lucena. In August 1929, Globo Hotel moved to its new location on Saint Friar Pedro Gonçalves Square, occupying the residence of its owner, Henriques Siqueira (known as "Seu Marinheiro").<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 92-93.</ref> That same year, under the order of Governor [[João Pessoa Cavalcanti de Albuquerque]], the centuries-old Church of Nossa Senhora da Conceição dos Militares (formerly the Chapel of São Gonçalo) beside the Redemption Palace was demolished, except for its tower.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervo-patrimonial/55.pdf |title=Palácio da Redenção |access-date=2025-10-14|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|ISMAEL; COSTA]], 1929, p. 2.</ref>

=== Name change until the 1950s ===
On July 26, 1930, João Pessoa was assassinated at Glória Confectionery, on ''Rua Nova'' (New Street), in [[Recife]], by his political opponent and personal enemy João Duarte Dantas. The event caused great national repercussion and became one of the triggers of the [[Revolution of 1930|1930 Revolution]], which was led by [[Paraíba]], Minas Gerais, and [[Rio Grande do Sul]]. His body was embalmed in Recife and transported by rail to the Paraíba capital, arriving on July 27, then taken to the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Snows, where it was laid in state until August 1. On that date, it was transported to the port of Cabedelo<ref>[[#Bibliography|ZENAIDE; ALBUQUERQUE]], 2023, p. 125-126.</ref> to be buried in [[Rio de Janeiro]] city, which occurred on August 7.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cpdoc.fgv.br/sites/default/files/verbetes/primeira-republica/PESSOA,%20Jo%C3%A3o.pdf |title=PESSOA, JOÃO |author=[[Fundação Getúlio Vargas]] (FGV) |access-date=2025-10-25 |language=pt-br}}</ref>

[[File:João Pessoa (PB).tif|thumb|left|Old photograph of President João Pessoa Square, with the monument "The Altar of the Fatherland" (''O Altar da Pátria'') in the middle<br /><small>([[Brazilian National Archives]])</small>]]
On September 1, 1930, during an ordinary session of the [[Legislative Assembly of Paraíba]] at the Santa Roza Theatre, a bill was introduced to change the capital's name from Parahyba do Norte to João Pessoa.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://zeoserver.pb.gov.br/jornalauniao/auniao2/servicos/copy_of_jornal-a-uniao/dec-30/1930/setembro/a-uniao-02-09-1930.pdf|title=A União – Orgam Official do Estado|date=1930-09-02|access-date=2022-12-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> The bill, voted on and approved in two rounds,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://zeoserver.pb.gov.br/jornalauniao/auniao2/servicos/copy_of_jornal-a-uniao/dec-30/1930/setembro/a-uniao-03-09-1930.pdf|title=A União – Orgam Official do Estado|date=1930-09-03|access-date=2022-12-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> was sanctioned on September 4 by the state president Álvaro Pereira de Carvalho, becoming State Law No. 700.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://zeoserver.pb.gov.br/jornalauniao/auniao2/servicos/copy_of_jornal-a-uniao/dec-30/1930/setembro/a-uniao-05-09-1930.pdf|title=A União – Orgam Official do Estado|date=1930-09-05|access-date=2022-12-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> In further tribute to João Pessoa, Comendador Felizardo Square was renamed President João Pessoa Square, and at its center, "The Altar of the Fatherland" (''O Altar da Pátria''), monument was built and inaugurated on September 8, 1933, with the presence of President Getúlio Vargas.<ref name="PAULINO, 2014, p. 283">[[#Bibliography|PAULINO]], 2014, p. 283.</ref>

In 1932, at the invitation of Antenor Navarro, the federal intervener in Paraíba, the architect Nestor de Figueiredo prepared the city's urban plan, called the "Remodeling and Expansion Plan of the City of João Pessoa".<ref>[[#Bibliography|ALMEIDA]], 2006, p. 129.</ref> His successor, Gratuliano da Costa Brito (1932–1934), who assumed office after Navarro's death, inaugurated the ''Paraíba Palace Hotel'', where the ''Correio da Manhã'' newspaper headquarters had once stood, facing Vidal de Negreiros Square,<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 69.</ref> and resumed the opening of President Epitácio Pessoa Avenue, completed in 1940,<ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 185.</ref> stretching five kilometers from Independence Square to Tambaú Beach.

Between January and September 1935, former mayor Walfredo Guedes Pereira returned to lead the capital. During his administration, the land where the Church of Nossa Senhora das Mercês stood was expropriated; it was demolished in 1936 to make way for what is now 1817 Square.<ref name="Meneses, 2014">[[#Bibliography|MENESES]], 2014, p. 117</ref> The following year, the federal intervener Argemiro de Figueiredo inaugurated the current headquarters of Lyceu Paraibano, which since its foundation had operated beside the Redemption Palace, where the Law School of Paraíba now stands.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.clickpb.com.br/esporte/liceu-paraibano-completa-170-anos-1623.html|title=Liceu Paraibano completa 170 anos|date=2006-03-24|access-date=2022-12-07|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.clickpb.com.br/educacao/liceu-paraibano-faz-aniversario-hoje-51556.html|title=Liceu Paraibano faz aniversário hoje|date=2009-03-24|access-date=2022-12-09|language=pt-br}}</ref> On November 1, 1939, after three years of construction, the [[Archdiocese of Paraíba]] and the city government of João Pessoa inaugurated the new Church of Our Lady of Mercy<ref name="Meneses, 2014"/> on Padre Meira Street, just a few meters from the original church. That same year, the Parque Sólon de Lucena was officially inaugurated, and in the following year, landscape architect [[Burle Marx]] designed its gardens.<ref>[[#Bibliography|KOURY]], 2005, p. 75.</ref>

In 1940, the Aeroclube da Paraíba was founded, beginning operations on November 10 in what is now the Tambauzinho neighborhood, on the former site of the Imbiribeira Field.<ref name="Sarmento, p. 23">[[#Bibliography|SARMENTO]], 2012, p. 23.</ref> Starting in 1941, the state government opened the João Pessoa–Cabedelo highway to connect the capital's urban area to the Port of Cabedelo,<ref>[[#Bibliography|CAMPOS; SOUZA; SILVEIRA; CASTRO]], 2007, p. 4.</ref> which had been operating since 1935 at the mouth of the Paraíba River. In the same year, a survey conducted by the Brazilian Navy officially recognized [[Ponta do Seixas]] as the easternmost point of Brazilian territory, a title previously disputed with Ponta de Pedras, in [[Pernambuco]].<ref>[[#Bibliography|TORNAGHI; FIGUEROA]], 1941, p. 894.</ref> The Paraiban historian Coriolano de Medeiros recorded in his ''Dicionário Corográfico do Estado da Paraíba'':<ref>[[#Bibliography|MEDEIROS]], 2016, p. 52.</ref>

[[File:Sobrado e capela anexa da Fazenda Ribamar.jpg|thumb|The manor house of the former Ribamar or Boi-Só farm, whose lands once encompassed part of Cabedelo and the current João Pessoa neighborhoods of Bessa, Brisamar, Estados, Ipês, Manaíra, and Pedro Gondim. The popular name Boi-Só refers to the Frenchman Boisôt, its first owner, from the Boisson family. In 1962, a chapel was built next to the manor house, and in 1980, the entire complex was listed as a historical landmark by the state. Since 2008, the farm has been located within the Alphaville condominium, on the border between the Ipês and Estados neighborhoods.<ref name="Sobrado da Fazenda Ribamar e Capela">{{cite web|access-date=2023-03-27 |title=Sobrado da Fazenda Ribamar e Capela |url=http://www.memoriajoaopessoa.com.br/acervopatrimonial/22.pdf|language=pt-br}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>]]

::''On September 5 [1941] observations were made at Ponta de Pedras, and on the 12th at Cabo Branco. Fortune smiled upon Paraíba, for Ponta do Seixas, at Cabo Branco, is the easternmost point of the national territory and, therefore, the easternmost point of the Americas. That Paraiban cape boldly projects about 1,683 meters farther east than Ponta de Pedras.''

On October 12, 1947, the first direct mayoral elections in João Pessoa's history took place. Among four candidates, Osvaldo Pessoa Cavalcanti de Albuquerque, of the Social Democratic Party (PSD), brother of João Pessoa and nephew of Epitácio Pessoa, was elected with 45.56% of the valid votes. He took office on March 14, 1948, and served until 1951, when he resigned.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://correiodaparaiba.com.br/politica/historia-politica/um-osvaldo-no-governo-outro-na-prefeitura-de-joao-pessoa/|title=Um Osvaldo no Governo, outro na Prefeitura de João Pessoa|author=SANTOS, Adelson Barbosa dos|date=2016-08-28|access-date=2021-10-07|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tre-pb.jus.br/eleicoes/e/eleicoes-anteriores/resultados-de-eleicoes|title=Resultado das Eleições 1947 na Paraíba|access-date=2021-10-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> Earlier, on October 11, 1948, the Municipal Code of Conduct came into effect.<ref name="Passos et al, 2012, p. 53"/><ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 188.</ref>

On Christmas Eve 1952, the paving of President Epitácio Pessoa Avenue was inaugurated<ref>[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2008, p. 69.</ref> using cobblestones,<ref name="SARMENTO et al, 2010, p. 7."/><ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 189.</ref> marking a decade in which the city's urban growth became increasingly evident, with several subdivisions emerging along the avenue.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 174</ref> These developments gave rise to the present-day neighborhoods of Brisamar, Cabo Branco, Estados, Expedicionários, Miramar, Pedro Gondim, Tambauzinho, Torre, and Tambaú, consolidating the city's expansion from the center toward the ocean.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MARTINS; MAIA]], 2015, p. 192.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|EGITO]], 2005, p. 47.</ref> Much of this land had once belonged to the Ribamar or Boi-Só Farm, whose first owner was the Frenchman Boisôt, from the Boisson family, in the 19th century.<ref name="Sobrado da Fazenda Ribamar e Capela"/>

In 1953, the Bust of Tamandaré was inaugurated<ref>[[#Bibliography|HIGINO JÚNIOR]], p. 53.</ref> and the Paraíba Aeroclub was relocated to an area expropriated by the state government, in what is now the Aeroclube neighborhood.<ref name="Sarmento, p. 23"/> Later in the same decade, Tambaú Beach received its first promenade, along with the opening of Almirante Tamandaré Avenue.<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 112.</ref> Starting in 1958, construction began on the Edifício Presidente João Pessoa, an eighteen-story building that became the city's first skyscraper, completed in the early 1960s.<ref>[[#Bibliography|CHAVES; TINEM]], 2012, p. 8.</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/2018/08/04/construcao-de-predios-altos-em-joao-pessoa-e-marco-na-historia-da-cidade.ghtml|title=Construção de prédios altos em João Pessoa é marco na história da cidade|author=REZENDE, Clara|date=2018-08-04|access-date=2023-03-13|language=pt-br}}</ref>

Between the 1940s and 1950s, João Pessoa's population grew by 26.5%, from 94,333 inhabitants in 1940<ref>{{cite web|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/65/cd_1940_p8_pb.pdf|title=Recenseamento Geral do Brasil|date=1940-09-01|location=[[Rio de Janeiro]]|access-date=2021-11-01|language=pt-br}}</ref> to 119,326 in 1950. Within the state, it ranked second, behind Campina Grande (173,206).<ref>{{cite web|access-date=2021-11-01 |date=1955 |location=[[Rio de Janeiro]] |title=Estado da Paraíba: censo demográfico |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/67/cd_1950_v16_t1_pb.pdf|language=pt-br}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> By 1960, after the emancipation of the districts of [[Cabedelo]] (1956) and [[Alhandra, Paraíba|Alhandra]] (1959),<ref name="Bezerra, 2016, p. 111">[[#Bibliography|BEZERRA]], 2016, p. 111.</ref> the population reached 153,175, still behind Campina Grande (204,583).<ref>{{cite web|access-date=2021-11-01 |author=IBGE |title=Censo demográfico de 1960: Rio Grande do Norte-Paraíba |url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/68/cd_1960_v1_t5_rn_pb.pdf|language=pt-br}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

=== 1960s and 1970s ===
In 1961, João Pessoa lost the district of [[Pitimbu]], which was elevated to the status of a municipality, and in 1963, the Vila do Conde, now the municipality of [[Conde, Paraíba|Conde]], also became independent.<ref name="Bezerra, 2016, p. 111"/> That same year, the industrial district of João Pessoa was created, and starting in 1965, the campus of the [[Federal University of Paraíba]] (UFPB) began construction,<ref>[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2008, p. 78.</ref> stimulating urban growth to the south and southeast of the city.<ref>[[#Bibliography|EGITO]], 2005, p. 49.</ref> Near the campus, three housing complexes were developed between 1969 and 1974, forming the present-day Castelo Branco neighborhood.<ref>[[#Bibliography|CASTRO]], 2015, p. 183.</ref> Two other factors driving the city's growth at the time were the extension of Avenida Pedro II and the opening of the current Avenida Cruz das Armas.<ref name="Passos et al, 2012, p. 53"/>

On February 5, 1966, during [[Military dictatorship in Brazil|Brazil's military regime]], President [[Humberto de Alencar Castelo Branco]] enacted Institutional Act No. 3, which imposed indirect elections for state governors, who would then appoint the mayors of their capitals. As a result, Mayor Domingos Mendonça Neto, elected in 1963, had his mandate revoked, and former mayor Damásio Barbosa de Franca was appointed in his place,<ref>[[#Bibliography|SOBREIRA]], 2016, p. 163-164.</ref> serving for five years. During his administration, the paving of Atlântica Avenue, now Senator Ruy Carneiro Avenue, began, starting from Epitácio Pessoa Avenue and connecting it to Manaíra beach.<ref name="Pereira, 2008, p. 210"/>

In June 1969, construction began on the Viaduto Damásio de Franca, named in honor of the mayor,<ref>[[#Bibliography|OLIVEIRA]], 2006, p. 91.</ref> which was inaugurated on July 17, 1970.<ref name="SARMENTO et al, 2010, p. 7.">[[#Bibliography|SARMENTO; CUNHA; FILHA; AZEVEDO]], 2010, p. 7.</ref> In the 1970 census, João Pessoa became the most populous municipality in Paraíba, with 221,546 inhabitants, surpassing Campina Grande (195,303),<ref>{{cite web|access-date=2021-11-02 |author=IBGE |date=1973 |location=[[Rio de Janeiro]] |title=Censo demográfico: Paraíba |url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/69/cd_1970_v1_t9_pb.pdf|language=pt-br}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> which had lost territory due to the creation of new municipalities. That same year, amid increasing verticalization of the city, a state constitutional amendment of 1967 limited high-rise construction along the coastal strip to two floors, between Penha and Formosa beaches, the latter in Cabedelo.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2009, p. 18.</ref>

After its construction began in 1969, Governor João Agripino inaugurated the Tambaú Hotel on March 6, 1971, just days before the end of his term. The hotel, designed by architect Sérgio Bernardes and built on the Tambaú Beach sands, was reopened on September 11 by Agripino's successor, Ernâni Sátiro.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ALMEIDA; BRAMBILLA; VANZELLA]], 2016, p. 41</ref> On April 20, 1972, the municipal coat of arms, flag, and banner were officially established by municipal law,<ref>[https://leismunicipais.com.br/a/pb/j/joao-pessoa/lei-ordinaria/1972/162/1624/lei-ordinaria-n-1624-1972-cria-o-brasao-de-armas-a-bandeira-e-o-estandarte-do-municipio-de-joao-pessoa-e-da-outras-providencias-1972-04-20 '''Lei nº 1624, de 20 de abril de 1972''']. Cria o brasão de armas, a bandeira e o estandarte do município de João Pessoa e dá outras providências.</ref> and the city received the Cabo Branco Lighthouse on the cliff near Ponta do Seixas the following day. In the same year, Senador Ruy Carneiro Avenue was fully paved.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2008, p. 168.</ref> Additionally, the first section of the BR-230 highway in the city was paved; it originally served as the João Pessoa Bypass, connecting the João Pessoa–Cabedelo Road (federalized and incorporated into the highway) to [[BR-101]] at the junction with Avenida Cruz das Armas.<ref>[[#Bibliography|DIEB; MARTINS]], 2017, p. 10.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|CAMPOS; SOUZA; SILVEIRA; CASTRO]], 2007, p. 6.</ref> At the time, the city was administered by Mayor Dorgival Terceiro Neto (1971–1974).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://antigo.paraiba.pb.gov.br/index-47145.html |title=Estruturação administrativa e obras viárias marcaram trajetória de Dorgival Terceiro Neto |date=2013-04-13 |access-date=2025-11-03 |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.al.pb.leg.br/6719/alpb-emite-nota-de-pesar-pela-morte-de-ex-governador.html |title=ALPB emite nota de pesar pela morte de ex-governador |author=DIAS, Damásio |date=2013-04-13 |access-date=2025-11-03 |language=pt-br}}</ref>

In 1973, the city gained two major avenues: Avenida Beira-Rio (renamed Avenida Ministro José Américo de Almeida in 1977), connecting the city center to Cabo Branco Beach, and Avenida Governador Flávio Ribeiro Coutinho, known as the “Retão de Manaíra”, which, together with Avenida Governador Argemiro de Figueiredo, facilitated northward expansion toward Bessa Beach and Cabedelo.<ref name="Pereira, 2008, p. 210">[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2008, p. 210.</ref><ref name="Pereira, 2008, p. 231">[[#Bibliography|PEREIRA]], 2008, p. 231.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|EGITO]], 2005, p. 52.</ref> At the same time, the city also expanded southwest and south, with new housing developments. In 1974, the Urban Development Plan (PDU) was drafted, resulting in the Urbanism Code,<ref name="Passos et al, 2012, p. 53">[[#Bibliography|PASSOS; SILVEIRA; PITA; BRAGA; SILVEIRA]], 2012, p. 53.</ref> established by Municipal Law 2,102 on December 31, 1975.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.planmob.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/C%C3%B3digo-de-Urbanismo.pdf|title=Código de Urbanismo|date=2001|access-date=2022-09-10|language=pt-br}}</ref>

On March 9, 1975, the Ernani Sátiro Stadium was inaugurated by the state government, with an inaugural match between Botafogo of João Pessoa and Botafogo of Rio de Janeiro, won 2–0 by the visiting team.<ref>[[#Bibliography|CARVALHO]], 2021, p. 55.</ref> The stadium was renamed José Américo de Almeida Filho Stadium the following year, in honor of a former Botafogo João Pessoa striker from the 1930s and son of ex-governor José Américo de Almeida.<ref>[[#Bibliography|MALAIA; FORTES]], 2020, p. 175.</ref> On August 24, 1975, during Soldier's Day celebrations, Parque Sólon de Lucena was the site of the Baltrão Tragedy, when a ferry carrying about 200 passengers—over three times its capacity—capsized, killing 35 people by drowning, including 29 children.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://jornaldaparaiba.com.br/noticias/vida_urbana/2015/08/23/tragedia-da-lagoa-o-doloroso-drama-de-jp-completa-40-anos|title=Tragédia da Lagoa: o doloroso drama de JP completa 40 anos|date=2015-08-23|access-date=2022-09-10|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.polemicaparaiba.com.br/?p=527259|title=O PASSEIO QUE NÃO ACABOU: Tragédia da Lagoa completa 45 anos nesta segunda-feira (24)|date=2020-08-22|access-date=2022-09-10|language=pt-br}}</ref>

By the late 1970s, new subdivisions emerged along the highway, giving rise to the present-day neighborhoods of Água Fria, Ernesto Geisel, and José Américo.<ref>[[#Bibliography|CAMPOS; SOUZA; SILVEIRA; CASTRO]], 2007, p. 7.</ref> At the same time, construction began on a road connecting BR-230 to Praia da Penha, now Hilton Souto Maior Avenue, replacing the old Penha Road and becoming the city's longest avenue, stretching 7.5&nbsp;km.<ref>[[#Bibliography|TRIGUEIRO]], 2018, p. 39-43.</ref>

=== 1980s and 1990s ===
[[File:Casario da Praça Antenor Navarro.jpg|thumb|left|Revitalized buildings in the Historic Center of João Pessoa, around Antenor Navarro Square]]

By 1980, João Pessoa had a population of 329,942, up from 221,546 in 1970, reflecting decades of urban and demographic growth.<ref name="Silva, 2015, p. 16">[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2015, p. 16.</ref> That year, the Institute of Historical and Artistic Heritage of the State of Paraíba (IPHAEP) listed several city monuments and historic sites for protection. In 1982, the boundaries of the Historic Center were defined, encompassing 117 hectares.<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 71.</ref> In April 1982, João Pessoa gained the José Lins do Rego Cultural Space,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://auniao.pb.gov.br/noticias/caderno_cultura/espaco-cultural-celebra-40-anos|title=Espaço Cultural celebra 40 anos|author=CABRAL, Guilherme|date=2022-04-13|access-date=2022-09-10|language=pt-br}}</ref> located on the site of the former Paraíba Aeroclub.<ref name="Sarmento, p. 23"/> In June 1983, the Tarcísio de Miranda Burity Housing Complex was inaugurated, giving rise to the Mangabeira neighborhood, named after the many [[mangaba]] trees in the area.<ref>[[#Bibliography|RODRIGUES]], 2016, p. 28-29.</ref> The following year, the Valentina de Figueiredo Residential Park was inaugurated (now the Valentina neighborhood),<ref>[[#Bibliography|SILVA]], 2014, p. 23.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|AGUIAR]], 2017, p. 15.</ref> extending urban growth beyond the Cuiá River. Its name honored Valentina Silva de Oliveira Figueiredo, mother of then-President João Figueiredo, who participated in the inauguration.<ref>[[#Bibliography|OLIVEIRA]], 2021, p. 43-44.</ref>

In 1985, João Pessoa celebrated its 400th anniversary. That same year, after the end of the military regime, the city returned to direct mayoral elections. Antônio Carneiro Arnaud won, serving from 1986 to 1988, succeeded by Wilson Braga, former governor of Paraíba.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fgv.br/cpdoc/acervo/dicionarios/verbete-biografico/arnaud-carneiro|title=CARNEIRO, Arnaud|access-date=2021-10-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> In 1989, the revitalization of the historic center began, led by the state government in partnership with the National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage (IPHAN), starting with Praça Dom Adauto and Praça São Francisco and later expanding throughout the 1990s. The city also inaugurated the Manaíra Shopping Center, now the largest in Paraíba.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.maispb.com.br/579726/manaira-se-destaca-como-o-decimo-maior-shopping-center-do-brasil.html |title=Manaira se destaca como o 10º maior shopping do país |date=2022-01-06 |access-date=2025-04-12|language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.maispb.com.br/757274/manaira-shopping-anuncia-nova-fachada-mais-seis-pavimentos-e-jardim-na-cobertura.html |title=Manaíra Shopping anuncia nova fachada, seis pavimentos e jardim na cobertura |date=2024-12-10 |access-date=2025-04-12|language=pt-br}}</ref> That year, the current Constitution of Paraíba was enacted, declaring the state's entire coastal zone as protected heritage, maintaining the prohibition on high-rise buildings along the shoreline established in 1970. The Constitution also included a provision for a plebiscite on the city's name,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.al.pb.leg.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/constituicao_estadual_EC_55.pdf |title=Constituição do Estado da Paraíba |access-date=2025-04-12|language=pt-br}}</ref> which was never held.

[[File:Mausoléu de João Pessoa, João Pessoa (PB).jpg|thumb|The João Pessoa Mausoleum, where the remains of the former president of Paraíba and his wife Maria Luíza have been since 1997, transferred from [[Rio de Janeiro]]]]

On April 2, 1990, the current municipal organic law was enacted, later amended over time.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sapl.joaopessoa.pb.leg.br/media/sapl/public/normajuridica/1990/14101/14101_texto_integral.pdf|title=Lei orgânica do município de João Pessoa|access-date=2021-10-10|language=pt-br}}</ref> In the early 1990s, the urban stretch of [[BR-230]] was duplicated.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ANDRADE; CARVALHO; SILVA; RIBEIRO]], 2007, p. 8.</ref> By 1991, João Pessoa's population reached 497,600,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/Tabela/202#/k/63513150/resultado|title=Tabela 202 – População residente, por sexo e situação do domicílio|author=IBGE|date=1991|access-date=2021-11-01}}</ref> a 51% increase from 1980. In 1992, during the [[Earth Summit]] in Rio de Janeiro, Mayor Carlos Mangueira declared João Pessoa the second greenest city in the world, after Paris.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/cidade-mais-verde-extremo-oriental-saiba-o-que-e-verdade-sobre-joao-pessoa.ghtml |title=Cidade mais verde? Extremo oriental? Saiba o que é verdade sobre João Pessoa |author=CARNEIRO, Krys |date=2017-08-05 |access-date=2025-04-12 |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://jornaldaparaiba.com.br/meio-ambiente/joao-pessoa-verde-meio-ambiente |title=Mesmo sem título de 2ª cidade mais verde do mundo, capital é sinônimo de meio ambiente |date=2021-08-05 |access-date=2025-04-12 |language=pt-br}}</ref> On December 30, 1992, the municipal master plan was enacted, serving as the basic framework for urban policy.<ref>[[#Bibliography|LIRA]], 2014, p. 28.</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://sapl.joaopessoa.pb.leg.br/media/sapl/public/normajuridica/1992/13013/13013_texto_integral.pdf|title=Plano Diretor da Cidade de João Pessoa|access-date=2022-09-10 |language=pt-br}}</ref> Two days later, Francisco Franca, son of former mayor Damásio Franca, took office, serving until 1996, when Cícero Lucena was elected.

In 1997, the remains of João Pessoa and his wife were transferred from Rio de Janeiro to João Pessoa, interred in a mausoleum built by the state government near the Redemption Palace and the Paraíba Law School.<ref name="fc030818.htm">{{cite web|url=https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/brasil/fc030818.htm|title=Após 67 anos, João Pessoa volta à Paraíba|author=SÁ, Xico|date=1997-08-03|publisher=[[Folha de S.Paulo]]|access-date=2021-10-11|language=pt-br}}</ref> The same year, [[Pope John Paul II]] elevated the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Snows to basilica status after major structural renovations under Bishop Dom Marcelo Carvalheira.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/2017/03/corpo-de-dom-marcelo-e-velado-na-catedral-basilica-de-joao-pessoa.html |title=Corpo de Dom Marcelo é velado na Catedral Basílica de João Pessoa |date=2017-03-27 |access-date=2025-04-12 |language=pt-br}}</ref>

=== 2000s-Present ===
On August 28, 2000, the Mata do Buraquinho was transformed into a botanical garden, named Benjamin Maranhão. On October 1, 2000, Cícero Lucena was re-elected with 74.02% of the vote, becoming the first mayor in João Pessoa's history to win a second term. During his second term, the orla promenade along Cabo Branco and Tambaú Avenues was renovated, and the avenues themselves were widened, along with Avenida Governador Flávio Ribeiro Coutinho (Retão de Manaíra).<ref>[[#Bibliography|LEANDRO]], 2006, p. 128.</ref> At the end of 2003, the Metropolitan Region of João Pessoa (RMJP) was created, initially consisting of the capital and eight municipalities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sapl.al.pb.leg.br/sapl/sapl_documentos/norma_juridica/5579_texto_integral|title=Lei Complementar nº 59, de 30 de dezembro de 2003|author=PARAÍBA (state)|date=2003|language=pt-br}}</ref>

In 2005, Ricardo Coutinho succeeded Lucena, elected in the first round in 2004. In December 2007, the Historic Center of João Pessoa became a national heritage site,<ref name="Não_nomeado-20230316153122">{{Cite web|title=Tombamento do Centro Histórico de João Pessoa completa dez anos|url=https://portalcorreio.com.br/tombamento-do-centro-historico-de-joao-pessoa-faz-dez-anos/|date=2017-12-12|access-date=2019-12-14|language=pt-br}}</ref> and in July 2008, the ''Cabo Branco Science, Culture, and Arts Station'', designed by [[Oscar Niemeyer]], was inaugurated near the [[Cabo Branco Lighthouse]]. Ricardo was re-elected in October 2008, then resigned on March 31, 2010, to run for governor of Paraíba, a position he held from 2011 to 2018.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fgv.br/cpdoc/acervo/dicionarios/verbete-biografico/coutinho-ricardo|title=Ricardo Vieira Coutinho|author=[[Fundação Getulio Vargas]] (FGV)|access-date=2022-09-10|language=pt-br}}</ref>

In August 2012, João Pessoa inaugurated its current convention center, housing the largest theater in the Northeast, the Teatro Pedra do Reino, which opened in August 2015. From 2013 to 2020, the city was administered by Luciano Cartaxo,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/2024/07/02/luciano-cartaxo-lanca-pre-candidatura-a-prefeitura-de-joao-pessoa.ghtml |title=Luciano Cartaxo lança pré-candidatura à prefeitura de João Pessoa |date=2024-07-02 |access-date=2025-01-07|language=pt-br}}</ref> during which the Largo de Tambaú was inaugurated at the boundary of Tambaú and Cabo Branco beaches.<ref>{{cite web|access-date=2025-01-07 |date=2020-09-20 |title=Largo de Tambaú vira ponto de encontro e de lazer na cidade de João Pessoa |url=https://espacopb.com.br/v/largo-de-tambau-vira-ponto-de-encontro-e-de-lazer-na-cidade-de-joao-pessoa|language=pt-br}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> In November 2020, Cícero Lucena was elected for his third term, and in 2024, he was re-elected for a fourth term, becoming the first mayor in the city’s history to achieve this milestone.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2024/Outubro/cicero-lucena-foi-reeleito-prefeito-de-joao-pessoa-pb |title=Cícero Lucena foi reeleito prefeito de João Pessoa (PB) |author=[[Tribunal Superior Eleitoral|TSE]] |date=2024-10-27 |access-date=2025-01-07 |language=pt-br}}</ref>

Finally, on December 17, 2024, the Legislative Assembly of Paraíba approved a constitutional amendment removing the provision for a plebiscite on the city's name, officially confirming ''João Pessoa'' as the capital's name.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/2025/01/03/promulgada-mudanca-na-constituicao-que-oficializa-joao-pessoa-como-nome-da-capital-da-paraiba.ghtml |title=Promulgada mudança na Constituição que oficializa 'João Pessoa' como nome da capital da Paraíba |date=2025-01-03 |access-date=2025-04-12 |language=pt-br}}</ref>
[[File:Centro de convenções de Joao Pessoa.jpg|800px|center|thumb|<div class="center">The João Pessoa Convention Center, named after the poet and politician from Paraíba, Ronaldo Cunha Lima (1936–2012)</div>]]

==Geography==
=== Environment ===
João Pessoa has {{convert|20|km|mi}} of beachfront.

João Pessoa has many green areas distributed among its avenues, parks, and residential neighborhoods, supporting its claim as "the second greenest city in the world" with more than {{convert|7|km2|sqmi}} of forested land, second only to Paris. This claim is a matter of conjecture, however, originating from a publicity stunt carried out by the city's mayor during an [[Earth Summit]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/pb/paraiba/noticia/cidade-mais-verde-extremo-oriental-saiba-o-que-e-verdade-sobre-joao-pessoa.ghtml|title=Cidade mais verde? Extremo oriental? Saiba o que é verdade sobre João Pessoa|date=August 5, 2017 |publisher=[[G1 (website)|G1]]|language=pt-BR}}</ref>

The city has a ''Benjamin Maranhão Botanical Garden'', which preserves the [[Atlantic forest]], the animals, and opens the visitation, which is the João Pessoa Botanical Garden, considered one of the largest remains of the natural Atlantic Forest in an urban area of Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sudema.pb.gov.br/servicos/servicos-ao-publico/jardim-botanico|title=Jardim Botanico|publisher=Sudema|language=pt-BR}}</ref> It is an excellent opportunity for people to be in contact with pure and crystalline nature. Among these, [[environmental education]] is a relevant element, allowing interesting approaches, from knowledge and interest in plants to stimulating curiosity and knowledge of this important space for visitation and research.

There are 18 stations distributed along the Rio and Preguiça trails using as criteria elements mainly related to history, botany and ecology, contributing to knowledge of the environment and the need for its preservation.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.destinoparaiba.pb.gov.br/ondeir/jardim-botanico/|title=Jardim Botanico|publisher=Destination Paraíba|language=pt-BR}}</ref> The area has about 515ha, of which 343ha are home to the Botanical Garden, where the flora is not very soft in the recreational and educational activities promoted on the spot. It includes the largest natural urban Atlantic forest reserve in the country, and an important historical heritage of the capital of [[Paraíba]].

===Hydrography===
In João Pessoa, there are about twelve rivers. The Jaguaribe River is born in the Esplanada complex, crosses the Benjamim Maranhão Botanical Garden, in the middle of the Atlantic Forest, and flows into the Atlantic Ocean on the border with the municipality of Cabedelo. The water to supply the houses is taken from the Gramame-Mumbaba system, from the Paraíba Water and Sewerage Company. In this system, these two rivers take turns to supply the city with water. However, the most historically important river is the Sanhauá River, because it was on its banks that the city was born and where the first houses were built.
<ref>{{cite web|url= http://www.cagepa.pb.gov.br|title=Site da Cagepa}}</ref>

There is also the Solón de Lucena Park Lagoon in the City Center. The lagoon was the main tourist spot in the city during the time when most of the city was far from the beaches. At the end of 2010, during the Christmas celebrations, the lagoon was revitalized and gained devices such as ambient music.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.de.ufpb.br/~ronei/JoaoPessoa/lagoa.htm|title=Parque Solon de Lucena |publisher=Universidade Federal da Paraíba}}</ref>

The capital of Paraíba has a coastline of about 24 kilometers in length, nine beaches in the municipality alone, in addition to the beaches of the Metropolitan Region, such as the city of Cabedelo, the city of Lucena and the District of Jacumã in the municipality of Conde, where the Tambaba Naturist Beach is located. Urban beaches are characterized by white sand beaches and crystal clear waters. Many have preserved the Atlantic Forest, as well as being ideal for bathing thanks to a natural barrier about 6 kilometers from the coast that protects a large part of the Pessoa and Cabedelo coast, allowing children to play in the calm waters. There is the Urban Tartarugas Project, which operates on the beaches of Bessa and Intermares, a spawning area for the hawksbill turtle, a scenario for environmental preservation. Surfing is also practiced in the city.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://viagem.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,barra-de-gramame,70002740964 |title=Barra de Gramame: uma das praias mais cênicas|publisher=Estadão}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://correiodaparaiba.com.br/cidades/joao-pessoa/400-mil-fieis-participam-da-procissao-em-romaria-da-penha-em-joao-pessoa/|title=400 THOUSAND FEIS PARTICIPAM DA PROCISSÃO EM ROMARIA DA PENHA EM JOÃO PESSOA|publisher=Correio da Paraíba|access-date=2021-02-08}}{{Dead link|date=December 2024 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

Among the main beaches, we can mention the ''Praia de Tambau'', composed of fine, beaten sand, with blue-green waters; the ''bessa beach'' is where the caribessa is located, a quiet beach with crystalline waters; also the ''Praia de Manaíra'', a totally urban beach, formed by coral reefs, which makes its waves weak and clear water in summer. It is the point of several kiosks and bars, with sports fields on its edge. The ''Cabo Branco beach'', white sand and warm water; ''Playa Seixas'', which is where the ''most eastern point of America'' is located. In addition to ''Praia da Penha'', where the historic Chapel of Our Lady of Penha, built in [[1763]], where the penha pilgrimage takes place every year, from the historic center to the beach.; The ''Jacarape beach'', where the [[Centro de Convenções Poeta Ronaldo Cunha Lima|Poet Ronaldo Cunha Lima Convention Center]] is located; then ''Praia do Sol'', which is a quiet and airy place; and the ''barra de gramame beach'', which situates the meeting of the river gramme with the sea.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://viagemeturismo.abril.com.br/blog/brasis/10-praias-da-paraiba-que-voce-precisa-conhecer/|title=Praias da Paraíba|publisher=editora abril|access-date=2021-02-08|language=pt-BR}}</ref>

===Climate===
João Pessoa has a [[tropical monsoon climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] ''Am'') with very warm to hot temperatures all year long and strong rainfall in most of the months; however, October to December have rather low rainfall.

==Demographics==
According to the [[2022 Brazilian census|2022 census]], João Pessoa had a population of 833,932. Its racial makeup was 50.6% [[Pardo]] (two or more races), 39.7% [[White Brazilian|White]], 9.2% [[Afro-Brazilian|Black]], 0.2% [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|Amerindian]] and 0.1% [[Asian Brazilian|Asian]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Censo 2022 – Panorama |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/}}</ref>

===Rail system===
The [[Sistema de Trens Urbanos de João Pessoa|João Pessoa Urban Trains System]] is operated by [[Diesel engine|diesel]] components on one railway line with a 30&nbsp;km extension spanning four municipalities, João Pessoa, Cabedelo, Bayeux and Santa Rita, constituted by the Cabedelo stretch, with 10 stations in operation, carrying around 10,100 passengers a day. Composed of three locomotives and 17 passenger cars, the trains form two compositions that conduct 28 trips a day.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cbtu.gov.br/index.php/pt/sistemas-cbtu/joao-pessoa/|title=João Pessa|publisher=CBTU-Brazilian company of urban trains|access-date=August 29, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180830041343/https://www.cbtu.gov.br/index.php/pt/sistemas-cbtu/joao-pessoa/|archive-date=August 30, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Culture==
The Historic Center of João Pessoa, recognized as [[National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage]] in December 2007,<ref name="Prefeitura de Joao Pessoa">{{cite web | author=Prefeitura de Joao Pessoa | title= João Pessoa National Heritage
 |url=http://www.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/secretarias/copacjp/patrimonio/ | access-date=March 19, 2023|date=July 27, 2022}}</ref> covers areas in the neighborhoods of the center, Róger, Jaguaribe, Tambiá and Varadouro. There are [[List of National Historic Heritage sites of Brazil|listed]] 37 hectares in area and estimated around 700 buildings, in addition to streets, squares and historic parks that integrate this set, comprising most of the neighborhoods of Varadouro and the Center of the city. Its buildings make up a scenario of different styles and eras full of townhouses, squares, colonial houses and secular churches, being considered the main architectural collection of Paraíba, reporting the various phases of local history, and one of the largest and most important historical sites in<ref name="Prefeitura de Joao Pessoa"/> Brazil.<ref>{{cite web | author=Prefeitura de Joao Pessoa | title= Tourism in João Pessoa – Historic Center |url=http://www.hotelguarani.com.br/turismo-joao-person-center-historico.php | access-date=March 19, 2023|date=}}</ref>

The delimited area has assets that represent various periods in the history of João Pessoa, such as the [[baroque]] of the Igreja da Ordem Terceira de São Francisco; from the [[rococo]] of the [[Church and Convent of the Third Order of Our Lady of Carmo]], [[Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of the Snows, João Pessoa|Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of the Snows]]; of the [[Neoclassical architecture]] of the [[Church of Saint Peter Gonzalez]], [[Church and Convent of Our Lady of the Rosary (João Pessoa)|Church and Convent of Our Lady of the Rosary]], of the [[Baroque architecture]] of the [[São Francisco Cultural Center]], [[Monastery of St. Benedict (João Pessoa)|Monastery of St. Benedict]], of the [[Mannerist style]] of the [[Church of Our Lady of Mercy (João Pessoa)|Church of Our Lady of Mercy]], all from the 17th century;<ref>{{cite web | author=José Chrispiniano | title= João Pessoa Historic Center is listed as a heritage site
 |url=http://www2.uol.com.br/historiaviva/noticias/centro_historico_de_joao_pessoa_e_tombado.html | access-date=March 19, 2023|date=}}</ref> of the [[colonial architecture of Brazil|colonial architecture]] and [[Eclecticism in architecture|eclectic architecture]] of civil houses, in addition to [[Art Nouveau]] and [[Art Deco]], from the [[1920s|20s]] and [[1930s|30s]] decades, predominant in square Antenor Navarro and in the former Hotel Globo, now transformed into a cultural center.<ref>{{cite web | author=Brasil Sabor | title= The historic center of João Pessoa
 |url=http://brasilsabor.com.br/por/roteiros/article/327 | access-date=March 19, 2023|date=}}</ref>

===Diving===
The [[coastline]] of João Pessoa includes the ''Alvarenga'' shipwreck, an artificial reef for developing marine life.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://turismo.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/o-que-fazer/esportes/surf/|title=mergulho|access-date=October 2, 2018|publisher=Secretary of Tourism of the Municipality of João Pessoa|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181003014317/https://turismo.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/o-que-fazer/esportes/surf/|archive-date=October 3, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

* '''Alvarenga Shipwreck''' : The Alvarenga was a vessel used to transport supplies to ships. It wrecked about 9.6&nbsp;km from the tip of '''''Bessa's Beach''''' and is submerged {{convert|20|m|ft}} deep. It remains whole, {{convert|20|m|ft|abbr=on}} in length by {{convert|5|m|ft|abbr=on}} in beam. It is possible for divers to penetrate the small bow and stern [[compartment (ship)|compartment]]s safely. On the prow the winch to hoist the anchor remains in view. It is also common to find large stingrays and shoals of fish, such as the top cock and the hook.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://turismo.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/o-que-fazer/esportes/surf/|title=Naufrágio Alvarenga|access-date=October 2, 2018|publisher=Secretary of Tourism of the Municipality of João Pessoa|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181003014317/https://turismo.joaopessoa.pb.gov.br/o-que-fazer/esportes/surf/|archive-date=October 3, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>
