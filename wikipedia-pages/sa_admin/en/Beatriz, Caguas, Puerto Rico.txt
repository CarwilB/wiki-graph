'''Beatriz''' is a [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrio]] in the municipality of [[Caguas, Puerto Rico]]. Its population in 2020 was 3,281.<ref name="Pico_1969">{{cite book |last1=Picó |first1=Rafael |last2=Buitrago de Santiago |first2=Zayda |last3=Berrios |first3=Hector H. |title=Nueva geografía de Puerto Rico: física, económica, y social, por Rafael Picó. Con la colaboración de Zayda Buitrago de Santiago y Héctor H. Berrios. |url=https://archive.org/details/nuevageografad00pic/page/247|publisher=San Juan Editorial Universitaria, Universidad de Puerto Rico,1969}}</ref><ref name="Law2015">{{cite book|author=Gwillim Law|title=Administrative Subdivisions of Countries: A Comprehensive World Reference, 1900 through 1998| url =https://books.google.com/books?id=nXCeCQAAQBAJ|accessdate=25 December 2018|date=20 May 2015|publisher=McFarland|isbn=978-1-4766-0447-3|page=300}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Census profile: Beatriz barrio, Caguas Municipio, PR |url=http://censusreporter.org/profiles/06000US7202507152-beatriz-barrio-caguas-municipio-pr/ |access-date=2023-08-14 |website=Census Reporter |language=en}}</ref>

==Geography==
Beatriz is located at the coordinates {{coord|18|10|23|N|66|4|37|W}}. According to the [[United States Census Bureau]], Beatriz has a total area of 15.89&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>, of which 15.89&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> is land and (0.03%) 0.01&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> is water.<ref name="2010 Census">{{cite book |url=https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/gpo35934/cph-2-53.pdf |title=Puerto Rico: 2010 Population and Housing Unit Counts.pdf |publisher=U.S. Dept. of Commerce, Economics and Statistics Administration, U.S. Census Bureau |year=2010}}</ref> Beatriz is located in the [[Sierra de Cayey]], a subrange of the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]] mountain range, making it one of the ''barrios'' of Caguas which are not located in the [[Caguas Valley]]. The name of the barrio comes from the Doña Beatriz Creek which crosses it and flows into the [[Turabo River]].

== Sectors and demographics ==
Barrios (which are, in contemporary times, roughly comparable to [[minor civil divisions]])<ref name="Barrio-Pueblo">{{cite web |title=US Census Barrio-Pueblo definition |url=https://factfinder.census.gov/help/en/barrio.htm |website=factfinder.com |publisher=US Census |accessdate=5 January 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170513190743/https://factfinder.census.gov/help/en/barrio.htm |archive-date=13 May 2017 |url-status=dead }}</ref> in turn are further subdivided into smaller local populated place areas/units called '''sectores''' (''sectors'' in English). The types of ''sectores'' may vary, from normally ''sector'' to ''urbanización'' to ''reparto'' to ''barriada'' to ''residencial'', among others.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.presupuesto.pr.gov/Presupuesto2015-2016/PresupuestosAgencias/229.htm|title=Agencia: Oficina del Coordinador General para el Financiamiento Socioeconómico y la Autogestión (Proposed 2016 Budget) |website= Puerto Rico Budgets|language=es|accessdate=28 June 2019}}</ref><ref>{{Citation | author1=Rivera Quintero, Marcia | title=El vuelo de la esperanza: Proyecto de las Comunidades Especiales Puerto Rico, 1997-2004 | publication-date=2014 | publisher=San Juan, Puerto Rico Fundación Sila M. The following sectors are in Beatriz:
{{lang|es|Alturas de Beatriz, Alturas de Caguas (Beatriz), Barrio Beatriz, Colinas de Villa Coquí, La Jurado, Las Abejas, Los Ortíz, Los Panes, Muñoz Grillo, Piñas I, Piñas II, Piñas III, and Villa Paolo.}}<ref name="sectores">{{cite web |title=Lista Sectores por Barrios Separados|url=https://caguas.gov.pr/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Lista-Sectores-por-Barrios-Separados.pdf|website=Municipio Autónomo de Caguas Oficina de Planificación Unidad de Información Geográfica y Estadísticas Vitales|access-date=7 January 2023|language=es}}</ref>

According to the 2010 Census, there were 4,353 people residing in Beatriz. The population density was 27,391 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>. Of 4,353 inhabitants, Beatriz was composed of 68.76% Whites, 13% were Black, 0.62% were American Indian, 0.05% were Asian, 0.02% were Pacific Islanders, 12.27% were of other races and 5.28% belonged to two or more races. In all, the population was 99.47% Hispanic or Latino.<ref name="2010 Census" />

==History==
Beatriz was in Spain's [[gazetteer]]s<ref>{{cite web | title=Anuario del comercio, de la industria, de la magistratura y de la administración. 1881 | website=Biblioteca Nacional de España | url=https://hemerotecadigital.bne.es/hd/es/viewer?id=9047075f-3c7e-411c-9e1e-41a2f650e1ba&page=1587 | language=es |page=1614| access-date=4 April 2023}}</ref> until Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became an [[Insular area|unincorporated territory of the United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Beatriz barrio was 868.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n253 162]| language=es}}</ref>

The area was mostly made up of subsistence farms in the 19th century. Botanist [[William Edwin Safford]] visited Beatriz in 1898 to document the local plant life.<ref>{{cite journal|author1=William Edwin Safford|title=Notes of a naturalist afloat|journal=American Fern Journal|date=April 1912|volume=2|issue=2|page=40}}</ref>

The [[1899 San Ciriaco hurricane]] devastated Beatriz, and many farmers began working for the newly established [[History of Puerto Rico#United States rule (1898–present)|military government]] of the United States of America building roads.<ref>{{cite book|author1=Rosa E. Carrasquillo|title=Our Landless Patria: Marginal Citizenship and Race in Caguas, Puerto Rico|date=2006|publisher=University of Nebraska Press|isbn=0803215371|page=86}}</ref> At the time, a store owned by wealthy landowner Don Ramón Álvarez was the center of economic life in Beatriz. In 1900, one of the store's cashiers Don Demetrio López was elected [[alcalde]] and comisario of Beatriz.<ref>{{cite book|author1=Rosa E. Carrasquillo|title=Our Landless Patria: Marginal Citizenship and Race in Caguas, Puerto Rico|date=2006|publisher=University of Nebraska Press|isbn=0803215371|page=32}}</ref>

Beatriz barrio was hit by [[Hurricane Maria]] on 20 September 2017 and many residents still did not have electricity as of March 2018, six months later.

== Landmarks and places of interest ==

* [[Carretera Central (Puerto Rico)|Carretera Central]], the historic road connecting [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] in the north to [[Ponce, Puerto Rico|Ponce]] in the south passes through Beatriz.
* Centro Los Panes, a [[Community centre|community center]] near La Jurado sector.
* [[Cerro Las Piñas]], a hill located in the boundary with [[Beatriz, Cayey, Puerto Rico|Beatriz, Cayey]] which offers panoramic views of the [[Caguas Valley]] and its surrounding mountains.
