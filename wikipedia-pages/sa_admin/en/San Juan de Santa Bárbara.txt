'''San Juan''' is a [[Districts of Costa Rica|district]] of the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]] canton, in the [[Heredia Province|Heredia]] province of [[Costa Rica]]. The district consists of two major neighborhoods: San Juan Arriba (Upper San Juan), and San Juan Abajo (Lower San Juan).<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |lang=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref>

==History==
Like the rest of the canton, before the Spanish settlers came, Santa Bárbara was originally occupied by the Huetares, an indigenous tribe. The Huetare King, Cacique Garabito, dominated the area.<ref name=RHC>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es|archive-date=22 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522195138/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|url-status=dead}}</ref> When the Spanish arrived, they originally called the area Churruca or Surruco.<ref name=centenario>{{cite book|title=Centenario del cantón Santa Bárbara de Heredia|year=1982|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/notas/seo.php/item.49/centenario-del-canton-santa-barbara-heredia.html|language=es}}{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]], [[Barva District|Barva]], and [[Alajuela (city)|Alajuela]], three neighboring cities, were populated and settled in the late 1700s. As trade increased between the three cities, the canton developed.<ref name=centenario />

In 1836, an Englishman named John Hale sold his land to residents of what is now San Juan.<ref name=RHC2>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=1|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140523012322/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=1|archivedate=23 May 2014}}</ref> The land was bought using profits from the sugarcane that was grown at the time.<ref name=RHC2 /> On 7 December 1848, the fourth canton of Heredia was created, with San Juan as a founding district.<ref name=RHC3>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=2|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140523011151/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133&limitstart=2|archivedate=23 May 2014}}</ref>

In 1852, Horacio Morales helped build the first chapel in San Juan, although many parishioners still went to Santa Bárbara. By 1885, the district educated as many boys as girls, with schools for both.<ref name=centenario />

San Juan inaugurated the canton's first [[hydroelectric plant]] in 1914 along the Porrosatí River that separates the districts of Santa Bárbara and San Juan. During [[Ricardo Jiménez Oreamuno]] second term in office, between 1924 and 1928, water delivery systems were installed in San Juan using water from the Potrerillos River.<ref name=RHC3 />

==Economy==
San Juan's main economic activity is agricultural production, particularly [[Coffee production in Costa Rica|coffee]].<ref name=centenario /> In 1973, 1,074 kilograms of coffee were produced in San Juan. Because of a sawmill in the district, another important activity was lumber production.<ref name=centenario />

There are various commercial businesses in San Juan, including restaurants and corner stores.<ref name="sbd">{{cite web |title=Santa Bárbara Digital |url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/ |accessdate=20 May 2014 |archive-date=21 May 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140521031649/http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/directorio/ |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Láscarez|first=Carlos|title=Tenían a menor como esclava|url=http://wvw.aldia.cr/ad_ee/2012/octubre/09/sucesos3348092.html|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Al Dia|date=9 October 2012|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> There is a coffee processing facility in San Juan which serves many local farmers.<ref>{{cite news|last=Barquero|first=Marvin|title=Amenazan con liquidar La Meseta|url=http://wvw.nacion.com/ln_ee/2004/junio/16/economia1.html|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> San Juan is locally known for a [[ceviche]] restaurant near its central plaza.<ref name="sbd" />

==Culture and education==
San Juan was featured in an episode of [[Repretel: Canal 11|Repretel's]] "Informe Once" (Eleven Report).<ref name="informe 11">{{cite AV media |people= |year=2010 |title=De Paseo por San Juan de Santa Barbara |trans-title=Visiting San Juan de Santa Barbara |medium=Analog VHF |language=Es |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaUF2VFGpB4 |accessdate=27 May 2014 |format= |time= |location=San Jose |publisher=Repretel, Canal 11 |id= |isbn= |oclc= |quote= |ref= }}</ref> The episode described several cottage industries in the district, including an agricultural producer and an ice-cream maker, as well as a country house in the traditional Costa Rican adobe and cane style.<ref name="informe 11" />

There are two private schools in San Juan: Colegio Bilingüe Nueva Esperanza (New Hope Bilingual High School), where 590 students attended in 2009, and Puente Verde (Green Bridge).<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> Colegio Bilingüe Nueva Esperanza, founded in 1993,<ref>{{cite web|title=Historia de Nueva Esperanza|url=http://www.nuevaesperanza.co.cr/index.php/es/template-layout/historia|publisher=Centro Eductativo Bilingüe Nueva Esperanza|accessdate=28 May 2014|language=Es|format=School website|year=2014}}</ref> was the first private technical school in the country, meaning students received specialized in computer skills.<ref>{{cite news|last=Mena|first=Fabio|title=Primer colegio técnico privado del país inicia lecciones en Heredia|url=http://www.crhoy.com/primer-colegio-tecnico-privado-del-pais-inicia-lecciones-en-heredia-w5l7l9x/|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Costa Rica Hoy|date=6 February 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Jiménez Badilla|first=Eillyn|title=Tiquicia estrena "cole" técnico privado|url=http://www.diarioextra.com/Dnew/noticiaDetalle/224688|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Diario Extra|date=7 February 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> The main public elementary school is Tranquilino Saenz Rojas. Students from San Juan usually attend Colegio Santa Bárbara High School,<ref name="MEPSB">Ministerio de Educación Pública: [http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara Santa Bárbara | Ministerio de Educación Pública] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140520215916/http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara |date=2014-05-20 }}, accessdate: 27 May 2014</ref> but may also go to the high schools in [[Alajuela]]. In 2012, resident Noelia Villalobos Solórzano received the [[Panama]]nian Rubén Darío Prize for her work in education.<ref>{{cite news|last=Ramirez|first=K.|title=Estudiante de CEU de Heredia recibirá Premio Rubén Darío|url=http://www.uned.ac.cr/acontecer/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1506:estudiante-de-ceu-de-heredia-recibira-premio-ruben-dario&catid=51:gestion-universitaria&Itemid=76|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Acontecer Digital|date=29 August 2012|language=Es}}</ref>

==Administration==
San Juan is served by the police force of [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Láscarez S.|first=Carlos|title=Balacera dentro de vivienda deja dos muertos en San Juan de Santa Bárbara de Heredia|url=http://www.nacion.com/sucesos/crimenes-asaltos/Balacera-vivienda-Santa-Barbara-Heredia_0_1414858758.html|accessdate=27 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=16 May 2014|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> An EBAIS, [[Costa Rican Social Security Fund|Equipo Básico de Atención Integral en Salud (Basic Medical Care Center)]], is located in San Juan, serving residents and non-residents of the district.<ref name="sbd" /> In addition, there is a private [[nursing home]] located in the district.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

The canton has investigated the possibility of making [[microbusiness]] outreach to the residents of San Juan, especially toward women and artisans.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> The canton's first priority, however, is to build a police station within the district.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

==Geography==
San Juan has an area of {{formatnum:4.45}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=16 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200916045223/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:1045}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

It has the lowest elevation of the canton. It is directly south of the municipality of [[Santa Bárbara de Heredia]], divided into two main neighborhoods: San Juan Arriba (Upper San Juan), and San Juan Abajo (Lower San Juan). In addition, there are other neighborhoods: Cinco Esquinas (Five Points), Villa Margarita, Tierra Santa (Holy Land), Calle Zapote (Zapote Street), and several developments.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|accessdate=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Routes 123 and 119 pass through San Juan.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /><ref>{{cite news|title=Paso regulado en la rutas 123 y 5 de Heredia|url=http://www.crhoy.com/paso-regulado-en-la-rutas-123-y-5-de-heredia/|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Costa Rica Hoy|date=19 July 2013|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref>

The Zanjón River, Quebrada la Claudia, and Porrosatí River run through San Juan.<ref name=centenario /> During the rainy season, San Juan is subject to flooding, as was the case in May 2014 when more than 100 houses were affected by clogged drainage systems.<ref>{{cite news|last=Rojas|first=Pablo|title=Aguaceros y alcantarillas llenas de basura propiciaron daños en decenas de viviendas|url=http://www.crhoy.com/aguaceros-y-alcantarillas-llenas-de-basura-propiciaron-danos-en-decenas-de-viviendas-u9l7m3x/|accessdate=28 May 2014|newspaper=Costa Rica Hoy|date=9 May 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> Like much of Costa Rica, San Juan is part of a high-risk zone, according to the Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos (National Risk Prevention Commission).<ref>{{cite web|title=AMENAZAS NATURALES CANTON DE SANTA BARBARA|url=http://www.cne.go.cr/Atlas%20de%20Amenazas/STABARBARA.htm|work=Government report|publisher=La Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos y Atención de Emergencias (CNE)|accessdate=28 May 2014|location=Alajuela, Costa Rica|language=Es}}</ref>
