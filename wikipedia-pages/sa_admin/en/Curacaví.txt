'''Curacaví''' ({{IPA|es|ku.ɾa.ka.ˈβi}}) is a [[List of cities in Chile|city]] and [[Communes of Chile|commune]] in the [[Melipilla Province]] of central [[Chile]]'s [[Santiago Metropolitan Region]]. Its climate is temperate Mediterranean with a long dry season, experiencing high temperatures in summer and low temperatures in winter. Curacaví is located on the [[Chile Route 68|Ruta 68]] between the coastal conurbation of [[Viña del Mar]] and [[Valparaíso]] and [[Santiago]] which has proved a popular home for expats working in Santiago.

Curacaví is a [[Communes of Chile|commune]] situated between the hills of the coastal mountain range in central [[Chile]], located in the Metropolitan Region of Santiago, specifically in [[Melipilla]] Province, with an area of 693&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> and borders the following municipalities: [[Casablanca, Chile|Casablanca]], [[Quilpué]], [[Lampa, Chile|Lampa]], [[Pudahuel]], [[Maipú, Chile|Maipú]], [[Padre Hurtado]], [[Melipilla]], and [[Maria Pinto]].

Curacaví is integrated along with the communes of [[Talagante]], [[Melipilla]], [[Peñaflor, Chile|Peñaflor]], [[Isla de Maipo]], [[Maria Pinto]], [[El Monte, Chile|El Monte]], [[San Pedro, Chile|San Pedro]] and [[Alhué]] in the number 31 Electoral District and also belongs to the 7th Chilean Senate District ([[Santiago west]]). The commune covers five districts: Curacaví, [[Bustamente, Chile|Bustamante]], [[Lo Prado]], [[Zapata, Chile|Zapata]] and [[Caren, Chile|Caren]].

==Rapid Expansion==
Curacaví is now home to various exclusive [http://www.lomasdelpuangue.cl/] [[gated communities]] called [[condominios]][https://web.archive.org/web/20150721232820/http://www.reservalaaurora.cl/proyecto.html]. These large luxury developments range from 500 hectares upwards with each individual property starting at around 1 hectare. The development of the condominios began in the 1990s but has accelerated dramatically in recent years. The area is therefore home to increasing numbers of wealthy [[Santiago]] commuters who are both Chilean and foreign. The momentum is largely due to fast transport links and a better quality of life in the face of Santiago's increasing pollution problems. Fast commuting times are now offered through the [[Chile Route 68|Ruta 68]] and the [[List of tunnels in Chile|Lo Prado tunnel]] which offers journey times of 35 minutes to Santiago.

==Geography and climate==
Immersed among the hills of the coastal mountain range, Curacaví occupies an area of {{convert|693.2|sqkm|0|abbr=on}}<ref name="INE"/> and borders the following communes: [[Casablanca, Chile|Casablanca]], [[Quilpué]], [[Lampa, Chile|Lampa]], [[Pudahuel]], [[Maipú, Chile|Maipú]], [[Padre Hurtado]], [[Melipilla]], and María Pinto. The climate is mild Mediterranean with a long dry season with high temperatures in the summer and low temperatures in the winter.

==Demographics==
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Curacaví spans an area of {{convert|693.2|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 24,298 inhabitants (12,351 men and 11,947 women). Of these, 15,645 (64.4%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 8,653 (35.6%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 27.5% (5,245 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/> There is a fast growing expat community in Curacaví [http://www.allchile.net/chileforum/viewtopic.php?t=11053] [http://southernpacificreview.com/category/curacavi/] [https://web.archive.org/web/20150128113739/https://www.dollarvigilante.com/blog/2012/11/10/the-weekend-vigilante-november-10th-2012.html]

==Administration==
As a [[Communes of Chile|commune]], Curacaví is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2012–2016 alcalde is Juan Pablo Barros Basso (IND), and the council members are:<ref name="Official"/>
* Lidia Araos Henriquez ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]])
* Cristian Galdames Santibañez ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]])
* Paul Alvarado Muñoz ([[Christian Democratic Party (Chile)|PDC]])
* Marco Antonio Guzmán Márquez ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]])
* Sandra Ponce Baeza ([[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]])
* Francisco Carrasco González ([[Communist Party of Chile|PC]])

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Curacaví is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Denise Pascal ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]]) and Gonzalo Uriarte (UDI) as part of the 31st electoral district, (together with [[Talagante]], [[Peñaflor, Chile|Peñaflor]], [[El Monte, Chile|El Monte]], [[Isla de Maipo]], [[Melipilla]], [[María Pinto]], [[Alhué]], [[San Pedro, Chile|San Pedro]] and [[Padre Hurtado]]). The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Guido Girardi Lavín ([[Party for Democracy (Chile)|PPD]]) and Jovino Novoa Vásquez (UDI) as part of the 7th senatorial constituency (Santiago-West).

==Road from Santiago to Valparaiso==

Curacaví is on the most-used old [[horse trail]] between Valparaiso and Santiago which had been formed during the [[Inca]] period. Since 1553 it was called the royal highway. The old road crossed [[Casablanca, Chile|Casablanca]], the [[Zapata, Chile|Zapata]] road and Curacaví finally entering [[Santiago]] on the road of [[San Pablo, Chile|San Pablo]]. The original royal highway was made between the years 1792 and 1797 however [[Ambrosio O'Higgins]] changed the old road by the current [[Lo Prado]] route.

=== Mercedes Canal ===

{{See also|José Manuel Balmaceda|Mercedes Canal}}

The Mercedes Canal (Spanish: ''[[Canal de las Mercedes]]''), which irrigates the whole valley of Curacavi was built by [[Jose Manuel Balmaceda]] before becoming president. The [[canal]] was started in 1854 and finished 30 years later with a total length of 120&nbsp;km and with a [[Volumetric flow rate|flow rate]] 10 m³/s.

==Parish School==
The first Parish School was built by the priest [[Don Eduardo Miles]] in 1885 which is still working and has done so almost without interruption educating much of the local population.
