'''San Felipe''' is the newest [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Baja California]]. Inaugurated on 1 January 2022, it is located approximately {{convert|190|km}} south of [[Mexicali]], the state capital.

==Geography==
[[File:Devils-Peak Sierra-SanPedroMartir BajaCalifornia Mexico.jpg|thumb|280px|[[Picacho del Diablo]], the highest point in Baja California, in the [[Sierra de San Pedro Mártir]]]]
[[File:Mountains01-Sierra SanPedroMartir-BajaCalifornia-Mexico.jpg|thumb|280px|right|Flora on the east side of the Sierra de San Pedro Mártir]]
[[File:San-Felipe Sierra-SanPedroMartir BajaCalifornia Mexico.jpg|thumb|280px|right|View of the San Felipe Desert and the Gulf of California from the Sierra de San Pedro Mártir]]
The municipality of San Felipe lies on the northeastern side of the [[Baja California Peninsula]]. It borders the municipalities of [[Mexicali Municipality|Mexicali]] to the north, [[Ensenada Municipality|Ensenada]] to the northwest, [[San Quintín Municipality|San Quintín]] to the west and south, and the [[Gulf of California]] to the east. The municipality also comprises various islands and islets in the Gulf, such as [[Roca Consag]] and the Islas Encantadas, e.g., [[Isla Coloradito]], [[Isla El Muerto]], [[Isla Encantada]], [[Isla Roca Lobos|Isla Lobos]], and [[Isla San Luis]]. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|10808|km2}}.<ref name=opinion>{{cite web |url=https://www.congresobc.gob.mx/Documentos/ProcesoParlamentario/Dictamenes/20210519_94_GOBERNACION.pdf |title=Dictamen No. 94 |language=es |publisher=[[Congress of Baja California]] |date=19 May 2021 |accessdate=7 January 2022}}</ref>

The municipality is situated in the [[San Felipe Desert]], which is located in the [[rain shadow]] formed by the [[Sierra de San Pedro Mártir]] to its west, and is the most arid region of North America.<ref name=desert>{{cite journal |last1=Peinado |first1=M. |last2=Macias |first2=M. Á.|last3=Delgadillo |first3=J. |last4=Aguirre |first4=J. L. |date=2006 |title=Major plant communities of North America's most arid region: The San Felipe Desert, Baja California, México |journal=Plant Biosystems |url=https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/11263500600947715 |volume=140 |issue=3 |pages=280–296 |access-date=7 January 2022 |doi=10.1080/11263500600947715|s2cid=85271382 |url-access=subscription }}</ref> [[Picacho del Diablo]], the highest point in Baja California at {{convert|3095|m}}, lies within the municipality.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.peakbagger.com/peak.aspx?pid=1537 |title=Picacho del Diablo |website=Peakbagger |access-date=7 January 2022 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200908222339/https://www.peakbagger.com/peak.aspx?pid=1537 |archive-date=8 September 2020}}</ref> Average annual precipitation rarely exceeds {{convert|70|mm}}.<ref name=desert/>

The southern part of the municipality extends into the [[Valle de los Cirios]] wildlife protection area, and the coastline of the northern part of the municipality is protected as part of the [[Alto Golfo de California Biosphere Reserve]].<ref name=desert/>

==History==
The [[Kiliwa people]] are the indigenous inhabitants of the area. Historically, they lived in the Sierra de San Pedro Mártir and made annual excursions to San Felipe Bay, where fresh water was available, to supplement their diet with seafood. At San Felipe Bay they gathered clams and mussels, and caught fish using a stone weir built across the entrance to the bay, as well as with hook and line.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=Terich |first=Thomas Anthony |chapter-url=https://mexfish.com/sflp/sflp/terich/terich05.htm |chapter=Chapter V: Early Indians of San Felipe |title=San Felipe, Baja California: A Study of the Evolution of Site Utility in a Mexican Fishing Village |date=January 1968 |degree=MA |publisher=[[California State University, Los Angeles|California State College at Los Angeles]] |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>

San Felipe Bay was named in 1721 by the expedition of the Jesuit missionary Pedro de Ugarte, brother of [[Juan de Ugarte]]. [[Fernando Consag]] made the first description of the bay in 1746, and [[Wenceslaus Linck]] became the first European to reach San Felipe by land in 1766.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=Terich |first=Thomas Anthony |chapter-url=https://mexfish.com/sflp/sflp/terich/terich06.htm |chapter=Chapter VI: Exploration of Northern Baja California and the upper Gulf |title=San Felipe, Baja California: A Study of the Evolution of Site Utility in a Mexican Fishing Village |date=January 1968 |degree=MA |publisher=California State College at Los Angeles |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>

Permanent settlement of San Felipe Bay began sometime around 1910–1915 with the establishment of fisheries for [[totoaba]] and later shrimp. A highway connecting San Felipe to Mexicali was built in 1950.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=Terich |first=Thomas Anthony |chapter-url=https://mexfish.com/sflp/sflp/terich/terich10.htm |chapter=Chapter X: Permanent settlement |title=San Felipe, Baja California: A Study of the Evolution of Site Utility in a Mexican Fishing Village |date=January 1968 |degree=MA |publisher=California State College at Los Angeles |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref> [[Diesel generator]]s were installed in the village in 1958,<ref>{{cite thesis |last=Terich |first=Thomas Anthony |chapter-url=https://mexfish.com/sflp/sflp/terich/terich11.htm |chapter=Chapter XI: San Felipe today |title=San Felipe, Baja California: A Study of the Evolution of Site Utility in a Mexican Fishing Village |date=January 1968 |degree=MA |publisher=California State College at Los Angeles |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref> and pipelines for drinking water in 1967.<ref name=opinion/>

After citizens demanded the creation of a new municipality for many years,<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Espejel |first1=Ileana |last2=Hernández |first2=Alberto |last3=Riemann |first3=Hugo |last4=Hernández |first4=Laura |year=2005 |title=Propuesta para un nuevo municipio con base en las cuencas hidrográficas |journal=Gestión y Política Pública |url=http://www.gestionypoliticapublica.cide.edu/num_anteriores/Vol.XIV_NoI_1ersem/Espejel.pdf |volume=14 |issue=1 |pages=129–168 |language=es |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>  the [[Congress of Baja California]] approved the creation of the municipality of San Felipe from territories previously part of the ''delegación'' of San Felipe in the municipality of Mexicali, and the ''delegación'' of [[Puertecitos]] in the municipality of Ensenada.<ref>{{cite web |last=Flores |first=Gerson |url=https://www.ensenada.net/noticias/nota.php?id=64845 |title=Se publicó municipalización de San Felipe |language=es |date=2 July 2021 |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref> The new municipality came into force on 1 January 2022.<ref>{{cite press release |author=<!--Not stated--> |title=FELICITA MARINA DEL PILAR A SAN FELIPE, SÉPTIMO MUNICIPIO DE BAJA CALIFORNIA |url=http://www.bajacalifornia.gob.mx/Prensa/Noticia/2603 |location=San Felipe |publisher=Government of Baja California |date=1 January 2022 |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>

==Administration==
San Felipe will conduct its first municipal elections in 2024. A transitional administration was appointed by the state in August 2021. Led by its president José Luis Dagnino López, it began operations on 1 January 2022.<ref>{{cite web |date=12 August 2021 |url=https://www.lavozdelafrontera.com.mx/local/jose-luis-dagnino-presidira-consejo-fundacional-de-san-felipe-7077961.html |title=José Luis Dagnino presidirá Consejo Fundacional de San Felipe |language=es |website=La Voz de la Frontera |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>

==Demographics==
San Felipe is the least populous municipality in Baja California. The areas which now make up the municipality recorded a population of 18,369 inhabitants in the 2010 Mexican Census.<ref name=opinion/> Most of the population lives in the municipal seat of [[San Felipe, Baja California|San Felipe]] which recorded 16,702 inhabitants in 2010,<ref name=opinion/> and 17,143 inhabitants in 2020.<ref name=panorama>{{cite web |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/702825197735.pdf |title=Panorama sociodemográfico de Baja California. Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 |page=12 |language=es |publisher=INEGI |date=2021 |accessdate=7 January 2022}}</ref> There are also 130 inhabited rural localities in the municipality.<ref name=opinion/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
Important economic activities in San Felipe include commerce, tourism, real estate, and fishing. San Felipe receives approximately 250,000 visitors per year, including a significant number of American and Canadian retirees.<ref name=opinion/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://sanfelipe.com.mx/about-san-felipe/description/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120314153918/http://sanfelipe.com.mx/about-san-felipe/description/ |url-status=usurped |archive-date=March 14, 2012 |title=About San Felipe |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>

The municipality is served by [[Mexican Federal Highway 3|Federal Highway 3]], which connects it to [[Ensenada, Baja California|Ensenada]] in the northwest, and [[Mexican Federal Highway 5|Federal Highway 5]], which connects it to Mexicali in the north and [[Mexican Federal Highway 1|Federal Highway 1]] in the south. The port of San Felipe is located just south of the municipal seat and serves both commercial and recreational boats. [[San Felipe International Airport]] is located in the municipality, but currently has no commercial service.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://digaohm.semar.gob.mx/cuestionarios/cnarioSanfelipe.pdf |title=San Felipe, Baja California |language=es |publisher=DIGAOHM |access-date=7 January 2022}}</ref>
