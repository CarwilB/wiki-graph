'''Estelí''' ({{IPA|es|esteˈli}}), officially '''Villa de San Antonio de Pavia de Estelí''' is a city and [[Municipalities of Nicaragua|municipality]] within the [[Estelí Department|Estelí department]]. It is the 8th largest city in [[Nicaragua]] due to the high urbanization of its municipality, at 84.8%, with an urban population of 111,244 (2022 estimate).<ref>[https://bcn.gob.ni/publicaciones/cartograf%C3%ADa-digital-y-censo-de-edificaciones Banco Central de Nicaragua - Cartografía Digital y Censo de Edificaciones en Nicaragua]</ref> It is also the eighth-largest municipality,<ref>[https://www.citypopulation.de/en/nicaragua/cities/ Citypopulation.de] Population of the major cities in Nicaragua</ref> and an active commercial center in the north, known as "the Diamond of the Segovia" (this name being created by Oscar Corea Molina in his radio show "Trampolin 43”) and the de facto capital of the north.

Estelí is on the [[Pan-American Highway]], 150&nbsp;km north of [[Managua]]. It enjoys a temperate climate most of the year due to its location in the north central highlands at a mean elevation of 844&nbsp;m (2769&nbsp;ft) above sea level.  The city is also surrounded by forested mountains of pines, oaks, and walnuts, and plateaus up to 1600 m above sea level, some of which are protected as natural reserves.

==History==

The first settlement of what would become the City of Estelí occurred in Villa Vieja in 1685. It was founded by a group of Spaniards fleeing Nueva Segovia which at that time suffered from pirate attacks. Villa Vieja was then replaced by a new settlement (San Antonio de Pavia) from where the city of Estelí has grown. Today there is evidence of the first church in the Villa Vieja sector of the city.<ref>Anuario de estudios centroamericanos, Issue 2 - Universidad de Costa Rica. Centro de Estudios Centroamericanos</ref>

The town is described in 1858 as "a little town in a small plain through which winds the river of the same name..." It is described as having a grist-mill and that "the country produces considerable wheat of medium quality."<ref>The States of Central America: Their Geography, Topography, Climate, Population, Resources, Productions, Commerce, Political Organization, Aborigines, Etc., Etc. - E. G. Squier Sampson Low, 1858</ref> Estelí was described in 1920 as having 8,000 inhabitants and as being a rich and growing center.<ref>United States Congressional serial set, Issue 4849</ref>

Estelí was the scene of heavy fighting in the civil war against the [[Somoza]] government from 1978 to 1979. The city was heavily air-bombed by the regime's National Guard and burnt by the [[FSLN]] guerrillas, which reduced many of the city's buildings to rubble. The human casualties were around 15,000; many of them were youths massacred on suspicion of being part of the insurrection due to the infiltration of anti-government guerillas from the Pacific of Nicaragua whom used the city as a war theater. Estelí rebuilt all but some structures, such as the Teatro Montenegro and the Government Palace, and a few may still show bullet holes.

==Economy and tourism==
[[File:MulticentroEsteli.jpg|left|thumb|A shopping center in Estelí]]
The land around Estelí has a suitable climate for growing tobacco for use in [[cigars]], and the town became a refuge for [[Cuban cigar]] makers after the [[Cuban Revolution]] in 1959. Estelí is one of the most important [[cigar]]-producing cities in the world.{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}} Estelí also has many [[language schools]]. Restaurants and hotels cater to tourists traveling to nearby natural reserves and other parts of the region. Natural Reserves around the area include [[Miraflor Natural Reserve|Miraflor]], [[Cerro Tisey-Estanzuela Nature Reserve|Tisey-Estanzuela]], [[Cerro Quiabuc–Las Brisas Nature Reserve|Las Brisas-Quiabuc]], Tomabú, Tepesomoto, and Moropotente.

According to the [[World Bank]] and [[International Finance Corporation]]'s Municipal Scorecard 2008, which complements the annual Doing Business report, the municipality of Estelí ranks 1st and 2nd, out of 143 municipalities in ten Latin American countries, in quality and efficiency to obtain a [[construction permit]] and municipal operating license respectively.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.municipalscorecard.org/pdf/regional/port/Regional%20Report.pdf |title=Archived copy |access-date=2010-03-10 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111008182447/http://www.municipalscorecard.org/pdf/regional/port/Regional%20Report.pdf |archive-date=2011-10-08 |url-status=usurped }}</ref>  Estelí has improved its performance from the Municipal Scorecard 2007, where it ranked 5th out of 65 municipalities in five countries.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.siteselection.com/issues/2007/jul/projectFinance/#ifcScorecard|title=Project Finance|date=July 2007|website=Siteselection.com|access-date=18 January 2018}}</ref>

==Infrastructure==
Estelí has near-full population coverage with its water-supply system. It also has extensive sewage disposal coverage.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.laprensa.com.ni/archivo/2008/septiembre/06/elecciones/noticias/270046_print.shtml|title=Transportistas proponen incremento de pasajes en Estelí|date=19 July 2017|website=Laprensa.com.ni|access-date=18 January 2018}}</ref>

At least three airfields have been built in Estelí throughout history, although none exists today. One of the first airfields documented played the role of alternate airport from 1930 to 1934 when [[Grupo TACA|TACA]] established the service to carry passengers with an El Salvador-Tegucigalpa-Danli-Ocotal-Estelí-Managua route. La Thompson, which is three miles north of Estelí, is where the second airstrip was built and used by a company named "Thonsson Corwell" to help complete a stretch of the [[Pan-American Highway]]. The third airfield was built in the north end of town in the 1980s but has now been urbanized.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://elesteliano.com/revista/ene2007/historia2.htm|title=Estelí Ayer|website=Elesteliano.com|access-date=18 January 2018}}</ref>

In 1940 efforts were made to expand [[Rail transport in Nicaragua|Nicaragua's railroad]] from El Sauce to the north via Estelí. Thirteen kilometers of rail, the town of Río Grande, and a [https://www.panoramio.com/photo/13723761 railroad bridge] were built with the intention of taking the rail up to Estelí along the Aquespalapa or Villanueva river, which originates in the Quiabuc mountains; however, the project was abandoned.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.manfut.org/managua/tren.html|title=Ferrocarril de Nicaragua|website=www.manfut.org|access-date=18 January 2018}}</ref> In 1950 there was a proposal to create a railroad branch from Estelí to Matagalpa which would eventually connect the city to other parts of the country including Prinzapolka Port in the Caribbean and Managua.<ref>United States Congressional serial set, Issue 4849 - 1905</ref>

==Sports==
Estelí is home to one of the most popular [[association football|football]] clubs in the country, the [[Real Estelí F.C.|Real Estelí]], nicknamed "El Tren del Norte" ("The Train of the North"). They play their home games at the [[Estadio Independencia (Nicaragua)|Estadio Independencia]], a name created by Oscar Corea Molina. [[Real Estelí Baloncesto]] plays in the [[Basketball Champions League Americas]].{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}}

It is also home to a professional baseball team, Estelí, which makes it one of only three cities in the country with both a professional football (soccer) and baseball team. During the 1970s, Estelí participated in the "[[Roberto Clemente]]" baseball tournament and had one of the best baseball teams in the country with the help of one-time big league players like [[Porfi Altamirano]] and Albert Williams.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.beisnica.com/|title=502 Bad Gateway nginx openresty 208.80.154.49|website=Beisnica.com|access-date=1 August 2019}}</ref>

Estelí also has a professional cycling team called Real Estelí Ciclismo,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.facebook.com/realestelic/|title=Real Estelí Ciclismo|website=Facebook.com|access-date=1 August 2019}}</ref> which is member of the Cycling Federation of Nicaragua.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.facebook.com/CiclismoNica/|title=Federación Nicaragüense de Ciclismo - FNC|website=Facebook.com|access-date=1 August 2019}}</ref> In May 2019, Real Estelí won first place in the Elite group of the Europa Tour 2019 cycling competition in San Salvador, El Salvador, hosted by the Cycling Federation of El Salvador.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.facebook.com/events/federacion-salvadore%C3%B1a-de-ciclismo/europa-tour-el-salvador-2019/667719097016159/|title=Europa Tour - El Salvador 2019|website=Facebook.com|access-date=1 August 2019}}</ref> The team also won second place in the Master B category.

==Gastronomy==
Due to its altitude Estelí can be quite chilly at night or in the morning and even cold in winter. Thus, like all northern mountainous regions of Nicaragua, Estelí's gastronomy consists of a hearty mountain diet of beef, game, veal, rabbit, geese, sausages, and heavy soups like albóndiga, queso, res, etc. There is a high consumption of milk products like smoked or spicy cheeses like ahumado and picante, and regional corn-based dishes such as montucas, salty-sour repochetas, and semi-sweet güirilas. For breakfast, chorizo, [[refried beans]], sour cream, and home-made style bread are more common and black coffee is the king of drinks any time of the day. Though illegal, [[Moonshine by country#Nicaragua|cususa]] (a clear alcoholic drink) is also consumed, especially on the outskirts of the city or in small cantinas (bars).{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}}
