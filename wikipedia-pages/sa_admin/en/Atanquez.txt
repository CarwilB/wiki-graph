'''Atanquez''' or '''San Sebastian''' is a Colombian town and [[corregimiento]] of [[Valledupar]] in the [[Department of Cesar]]. Atanquez is located on the [[Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta]] mountain range at approximately 2,000 m over sea level. Atanquez is known for being predominantly inhabited by the [[Indigenous peoples in Colombia|indigenous]] ethnic group [[Kankuamos]] among others and [[mestizo]] groups.

==History==

Atanquez was an indigenous settlement founded according to oral tradition by a ''Mama'' (spiritual leader) named Tukaka.<ref name="SpanishAtanquez">{{Cite web |url=http://informacion.atanquez.com/ |title=Atanquez: history |language=es |access-date=2008-04-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080327091817/http://informacion.atanquez.com/ |archive-date=2008-03-27 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In the mid-18th century the Spanish had already colonized the plains surrounding the [[Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta]] mountain range and decided to also colonize the mountains and re-found the indigenous settlements. These indigenous villages were Atanquez, Maraocaso and El Rosario which grew in population due to the displacement caused during the period of Spanish conquest of the [[Chimila people|Chimila]] and [[Guanebucanes]] in the lower and surrounding areas of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta mountains in the 16th century.<ref>{{Harvnb|Gutierrez Hinojosa|2000|p=125}}</ref>

The [[Kogi people|Kogui]] named these the "Wiwa", while the [[Arhuaco people|Arhuaco]] called them "Arzario". Former African slaves also established in Atanquez and formed the "[[Palenque]] of Atanquez". The Spanish sent Capuchin and Dominican friars to "civilize" the indigenous and named it San Sebastian.<ref>{{Harvnb|Gutierrez Hinojosa|2000|p=127}}</ref> According to an order of the [[Royal Audience of Santa Fe de Bogota]] by a Sub-delegate Judge of Land named Don Agustín de la Sierra in 1781. The corregimiento of Atanquez was created by Municipal Accord of Valledupar 02 on January 4, 1906, and ordered by then [[List of mayors of Valledupar|Mayor of Valledupar]], [[Moisés Martínez]]. Atanquez also became part of the indigenous [[Kankuamo people|Kankuamo]] [[Resguardo]] created by Resolution 012 of April 10, 2003, as established by the Colombian Institute of Agrarian Reform (INCORA).<ref name="SpanishAtanquez" />

===Colombian armed conflict===

{{see also|Colombian armed conflict}}

Between 1986 and 2003 the [[Colombian armed conflict]] affected the region of Atanquez intensively with 197 Kankuamos murdered by paramilitary and guerrilla groups. The region was influenced by the paramilitary group [[United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia]] (AUC), and two guerrillas; the [[Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia]] (FARC) and the [[National Liberation Army (Colombia)|National Liberation Army]] (ELN).<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.plazamayor.net/antropologia/boant/cronicas/oct0303.html |title=Plazamayor.net: Guerrilla y paramilitares han asesinado este año a 44 indígenas kankuamos |language=es |access-date=2008-04-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110724013150/http://www.plazamayor.net/antropologia/boant/cronicas/oct0303.html |archive-date=2011-07-24 |url-status=dead }}</ref> These groups flourished in the region due to the [[marijuana]] bonanza of the 1970s and later the use of their traditional [[Coca]] plants to produce [[cocaine]] and the introduction of [[Opium poppy|amapola]] plantations.<ref>[http://www.urosario.edu.co/fase1/economia/CEODD/docs/BI-CEODD-10.pdf {{in lang|es}} Universidad del Rosario: Pasado y Presente del Contrabando en La Guajira Aproximaciones al Fenomeno de ilegalidad en la Region]{{Dead link|date=May 2019 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==Economy==

The economy of Atanquez is based on agriculture and the production of indigenous art crafts. The main products are [[panela]], [[liquor]] from [[maize]] and [[sugar cane]], [[fique]], production of medicinal plants, local fruits and vegetables, [[Cocoa bean|Cacao]] and [[coffee]] which is commercialize in Valledupar.<ref>{{Harvnb|Gutierrez Hinojosa|2000|p=126}}</ref>
