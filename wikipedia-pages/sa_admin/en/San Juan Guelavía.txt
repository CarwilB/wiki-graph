'''San Juan Guelavía''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] in [[Oaxaca]] in south-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 17.86&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>. It is part of the [[Tlacolula District]] in the east of the [[Valles Centrales de Oaxaca|Valles Centrales Region]].

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 2,940.<ref>{{cite web|title=San Juan Guelavia|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/oaxaca/municipios/20197a.htm|work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|accessdate=June 12, 2009}}</ref>

==Geography==
San Juan Guelavia is on the river and one of the few communities in the valley that had historically set up irrigation canals.<ref name="Lees1973">{{cite book|last=Lees|first=Susan H.|title=Sociopolitical Aspects of Canal Irrigation in the Valley of Oaxaca|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=SjQNAAAAYAAJ|accessdate=29 November 2015|year=1973|publisher=University of Michigan}}</ref>

==History==
Early Spanish migration to the area consisted of cattle ranchers who moved their cattle from communal usage pastures in the mountains to communal pastures in the valley.<ref name="Taylor1972">{{cite book|last=Taylor|first=William B.|title=Landlord and Peasant in Colonial Oaxaca|url=https://archive.org/details/landlordpeasanti0000tayl|url-access=registration|accessdate=29 November 2015|year=1972|publisher=Stanford University Press|isbn=9780804707961|pages=[https://archive.org/details/landlordpeasanti0000tayl/page/88 88]–}}</ref> In 1539, [[Bartolome Sanchez]] was granted an ''estancia de granado mayor'' (permanent land holding rights) near what is now San Juan Guelavía.<ref name="Taylor1972"/>

Guelavia's sixteenth century church has a large number of colonial-era ''santos'', statues of Roman Catholic saints.<ref>[http://www.aug.edu/augusta/santos/guelavia/index.html Santos in Oaxaca's Ancient Churches: San Juan Guelavia]. Retrieved 2012-04-13.</ref>

During the [[Mexican Revolution]], in 1914, General [[Juan M. Brito]] stationed his troops near San Juan Guelavia to oppose the Federalist aims of [[Venustiano Carranza]].<ref name="Cook2014">{{cite book|last=Cook|first=Scott|title=Land, Livelihood, and Civility in Southern Mexico: Oaxaca Valley Communities in History|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DOZlAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA337|accessdate=29 November 2015|date=2014-05-15|publisher=University of Texas Press|isbn=9780292754782|pages=337–}}</ref> After the war, Brito spent time in a prison in [[Mexico City|the Federal District of Mexico City]] before returning to San Juan Guelavia, where he established himself as a businessman running a store and also the local ''jefe'' strong man controlling the local communities through force of his armed followers.<ref name="Cook2014"/>
