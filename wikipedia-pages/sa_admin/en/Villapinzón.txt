'''Villapinzón''' is a municipality and town of [[Colombia]] in the [[Almeidas Province]], part of the [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]]. The urban centre is situated at an elevation of {{convert|2715|m|ft}} on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], at a distance of {{convert|80|km|mi}} from the capital [[Bogotá]]. Villapinzón borders [[Ventaquemada]] ([[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]]) and [[Lenguazaque]] in the north, [[Chocontá]], [[Tibiritá]] and [[La Capilla]] in the south, Chocontá and Lenguazaque in the west and Ventaquemada, [[Turmequé]] and [[Úmbita]] in the east. The municipality is located in the uppermost part of the [[Bogotá River|Bogotá River Basin]]; the origin of the river is within the municipality Villapinzón at an elevation of {{convert|3400|m|ft}}.<ref name=websiteVillapinzon>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.villapinzon-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Villapinzón]{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Villapinzón was named Hato Viejo for most of its history. In 1903, the name was changed to "Pinzón", honouring [[Próspero Pinzón|Próspero Pinzón Romero]], commander of the army and Minister of War of Colombia under [[list of presidents of Colombia|Colombian president]] [[José Manuel Marroquín]]. Próspero Pinzón Romero was born in Hato Viejo. A year later, it became Villapinzón.<ref name=websiteVillapinzon/>

== History ==
The history of Villapinzón is relatively young; historian Ramón Correa studied the pre-Columbian history of the area and did not find evidence Villapinzón was populated before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca]]. Villapinzón was founded on October 14, 1776 by Francisco de Vargas Figueroa.<ref name=websiteVillapinzon/>

== Economy ==
Economically, Villapinzón is characterized by a large tannery (curtido) industry. Additionally, agriculture and livestock farming are important.<ref name=websiteVillapinzon/>
