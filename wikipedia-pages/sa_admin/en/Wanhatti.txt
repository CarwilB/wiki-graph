'''Wanhatti''' is a village and resort in [[Suriname]], located in the [[Marowijne District|Marowijne district]] on the [[Cottica River]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.statoids.com/ysr.html|title=Suriname Ressorts|website=www.statoids.com|access-date=2019-08-08}}</ref> The resort is inhabited by the [[Ndyuka people|Ndyuka Maroons]], and has a population of 468 people as of 2012.<ref name="census"/> The village is primarily inhabited by [[Ndyuka people|Ndyuka]] of the Ansu clan or ''lo''.{{sfn|Thoden van Velzen|Hoogbergen|2011|p=112}}

==Name==
Wanhatti means "one heart" in both [[Sranan Tongo]] and [[Ndyuka language|Ndyuka]] and refers to the fact that the village is a merger of the villages of Agiti Ondoo and Walimbumofu.{{sfn|Van Wetering|Thoden van Velzen|2013|p=153}}{{sfn|Thoden van Velzen|Hoogbergen|2011|p=112}}

==History==
Agiti Ondoo was the site of a Gaan Gadu shrine.{{sfn|Van Wetering|Thoden van Velzen|2013|pp=154-155, 177-185}}

In 1972, a road was built to connect Wanhatti to the [[East-West Link (Suriname)|East-West Link]]. The road partially uses the path of the old defence line,<ref name="DWT road">{{cite news |last=Mulder |first=Nikki |date=2013-02-23 |title=Hoop voor vergeten dorpen aan de Cottica |url=http://nikkimulder.wordpress.com/2013/02/27/hoop-voor-vergeten-dorpen-aan-de-cottica/ |newspaper=[[De Ware Tijd]] |location=Paramaribo |access-date=15 May 2020 }}</ref> build between 1770 and 1778 to protect the plantations of the [[Surinam (Dutch colony)|Colony of Suriname]] against attacks by the Maroons.<ref>{{cite book|last=Bruijning|first=C.F.A.|date=1977|title=Encyclopedie van Suriname|publisher=Elsevier|isbn=9010018423|language=nl}}</ref> The road improved conditions in the village, and it now has a school, and a connection to the [[telephone]] network.<ref name="DWT road"/> The [[Surinamese Interior War]] fought during the 1980s left its toll on the resort, and caused a large part of the population to flee.{{sfn|Van Wetering|Thoden van Velzen|2013|p=288}} In September 2010, a health care clinic was opened in the village.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://parbode.com/weerzien-met-wanhatti/|title=Weerzien met Wanhatti|website=Parbode|access-date=15 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> In 2011, the road was extended to reach the village [[Lantiwee]],<ref name="DWT road"/> and it is hoped that this will lead to a return of farmers in the area.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.nieuws-suriname.nl/ontsluiting-van-dorpen-te-cottica/|title=Ontsluiting van dorpen te Cottica|access-date=15 May 2020|language=nl|website=GFC nieuws via Nieuws Suriname}}</ref> On 3 June 2011, a [[library]] was opened in the village.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://werkgroepcaraibischeletteren.nl/wanhatti-krijgt-eigen-bibliotheek/|title=Wanhatti krijgt eigen bibliotheek|website=Suriname.nu via Werkgroup Caraibische Letteren|access-date=15 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> 

Besides Wanhatti itself, the resort contains among others the villages of [[Lantiwee]], [[Pikin Santi]], [[Pinatjaimi]], and [[Tamarin, Suriname|Tamarin]].

==Aluku Maroons==
Fort Buku, a famous [[Aluku]] Maroon fort of the [[Boni (guerrilla leader)|Boni]] War that fell in 1772, is located within the Wanhatti resort, however the fort itself still has to be discovered as of 2020.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://werkgroepcaraibischeletteren.nl/fort-boekoe-blijft-ongrijpbaar/ |title=Fort Boekoe blijft ongrijpbaar |author=Moll, Tom van |date=1 February 2012 |publisher=Parbode |access-date=15 May 2020}}</ref> The Ndyuka signed a peace treaty in 1760.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.culturalsurvival.org/publications/cultural-survival-quarterly/ndyuka-treaty-1760-conversation-granman-gazon|title=The Ndyuka Treaty Of 1760: A Conversation with Granman Gazon|website=Cultural Survival|language=nl|access-date=15 May 2020}}</ref> Boni also desired a peace treaty, but the [[Society of Suriname]], despite contrary advice from the Dutch government, wanted to persecute and destroy the Aluku.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch West-Indië - Page 154 - Boschnegers|website=[[Digital Library for Dutch Literature]]|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/benj004ency01_01/benj004ency01_01.pdf|date=1916|access-date=11 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> In 1777, the [[France|French]] send a diplomatic mission to Suriname, to discuss the refugee crisis.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch West-Indië - Page 508 - Nepveu (Jan of Jean)|website=[[Digital Library for Dutch Literature]]|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/benj004ency01_01/benj004ency01_01.pdf|date=1916|access-date=11 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>
