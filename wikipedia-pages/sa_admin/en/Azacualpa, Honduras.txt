'''Azacualpa''' ({{IPA|es|asaˈkwalpa}}) is a [[Municipality (Honduras)|municipality]] in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]], Honduras. It covers an area of {{cvt|222.3|km2}} and had a population of 20,210 inhabitants according to the 2013 national census. It was officially established on 16 May 1960.

== History ==
Azacualpa was earlier part of the municipality of [[Macuelizo]]. On 15 August 1958, a request for declaration of it as a municipality was submitted. It was officially declared so by Resolution No. 33 on 16 May 1960 during the reign of president [[Ramón Villeda Morales]]. The first council took office on 22 May 1960.<ref name="Edu">{{cite web|title=Municipio de Azacualpa, Santa Bárbara|url=https://edu-honduras.info/Los-Departamentos/16-Santa-Barbara/Azacualpa.html|work=Edu-Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026|lang=es}}</ref><ref name="Red">{{cite web|url=https://redhonduras.hn/en/geography/municipality-azacualpa/|title=Municipality of Azacualpa|work=Red Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref>

==Etymology==
There are several theories as to the meaning of the name "Azacualpa". It might mean "in the pyramid" as per Mexican usage. In ''Etimología de Honduras'', it is stated that it is probably derived from [[Nahuatl]] language meaning "abandoned or uninhabited village".<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/>

== Geography ==
Azacualpa is located in the northern part of the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] in Honduras.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/|title=Administrative divisions of Honduras|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Macuelizo]] to the south, [[Nuevo Celilac]] and [[Quimistan]] to the east, and [[La Unión, Lempira|La Unión]], and [[Nueva Frontera]] to the west. It also shares a land border with Guatemala.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/> The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|222.3|km2}} and an average altitude of {{cvt|246|m}}.<ref name="Edu"/>

== Administrative divisions ==
The municipality comprises ten aldeas (villages) and their associated caseríos (hamlets).<ref name="INE2013">{{cite report|url=https://temp.ine.gob.hn/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/254-Santa-Barbara-Azacualpa.pdf|work=Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE)|title=XVII Censo Nacional de Población y VI de Vivienda 2013: Tomo 254, Municipio de Azacualpa, Departamento de Santa Bárbara|date=February 2025|access-date=1 April 2026|lang=es}}</ref>{{rp|page=20}}

== Demographics ==
At per the 2013 census, Azacualpa had a total population of 20,210 inhabitants, of whom 10,036 (49.7%) were male and 10,174 (50.3%) were female. Of the total population, 12,561 (62.2%) lived in the urban area and 7,649 (37.8%) in the rural areas.<ref name="CP1">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/santa_b%C3%A1rbara/1604__azacualpa/|title=Azacualpa|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19, 20}}

About 0.59% of the population were classified as [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and rest as non-indigneous.<ref name="CP1"/> By broad age group, 35.6% of the population were aged 0–14, 58.5% were aged 15–64, and 5.9% were aged 65 and over. The median age was 20.9 years and the mean age was 25.9 years. Among the population aged 15 and over, the 2013 census recorded an illiteracy rate of 19.7%, lower than the departmental average of 22.2%.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}}
