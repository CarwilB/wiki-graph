'''Salesópolis''' is a Brazilian [[municipality]] in the state of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]], located at the eastern edge of the [[Metropolitan Region of São Paulo|São Paulo Metropolitan Region of São Paulo]],<ref>[http://governo-sp.jusbrasil.com.br/legislacao/1028148/lei-complementar-1139-11 Lei Complementar nº 1.139, de 16 de junho de 2011]</ref> within the Upper Tietê River Basin, approximately 100 kilometres from the state [[São Paulo|capital]].  The municipality comprises the main town and the district of Nossa Senhora dos Remédios.

Salesópolis is one of the areas in the region that still features extensive [[Atlantic Forest|native vegetation]], while also experiencing the typical pressures of both urban and rural zones, including illegal land subdivision, as well as unregulated agroforestry and tourism activities.

Six notable characteristics stand out in Salesópolis:
#The still-preserved natural environment and relatively small urban area
#The headwaters of the Tietê River, which remain little known despite the national importance of this watercourse
#The presence of reservoirs and islands, with about eighty miles of shoreline, regarded as assets of aesthetic and scenic value
#The proximity to the [[Serra do Mar]] and the inclusion of its territory within the [[Serra do Mar State Park]]
#The extensive reforestation with eucalyptus
#The horticulture and fruit farming found in the municipality, particularly around the District of Nossa Senhora dos Remédios.<ref>OTAKE, Ruy; UMEOKA, Márcia; CARVALHO, Elza Maria Braga de (Org.). Plano diretor participativo de Salesópolis. Salesópolis: Prefeitura da Estância Turística de Salesópolis, 2006.</ref>

Salesópolis was founded in the 19th century as São José do Paraitinga, and later became the parish of Santana de Mogi das Cruzes in 1838. The parish was elevated to municipality status on March 24, 1857, but renamed Salesópolis in 1905 after a visit of the president of Brazil, [[Manuel Ferraz de Campos Sales|Campos Sales]] (1841–1913).<ref name="i"/>

=== Religion ===
As shown by the 2022 Census,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Panorama do Censo 2022 |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/ |access-date=2025-11-04 |website=Panorama do Censo 2022 |language=pt-BR}}</ref> the municipality is predominantly Roman Catholic (72.5% of the population). This influence is evident in the town's historical heritage—such as the Main Church—and in its cultural life, which largely revolves around religious events like the Feast of the Holy Spirit and celebrations of specific saints dating back to the town's foundation.

Other inhabitants belong to various Protestant denominations (16%) and other faiths (6.7%), including [[Afro-Brazilians|Afro-Brazilian]] religions, [[Kardecist spiritism|Kardecist]] Spiritism, [[Mormons]], and [[Jehovah's Witnesses|Jehovah's Witnesses]]. Some of these groups hold their worship services in other municipalities. There are no records of adherents of [[Judaism]], [[Islam]], or [[Eastern religions]] in Salesópolis. There are also no significant events involving these religious denominations in the municipality.

There has also been a noticeable growth in the number of people without any religious affiliation (4.8%), including atheists, agnostics, and those who simply do not identify with an organised religion.

== Geography ==
The boundaries of Salesópolis are [[Santa Branca]] to the north, [[Paraibuna]] to the northeast, [[Caraguatatuba]] to the east, [[São Sebastião, São Paulo|São Sebastião]] to the southeast, [[Bertioga]] to the south, [[Biritiba-Mirim]] to the west, and [[Guararema]] to the northwest.

The Upper Tietê River Basin covers nearly the same area as the [[Greater São Paulo|Metropolitan Region of Greater São Paulo]], with a total area of about {{cvt|2310|sqmi}}, and is composed of 34 municipalities. Salesópolis, located within this basin and about {{cvt|60|mi}} from the state capital, has an area of approximately {{cvt|164|sqmi}}, with 98% of its territory lying within the [[Protection and Recovery Area of Water Sources]] (APRM-ATC).<ref>{{Cite web |title=Lei nº 15.913, de 02 de outubro de 2015 |url=http://www.al.sp.gov.br/leis/legislacao-do-estado/ |access-date=2025-11-08 |website=www.al.sp.gov.br |language=pt-br}}</ref>

The region’s terrain is characterised by hills and rolling uplands, with altitudes ranging from roughly {{cvt|2430|to|3610|ft}}, and the [[Serra do Mar]] mountain range standing out in the southern, southeastern, and southwestern parts of the area. Floodplains can be found along the lower stretches of the rivers.<ref>SÃO PAULO (Estado). Salesópolis. Disponível em: http://www.fundacaofia.com.br/ gdusm/salesopolis.htm>. Acesso em: 01 ago. 2011.</ref>

The Tietê River Basin has two main tributaries. The ''Claro'' River, whose basin was expropriated by the state government, flows into the Tietê River, and its waters are dammed by the Ponte Nova Reservoir. The ''Paraitinga'' River rises in Paraibuna, passes through urban areas, and flows into the Tietê River in the municipality of Biritiba Mirim.

The Tietê River is the main source of public water supply for the [[Greater São Paulo|Metropolitan Region of São Paulo]].<ref>JOHNSSON, Rosa Maria Formiga; LOPES, Paula Duarte (Org.). Projeto Marca d’Água: seguindo as mudanças na gestão das bacias hidrográficas do Brasil: caderno 1: retrato 3 x4 das bacias pesquisadas, 2003.</ref> Its source lies at an altitude of around {{cvt|3380|ft}}, among the crystalline rocks of the Atlantic Plateau, in the district of Pedra Rajada, springing from several natural springs emerging from a sandy layer.<ref>LEITE, Marcelo (Org.). Nos caminhos da biodiversidade paulista. São Paulo: Secretaria de Estado do Meio Ambiente, 2007.</ref>
