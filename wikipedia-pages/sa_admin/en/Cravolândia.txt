'''Cravolândia''' ([[Portuguese language|Portuguese]]: ''Cloveland'') is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the [[states of Brazil|state]] of [[Bahia]] in the [[Nordeste|North-East region]] of [[Brazil]]. Cravolândia covers {{convert|182.585|km2|mi2|abbr=on}}, and has a population of 5,351 with a population density of 31 inhabitants per square kilometer. It borders the municipalities of [[Santa Inês]], [[Itaquara]], [[Ubaíra]] and [[Wenceslau Guimarães]]. Cravolândia is located {{Convert|317|km|mi}} from [[Salvador, Bahia|Salvador]], the state capital of Bahia, and is connected to Salvador by federal highways  [[BR-116]] and [[BR-101]].<ref name="b"/><ref name="i"/>

==History==

Cravolândia takes its name from the first mayor of the municipality, Mario da Silva Cravo.{{Ref|A|A}} Cravo was a coffee producer and a noted a noted brand of coffee, ''Café Cravo'', was produced and exported from Cravolândia in the 1950s. The coffee crop was decimated by [[Pest (organism)|agricultural pests]] in the 1960s. Small-scale coffee cultivation resumed in the 1970s but has never reached the levels of production of earlier decades.<ref name="b"/><ref name="i"/>

Cravolândia was originally part of a region known as ''Olhos D'Água do Carrasco'', a reference to its role as a place for [[Droving|drovers]] to stop for water in the movement of their livestock. This name was replaced by Igatiquira, an indigenous name meaning "small ounce". Cravolândia was originally part of the municipality of [[Jequiriça]] and later of Santa Inês. It became an independent municipality under Bahia State Law no. 1714 on July 16, 1962.<ref name="b"/><ref name="i"/>

==Footnotes==

:A.{{Note|A}}Mario da Silva Cravo is the father and grandfather of the Bahian artists [[Mário Cravo Júnior]] (1923-2018) and [[Mário Cravo Neto]] (1947-2009). Cravo Júnior and Cravo Neto, however, were born and raised in Salvador, and were not residents of Cravolândia.
