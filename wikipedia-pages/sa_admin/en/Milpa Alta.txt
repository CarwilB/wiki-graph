'''Milpa Alta''' is a [[Boroughs of Mexico City|borough]] (''demarcación territorial'') in [[Mexico City]]. It lies in the southeast corner of the nation's capital, bordering the [[State of Mexico]] and [[Morelos]]. It is the least populated, second largest and most rural of all the boroughs.  It is also one of the most traditional areas of the city, with over 700 religious and secular festivals during the year and an economy based on agriculture and food processing, especially the production of [[opuntia|nopal cactus]], [[barbacoa]] and [[Mole (sauce)|mole sauce]].

==Geography==
[[File:San pedro atocpan.jpg|thumb|left|250px|Overlooking San Pedro Atocpan]]
[[File:El Teuhtli.JPG|thumb|left|250px|View of the Teuhtli volcano]]
The borough of Milpa Alta is located in the southeast of Mexico City bordering the boroughs of [[Xochimilco]], [[Tláhuac]] and [[Tlalpan]], with the state of Morelos to the south and the State of Mexico to the west.<ref name="encmuc">{{cite web |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM09DF/delegaciones/09009a.html |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130221025902/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/EMM09DF/delegaciones/09009a.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 21, 2013 |title=Milpa Alta |year=2010 |publisher=Enciclopedia de Los Municipios y Delegaciones de México Distrito Federal |language=es |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref> It has the second largest territorial extension after Tlalpan, occupying 268.6km2.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="sustenable">{{cite web |url=http://ccg2.siap.gob.mx/df/mun_09009_MILPAALTA/docu/plandedesarrollo.pdf |title=PLAN DELEGACIONAL PARA EL DESARROLLO RURAL SUSTENTABLE |publisher=CONSEJO DELEGACIONAL PARA EL DESARROLLO RURAL SUSTENTABLE EN LA DELEGACION MILPA ALTA |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Borough Plan for Sustainable Rural Development }}{{dead link|date=September 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

The terrain is rugged mostly consisting of volcanic peak along with some small flat areas mostly formed in the [[Cenozoic Era]].<ref name="sustenable"/> City officials have classified the entire borough as a conservation zone, important for its role as an aquifer recharge area as well as its forests. Forest, farmland and grazing areas constitute 98.1% of the total surface area.<ref name="tenencia">{{cite journal |author= Roberto Bonilla Rodríguez |date=September–December 2009 |title= Agricultura y tenencia de la tierra en Milpa Alta. Un lugar de identidad |trans-title=Agriculture and land tenency in Milpa Alta |journal=Argumentos |volume=22 |issue=61 |location=Mexico City |language=es |issn=0187-5795 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |url=http://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0187-57952009000300011  }}</ref>

It has an average altitude of 2,420 meters above sea level with altitudes varying between 2,300 and 3,600.<ref name="encmuc"/> It is part of the [[Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt]] and the [[Sierra Chichinautzin]] volcanic mountain chain, which separates Mexico City from the state of Morelos.<ref name="sustenable"/> The borough is divided into three zones: Ajusco-Teuhtli, the lowest elevations, Topilejo-Milpa Alta in the medium range and Cerro-Tlicuaya at the highest elevations.<ref name="nahuasde">{{cite web |url=http://www.cdi.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=628&Itemid=62  |title= Nahuas de Milpa Alta  |date=October 22, 2009 |publisher=Comisión Nacional para el Desarrollo de los Puelbos Indígenas |location=Mexico |language=es |trans-title=The Nahuas of Milpa Alta |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref> The main elevations are volcanic and include Cuautzin (3,510), Tulmiac, Ocusacayo (3,220), La Comalera (3,230), San Bartolo (3,200), Tláloc (3,510), Chichinautzin (3,470), Yecahuazac, Quimixtepec, El Oclayuca (3,140), El Pajonal (3,100), El Ocotécatl (3,480), Acopiaxco (3,320), Tetzacoatl (3,310), Tehutli (2,800) Cilcuayo (3,580), Nepanapa (3,460), Texalo (3,560), Oclayuca (3,390), San Miguel (2,988) .<ref name="sustenable"/>

The area belongs to the [[Amacuzac River]] basin but only small streams run on the surface.<ref name="encmuc"/> It has ample surface water but confined to small springs and streams which over time have formed a succession of narrow valleys or micro-basins, which are important for recharging the [[Valley of Mexico]]’s aquifers. These micro-basins include Cilcuayo, Río Milpa Alta and Cocpiaxco and contain the borough’s main towns.<ref name="sustenable"/>

Most of the area has a temperate climate, with cold climates found at the highest elevations.<ref name="nahuasde"/> The average annual temperature is {{convert|15.6|C|1}} with average lows at {{convert|13.7|C|1}} and averages highs at {{convert|16.6|C|1}}. Average annual precipitation is {{convert|731|mm|2|abbr=on}}. Freezing temperatures occur occasionally from October to March especially in the higher elevations. The windiest months are February and March. On the [[Köppen climate classification|Koppen scale]], its climate is described as C (W2) (w) b (i’)  which signified a relatively moist climate, with a rainy season in the summer especially in July and August. However, the climate varies substantially in the territory based mostly on altitude, with six sub-climates: C(E)(w2) which is relatively cold, C(E)(m) also relatively cold but wetter, C(w1) a temperate climate,  C (w2)  a températe and relatively wet climate, C ( w1) temperate and relatively dry and C ( w2) which is relatively cold with rains falling mostly in the highest areas.<ref name="sustenable"/>

The area has a number of species found nowhere else. The natural vegetation is mostly forest with a mix of pine, [[oyamel fir]] and [[Quercus agrifolia|holm oak]], with some concentrations of ''[[Abies religiosa]]''. The most intact forest is found in the small canyons of the Nepanapa volcano and the west side of the Tláloc volcano. Other vegetation include fruit trees such as [[tejocote]] (''Crataegus pubescens''), [[Prunus serotina|capulin]] (''Prunus serotona'' ssp ''capulli'') blackberry (''[[Rubus adenotrichus]]'') and well as various scrubs, grass and flowers. The rugged terrain presents a number of micro climates which favor certain species. Depending on conditions oak, cedar, strawberry trees (''[[Arbutus]]''), pirul (''[[Schinus molle]]''), tepozan (''[[Buddleja cordata]]''), nopal cactus and [[Agave americana|maguey]] can be found.<ref name="sustenable"/> Wildlife includes 59 species of mammals such as [[Volcano rabbit|zacatuche rabbits]] (''Romerolagus diazi''), which in danger of extinction, along with coyotes, deer, lynx and moles. However, only sixteen of these species are still commonly seen because of [[habitat destruction]].<ref name="sustenable"/> Species such as wild boar, bobcats and opossums are extinct in the area.<ref name="nahuasde"/>  There are about 200 bird species native to the area in 128 classes, 33 families and eleven orders. Eighty percent of the species live in the area year round. There are twenty four amphibian species from ten classes, seven families and two orders and fifty six species of reptiles from thirty one classes, ten families and two orders. A notable areas for these two classes of animal is the corridor between the Ajusco and Chichinautzin mountains.<ref name="sustenable"/>

While named after Villa Milpa Alta, the borough is not concentrated on a single community like Tlahuac or Xochimilco but rather is composed of twelve main towns all of which are rural. This limits the area’s connectivity with the urban zone of Mexico City.<ref name="tenencia"/><ref name="conservan">{{cite news |title= Conservan el ultimo bastion rural del DF |author= Ivan Sosa and Ariadna Bermeo|newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=June 27, 2003 |page=3 |language=es |trans-title=Preserve the last rural bastion of Mexico City }}</ref> Main communities in the borough include [[San Pedro Atocpan]], Villa Milpa Alta (formerly called Malacachtepec), San Bartolome Xicomulco, San Francisco Tecoxpa, Santa Ana Tlacotenco, San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan, San Juan Tepanahuac, San Agustin Ohtenco, San Antonio Tecómitl, San Pablo Oztotepec and San Jerónimo Miacantla.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="sustenable"/> San Agustin Ohtenco is considered to be the smallest community in Mexico City.<ref name="encmuc"/> These main towns are subdivided into twenty nine neighborhoods called barrios and there are 225 communities in the borough total.<ref name="tenencia"/><ref name="microrreg"/> Villa Milpa Alta has seven barrios, San Mateo (the largest), La Concepción, Los Angeles, Santa Cruz, San Agustin, Santa Martha and La Luz.<ref name="adapon5">Adapon, Joy. p.5</ref>

==Demographics and culture==
[[File:MaravillasMoleSPA.JPG|thumb|Mole stand at the Feria de Mole in San Pedro Atocpan]]
[[File:Day of the Dead at Tecomitl Cemetery.jpg|thumb|Day of the Dead at the Tecomitl cemetery]]
[[File:GloboCantolla70.JPG|thumb|Sky lantern and hot air balloons at the Feria de Cantolla]]
With a population of 152,685 {{As of|2020|lc=y}},<ref name="Data México"/> Milpa Alta has the lowest population of Mexico City’s sixteen boroughs.<ref name="microrreg">{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=09&mun=009 |title= Milpa Alta Resumen municipal  |publisher=Government of Mexico |location=Mexico |language=es |trans-title=Milpa Alta municipal summary |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref><ref name="olvidado">{{cite news |title= Milpa Alta, un rincón "olvidado" de la capital |url= http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/ciudad/99953.html |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=January 22, 2010 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es|trans-title=Milpa Alta, a "forgotten" cornier of the capital }}</ref> About half of the borough’s residents live in or near Villa Milpa Alta and about eighty percent are under forty.<ref name="adapon5"/> It is also one of the city’s most rural and traditional areas. There is a dual system of government, administrative and agricultural, with the latter mostly tasked with the administration of common lands. The social organization of the area is traditional, based on families headed by a male, nuclear in the towns and extended in the more rural areas.<ref name="nahuasde"/> Men still hold most of the paying jobs, with most women classed as homemakers, although many of these work in family business, generally for no salary.<ref name="adapon5"/> While it has the lowest crime rates overall, it does have problems with alcoholism in men leading to domestic violence.<ref name="olvidado"/> The borough is gaining population from migration from places like the State of Mexico, [[Puebla]] and [[Oaxaca]].<ref name="nahuasde"/> and few people migrate out.<ref name="adapon52">Adapon, Joy. p.52</ref>

The borough contains a number of towns and localities. Those considered by the government to be urban (with 2010 population figures in parentheses) are: [[San Antonio Tecómitl]] (24,397), [[Villa Milpa Alta]] (18,274), [[San Pablo Oztotepec]] (15,507), [[San Salvador Cuauhtenco]] (13,856), [[San Francisco Tecoxpa]] (11,456), [[Santa Ana Tlacotenco]] (10,593), [[San Pedro Atocpan]] (8,283), [[San Bartolomé Xicomulco]] (4,340), [[San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan]] (3,676), and [[San Nicolás Tetelco]] (3,490).<ref name=SEDESOL>{{cite web|title=Milpa Alta|url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=09&mun=009|work=Catálogo de Localidades|publisher=[[Secretaría de Desarrollo Social]] (SEDESOL)|access-date=17 April 2014}}</ref> In addition, there are approximately 250 rural settlements with populations each of less than 1,000.<ref name=SEDESOL/>

Before the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|arrival of the Spanish]], the area was dominated by the [[Nahua peoples|Nahuas]].<ref name="palapa">{{cite web |url=http://www.cdi.gob.mx/ecoturismo/df_chicahuac_zacacalli.html |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130221040650/http://www.cdi.gob.mx/ecoturismo/df_chicahuac_zacacalli.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 21, 2013 |title=La Gran Palapa Chicahuac Zacacalli |publisher=Comisión Nacional para el Desarrollo de los Pueblos Indígenas |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref> Its one of the few places left in the city with [[Nahuatl]] speaking communities, with 4,007 people speaking an indigenous language {{As of|2010|lc=y}}.<ref name="microrreg"/><ref name="olvidado"/> The use of Nahuatl is widespread in the borough,<ref name="señorio">{{cite news |title= El señorío de Milpa Alta |author=Angeles Gonzalez Gamio |url=http://www.jornada.unam.mx/2011/05/15/opinion/036a1cap |newspaper=La Jornada |location=Mexico City |date= May 15, 2011 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=The dominion of Milpa Alta }}</ref> with the most concentrated in the towns of Santa Ana Tlacotenco,  San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan and San Pablo Oztotepec. Ethnic Nahuas are found in all of the borough’s main towns.<ref name="nahuasde"/><ref name="palapa"/> There are also efforts to preserve and promote the use of the language in the borough.<ref name="señorio"/>

The Nahuas are primarily Catholic with a number of indigenous beliefs still remaining and blended in. Most of these have to do with the agricultural cycle, often represented by veneration to the saints. Traditional medicine is still practiced by a number of Nahuas in combination with modern medicine. Starting in the 1950s, evangelical movements such as the [[Jehovah’s Witnesses]] have made inroads into the area.<ref name="nahuasde"/>  Traditional housing among the Nahuas is of [[adobe]], but these are being replaced by cement and cinderblock constructions. Traditional handcrafts include embroidered clothing and articles made from maguey fiber ([[ixtle]]).<ref name="nahuasde"/>  The current community struggles to maintain its identity and culture and to prevent being absorbed into the urban sprawl of Mexico City. One way of doing this is through ecotourism on tribal land including guided hikes, zip lines, [[temazcal]]s and camping. The community also sponsors reforestation events.<ref name="palapa"/>

The borough lacks theaters, shopping centers, supermarkets and hotels.<ref name="olvidado"/> However, it is one of the boroughs in the city that conserves many of its traditional religious festivals, with about 700 per year, about two per day somewhere in the borough.<ref name="olvidado"/> these fairs and festivals bring 1,200,000 visitors each year to the borough.<ref name="ferias">{{cite news |title=Secretaria de Turismo DF anuncia ferias en Milpa Alta |url=http://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/item/12899/26 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200613053330/https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/item/12899/26 |url-status=dead |archive-date=June 13, 2020 |newspaper=El Financiero |location=Mexico City |date=March 31, 2012 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=The Secretary of Tourism of Mexico City announces festival in Milpa Alta }}</ref> Each community in the borough, including the twelve main towns and each of these towns’ neighborhoods has a patron saint celebrated once a year.<ref name="nahuasde"/> The areas with the most festivals and other events are San Francisco Tecoxpa, San Pedro Atocpan, San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan, San Salvador Cuauhtenco, Santa Ana Tlacotenco, San Pablo Ozotepec, San Agustín Ohtenco, Villa Milpa Alta, San Jerónimo Miacatlán and San Juan Tepanahuac.<ref name="ferias"/> The most important saint day in the entire borough is that of [[Our Lady of the Assumption]] in August.<ref name="nahuasde"/> Other important religious events include the passion play, held jointly by the towns of San Francisco Tecoxpa, San Pedro Atocpan, San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan and San Antonio Tecómitl and requires six months of preparations. Carnival is celebrated in San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan, San Antonio Tecómitl, San Pablo Oztotepec and Villa Milpa Alta.<ref name="ferias"/> [[Day of the Dead]] is celebrated in the borough with altars, the cleaning and decorating of gravesites, masses and vigils like many other places in Mexico but it is also celebrated with the release of [[sky lantern]]s in communities such as San Agustin Ohtenco, as well as publicly held events such as concerts, the creation of monumental [[cartonería|papier-mâché]] skulls and even [[Mesoamerican ball game]]s.<ref name="difuntos">{{cite news |title=Celebran con globos de cantoya a los difuntos en Milpa Alta |url=http://www.elporvenir.com.mx/notas.asp?nota_id=624344 |newspaper=El Porvenir |location=Mexico City |date=November 2, 2012 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Celebrate the dead with sky lanterns in Milpa Alta |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151018230054/http://www.elporvenir.com.mx/notas.asp?nota_id=624344 |archive-date=October 18, 2015 }}</ref> [[Holy Week in Mexico|Holy Week]] is very important in the borough, especially the Passion Play held each year to reenact the passion and death of Jesus. The event involves over sixty actors, all residents of the borough chosen yearly. The current tradition was started in 1905, although it was suspended during the [[Mexican Revolution]]. The scenes of the play are enacted in several locations. [[Palm Sunday]] in San Agustin el Alto, the Asunción parish to the main church in Villa Milpa Alta for [[Good Friday]] .<ref name="semana">{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/semana-santa-en-milpa-alta-distrito-federal.html |title= La Semana Santa en Milpa Alta, Distrito Federal |author= Antonio Molina Martínez |date= April 2011 |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Holy Week in Milpa Alta, Federal District |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref>

Secular events include the Festival of Corn and Pulque in San Antonio Tecomitl in September,<ref name="pulque">{{cite news |title= Invitan a degustar pulque en Milpa Alta |author= SHARENII GUZMÁN ROQUE |url= http://www.eluniversaldf.mx/home/nota51031.html |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=September 29, 2012 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es|trans-title=Invitation to enjoy pulque in Milpa Alta }}</ref> Feria de la Nieve (Ice Cream Fair) in San Antonio Tecomitl in March, Feria Ganadera, Gastronómica y Artesanal (Livestock, Gastronomy and Handcraft Fair) in San Pablo Ozotepec in April, the Festival de Juegos Autóctonos celebrating native toys in San Juan Tapanáhuac,<ref name="ferias"/> the annual fair of San Lorenzo Tlacoyucan in August,<ref name="fundacion">{{cite news |title= Milpa Alta celebra 480 años de su fundación |author= PHENÉLOPE ALDAZ |url=http://www.eluniversaldf.mx/home/nota49307.html |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=August 8, 2012 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Milpa Alta celebrates 480 years since its foundation }}</ref> and the entire borough celebrates the founding of Villa Milpa Alta on 22 August with a Regional fair and lighting of a New Fire in the crater of the Teutli volcano. In 2012, this event celebrated the town’s 480th anniversary.<ref name="fundacion"/>

The borough is home to four significant balloon events, which together are called the Magic Route of Light. It begins with the Festival Multicultural de Globos de Cantolla (Multicultural Festival of [[sky lantern|Sky Lanterns]]) in September in Santa Ana Tlacotenco, with the main event of 3000 lanterns launched at once. It is followed by the Concurso Nacional de Globos y Faroles de Papel de China and the Encuentro Internacional de Constructores de Globos de Papel in San Agustin Ohtenco and San Antonio Tecomitl in November. The last is the Noche de Luces (Night of Lights) in San Francisco Tecoxpa in late November.<ref name="laruta">{{cite news |title= La Ruta Mágica de la Luz, Milpa Alta |author= Zaira Vázquez Espejo |url=http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/articulos/73649.html  |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=September 21, 2012 |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=The Magical Route of Light, Milpa Alta }}</ref>

==History==
The name Milpa Alta means “high cornfield.” “Alta” means high in Spanish and “milpa” is a Mexican Spanish word from Nahuatl referring to cornfields interspersed with other crops such as squash and maguey.<ref name="encmuc"/> The Nahua name for the area is Momochco Malacateticpac, which means “place of altars surrounded by mountains.” This name is derived from the various volcanoes in the area.<ref name="sustenable"/>

The recorded history of the area begins around 1240 when a [[Chichimeca]] group migrated into the Valley of Mexico from the north and founded the Malacachtepec Momozco dominion. They formed settlements in what is now the borough in places such as Malacatepec Momoxco, Ocotenco, Texcalapa, Tototepec, Tepetlacotanco, Huinantongo and Tlaxcomulco. In 1440, Mexica leader Hueyitlahuilli subdued these settlements and installed a leader. The capital of this dominion was centered on the town of Milpa Alta, in what are now the Santa Cruz, Los Angeles, San Mateo and Santa Martha neighborhoods with the name of Malacatepec Momoxco. The area formed a strategic point controlling the road between Tenochtitlan and Oaxtepec and Cuernavaca. At this time several lakeside docks, a ceremonial center, barracks and tribute collections centers were constructed, remnants of which remain.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="señorio"/>

The indigenous of this area, allied with the [[Aztec]]s, struggled against the Spanish for about 100 years before being subdued.<ref name="señorio"/> This caused many of the indigenous here to abandon their lands and hide in the mountains, making incursions into Spanish held territory to plunder. In 1528, a peace pact was made with these people and the following year, Spanish authorities acknowledge their right to own land and have local governors; however, they were required to pay tribute to the Spanish and convert to Christianity.<ref name="encmuc"/> The Spanish mostly kept their promise to allow indigenous rule except for a brief period in the 17th century. Organizationally, it was considered to be a district of Xochimilco. Compared to the rest of the valley area, it had little contact with the Spanish, allowing it to retain much of its indigenous character.<ref name="nahuasde"/>

The [[Franciscans]] were in charge of evangelization, naming Our Lady of the Assumption as patron.<ref name="nahuasde"/> For evangelization purposes, a modest hermitage dedicated to Saint Martha was constructed with later churches constructed in Tlatatlapocoyan and San Lorenzo. In 1570, the monastery and church of the Assumption was begun, taking a century to construct.<ref name="encmuc"/>

After [[Mexican War of Independence|Independence]], the area was initially part of the State of Mexico. The area’s incorporation into the Federal District of Mexico City began in 1854, when the district was expanded by [[Antonio López de Santa Anna]] expanded including part of what is now Milpa Alta.<ref name="señorio"/> However, towns such as San Pedro Atocpan were municipalities in the State of Mexico in the 19th century.<ref name="magico">{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/san-pedro-atocpan.html  |title= San Pedro Atocpan |date= 18 January 2012 |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |location=Mexico City |language=es |access-date=November 11, 2012 }}</ref> In 1903, the Federal District of Mexico City was expanded to include the rest of Milpa Alta.<ref name="nahuasde"/>

During the Mexican Revolution, the area generally sympathings with the [[Liberation Army of the South]].<ref name="encmuc"/> In 1914 this army marched to Mexico City from the state of Morelos against the regime of [[Victoriano Huerta]]. Part of that march included the occupation of the Milpa Alta area, forming a base. Here [[Emiliano Zapata]] ratified the [[Plan of Ayala]] on July 19, 1914.<ref name="zapata">{{cite web |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/zapata-y-su-paso-por-milpa-alta-distrito-federal.html |author= Edgar Anaya |title= Zapata y su paso por Milpa Alta, Distrito Federal  |date= 7 August 2017 |publisher= Mexico Desconocido magazine|location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Zapata and his pass through Milpa Alta }}</ref>

Reorganization of the Federal District of Mexico City created the modern borough, with the government in Villa Milpa Alta in 1929.<ref name="tenencia"/>

While part of the Federal District, most residents still talk about Mexico City as a separate entity.<ref name="adapon5"/> However, it is part of [[Greater Mexico City]].<ref name="tenencia"/> The pace of urbanization in the borough is slower in Milpa Alta than other outlying areas of the Federal District, but the growth of Mexico City since the mid 20th century has been affecting it.<ref name="tenencia"/><ref name="conservan"/>  Production of corn began to decrease in the mid 20th century.<ref name="adapon5"/> In the 1970s, this process began to hasten.<ref name="tenencia"/> The two major problems associated with this is illegal settlements or [[squatting]] on common land and [[illegal logging]]. Both of these are most serious in San Salavador Cuauhtenco, where squatters who have been there for years demand regularization and services and enforcers of environmental laws are threatened by residents.<ref name="conservan"/><ref name="peligroso">{{cite news |title= Ven peligroso vigilar bosques de Milpa Alta |url=http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/ciudad/111641.html |newspaper=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date= May 18, 2012  |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Protecting forests seen as dangerous in Milpa Alta }}</ref>

==Socioeconomics==
Milpa Alta is officially classed as one of the poorest in the Federal District by income, with 48.6% considered to be below the poverty line.<ref name="pobres">{{cite news |title= Milpa Alta, Tláhuac, Iztapalapa y A. Obregón, las delegaciones más pobres |url=http://www.jornada.unam.mx/2011/12/03/capital/030n2cap |newspaper=La Jornada |location=Mexico City |date= December 3, 2011 |page=30  |access-date=November 11, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Milpa Alta, Tláhuac, Iztapalapa and A. Obregon, the poorest boroughs }}</ref> However it is also considered to have a low level of socioeconomic marginalization.<ref name="microrreg"/> The discrepancy is likely due to the fact that the economy is based on cash and not all economic activity is reported.<ref name="adapon5"/> Most of the land is held in common, either in [[ejido]]s or other arrangements. There is a problem with the lack of formal titles to land, which has allowed irregular settlements.<ref name="nahuasde"/> There is a telegraph office, a post office and various city agencies. Medical attention is mostly provided by two large clinics, one administered by [[UNAM]] and the other by [[ISSSTE]] and various small ones in various towns.<ref name="nahuasde"/> It has little in the way of commerce and services, with no large chain stores, few banks, no movie theaters or little else in the way of entertainment.<ref name="tenencia"/>

The main economic activities of the borough are agriculture and food processing. Most agriculture is still done with traditional methods with only those of greater resources using machinery such as tractors.<ref name="nahuasde"/> The most important crop is the nopal cactus, with fields found just about everywhere including spaces between houses in the towns.<ref name="tenencia"/><ref name="olvidado"/> Founded in 1986, the Feria del Nopal is held in Villa Milpa Alta in June with the aim of promoting the consumption of the paddle cactus. The main event is the culinary exhibition of dishes made with the vegetable along with cultural, social, sporting and artistic events.<ref name="encmuc"/>  Other important crops include corn, beans, animal feed, fava beans, peas and honey. [[Pulque]] is produced for local consumption.<ref name="nahuasde"/>

One important processed food in Milpa Alta is the making of [[barbacoa]], sheep meat cooked in a pit oven lined with maguey leaves. This is made principally for weekend sales for traditional markets and street stands in most of Mexico City.<ref name="sabor">{{cite news |title= El sabor de Milpa Alta |author= Tatiana Adalid |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=May 3, 2000 |page=8 |language=es |trans-title=The flavor of Milpa Alta }}</ref> The most important barbacoa producing area is Barrio San Mateo in Villa Milpa Alta.<ref name="adapon5"/> About three thousand sheep are slaughtered and prepared as barbacoa each week in Milpa Alta,<ref name="adapon5"/> but barbacoa is increasingly being made with sheep meat imported from [[Australia]], [[New Zealand]] and the United States as it is cheaper than that produced in Mexico.<ref name="sabor"/> The barbacoa business in the area began in the 1940s and since then has been successful enough to allow many families to send their children to school and become professionals. Despite the younger generation’s higher education, many still participate in the family business. Its success has also allowed barbacoa families to gain a certain amount of prestige in the community.<ref name="adapon5051">Adapon, Joy. p.50-51</ref>

Even more important is the creation of pastes and powders to make [[mole (sauce)|mole sauce]]. These are created from the grinding and blending of twenty or more ingredients, which always include a variety of chili peppers. The mole sauces made are of various types such as rojo, verde, almendrado and about twenty others with trademarks.<ref name="encmuc"/><ref name="señorio"/> The main producer of mole in the borough is [[San Pedro Atocpan]], with almost all its residents involved in its production in some way.<ref name="encmuc"/> Most of this production is done in families or small cooperatives.<ref name="nahuasde"/> The restaurants in San Pedro Atocpan also specialize in mole and receive about 8,000 customers each week.<ref name="sabor"/><ref name="gastronomia">{{cite web |url=http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/gastronomia.html |title=Ven Milpa Alta Te Espera, Disfruta de… Esquisitos sabores ancestrales |publisher=Borough of Milpa Alta |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Come, Milpa Alta awaits you, Enjoy Exquisite Ancestral Flavors |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111008084551/http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/gastronomia.html |archive-date=2011-10-08 }}</ref> The Feria Nacional del Mole occurs each year in San Pedro Atocpan in October and receives thousands of visitors to the festival site as well as the forty restaurants in the town that serve meats in mole sauce.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The only other industry in Milpa Alta is small handcraft workshops making articles such as leather goods, furniture and textiles.<ref name="tenencia"/>

==Education==
Although there are various primary schools, two technical middle schools and two high school (one a vocational school run by the [[Instituto Politécnico Nacional]]), Milpa Alta has the highest adult illiteracy rate at 5.6%.<ref name="nahuasde"/><ref name="olvidado"/> Recently a technological institute called the Instituto Technológico de Milpa Alta was opened. Another educational and cultural institution is the Fábrica de Artes y Oficio Milpa Alta. It is the second of its type in the Federal District of Mexico City, patterned after the successful [[Fábrica de Artes y Oficios Oriente]] in [[Iztapalapa]]. Its function is to provide cultural and entertainment options to residents as well as classes in various arts and trades. In addition it supports the customs and traditions of the twelve indigenous communities found in the borough.<ref name="faro">{{cite web |url=http://www.cultura.df.gob.mx/index.php/recintos/faros/faro-de-milpalta |title=FARO de Milpa Alta |language=es |access-date=November 11, 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121010070408/http://www.cultura.df.gob.mx/index.php/recintos/faros/faro-de-milpalta |archive-date=October 10, 2012 }}</ref> It also hosts an annual [[Pantomime]], Clown and Circus Festival sponsored by the city’s secretary of culture. The purpose of the event is to promote the circus arts.<ref name="clown">{{cite web |url=http://www.cultura.df.gob.mx/index.php/cartelera/details/8291-3er-festival-de-pantomima-clown-y-circo-milpa-alta-2012 |title=3ER. ==Transportation==
Public transportation includes thirteen major bus route connecting the borough to [[Metro Tasqueña]], [[Metro Tlahuac]], the [[Central de Abasto, Mexico City|Central de Abastos]], [[La Merced Market, Mexico City|La Merced Market]], Xochimilco and [[Metro Santa Marta|Santa Martha Acatitla]], along with 23 smaller routes which are operated by private contractors.<ref name="conoce">{{cite web |url=http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/Racceso.html |title=Rutas de Acceso |publisher=Borough of Milpa Alta |location=Mexico city |language=es |trans-title=Access Routes |access-date=November 11, 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120905165940/http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/Racceso.html |archive-date=September 5, 2012 }}</ref> It takes about two hours by public transportation to travel from the center of Mexico City to Villa Milpa Alta. It can take up to three if traffic is bad, but lately, the subway made close Milpa Alta to the rest of the city via Tecomitl.<ref name="olvidado"/> For a short time beginning in the late 1980s, Milpa Alta was also served by [[trolleybus]]es of [[Servicio de Transportes Eléctricos]], with a route from [[Tláhuac]], but this service lasted only from October 1988<ref name="tm175-p8">{{cite magazine |last1=Morgan |first1=Steve |title=Mexico Review: Part 3 |date=January–February 1991 |magazine=Trolleybus Magazine |volume=27 |issue=175 |publisher=National Trolleybus Association|location=UK |page=8 |issn=0266-7452 |oclc=62554332}}</ref> to early 1991.<ref name="tm180">{{cite magazine |title=Trolleynews [regular news section]  |date=November–December 1991 |magazine=Trolleybus Magazine |volume=27 |issue=180 |publisher=National Trolleybus Association|location=UK |page=146 |issn=0266-7452 |oclc=62554332}}</ref>

Most of the borough is accessible by road. This has facilitated problems such as illegal logging and irregular homesteading. The main access roads to the borough are the federal highway connecting the south of the city with [[Oaxtepec]] in Morelos, the highway connecting San Pablo-Xochimilco and the Tulyehualco-Milpa Alta road.<ref name="sustenable"/>

==Landmarks==
[[File:AsuncionMariaMilpaAlta02.JPG|thumb|175px|Asuncion de Maria Church in Villa Milpa Alta]]
Most of the borough’s landmarks are churches and chapels dating from the colonial period. 
The Santa Marta Chapel is considered to be the founding church of Milpa Alta. From here the churches of the 17th, 18th and 19th centuries were founded. The first of these is the Parish of the Assumption of Mary constructed in the 16th century along with its former monastery in Villa Milpa Alta. Other colonial era churche/chapels include Nuestra Señora de la Concepción Chapel (1767), Santa Cruz Chapel, San Agustín el Alto Chapel (16th century), San Francisco de Asís Chapel (16th century), San Jeronimo Chapel (16th century), San Juan Bautista Chapel (16th, 17th and 19th centuries),  San Lorenzo Martir Chapel (1605), Calvario Hermitage (16th and 17th centuries), San Pablo Apostol Parish  (16th can 17th centuries), La Lupita Chapel (16th century), San Pedro Apostol Church (17th century), San Martin Chapel (16th and 17th centuries), Santa María de Guadalupe Chapel (16th and 17th centuries), San Francisco Chapel (16th century), Divino Salvador Chapel (16th century), Nuestra Señora de Santa Ana Parish (17th century) and the San Bartolome Chapel (17th century).<ref name="virreinal">{{cite web |url=http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/arquitectura.html |title=Ven Milpa Alta Te Espera, Disfruta de… Arquítectura Vírreinal |publisher=Borough of Milpa Alta |location=Mexico City |language=es |trans-title=Come, Milpa Alta awaits you, Enjoy Colonial Architecture |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120116065319/http://www.milpa-alta.df.gob.mx/turismointernet/arquitectura.html |archive-date=2012-01-16 }}</ref>

La Casona is one of the most important civil buildings in Milpa Alta both because of its construction and because of its history. It was constructed at the end of the 19th century and was the home of [[Rafael Coronel]]. Another important structure is the former headquarters of the Liberation Army of the South, now a museum.<ref name="virreinal"/>
