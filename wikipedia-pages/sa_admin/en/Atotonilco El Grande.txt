'''Atotonilco El Grande''' is a town and one of the 84 [[municipalities of Hidalgo]], in central-eastern [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|426.6|km2}}.

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 23,823.<ref>{{cite web|title=Atotonilco El Grande |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13012a.htm |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |accessdate=December 27, 2008 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070603201724/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/hidalgo/municipios/13012a.htm |archivedate=June 3, 2007 }}</ref>

==History==
In prehispanic times, Atotonilco was an [[Otomi]] settlement, though there were also some [[Nahuas]]. Atotonilco was a city-state that had subject towns, of which [[Acatlán, Hidalgo|Acatlán]] and [[Huasca de Ocampo]] (then known as Cuachquezaloyan) were the major two. Maguey was an important resource of the region. Atotonilco was conquered by [[Moctezuma I]], and became the center of a tributary province which grew with later conquests, including [[Tulancingo]]. It offered [[Manta (dress)|''mantas'']], warrior costumes, and staple foods as tribute to the Aztecs.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Berdan |first1=Frances |title=Aztec imperial strategies |date=1996 |publisher=Dumbarton Oaks Research Library and Collection |location=Washington, D.C |isbn=9780884022114 |pages=290-291}}</ref>
