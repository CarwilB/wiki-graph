'''Kabalebo''' is a [[resorts of Suriname|resort]] in [[Suriname]], located in the [[Sipaliwini District]].  Its population at the 2012 census was 2,291.<ref name="census"/>

==Geography==
Kabalebo is a resort in Suriname named after the [[Kabalebo River]] that flows through this area.
Clockwise, the Kabalebo resort borders the Upper [[Coppename River]] and resort to the East, it's adjacent to the [[Coeroeni River]] and resort in the South, bordered in the North across the Courantyne River to [[Guyana]] and also to [[Nickerie District|Nickerie]]. Kabalebo is an area around the river Kabalebo and it was formerly in the district of [[Nickerie District|Nickerie]], but since the re-organisation of the districts it lies in district [[Sipaliwini District|Sipaliwini]]. The residents are mainly Indigenous people, the original inhabitants of Suriname.<ref name="census"/> The major tribes are the [[Lokono]] and [[Warao people|Warao]].<ref name="planning"/>

The bigger villages in this resort are [[Apoera]] ''(Apura)'', [[Bakhuys]] ''(Bakhuis)'', Section and [[Washabo]] ''(Wasjabo)''. Smaller villages include: [[Lucie, Suriname|Lucie]], [[Sandlanding]], and [[Wanapan]]. Clinics and schools are located in Apura, and Washabo.<ref name="planning"/>

==Economic Development==
There is little economic activity in Kabalebo, although there have been attempts in the past to change this. There is the bauxite mine in the [[Bakhuis Mountains|Bakhuis mountain]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nsi-ins.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Surinames-Bakhuis-Bauxite-Mine-An-Independent-Review-of-SRKs-Impact-Assessment.pdf|title=Suriname's Bakhuis Bauxite Mine|access-date=24 May 2020|website=The North-South Institute|archive-date=18 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190618190937/http://www.nsi-ins.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Surinames-Bakhuis-Bauxite-Mine-An-Independent-Review-of-SRKs-Impact-Assessment.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> range with the corresponding (never used and in the meantime declining) railway line to [[Apoera]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nicospilt.com/index_suriname.htm|title=Langs de Rails|website=Nico Split|access-date=25 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> and during the reign of Prime Minister [[Henck Arron]] (1975-1980), there were plans, part of the [[West Suriname Plan]], to build a dam in Kabalebo for power generation for the benefit of the mining activities. The construction of the reservoir is still considered.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.nsi-ins.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/West-Suriname-WAT-Betekent-Een-Geintegreerde-Aluminium-Industrie-Voor-De-Inheemse-Gemeenschappen.pdf |title=nsi-ins.ca |access-date=2013-05-20 |archive-date=2019-06-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190618190907/http://www.nsi-ins.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/West-Suriname-WAT-Betekent-Een-Geintegreerde-Aluminium-Industrie-Voor-De-Inheemse-Gemeenschappen.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> A project of permanent agriculture is under way with 100 [[hectare]]s of [[Xanthosoma sagittifolium|American taro]], 80 hectares of [[cassave]] and 3 hectares of [[cashew]] tree.<ref name="planning">{{cite web|url=https://www.planningofficesuriname.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/STRUCTUUR-ANALYSE-IV.pdf| title=Structuur analyse|website=Planning Office Suriname|access-date=25 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>

==Tourist Activity==
In the Midwest of Suriname, in the middle of the [[Amazon rain-forest]], a jungle-accommodation  has been built; the Kabalebo Nature Resort. Kabalebo is also called a bird watching paradise for bird watchers in the jungle.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://kabalebo.com/site/|title=Nature Kalebo Resort|access-date=19 May 2020}}</ref>

The [[Kabalebo Airstrip]] (ICAO: SMKA), was constructed as part of [[Operation Grasshopper]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mets.sr/portfolio/kabalebo-nature-resort/|title=Kabalebo Nature Resort|website=METS.sr|access-date=24 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>
