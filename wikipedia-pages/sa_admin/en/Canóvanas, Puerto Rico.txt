'''Canóvanas''' ({{IPA|es|kaˈnoβanas}}, {{IPA|es|kaˈnoβanaʔ|local}}) is a [[Canóvanas barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] in [[Puerto Rico]], located in the northeastern region, north of [[Juncos, Puerto Rico|Juncos]] and [[Las Piedras, Puerto Rico|Las Piedras]]; south of [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]]; east of [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]]; and west of [[Río Grande, Puerto Rico|Río Grande]]. Canóvanas is spread over 6 barrios and [[Canóvanas barrio-pueblo|Canóvanas Pueblo]] (the downtown area and administrative center). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

==History==
[[File:Smithsonian 1901 map of Puerto Rico caciques.png|thumb|Early 20th c. map published by the Smithsonian Institution Bureau of American Ethnology showing the ''Cayniabon'' area under Chief Canobana|left]]
The region of what is now Canóvanas belonged to the [[Taíno]] region of ''Cayniabón'', also the [[Taíno language|native name]] of the [[Río Grande de Loíza|Grande de Loiza River]], which stretched from the central eastern region of Puerto Rico to the northeast coast of the island.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webring.org/l/rd?ring=taino;id=2;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.taino-tribe.org%2Fjatiboni-s.html|title=Gobierno Tribal del Pueblo Jatibonicu Taíno de Puerto Rico|access-date=February 3, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005010944/http://www.webring.org/l/rd?ring=taino;id=2;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.taino-tribe.org%2Fjatiboni-s.html|archive-date=October 5, 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref> The region was led by cacique ''Canobaná'', from which the actual name is derived, in the south half, and female Cacica ''Loaiza'' in the north (mostly modern day [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]]). During the [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish colonization]], the region of Canóvanas was granted to Miguel Díaz, who turned the [[Taíno]] yucayeque into a ranch. It is said that Canóbana, along with Loaiza, were supporters of the Spanish regime and did not join the [[Taino rebellion of 1511]].<ref name="enciclopedia">{{cite web|url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=07121801&page=2|title=Fundación e Historia: Canóvanas|work=Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico|access-date=February 22, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140111161231/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=07121801&page=2|archive-date=January 11, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Canóvanas was a [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|''barrio'']] (district) of the municipality of [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]] for over 400 years. In 1902, the [[Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico]] approved a law for the consolidation of certain municipalities. As a result, both Canóvanas and Loíza were incorporated into the municipality of [[Río Grande, Puerto Rico|Río Grande]]. However, in 1905 a new law revoked the previous one, returning Canóvanas to its previous state of ''barrio'' of Loíza.<ref name="psh">{{cite web |url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/canovanas.html |title=Canóvanas... Pueblo Valeroso |work=Proyecto Salon Hogar |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222022029/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/canovanas.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012 }}</ref>

In 1909, the Municipal administration of Loíza was transferred to the barrio of Canóvanas, which was more developed than the Loíza region. Also, the construction of the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]] facilitated the communication with Canóvanas. As a result of the transfer, land was acquired to build a new [[city hall]], a town square, a [[slaughterhouse]], and a cemetery. A {{convert|20|acre|ha|adj=on}} plot of land was purchased by Don Luis Hernaiz Veronne, a townhall senator and local farmer. The site location was strategic, to intercept traffic from the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]], and from other nearby roads like the [[Puerto Rico Highway 185|PR-185]].

However, the transfer was not well received by the residents of the original town of Loíza, then renamed '''Loiza Aldea'''. It was not until a law was passed on June 30, 1969, that both municipalities were recognized as having "clearly different population nuclei" recommending the establishment of two separate municipalities. The change was approved in 1970 by Governor [[Luis A. Ferré]].<ref name="psh" />

Like other nearby towns, the proximity of Canóvanas to the capital, [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]], has allowed extraordinary urban and commercial development in the region. The municipality became renown in the 1990s for popularizing the urban legend of the [[chupacabra]]s.<!-- will integrate later
Barrio Canóvanas was for two centuries an agricultural zone where [[sugar cane]] was cultivated and sugar produced in [[sugar mill|mills]] belonging to several local families; the Zequeira, Gambaro, Calderón, and later, families of [[Irish immigration to Puerto Rico|Irish origin]]; the Seary, Quidgley, Soegard, Skerret, and others.
Other barrios of Loíza (which later became part of Canóvanas) were those in the rural, mountainous parts of the municipality; Hato Puerco, Cambalache, Cubuy and Lomas. Hato Puerco is mentioned in Municipal Protocols as early as 1827.In 1970, Loíza and Canóvanas were separated as municipalities and the old rural barrios of Hato Puerco, Cubuy and Lomas, as well as Torrecilla Alta, a former sugarcane workers colony next to the old Central Canovanas, were all incorporated into the new municipality.-->

==Geography==
Canóvanas sits on the [[Northern Coastal Plain (Puerto Rico)|Northern Coastal Plain]] region of Puerto Rico. It is bordered by the municipalities of [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]], [[Río Grande, Puerto Rico|Río Grande]], [[Las Piedras, Puerto Rico|Las Piedras]], [[Juncos, Puerto Rico|Juncos]], [[Gurabo, Puerto Rico|Gurabo]], and [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]].<ref name="psh" /> Canóvanas covers only 28 square miles (72.8&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>).<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/canovanas-municipality/|title=Canóvanas Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org}}</ref>

Canóvanas combines flat [[alluvial plain]]s in the center and north, areas with both gentle hills and rugged, deeply dissected mountainous areas made up of [[volcaniclastic]] rocks ([[lava]] flows and exposed intrusive [[igneous rock]]s) to the southeast and south. The [[Cuchilla de Santa Inés]], a karstic limestone hill ([[mogote]]) with an elevation of 328 feet, rises from coastal sediments on the northeast of the municipality near San Isidro, while the [[Cuchilla El Asomante]] lies at the south with elevations that range from 656 to 2,296 feet.

On the southeast, Canóvanas features portions of the [[Sierra de Luquillo]] mountain range, with the Cerro El Negro being the tallest peak in the municipality at 2,592 feet. Other notable peaks are La Peregrina (1,903 feet) and Pitahaya (951 feet), both located at Barrio Hato Puerco.<ref name="enciclopedia" />

===Water features===
Much of the flat plains are part of the flood-prone alluvial valley of the [[Río Grande de Loíza]] and its main tributaries, the [[Río Canóvanas]] and [[Río Canovanillas]]. [[Flood]]s are typical during the storm season, between June and November. Other important tributaries are the [[Río Herrera]] and [[Río Cubuy (Loiza, Puerto Rico)|Río Cubuy]], as well as numerous creeks.<ref>{{cite web | title= 50061800 RIO CANOVANAS NR CAMPO RICO, PR | website=USGS Water Data for the Nation | url=https://pr.water.usgs.gov/data/gis/drainage/DA_50061800_lttr.pdf}}</ref>

Official population records for Canóvanas start in 1980, after the municipality was officially separated from [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]]. In 30 years, the population has increased by almost 50% according to the [[2010 United States Census|2010 census]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Población de Puerto Rico por Municipios: 1930–2000|url=http://ceepur.org/censo2000/1930-2000.html|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130321155203/http://ceepur.org/censo2000/1930-2000.html|archive-date=March 21, 2013|work=CEEPUR}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|date=March 24, 2011|title=Población de Puerto Rico por Municipios, 2000 y 2010|url=http://electionspuertorico.org/referencia/censo2010/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120603161542/http://electionspuertorico.org/referencia/censo2010/|archive-date=June 3, 2012|access-date=February 22, 2013|work=Elections Puerto Rico}}</ref>

According to the 2010 Census, 61% of the population identifies themselves as [[White Puerto Ricans|white]], and 21.6% as [[Afro–Puerto Ricans|black]]. Also, 48.6% of the population identified themselves as males, and 51.4% as females. Finally, 26.7% of the population is under 18 years old. The next biggest percentage of population (21.5%) is between 35 and 49 years old.<ref>{{cite web|title=2010 Census Interactive Population Search: Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|url=https://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130308164225/http://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|archive-date=March 8, 2013|work=US Census 2010}}</ref>

==Tourism==
Although Canóvanas is not particularly known for its touristic importance, there are several landmarks and places of interest to visit. The [[Jesús T. Piñero House]] is located along the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]]. The residence, which was built around 1931, houses a museum dedicated to the life of [[Jesús T. Piñero]], first Puerto Rican governor of the island.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canovanas.pr/getdoc/9c9564b1-03ab-408f-aa1a-630496c326cb/FLYER-CASA-MUSEO-JESUS--T-PINERO.aspx|title=Casa Museo Jesús T. Piñero|work=Canóvanas.PR|access-date=March 1, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140822133808/http://www.canovanas.pr/getdoc/9c9564b1-03ab-408f-aa1a-630496c326cb/FLYER-CASA-MUSEO-JESUS--T-PINERO.aspx|archive-date=August 22, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/Casa-Museo-Jesus-T--Pinero.aspx|title=Casa Museo Jesús T. Piñero|work=Canóvanas.PR|access-date=March 1, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140822205417/http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/Casa-Museo-Jesus-T--Pinero.aspx|archive-date=August 22, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The [[Hipódromo Camarero]] is also a tourist attraction for horserace enthusiasts of the island and the [[Caribbean]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/Hipodromo-El-Comandante.aspx|title=Hipódromo Camarero|work=Canóvanas.PR|access-date=March 1, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140823030829/http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/Hipodromo-El-Comandante.aspx|archive-date=August 23, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> Other places of interest are the ruins of the [[Central Canóvanas|Canóvanas Sugar Mill]], El Español Bridge, the [[Ceiba|Old Ceiba Tree]], and Villarán Park, which features the historic [[Villarán Bridge]], the only one of its kind in Puerto Rico and the United States.

To stimulate local tourism, the [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]] launched the ''Voy Turistiendo'' ("I'm Touring") campaign, with a passport book and website. The Canóvanas page lists {{lang|es|Pico el Toro}}, {{lang|es|El Hipódromo}} and the [[Canóvanas barrio-pueblo|Pueblo]] with its historic architecture, as places of interest.<ref>{{cite book | title= Pasaporte: Voy Turisteando |publisher=Compañia de Turismo de Puerto Rico | year=2021| language=es}}</ref>

==Economy==
===Agriculture===
The economy of Canóvanas has traditionally relied on agriculture, primarily [[sugarcane]] and [[Coffea arabica|coffee]]. There was an important [[sugar mill]] located in the [[Puerto Rico Highway 185|PR-951]] from Canóvanas to Loíza. It belonged to ''Loíza Sugar Company'', and then to ''Fajardo Sugar Company''. However, the mill closed in 1965.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://blogs.uprm.edu/canadeazucar/central-canovanas/|title=Centrales Azucareras de Puerto Rico|work=UPRM|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090214224457/http://blogs.uprm.edu/canadeazucar/central-canovanas/|archive-date=February 14, 2009|url-status=live}}</ref> In 1999, the structure was declared of historical importance by the [[Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/La-Central-Azucarera.aspx|title=Central Azucarera – Río Grande de Loíza|work=Canovanas website|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140822234024/http://www.canovanas.pr/Lugares-de-Interes/La-Central-Azucarera.aspx|archive-date=August 22, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> There's also growth of minor vegetables and fruits, as well as a minor cattle and poultry industry. Most of Canóvanas flat areas are subject to occasional flooding and are used as pastures for cattle.

''Best Iguana Puerto Rico Meat'' in Canóvanas is the only company in Puerto Rico certified for processing, packaging and distributing [[iguana meat]]. The [[green iguana]] is an [[Invasive species in the United States|invasive species]] of Puerto Rico.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.informeagricola.com/iguana-odiosa-plaga/ |title=Iguana: Odiosa plaga - Informe Agrícola |access-date=July 13, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190713232709/https://www.informeagricola.com/iguana-odiosa-plaga/ |archive-date=July 13, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Commerce===
In recent years, Canóvanas economy has shifted to commerce and industry, supplemented by the production of fresh milk. There has also been an increase in retail businesses. There are three main shopping malls, located along the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]] in the Canóvanas region. These malls such as [[The Outlet 66 Mall]] are the location of main stores like [[Wal-Mart]], [[Marshalls]], [[Burlington (department store)|Burlington]], and others.

Canóvanas is referred to as "The Door to the East" due to its location on the eastern edge of the [[San Juan Metropolitan Area]] sprawl, and its proximity to the northeast region of Puerto Rico. Also the expansion of [[PR-66|Route 66]] has sparked new interest in Canóvanas as an industrial and commercial sector. Recently the largest local supermarket chain brand [[Supermercados Econo|Econo]], has started the construction of its consolidated operations facilities with the construction of a super complex which will have the administrative headquarters, cold storage warehouse and main storages.

The municipal government has an economic development plan called ''Canovanas Opened for Business'' in which it provides fast legal and permit assistance, tax breaks and incentives for new commercial opportunities moving or establishing their businesses in Canóvanas. This has prompted the emergence of new small businesses and high demand for professional and technical services.

===Industrial===
The industrial sector is growing with large international pharmaceuticals like [[AstraZeneca]], IPR Pharmaceuticals, QBD, and other manufacturing plants in Canóvanas.

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Canóvanas celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in October. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de Nuestra Sra. del Pilar}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref>{{cite encyclopedia|url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/ing/article.cfm?ref=07121801&page=3|title=Canóvanas: Events|encyclopedia=Encyclopedia Puerto Rico|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140823055542/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/ing/article.cfm?ref=07121801&page=3|archive-date=August 23, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Canóvanas include:
* May—Holy Cross Festival
* December—''Christmas in the Country''

===Sports===
Although Canóvanas has no professional sports team currently active, several of its past teams have been notable. Traditionally, local sports teams bear the nickname of "Indios". The [[Indios de Canóvanas]], of the [[Baloncesto Superior Nacional]], won the championship two years in a row (1983–1984) and reached the finals in 1988.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bsnpr.com/estadisticas/campeonatos.asp|title=Campeonatos Baloncesto Superior Nacional|work=BSNPR.com|author=VICOMPR|year=2013|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130318172114/http://bsnpr.com/estadisticas/campeonatos.asp|archive-date=March 18, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref> Guard [[Angelo Cruz]] and center [[Ramón Ramos]] were two of the key players of the team during that era. However, the team disappeared during the 1990s. There have been movements to reestablish the team, but they've been unsuccessful.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.primerahora.com/clamanporlosindiosdecanovanas-475984.html|title=Claman por los Indios de Canóvanas|newspaper=[[Primera Hora (Puerto Rico)|Primera Hora]]|author=Rosa, Jessica|date=February 18, 2011|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110221105411/http://www.primerahora.com/clamanporlosindiosdecanovanas-475984.html|archive-date=February 21, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

The Indias of Canóvanas team, from the [[Liga de Voleibol Superior Femenino]], also won a number of championships.

Canóvanas is also the location of [[Hipódromo Camarero]], Puerto Rico's only horse racetrack. The track, which was formerly named [[El Nuevo Comandante]] was established in 1976.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://hipodromo-camarero.com/sobre-nosotros/nuestra-historia/|title=Nuestra Historia: Hipódromo Camarero|work=Hipódromo Camarero|author=Colón, Jorge|year=2013|access-date=February 28, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130302144339/http://hipodromo-camarero.com/sobre-nosotros/nuestra-historia/|archive-date=March 2, 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Government==
[[File:Canovanas City Hall.JPG|thumb|220px|Canóvanas City Hall]]
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a mayor, elected every four years. The current mayor of Canóvanas is [[Lornna Soto]], of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP), who was first elected in the [[2012 Puerto Rican General Election]]. She is the daughter of long-serving former Mayor [[Chemo Soto|Jose "Chemo" Soto]], who distinguished himself for his eccentricities in clothing,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.primerahora.com/chemosotoesunfashionista-572900.html|title=Chemo Soto es un "fashionista"|work=[[Primera Hora (Puerto Rico)|Primera Hora]]|author=Rodríguez, Arys|date=October 29, 2011|access-date=February 25, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120503021552/http://www.primerahora.com/chemosotoesunfashionista-572900.html|archive-date=May 3, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref> and for successfully using the urban legend of the [[Chupacabra]] to promote the city.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.primerahora.com/chemosotoestalistoparasaliracapturaralchupacabras-660838.html|title="Chemo" Soto está listo para salir a capturar al chupacabras|work=[[Primera Hora (Puerto Rico)|Primera Hora]]|author=Figueroa, Bárbara and Mara Resto|date=June 19, 2012|access-date=February 25, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120924094435/http://www.primerahora.com/chemosotoestalistoparasaliracapturaralchupacabras-660838.html|archive-date=September 24, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref> His daughter, [[Lornna Soto|Lornna]], also served as a member of the [[Senate of Puerto Rico]] from 2004 to 2013.

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VIII]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Marissa Jiménez]] and [[Héctor Joaquín Sánchez Álvarez]] were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Carolina_VIII.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241115225812/https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Carolina_VIII.xml |date=November 15, 2024 }} on CEEPUR</ref> Representatives [[Wanda Del Valle Correa]] (District 38) and [[Carmen Medina Calderón]] (District 37) represent different regions of Canóvanas in the [[Puerto Rico House of Representatives|House of Representatives]].

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=June 15, 2021}}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag of Canóvanas features a purple background with a wide yellow band across, and the town's [[coat of arms]] in the center.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=CANOVANAS | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=February 19, 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/CANOVANAS.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001501/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/CANOVANAS.html | archive-date=February 19, 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=September 16, 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The coat of arms features a shield with the same colors (purple background and a yellow band). The colors are taken from the banner of the "Hijos y Amigos Ausentes de Canóvanas". A broken chain symbolizes the separation of Canóvanas from [[Loiza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]]. The crown in the middle represents the supremacy of Cacique Canobaná. The laurels are a symbol of the 23 consecutive wins achieved by the Loíza Indians basketball team, establishing a record in Puerto Rico, also represented by the basket in the middle. The rising sun, with its sixteen rays of light, indicate the sprouting of a new municipality in Puerto Rico and the number of incumbent mayors before Canóvanas was separated from Loíza.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

The coat of arms also features a white banner below with the inscription "1130 1909, Canobaná del Cayniabón, 8–16 1970". The first date, November 30, 1909, is the date of the installation of the municipal seat of Loíza in Canóvanas. The second date, August 15, 1970, is the date of the official founding of Canóvanas as a separate municipality. The names of Canobaná and Cayniabón make reference to the [[Taíno people|Taíno]] heritage of the region. Finally, a [[coronet]] in the form of a three-tower mural crown stands above the shield.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canovanas.pr/Datos-de-Interes/Simbolos.aspx|title=Símbolos de Canóvanas|work=Canovanas.PR|access-date=March 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140822225733/http://www.canovanas.pr/Datos-de-Interes/Simbolos.aspx|archive-date=August 22, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

===Nicknames===
Canóvanas is known by various names. It is known as the "Pueblo Valeroso" after Cacique Yuira lost her life defending the Spanish people from her own people, the [[Taíno]]s. It is also known as the "City of Indians" because of its important [[Taíno]] heritage. Canóvanas is also known as the "City of Races", because of the [[Hipódromo Camarero]], and the "[[Chupacabras|Town of the Chupacabras]]" because of the alleged sightings of the creature, and the beliefs in it of former mayor, [[José Chemo Soto]].<ref name="psh" />

==Transportation==
[[File:PR DR4339 4 20 2018 CANOVANAS-1904 medium.jpg|thumb|A new bridge was constructed in 2018 after the previous bridge collapsed from flooding due to [[Hurricane Maria]].]]
The main road to Canóvanas is the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]] that crosses the municipality from east to west. Distance from the [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|capital]] is roughly 15 minutes.<ref name="psh" /> Other roads that lead to Canóvanas are [[Puerto Rico Highway 185|PR-185]] that enters between the [[Lomas, Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|Lomas]] and [[Hato Puerco, Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|Hato Puerco]] barrios, [[Puerto Rico Highway 186|PR-186]] of the [[Cubuy, Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|Cubuy]] ward, and [[PR-957]] of the Hato Puerco ward. Roads 874 and 188 enter the town from the north, the former at [[Torrecilla Alta, Canóvanas, Puerto Rico|Torrecillas Alta]] from [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]] and the latter at [[Canóvanas barrio-pueblo|Canóvanas Pueblo]] from [[Loíza, Puerto Rico|Loíza]]. In 2012, the [[Puerto Rico Highway 66|PR-66]], which starts in [[Carolina, Puerto Rico|Carolina]], was extended to lead directly into Canóvanas.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/arecorrercasitodalacostanortesindetenerse-1343421.html|title=A recorrer casi toda la costa norte sin detenerse|work=[[El Nuevo Día]]|author=Caquías, Sandra|date=September 14, 2012|access-date=March 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121117054321/http://www.elnuevodia.com/arecorrercasitodalacostanortesindetenerse-1343421.html|archive-date=November 17, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>

There is also a terminal for public cars in front of the town square, as well as service provided by taxis, and independent public cars.

Canovanas is reasonably close to [[Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport]] in Carolina and to the [[Port of San Juan]].

There are 29 bridges in Canóvanas.<ref name="bridge">{{cite web |title=Canóvanas Bridges |url=http://bridgereports.com/pr/canovanas/ |website=National Bridge Inventory Data |publisher=US Dept. of Transportation |access-date=February 20, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190221000541/http://bridgereports.com/pr/canovanas/ |archive-date=February 21, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>
