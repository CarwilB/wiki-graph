'''Grand-Goâve''' ({{IPA|fr|ɡʁɑ̃ ɡɔav}}; {{langx|ht|Grangwav}}) is a [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] in the [[Léogâne Arrondissement]] in the [[Ouest (department)|Ouest]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of southwestern [[Haiti]].

The [[Rivière de Grand Goâve]] passes to the east of the town.  It is bridged by National Route No. 2 to the south and forded by Rue Tonnere to the east.

==History==
The town is one of the oldest cities of the country, and was named ''Goâve'' by the [[Amerindian]]s. The Spanish called it ''Aguava'' at the end of the 16th century. After French colonization through the releasing of the Spanish, the French divided the city into two halves, Grand-Goâve and [[Petit-Goâve]].

===2010 earthquake===
Grand-Goâve was 90% destroyed by the [[2010 Haiti earthquake|earthquake]] of 12 January 2010.<ref>{{in lang|fr}} Le Quotidien, [http://www.cyberpresse.ca/le-quotidien/le-quotidien-du-jour/201001/21/01-941429-grand-goave-devastee-le-cri-du-coeur-de-rita-vallee.php "Grand-Goâve dévastée: le cri du coeur de Rita Vallée"], '''Dave Ainsley''', ''21 January 2010'' (accessed 24 January 2010)</ref> All public buildings were destroyed, including the schools, city hall, and police station.<ref>{{in lang|fr}} La Presse (Montreal), [http://www.cyberpresse.ca/international/amerique-latine/seisme-en-haiti/201001/18/01-940162-deux-quebecoises-sont-mortes-a-grand-goave.php "Deux Québécoises sont mortes à Grand-Goâve"], '''Émilie Côté''', ''18 January 2010'' (accessed 21 January 2010)</ref> The trembling created a landslide which formed a [[landslide dam]] that can potentially contain a large basin of water. A dam collapse would directly outflow the contents through the city. The dam is located on the [[Rivière de Grand Goâve]], a dozen kilometres from the city.<ref>{{in lang|fr}} La Presse, [http://www.cyberpresse.ca/international/amerique-latine/seisme-en-haiti/201002/15/01-949725-un-barrage-menace-de-ceder-a-grand-goave.php "Un barrage menace de céder à Grand-Goâve"], '''Philippe Mercure''', ''15 February 2010'' (accessed 16 February 2010)</ref>

On the 19th, 1300 US Marines were deployed equally between [[Petit-Goâve]] and Grand-Goâve.<ref>{{in lang|fr}} Agence France-Presse, [http://www.cyberpresse.ca/international/amerique-latine/seisme-en-haiti/201001/19/01-940784-les-americains-passent-a-la-vitesse-superieure.php "Les Américains passent à la vitesse supérieure"], '''Clarens Renois''', ''19 January 2010'' (accessed 28 January 2010)</ref> [[Catholic Relief Services]] have been distributing the aid that the US military has been delivering.<ref>IPS, [http://www.ipsnews.net/news.asp?idnews=50144 "HAITI: Local Leaders Shut Out of Military-Run Relief Efforts"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100130114834/http://www.ipsnews.net/news.asp?idnews=50144 |date=2010-01-30 }}, '''Ansel Herz''', ''28 January 2010'' (accessed 29 January 2010)</ref> {{USS|Bataan|LHD-5|6}} was deployed in [[Baie de Grand Goâve]] to help rebuild Grand-Goâve.<ref>Windsor Star, [https://windsorstar.com/news/World+Report/2507562/story.html "World Report"]{{Dead link|date=June 2024 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}, '''Star News Service''', ''1 February 2010'' (accessed 2 February 2010)</ref>

As of 9 February 2010, the US [[24th Marine Expeditionary Unit]] is rotating out of Haiti, having been replaced by the US [[22nd Marine Expeditionary Unit]], in their position on {{USS|Bataan|LHD-5|6}} and [[Carrefour, Ouest|Carrefour]], [[Léogâne]], Grand-Goâve and Petit-Goâve.<ref>WITN, [http://www.witn.com/stateregional/headlines/83871422.html "24th Marine Expeditionary Unit Leaving Haiti"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718073539/http://www.witn.com/stateregional/headlines/83871422.html |date=2011-07-18 }}, '''NBC News''', ''9 February 2010'' (accessed 10 February 2010)</ref><ref>DVIDS, [http://www.dvidshub.net/?script=news/news_show.php&id=45117 "22nd MEU Moves Into Carrefour"], '''22MEU''', ''8 February 2010'' (accessed 10 February 2010)</ref>
