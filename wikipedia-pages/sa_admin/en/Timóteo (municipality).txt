'''Timóteo''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the interior of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]], in the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]] of Brazil. Situated in the [[Vale do Rio Doce (mesoregion)|Vale do Rio Doce]], it is part of the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area|Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]] and lies approximately {{convert|200|km|mi}} east of the state capital, [[Belo Horizonte]]. The municipality covers an [[area]] of just over {{convert|140|km2|sqmi}}, with {{convert|18|km2|sqmi}} classified as [[Urban area|urban]], and its [[Population (human biology)|population]] was estimated at 84,087 inhabitants in 2024.

The settlement of Timóteo began in the mid-19th century when Francisco de Paula e Silva acquired three ''sesmarias'' in the region and established himself in one, located in the present-day Alegre neighborhood near the Timóteo Stream. Francisco developed agriculture and cattle raising, fostering the formation of a village later named São Sebastião do Alegre. In 1938, the area became a district under [[Antônio Dias]], already bearing the name Timóteo. In the following decade, it was selected as the site for the industrial hub of Acesita, now known as [[Aperam South America]]. This development was facilitated by the ease of receiving raw materials and transportation via the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]], the availability of water from the [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]], and wood in the vast local forests. In 1948, Timóteo was annexed to the municipality of [[Coronel Fabriciano]].

At Acesita's request, a workers' village was constructed parallel to the original urban core, but its growth spurred Timóteo's emancipation in 1964. This led to the city being divided into two distinct clusters: one comprising neighborhoods built by the company around the Centro-Norte, still referred to as "Acesita" despite the company’s name change, and the other stemming from the original settlements in the Centro-Sul, known as "Timóteo." Although the service sector has grown, industry remains the main source of municipal revenue, and its presence has contributed to the formation of the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region, one of the main urban centers in the state's interior.

Approximately 35% of Timóteo’s area is protected by the [[Rio Doce State Park]], the largest [[Atlantic Forest]] reserve in Minas Gerais. The city is known for traditions such as [[handicraft]]s, [[samba school]]s, and ''congado'', as well as attractions such as the Ana Moura Peak; the São José Operário Church, built by Acesita for the religious activities of the former workers' village; and the 29 de Abril and 1º de Maio squares, key leisure and event venues in the region. Through the Aperam Acesita Foundation, Aperam South America maintains a cultural center featuring a theater, a museum, and spaces for exhibitions, courses, and theater classes, while also offering workshops in schools and public areas.

==History==

===Colonization of the region===
Exploration of the region, initially known as the ''Sertões do Rio Doce'', began in the late 16th century with expeditions seeking precious metals. However, settlement was restricted in the early 17th century to prevent [[gold]] smuggling from areas near [[Diamantina, Minas Gerais|Diamantina]]. Colonization was permitted in 1755,<ref name="Circuito_HistóriaFabriciano">{{cite web|url=http://www.circuitomataatlantica.tur.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=159 |title=Coronel Fabriciano |publisher=Circuito Turístico Mata Atlântica de Minas Gerais |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140209120652/http://www.circuitomataatlantica.tur.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=159 |archive-date=February 9, 2014}}</ref> but the presence of indigenous groups in the [[Vale do Rio Doce (mesoregion)|Vale do Rio Doce]] posed challenges to development. To protect settlers, "quarters" were established in the early 19th century under the "4th Division of Rio Doce," which included the current municipality.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> The area was home to the Borun (also called [[Botocudo]]s) indigenous people, who inhabited the vast local [[Atlantic Forest]].<ref name="DescubraMinas_PERD"/> In 1820, the region, then known as Alegre, was under the Onça Pequena Quarter, headquartered in [[Jaguaraçu]]. During this period, Guido Marlière, the commander of the Rio Doce divisions, encouraged the [[catechesis]] of indigenous groups in eastern Minas Gerais in order to facilitate colonization and avoid systematic extermination.<ref name="Câmara_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.cam.mg.gov.br/historia/casa-de-memoria/cronologia |title=Cronologia |publisher=Câmara Municipal |date=2002 |access-date=October 17, 2016 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120805230121/http://www.timoteo.cam.mg.gov.br/historia/casa-de-memoria/cronologia |archive-date=August 5, 2012}}</ref>
[[File:Vista parcial do B. Alegre, Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|left|The Alegre neighborhood, where the settlement of the current municipality began.]]

Despite a ban on attacks against indigenous peoples in 1831,<ref name="Câmara_História"/> they were nearly extinct in the Vale do Rio Doce by 1832, as documented, encouraging further settlement and colonization.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> On September 11, 1831, Francisco de Paula e Silva (known as Chico Santa Maria, from [[Santa Maria de Itabira]]) arrived with his family and numerous slaves, settling near the present-day Alegre neighborhood. Chico Santa Maria received three ''sesmarias'' (Alegre, Limoeiro, and Timóteo)<ref name="PrefeituraFabri_PrimeirosHab.">{{cite web|url=http://www.fabriciano.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/6499/Primeiros-habitantes |title=Primeiros habitantes |publisher=Prefeitura de Coronel Fabriciano |author=Assessoria de Comunicação |date=July 3, 2009 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141021113146/http://www.fabriciano.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/6499/Primeiros-habitantes |archive-date=October 21, 2014 |url-status=dead}}</ref> in the region along the watercourse he named "Timóteo Stream," where he developed agriculture and cattle raising<ref name="Prefeitura_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/1/historia-de-timoteo |title=Historia de Timóteo |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214757/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/1/historia-de-timoteo |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> and hosted [[Tropeiro|''tropeiros'']] seeking rest.<ref name="PrefeituraFabri_PrimeirosHab."/> The name "Timóteo" is attributed to a ''tropeiro'' who settled in the area and opened a store that became a key reference point,<ref name="Diário_Nome"/> also known as the "Paragem do Ribeirão do Timóteo."<ref name="IBGE_Histórico">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/timoteo.pdf |title=Timóteo - Histórico |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2007 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731192749/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/timoteo.pdf |archive-date=July 31, 2013}}</ref> Another account suggests that a pioneer honored his European nephew, Timóteo.<ref name="Diário_Nome">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=46069 |title=História revelada |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=April 17, 2010 |access-date=July 16, 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120729221145/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=46069 |archive-date=July 29, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

By around 1840, Alegre had solidified as a village and was annexed to the <u>''[[Freguesia]]''</u> of Santana do Alfié. Over the next few decades, Manoel Archanjo de Andrade (around 1855), Antônio Pacífico Fraga (1877), and the brothers João and Manoel Lino de Sá from [[Joanésia]] (who acquired land from José Luiz de Paula, heir of Francisco de Paula e Silva, in 1885) purchased land. The holdings of the deceased Chico Santa Maria were divided among his children and grandchildren in 1890.<ref name="Câmara_História"/> In 1895, Manoel Berto de Lima founded the Our Lady of the Rosary Congado Group. In 1907, Francisco Malachias donated land dedicated to [[Saint Sebastian]] at the site of the current 29 de Abril Square in Centro-Sul, used to build a small wooden church, leading the settlement to be known as São Sebastião do Alegre.<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

===Urban formation===
[[File:Fachada da Casa da Memória, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|The house on Mateus Araújo Street, also known as the House of Memory, was built in 1922 in the Centro-Sul area.]]
The construction of the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]] (EFVM) in eastern Minas Gerais spurred population growth near the railway during the 1910s and 1920s. In 1921, pharmacist Raimundo Alves de Carvalho settled in the current Horto neighborhood in Ipatinga to support the construction of the railway.<ref name="AmoIpat_RaimundoA">{{cite web |url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=547 |title=Personagens do Vale do Aço - Raimundo Alves de Carvalho |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |date=October 7, 2014 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141118223604/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=547 |archive-date=November 18, 2014}}</ref> Raimundo acquired land in São Sebastião do Alegre, donating a plot for the construction of the village’s first school, the São Sebastião do Alegre Mixed Rural School, in 1922,<ref name="AmoIpat_RaimundoA"/> the same year the first post office was established. In 1925, the Timotinho Municipal Mixed School was inaugurated in the Ana Moura neighborhood, also on land donated by the pharmacist.<ref name="Câmara_História"/> Both schools were later incorporated into the Dona Angelina Alves School (now Angelina Alves de Carvalho Municipal School), named after Raimundo’s mother.<ref name="AmoIpat_RaimundoA"/> Meanwhile, the village was incorporated into the district of São José do Grama (now Jaguaraçu), created on September 7, 1923, then part of [[São Domingos do Prata]]. In 1928, the pharmacist established the Dona Angelina Farm near the village, along the [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]].<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

In the early 1930s, an office of the Belgo-Mineira Steel Company was established in [[Coronel Fabriciano]] to centralize timber extraction and charcoal production in the Rio Doce region to fuel its furnaces in [[João Monlevade]].<ref name="IBGE_HistóriaFabri">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/coronelfabriciano.pdf |title=Coronel Fabriciano - Histórico |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2007 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141025204356/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/coronelfabriciano.pdf |archive-date=October 25, 2014}}</ref> This led to large-scale land negotiations for timber extraction, resulting in extensive deforestation. Concerned about preserving the native Atlantic Forest, Bishop Helvécio Gomes de Oliveira spearheaded the creation of the [[Rio Doce State Park]] in 1944, the first conservation unit in Minas Gerais.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=38751&t=perd-completa-69-anos-de-preservacao |title=Perd completa 69 anos de preservação |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=July 13, 2013 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214827/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=38751&t=perd-completa-69-anos-de-preservacao |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> By state decree No. 148 of December 17, 1938, the former São Sebastião do Alegre village ceased to belong to Jaguaraçu and was elevated to district status, annexed to [[Antônio Dias]] under the name "Timóteo."<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> Its establishment occurred on January 1, 1940, with Joaquim Ferreira de Souza as the first [[justice of the peace]].<ref name="Acesita_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.acesita.org/historia.html |title=Acesita: Capital do Inox |publisher=Acesita.org |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140313033024/http://www.acesita.org/historia.html |archive-date=March 13, 2014 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Sede da antiga Fazenda Dona Angelina, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|Former Dona Angelina Farm headquarters, used as a commercial point in Centro-Norte, photographed in 2019 (demolished in 2022).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://plox.com.br/noticia/12/07/2022/ao-vivo-plox-fala-direto-do-antigo-hotel-e-restaurante-acesita-em-timoteo |title=Plox fala direto do antigo Hotel e Restaurante Acesita, em Timóteo |publisher=Plox |date=July 12, 2022 |access-date=April 4, 2023 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230404173931/https://plox.com.br/noticia/12/07/2022/ao-vivo-plox-fala-direto-do-antigo-hotel-e-restaurante-acesita-em-timoteo |archive-date=April 4, 2023}}</ref>]]

The area benefited from Vitória-Minas Railway stops at the Baratinha (opened June 9, 1924), Ana Matos (August 26, 1925), and Sá Carvalho (August 26, 1925) stations.<ref name="EFVM1960">{{cite web|url=http://vfco.brazilia.jor.br/estacoes-ferroviarias/1960-sudeste-EFVM/Estrada-de-Ferro-Vitoria-Minas.shtml |title=G1 - Guia Geral das Estradas de Ferro - 1960 |publisher=Centro-Oeste |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140519151431/http://vfco.brazilia.jor.br/estacoes-ferroviarias/1960-sudeste-EFVM/Estrada-de-Ferro-Vitoria-Minas.shtml |archive-date=May 19, 2014}}</ref><ref name="Estações_EFVM">{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/efvm.htm |title=EFVM - Estrada de Ferro Vitória a Minas |publisher=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141007090659/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/efvm.htm |archive-date=October 7, 2014}}</ref> The availability of the railway, watercourses, and timber for extraction were decisive factors in studies by Itabira Iron, led by [[Percival Farquhar]] and entrepreneurs Amyntas Jacques de Moraes and Athos de Lemos Rache, which identified Timóteo as the optimal location for their steel complex, leading to the founding of [[Aperam South America|Acesita]] (Aços Especiais Itabira) on October 31, 1944. A loan from the [[Banco do Brasil]] secured the project’s consolidation.<ref name="Acesita_Acesita"/> On June 3, 1947, the Acesita Station was inaugurated and served by Vitória-Minas Railway stops.<ref name="EFVM1960"/>

Originally, [[Itabira]] was designated to host the complex, but its rugged terrain made it unfeasible. Iron ore extracted in Itabira could be transported to Timóteo via the railway, which also facilitated connections to port complexes on the [[Espírito Santo|Espírito Santo coast]].<ref name="UCAM_25">{{Harvtxt|ref=UCAM|Sampaio|2008|p=25}}</ref> The chosen site for the factory was the Dona Angelina Farm, purchased from Raimundo Alves de Carvalho.<ref name="Acesita_Acesita">{{cite web |url=http://www.acesita.org/acesita.html |title=Um pouco de História |publisher=Acesita.org |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140312213209/http://www.acesita.org/acesita.html |archive-date=March 12, 2014 |url-status=dead}}</ref> A planned industrial village for 2,000 inhabitants was also envisioned, while the village had only 200 residents.<ref name="Diário_Identidades">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=19063&t=definicao-de-identidades |title=Definição de identidades |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=October 3, 2007 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214840/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=19063&t=definicao-de-identidades |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> The urban project was designed by engineer Romeu Duffles Teixeira in 1946, though not fully implemented as planned.<ref name="ANPUR_2">{{Harvtxt|ref=ANPUR|Quecini|2009|p=2}}</ref> On December 27, 1948, the Timóteo district was incorporated into the newly emancipated municipality of Coronel Fabriciano, driven by the momentum from Acesita’s arrival.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/>

===Economic expansion and emancipation===
[[File:Primeira locomotiva da Acesita, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|First locomotive used by Acesita (now [[Aperam South America]]), displayed at the Aperam Acesita Foundation.]]
Acesita’s establishment attracted workers and employees from across Brazil and immigrants, solidifying the urbanization of both Timóteo and neighboring Coronel Fabriciano. In the following years, the company built several residential complexes for its employees, giving rise to neighborhoods such as Quitandinha (the oldest), Bromélias, Funcionários, Vila dos Técnicos, and Olaria.<ref name="Acesita_História"/> In 1949, Acesita produced its first [[pig iron]] run. Its presence catalyzed the industrial development of the Vale do Aço, reinforced by the establishment of [[Usiminas]] in the Fabriciano district of [[Ipatinga]] in the late 1950s.<ref name="UCAM_21e22">{{Harvtxt|ref=UCAM|Sampaio|2008|pp=21–22}}</ref>

By 1950, with a population of 11,813 people,<ref name="UCAM_29">{{Harvtxt|ref=UCAM|Sampaio|2008|p=29}}</ref> Timóteo gained access to treated water, and in 1952, the Acesita Hospital was established, later renamed Vital Brazil Hospital and Maternity<ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="HMBV_História"/> and then Hospital and Maternity of Timóteo.<ref name="Plox_HMT"/> By 1958, the company had built 2,734 residences,<ref name="PDDI1_93a100"/> but in 1964, it began charging workers for housing.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> Neighborhoods reflected employee hierarchies; for instance, the Funcionários neighborhood was reserved for doctors, engineers, and managers, located closer to the plant with relatively higher-quality housing.<ref name="Simpósio_10">{{Harvtxt|ref=Simpósio|Furtado, E.S. de Oliveira e S.S. de Oliveira|2005|p=10}}</ref> Beyond schools, the company built sports courts and clubs, hosting tournaments, dances, and theater performances.<ref name="Acesita_História"/>
[[File:Vista da usina da Aperam South America em Timóteo MG a partir de Coronel Fabriciano.JPG|thumb|left|The Aperam South America plant as seen from [[Coronel Fabriciano]].]]

The movement for Timóteo’s political autonomy began in 1947 but initially failed. It was revived in 1954 and later approved by the Coronel Fabriciano City Council and enacted by the [[Legislative Assembly of Minas Gerais]] (ALMG) through state law No. 2,764 of December 30, 1962, alongside Ipatinga’s emancipation.<ref name="Câmara_História"/><ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="G1_53anos"/> However, Governor [[Magalhães Pinto|José de Magalhães Pinto]] vetoed the emancipation of both districts under pressure from an alliance with Fabriciano’s then-mayor Cyro Cotta Poggiali. The Fabriciano municipal government was unwilling to forgo the revenue generated by Acesita and Usiminas,<ref name="UCAM_43e44">{{Harvtxt|ref=UCAM|Sampaio|2008|pp=43–44}}</ref><ref name="AmoIpat_Jogada">{{cite web|url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=56 |title=Emancipação dos municípios contou com uma jogada política |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303173819/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=56 |archive-date=March 3, 2016}}</ref> and the governor aimed to maintain political, administrative, economic, and financial unity in the steel hub.<ref name="UCAM_43e44"/><ref>{{Cite journal |author=Câmara Municipal de Ipatinga |year=1990 |title=Ipatinga: Um Retrato Sócio Cultural |journal=Câmara Municipal de Ipatinga |volume=1 |pages=8–9}}</ref>

A rift between the Fabriciano mayor and Magalhães Pinto prompted the governor to reconsider the veto,<ref name="AmoIpat_Jogada"/> enabling a new commission to secure Timóteo’s emancipation approval by the state’s Interior Secretariat on April 28, 1964. This process also saw the emancipation of Ipatinga, [[João Monlevade]], and [[Bela Vista de Minas]].<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoÚltimo">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=332 |title=A emancipação político-administrativa de Ipatinga - Último capítulo |publisher=Em Amo Ipatinga |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193240/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=332 |archive-date=June 15, 2016}}</ref> The independence of the districts was announced on a stage in [[Centro (Coronel Fabriciano)|Fabriciano’s Centro]] around noon on the same day, formalized in the Official Gazette the following day, April 29.<ref name="G1_53anos">{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/g1-vales-faz-reportagem-especial-em-comemoracao-aos-53-anos-de-ipatinga-e-timoteo.ghtml |title=G1 Vales faz reportagem especial em comemoração aos 53 anos de Ipatinga e Timóteo |publisher=G1 |author=InterTV dos Vales |date=April 28, 2017 |access-date=May 8, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170508154740/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/g1-vales-faz-reportagem-especial-em-comemoracao-aos-53-anos-de-ipatinga-e-timoteo.ghtml |archive-date=May 8, 2017}}</ref> Virico Fonseca was appointed [[intendant]],<ref name="Câmara_História"/> but José Antônio de Araújo was the first elected mayor, taking office on December 3, 1965.<ref name="AmoIpat_Acesita">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=316 |title=Cia. Aços Especiais Itabira-ACESITA |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |access-date=October 17, 2016 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20161014151437/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=316 |archive-date=October 14, 2016}}</ref>

===Urban development===
[[File:Trecho da Avenida Belo Horizonte no distrito Cachoeira do Vale, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|Belo Horizonte Avenue in the Cachoeira do Vale district, a former section of the [[BR-381]] highway crossing the city.]]
Acesita’s responsibilities for public services were transferred to the municipal government in 1969. Until then, the urban core around the company consisted almost exclusively of its workers, as the infrastructure was initially built solely for them and their families. This situation divided the urban area into two clusters: one comprising company-built neighborhoods around Centro-Norte, still called "Acesita," and the other from the original São Sebastião do Alegre settlements in Centro-Sul, referred to as "Timóteo."<ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14">{{Harvtxt|ref=ANPUR|Quecini|2009|pp=6–9; 14}}</ref> As Centro-Sul received minimal investment from Acesita, it experienced relatively higher rates of poverty and deprivation.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> An attempt to integrate the two cores was the construction of the Primavera neighborhood in the 1970s through a partnership between the municipal government and the state, on land donated by Acesita.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/>

Alongside the development of the original workers’ village, significant growth occurred in peripheral areas between the 1960s and 1970s, driven by non-industrial populations attracted by local progress,<ref name="PDDI1_93a100">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=93–100}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_80">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=80}}</ref> leading to relatively recent occupations in neighborhoods such as Ana Moura, Alvorada, Limoeiro, and Macuco, as well as the Cachoeira do Vale district along the [[BR-381]] highway,<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> established on May 13, 1976.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> This district, located in a lowland along the Piracicaba River, was severely impacted by flooding during the [[1979 Brazil floods|1979 floods]].<ref name="Diário_Nívelpreocupa">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=39979&t=nivel-do-rio-piracicaba-preocupa |title=Nível do Rio Piracicaba preocupa |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=December 24, 2013 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214902/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=39979&t=nivel-do-rio-piracicaba-preocupa |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> That year marked the municipal administration’s first concerns about flood impacts,<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> though the district continued to face recurrent flooding due to its location in the river’s floodplain.<ref name="PDDI2_442e447a450">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=442; 447–450}}</ref>

Between 1979 and 1981, a plebiscite proposed renaming the municipality to "Acesita," which garnered 12,861 votes in favor and 4,908 votes against but was rejected by the [[Legislative Assembly of Minas Gerais]] (ALMG).<ref name="JVA_Acesita">{{cite web|url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=27858 |title=Mudança em siderúrgica reacende discussão entre Acesita e Timóteo |publisher=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=October 1, 2007 |access-date=July 15, 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231154655/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=27858 |archive-date=December 31, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> However, the city’s population and economic growth stagnated in the 1980s due to a nationwide crisis. Of 55 companies planning to establish operations in Timóteo, only seven secured funding.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> Acesita’s accumulated debts led to its privatization,<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> finalized on October 23, 1992. Subsequent cost-cutting measures, including staff and position reductions and workforce modernization, were implemented, which led to layoffs that strained the local economy's ability to absorb displaced workers, though dismissals were carried out gradually until 1994.<ref name="FGV">{{cite web|url=http://bibliotecadigital.fgv.br/dspace/bitstream/handle/10438/8146/000077063.pdf?sequence=2 |title=Uma análise do processo de privatização brasileiro, com ênfase no setor siderúrgico |pages=17–23 |author=Ana Cristina Gazolla Braga |date=March 8, 1996 |publisher=Fundação Getúlio Vargas |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170110090014/http://bibliotecadigital.fgv.br/dspace/bitstream/handle/10438/8146/000077063.pdf?sequence=2 |archive-date=January 10, 2017}}</ref>

In the early 1990s, the creation of the first Municipal Master Plan to regulate urban planning and environmental issues, combined with Acesita’s privatization, reduced the public administration’s dependence on the company.<ref name="ANPUR_10a11">{{Harvtxt|ref=ANPUR|Quecini|2009|pp=10–11}}</ref> Nonetheless, Acesita continued investing in arts and cultural facilities, notably through the Acesita Foundation (now Aperam Acesita Foundation), established in 1994.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Camarotti |first1=Ilka |last2=Spink |first2=Peter |year=2002 |title=Redução da pobreza e dinâmicas locais |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=f-U3IFOBnkUC&pg=PP1 |edition=2 |location=Rio de Janeiro-RJ |publisher=FGV |page=20 |chapter=Capítulo 1 - Fundação Acesita |isbn=85-225-0354-0 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170109214911/https://books.google.com.br/books?id=f-U3IFOBnkUC&lpg=PP1&dq=isbn%3A8522503540&hl=pt-BR&pg=PP1#v=onepage&q&f=false |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> It also retained ownership of vast urban and rural lands in the municipality.<ref name="ANPUR_10a11"/>

In the 2000s, Timóteo faced a political crisis marked by successive mayoral changes, starting with the impeachment of Geraldo Nascimento de Oliveira ([[Workers' Party (Brazil)|PT]]) and his deputy on September 20, 2006. Leonardo Rodrigues Lelé da Cunha ([[Brazilian Democratic Movement|PMDB]]), the runner-up in the 2004 election, was appointed in his place. Geraldo Nascimento returned via an injunction two months later but was impeached again in October 2007. Due to legal disputes, Leonardo could not take office, and his deputy Geraldo Hilário Torres ([[Brazilian Social Democracy Party|PSDB]]) was sworn in.<ref name="TRE_Cassação">{{cite 2016}}</ref> Geraldo Nascimento secured another injunction in December 2007,<ref name="Conjur_Cassação">{{cite web|url=http://www.conjur.com.br/2008-fev-16/prefeito_cassado_timoteo_mg_permanece_cargo upheld by the [[Superior Electoral Court|TSE]] in July 2008, when Geraldo Hilário Torres reassumed office.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/Noticias/Politica/0,,MUL633910-5601,00-APOS+CASSACAO+VICE+DO+COLOCADO+ASSUME+PREFEITURA+DE+TIMOTEO.html |title=Após cassação, vice do 2º colocado assume Prefeitura de web|url=http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/politica/2010/09/09/interna_politica,179003/tre-mg-confirma-cassacao-do-prefeito-de-timoteo.shtml |title=TRE-MG confirma cassação do prefeito de Timóteo |publisher=Portal Uai |author=Luisa Brasil |date=September 9, 2010 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161005193527/http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/politica/2010/09/09/interna_politica,179003/tre-mg-confirma-cassacao-do-prefeito-de-timoteo.shtml |archive-date=October 5, 2016}}</ref>

In 2007, the [[ArcelorMittal]] group acquired Acesita and renamed it ArcelorMittal Timóteo.<ref name="JVA_Acesita"/> In January 2011, it joined the [[Aperam]] group, becoming [[Aperam South America]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=88876 |title=ArcelorMittal Inox Brasil passa a integrar Aperam |publisher=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=January 26, 2011 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120905200012/http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=88876 |archive-date=September 5, 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Despite these changes, many residents still refer to the plant and the Centro-Norte area as "Acesita" due to its historical influence. This often confuses outsiders, who may think Timóteo and "Acesita" are separate cities, as the municipal administrative center is in Centro-Sul. The former company name persists in street names and commercial establishments.<ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="JVA_Acesita"/>

==Geography==
The municipality’s area is {{convert|144.381|km2|sqmi}}, representing 0.0247% of [[Minas Gerais]], 0.0157% of the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]], and 0.0017% of Brazil’s total territory.<ref name="CNM_Dados">{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100131808 |title=Dados gerais Timóteo - MG |publisher=Confederação Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |access-date=March 30, 2010}}</ref> Of this, {{convert|18.13|km2|sqmi}} are urban.<ref name="IBGE_Timóteo"/> According to the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] effective since 2017, established by the IBGE,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Divisão Regional do Brasil |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=September 5, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170905163107/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=September 5, 2017}}</ref> Timóteo belongs to the Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions of Ipatinga.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Base de dados por municípios das Regiões Geográficas Imediatas e Intermediárias do Brasil |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=February 10, 2018}}</ref> Previously, under the [[Microregions of Brazil|microregion]] and [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregion]] divisions, it was part of the Ipatinga microregion within the Vale do Rio Doce mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web|url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=Divisão Territorial Brasileira 2016 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=September 5, 2017}}</ref>

===Topography and hydrography===
[[File:Rio Piracicaba entre Coronel Fabriciano e Timóteo MG com a Serra do Timóteo ao fundo.JPG|thumb|right|260px|The [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]] between [[Coronel Fabriciano]] and Timóteo, seen from the "old bridge." In the background to the right is the Serra do Timóteo.]]
Timóteo is located in the Vale do Rio Doce interplateau depression, where the terrain is the result of the [[fluvial erosion]] of [[Precambrian]] [[Granite gneiss|granite-gneiss rocks]].<ref name="CBH-Doce_Caracterização">{{cite web|url=http://www.riodoce.cbh.gov.br/bacia_caracterizacao.asp |title=Caracterização da Bacia Hidrográfica |publisher=Comitê da Bacia Hidrográfica do Rio Doce |access-date=July 25, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141006105252/http://www.riodoce.cbh.gov.br/bacia_caracterizacao.asp |archive-date=6 October 2014}}</ref> The geological complex includes [[gneiss]]ic, [[Igneous rock|magmatic]], and [[Metamorphic rock|metamorphic]] rocks, such as [[Biotite|biotite-gneiss]], [[Granite|granitic]] rocks, and [[Granite gneiss|granite-gneiss]].<ref name="PDDI1_63a65">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=63–65}}</ref> The topography is diverse, with 50% of Timóteo's area comprising undulating hills, 30% being mountainous, and the final 20% being level.<ref name="cidadesnet.com">{{cite web|url=http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/timoteo.htm |title=Timóteo - MG |publisher=Cidades.Net |access-date=July 13, 2010 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130425120515/http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/timoteo.htm |archive-date=April 25, 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The highest elevations are found in the west, reaching 864 meters at Ana Moura Peak. The lowest altitudes are found along riverbanks, with the lowest recorded being 225 meters at the [[River mouth|mouth]] of the Piracicaba River in the Doce River.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/><ref name="PDDI1_111a114">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=111–114}}</ref> The municipal center is at 350 meters.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> The Piracicaba River’s location at the city’s northern boundary and the flat terrain nearby facilitated the establishment of the Vitória-Minas Railway and Acesita, and later the urban expansion of Timóteo, which was forced toward higher elevations. Consequently, significant occupation, especially by lower-income groups, has occurred in steeply sloped areas.<ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/> The [[Rio Doce State Park]] region, in the east and south, features alternating hills and fluvial plains.<ref name="DescubraMinas_PERD">{{cite web|url=http://www.descubraminas.com/Turismo/ParqueApresentacao.aspx?cod_destino=796 |title=Parque Estadual do Rio Doce - Apresentação |publisher=Descubra Minas |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214545/http://www.descubraminas.com/Turismo/ParqueApresentacao.aspx?cod_destino=796 |archive-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_63a65"/><ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/>

The [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]] flows through the city, forming the boundary with Coronel Fabriciano, within its sub-basin, part of the [[Doce River Basin]].<ref name="PDDI2_457e458"/> The mouth of the Piracicaba River in the [[Doce River]] lies at the Rio Doce State Park’s boundary with Ipatinga.<ref>{{Cite journal |author=Consórcio Ecoplan-Lume |year=2010 |title=Plano de Ação de Recursos Hídricos da Unidade de Planejamento e Gestão dos Recursos Hídricos Piracicaba PARH Piracicaba |page=10 |journal=Plano Integrado de Recursos Hídricos da Bacia do Rio Doce e dos Planos de Ações de Recursos Hídricos para as Unidades de Planejamento e Gestão de Recursos Hídricos no Âmbito da Bacia do Rio Doce |publisher=Comitê da Bacia Hidrográfica do Rio Doce |url=http://www.cbhdoce.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/PARH_Piracicaba.pdf |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190602153209/http://www.cbhdoce.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/PARH_Piracicaba.pdf |archive-date=June 2, 2019}}</ref> An alluvial aquifer beneath the Piracicaba’s mouth supplies most of the Vale do Aço’s water.<ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/><ref name="JVA_Seca">{{cite web |url=http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=81202 |title=Falta de chuvas reduziu reservas |publisher=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=February 27, 2010 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120118151458/http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=81202 |archive-date=18 January 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The municipal territory includes five smaller sub-basins: the Atalho, Celeste, and Limoeiro streams, and the Belém ({{convert|190|km2|sqmi}}) and Timotinho ({{convert|39|km2|sqmi}}) rivers.<ref name="PDDI2_457e458">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=457–458}}</ref> The Timotinho River, the main watercourse crossing the city, suffers from severe pollution and unregulated occupation of its banks, contributing to flooding during the rainy season.<ref name="PDDI2_63e112a113">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=63; 112–113}}</ref> Rio Doce State Park, which is crossed by the Doce River, has hundreds of small [[Spring (hydrology)|springs]],<ref name="IBGE_MapaI">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/SE-23-Z-D-V.jpg |title=Folha SE-23-Z-D-V world’s largest [[Lake|lacustrine]] systems.<ref name="EM_Rejeitos">{{cite |archive-date=August 13, 2016}}</ref>

===Neighboring municipalities and metropolitan region===
{{Main|Vale do Aço metropolitan area}}

Timóteo borders [[Coronel Fabriciano]] and [[Ipatinga]] to the north, [[Caratinga]] and [[Bom Jesus do Galho]] to the east, [[Marliéria]] to the south, [[Antônio Dias]] to the west, and [[Jaguaraçu]] to the southwest.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /> Rapid regional growth has blurred political boundaries, forming the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area|Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]] (RMVA), encompassing Timóteo, Coronel Fabriciano, Ipatinga, and [[Santana do Paraíso]], as well as 24 other municipalities in the metropolitan area. As the seat of [[Aperam South America]] and other metal-mechanical and raw material supplier companies, Timóteo plays a key role as an employer for surrounding cities.<ref name="ABEP_15">{{Harvtxt|ref=Dinâmica|ABEP|2012|p=15}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_60a61">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=60–61}}</ref> The region gained international recognition due to major companies such as Aperam South America (Timóteo), Cenibra ([[Belo Oriente]]), and [[Usiminas]] (Ipatinga), all of which export large volumes of products. Despite its recent settlement, the region is one of the state’s main urban hubs.<ref name="ABEP_15" />

===Climate===
[[File:Pico do Ana Moura visto de Coronel Fabriciano ao entardecer.JPG|thumb|left|A view of the Ana Moura Peak in the distance at sunset on a spring afternoon.]]
Timóteo’s [[climate]] is classified as [[tropical wet and dry climate]]<ref name="IBGE_Clima">{{cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/BrasilClimas.jpg |title=Brasil - Climas |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=November 14, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012015042/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/BrasilClimas.jpg |archive-date=October 12, 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref> (type ''Aw'', according to the [[Köppen climate classification|Köppen system]]),<ref name="Climatologia-Timóteo-MG">{{cite web|url=http://pt.climate-data.org/location/24944/ |title=Clima: Timóteo |publisher=Climate Data |access-date=June 26, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150623011027/http://pt.climate-data.org/location/24944/ |archive-date=June 23, 2015}}</ref> with an average annual [[temperature]] of {{convert|23|°C|°F}} and average precipitation of {{convert|1430|mm|in}}/year, concentrated between October and April.<ref name="Tempo-Agora-Timóteo-MG"/> The [[Wet season|rainy season]] includes the warmer months, while the [[dry season]] covers milder months. [[Autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] are transitional seasons.<ref name="Climatologia-Timóteo-MG"/><ref name="Weatherspark">{{cite web|url=https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30688/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Tim%C3%B3teo-Brasil-durante-o-ano |title=Condições meteorológicas médias de Timóteo |publisher=Weather Spark |access-date=August 30, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180830215644/https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30688/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Tim%C3%B3teo-Brasil-durante-o-ano |archive-date=August 30, 2018}}</ref> The transition between dry and wet seasons features [[thunderstorm]]s and high [[thermal amplitude]], especially from late [[winter]] to spring.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0029508-primavera-pode-trazer-temporais |title=Primavera pode trazer temporais |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=September 13, 2010 |access-date=September 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200907122213/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0029508-primavera-pode-trazer-temporais |archive-date=September 7, 2020}}</ref>

===Ecology and environment===
[[File:Arborização no interior do Oikós, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|Tree cover within Oikós, a preservation area integrated with the [[Rio Doce State Park]] maintained by Aperam South America.]]
The native vegetation belongs to the [[Atlantic Forest]] domain, with the original biome covering {{convert|116.17|km2|sqmi}}, equivalent to 80.46% of the municipality's area.<ref name="PDDI1_63a65"/><ref name="PDDI1_118a120">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=118–120}}</ref> Notably, {{convert|53.2|km2|sqmi}} of the municipal area is protected by the [[Rio Doce State Park]] (PERD),<ref name="PDDI1_49">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=49}}</ref> established in 1944 to safeguard the remaining native forest against deforestation driven by local industrial complexes. In addition to encompassing one of the world's largest lake systems, this conservation unit represents the largest Atlantic Forest reserve in Minas Gerais, extending into the municipalities of [[Dionísio, Minas Gerais|Dionísio]] and [[Marliéria]].<ref name="DescubraMinas_PERD"/> The [[biodiversity]] is remarkable, providing a habitat for 325 bird species and 77 mammal species, including endangered species such as the [[jaguar]], [[solitary tinamou]], and [[southern muriqui]].<ref name="EM_Rejeitos"/>

Furthermore, Timóteo has an [[Environmental protection area (Brazil)|environmental protection area]] (APA), which, together with preservation areas in neighboring municipalities, forms an [[ecological corridor]] connecting other regions of the Steel Valley to the PERD.<ref name="PDDI1_120a124">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=120–124}}</ref> The APA Serra do Timóteo serves as a buffer zone for the PERD and was recognized in 2003.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.timoteo.cam.mg.gov.br/noticias/camara-realiza-tres-encontros-com-especialistas-sobre-a-apa-serra-de-timoteo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160112232033/http://www.timoteo.cam.mg.gov.br/noticias/camara-realiza-tres-encontros-com-especialistas-sobre-a-apa-serra-de-timoteo |archive-date=January 12, 2016 |title=Câmara realiza três encontros com especialistas sobre a APA Serra de Timóteo |author=Câmara Municipal |date=February 25, 2015 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://ong.prosperustec.com.br/mprd/wp-content/uploads/2008/10/plano-de-manejo_enc4.pdf |title=Unidade de conservação e zona de amortecimento |author=Instituto Estadual de Florestas de Minas Gerais (IEF-MG) |page=82 |date=October 20, 2008 |publisher=Prosperus |access-date=January 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170930083945/http://ong.prosperustec.com.br/mprd/wp-content/uploads/2008/10/plano-de-manejo_enc4.pdf |archive-date=September 30, 2017}}</ref> However, it has faced irregular and invasive occupations due to urban expansion in the Alphaville, Macuco, and Recanto Verde neighborhoods. The Oikós Project, located within the urban area, is a revegetation initiative by [[Aperam South America]] integrated with the Serra do Timóteo and the Rio Doce State Park.<ref name="PDDI1_120a124"/> It serves local schools and the community through visits, lectures, ecological activities, and programs organized by the Aperam Acesita Foundation, featuring trails and recreational areas dedicated to children.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=1998 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214654/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=1998 |archive-date=January 9, 2017 |title=Projeto do Oikós convida a descobrir encantos da floresta |author=Jornal Diário Popular |date=July 12, 2012 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=1757 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214738/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=1757 |archive-date=January 9, 2017 |title=Interdisciplinaridade nas trilhas do Oikós |author=Prefeitura |date=April 28, 2014 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The presence of the Rio Doce State Park contributes to Timóteo having approximately 1,000 square meters of green space per inhabitant, one of the highest rates in Brazil.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=24790&t=mil-metros-de-area-verde-por-habitante |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109214959/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=24790&t=mil-metros-de-area-verde-por-habitante |archive-date=January 9, 2017 |title=Mil metros de área verde por habitante |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=June 2, 2009 |access-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> Focused on environmental conservation, the city also promotes ecological awareness campaigns involving the population and urban [[tree planting]] programs. The municipality has an Integrated Urban Tree Planting Plan, aimed at planning and managing tree species to ensure proper interaction with the population and the environment.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=12166 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109215015/http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=12166 |archive-date=January 9, 2017 |title='Árvore certa, no lugar certo' realiza plantio de mudas |author=Jornal Diário Popular |date=September 30, 2015 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

[[File:Incêndio na Serra do Timóteo em Timóteo MG visto de Coronel Fabriciano.JPG|thumb|left|Fire affecting the Serra do Timóteo in September 2024]]
Some of the main environmental challenges in the city include [[flood]]s, which during the rainy season cause significant damage in the lower and more populated areas along the Piracicaba River, particularly in the Cachoeira do Vale district and neighborhoods along smaller waterways, as well as landslides on hills and slopes. These issues are often caused by residential construction in irregular zones,<ref name="PDDI2_442e447a450"/><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=1415 |author=Prefeitura |date=January 8, 2014 |access-date=January 9, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> in addition to waste and sewage discharged into streams and rivers.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=46310&t=plano-de-saneamento-passa--por-analise-da-procuradoria- |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170109215106/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=46310&t=plano-de-saneamento-passa--por-analise-da-procuradoria- |archive-date=January 9, 2017 |title=Plano de Saneamento passa por análise da Procuradoria |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=June 9, 2016 |access-date=January 9, 2017}}</ref> The city hall and agencies such as the Brazilian Geological Service (CPRM) maintain rainfall stations and monitoring points for the Piracicaba River's water levels in Timóteo and other municipalities in the river basin, and in case of alerts, residents of riverside areas are evacuated.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/rehi/riodoce/rel_riodoce2004.pdf |title=Relatório Técnico do Período de Operação de dezembro de 2003 a março de serves as a basis for other preventive measures.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=October 31, 2014 |title=Empresa realiza mapeamento de áreas de risco em Timóteo, em MG ===Indicators and inequality===
[[File:Vista parcial do B. Ana Rita com o Centro-Sul e Bela Vista ao fundo, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|Ana Rita neighborhood (foreground) with Centro-Sul in the background on the left and a slum in Bela Vista on the right]]
The Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Timóteo is considered high by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP), with a value of 0.771 (the 227th highest in Brazil and the 17th highest in Minas Gerais). The city has most indicators close to the national average according to the UNDP. Specifically, the education index is 0.742, the longevity index is 0.836, and the income index is 0.737.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

From 2000 to 2010, the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to half a minimum wage decreased by 64.6%, and in 2010, 94.3% of the population lived above the [[poverty line]], 4.5% were at the poverty line, and 1.2% were below it.<ref name="ODM_1">{{cite web |url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031807/timoteo---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170111185923/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031807/timoteo---mg |archive-date=January 11, 2017 |title=1 - acabar com a fome e a miséria |author=Portal ODM |access-date=January 11, 2017}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.489, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web |url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031807/timoteo---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170111185942/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031807/timoteo---mg |archive-date=January 11, 2017 |title=Perfil municipal |author=Portal ODM |access-date=January 11, 2017}}</ref> The share of the city's total income held by the richest 20% of the population was 53.5%, or 12 times higher than that of the poorest 20%, which was 4.5%.<ref name="ODM_1"/> In 2013, according to the city hall, there was a housing deficit of approximately {{formatnum:4500}} units.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=4107 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170110191310/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=4107 |archive-date=January 10, 2017 |title=Prefeito de Timóteo apresenta secretários |author=Jornal Diário Popular |date=January 2, 2013 |access-date=January 10, 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 2022, according to the IBGE census, 11.83% of the population lived in [[slum|slums and urban communities]],<ref name="Diário_Favelas"/> representing the ninth-largest proportion of residents in such conditions among Minas Gerais municipalities.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=November 8, 2024 |title=Infógrafo: Veja quantas pessoas moram em favelas na sua cidade name="Diário_Favelas">{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0119931-coronel-fabriciano-e-ipatinga-estao-entre-as-10-cidades-mineiras-com-mais-favelas-revela-censo |title=Coronel According to the 2022 IBGE census, the municipal population aged 10 and over comprised {{formatnum:30896}} [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (42.95%), {{formatnum:30791}} [[Catholic Church|Catholics]] (42.8%), {{formatnum:5501}} [[Irreligion|people with no religion]] (7.65%), 540 [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (0.75%), with the remaining 5.85% distributed among other religions.<ref name="IBGE_Religião2022">{{cite web |author= |title=Grandes grupos de religião |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/indicadores.html?localidade=3168705&tema=10 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250607115337/https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/indicadores.html?localidade=3168705&tema=10 |archive-date=June 7, 2025 |access-date=June 7, 2025 |website=Panorama do Censo 2022}}</ref> The municipality is part of Pastoral Region III of the [[Diocese of Itabira-Fabriciano]] and is divided into four [[parish]]es: São Francisco de Assis, São João Batista, São José, and São Sebastião.<ref name="Diocese_Paróquias">{{cite web |url=https://dioceseitabira.org.br/paroquias/ |title=Paróquias |author=Diocese de Itabira-Fabriciano |access-date=October 22, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20251022184138/https://dioceseitabira.org.br/paroquias/ |archive-date=October 22, 2025}}</ref> Their origins trace back to the Catholic faith inherited from the first settlers, maintained by the workers who came to build Acesita.<ref name="Acesita_História"/>

The São José Parish, located near the former workers' village and established in the 1950s, offered literacy, sewing, and painting courses to early residents. The São Sebastião Parish, corresponding to the oldest urban core, was established in the 1960s.<ref name="Acesita_História"/> Meanwhile, the São João Batista Parish, based in the Cachoeira do Vale district, was created in 2017.<ref name="Diocese_Paróquias"/> The growth of Protestant religions has been significant since the 1990s, making Timóteo one of the largest evangelical communities in the state,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=35554&t=numero-de-evangelicos-cresce |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170110191334/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=35554&t=numero-de-evangelicos-cresce |archive-date=January 10, 2017 |title=Número de evangélicos cresce |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=July 1, 2012 |access-date=January 10, 2017}}</ref> surpassing the number of Catholics in the 2022 census.<ref name="IBGE_Religião2022"/>

==Politics and subdivisions==
[[File:Paço Municipal de Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|left|Municipal Palace in the São José neighborhood, housing the City Hall and City Council.]]

Municipal administration is carried out by the [[Executive (government)|Executive]] and [[Legislature|Legislative]] branches. ==Economy==
[[File:Vista do Centro de Economia Informal Raimundo de Souza Neto, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|Raimundo de Souza Neto Informal Economy Center, a street market in Centro-Norte.]]
[[File:Interior da Feira do Timirim, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|Interior of the Timirim Fair, held in the João XXIII neighborhood.]]
In Timóteo's [[Gross Domestic Product]] (GDP), the industry and [[service sector]] stand out. According to 2021 IBGE data, the municipality's GDP at current prices was R${{formatnum:5611338.76}} thousand.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> {{formatnum:728712.20}} thousand were from taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices, and the per capita GDP was R${{formatnum:61482.00}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/38/46996 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102152010/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/38/46996 |archive-date=January 2, 2025 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios - 2021 |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2021 |access-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref> In 2010, 64.9% of the population over 18 was [[Workforce|economically active]], with an unemployment rate of 11.55%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> Notably, about 16.8% of the population commuted to another municipality for work, given the location and easy access to industrial complexes in other municipalities of the Steel Valley Metropolitan Region.<ref name="PDDI2_304">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=304}}</ref>

In 2022, wages and other remunerations totaled {{formatnum:781699}} thousand [[Brazilian real|reais]], with an average monthly salary of 2.7 [[minimum wages]]. There were {{formatnum:3656}} local units and {{formatnum:3533}} active companies.<ref name="IBGE_Empresas">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/19/143491 |title=Cadastro Central de Empresas |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2022 |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102162244/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/19/143491 |archive-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref> According to the IBGE, in 2010, 51.01% of households had a monthly per capita income of less than one minimum wage ({{formatnum:12509}} households), 37.05% had between one and three minimum wages per person ({{formatnum:9134}} households), 5.54% had between three and five minimum wages ({{formatnum:1359}} households), 3.63% had over five minimum wages (890 households), and 2.56% had no income (628 households).<ref name="SIDRA_Rendimento">{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/Tabela/3261 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170110180120/https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/3261%23/n6/3168705/v/all/p/all/c386/all/d/v1000096%202/l/t,v+c386,p/resultado |archive-date=January 10, 2017 |title=Domicílios particulares permanentes, por classes de rendimento nominal mensal domiciliar per capita |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2010 |access-date=January 10, 2017}}</ref>

===Agriculture===
In 2021, livestock and agriculture contributed {{formatnum:1663.03}} thousand reais to Timóteo's economy,<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> while in 2010, 1.75% of the economically active population was employed in this sector.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> According to the IBGE, in 2023, the municipality had a herd of {{formatnum:1370}} [[poultry]], {{formatnum:1031}} [[cattle]], 152 [[horses]], 73 [[pigs]], and 31 [[sheep]].<ref name="Agropecuária"/> In the same year, the city produced {{formatnum:777}} thousand liters of milk from 215 [[cows]], 7,000 dozen eggs from {{formatnum:550}} hens, and {{formatnum:103500}} kilograms of [[honey]].<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/18/16459 |title=Pecuária |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102162635/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/18/16459 |archive-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref> In 2019, Timóteo and neighboring Ipatinga were the two Minas Gerais municipalities exporting the most honey internationally, according to the state government.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.em.com.br/app/colunistas/marta-vieira/2021/01/15/interna_marta_vieira%2C1229254/apicultura-de-minas-preve-2021-de-expansao-e-novas-floradas.shtml |title=Apicultura de Minas prevê 2021 de expansão e novas floradas |author=Marta Vieira |date=January 15, 2021 |publisher=Jornal Estado de Minas |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210118132148/https://www.em.com.br/app/colunistas/marta-vieira/2021/01/15/interna_marta_vieira%2C1229254/apicultura-de-minas-preve-2021-de-expansao-e-novas-floradas.shtml |archive-date=January 18, 2021}}</ref>

In permanent agriculture, in 2023, the crops with the largest planted areas were [[banana]]s (9 hectares) and [[coconut]]s (1 hectare).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/15/11863 |title=Lavoura Permanente |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102163013/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/15/11863 |archive-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref> [[Family farming]] is prominent in Timóteo, particularly through the cultivation of [[vegetable]]s, honey production, and [[biscuit]] manufacturing.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0096559-associacao-da-agricultura-familiar-de-timoteo-recebe-veiculo |title=Associação da Agricultura Familiar de Timóteo recebe veículo |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=April 18, 2022 |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102163428/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0096559-associacao-da-agricultura-familiar-de-timoteo-recebe-veiculo |archive-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref> Part of the local agricultural production is sold at local markets. The Timirim Fair, held in the João XXIII neighborhood, is one of the main regular open-air markets in the Steel Valley Metropolitan Region.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.minasgerais.com.br/pt/atracoes/timoteo/feira-livre-do-timirim |title=Feira Livre do Timirim |author=Secretaria de Estado de Cultura e Turismo de Minas Gerais |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102163454/https://www.minasgerais.com.br/pt/atracoes/timoteo/feira-livre-do-timirim |archive-date=January 2, 2025}}</ref>

===Industry and services===
[[File:Escritório Central da Aperam South America, Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|right|Central office of [[Aperam South America]]]]
In 2021, industry contributed {{formatnum:3162686.42}} thousand reais to the municipal GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> Much of this is due to the presence of [[Aperam South America]],<ref name="ABEP_4">{{Harvtxt|ref=Dinâmica|ABEP|2012|p=4}}</ref> the largest producer of [[stainless steel]] and [[electrical steel]] in [[South America]], with an annual production capacity of 800,000 to 900,000 tons of crude steel (in slabs). In 2015, it employed over {{formatnum:3200}} workers in Timóteo and northern Minas Gerais at Aperam Bioenergia, with about 20% of its production exported.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.otempo.com.br/capa/economia/aperam-est%C3%A1-investindo-us-26-milh%C3%B5es-em-minas-gerais-1.1010449 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170111184841/http://www.otempo.com.br/capa/economia/aperam-est%C3%A1-investindo-us-26-milh%C3%B5es-em-minas-gerais-1.1010449 |archive-date=January 11, 2017 |title=Aperam está investindo US$26 milhões em Minas Gerais |author=Helenice Laguardia |work=Economia |date=March 18, 2015 |publisher=O Tempo |access-date=January 11, 2017}}</ref> The establishment of this industrial complex attracted supplier companies, complementary businesses, and service providers to Timóteo and surrounding municipalities.<ref name="PDDI1_60a61"/>

Timóteo has two [[industrial district]]s, one being the Limoeiro Industrial District, located between the Limoeiro and Alphaville neighborhoods, and the other along the [[BR-381]], between Aperam's industrial core and the Cachoeira do Vale district.<ref name="PDDI1_205a206">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=205–206}}</ref> Other industrial sectors include the production of clothing and accessories, extraction and processing of non-metallic minerals, manufacturing of furniture and fixtures, food and beverage production, machinery manufacturing, and products derived from metallurgy.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> According to 2010 statistics, 0.43% of the workforce was employed in extractive industries and 22.64% in manufacturing.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

In 2010, 8.72% of the employed population worked in construction, 0.92% in public utilities, 15.30% in commerce, and 44.20% in services.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> In 2021, {{formatnum:1275599.13}} thousand reais of the municipal GDP came from the gross added value of the service sector, and {{formatnum:442677.99}} thousand reais from public administration.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> Commercial activity in Timóteo is particularly significant in the Centro-Norte, where Acesita established a commercial center in the 1960s to serve the former workers' village.<ref name="ANPUR_5">{{Harvtxt|ref=ANPUR|Quecini|2009|p=5}}</ref> This area, now known as Acesita, is considered a metropolitan hub, attracting consumers from nearby cities due to its public services and commerce. More recently, commercial activity has expanded in the Funcionários and Timirim neighborhoods, which have easy access to Centro-Norte.<ref name="PDDI1_142e147a155">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=142; 147–155}}</ref>

Timóteo has two [[cemeteries]], namely Recanto da Paz, in the Bela Vista neighborhood, and Jardim da Saudade, in the Santa Maria neighborhood.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2014/10/cemiterios-do-vale-do-aco-intensificam-trabalhos-para-o-dia-dos-finados.html |title=Cemitérios do Vale do Aço são preparados para o Dia de Finados |publisher=G1 |date=30 October 2014 |access-date=30 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190830165419/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2014/10/cemiterios-do-vale-do-aco-intensificam-trabalhos-para-o-dia-dos-finados.html |archive-date=30 August 2019}}</ref> In 2022, 590 deaths were recorded due to [[morbidity]], with diseases of the [[circulatory system]] being the leading cause of death (25.42%), followed by [[Neoplasm|tumors]] (18.47%).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/17/15752 |title=Morbidade |publisher=IBGE Cidades |date=2022 |access-date=2 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102163857/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/17/15752 |archive-date=2 January 2025}}</ref> During the same year, 894 live births were recorded, with the [[infant mortality]] rate standing at 5.59 deaths of children under one year of age per thousand live births.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/39/30279 |title=Taxa de mortalidade infantil |publisher=IBGE Cidades |date=2022 |access-date=2 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102164232/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/39/30279 |archive-date=2 January 2025}}</ref> It is noteworthy that in 2010, 1.3% of girls aged 10 to 17 gave birth.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

===Housing, services, and communications===
[[File:Prédios no B. Funcionários, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|Residential buildings in the Funcionários neighborhood]]
In 2022, Timóteo had {{formatnum:30026}} occupied permanent private households. Of these, {{formatnum:21984}} were houses (73.22%), {{formatnum:7769}} were apartments (25.87%), 137 were houses in villages or condominiums (0.46%), 101 were [[tenements]] (0.34%), and 35 were degraded or unfinished structures (0.12%).<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Of the total occupied households, {{formatnum:21393}} were owned (71.20%), with {{formatnum:19263}} fully paid and {{formatnum:2130}} in the process of acquisition; {{formatnum:6611}} were [[Renting|rented]] (22.00%); {{formatnum:1905}} were borrowed or lent (6.34%); and 138 were occupied in other ways (0.46%).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3168705/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |title=Tabela 9930 - Domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados, por número de cômodos, condição de ocupação do domicílio e tipo do domicílio |publisher=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2022 |access-date=2 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250102164647/https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3168705/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |archive-date=2 January 2025}}</ref> The municipality has a Municipal Master Plan that regulates geographic and urban parameters for the construction of residences, buildings, and commercial establishments.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/pol%C3%ADtica-e-economia/decreto-regulamenta-ocupacao-solo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170111190312/http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/pol%C3%ADtica-e-economia/decreto-regulamenta-ocupacao-solo |archive-date=11 January 2017 |title=Decreto regulamenta ocupação do solo |publisher=Plox |date=30 March 2011 |access-date=11 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Water supply and sewage collection services are provided by the Minas Gerais Sanitation Company (Copasa).<ref name="PDDI1_198a202">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=198–202}}</ref> In 2022, according to IBGE, 91.46% of households had the general water network as their main supply source, and 99.90% had a bathroom for exclusive household use. Regarding [[sewage disposal]], 94.53% of households were served by the general or stormwater network.<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Much of the water used to supply the city and the Vale do Aço region comes from an alluvial aquifer located underground, extracted and treated at Copasa's [[water treatment plant]] in the Amaro Lanari neighborhood, in Coronel Fabriciano.<ref name="JVA_Seca"/> However, Timóteo has three smaller water intake points that exclusively serve the municipality.<ref name="PDDI1_198a202"/> Sewage, on the other hand, was released directly into the urban area's watercourses without treatment<ref name="PDDI2_457e458"/><ref name="PDDI1_198a202"/> until 2019, when a [[wastewater treatment plant]] began operating in the Limoeiro neighborhood, initially intended to serve 165,000 residents of Timóteo and Coronel Fabriciano.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0071258-estacao-de-tratamento-de-esgoto-vai-comecar-a-operar-em-setembro-informa-copasa |title=Estação de Tratamento de Esgoto começar a operar em setembro, informa Copasa |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=31 August 2019 |access-date=2 September 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190902200630/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0071258-estacao-de-tratamento-de-esgoto-vai-comecar-a-operar-em-setembro-informa-copasa |archive-date=2 September 2019}}</ref>

This area was closed and designated for preservation, but soil and water contamination persisted due to the lack of environmental recovery.<ref name="PDDI2_421a425">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=421–425}}</ref>

===Safety and crime===
[[File:Pelotão do Corpo de Bombeiros de Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|left|Fire brigade of the Fire Department in Cruzeirinho]]

Timóteo |work=Polícia Militar de Minas Gerais |publisher=Polícia Militar do Estado de Minas Gerais (PMMG) |access-date=11 January 2017}}</ref> The Civil Police aims to investigate and address crimes and infractions<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.pc.mg.gov.br/internas/servicos/iServicos.php |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130924204210/http://web.archive.org/web/20070302103809/http://www.pc.mg.gov.br/internas/servicos/iServicos.php |archive-date=24 September 2013 |title=Serviços |publisher=Polícia Civil do Estado de Minas Gerais (PCMG) |date=2007 |access-date=11 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> and is represented in Timóteo by an Integrated Public Safety Area (AISP), subordinate to the Ipatinga Regional Civil Police Station.<ref name="PDDI3_548a549"/> The city is served by a Fire Department brigade<ref>{{cite web|url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/98491-6/cidades/bombeiros-de-ipatinga-elevado-a-3a-companhia-independente |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160103181426/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/98491-6/cidades/bombeiros-de-ipatinga-elevado-a-3a-companhia-independente |archive-date=3 January 2016 |title=Bombeiros de Ipatinga elevado à 3ª Companhia Independente |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=4 November 2015 |access-date=11 January 2017}}</ref> and also has a [[civil defense]], subordinate to the municipal government.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=39774&t=defesa-civil-em-timoteo--monitora-areas-de-risco- |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170111183827/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=39774&t=defesa-civil-em-timoteo--monitora-areas-de-risco- |archive-date=11 January 2017 |title=Defesa Civil em Timóteo monitora áreas de risco |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=26 November 2013 |access-date=11 January 2017}}</ref>

===Transportation===
[[File:Trem da Vale com minério de ferro na EFVM e BR-381 em Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|[[Vale S.A.|Vale]] train transporting iron ore on the [[Vitória-Minas Railway|EFVM]] to the left of [[BR-381]] in Timóteo]]
Timóteo is served by the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]] (EFVM), operated by [[Vale S.A.|Vale]], which is a key means of transporting [[iron ore]] extracted in [[Itabira]] to Aperam South America and for exporting the company's production.<ref name="Acesita_História"/> The Mário Carvalho Station facilitates passenger transport with daily stops for trains traveling between the metropolitan areas of [[Greater Vitória|Vitória]] and [[Greater Belo Horizonte|Belo Horizonte]]. Among regular public transportation options, the EFVM is the most affordable travel option for several cities with stations.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://vfco.brazilia.jor.br/Carros/efvm/estacao-ferroviaria-Mario-Carvalho.shtml |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112175324/http://vfco.brazilia.jor.br/Carros/efvm/estacao-ferroviaria-Mario-Carvalho.shtml |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Estação Mário Carvalho |publisher=Centro-Oeste |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/mario.htm |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120729040108/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/mario.htm |archive-date=29 July 2012 |title=Mário Carvalho |publisher=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |date=25 February 2006 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref>

The municipality has one of the region's main bus terminals, located in Centro-Norte and inaugurated in January 1983.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=58124&t=terminal-rodoviario-passa-por-reforma |archive-url=https://archive.today/20180630140933/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=58124&t=terminal-rodoviario-passa-por-reforma |archive-date=30 June 2018 |title=Terminal rodoviário passa por reforma |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=11 April 2018 |access-date=30 June 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=5348 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112175404/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=5348 |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Timóteo anuncia obras de infraestrutura em 17 ruas |publisher=Jornal Diário Popular |date=17 May 2013 |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The city is served by [[BR-381]], the main access route to the state capital and [[Espírito Santo]]; and by LMG-760, connecting the urban area to [[BR-262 (Brazil highway)|BR-262]] through the Cava Grande district in Marliéria.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=110656 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150702141046/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=110656 |archive-date=2 July 2015 |title=DER cancela pedido de licença para a pavimentação da MG-760 |publisher=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=10 September 2014 |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="Google">[http://maps.google.com.br/ Google Maps]. Accessed on 12 January 2017.</ref> The municipal vehicle fleet in 2023 totaled {{formatnum:50371}} vehicles, including {{formatnum:27825}} cars, {{formatnum:12893}} motorcycles, {{formatnum:3123}} pickups, {{formatnum:1557}} SUVs, {{formatnum:1137}} trucks, 981 trailers, 895 scooters, 556 utility vehicles, 407 mopeds, 313 semi-trailers, 262 tractor-trailers, 201 minibuses, 186 buses, 17 tricycles, nine wheeled tractors, four sidecars, and five classified as other types of vehicles.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/22/28120 |title=Frota |publisher=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=2 January 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102165133/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/timoteo/pesquisa/22/28120 |archive-date=2 January 2025}}</ref> The urban area occasionally experiences traffic congestion due to increased vehicle flow.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=1272 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112175456/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=1272 |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Prefeitura altera trânsito em rua do bairro Garapa |publisher=Prefeitura |date=14 November 2013 |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Near commercial hubs, parking availability is sometimes limited, impacting local businesses. Proposals such as implementing a paid parking system have been considered and implemented.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=828 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112175515/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=828 |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Timóteo estuda a reativação do 'Faixa Azul' em setembro |publisher=Prefeitura |date=9 July 2013 |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aciati.com.br/noticia/437/lojas-credenciadas-para-venda-dos-taloes-do-faixa-azul |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112175526/http://www.aciati.com.br/noticia/437/lojas-credenciadas-para-venda-dos-taloes-do-faixa-azul |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Lojas credenciadas para venda dos talões e ruas com Faixa Azul |publisher=Associação Comercial Industrial, Agropecuária e de Prestação de Serviços de Timóteo (ACIATI) |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Culture==
[[File:Praça 1º de Maio, Timóteo MG.JPG|left|thumb|May 1st Square, a leisure and event space in Centro-Norte]]
The first investments in leisure facilities were made by the former Acesita company to encourage workers to settle in the company’s workers' village. Initially, civic celebrations and football tournaments were organized, followed by the creation of clubs such as the Elite Club and the Workers’ Club, which also hosted theater and music performances.<ref name="Acesita_História" /><ref name="ANPUR_3">{{Harvtxt|ref=ANPUR|Quecini|2009|p=3}}</ref> The Marabá Cinema, originally Acesita Cinema, was established in the 1950s and relocated to a new building in the commercial center of the workers' village (now Centro-Norte) in the 1970s, but it was deactivated and converted into a commercial establishment in the 1990s.<ref name="UNB_159">{{Harvtxt|ref=UNB|Luciano|2013|p=159}}</ref> Centro-Sul, meanwhile, had older cultural traditions, such as the Nossa Senhora do Rosário Congado Group, dating back to 1895, and street carnival celebrations in the 1940s.<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

Following emancipation in 1964, the municipal government increased investments in public squares and sports centers throughout the city.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/> Timóteo has a municipal culture council and a heritage preservation board, both with equal representation and deliberative and oversight roles.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=638&ordem=6.2&munic=3168705&uf=&nome=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112201426/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=638&ordem=6.2&munic=316870&uf=&nome=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Conselho municipal de cultura e de preservação do patrimônio |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref> There are also municipal laws protecting tangible cultural heritage, managed by a dedicated municipal culture department, which oversees cultural activities in the municipality.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=632&ordem=6.1&munic=3168705&uf=&=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112201630/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=632&ordem=6.1&munic=316870&uf=&nome=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Órgão gestor e legislação da cultura |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref> Despite public sector involvement, Aperam South America, through the Aperam Acesita Foundation, also supports arts and cultural facilities, including projects funded by the [[Rouanet Law]].<ref name="Espaço_Arte">{{cite web|url=http://brasil.aperam.com/revista-espaco/no-56-juniojulio-2010/cultura/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112201920/http://brasil.aperam.com/revista-espaco/no-56-junjul-2010/cultura/ |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Arte em páginas |publisher=Revista Espaço |date=1 August 2010 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref>

In 2013, the following tangible assets were listed as municipal [[cultural heritage]]: the forest of the Rio Doce State Park, Percival Farquhar State School, the former Technical Metallurgy School, the "Biquinha" spring, and Getúlio Vargas State School. The only listed movable asset was the Oratory of the Holy Spirit.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/><ref name="PDDI3_653">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=653}}</ref> However, in 2022, the Hoffmann Kiln was also listed.<ref name="CaminhosGerais_Forno">{{cite web|url=https://revistacaminhosgerais.com.br/noticias/marco-do-inicio-da-industrializacao-do-vale-do-aco-recebera-restauro-e-se-transformara-em-museu/ |title=Localizado no Bairro Novo Horizonte, em Timóteo, o Forno Hoffman é tombado pelo município |author=Mário Carvalho Neto |date=26 May 2023 |publisher=Revista Caminhos Gerais |access-date=6 September 2023 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230906154131/https://revistacaminhosgerais.com.br/noticias/marco-do-inicio-da-industrializacao-do-vale-do-aco-recebera-restauro-e-se-transformara-em-museu/ |archive-date=6 September 2023}}</ref> The municipality’s cultural policy score for the 2024 Cultural ICMS calculation was 3.00 on a scale of 0 to 4. The total score, factoring in conservation, expenditure, and the number of listed or registered assets, was 9.83.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |title=ICMS Critério do Patrimônio Cultural |publisher=Instituto Estadual do Patrimônio Histórico e Artístico de Minas Gerais (IEPHA) |page=35 |access-date=6 September 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230906154209/http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |archive-date=6 September 2023}}</ref>

===Cultural expressions and spaces===
[[File:Fachada do Centro Cultural da Fundação Aperam Acesita, Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|right|Aperam Acesita Foundation Cultural Center]]
The city boasts a rich and diverse folklore. According to IBGE in 2012, there are artistic groups dedicated to choirs, folklore, and musical performances.<ref name="IBGE_Grupos">{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3762&ordem=8.3&munic=3168705&uf=&nome=Timóteo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112202227/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3762&ordem=8.3&munic=316870&uf=&nome=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Grupos artísticos |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref> [[Handicraft]]s are one of the most prominent forms of cultural expression in Timóteo, with IBGE noting that the main artisanal activities involve fruits and seeds, wood, and metal.<ref name="IBGE_Artesanato">{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=679&ordem=6.4&munic=3168705&uf=&nome=Timóteo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112203319/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=679&ordem=6.4&munic=316870&uf=&nome=Tim%C3%B3teo |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Principais atividades artesanais |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref> Another significant cultural expression is the Nossa Senhora do Rosário Congado Group, introduced by Manoel Berto de Lima in 1895, which performs in honor of [[Our Lady of the Rosary]] during festive occasions.<ref name="Prefeitura_Eventos">{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/15/localizacao.aspx |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112203258/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/15/localizacao.aspx |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Eventos |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref>

Carnival celebrations originated in what is now Praça 29 de Abril, in Centro-Sul, where residents began gathering in the 1940s to dance in masks and costumes, inspired by ''[[marchinha]]s'' played on battery-powered radios. In the 1960s, these festivities moved to city clubs, evolving with the formation of [[samba school]]s and [[carnival block]]s. In the 1980s, the municipal government, in partnership with Acesita, began organizing a street carnival in Centro-Norte, involving local carnival groups.<ref name="Acesita_História" /> Though not held regularly, it has attracted over 40,000 people, featuring block parades and musical performances.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=300 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170112234923/http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=300 |archive-date=12 January 2017 |title=Carnaval de Timóteo coloca 40 mil na rua |publisher=Jornal Diário Popular |date=22 February 2012 |access-date=12 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Cantata de Natal 2010 da ArcelorMittal Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|Aperam Acesita Foundation during its [[Christmas carol]]]]

The Feast of [[Saint Sebastian]] (the municipal patron saint), the Saint Sebastian Rustic Race, the Rosário Feast (with the congado group’s participation), the city’s anniversary celebrations in April, the Feast of [[Our Lady of Aparecida]], and the [[Christmas carol]] by the Aperam Acesita Foundation are other examples of popular cultural expressions in the municipality.<ref name="PDDI3_642a646">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=642–646}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Festas">{{cite |title=Principais festas populares |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2005 |access-date=12 January 2017}}</ref> On [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], carpets are crafted on neighborhood streets by parishes, continuing a tradition that dates back to 1930.<ref name="Prefeitura_Eventos"/> Among the cultural spaces dedicated to preserving and promoting popular expressions, the city has publicly maintained libraries, theaters, stadiums, sports gyms, clubs, recreational associations, and a public archive, according to IBGE in 2005 and 2012.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3737&ordem=8.2&munic=3168705&uf=&nome=Timóteo ===Landmarks and attractions===
====Rural and natural attractions====
[[File:Vista do Pico do Ana Moura (1).JPG|thumb|right|View of the Petrópolis neighborhood from the summit of Ana Moura Peak]]
Timóteo is part of the Minas Gerais Atlantic Forest Tourist Circuit, established in 2010 by the Minas Gerais State Tourism Secretariat to promote tourism in participating cities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |title=Circuitos Turísticos de Minas Gerais |publisher=Secretaria de Turismo de Minas Gerais |date=18 March 2013 |access-date=13 January 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731133231/http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |archive-date=31 July 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Among the natural attractions, the Ana Moura Peak stands out as the municipality’s highest point, reaching 864 meters in altitude. Its summit is open to visitors and features communication antennas, a paragliding launch ramp, and is used for climbing.<ref name="PDDI2_368a369">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=368–369}}</ref>

In the Petrópolis neighborhood, a key access point to the Ana Moura Peak, there are farm hotels, rural properties, and trails used for hiking and motocross.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=5118 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114162647/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=5118 |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Turismo no Pico Ana Moura será retomado em Timóteo |publisher=Jornal Diário Popular |date=16 April 2013 |access-date=13 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.montanha.bio.br/web_cem/anamoura.htm |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114162708/http://www.montanha.bio.br/web_cem/anamoura.htm |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Pico Ana Moura |publisher=Escaladas de Minas |access-date=13 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The forests of the [[Rio Doce State Park]] (PERD), listed as municipal cultural heritage, are also noteworthy. At the Oikós Project’s environmental education center, maintained by Aperam South America and integrated with the PERD, visitors can explore a preserved section of the Atlantic Forest, with trails, lookouts, a ropes course, and a library.<ref name="PDDI2_368a369"/>

====Urban landmarks====
[[File:Igreja São José Operário de Acesita, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|São José Operário Church in 2017]]
The urban area features several attractions considered major city landmarks due to their heritage or symbolic importance. The São José Operário Church in Praça 1º de Maio, also known as "São José de Acesita Church" or "Centro Church," was built by Acesita in 1947 to serve the religious needs of the company’s workers' village.<ref name="Diário_Igrejinha">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=46825&t=a-reforma-da-igrejinha-em-timoteo- igrejinha em Timóteo |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=16 September 2014 |access-date=13 January 2017}}</ref> The church, which can seat about 100 people and accommodate 300 standing, is too small for some events, which attract over {{formatnum:1500}} attendees, requiring outdoor activities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2013/07/26/interna_gerais,427775/ex-metalurgicos-tentam-evitar-ampliacao-de-capela-em-timoteo-no-vale-do-aco.shtml |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Ex-metalúrgicos tentam evitar ampliação de capela em Timóteo, no Vale do Aço |publisher=Jornal Estado de Minas |date=26 July 2013 |access-date=13 January 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2014/01/15/interna_gerais,488215/paroquia-e-proibida-pela-justica-de-fazer-reformas-na-igreja-sao-jose-em-timoteo.shtml |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114162845/http://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2014/01/15/interna_gerais,488215/paroquia-e-proibida-pela-justica-de-fazer-reformas-na-igreja-sao-jose-em-timoteo.shtml dedicated to Our Lady of Mount Carmel in 2025.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.jornalbairrosnet.com.br/2025/destaques/nossa-senhora-do-carmo-e-a-nova-padroeira-da-igreja-catolica-de-acesita-timoteo-mg/ |title=Nova Igreja Católica do Centro Norte, em Timóteo, The São José do Timirim Church, located atop the Timirim neighborhood, began construction in 1950 to serve as the main church of the São José Parish. Completed 27 years later, it was modeled after the [[Saint Peter’s Basilica]] in [[Vatican City]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Verde">{{cite web |url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=194 |title=Timóteo uma cidade verde |publisher=Prefeitura |date=26 April 2010 |access-date=14 July 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120111160605/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias.aspx?cd=194 |archive-date=11 January 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The São Sebastião Parish Church, headquarters of the São Sebastião Parish in Centro-Sul, began construction in 1950 to replace a wooden church built in 1915, which itself had replaced the settlement’s first chapel.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/aconteceu/par%C3%B3quia-s%C3%A3o-sebasti%C3%A3o-promove-campanha-para-reforma-da-igreja-em-tim%C3%B3teo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231165957/http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/aconteceu/par%C3%B3quia-s%C3%A3o-sebasti%C3%A3o-promove-campanha-para-reforma-da-igreja-em-tim%C3%B3teo |archive-date=31 December 2012 |title=Paróquia São Sebastião promove campanha para reforma da igreja em Timóteo |publisher=Plox |date=19 June 2009 |access-date=14 July 2010 |url-status=dead}}</ref> At the access roundabout to Coronel Fabriciano via the old [[BR-381]] bridge, the Sinergia Monument stands,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=33631&t=timoteo-quer-municipalizar-trecho-da-br-381 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114162941/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/ler_noticia.php?id=33631&t=timoteo-quer-municipalizar-trecho-da-br-381 |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Timóteo quer municipalizar trecho da BR-381 |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=24 November 2011 |access-date=14 January 2017}}</ref> designed by sculptor Wilma Noel. Installed in the 1990s, it represents the municipal administrations of the three main Vale do Aço cities (Coronel Fabriciano, Ipatinga, and Timóteo), also known as the "Three Cities" monument.<ref name="USP_263">{{Harvtxt|ref=USP|Quecini|2007|p=263}}</ref>
[[File:Vista parcial da Praça 29 de Abril, Timóteo MG2.JPG|thumb|right|April 29 Square, a reference point in Centro-Sul]]

In the Timirim neighborhood, the Praça do Coliseu, designed by architect Éolo Maia and built in 1983, features an open-air theater for cultural activities.<ref name="PDDI3_642a646"/><ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/> Also in 1983, Éolo Maia designed the Percival Farquhar State School in the Vale Verde neighborhood, notable for its self-supporting solid brick structures.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio">{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/14/pontos-turisticos |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114163036/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/14/pontos-turisticos |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Pontos turísticos |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=14 January 2017}}</ref> The Hoffmann Kiln, located in the Novo Horizonte neighborhood, was built in 1945 to produce bricks for the Acesita workers' village’s residences. Deactivated in the 1960s, it briefly served as a nightclub but was later sealed off due to its deteriorating condition. Despite partial collapse of its walls and roof, it is used for studies and occasionally hosts cultural activities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=2715 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114163102/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=2715 |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Comunidade conhecerá projeto de restauração do Forno Hoffmann |publisher=Jornal Diário Popular |date=6 September 2012 |access-date=14 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias/3432/exposicao-revela-forno-hoffmann |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114163156/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/noticias/3432/exposicao-revela-forno-hoffmann |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Exposição revela Forno Hoffmann |publisher=Prefeitura |date=6 June 2016 |access-date=14 January 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> In May 2023, a service order was signed for the development of a restoration project.<ref name="CaminhosGerais_Forno"/>

Other cultural assets include the former Technical Metallurgy School, built by Acesita in 1963 and acquired by the municipal government in 1994 after its deactivation; the "Biquinha," a spring used by early 20th-century residents of the São Sebastião de Timóteo settlement; and the Getúlio Vargas State School, located in the Funcionários neighborhood and inaugurated in 1948. As previously mentioned, these sites are listed as municipal cultural heritage.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/><ref name="PDDI3_653"/> Also noteworthy are April 29 Square and May 1 Square, in Centro-Sul and Centro-Norte respectively, which are among the region’s main leisure and event venues.<ref name="PDDI3_634">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=634}}</ref>

===Sports===
[[File:Entrada do Estádio José de Oliveira Freitas, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|left|Entrance to José de Oliveira Freitas Stadium in João XXIII, home of Acesita Esporte Clube.]]
Timóteo offers a range of spaces and facilities for sports, including clubs, football fields, and gyms,<ref name="PDDI3_672">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=672}}</ref> but [[Association football|football]] received the earliest support due to its popularity. The Florestino Social Club, founded in Centro-Sul on 12 October 1938, was the first football club in the current municipality.<ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="UNB_156a158"/> However, the former Acesita company organized the first tournaments and established the Acesita Esporte Clube in the 1940s to provide leisure for its workers, encouraging the creation of new teams.<ref name="UNB_156a158">{{Harvtxt|ref=UNB|Luciano|2013|pp=156–158}}</ref>

Teams formed until the 1960s received support from Acesita to sustain themselves, but Acesita Esporte Clube was the municipality’s most successful team.<ref name="UNB_156a158"/> It competed in professional Minas Gerais championships and holds multiple titles in the main amateur municipal leagues.<ref name="Acesita_História"/><ref name="AcesitaEC_História"/> During this period, the company expanded investments in other sports, hosting tournaments and building the Acesita Esporte Clube’s social headquarters and the ALFA (Acesita Employees’ Leisure Association) sports complex, completed in the 1980s. However, ALFA was deactivated, and its area was sold in 2019.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0072129-clube-alfa-e-vendido-por-r-425-milhoes |title=Clube Alfa é vendido por R$ 4,25 milhões |publisher=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2 October 2019 |access-date=8 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191008230052/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0072129-clube-alfa-e-vendido-por-r-425-milhoes |archive-date=8 October 2019}}</ref> Since the 1950s, the Elite Club and Workers’ Club existed, but the Elite Club focused on dances and events, while the Workers’ Club, aimed at lower-income workers, prioritized sports.<ref name="UNB_156a158"/> Investments in public squares and sports centers were also intensified by the municipal government after emancipation.<ref name="ANPUR_6a9e14"/>
[[File:Entrada do Ginásio Iorque José Martins, Timóteo MG.JPG|thumb|right|Iorque José Martins Gymnasium]]

In addition to recreational facilities, the club offers courses in some of these sports and operates the Acesita football school.<ref name="AcesitaEC_História">{{cite include Joaquim Cirilo, the Vila Nova field in the Quitandinha neighborhood;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/esportes/timoteo-tera-campeonato-acesitano-master ===Holidays and symbols===
In Timóteo, there are three municipal [[Public holiday|holidays]] and eight national holidays, in addition to optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the city’s anniversary, celebrated on 29 April; [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], which in 2025 is observed on 19 June; and the [[Assumption of Mary|Assumption of Our Lady]], on 15 August.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=7945 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170114190310/http://diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=7945 |archive-date=14 January 2017 |title=Excesso de feriados prejudica o comércio |author=Jornal Diário Popular |work=Jornal Diário Popular MG |date=11 April 2014 |access-date=14 January 2017 }}</ref>

The municipality also has official symbols, namely the flag and the coat of arms. These were designed by João Pinto, a draftsman from the former Acesita company, and were officially adopted by Law No. 179 on 22 January 1968. The flag consists of the municipal coat of arms centered on a blue, white, and red rectangle. The coat of arms, in turn, features three stars symbolizing the Executive, Legislative, and Judicial branches. The triangle represents the balance between these branches; the Olympic flame symbolizes commerce; and the gear and anvil represent industry.<ref name="Prefeitura_Símbolos">{{cite web |url=http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/pagina/index.php?pag_descricao=Pagina_Simbolos |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121130113943/http://www.timoteo.mg.gov.br/pagina/index.php?pag_descricao=Pagina_Simbolos |archive-date=30 November 2012 |title=Símbolos Oficiais do Município |author=Prefeitura |access-date=2 March 2025 }}</ref>
