'''Gravataí''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Sul]], located north of the state [[Porto Alegre|capital]], approximately 23 km away. It is one of the 32 municipalities within the [[Greater Porto Alegre|Porto Alegre Metropolitan Region]] (RMPA). The municipality covers an area of {{convert|463.758|km2}}, with {{convert|121.37|km2}} designated as the [[urban area]],<ref name="Embrapa_RS">{{cite web |url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/rs.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEBm9yq?url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/rs.html |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Rio Grande do Sul |author=Embrapa Monitoramento por Satélite |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> and its population was recorded in 2022 as {{formatnum:265070}} inhabitants, making it the sixth most populous municipality in the state and the third in the RMPA.<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2010">{{cite web|access-date=11 December 2010 |date=29 November 2010 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |title=Censo Populacional 2010 |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/censo2010/populacao_por_municipio.shtm }}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

Gravataí was initially established in 1763, with official emancipation occurring in 1880. The name is believed to derive from a combination of ''[[Bromelia pinguin|gravatá]]'', a species of [[Apiaceae]] (formerly Umbelliferae) abundant in the region, and ''hy'', meaning river in the [[Guarani language]].<ref name="Câmara_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/historia.asp |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xm60QRCM?url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/historia.asp |archive-date=7 April 2011 |title=The Beginning of the City |author=Municipal Chamber |access-date=7 April 2011}}</ref> Today, the municipality's primary economic driver is the industrial sector, with the Gravataí Automotive Industrial Complex of [[General Motors]] serving as a significant source of revenue, positioning the city as a hub for Brazil's metal-mechanical industry.<ref name="CIAG_Sete anos">{{cite web |url=http://www.ciag.com.br/noticias.php?noticia=48 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvF7KxmX?url=http://www.ciag.com.br/noticias.php?noticia=48 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=CIAG: Seven Years of Activities |author=Gravataí Automotive Industrial Complex (CIAG) |date=2007 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

The municipality also boasts a rich cultural heritage that includes handicrafts, theater, music and sports. One of its most prominent and traditional football clubs is the [[Cerâmica Atlético Clube]], founded in April 1950. Gravataí hosts several annual events, including the Feast of [[Our Lady of Navigators]], the Bromeliad Festival, and the Gravataí International Cycling Tour, alongside notable tourist attractions such as the Agostinho Martha Municipal Museum, which showcases the colonial history of the Gravataí River Valley.<ref name="FeriasTur_Gravataí">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/7748/gravatai-rs.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6j63YX?url=http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/7748/gravatai-rs.html |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Gravataí |publisher=Ferias Tur |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Gravataí |language=pt}}</ref>

== History ==
=== Origins ===
During the expansion of its territories to [[South America]] in the 18th century, the [[Portuguese Crown]] granted ''sesmaria'' letters of concession to settlers already residing in the region to encourage settlement. Pedro Gonçalves Sandoval, a native of [[Lima]], [[Peru]], received the first ''sesmaria'' for inhabiting the area known as the Gravataí corner, within the fields of [[Viamão]].<ref name="Prefeitura_História">{{cite web |url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/historia.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xm5xk0GF?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/historia.php |archive-date=7 April 2011 |title=The History of Gravataí |author=Municipality |access-date=7 April 2011}}</ref> During this period, Captain João Lourenço Veloso also received a ''sesmaria'', taking possession of lands in the same region, further northeast, near Morro Itacolomi. Part of these lands was later purchased by the Portuguese Crown to establish the Aldeia dos Anjos (Village of the Angels). This was the initial settlement of the village, which was later relocated to the current central area of Gravataí.<ref name="Prefeitura_História" />

Since pre-colonial times, [[Portugal]] and [[Spain]] had been encroaching on each other's territories. To address this, they signed the [[Treaty of Madrid (13 January 1750)|Treaty of Madrid]] in 1750, stipulating that Portugal would return the [[Colonia del Sacramento|Sacramento Colony]], established in Spanish territory, in exchange for the [[Misiones Orientales|Seven Towns of the Missions]], located further northeast. To populate the Seven Towns of the Missions, the Portuguese planned to bring settlers from the densely populated [[Azores]] archipelago. As a result of this agreement and the subsequent [[First Treaty of San Ildefonso|Treaty of San Ildefonso (1777)]], the [[Guaraní people|Guarani]] inhabitants of the Seven Towns of the Missions were required to leave the region. Unwilling to abandon their lands, the Guarani initiated the [[Guaraní War|Guarani War]]. Following the war, thousands of indigenous people fled to Portuguese territory, settling near the Rio Pardo, now known as the [[Santa Maria River (Rio Grande do Sul)|Santa Maria River]]. From this group of refugees, approximately one thousand Guarani were brought in 1762 by Captain Antônio Pinto de Carneiro to the vicinity of the [[Gravataí River]], marking the beginning of the Aldeia dos Anjos settlement. Notably, the village existed in practice before its official founding date of April 8, 1763. Due to the turmoil caused by the Guarani War, the Azorean settlers originally intended for the Seven Towns of the Missions were redirected to other areas, namely the [[Jacuí River]] Valley (in the center of the state) and the Gravataí River Valley.<ref name="Prefeitura_História" />

With the arrival of José Marcelino de Figueiredo, Governor of the São Pedro Province, in 1772, the Aldeia dos Anjos began to flourish. Figueiredo urbanized the settlement, constructing schools, brick kilns, and mills. The Tapes Indians, refugees from the Jesuit Missions in [[Uruguay]], were settled in Gravataí by Figueiredo, who taught them wheat cultivation, a practice they later adopted.<ref name="IBGE_História">{{cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/riograndedosul/gravatai.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xm5zOqzk?url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/riograndedosul/gravatai.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-date=2011-04-07 |title=History |author=Cities@ - IBGE |access-date=10 March 2011}}</ref>

=== Administrative and political formation ===
With the establishment of the first four municipalities in Rio Grande do Sul on October 7, 1809, the Aldeia dos Anjos was elevated to the status of a district of [[Porto Alegre]]. On June 11, 1880, due to the heavy traffic of carts carrying goods, primarily from the North Coast and Santo Antônio da Patrulha, it was upgraded to a ''vila'' (town). Four months later, on October 23, 1880, the municipality of Gravataí was officially established. The name originates from the [[Guarani language]], where ''[[Bromelia pinguin|gravatá]]'' refers to a species of [[Apiaceae]], mistakenly classified as a Bromeliad, abundant in the region, and ''hy'' translates to river. Until the late 19th century, the correct spelling of the municipality was "Gravatahy," literally meaning "River of the Gravatás."<ref name="Câmara_História"/>

By provincial act no. 1578 of April 24, 1886, the districts of Glória and Costa do Ipiranga were created. By municipal act no. 48 of December 26, 1912, the district of Canoas was established. By municipal law no. 99 of July 20, 1917, the district of Butiá was created. By municipal law no. 3 of June 7, 1957, the district of Cachoeirinha was formed. By municipal law no. 1 of January 8, 1958, the district of Ipiranga was created. By municipal law no. 302 of January 9, 1958, the district of Dom Feliciano was established. By municipal law no. 719 of November 4, 1966, the district of Barnabé was created. By municipal decree no. 1396 of December 29, 1977, the district of Barro Vermelho was formed. By municipal law no. 121 of May 16, 1983, the district of Itacolomi was created. Over the years, several of these districts were either elevated to city status or dissolved, leaving five districts today: Gravataí, Barro Vermelho, Ipiranga, Itacolomi, and Morungava.<ref name="IBGE_História"/>

=== Economic and social development ===
In the final decades of the 19th century, the region experienced significant growth, largely driven by the cultivation of [[cassava]] and the export of its flour to other parts of Brazil and abroad through the Passo das Canoas. Cassava flour production fueled economic development in the municipality until the first half of the 20th century.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> In the 1930s, José Loureiro da Silva took over the municipal government, marking a significant developmental phase for Gravataí. His key achievements included the implementation of the city's electrical system, the widening and paving of initial streets, the construction of a road connecting Gravataí to Porto Alegre, and the current urban planning of the city center.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

From the 1960s onward, the city underwent rapid industrialization. Key factors in transitioning from an agrarian economy included the construction of the [[BR-290 (Brazil highway)|BR-290]] highway (also known as the "Freeway") and the establishment of an industrial district.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/> This development, combined with the growth of neighboring cities, led to the creation of the [[Greater Porto Alegre|Porto Alegre Metropolitan Region]] on June 8, 1973, by federal complementary law no. 14,<ref>{{Cite wikisource|Federal Complementary Law of Brazil 14 of 1973}}</ref> encompassing Gravataí and 31 other municipalities. By 2001, with nearly four million inhabitants, it was the 102nd largest urban agglomeration globally.<ref name="bevoelkerungsstatistik">{{cite web |url=http://bevoelkerungsstatistik.de/wg.php?x=1132419689&men=gcis&lng=de&gln=xx&dat=32&srt=pnan&col=aohdq&pt=a&va=x |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xm62TAJO?url=http://bevoelkerungsstatistik.de/wg.php?x=1132419689&men=gcis&lng=de&gln=xx&dat=32&srt=pnan&col=aohdq&pt=a&va=x |archive-date=7 April 2011 |title=World: Urban Agglomerations |author=World Gazetteer |language=German |access-date=7 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Recent history ===
A pivotal event for the city's economy was the establishment of the General Motors Industrial Complex between the late 1990s and early 2000s. The announcement of its establishment on March 17, 1997, marked a milestone in the municipality's development, as the company joined the large-scale industrial park and local commerce. The complex solidified the city's industrial profile and established Gravataí as one of Brazil's major metal-mechanical industry hubs.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

In recent years, rural areas have increasingly been replaced by urban areas to meet the demands of urban expansion, driven by the growth of productive activities (industry, commerce, and services) and rising housing needs due to population concentration. The boundary between rural and urban areas is becoming less distinct, with the rural population declining annually.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/><ref name="População_Sexo">{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/demografia/mu_dem_pop_total.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvGvDl2h?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/demografia/mu_dem_pop_total.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Demography - Total Population |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM) |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

== Geography ==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], the municipality spans an area of {{convert|463.758|km2}}, with {{convert|121.37|km2}} forming the [[urban area]] and the remaining {{convert|376.45|km2}} comprising the [[rural area]].<ref name="Embrapa_RS"/> It is located at 22°56'36" south [[latitude]] and 50°59'38" west [[longitude]], approximately 22 kilometers south of the [[Porto Alegre|state capital]], and is part of the [[Greater Porto Alegre|Porto Alegre Metropolitan Region]] (RMPA). Its neighboring municipalities are [[Novo Hamburgo]] and [[Taquara]] to the north; [[Alvorada]] and [[Viamão]] to the south; [[Glorinha]] to the east; and [[Cachoeirinha]] and [[Sapucaia do Sul]] to the west.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano">{{cite web |url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/fundacoes/fmma/arquivos/download.php?arquivo=Volume-2.doc |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xokqMa8j?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/fundacoes/fmma/arquivos/download.php?arquivo=Volume-2.doc |url-status=dead |archive-date=2011-04-09 |title=Volume 2 - Environmental Plan |author=Mario Buede Teixeira |publisher=Municipality |access-date=8 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Geomorphology, geology, and hydrography ===
[[File:Morro itacolomi.JPG|thumb|right|View of Morro Itacolomi, a significant rocky formation in the city, declared a [[cultural heritage]] site of Rio Grande do Sul in 2003 by state law No. 149.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://360graus.terra.com.br/ecologia/default.asp?did=10074&action=news |title=Morro do Itacolomi Declared Cultural Heritage in RS |author=Odilei Medeiro |date=28 March 2004 |publisher=360 Graus |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>]]
The terrain of Gravataí and the Porto Alegre Metropolitan Region is characterized by three major morphostructural domains: sedimentary deposits, [[sedimentary basin]]s, and sedimentary covers, alongside complex [[basement (geology)|basement]] structures. The geology of the RMPA is defined by four tectono-structural domains, distinguished by their depositional environment, origin, litho-structural characteristics, and age. These include the Canguçu Ridge (represented by the Arroio dos Ratos Gneissic Complex, comprising [[orthogneiss]]es ranging from tonalitic to [[granodiorite]]), the Dom Feliciano Belt (consisting of undeformed [[granite]] rocks associated with acidic [[volcanic rock]]s such as [[dacite]]s, [[rhyolite]]s, and [[rhyodacite]]s), the [[Paraná Basin]] (represented by rocks from the [[Permian]] and [[Triassic]] periods), and the Coastal Plain and Alluvia.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

Among the various geological formations found in the municipality, those with the lowest fossil potential are the most widespread: Quaternary deposits, the Serra Geral Formation, and the [[Botucatu Formation]]. However, localized occurrences of highly fossiliferous geological units can be found in Gravataí, including the [[Rio do Rasto Formation]], the Estrada Nova Formation, and the [[Rio Bonito Formation]]. These units represent Permian deposits (295–245 million years old) in the state, containing records of life from the end of the [[Paleozoic]] period. Fossil records of animals and plants are also reported for these formations in various other regions of the state.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

The municipality is primarily situated within the [[Gravataí River]] hydrographic basin, with a smaller portion in the [[Rio dos Sinos|Sinos River]] basin. The Gravataí River originates in [[Santo Antônio da Patrulha]], and its sources are not clearly defined, as the flooded plains form a channel that narrows over 16 km. From the point known as Passo do Vau, the narrowest part of this channel corresponding to the funneling of the wetlands, to its mouth, the river extends 39 km. Most urbanized areas along the riverbed are concentrated in its lower section. The [[stream]]s that flow into the right bank of the Gravataí River include the Pinto Stream, Demétrio Stream, Barnabé Stream, Brigadeiro Stream, and Grande Stream.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

=== Climate ===
The [[climate]] of Gravataí is [[subtropical climate|subtropical]] (classified as Cfah under the [[Köppen climate classification]]),<ref name="vetmedunivienna">{{cite web |url=http://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/pics/kottek_et_al_2006.gif |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEFJDU9?url=http://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/pics/kottek_et_al_2006.gif |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger Climate Classification |author=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger Climate Classification |publisher=Institute for Veterinary Public Health |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> with two distinct seasons: warm summers and cold winters. This is influenced by both its latitude and the effects of [[anticyclone]]s from the [[Atlantic Ocean]] and the mobile polar anticyclone. The warmest month, February, has an average temperature of 25.5°C, with an average high of 30°C and a low of 21°C. The coldest months, June and July, have an average temperature of 15°C, with average highs of 19°C and lows of 11°C.<ref name="Clima"/> The region is predominantly affected by [[wind]]s from the southern and eastern quadrants, originating from subtropical high-pressure systems common in the area due to its geographical position.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

Precipitation is characterized by consistent rainfall distribution throughout the year, with no distinct dry season. However, dry periods occasionally occur due to the absence of the mobile polar anticyclone's influence.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/> The average annual precipitation is {{convert|1346|mm}}, with April being the driest month, recording {{convert|86|mm}}. August and September, the wettest months, average {{convert|140|mm}}. During the rainy season, flooding and landslides are common in some areas.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metsul.com/secoes/visualiza.php?cod_subsecao=1&cod_texto=933 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEGtmF1?url=http://www.metsul.com/secoes/visualiza.php?cod_subsecao=1&cod_texto=933 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=MetSul Maps the Flood Situation and the Impact of Rain on Rivers |author=Eugenio Hackbart |date=27 September 2007 |publisher=Metsul |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> [[Hailstorm]]s are rare in the city, but a notable event occurred on August 27, 2010.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/?Noticia=188392 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEIt4vL?url=http://www.correiodopovo.com.br/Noticias/?Noticia=188392 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Chuva de granizo atingiu Porto Alegre e Gravataí na madrugada |author=Correio do Povo |date=27 August 2010 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Ecology and environment ===
Gravataí features a variety of original and anthropic vegetation formations in both urban and rural areas. These include: Pioneer Formations (found in the low, flood-prone plains of the [[Gravataí River]], with diverse vegetation dominated by shrubby and arboreal species); Seasonal Semideciduous Forest (once present in a forest in the northern part of the city, now largely replaced by housing developments and industries, with only a few remnants remaining); Secondary Vegetation (naturally occupying areas where original forest, shrub, and herbaceous vegetation has been removed); Agriculture and Reforestation (areas previously covered by forests now used for agriculture, livestock, orchards, and reforestation); and Urban Vegetation, located in squares, parks, and public thoroughfares.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

The city currently has 22 tree-lined squares, many featuring native species. There are three parks with abundant green spaces, two of which are publicly maintained: the ''Jayme Caetano Brum'', covering 13,837.92 m², and the ''Parcão da '79''', spanning 29,000.00 m². The third, ''Pampas Safari'', is a private park covering 320 hectares, home to over 1,300 specimens of global fauna, making it the largest safari park in [[South America]]. The municipal administration, guided by the Urban Tree Code, is responsible for adapting the city's [[tree planting]] to environmental conditions, using appropriate plant species for each context.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/> Gravataí also has three conservation units: the ''Banhado Grande'' Ecological Reserve, established by the state through Decree No. 38,971 of October 23, 1998, covering 400 hectares; Morro Itacolomi, considered an ecological and scenic preservation area with native vegetation; and the São Pedro Province Estate, spanning 400 hectares with native tree cover.<ref name="Prefeitura_Plano"/>

== Demography ==
According to the 2022 IBGE data, the municipality's population is {{formatnum:265070}} inhabitants, making it the sixth most populous municipality in the state, with a [[population density]] of 566.04 inhabitants per km².<ref name="IBGE_Pop_2022">{{cite web|access-date=28 June 2023 |date=2023 |publisher=[[IBGE]] |title=Panorama do Censo 2022 |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> According to the 2000 census, 49.38% of the population were men (114,777 inhabitants), 50.62% were women ({{formatnum:117670}} inhabitants), 91.19% ({{formatnum:211969}} inhabitants) lived in the urban area, and 8.81% ({{formatnum:20478}} inhabitants) resided in the rural area.<ref name="UFMS">{{cite web |url=http://www.caminhos.ufms.br/matrizdados/rs/gravatai.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvESUyEw?url=http://www.caminhos.ufms.br/matrizdados/rs/gravatai.html |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Gravataí (RS) |author=[[Federal University of Mato Grosso do Sul]] (UFMS) |date=26 April 2005 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> In 2000, according to IBGE census data, the population of Gravataí consisted of {{formatnum:204506}} Whites (87.91%), 15,155 Blacks (6.51%), {{formatnum:10757}} ''[[Pardo|Pardos]]'' (4.62%), 257 Asians (0.11%), 1,131 Indigenous (0.49%), and 822 undeclared (0.35%).<ref name="SIDRA_Raça">{{cite web |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P |title=Population of Gravataí by Race and Color |author=Sidra (IBGE Automatic Data Recovery System) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

The Municipal [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Gravataí is considered high by the United Nations Development Programme ([[United Nations Development Programme|UNDP]]), with a value of 0.736, ranking 180th in Rio Grande do Sul (out of 496 municipalities), 393rd in Southern Brazil (out of 1,188), and 876th nationwide (out of 5,565).<ref name="PNUD_IDH">{{cite web|access-date=29 May 2015 |date=2010 |publisher=Programa das Nações Unidas para o Desenvolvimento (PNUD) |title=Ranking decrescente do IDH-M dos municípios do Brasil |url=http://www.pnud.org.br/arquivos/ranking-idhm-2010.pdf |work=Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> The city’s indicators are generally average and align with national averages according to the UNDP.<ref name="UFMS"/>

The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], is 0.41, where 1.00 represents the worst score and 0 represents the best.<ref name="IBGE_Pobreza"/> In 2003, the poverty incidence, as measured by IBGE, was 29.39%, with a lower limit of 17.37%, an upper limit of 41.41%, and a subjective poverty incidence of 20.96%.<ref name="IBGE_Pobreza">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEUizjs?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=mpobreza2003&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Social Indicators of Brazilian Municipalities |author=IBGE |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Igreja Nossa Senhora dos Anjos.JPG|thumb|right|165px|Facade of the Nossa Senhora dos Anjos Church]]
Reflecting the cultural diversity of Gravataí, the city is home to a wide range of religious expressions. While it developed on a predominantly [[Catholicism|Catholic]] social foundation, numerous [[Protestantism|Protestant]] denominations are now present.<ref name="Religião"/>

The city is home to various Protestant or Reformed denominations, including the [[Assembleia de Deus|Assembly of God]], Maranata Christian Church, [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian Church]], [[Baptist Church|Baptist churches]], the [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], and the [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], among others. According to the 2000 IBGE census, Gravataí’s population comprised [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (77.27%), [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]] (12.50%), [[Irreligion|non-religious individuals]] (4.72%), [[Umbanda|Umbandists]] (1.82%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (1.64%), and 2.06% belonging to other religions.<ref name="Religião">{{cite web |url=http://www.mai.org.br/tabelas/municipios/municipioslista.php?a=search&value=1&SearchField=AnyField&SearchOption=Contains&SearchFor=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEcUsFG?url=http://www.mai.org.br/tabelas/municipios/municipioslista.php?a=search&value=1&SearchField=AnyField&SearchOption=Contains&SearchFor=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Religion |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

The Nossa Senhora dos Anjos Church, located in the city center and inaugurated in 1772 in the Portuguese [[Baroque]] style, became the seat of the [[Apostolic vicariate|Vicariate]] of Gravataí under the [[Archdiocese of Porto Alegre]], the [[List of Catholic dioceses in Brazil|ecclesiastical circumscription]] to which the municipality belongs, in 2001.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/portal/index.php?q=atrativo&id=802&bd=&fg=2 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEdVM3w?url=http://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/portal/index.php?q=atrativo&id=802&bd=&fg=2 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Nossa Senhora dos Anjos Mother Church |author=Rio Grande do Sul Tourism Secretariat (SETUR) |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> This Vicariate is further divided into four pastoral areas and 28 parishes, encompassing Gravataí, [[Alvorada]], [[Cachoeirinha]], and [[Viamão]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.arquidiocesepoa.org.br/link.aspx?id=123 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEf90ww?url=http://www.arquidiocesepoa.org.br/link.aspx?id=123 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Pastoral Areas |author=[[Archdiocese of Porto Alegre]] |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> Gravataí is also the birthplace of the first bishop of Rio Grande do Sul, Feliciano José Rodrigues de Araújo Prates, born on July 13, 1781, in what was then the Aldeia dos Anjos. Feliciano received the subdiaconate on November 20, 1803, and the diaconate on November 27, 1803, both conferred by the bishop of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]], Dom Mateus de Abreu Pereira, at the Lapa Seminary Chapel in [[Rio de Janeiro (city)|Rio de Janeiro]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/bishop/bropc.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEgShVx?url=http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/bishop/bropc.html |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Bishop Feliciano José Rodrigues de Araujo Prates (de Castilhos) |author=Catholic Hierarchy |language=en |date=19 February 2011 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

== Government and politics ==
The first intendant of the municipality was João Maria da Fonseca, and the first executive leader and [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]] of the municipality was José Loureiro da Silva. Over 29 terms, 22 mayors have governed Gravataí, in addition to eight executive agents.<ref name="Prefeitura_História"/>

The [[legislative power]] is represented by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of 21 councilors elected for four-year terms. The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the Executive, particularly the municipal [[budget]] (known as the Budget Guidelines Law).

Gravataí operates under an organic law established on April 2, 1990.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/ExtraDocs/lei_organica.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEyeAyH?url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/ExtraDocs/lei_organica.pdf |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Organic Law of Gravataí |author=Municipal Chamber |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> The city also serves as the seat of a ''[[comarca]]''.<ref name="comarca">{{cite web |url=http://www.tjrs.jus.br/site_php/noticias/mostranoticia.php?assunto=1&categoria=1&item=18708 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEx95V0?url=http://www.tjrs.jus.br/site_php/noticias/mostranoticia.php?assunto=1&categoria=1&item=18708 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Gravataí Comarca Celebrates Solidarity Achievement |author=Rio Grande do Sul Court of Justice (TJRS) |date=7 January 2004 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> In 2010, the municipality had {{formatnum:173945}} voters, the seventh largest electorate in the state, including {{formatnum:2614}} illiterate voters and {{formatnum:2835}} voters under 18 years old.<ref name="Eleitores">{{cite web |url=http://www.onacional.com.br/noticias/politica/6012 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvEzxCjA?url=http://www.onacional.com.br/noticias/politica/6012 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Electorate Grew 8.5% in Four Years |author=O Nacional |date=20 July 2010 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

[[File:Chevrolet Celta 2013.jpg|thumb|right|The [[Chevrolet Celta]] is an [[economy car]] produced by [[General Motors]] in Gravataí.<ref name="CIAG_Sete anos"/>]]
Agriculture is the least significant sector of Gravataí’s economy. Of the city’s total GDP, {{formatnum:15279}} thousand [[Brazilian real|reais]] is the gross value added by agriculture.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2008" /> According to the IBGE, in 2009, the municipality had a livestock population of {{formatnum:18300}} [[cattle]], {{formatnum:2958}} [[equines]], {{formatnum:3928}} [[pigs]], {{formatnum:1311}} [[Bubalina|buffaloes]], 970 [[goats]], 17 [[donkeys]], 33 [[mules]], 588 [[sheep]], 490 [[rabbits]], and {{formatnum:38460}} [[poultry]], including {{formatnum:24250}} [[Chicken|hens]], {{formatnum:11760}} [[roosters]], broilers, and chicks, as well as {{formatnum:2450}} [[quails]].<ref name="Agropecuária" /> In 2009, the city produced {{formatnum:2990}} thousand liters of milk from {{formatnum:1470}} [[cows]]. It also produced 132,000 dozen chicken eggs, 58,000 dozen quail eggs, {{formatnum:1940}} kilograms of [[honey]], and 349 kilograms of [[wool]] from sheep. A total of 294 sheep were [[Sheep shearing|sheared]].<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2009&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvF4QH9k?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=prodpec2009&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Livestock 2009 |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> In temporary [[crop]]s, the main products are [[rice]] (5,940 tons), [[cassava]] (1,950 tons), and [[sugarcane]] (750 tons).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2009&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvF5Jqsn?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=lavtemp2009&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Temporary Crops 2009 |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

; Secondary sector
The industry is currently the most significant sector for the municipality’s economy. {{formatnum:2371233}} reais of the municipal GDP comes from the gross value added by the industrial sector (secondary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2008"/> A significant portion of these profits originates from the Gravataí Automotive Industrial Complex of [[General Motors]] Brazil, established on March 17, 1997, and inaugurated on July 20, 2000. Since then, the municipality has gained national prominence in the sector. The factory currently produces approximately 240,000 vehicles annually, accounting for 40% of GM’s total production in Brazil.<ref name="CIAG_Sete anos"/> The complex occupies a total area of 386 hectares, with 940,000 m² of built area in 2007, and 50 hectares dedicated to environmental preservation.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ciag.com.br/menus.php?menu=46 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvF9Segx?url=http://www.ciag.com.br/menus.php?menu=46 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Gravataí Automotive Industrial Complex |author=Gravataí Automotive Industrial Complex (CIAG) |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

; Tertiary sector
The service sector contributes {{formatnum:2002743}} reais to the municipal GDP.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2008"/> The tertiary sector is currently the second-largest contributor to Gravataí’s GDP. According to the IBGE, in 2008, the city had 7,489 local units, 7,290 companies and active commercial establishments, and {{formatnum:104358}} workers, with {{formatnum:56664}} total employed personnel and {{formatnum:47694}} salaried employees. [[Salary|Salaries]] and other remunerations totaled {{formatnum:899229}} reais, with an average monthly salary across the municipality of 3.6 [[minimum wage]]s.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFB7zZa?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=empresas&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Central Business Register Statistics |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

== Urban infrastructure ==
The municipality has robust infrastructure. In 2000, it had {{formatnum:67031}} [[housing unit]]s, including [[apartment]]s, [[house]]s, and rooms. Of these, {{formatnum:56444}} were owned properties, with {{formatnum:47645}} fully paid (71.08%), {{formatnum:8799}} under acquisition (13.13%), and {{formatnum:5405}} rented (8.06%). {{formatnum:4178}} properties were provided, with {{formatnum:1132}} by employers (1.69%) and {{formatnum:3046}} provided otherwise (4.54%). {{formatnum:1004}} were occupied in other ways (1.50%).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFDiCPu?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Infrastructure - Permanent Private Households and Residents |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CMN) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> The municipality provides treated water, electricity, sewage, urban cleaning, fixed telephony, and mobile telephony. In 2000, 73.73% of households were served by the general water supply network;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFF18BW?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_abastecimento.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Infrastructure - Water Supply |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CMN) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> 96.53% of households had garbage collected by cleaning services;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFGEOhH?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_lixo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Infrastructure - Waste Destination |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CMN) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> and 33.88% of residences had access to general sewage or stormwater networks.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFHS5DT?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_esgotamento.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Infrastructure - Sanitary Sewage |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CMN) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Healthcare ===
[[File:Hospital Dom João Becker 001.JPG|thumb|right|Dom João Becker Hospital]]
In 2009, the municipality had 71 health facilities, including hospitals, [[emergency department]]s, [[Community health center|health centers]], and [[dentistry]] services, with 37 private and 34 public facilities. These provided 233 beds for [[hospitalization]], all in private facilities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFJYgnd?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=assismed&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Health Services 2009 |language=Portuguese |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> As of April 2010, there were 2,539 healthcare professionals.<ref name="Datasus_SaúdeGravataí">{{cite web |url=http://tabnet.datasus.gov.br/tabdata/cadernos/RS/RS_Gravatai_Geral.xls |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFKlSdn?url=http://tabnet.datasus.gov.br/tabdata/cadernos/RS/RS_Gravatai_Geral.xls |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Health Information Notebook - General Information |author=DATASUS |format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]] |date=10 April 2010 |access-date=10 April 2011}}</ref> The city has only one general hospital, which is philanthropic. Gravataí belongs to the Porto Alegre CRS 01 Health Region. In 2009, there were 85,652 women of childbearing age (10 to 49 years).<ref name="Datasus_SaúdeGravataí"/> The Human Development Index (HDI) for longevity in Gravataí is 0.862,<ref name="PNUD_IDH"/> with a [[life expectancy]] of 73.59 years.<ref name="UFMS"/>

The Dom João Becker Hospital, managed by the Coração de Maria Nucleus, is the only facility in the city capable of performing complex medical procedures. It offers 189 beds through the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|Unified Health System]] (SUS) and provides emergency services, hospitalization, diagnostic facilities (laboratory tests, X-rays, ultrasound, CT scans, MRI, and elective surgeries), as well as on-call pediatric, clinical, surgical, obstetric, anesthesiology, and intensive care specialists operating in the [[Intensive care unit|Intensive Care Unit]] (ICU).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://cnes.datasus.gov.br/Exibe_Ficha_Estabelecimento.asp?VCo_Unidade=4309202232049 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFMP0Vn?url=http://cnes.datasus.gov.br/Exibe_Ficha_Estabelecimento.asp?VCo_Unidade=4309202232049 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Health Facility - Dom João Becker Hospital |author=DATASUS |date=10 March 2011 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref>

=== Education ===
[[File:Colégio Dom Feliciano.JPG|thumb|right|View of Colégio Dom Feliciano, a private school in the city]]
The municipality has schools in all its regions. The [[rural area]] population has easy access to schools in nearby urban neighborhoods due to the high urbanization rate. The average Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) among Gravataí’s public schools in 2009 was 4.1, close to the national average of 4.0 for municipal and state schools.<ref name="IDEB_Índice">{{cite web |url=http://sistemasideb.inep.gov.br/resultado/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFOHtvk?url=http://sistemasideb.inep.gov.br/resultado/ |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Basic Education Development Index (IDEB) - Results and Goals |author=Federal Government |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> In 2009, the municipality had approximately {{formatnum:51832}} enrollments, {{formatnum:2412}} teachers, and 192 schools in both public and private networks.<ref name="IBGE_Educação"/> The Human Development Index (HDI) for education was 0.636,<ref name="PNUD_IDH"/> compared to Brazil’s 0.849.<ref name="UFMS"/>

According to data from the [[Instituto Nacional de Estudos e Pesquisas Educacionais Anísio Teixeira|Anísio Teixeira National Institute for Educational Studies and Research]] (INEP) and the [[Ministry of Education (Brazil)|Ministry of Education]] (MEC), the illiteracy rate among individuals aged 18 to 24 in 2000 was 1.590%,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_analfabetismo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFRFHSV?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_analfabetismo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Education |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> while the adult literacy rate that year was 94.87% (compared to Brazil’s 84%).<ref name="Educacional_Analfabetismo">{{cite web |url=http://www.educacional.com.br/noticiacomentada/031212_not01_imprimir.asp?strTitulo=Afinal,%20quantos%20analfabetos%20existem%20no%20Brasil? |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yIHgy4UI?url=http://www.educacional.com.br/noticiacomentada/031212_not01_imprimir.asp?strTitulo=Afinal,%20quantos%20analfabetos%20existem%20no%20Brasil? |archive-date=29 April 2011 |title=After All, How Many Illiterates Are There in Brazil? |author=Educacional |date=3 December 2003 |access-date=28 April 2011}}</ref><ref name="UFMS"/> The gross school attendance rate in 2000 was 81.930%,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_frequencia.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yIHgy4UI?url=http://www.educacional.com.br/noticiacomentada/031212_not01_imprimir.asp?strTitulo=Afinal,%20quantos%20analfabetos%20existem%20no%20Brasil? |archive-date=29 April 2011 |title=Education - School Attendance |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> compared to the national rate of 81.89%.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.seplan.am.gov.br/arquivos/download/arqeditor/indicadores/perf_mun/Condensadov3/Conteudo/subregiao7/24-frequencia.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5yIHiACnu?url=http://www.seplan.am.gov.br/arquivos/download/arqeditor/indicadores/perf_mun/Condensadov3/Conteudo/subregiao7/24-frequencia.html |archive-date=29 April 2011 |title=Gross School Attendance Rate of the 7th Rio Negro - Solimões Subregion |author=Amazonas State Secretariat for Planning and Economic Development |date=2000 |access-date=28 April 2011}}</ref> A total of 4,124 inhabitants had less than one year of schooling or no formal education.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_ano_estudo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFTg58n?url=http://www.cnm.org.br/educacao/mu_edu_ano_estudo.asp?iIdMun=100143172 |archive-date=13 April 2011 |title=Education - Years of Study |author=National Confederation of Municipalities (CNM) |date=2000 |access-date=13 April 2011}}</ref> In 2010, 539 students attended special education programs, and 223 children were enrolled in daycare centers. === Security and crime ===
As in most medium and large Brazilian municipalities, crime is a challenge in Gravataí. In 2008, the [[homicide]] rate was 23.3 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 27th in the state and 729th nationally.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/HTN.xls |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5wqdf8OBT?url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/HTN.xls |archive-date=1 March 2011 |title=Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Homicides in Municipalities with 10,000 or More Inhabitants |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=13 April 2011 |format=xls}}</ref> The [[suicide]] rate that year was 4.1 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 154th in the state and 1450th nationally.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5wqdgmkkY?url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls |archive-date=1 March 2011 |title=Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Suicides in Municipalities with 10,000 or More Inhabitants |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=13 April 2011 |format=xls}}</ref> The traffic accident mortality rate was 7.5 per 100,000 inhabitants, placing it 142nd in the state and 2296th nationally.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5wqdiQILu?url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls |archive-date=1 March 2011 |title=Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Traffic Accident Deaths in Municipalities with 10,000 or More Inhabitants |author=Sangari |date=2011 |access-date=13 April 2011 |format=xls}}</ref>

Until the early 2000s, crime rates in Gravataí were significantly lower than those in other cities of the [[Greater Porto Alegre|Greater Porto Alegre Metropolitan Area]] (RMPA).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/vereadoresnotcompleta.asp?sId=361 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFibRRb?url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/vereadoresnotcompleta.asp?sId=361 |archive-date=April 13, 2011 |title=Segurança Pública |publisher=Câmara Municipal |date=June 8, 2005 |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Public Safety |language=pt}}</ref> After the mid-2000s, the city surpassed the threshold of 10 homicides per 100,000 inhabitants, which, according to the [[World Health Organization|WHO]], marks the onset of endemic violence.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://noticias.terra.com.br/mundo/america-latina/onu-america-latina-e-unica-regiao-do-mundo-com-aumento-de-homicidios,3939edf6cad42410VgnCLD2000000ec6eb0aRCRD.html |title=ONU: América Latina é única região do mundo com aumento de homicídios |access-date=May 6, 2014 |trans-title=UN: Latin America is the Only Region in the World with an Increase in Homicides |language=pt}}</ref> However, despite the rise in violence, Gravataí's homicide rate remains lower than that of Porto Alegre and the other three largest cities in the RMPA; in 2015, the rate was 28.9 homicides per 100,000 inhabitants in Gravataí compared to 40.3 in the capital.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.ssp.rs.gov.br/upload/20160208175424indicadores_por_municipio_e_por_mes___2015___site.xls |title=Indicadores Criminais - SSP - Por Município - 2015 |access-date=June 10, 2016 |trans-title=Criminal Indicators - SSP - By Municipality - 2015 |language=pt |archive-date=August 4, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160804132236/http://www.ssp.rs.gov.br/upload/20160208175424indicadores_por_municipio_e_por_mes___2015___site.xls |url-status=dead }}</ref>

== Services and communications ==

Water supply in the city is managed by the Companhia Riograndense de Saneamento (CORSAN), which serves 98% of the urban area, although only 21.9% of households have access to sewage collection and treatment services.<ref name="ClicRBS_Eleicoes2008"/> The Rio Grande Energia (RGE) is responsible for the city's electricity supply, with public lighting available throughout the urban area and 90% of households (including those in rural areas).<ref name="ClicRBS_Eleicoes2008"/> The city also has access to dial-up and [[broadband]] ([[Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line|ADSL]]) internet services provided by various free and paid [[Internet service provider|internet service providers]]. Mobile phone services are offered by multiple operators. Gravataí's area code ([[Direct distance dialing|DDD]]) is 051,<ref name="Guiatel_DDD">{{Cite web|url=http://www.guiatel.com.br/info_rs1.htm |title=Rio Grande do Sul |publisher=Guiatel |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Rio Grande do Sul |language=pt}}</ref> and its [[Postal code|Postal Code]] (CEP) ranges from 94000-001 to 94329-999.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.buscacep.correios.com.br/ |title=Busca CEP |publisher=[[Empresa Brasileira de Correios e Telégrafos|Correios]] |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Postal Code Search |language=pt}}</ref> On January 8, 2009, the municipality began offering [[Local number portability|<span title="Portability">number portability</span>]], along with other cities sharing the same DDD.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/geral/09/01/2009/ddd-51-lidera-pedidos-de-troca |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFpP3fD?url=http://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/geral/09/01/2009/ddd-51-lidera-pedidos-de-troca |url-status=dead |archive-date=April 13, 2011 |title=DDD 51 lidera pedidos de troca |author=Maurício Renner |date=January 9, 2009 |publisher=Baguete |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=DDD 51 Leads Number Portability Requests |language=pt}} [http://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/geral/09/01/2009/ddd-51-lidera-pedidos-de-troca Alt URL]</ref>

Since November 5, 2009, the city has had access to digital television signals, received through [[RBS TV]] (Rede Brasil Sul de Televisão).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.dicasparacomputador.com/tv-digital-no-rio-grande-do-sul-no-dia-5-de-novembro-tudo-sobre-a-tv-digital |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvFrYdZp?url=http://www.dicasparacomputador.com/tv-digital-no-rio-grande-do-sul-no-dia-5-de-novembro-tudo-sobre-a-tv-digital |archive-date=April 13, 2011 |title=TV Digital no Rio Grande do Sul no dia 5 de Novembro - == Transportation ==

In 2009, the municipal vehicle fleet consisted of {{formatnum:86375}} vehicles, including {{formatnum:62162}} cars, {{formatnum:13859}} motorcycles, {{formatnum:3823}} pickup trucks, {{formatnum:2345}} mopeds, {{formatnum:2774}} trucks, 422 minibuses, 489 buses, 376 tractor-trucks, and 126 wheeled tractors.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=frota&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvULjGQt?url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/xtras/csv.php?tabela=frota&codmun=430920&nomemun=Gravata%ED |archive-date=April 14, 2011 |title=Frota 2009 |publisher=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2009 |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=2009 Fleet |language=pt}}</ref> The city's duplicated and paved avenues, along with numerous [[traffic light]]s, facilitate traffic flow, but the rapid increase in the number of vehicles during the first decade of the 21st century has led to increasingly slow traffic, particularly in the municipal seat. Additionally, finding parking spaces in the city's commercial center has become challenging, causing some losses to local commerce.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://zerohora.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1§ion=Geral&newsID=a3198579.xml |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvUMuMXS?url=http://zerohora.clicrbs.com.br/zerohora/jsp/default.jsp?uf=1&local=1&section=Geral&newsID=a3198579.xml |archive-date=April 14, 2011 |title=Acompanhe o movimento das rodovias |publisher=ClicRBS |date=February 4, 2011 |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Monitor Highway Traffic |language=pt}}</ref> The city also has a [[public transportation]] system managed by the Municipal Secretariat of Traffic and Transportation of Gravataí (SMTT).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ebandreporter.com.br/cidades/00005622-mudancas_no_transporte_publico_de_gravatai.html |title=Mudanças no Transporte Público de Gravataí |author=Kleriton Vargas |date=February 22, 2011 |publisher=eBand Reporter |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Changes in Gravataí's Public Transportation |language=pt}}</ref> This municipal agency is responsible for regulating the city's transportation and traffic systems, managing public transportation, overseeing traffic flow, and, through its traffic agents, issuing citations to drivers who commit traffic violations.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/secretarias/transito/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvUSVkyM?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/secretarias/transito/ |archive-date=April 14, 2011 |title=SMTT - Secretaria Municipal de Trânsito Transporte |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=SMTT - Municipal Secretariat of Transit and Transportation |language=pt}}</ref>

Due to the scarcity of rivers, Gravataí has little tradition of waterway transportation. The city has no airports within its territory, but the [[Salgado Filho Porto Alegre International Airport|Salgado Filho International Airport]] in [[Porto Alegre]] is approximately 20 km from the city center. Gravataí also has a [[bus terminal]] located in Vera Cruz.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ibiubi.com.br/entretenimento/esta%C3%A7%C3%A3o-rodovi%C3%A1ria+rodovi%C3%A1rias+av-gravata%C3%AD+vera-cruz+gravatai/IUID159450/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xvUTooXM?url=http://www.ibiubi.com.br/entretenimento/esta%C3%A7%C3%A3o-rodovi%C3%A1ria+rodovi%C3%A1rias+av-gravata%C3%AD+vera-cruz+gravatai/IUID159450/ |archive-date=April 14, 2011 |title=Estação Rodoviária |publisher=Ibiubi |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Bus Terminal |language=pt}}</ref> The city is served by the RS-118 (connecting to [[Sapucaia do Sul]] and the Itapuã district in Viamão), the RS-020 (to [[São José dos Ausentes]]), and the RS-030 (to [[Tramandaí]]). The [[BR-290 (Brazil highway)|BR-290]] (Osvaldo Aranha Highway) is the primary link between Gravataí, the Porto Alegre Metropolitan Area, and the state coast in [[Osório, Rio Grande do Sul|Osório]]; the section connecting the metropolitan area to the coast is known as the ''Freeway''. It also connects the RMPA to [[Uruguaiana]], on the border with [[Argentina]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://maps.google.com.br/ |title=Google Maps |access-date=April 13, 2011 |trans-title=Google Maps |language=pt}}</ref>

== Culture ==

The Gravataí Municipal Secretariat of Culture, Sports, and Leisure (SMCEL) oversees the city's cultural sector, aiming to plan and implement cultural policies through programs, projects, and activities that promote cultural development. As part of the municipality's indirect public administration, it enjoys administrative and financial autonomy, supported by budgetary allocations, its own assets, revenue application, and the ability to sign contracts and agreements with other institutions.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/secretarias/esportes/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xwwSN8lW?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/secretarias/esportes/ |archive-date=April 14, 2011 |title=SME - Secretaria Municipal de Esporte, Recreação e Lazer |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=April 14, 2011 |trans-title=SME - Municipal Secretariat of Sports, Recreation, and Leisure |language=pt}}</ref>

=== Tourism and events ===

Gravataí boasts several tourist attractions, including the Nossa Senhora dos Anjos Matrix Church, whose construction began in 1772 and was built in the Portuguese [[Baroque]] style; the Dom Feliciano College, the municipality's first private school, currently maintained by the Sisters of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, known for its architecture featuring an arch connecting the school buildings over José Loureiro da Silva Avenue; CTG Aldeia dos Anjos, which has won the ENART championship 10 times; the City Hall Building, the city hall's headquarters, constructed in an [[Eclecticism|eclectic style]] with [[Neoclassicism|neoclassical]] and [[Modernism|modern]] characteristics, acquired in 1894 for the municipal administration and renovated in 1996; the Santa Cruz Chapel, built by indigenous people to house a [[cross]] and serve as a place of worship and prayer for the early Gravataí settlers, which was in ruins by 1909 but rebuilt with community donations and completed in 1944; the Agostinho Martha Municipal Museum, whose collection tells the colonial history of the Gravataí River Valley, highlighting a sugarcane mill, a handloom, and the entire artisanal weaving complex, as well as a room with furniture from the [[Azores]], and housing the Municipal Historical Archive; the Fonseca Manor, known since 1877, featuring characteristics of [[Portuguese architecture|Portuguese colonial architecture]] brought by Azorean settlers; and the Bina Manor, built in the Portuguese style in 1882, part of a 40-hectare rural property called Sítio do Sobrado, whose basement once housed a slave camp with a significant number of enslaved people. This site now serves as the headquarters of the Municipal Environmental Foundation.<ref name="FeriasTur_Gravataí"/>

To promote local socioeconomic development, the Gravataí municipal government, sometimes in partnership with local companies, invests in festivals and events. These events often attract visitors from other cities, requiring improved municipal infrastructure and encouraging the professionalization of the sector, which benefits not only tourists but also the entire local population. Activities take place throughout the year.<ref name="Prefeitura_Eventos2011"/> The city's Official Events Calendar, published annually by the municipal government, includes: the Feast of [[Our Lady of Navigators]], held on February 2; the Gravataí Carnival, in February or March; the Passion of Christ, in March or April; the Bromeliad Festival, in April; [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]] celebrations, in May or June; the Homeland Week and Civic Parade, in September; the Farroupilha Week, in September; and the Black Consciousness Week in the Municipality, in November.<ref name="Prefeitura_Eventos2011">{{Cite web|url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/eventos-oficiais.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6kDJFY?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/eventos-oficiais.php |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Calendário de Eventos Oficiais 2011 |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=2011 Official Events Calendar |language=pt}}</ref>

=== Sports ===

As in much of Brazil, the most popular sport in Gravataí is [[Association football|football]]. The city is home to several clubs, including the Clube Esportivo Alvi-Rubro, the oldest in the municipality, founded on May 7, 1933;<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/noticiascompleta.asp?sId=670 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6lLowl?url=http://www.cmgravatai.rs.gov.br/noticiascompleta.asp?sId=670 |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Sessão Solene marca 75º aniversário do Alvi-Rubro |publisher=Câmara Municipal |date=May 15, 2008 |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Solemn Session Marks the 75th Anniversary of Alvi-Rubro |language=pt}}</ref> the América Futebol Clube, established in 1950;<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/noticias.php?id=4689 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6op2Yi?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/noticias.php?id=4689 |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Prefeito renova convênio para realização do Torneio Anual do América |publisher=Prefeitura |date=April 4, 2006 |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Mayor Renews Agreement for the Annual América Tournament |language=pt}}</ref> and the [[Cerâmica Atlético Clube]], founded on April 19, 1950. The municipality's main football stadium is the Antônio Vieira Ramos Stadium, inaugurated on March 2, 2008, and home to the Cerâmica Atlético Clube.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ceramicaatleticoclube.com.br/site/estrutura_estadio.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6sUZEm?url=http://www.ceramicaatleticoclube.com.br/site/estrutura_estadio.php |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Estádio |publisher=Cerâmica Atlético Clube |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Stadium |language=pt}}</ref>

In addition to the Gravataí City Football Championship, a significant municipal football competition, the city organizes several other annual sporting events involving various disciplines. These include: the Municipal Stage of the Gaucho Summer Sports Circuit, in February; the Gravataí School Games, in March; the Gravataí International Cycling Tour, in April; the Hachiman Karate Cup and the Aldeião Municipal Futsal Cup, in May; the Taekwondo Tournament, the Gravataí Table Tennis Cup, and the South Gravataí Canoeing Cup, in June; the Municipal Women's Futsal Championship, in July; the Municipal Men's/Women's Volleyball Championship, in September; and the Bromeliad City Mountain Bike Event, in October.<ref name="Prefeitura_Eventos2011"/>

=== Holidays ===

Gravataí observes two municipal holidays, eight national holidays, and three optional holidays. The municipal holidays are [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], celebrated on the Thursday following the Sunday of the [[Trinity|Holy Trinity]], and the day of [[Titles of Mary, mother of Jesus|Our Lady of the Angels]], the city's patron saint, on August 2.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/feriados.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5xz6u5JAS?url=http://www.gravatai.rs.gov.br/site/cidade/feriados.php |archive-date=April 16, 2011 |title=Feriados Oficiais |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Official Holidays |language=pt}}</ref> According to Federal Law No. 9,093, enacted on September 12, 1995, municipalities may have up to four municipal holidays, including [[Good Friday]].<ref name="GuiaTrabalhistaCarnaval">{{Cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/5wv0awGzr?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-date=March 3, 2011 |title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=April 15, 2011 |trans-title=Carnival - Is It a Holiday?
