'''Atima''' is a [[Municipality (Honduras)|municipality]] in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]], Honduras. It covers an area of {{cvt|203.4|km2}} and had a population of 17,648 inhabitants according to the 2013 national census. It was officially established on 18 September 1887.

== History ==
During the 1801 population census, Atima was part of the town of Tencoa. It was later known as the village of San José de Atima, and was part of Viejo Celilac. It was officially established as a municipality on 18 September 1887, and was part of the District of Hills in the Honduran Territorial Political Division of 1896. "Atima" is derived from Mexican usage, and means "place where water is drunk".<ref name="Edu">{{cite web|title=Municipio de Atima, Santa Bárbara|url=https://edu-honduras.info/Los-Departamentos/16-Santa-Barbara/Atima.html|work=Edu-Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026|lang=es}}</ref>

== Geography ==
Atima is located in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] in Honduras.<ref name="Red">{{cite web|url=https://redhonduras.hn/en/geography/municipality-atima/|title=Municipality of Atima|work=Red Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/|title=Administrative divisions of Honduras|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[San Luis, Santa Bárbara|San Luis]] to the north, [[La Unión, Lempira|La Unión]] to the south, [[Nuevo Celilac]] and [[San Nicolás, Santa Bárbara|San Nicolás]] to the east, and [[La Unión, Honduras|La Unión]], and [[Naranjito, Santa Bárbara|Naranjito]] and [[Lepaera]] to the west. The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|203.4|km2}}.<ref name="Edu"/> It is located on the banks of the San José River.<ref name="Red"/> The San Juan de Atima River runs to the west of the municipality, and the Caracoles Mountain also lies to the west.<ref name="Edu"/>

== Demographics ==
At per the 2013 census, Atima had a total population of 17,648 inhabitants, of whom 8,971 (50.8%) were male and 8,677 (49.2%) were female, with a masculinity index of 103.4 men per 100 women. Of the total population, 2,135 (12.1%) lived in the urban area and 15,513 (87.9%) in the rural areas. The intercensal population growth rate between 2001 and 2013 was 3.1% per year, the second highest in the department.
<ref name="CP1">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/santa_b%C3%A1rbara/1603__atima/|title=Atima|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19, 20}}

About 7.13% of the population were classified as [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and rest as non-indigneous.<ref name="CP1"/> By broad age group, 39.2% of the population were aged 0–14, the fifth highest proportion in the department, 56.5% were aged 15–64, and 4.3% were aged 65 and over, the lowest proportion in the department. The median age was 19.5 years and the mean age was 23.9 years. Among the population aged 15 and over (10,735 persons), the 2013 census recorded an illiteracy rate of 23.6%, slightly higher than the departmental average of 22.2%.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}} The municipality had 3,761 occupied private dwellings, with an average household size of 4.6 persons, the third highest in the department.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=23, 27}}

In 2021, two workers were stabbed fatally in the village of Berlín.<ref>{{cite web|title=Dos muertos y varios heridos tras ataque en Atima, Santa Bárbara|url=https://www.elheraldo.hn/sucesos/1456298-466/matan-dos-hombres-atima-santa-barbara-ataque|access-date=4 February 2022|work=Diario El Heraldo|language=es-HN}}</ref>

==Economy==
The municipality is the largest producer of coffee in the department with its terrain and climate favoring its cultivation.<ref name="Red"/>
