'''São Gonçalo do Amarante''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the [[Greater Natal|Metropolitan Region of Natal]], in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Norte]], in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast Region]] of Brazil. It spans a territorial area of approximately 249.800 [[Square kilometre|km<sup>2</sup>]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/cidades-e-estados/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante.html |title=Brasil / Rio Grande do Norte / São Gonçalo do Amarante |access-date=2021-01-09}}</ref> It is the fourth most populous municipality in the state, following [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]], [[Mossoró]], and [[Parnamirim]], with a population of 124,495 inhabitants in 2025.<ref name="IBGE" />

The municipality is known for being the site of one of the most significant events in the [[history of Rio Grande do Norte]] and Brazilian [[Catholicism]], when Dutch forces massacred eighty individuals in an event known as the Uruaçu Massacre,<ref>{{cite web |last=RN |first=Do G |date=2013-10-02 |title=Conheça a história dos Mártires de Cunhaú e Uruaçu, no RN |url=http://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2013/10/conheca-historia-dos-martires-de-cunhau-e-uruacu-no-rn.html |access-date=2019-01-29 |work=Rio Grande do Norte |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Moura |first=Renata |url=https://www.bbc.com/portuguese/brasil-41568388 |title=O massacre holandês há 372 anos que levou o papa Francisco a decretar a santidade de 30 brasileiros |date=2017-10-14 |publisher=BBC News Brasil |language=en-GB}}</ref> which occurred in 1645. In 2017, these martyrs were canonized as [[saint]]s in [[Saint Peter's Square]], in a ceremony presided over by [[Pope Francis]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Mártires de Cunhaú e Uruaçu são declarados santos |url=https://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/martires-de-cunhau-e-uruacu-sao-declarados-santos.ghtml |work=G1 |date=15 October 2017 |access-date=2019-01-29 |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Mártires de Cunhaú e Uruaçu são proclamados santos pelo Papa Francisco |url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/ma-rtires-de-cunhaao-e-uruaa-u-sa-o-proclamados-santos-pelo-papa-francisco/394739 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190130000259/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/ma-rtires-de-cunhaao-e-uruaa-u-sa-o-proclamados-santos-pelo-papa-francisco/394739 |archive-date=2019-01-30 |access-date=2019-01-29 |work=Tribuna do Norte |language=pt-br}}</ref>

Throughout its history, São Gonçalo do Amarante lost its autonomy several times until achieving definitive emancipation in 1958, when it separated from [[Macaíba]]. Since 2014, it has been home to the [[Greater Natal International Airport|Governador Aluízio Alves International Airport]], a Brazilian [[Airport|airport complex]], which was the first Brazilian airport to be [[Privatization|privatized]]<ref>{{cite web |title=EBC |url=http://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil/noticia/2011-11-25/governo-assinara-contrato-de-concessao-do-primeiro-aeroporto-privatizado-do-pais |work=memoria.ebc.com.br |access-date=2019-01-29 |language=pt-br}}</ref> and serves the cargo and passenger transport demands of the Natal Metropolitan Region.

==History==
[[File:Victor Meirelles02.jpg|thumb|left|150px|Portrait of Felipe Camarão, by [[Victor Meirelles|Victor Meirelles de Lima]].]]

The territory that now constitutes São Gonçalo do Amarante was originally inhabited by the [[Potiguara|Potiguara Indians]], among whom ''Poti'', known as [[Filipe Camarão]], stood out, originating from a community in [[Extremoz]].<ref name="História_Prefeitura">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/saogoncalo.php#localizacao |title=História de São Gonçalo do Amarante |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=2013-05-14 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GbrhjupF |archive-date=2013-05-14}}</ref><ref name="História_Câmara">{{cite web |url=http://cmsga.rn.gov.br/portal2011/a-camara/a-cidade.html |title=Município de São Gonçalo do Amarante |publisher=Câmara Municipal |access-date=2013-05-14 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GbroPfp8 |archive-date=2013-05-14}}</ref><ref name="História_IBGE">{{cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/riograndedonorte/saogoncalodoamarante.pdf |title=História |publisher=IBGE |access-date=2013-05-14 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6Gmik4Skr |archive-date=2013-05-21}}</ref>

The municipality's name derives from [[Gundisalvus of Amarante]], a Portuguese ecclesiastic and the patron saint of the city of [[Amarante, Portugal|Amarante]] in [[Portugal]].<ref>{{cite web |title=S. Gonçalo de Amarante |url=https://www.cm-amarante.pt/pt/s-goncalo-de-amarante |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160603104243/https://www.cm-amarante.pt/pt/s-goncalo-de-amarante |archive-date=2016-06-03 |access-date=2025-07-02}}</ref> São Gonçalo do Amarante saw its first settlers in the 17th century,<ref name="História_IBGE"/> with the family of Estevão Machado de Miranda, who were soon massacred by the Dutch in the massacre of Cunhaú and Uruaçu in 1645. It was not until 1689 that expeditions from [[Pernambuco]] led to the repopulation of the area after the expulsion of the invaders.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/><ref name="História_Câmara"/><ref name="História_IBGE"/>

In the 18th century, in 1710, near the Potengi Mill, Paschoal Gomes de Lima and Ambrósio Miguel Sirinhaém, both Portuguese natives, settled with their families along the banks of the [[Potenji River|<span title="Potenji River">eponymous river</span>]]. After settling, they built their residences and constructed a small church dedicated to the saint Gundisalvus of Amarante. The municipality's name comes from the stone statue of the saint that was placed on the altar.<ref name="História_IBGE"/>

On 11 April 1833, during the administration of Manoel Lobo de Miranda Henrique, the municipality of São Gonçalo do Amarante was established. In 1856, under the governance of Antônio Bernardes de Passos, the municipality was struck by a [[cholera]] epidemic that claimed 171 lives. In 1868, through a provincial law sanctioned by Governor Gustavo Augusto de Sá, the municipality lost its autonomy and was annexed to the municipality of [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]], the provincial capital. Six years later, the village was detached and restored to municipal status.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/><ref name="História_Câmara"/>

However, in 1879, the population of São Gonçalo do Amarante suffered a setback when the municipal government seat was transferred to the village of [[Macaíba]], previously known as ''Cuité''. In 1890, shortly after the [[Proclamation of the Republic (Brazil)|proclamation of the republic]], José Inácio Fernandes Barros, the vice-president of Rio Grande do Norte, elevated the village of São Gonçalo do Amarante, then part of Macaíba, to municipal status.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/><ref name="História_Câmara"/>

With State Decree-Law No. 268 of 1943, São Gonçalo do Amarante once again lost its political autonomy, reverting to a district of Macaíba under the name Felipe Camarão, and losing part of its territory, which formed the current municipality of [[São Paulo do Potengi]]. It was only with the enactment of State Law No. 2324 on 11 December 1958, that the district definitively gained political emancipation, and its name was changed from ''Felipe Camarão'' back to ''São Gonçalo do Amarante''.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/><ref name="História_Câmara"/>

==Geography==
According to the division established by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) in 2017, São Gonçalo do Amarante belongs to the intermediate and immediate geographic regions of Natal.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Divisão Regional do Brasil |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=2019-03-29 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170919225405/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=2017-09-19}}</ref> Previously, under the division into [[Microregions of Brazil|microregions]] and [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregions]], it was part of the Macaíba microregion, which was included in the East Potiguar mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web |url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=Divisão Territorial Brasileira 2016 |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=2019-03-29}}</ref>

Part of the [[Greater Natal|Metropolitan Region of Natal]] (RMN)<ref>{{cite web |date=1997 |title=Lei Complementar nº 152 de 16 de janeiro de 1997 |url=https://leisestaduais.com.br/rn/lei-complementar-n-152-1997-rio-grande-do-norte-institui-a-regiao-metropolitana-de-natal-e-da-outras-providencias-2021-07-15-versao-compilada?origin=instituicao#:~:text=Complementar%20682%2F2021-,LEI%20COMPLEMENTAR%20N%C2%BA%20152%2C%20DE%2016%20DE%20JANEIRO%20DE%201997,Natal%20e%20d%C3%A1%20outras%20provid%C3%AAncias. |access-date=2013-05-14}}</ref> and the Polo Costa das Dunas,<ref>{{cite web |date=2005-04-14 |title=DECRETO Nº 18.186, DE 14 DE ABRIL DE 2005 |url=https://www.rcambiental.com.br/ver-ato/DEC-RN-18186-2005/01937d9d-509d-7bd3-90a2-ab18e549610b |access-date=2013-05-17}}</ref> São Gonçalo do Amarante is located seventeen kilometers from the center of [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]], the state capital, with which it borders to the east.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.distanciasentrecidades.com/distancia-sao-goncalo-do-amarante-rio-grande-do-norte-a-natal |title=Distância de São Gonçalo do Amarante a Natal |access-date=2022-02-10}}</ref> Its other boundaries are [[Ceará-Mirim]] and [[Extremoz]] to the north, [[Macaíba]] to the south, and [[Ielmo Marinho]] to the west.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante">{{cite web |url=http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/idema/DOC/DOC000000000016674.PDF |title=Perfil do seu município: São Gonçalo do Amarante |author=Instituto de Desenvolvimento Econômico e Meio Ambiente do Rio Grande do Norte (IDEMA-RN) |date=2013 |access-date=2022-02-10 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20220210215738/http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/idema/DOC/DOC000000000016674.PDF |archive-date=2022-02-10}}</ref> Its territorial area spans {{convert|249.8|km2|abbr=on}}<ref name="IBGE_Área"/> (0.473% of the state's surface), of which {{convert|31.986|km2||abbr=on}} constitute the urban area (2019).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/Tabela/8418#/k/-497573414/resultado |title=Tabela 8418 - Áreas urbanizadas, Loteamento vazio, Área total mapeada e Subcategorias |author=[[Instituto Brasileiro de [[File:SGA (RN) e municípios limítrofes.svg|300px|center]]

The municipality is predominantly flat, consisting of low-lying areas with yellow and/or red clay soils. Its formation includes coastal sediments near the floodplains of the Potengi River and the terraces of the [[Barreiras Formation|Barreiras Group]].<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante"/> [[Latosol]] is the dominant soil type, with smaller areas of [[quartz sand]] to the east and, to the southwest, [[planosol]] and red-yellow [[podzol]] (now classified as [[luvisol]]). Along the banks of the [[Potenji River|Potengi River]], both alluvial and mangrove soils are found,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.uep.cnps.embrapa.br/solos/rn/saogoncalodoamarante.pdf |title=Mapa Exploratório-Reconhecimento de solos do município de São Gonçalo do Amarante, RN |author=[[Empresa Brasileira de Pesquisa Agropecuária]] (EMBRAPA) |access-date=2022-02-10 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20220210215334/http://www.uep.cnps.embrapa.br/solos/rn/saogoncalodoamarante.pdf |archive-date=2022-02-10}}</ref> covered by floodplain forests and [[mangrove]]s, respectively, both part of the [[Atlantic Forest]] biome, which also includes subperennial and deciduous forests and coastal tablelands.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante"/> However, 94% of the municipality's territory lies within the [[Caatinga]] biome, while the remaining 6% lies within the Atlantic Forest,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://infosanbas.org.br/municipio/sao-goncalo-do-amarante-rn/ |title=São Gonçalo do Amarante, RN |access-date=2022-02-10}}</ref> creating an [[ecotone]] between the two biomes.

The predominant [[drainage basin]] covers 82.65% of the municipality's territory and is that of the Potengi River, with the remaining 17.35% in the Doce River basin. The main river crossing the municipality is the [[Potenji River|Potengi River]], which originates in [[Cerro Corá, Rio Grande do Norte|Cerro Corá]] and has a course of 176 kilometers, flowing into the [[Atlantic Ocean]] in Natal. Other significant rivers include Camaragibe and Prata. The local [[hydrography]] is also marked by the Bela Vista Lagoon and the Guajirus, Onça, Santo Antônio, Serrinha, and Tapará streams.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/><ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante"/>

The [[climate]] is [[Tropical savanna climate|tropical wet and dry]]<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante"/> ([[Köppen climate classification|''Aw'']]), with [[rainfall]] concentrated between March and July. According to data from the Rio Grande do Norte Agricultural Research Company (EMPARN), since 1992, the highest 24-hour rainfall recorded in São Gonçalo do Amarante was {{convert|206|mm|in}} on 30 July 1998.<ref name="Relatório pluviométrico"/> Since October 2018, when EMPARN installed an [[automatic weather station]] in the city, the lowest [[temperature]] was recorded on 25 August 2020 ({{convert|18.4|°C|°F}}), and the highest on 12 January 2019 ({{convert|35|°C|°F}}).<ref name="Relatório de variáveis meteorológicas"/>

The [[Population (human biology)|population]] of São Gonçalo do Amarante grew by 32.1% between the [[2010 Brazilian Census|2010]] and [[2022 Brazilian Census|2022]] censuses, at a geometric growth rate of 2.32% per year,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122278 |title=População residente / Taxa de crescimento geométrico (Unit: %) |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> increasing from {{formatnum:87668}} people in 2010<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/23/27652?detalhes=true |title=Censo 2010: sinopse |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> to {{formatnum:115838}} people in 2022,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122232 |title=População residente (Unit: people) |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> with a [[population density]] of {{convert|463.72|PD/km2|PD/sqmi}}.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/10102/122229?tipo=ranking&indicador=122231 |title=Território / Densidade demográfica (Unit: inhabitants per square kilometer) |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> The average household size in the last census was 2.9 residents per household.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/4712#/n6/2412005/v/5930/p/all/d/v5930%202/l/v,p,t/resultado |title=Tabela 4712 - Domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados, Moradores em domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados e Média de moradores em domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> With 51.44% of inhabitants being female and 48.56% male, the [[sex ratio]] was 94.4 ({{formatnum:9440}} men per ten thousand women).<ref name="panorama">{{cite web |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/indicadores.html?localidade=2412005&tema=1 |title=Tema: população |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> In terms of [[Race and ethnicity in Brazil|race or ethnicity]], 53% were [[Pardo Brazilians|mixed race]], 34.06% [[White Brazilians|white]], 12.44% [[Black Brazilians|black]], 0.35% [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous]], and 0.16% [[Asian Brazilians|Asian]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9605#/n6/2412005/v/all/p/last%201/c86/all/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t/resultado |title=Tabela 9605 - População residente, por cor ou raça, nos Censos Demográficos |author=IBGE |access-date=2024-09-13}}</ref> The median age of the population was 32 years, ranging from 30 years for whites and indigenous people to 37 for blacks.<ref name="panorama"/>

According to the 2010 census, the last to release data on religion, 70.44% of inhabitants were [[Catholic Church in Brazil|Catholics]], 20.2% [[Protestantism|evangelicals]], and 7.84% [[Irreligion in Brazil|had no religion]], while other denominations accounted for 1.52%.<ref name="SIDRA-Religião">{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/2094#/n6/2407104/v/all/p/last%201/c86/0/c133/all/d/v93%200,v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t+c133/resultado |title=Tabela 2094 - População residente por cor ou raça e religião |author=IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=2021-07-29}}</ref> Within the [[Catholic Church]], the municipality belongs to the [[Archdiocese of Natal]] and has four parishes: São Gonçalo, Santo Antônio, São Lucas, and Santo Expedito.<ref>{{cite web |title=Guia de Paróquias |url=https://www.arquidiocesedenatal.org.br/c%C3%B3pia-vicariato-urbano |access-date=2022-02-10}}</ref> São Gonçalo do Amarante also is home to various Protestant or reformed denominations, with the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assemblies of God]] and the [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]] being the main ones.<ref name="SIDRA-Religião"/>

The municipality's [[Human Development Index]] is considered to be medium, according to data from the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP). According to the 2010 report, released in 2013, its value was 0.661, ranking it 16th in the state and 2870th nationally. Considering only the longevity index, its value is 0.829, the income index is 0.619, and the education index is 0.564. In 2010, 78.75% of the population lived above the [[poverty line]], 12.72% between the indigence and poverty lines, and 8.53% below the indigence line. In the same year, the richest 20% held 48.63% of the municipal income, while the poorest 20% held only 3.8%, with the [[Gini coefficient|Gini index]], which measures [[social inequality]], at 0.46.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/9/erradicacao-da-pobreza/BRA002024130/sao-goncalo-do-amarante---rn |title=ODS 01 Erradicação da pobreza |access-date=2022-02-10}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=ODS 10 Redução de desigualdades |url=https://brasil.un.org/pt-br/sdgs/10 |access-date=2022-02-10}}</ref>

==Politics==
According to the [[organic law]] of São Gonçalo do Amarante, enacted on 3 April 1990, and amended subsequently, municipal administration is conducted by two independent and harmonious branches: the [[Executive (government)|executive]] and the [[Legislature|legislative]]. The executive is led by the [[mayor]] and their cabinet of secretaries. The legislative is exercised by the [[Câmara municipal|Municipal Chamber]], composed of seventeen councilors. The chamber's headquarters, established on 1 April 1960, is the Poti Cavalcanti Palace, formerly the public jail.<ref name="História_CMSGARN">{{cite web |url=http://cmsga.rn.gov.br/portal2011/a-camara/a-historia.html |title=História da Câmara de São Gonçalo do Amarante |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdPN0tRA |archive-date=2013-05-15 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Complementing the legislative process and the work of the secretaries, several municipal councils are active, including those for School Feeding, Social Assistance, Children's and Adolescents’ Rights, Education, Women, and Health.<ref name="São Gonçalo do Amarante" /> São Gonçalo do Amarante is home to a district of the state judiciary, located at the Desembargador Ivan Meira Lima Forum.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.mprn.mp.br/portal/inicio/servicos/legislacao/leis-complementares/18-portal-legislacao-leis-complementares-1999/130-lc-no-165-de-28-de-abril-de-1999/file |title=LEI COMPLEMENTAR Nº 165, DE 28 DE ABRIL DE 1999 |publisher=Ministério Público do Rio Grande do Norte |access-date=2015-07-09 |archive-date=8 July 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150708223306/http://www.mprn.mp.br/portal/inicio/servicos/legislacao/leis-complementares/18-portal-legislacao-leis-complementares-1999/130-lc-no-165-de-28-de-abril-de-1999/file |url-status=dead }}</ref> According to the [[Superior Electoral Court]], São Gonçalo do Amarante belongs to the 51st Electoral Zone of Rio Grande do Norte<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.tse.jus.br/eleitor/estatisticas-de-eleitorado/consulta-por-municipio-zona |title=Eleitor / Estatísticas de eleitorado / Consulta por Município/Zona |publisher=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral |access-date=2015-07-09}}</ref> and had, as of April 2022, {{formatnum:71400}} voters, representing 2.836% of the state's total electorate.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.tse.jus.br/eleitor/estatisticas-de-eleitorado/consulta-quantitativo |title=Eleitor / Estatísticas de eleitorado / Consulta Quantitativo |access-date=2022-05-10 |publisher=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral}}</ref>

==Economy==
The [[Gross Domestic Product]] of São Gonçalo do Amarante in 2010 was {{BRL|953855}}, while the [[GDP per capita]] was {{BRL|10876.34}}.<ref name="IBGE_Cidades_SGARN">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/link.php?uf=rn&codmun=241200&nomemun=S%C3%A3o_Gon%C3%A7alo_do_Amarante |title=São Gonçalo do Amarante - RN |publisher=IBGE Cidades |access-date=2013-05-15}}</ref>

The [[primary sector]] contributes the smallest share to the municipal GDP.<ref name="IBGE_Cidades_SGARN"/> Notable is the practice of [[subsistence agriculture]], with cultivation focused on producing [[fruit]]s and [[vegetable]]s. In terms of [[livestock farming]], [[cattle]] (for [[milk]] production), [[goat]]s, and [[sheep]] stand out. In terms of [[fishing]], the cultivation of [[crustacean]]s and [[Mollusca|mollusks]], particularly [[shrimp]], [[mussel]]s, [[oyster]]s, and [[Mytella strigata|charru mussels]], is prominent. São Gonçalo do Amarante also has two [[Apiary|apiaries]], one for raising [[bee]]s and another for producing [[honey]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Economia">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/economia.php |title=São Gonçalo rumo ao desenvolvimento |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdTt1Osc |archive-date=2013-05-15}}</ref>

In the [[secondary sector]], the most prevalent industry is ceramics, particularly the production of [[brick]]s. Eighteen kilometers from the municipal seat, the Serrinha Community is located, where mineral extraction occurs through [[quarries]] used for paving streets and avenues and in construction.<ref name="Prefeitura_Economia"/> São Gonçalo do Amarante has an industrial district near the border with Natal, where seventeen companies from various sectors operate.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/detalhenoticia.php?idNoticia=126 |title=Distrito industrial recebe ação de serviços urbanos |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdgQFqrH |archive-date=2013-05-15}}</ref>

In the [[tertiary sector]], [[commerce]] in São Gonçalo do Amarante is notable for the sale of food products in establishments such as bars, snack bars, markets, grocery stores, and supermarkets. In addition to food products, there is also significant trade in cultural artifacts, rubber, building materials, pharmaceutical products, clothing, and textiles.<ref name="Prefeitura_Economia"/>

===Healthcare===
The healthcare network of São Gonçalo do Amarante includes thirty primary healthcare units, four [[Community health center|health centers]], two psychosocial care centers (CAPS), and one general hospital (as of August 2018),<ref>{{cite web |title=Infraestrutura Urbana & Social |url=https://relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/longevidade/infraestrutura_urbana_e_social/BRA002024130/sao-goncalo-do-amarante---rn |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220211224351/https://relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/longevidade/infraestrutura_urbana_e_social/BRA002024130/sao-goncalo-do-amarante---rn |archive-date=2022-02-11 |access-date=2022-02-11 |work=Centro de Inovação Sesi - Longevidade e Produtividade}}</ref> namely the Belarmina Monte Maternity Hospital, a philanthropic institution linked to the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|Unified Health System]] (SUS), inaugurated in December 1976 and managed since November 2008 by the São Camilo Beneficent Society, funded by the Municipal Health Secretariat. It offers beds for hospitalization in clinical medicine, pediatrics, surgery, and obstetrics.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saocamilosaogoncalo.org.br/instituciona/historico-do-hospital |title=Histórico do Hospital |access-date=2013-05-24 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130814142313/http://www.saocamilosaogoncalo.org.br/instituciona/historico-do-hospital |archive-date=2013-08-14}}</ref>

Data from the [[Ministry of Health (Brazil)|Ministry of Health]] indicate that, between 2001 and 2016, the municipality recorded {{formatnum:5860}} cases of [[Dengue fever|dengue]], 42 of [[leishmaniasis]], and thirteen of [[malaria]]; from 1990 to 2020, there were 247 notifications of [[AIDS]], resulting in a rate of 16.4 cases per 100,000 inhabitants (2020).<ref name="ODS 03 Saúde e bem estar"/> In 2019, 604 deaths were recorded due to [[morbidity]], with diseases of the [[circulatory system]] and [[neoplasm|tumors]] being the primary causes, with higher incidence among men.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/17/15752?ano=2019 |title=Morbidade |author=IBGE |date=2019 |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref> In the same year, {{formatnum:1410}} live births were recorded, mostly via [[caesarean section]], with an [[infant mortality]] rate of 14.89 deaths of children under one year per thousand live births.<ref name="ODS 03 Saúde e bem estar">{{cite web |title=ODS 03 Saúde e bem estar |url=https://www.ipea.gov.br/ods/ods3.html |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/39/30279?ano=2019 |title=Taxa de mortalidade infantil |author=IBGE |date=2019 |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref>

===Education===
The education component of the HDI in the municipality reached 0.564 in 2010, while the [[literacy rate]] for the population over ten years, as indicated by the 2010 census, was 86.1% (87.7% for women and 84.4% for men).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/1383#/n6/2412005/v/all/p/all/c2/all/d/v1646%201/l/v,p+c2,t/resultado |title=Tabela 1383 - Taxa de alfabetização das pessoas de 10 anos ou mais de idade por sexo |date=2010 |author=IBGE |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref> The expected years of schooling was 9.18 years, below the state average of 9.54 years.<ref name="sec-educacao"/>

School attendance for children aged five to six was 90.92%, and for those aged eleven to thirteen in the final years of elementary school, it was 80.82%. Among youth, these rates were lower, with 45.35% of those aged fifteen to seventeen having completed elementary school and 26.58% of those aged eighteen to twenty having completed high school. Among the population aged 25 and older, 18.42% were [[illiterate]], 43.75% had completed elementary school, 28.09% had completed high school, and only 2.96% had completed [[higher education]]. More recent data from 2014 showed an elementary school dropout rate of 3.8%, reaching nearly 20% in high school.<ref name="sec-educacao">{{cite web |url=http://atlasbrasil.org.br/perfil/municipio/241200#sec-educacao |title=Educação |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref>

The educational network of São Gonçalo do Amarante covers all levels of education, from [[primary education]] to [[secondary education]], in both public and private sectors, with a total of {{formatnum:22991}} enrollments (2020 school census), the majority ({{formatnum:14589}}) in [[elementary education]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/sao-goncalo-do-amarante/pesquisa/13/78117?ano=2020 |title=Censo escolar - sinopse |author=IBGE |date=2020 |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref> Among higher education institutions, the Federal Institute of Rio Grande do Norte (IFRN) stands out, inaugurated in December 2012 on the same site as the former Rockefeller Farm.<ref>{{cite web |title=Histórico |url=https://portal.ifrn.edu.br/campus/saogoncalo/o-campus/direcao-geral/ |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://portal.ifrn.edu.br/campus/saogoncalo/noticias/inauguracao-do-campus-sao-goncalo-do-amarante |title=Inauguração do Câmpus São Gonçalo do Amarante |date=2012-12-03 |access-date=2022-02-11}}</ref>

São Gonçalo do Amarante holds a prominent position in Rio Grande do Norte for its cultural production. It is home to the Poti Cavalcanti Municipal Theater, inaugurated in 2003 with a capacity for 238 people, considered the cultural hub of the municipality, hosting various events.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/cultura_teatro.php |title=Teatro Municipal de São Gonçalo do Amarante: templo de nossa cultura |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdxhjmIi |archive-date=2013-05-15}}</ref> It is the birthplace of Militana Salustino do Nascimento, known as [[Dona Militana]] (1925–2010), an icon of Rio Grande do Norte's popular culture.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/cultura_donamilitana.php |title=Dona Militana – A Romanceira do Brasil |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6Gdxpi47h |archive-date=2013-05-15 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Municipal holidays include 28 January, the day of the patron saint [[Gundisalvus of Amarante]]; 29 October, the day of the patron [[Benedict the Moor|Saint Benedict]]; and 11 December, the date of political emancipation, the latter established in 1990 and the others in 1978.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/feriados-municipais/ |title=Feriados Municipais |date=30 January 2018 |access-date=2022-02-10 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20211229022641/https://saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/feriados-municipais/ |archive-date=2021-12-29}}</ref>

===Religiosity and events===
Religiosity in São Gonçalo do Amarante is closely tied to the Martyrs of Cunhaú and Uruaçu. They were killed in a massacre on 3 October 1645, for resisting the Dutch. Also called ''Flamengos'', the Dutch invaded the territory corresponding to the current [[Northeast Region, Brazil|northeast Brazil]] to collect debts from the Portuguese for building mills in the [[Colonial Brazil|colony]]. As a result of this episode, few survived, and around eighty people died, taken to neighboring communities and the ''Keulen Castle'', now the [[Forte dos Reis Magos|Fortaleza dos Reis Magos]]. In 1989, the massacre victims were recognized as martyrs and beatified by [[Pope John Paul II]] on 5 March 2000. The Monument to the Martyrs was erected at the massacre site and inaugurated nine months later.<ref name="Religiosidade"/>

The municipality's cultural calendar includes major events such as the feast of [[Saint Sebastian]], celebrated in some villages on 19 January; the feast of Saint Gundisalvus on 28 January, a municipal holiday; the Drivers’ Festival on 30 May; the feast of [[Anthony of Padua|Saint Anthony]] in the Santo Antônio do Potengi district on 12 June; the traditional Santa Terezinha festival and donkey race, both in September in the Guanduba community; the feasts of [[Francis of Assisi|Saint Francis]] (in the Alagadiço Grande and Rio da Prata communities), [[Benedict the Moor|Saint Benedict]], [[Expectation of the Blessed Virgin Mary|Our Lady of Expectation]] (in the Rego Moleiro village), and the commemoration of the Martyrs of Uruaçu in October; the feast of Saint Jude in the Jacaré-Mirim and Uruaçu villages in November; and the feasts of [[Saint Lucy]] (in Igreja Nova and the Serrinha community) and political emancipation in December.<ref name="História_Prefeitura"/>

===Cuisine and handicrafts===
The cuisine of São Gonçalo do Amarante features traditional dishes such as [[shrimp]], [[free-range chicken]], and various other [[crustacean]]s. In the rural area, the Pajuçara community, though sparsely populated, is a reference for gastronomy in the Natal Metropolitan Region, with several establishments where shrimp is the most consumed dish. From it, various dishes are prepared, such as shrimp au gratin and shrimp pap, combined with other foods. Additionally, the production of [[sweets]] and [[liqueur]]s flavored with tropical fruits stands out in the Rio da Prata community.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/gastronomia.php |title=Gastronomia |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdwFXkPB |archive-date=2013-05-15}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalorn.com.br/cultura/culinaria |title=Gastronomia |publisher=Portal São Gonçalo do Amarante |access-date=2013-05-15 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdwL2cox |archive-date=2013-05-15}}</ref>

[[Handicraft]]s are another form of spontaneous cultural expression in São Gonçalo do Amarante, with distinctive artisanal production found throughout various parts of the municipality, crafted in accordance with local culture and way of life. Several groups bring together artisans from the region, providing spaces for the creation, exhibition, and sale of handmade products made from regional [[raw material]]s such as [[clay]], [[liana]], scraps, and [[sisal]]. These items are typically sold at fairs, exhibitions, or craft shops, such as the Craft Market located in the Santo Antônio do Potengi district along the RN-160 state highway.<ref name="Prefeitura">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/artesanato.php |title=Nosso artesanato ganha prestígio nacional |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=15 May 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdkNL3iN |archive-date=15 May 2013 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Objetos feitos artesanalmente">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalorn.com.br/cultura/artesanato |title=Objetos feitos artesanalmente |publisher=Portal São Gonçalo do Amarante |access-date=15 May 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdkYL2rK |archive-date=15 May 2013 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Monuments and historical sites===
; Settlements
The settlement of ''Barreiros'' is one of the oldest in the municipality, located near the Potengi River estuary, where fishing is practiced. During the early settlement of São Gonçalo do Amarante, it housed a river port that provided access to Natal.<ref name="Monumentos"/>
The former settlement of ''Poço Limpo'' emerged in the second half of the 19th century and was the scene of several conflicts. It belonged to the municipality of São Gonçalo do Amarante until 1943 and corresponds to the current municipality of [[Ielmo Marinho]].<ref name="Comunidades_históricas"/>

The settlement of ''Regomoleiro'' is notable for two significant events in the municipality's history: the first was the death of Jacob Rabbi, a defender of Dutch interests in the region, and the second was the participation of Maria do Carmo Brito, the first woman to serve as president of the São Gonçalo do Amarante City Council. Its original name was Rodrigo Moleiro, after the owner of an old grain mill. In 1910, the municipal administration proposed renaming it Alberto Maranhão, but the change never took effect.<ref name="Comunidades_históricas"/> The Rego Moleiro chapel, one of the oldest structures in São Gonçalo do Amarante, is located in this settlement.<ref name="Religiosidade">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/religiosidade.php |title=Religiosidade |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=15 May 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdrliCvc |archive-date=15 May 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The settlement of ''Santo Antônio dos Barreiros'' was established as a village in 1885, originating from the interests of a landowning family that inhabited the area. It emerged near the Barreiros village, hence its name. In the 1970s, it was renamed Santo Antônio do Potengi, which has since undergone a process of [[urbanization]]. It preserves important historical landmarks of the municipality, such as the old Matoso family mansion and the São Francisco sugar mill.<ref name="Comunidades_históricas"/>

; Churches
The ''Utinga Chapel'' was built around 1730, according to official documents, and is dedicated to [[Our Lady of Perpetual Help]]. The chapel served as a route for Dutch occupation in the 18th century and has been listed as a heritage site by the José Augusto Foundation of Rio Grande do Norte since 1989. It is believed that the oldest road in the state, connecting [[Baía da Traição]] in [[Paraíba]] to Natal, passed through the Utinga Chapel. The term Utinga, in the indigenous language, means ''white water''.<ref name="Monumentos"/><ref name="Comunidades_históricas"/>

[[File:Igreja Matriz de São Gonçalo do Amarante.jpg|left|thumb|Parish church of São Gonçalo do Amarante]]
The ''Santo Antônio do Potengi Chapel'' was constructed in 1885 (the year carved on the chapel's facade to this day) and is situated in an elevated area, offering a brief view of the urban area of São Gonçalo do Amarante. A possible legend suggests that the chapel was built in honor of two women who discovered an image of Saint Anthony inside a cave. They took the image home, but it reportedly disappeared and was later found in the same cave where it was originally discovered.<ref name="Religiosidade"/>

The ''Parish Church'' was built in the 18th century, in 1719, and completed only in the 19th century. It houses several antique wooden statues and [[altar]]s that feature [[Baroque in Brazil|Baroque]]-style characteristics. It is considered the most representative historical monument of São Gonçalo do Amarante and was listed by the [[National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage]] (IPHAN) in 1963.<ref name="Monumentos">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalorn.com.br/cultura/monumentos |title=Construções históricas |publisher=Portal São Gonçalo do Amarante |access-date=15 May 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdlWiPnl |archive-date=15 May 2013 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The ''New Church'' was founded in 1867 by Joaquim Félix de Lima and is dedicated to the [[Immaculate Conception]]. Also built in the Baroque style, it is another significant monument of the municipality. It is located opposite a square where a cross once stood, formerly used for donkey races.<ref name="Comunidades_históricas">{{cite web |url=http://www.saogoncalo.rn.gov.br/comunidadeshistoricas.php |title=Comunidades Históricas |publisher=Prefeitura |access-date=15 May 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/6GdlNFHJY |archive-date=15 May 2013 |url-status=live}}</ref>

; Other monuments
The ''Olho d’Água do Lucas Mansion'' was built in the mid-19th century, in 1853, and is named after the Lucas family, its first owners. Today, the monument contains remnants of the local sugar mill and ancient drawings made by [[slaves]].<ref name="Monumentos"/>

The ''Martyrs’ Cross'' is located five kilometers from the municipal seat, in the Uruaçu settlement, and dates back to the early 17th century, around 1609. It served as a symbol of local resistance against the Dutch, where residents of the Potengi sugar mill fought against abuses by the Dutch administration. On 3 October 1645, one of the most significant events in the [[history of Rio Grande do Norte]], the [[Cunhaú and Uruaçu Massacre|Uruaçu Massacre]], took place near the current Uruaçu settlement, which houses the ''Martyrs’ Monument''. Three kilometers away is the Marty_daily life. Several groups bring together artisans from the region, providing spaces for the creation, exhibition, and sale of handmade products made from regional raw materials such as clay, liana, fabric scraps, and sisal. These items are typically sold at fairs, exhibitions, or craft shops, such as the Craft Market located in the Santo Antônio do Potengi district along the RN-160 state highway.<ref name="Prefeitura"/><ref name="Objetos feitos artesanalmente"/>
