'''Tizimín Municipality''' is a [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Yucatán]]. The municipality is located in the north-east of the [[Mexican state]] of [[Yucatán (state)|Yucatán]], and it is the largest municipality in the state with a territory that is 11% of the total area of the state. As of 2005 it also has the second largest population of any municipality in the state,<ref name="Census Results by Locality, 2005"/> the largest being [[Mérida, Yucatán (municipality)|Mérida]] and the third largest being [[Valladolid, Yucatán|Valladolid]].

The city of Tizimín is the municipal seat and is located at about 160&nbsp;km east of [[Mérida, Yucatán]], 200&nbsp;km west of [[Cancún, Quintana Roo]], and 42&nbsp;km south of [[Río Lagartos]], the city's traditional [[sea port]] on the [[Gulf of Mexico]]. The municipal seat's population of 42,283 is about 60% of the total population of the municipality.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/><ref name="Census Results by Locality, 2005"/>

The word "Tizimin" means "[[tapir]]" in the [[Maya language]]. With the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán]], Tizimín was established as a Spanish colonial town in 1544.

==Boundaries==
The [[Gulf of Mexico]] is its northern border and [[Quintana Roo]] is its eastern border. And the municipality borders the following municipalities:  to the south [[Temozón Municipality|Temozón]], [[Calotmul Municipality|Calotmul]] and [[Chemax Municipality|Chemax]]; and to the west [[Sucilá Municipality|Sucilá]], [[Panabá Municipality|Panabá]] and [[Río Lagartos Municipality|Río Lagartos]].

==Economy==

The city of Tizimín is the second most economically important city of Yucatán state, only after the municipality of Mérida. It has good diversification of commerce. The main activities take place in the agricultural, forestry, industry, fisheries and tourism. Its production of meat, milk and cheese serving a large percentage of the needs of the state of Yucatán and the adjacent territory upstate Quintana Roo, including a good part of the robust demand for Cancun, the town that is one of the main drivers of the economy in southeastern Mexico.

Supporting this active economic development, the municipality has a broad network of educational infrastructure, health facilities, highways and hotels.

==Festivals==
Tizimín is known for its festival in honor of the [[Three Wise Men|Three Kings]]. The festivities begin on December 29 and conclude on January 6 of the following year.

In Sucopo there is a festival honoring the Virgin of the Candelaria, from 28 to 31 January.

==History==

In the days before the [[Spanish conquest of Maya|Spanish conquest of Yucatán]], Tizimin municipality was a part of the province of Cupules.

'''1542''' The Spanish captain Sebastian Burgos, with his companion [[Francisco de Montejo]] (a Nephew), conquered the territory.

'''1543''' The town of [[Valladolid, Yucatán|Valladolid]] is founded on the banks of the lagoon Chouac-Ha. (Chouac-Ha lagoon, which was the original location of the now city of Valladolid, now belongs to the municipality of Tizimin.) While [[Valladolid, Yucatán|Valladolid]] was founded in here, it was later abandoned as the residents had too many problems with [[mosquito]]es and humidity. As the site is part of the municipality of Tizimin, the fact that this historical event that occurred in the municipality is highlighted as part of its history.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/>

'''1549''' Tizimin was a land parcel owned by Captain Sebastian de Burgos.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/>

'''1824''' The State Congress decrees the reestablishment of Indian republics. Tizimin was one of those republics.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/>

'''1835''' A conservative unitary system of government was instituted in Mexico (a centralized dictatorship unconstitutionally brought forth and held by the then-President: [[Antonio López de Santa Anna|Santa Anna]]. [[Yucatán (state)|Yucatán]] became a department, and authority was imposed from the center.

'''1838''' Discontent increased and an insurrection erupted in Tizimín in May, advocating Yucatecan independence.

'''1870''' This year the parish priest of the village of Tizimin, Manuel Luciano Perez,<ref name="Edmonson, Munro S. 1982">Edmonson, Munro S.  (Translator)  ''The Ancient Future of the Itza: The book of Chilam Balam of Tizimin'', page ix. University of Texas Press (1982) {{ISBN|0-292-72106-4}}</ref> sends a rare manuscript that has been called [[Chilam Balam]] of Tizimin to the Bishop of Yucatán, Crescendo Carrillo y Ancona,<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/> with the remark that it has been in his possession for a number of years.<ref name="Edmonson, Munro S. 1982"/> It is now preserved in the National Museum of Anthropology and History in Mexico City.

'''1913''' On November 30 the inauguration of the railway from Espita-Tizimin, on this day the first locomotive arrives at the station in Tizimin.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/>

'''1943''' On May 29 start of the fourth centenary celebrations of the founding of Valladolid, in the Chouac-Ha (in this place was founded the city of Valladolid on the site that belongs to the municipality of Tizimin).<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/>

'''1975''' For the first time in the history of Yucatán, a European monarch comes to visit Tizimin and its people. On February 28 Queen Elizabeth II of the United Kingdom of Great Britain formally inaugurated the zoo and botanical garden at Tizimin.<ref name="Encyclopedia of the Municipalities of Mexico: Yucatan"/> Queen Elizabeth II also visited Uxmal and until recently the chain that was added so that she could climb the temple of the magician was still in place.
