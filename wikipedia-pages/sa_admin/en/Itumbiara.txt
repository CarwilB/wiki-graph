'''Itumbiara''' ({{IPA|pt|itũw̃biˈaɾɐ}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in [[Brazil]], located in the southern part of the state of [[Goiás]], on the border with [[Minas Gerais]]. It is a "sister city" to the [[List of municipalities in Minas Gerais|Minas Gerais municipality]] of [[Araporã]]. The city lies south of the [[List of capitals of subdivisions of Brazil|state capital]], [[Goiânia]], approximately 204 kilometers away, and 411 kilometers from the federal capital, [[Brasília]].<ref name="Dados sobre Itumbiara">{{Cite web|url=http://www.emsampa.com.br/xspxdf.htm|title=Distâncias entre Cidades |author=Em Sampa |access-date=1 November 2010 |date=25 December 2011 }}</ref> Covering an area of {{convert|2447|km2}}, Itumbiara is the thirteenth most populous municipality in Goiás, with a population of {{formatnum:107970}} inhabitants according to the [[2022 Brazilian Census|2022 Census]] by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE).<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021">{{cite web|access-date=17 August 2024 |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2022 |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios |url=https://www.ibge.gov.br/estatisticas/economicas/contas-nacionais/9088-produto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios.html?=&t=resultados}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref><ref name="IBGE" />

The history of Itumbiara dates back to the early 1820s, when a road was constructed across the [[Paranaíba River]] to connect [[Uberaba]] with central Goiás. The influx of settlers, drawn primarily by the road's construction, led to the establishment of a district named Santa Rita do Paranaíba in 1852. Following the [[Proclamation of the Republic (Brazil)|Proclamation of the Republic]], the municipality was officially established in 1909, still under the name Santa Rita do Paranaíba. The name "Itumbiara," derived from the [[Tupi language]], was proposed by engineer Inácio Pais Leme, who oversaw the road's construction.

Itumbiara is regarded as the gateway to Goiás.<ref name="SEPLAN">{{Cite web|url=http://www.seplan.go.gov.br/sepin/pub/rank/2007/Itumbiara.pdf|title=Itumbiara (GO) - 5º Lugar|author=Secretaria de Planejamento do Estado de Goiás|date=2008|access-date=11 February 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140112071018/http://www.seplan.go.gov.br/sepin/pub/rank/2007/Itumbiara.pdf|archive-date=2014-01-12|url-status=dead}}</ref> It is also one of the state's largest [[export]]ers, owing to its proximity to Minas Gerais and [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]], which facilitates the distribution of agricultural products from southwestern Goiás to the [[South Region, Brazil|South]] and [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast]] regions.<ref name="Itumbiara3">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/2204/itumbiara-go.html|title=Itumbiara - GO|publisher=Ferias Tur|access-date=3 November 2010}}</ref> Key tourist attractions include the [[Paranaíba River]], the Salitre Waterfall, the Beira Rio Lighthouse, and the Affonso Penna Bridge, which connects Goiás and Minas Gerais. Water sports are also popular in the city.<ref name="Itumbiara3"/><ref name="Itumbiara 4">{{Cite web|url=http://www.cidades.com.br/cidade/itumbiara/000989.html |title=Informações sobre Itumbiara, GO |publisher=Cidades |access-date=3 November 2010 }}</ref> The ''Ranking Connected Smart Cities'', which identifies Brazil's most [[Smart city|intelligent]] and high-potential cities, ranked Itumbiara as the 8th most intelligent and high-potential city in the Central-West Region and the 3rd in Goiás in 2021.<ref name="Itumbiara Ranking Connected Smart Cities">{{Cite web|url=https://app.powerbi.com/view?r=eyJrIjoiMWJjYTgzZGUtNGZkOC00YmM1LTljMDgtODU1ZmQ4NDlmNTRiIiwidCI6IjA0ZTcxZThlLTUwZDMtNDU1ZC04ODAzLWM3ZGI4ODhkNjRiYiJ9&embedImagePlaceholder=true&pageName=ReportSection|title=Ranking Geral - Ranking Connected Smart Cities |publisher=Urban Systems |access-date=21 September 2021}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
[[File:Etimologia de Itumbiara.svg|thumb|left|According to [[Eduardo de Almeida Navarro|Navarro]], the toponym originates from [[Tupi language|Tupi]]: ''ytu'', meaning a waterfall, and ''piara'', meaning a path. Both terms are in a [[genitive case|genitive relationship]], implying origin or possession, which allows for the inversion of terms (as indicated by the arrows). The transformation of 'p' to 'mb' occurs only when the preceding vowel is nasal. Thus, this change appears to be an error.]]
In the [[Tupi language]], the toponym Itumbiara translates to "Path to the Waterfall," derived from the combination of ''ytu'' ("waterfall")<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://falabonito.wordpress.com/2007/07/05/cidades-brasileiras-cujos-nomes-tem-origem-no-tupi-guarani/ |title=Cidades Brasileiras cujos Nomes têm Origem no Tupi-Guarani |date=5 July 2007 |publisher=falabonito.wordpress.com |access-date=21 September 2021 }}</ref> and ''piara'' ("path to").<ref>{{Cite book |last=Navarro |first=E. A. |title=Método Moderno de Tupi Antigo: a língua do Brasil dos primeiros séculos |trans-title=Modern Method of Old Tupi: The Language of Brazil in the First Centuries |language=pt |edition=3rd |location=São Paulo |publisher=Global |year=2005 |page=395}}</ref> Inácio Pais Leme, an explorer of the region, named a tax post along the road he built connecting the village of Santa Rita do Paranaíba (now a district of the municipality) to [[Cachoeira Dourada]].<ref name="Itumbiara3" />

== History ==
Around 1824, General Cunha Matos initiated the construction of a road linking the Goiás locality of [[Anhanguera, Goiás|Anhanguera]] to the Minas Gerais city of [[Uberaba]]. The beginning and end of the road required the crossing of the [[Paranaíba River]], which forms the border between [[Goiás]] and [[Minas Gerais]].<ref name="História de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web |url=http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=227 |title=Breve Histórico do Município de Itumbiara, 101 anos |publisher=Prefeitura Municipal de Itumbiara |access-date=3 November 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120111111208/http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=227 |archive-date=2012-01-11 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

This crossing was established at a port, where the state government set up a revenue collection post. As an interstate crossing, the post aimed to regulate traffic at that point. The fertile lands in the region, ideal for agriculture and cattle raising, coupled with this development, led to the gradual formation of a small settlement. The residents of this nascent urban center built a chapel and dedicated it to [[Rita of Cascia|Saint Rita]]. In homage to their patron saint, the settlement was named Porto de Santa Rita.<ref name="História de Itumbiara 1" />

On August 21, 1852, provincial resolution No. 18 elevated the area to the status of a district named Santa Rita do Paranaíba. It was established as a municipality by State Law No. 349 on July 16, 1909, under Governor Urbano Coelho de Gouveia. The municipality was officially established on October 12, 1909, and was elevated to city status by State Law No. 518 on July 27, 1915, and to a ''[[comarca]]'' by Law No. 621 on July 29, 1918. Despite the abundance of water surrounding the city, [[electricity]] was not introduced until 1933.<ref name="História de Itumbiara 1" />

Itumbiara was the scene of two revolutions in the 1930s. The first significant conflict was part of the [[Revolution of 1930]], pitting forces loyal to President [[Washington Luís]] against supporters of the reformist ideas of future President Getúlio Vargas. Approximately one hundred Itumbiara residents participated in this episode. Washington Luís Avenue, located in the city center, marks the site of the conflict. The second violent confrontation in the city occurred during the [[Constitutionalist Revolution|1932 Constitutionalist Revolution]], between the [[Brazilian Army|Army]] and rebel forces from [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]]. This revolutionary movement, primarily composed of lawyers, teachers, merchants, and university students, aimed to demand immediate general elections for the [[President of Brazil|President of the Republic]], Deputies, and Senators, and the drafting of a new Brazilian Constitution. Although President [[Getúlio Vargas]] suppressed the São Paulo revolutionaries, elections were held in 1933, and Brazil transitioned to a state of law with the promulgation of the [[Brazilian Constitution of 1934|1934 Constitution]]. The Affonso Penna Bridge still bears bullet marks from this episode in [[History of Brazil|Brazilian history]].<ref name="História de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.achetudoeregiao.com.br/go/itumbiara/historia.htm |title=Historia de Itumbiara - GO |publisher=Ache Tudo |access-date=4 November 2010 }}</ref>

Engineer Inácio Pais Leme, who built the "Itumbiara" road from Santa Rita do Paranaíba to [[Cachoeira Dourada]], forty kilometers from the city, proposed naming the municipality after the road, which in [[Tupi language|Tupi]] means "Path to the Waterfall." The Goiás government approved this suggestion.<ref name="História de Itumbiara 1" />

Itumbiara's first municipal administrator was Mr. Antônio Joaquim da Silva, appointed shortly after its emancipation. Other pioneering figures who held significant roles during the municipality's establishment included Major Rogério Prates Cotrim, the first municipal judge; José Olimpio Xavier de Barros, Public Prosecutor; Pedro Gomes de Oliveira, Notary Public; and Major Militão Pereira de Almeida, the first police chief. In the [[Legislative Branch]], pioneers included Jacinto Brandão as President and Colonel Sidney Pereira de Almeida as secretary.<ref name="Primeiras autoridades de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web|url=http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=226|title=Primeiras Autoridades|publisher=Prefeitura Municipal de Itumbiara|access-date=3 November 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120111112540/http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=226|archive-date=2012-01-11|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Itumbiara's strategic geographic location has made it one of the most developed cities in Brazil's [[Central-West Region, Brazil|Central-West Region]]. The municipality has grown steadily and become one of Goiás's leading exporters, alongside the capital, [[Goiânia]], and other cities such as [[Anápolis]] and [[Rio Verde, Goiás|Rio Verde]].<ref name="Itumbiara3" />

The municipality lost parts of its geographic area with the emancipation of the districts of [[Panamá, Goiás|Panamá]] in 1952, [[Cachoeira Dourada]] in 1982—a major tourist attraction at the time of its emancipation—and [[Inaciolândia]] in 1993. It has hosted visits from eight [[President of Brazil|Brazilian Presidents]]: [[Afonso Pena]], [[Juscelino Kubitschek]], [[Emílio Garrastazu Médici]], [[Ernesto Geisel]], [[João Figueiredo|João Batista de Oliveira Figueiredo]], [[Fernando Collor de Mello]], [[Fernando Henrique Cardoso]], and [[Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva]].<ref name="História de Itumbiara 2" />

== Geography ==
Itumbiara spans an area of 2,461 km², accounting for 0.7237% of Goiás's territory, 0.1538% of the [[Central-West Region, Brazil|Central-West Region]], and 0.029% of Brazil's total surface area.<ref name="cnm.org.br">{{cite web|access-date=24 February 2011 |date=2010 |publisher=Confederação Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |title=Infra-Estrutura - Domicílios Particulares Permanentes e Moradores (Itumbiara) |url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> The urbanized area of the municipality covers 19.3424 km², making it one of the largest urbanized areas among Goiás municipalities.<ref name="urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br">{{Cite web|url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/go.html|title=Goiás|publisher=Embrapa Monitoramento por Satélite |access-date=31 July 2010}}</ref> The city is situated at an elevation ranging from 320 to 448 meters, depending on the location, with the highest points not exceeding 800 meters.<ref name="Geografia de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/historicos_cidades/historico_conteudo.php?codmun=521150 |title=Acidentes geográficos |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |author=@Cidades |access-date=11 February 2011}}</ref> Itumbiara borders the following municipalities: [[Goiatuba]], [[Panamá, Goiás|Panamá]], and [[Buriti Alegre]] to the north; the state of [[Minas Gerais]] to the south; Buriti Alegre to the east; and [[Bom Jesus de Goiás]] and [[Cachoeira Dourada]] to the west.<ref name="Limites de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web |title=Limites do município de Itumbiara |url=http://citybrazil.uol.com.br/go/itumbiara/geral_detalhe.php?cat=2 |access-date=11 February 2011 |publisher=City Brazil - UOL}}</ref>

[[File:Itumbiara go furnas.jpg|thumb|Vila de Furnas, one of the city's 58 neighborhoods]]

The municipality features predominantly mountainous terrain with undulating sections, though most of the landscape is flat, ideal for agriculture and pasture. The Santa Rita Range, along the [[Paranaíba River]], extends its foothills inland, separating water masses. Besides this range, there are several hills, none of which are particularly prominent. The region's altitude varies from 320 to 448 meters.<ref name="Relevo de Itumbiara 1" />

The vegetation cover of Itumbiara is typically formed by fields and ''[[cerrado]]'', with common species including [[Anadenanthera colubrina|angico]], [[aroeira]], [[Poaceae|grasses]], [[Hymenaea courbaril|courbaril]], [[Byrsonima crassifolia|nance]], [[Caryocar brasiliense|pequi]], and [[Hibiscus tiliaceus|sea hibiscus]].<ref name="Relevo de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web|url=http://almanet.8m.com/relevo.html|title=Relevo - Itumbiara|publisher=AlmaNet|access-date=22 February 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428232546/http://almanet.8m.com/relevo.html|archive-date=2014-04-28|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Itumbiara has no administrative regions and is divided into 58 neighborhoods. Among the most populous are Afonso Pena, Novo Horizonte, Vila Vitória, Nossa Senhora da Saúde, and Cidade Jardim.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.jornaloregional.com.br/reportagens.htm |title=Bairros de Itumbiara |access-date=25 January 2015 |publisher=Jornal O Regional |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090321063741/http://www.jornaloregional.com.br/reportagens.htm |archive-date=2009-03-21 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Hydrography ===
[[File:MedioParanaiba-AraporaXItumbia-MGxGO.jpg|thumb|[[Paranaíba River]], on the border between Itumbiara and the [[Minas Gerais]] municipality of [[Araporã]].]]

Itumbiara is located within the Paraná River Basin, in the Paraná hydrographic region. The municipal seat is bathed by the [[Paranaíba River]], Trindade Stream, and the Água Suja, Pombas, and Buriti creeks. The territory is also crossed by the [[Rio dos Bois (Paranaíba River tributary)|Rio dos Bois]] and [[Meia Ponte River]], along with notable streams such as Santa Maria, Campanha, Bom Sucesso, Boa Vereda, Medes, and Posses. Hundreds of smaller creeks are also present.<ref name="Geografia de Itumbiara 1" /><ref name="Hidrografia de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web |title=Hidrografia |url=https://cbhparanaiba.org.br/a-bacia/regioes-hidrograficas |access-date=11 February 2011 |publisher=CBH Paranaiba}}</ref><ref name="Hidrografia de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web|url=http://mochileiro.tur.br/itumbiara.htm |title=Cidade de Itumbiara |publisher=Mochileiro Tur|access-date=11 November 2010}}</ref>

The Paranaíba River originates in [[Minas Gerais]], in the Mata da Corda region, in the municipality of [[Rio Paranaíba]], which shares the river's name. It is one of the tributaries forming the [[Paraná River]], alongside the [[Rio Grande (Paraná River tributary)|Rio Grande]]. The Rio dos Bois originates in the Congumé Range, in [[Americano do Brasil]], and flows into the [[Paranaíba River]]. The Meia Ponte River has its source in the Brandões Range, in [[Itauçu]].<ref name="Hidrografia de Itumbiara 3">{{Cite web |url=http://www.seplan.go.gov.br/sepin/pub/anuario/2005/situacao_fisica/bacias/bacia_paranaiba.htm |title=Bacia do rio Paranaíba |publisher=Secretaria de Planejamento do estado de Goiás (SEPLAN-Goiás) |access-date=25 March 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081024054256/http://www.seplan.go.gov.br/sepin/pub/anuario/2005/situacao_fisica/bacias/bacia_paranaiba.htm |archive-date=24 October 2008 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Due to the significant energy potential of the Paranaíba River, large hydroelectric plants have been built in Itumbiara's territory, including the [[Itumbiara Hydroelectric Plant]], the largest hydroelectric facility operated by [[Eletrobras Furnas|Furnas Centrais Elétricas S.A.]], which forms an artificial lake.<ref name="Relevo de Itumbiara 1" />

The climate of Itumbiara is [[Tropical climate|tropical]] (type ''Aw'' according to the [[Köppen climate classification|Köppen classification]]),<ref name="Clima de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web |url=http://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/pics/kottek_et_al_2006.gif |title=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification |author=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification |publisher=Institute for Veterinary Public Health |access-date=25 March 2011 }}</ref> with a [[wet season]] from October to April, characterized by moderately high temperatures, and a [[dry season]] from May to September, with milder temperatures. Autumn and spring serve as transitional seasons. Sudden temperature changes are common in the region. The most significant climatic variations occur from mid-August to January, when the weather becomes substantially hot, with [[apparent temperature]]s approaching or exceeding {{convert|40|°C}}.<ref name="Clima de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web |title=Clima e temperatura do município de Itumbiara |url=https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/29959/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Itumbiara-Goi%C3%A1s-Brasil-durante-o-ano |access-date=11 February 2011 |publisher=Weather Park}}</ref>

According to data from the [[National Institute of Meteorology]] (INMET), since June 1989, the lowest temperature recorded in Itumbiara was {{convert|1.9|°C}} on July 18, 2000, and the highest reached {{convert|42|°C}} on October 7, 2020.<ref name="BDMEP-INMET"/><ref name="INMET-Itumbiara-GO-A035"/> The highest 24-hour [[precipitation]] accumulation was {{convert|165.2|mm}} on December 12, 2005. Accumulations of {{convert|100|mm}} or more were also recorded on December 27, 1996 ({{convert|122.5|mm}}), March 16, 2019 ({{convert|122.2|mm}}), December 8, 2003 ({{convert|113|mm}}), and January 16, 1991 ({{convert|100.5|mm}}).<ref name="BDMEP-INMET"/><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://tempo.inmet.gov.br/Graficos/A035|title=Chuva acumulada em 24h|author=INMET|access-date=July 13, 2020}}</ref>

== Demography ==
According to the 2022 census by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), Itumbiara's population was {{formatnum:107970}} inhabitants, making it the [[List of municipalities in Goiás by population|12th most populous municipality in Goiás]], with a [[population density]] of 44.1 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>. The 2010 census reported that 50.7% of the population were women ({{formatnum:47117}} inhabitants), 49.3% were men ({{formatnum:45825}} inhabitants), 95.8% ({{formatnum:89000}} inhabitants) lived in the [[urban area]], and 4.2% ({{formatnum:3942}} inhabitants) resided in the [[rural area]].<ref name="Demografia do município goiano de Itumbiara 1">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=521150&idtema=1&search=goias |title=Censo demográfico 2010: Sinopse |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2010 |access-date=20 December 2013 }}</ref>

Itumbiara's population grew by 14.14% over the last decade.<ref name="CP">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ogenial.com.br/brasil/goias-itumbiara |title=Município de Itumbiara (GO) |author=O Genial |date=2010 |access-date=24 February 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428232604/http://www.ogenial.com.br/brasil/goias-itumbiara |archive-date=2014-04-28 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Between 1991 and 2000, the municipality recorded an average annual population growth rate of 2.97%, increasing from {{formatnum:81143}} inhabitants in 1991 to {{formatnum:86695}} in 2000. However, the urbanization rate grew by only 2.66%, rising from 63% in 1991 to 64.67% in 2000.<ref name="SEPLAN" /> In 2000, the municipality's population accounted for 1.82% of Goiás's population and 0.04% of Brazil's population.<ref name="SEPLAN" />

=== Ethnic composition ===
The cultural, political, and economic influences inherited from the [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]] are prominent and defining in Itumbiara. However, looking further back in history, the contributions of Indigenous peoples to the ethnic composition cannot be overlooked. Indigenous groups were the first to inhabit [[Brazil]].<ref name="Composição étnica de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web|url=http://pt.scribd.com/doc/30317059/GEO-HISTORIA-DE-GOIAS |title=História de Goiás |author=Scribd |date=26 March 2011 |access-date=26 March 2011 }}</ref>

Itumbiara's demographic history reflects the intermingling of the three primary ethnic groups that form the Brazilian population: [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|Indigenous]], [[Europeans|European]], and [[Black people|African]], resulting in the region's ''[[mestiço]]'' population. Later, with the arrival of immigrants, a unique cultural blend emerged, characterizing both the city and the [[Goiás|state]], along with their values and way of life. There is a notable community of [[Japanese Brazilians]], stemming from [[Japanese immigration in Brazil|Japanese immigration]] in the 1930s, primarily farmers.<ref name="Composição étnica de Itumbiara 3">{{Cite web |url=http://revistagloborural.globo.com/EditoraGlobo/componentes/article/edg_article_print/1,3916,1682623-1484-1,00.html |title=100 anos de Brasil |author=Globo Rural |date=June 2008 |access-date=2 March 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090211060537/http://revistagloborural.globo.com/EditoraGlobo/componentes/article/edg_article_print/1,3916,1682623-1484-1,00.html |archive-date=2009-02-11 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Additionally, Itumbiara stands out nationally not only for attracting migrants but also for sending emigrants abroad, particularly to [[North America]]. It ranks among Brazilian cities with the highest number of natives living overseas, alongside [[Governador Valadares, Minas Gerais|Governador Valadares]] ([[Minas Gerais|MG]]) and [[Piracanjuba]] ([[Goiás|GO]]).<ref name="Composição étnica de Itumbiara 4">{{Cite web |url=http://www.comunidadenews.com/comportamento/goias-ja-e-o-segundo-estado-que-mais-exporta-imigrante-diz-estudo-3383 |title=Goiás já é o segundo estado que mais exporta imigrante, diz estudo |author=Comunidade News |date=26 February 2011 |access-date=2 March 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110211224202/http://www.comunidadenews.com/comportamento/goias-ja-e-o-segundo-estado-que-mais-exporta-imigrante-diz-estudo-3383 |archive-date=2011-02-11 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

According to the 2000 census by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], Itumbiara's population comprises: Whites (65.35% or {{formatnum:53211}} inhabitants), ''[[Pardo]]s'' (28.49% or {{formatnum:23197}} inhabitants), Blacks (5.37% or {{formatnum:4377}} inhabitants), Indigenous (0.29% or {{formatnum:233}} inhabitants), and Asians (0.26% or {{formatnum:209}} inhabitants). Additionally, {{formatnum:203}} individuals did not declare their ethnicity, representing 0.25% of the total population.<ref name="Composição étnica de Itumbiara 5">{{Cite web |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/cd/cd2000cgp.asp?o=7&i=P |title=Tabela 2094 — População residente por cor ou raça e religião |date=2000 |access-date=2 March 2011}}</ref><ref name="Composição étnica de Itumbiara 6">{{Cite web |publisher=IBGE |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/cd/cd2000cgp.asp?o=7&i=P|title=Tabela 2094 — População residente por cor ou raça e religião |date=2000 |access-date=28 January 2011}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Igreja Santa Rita de Cássia, Itumbiara.jpg|thumb|left|Santa Rita de Cássia Church.]]
Reflecting Itumbiara's cultural diversity, the city hosts a variety of religious practices. Although it developed within a predominantly Catholic social framework due to colonization and immigration, today the city is home to numerous Protestant denominations, as well as practices such as [[Candomblé]], Eastern religions, [[Restorationism|Christian restorationist faiths]], [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]], and others. In recent years, Protestant denominations and Spiritism have seen significant growth in the city.<ref name="Religião de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P|title=Amostra - Características gerais da população|access-date=9 February 2011|author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística}}</ref>

According to 2000 data from the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]], Itumbiara's population is composed of: [[Catholic Church|Catholics]] (44.74%); [[Pentecostalism|Evangelicals]] (39.25%); [[Irreligion|people without religion]] (7.56%); [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (7.55%); and Eastern religions (0.10%). Among Protestant churches, notable ones include the [[Assembleias de Deus|Assembleia de Deus]] (2.69%), [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]] (0.86%), and [[Presbyterianism|Presbyterian Church]] (0.44%). Among Christian restorationist denominations, [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (0.34%) and [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (0.03%) stand out. [[Umbanda]] and [[Candomblé]] together account for 0.15% of the religious population. Esoteric traditions are practiced by 0.11% of the population, and 0.04% did not declare any religion.<ref name="Religião 3">{{Cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P|title=Amostra - Características gerais da população (2)|access-date=9 February 2011|author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística}}</ref>

== Politics ==
The [[executive branch]] of Itumbiara's municipal government is led by the [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]], supported by a cabinet of [[Secretary of state|secretaries]] and equivalent directors, elected through direct voting for a four-year term, with the possibility of re-election for a second consecutive term.<ref name="CâmaraItumbiaraGO_LeiOrgânica"/><ref name="GovMunicipal">{{Cite web|url=http://www.guiadedireitos.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1061&Itemid=279|title=Governo Municipal|access-date=15 June 2014|archive-date=10 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111010182828/http://www.guiadedireitos.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1061&Itemid=279|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/Emendas/Emc/emc16.htm|title=EMENDA CONSTITUCIONAL Nº 16, DE 04 DE JUNHO DE 1997|date=4 June 1997|publisher=Planalto|access-date=9 March 2014}}</ref> The current head of the municipal executive is Francisco Domingues de Faria, affiliated with the Brazilian Labor Party (PTB), elected in the 2012 municipal elections with {{formatnum:59.32|%}} of valid votes.<ref name="prefeito">{{Cite web |author= |date=7 October 2012 |title=Itumbiara GO - Apuração 1º turno (prefeito) |url=http://g1.globo.com/go/goias/apuracao/itumbiara.html |access-date=7 October 2012 |website=G1}}</ref>

The [[legislative branch]] is exercised by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of thirteen councilors elected for four-year terms.<ref name="CâmaraItumbiaraGO_LeiOrgânica"/> In the current legislature, which began in 2013, the chamber consists of three seats from the [[Brazilian Labour Party (1981)|Brazilian Labor Party]] (PTB), two from the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB), one from the [[Workers' Party (Brazil)|Workers' Party]] (PT), one from the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB), one from the [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|Social Democratic Party]] (PSD), one from the [[Social Christian Party (Brazil)|Social Christian Party]] (PSC), one from the [[Liberal Party (Brazil, 2006)|Liberal Party]] (PR), one from the [[Communist Party of Brazil]] (PCdoB), one from the [[Cidadania|Popular Socialist Party]] (PPS), and one from the [[Democrats (Brazil)|Democrats]] (DEM).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://eleicoes.uol.com.br/2012/raio-x/vereadores/?city=%20Itumbiara&uf=GO&id=5211503|title=Raio-X das Câmaras Municipais|author=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral|date=2012|publisher=Eleições UOL 2012|access-date=22 June 2014}}</ref> The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law).<ref name="CâmaraItumbiaraGO_LeiOrgânica">{{Cite web |date=1990 |title=Lei Orgânica do Município de Itumbiara |url=https://leismunicipais.com.br/lei-organica-itumbiara-go |access-date=22 June 2014 |publisher=Câmara Municipal de Itumbiara}}</ref>

Itumbiara is the seat of a third-tier ''[[comarca]]'' with four courts: two civil, one criminal, and one special court.<ref name="comarca">{{Cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/painel/historico.php?lang=&codmun=521150&search=goias |title=Itumbiara (GO) - Histórico |author=cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=26 February 2015 }}</ref> According to the [[Superior Electoral Court]] (TSE), as of December 2013, the municipality had {{formatnum:70687}} voters, representing {{formatnum:1.634|%}} of Goiás's total.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/consulta-quantitativo|title=Eleições / Estatísticas eleitorais / Consulta Quantitativo|publisher=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral|access-date=22 June 2014|archive-date=10 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140610023817/http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/consulta-quantitativo|url-status=dead}}</ref> The first political party in Itumbiara was the Democrata Santaritense, founded by Major Militão Pereira de Almeida and Colonel Sidney Pereira de Almeida.<ref name="Artes em Itumbiara 1" />

Regarding political representation, [[List of political parties in Brazil|32 of Brazil's 35 political parties]] are represented in the municipality.<ref name="Partidos políticos">{{Cite web |url=http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/filiados |title=Filiados - Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE) |publisher=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE) |date=April 2016 |access-date=29 May 2016 |archive-date=9 April 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140409121156/http://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas/filiados |url-status=dead }}</ref> According to data from the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) as of April 2016, the political party with the most members in Itumbiara is the [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|Brazilian Democratic Movement Party]] (PMDB), with {{formatnum:1714}} members, followed by the [[Progressistas|Progressive Party]] (PP) with {{formatnum:1671}} members, and the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB) with {{formatnum:1396}} members. Rounding out the top five parties by membership are the [[Workers' Party (Brazil)|Workers' Party]] (PT) with {{formatnum:1094}} members and the [[Brazilian Socialist Party]] (PSB) with {{formatnum:459}} members. The [[Free Fatherland Party (Brazil)|Free Fatherland Party]] (PPL) and the [[Brazilian Communist Party]] (PCB) have the least representation, with one member each.<ref name="Partidos políticos"/> Three parties have no representation in Itumbiara: [[Workers' Cause Party]] (PCO), [[New Party (Brazil)|New Party]] (NOVO), and the [[Brazilian Woman's Party]] (PMB).<ref name="Partidos políticos" />

== Economy ==
Itumbiara's [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) is the seventh largest in [[Goiás]]. It excels particularly in the [[service sector]]. According to 2018 data from the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), the municipality's GDP was R$ {{formatnum:4187512.28|million}}, with a per capita GDP of R$ {{formatnum:49832.28}},<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021" /> placing it 16th among municipalities in the [[Central-West Region, Brazil|Central-West]] and 261st among Brazilian municipalities in terms of [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]].<ref name="Economia de Itumbiara 2">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/economia/pibmunicipios/2005_2009/tabelas_pdf/tab08.pdf |title=Tabela 8 - Posição ocupada pelos 30 maiores municípios em relação ao Produto Interno Bruto apreços correntes e participações percentuais relativa e acumulada dos municípios nas Grandes Regiões, segundo os municípios e as respectivas Unidades da Federação - 2009 |date=2009 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=25 January 2015}}</ref><ref name="Economia de Itumbiara 3">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/comparamun/compara.php?lang=&order=dado&dir=desc |title=Produto interno bruto dos municípios - 2012 Pib a preços correntes » comparação entre os municípios escolhidos |date=2012 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=25 January 2015 }}</ref>

Of the city's total GDP, R$ {{formatnum:245834|million}} comes from the gross value added by agriculture (primary sector), R$ {{formatnum:911098|million}} from industry (secondary sector), R$ {{formatnum:406492|million}} from taxes on products net of subsidies, and R$ {{formatnum:1511568|million}} from service provision (tertiary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021" /> The service sector is currently the primary contributor to Itumbiara's GDP.

Itumbiara is also one of Goiás's most competitive municipalities. Its easy access to the [[South Region, Brazil|South]] and [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast]] of Brazil facilitates product distribution. This is a key factor in the production, expansion, and establishment of new agro-industries in the Diagri – Itumbiara Agroindustrial District, fostering local economic growth and development.<ref name="SEPLAN" /> Itumbiara is Goiás's largest exporter.<ref name="Itumbiara3" />

=== Primary sector ===
; Agriculture
Located in the fertile valley of the [[Paranaíba River]], Itumbiara relies on agriculture as a primary source of income. Although it remains a significant part of the local economy, agriculture is gradually losing ground to industry.<ref name="comarca" />

According to the 1996 Agricultural Census, Itumbiara's cultivated area included {{formatnum:50489}} hectares of temporary crops, {{formatnum:720}} hectares of permanent crops, {{formatnum:17085}} hectares of natural pastures, {{formatnum:106946}} hectares of artificial (planted) pastures, {{formatnum:20222}} hectares of natural and artificial forests, and {{formatnum:2132}} hectares of irrigated crops. The total area is {{formatnum:246460}} hectares, of which {{formatnum:202610}} are suitable for agriculture and pasture formation.<ref name="comarca" /> In 1996, the municipality had {{formatnum:1240}} rural properties, {{formatnum:981}} tractors, {{formatnum:755}} mechanically powered plows, {{formatnum:19}} animal-drawn plows, {{formatnum:141}} harvesters, and {{formatnum:3645}} planting machines.<ref name="comarca" />

Itumbiara has 39 dry storage and warehousing facilities, sufficient to handle the municipality's production and that of neighboring municipalities. In 1997, it had a storage capacity of {{formatnum:573500}} tons or {{formatnum:9550000}} 60-kilogram sacks, distributed as follows: {{formatnum:346141}} m<sup>3</sup> of conventional warehouses (equivalent to {{formatnum:186920}} tons), {{formatnum:193240}} tons in bulk warehouses, and {{formatnum:193340}} tons in silo warehouses.<ref name="comarca" />

In 2006, the agricultural sector generated {{formatnum:4441}} direct jobs, with {{formatnum:3548}} positions filled by men and {{formatnum:893}} by women. Of these, {{formatnum:4142}} jobs were held by individuals over 14 years old.<ref name="Agropecuária">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|title=Itumbiara (GO) - Censo Agropecuário 2006|publisher=IBGE - Cidades@|access-date=9 February 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1|archive-date=30 April 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

; Livestock
According to the 1996 Agricultural Census, Itumbiara had a bovine herd density of 66.09 heads per km<sup>2</sup>, with a total of {{formatnum:162896}} cattle. By 2006, the number of cattle had decreased to {{formatnum:120792}}, alongside {{formatnum:2271}} horses, {{formatnum:50}} buffalo, and {{formatnum:16}} donkeys.<ref name="Agropecuária" />

Other notable animals in 1996 included {{formatnum:46}} buffalo, {{formatnum:5886}} pigs, {{formatnum:756}} sheep, {{formatnum:2013}} goats, and {{formatnum:95580}} poultry (chickens, hens, roosters), producing {{formatnum:140438}} dozen eggs annually. Other birds (ducks, geese, turkeys) totaled {{formatnum:10235}} units.<ref name="comarca" />

=== Secondary sector ===
Itumbiara is a significant industrial hub at the state level. Industry plays a major role in the local economy and has been expanding in recent years. It is particularly noted for products derived from [[maize]], [[soybean]], [[cotton]], and [[milk]]. However, the primary industrial economic drivers are metallurgy, footwear, textiles, mechanics, and food processing.<ref name="comarca" />

The municipality has an industrial district along the [[BR-452 (Brazil highway)|BR-452]], with access to the [[BR-153 (Brazil highway)|BR-153]], which connects [[Goiás]], [[Minas Gerais]], [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]], and [[Brasília]]. The industrial district has stable infrastructure, including electricity, water, sewage, telephone, and asphalt. Covering approximately {{formatnum:1100000|m²}}, it is divided into blocks and streets and is located 8 kilometers from the city center. Industries benefit from the FOMENTAR program, which finances up to 70% of ICMS for 15 years at 2.6% annual interest, along with municipal incentives such as land cleanup assistance. Currently, {{formatnum:167}} industries operate in the district.<ref name="comarca" />

Itumbiara hosts several national exporting companies, including Caramuru Alimentos (soybean exporter), Louis Dreyfus Commodities (cotton derivative exporter), [[JBS S.A.|JBS]] (leather exporter), and Stemac (energy generator manufacturer).<ref name="SEPLAN" /><ref name="Stemac">{{Cite web |url=http://www.acii.org.br/Inicio/stemac_visita_acii.html |title=STEMAC Visita ACII |publisher=ACII |date=6 February 2012 |access-date=16 May 2015 |archive-date=3 March 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303213302/http://www.acii.org.br/Inicio/stemac_visita_acii.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Tertiary sector ===
Itumbiara is a commercial distribution hub, standing out in the [[Microregion of Meia Ponte|Meia Ponte Microregion]]. All 18 neighboring municipalities rely on Itumbiara's commerce, which accounts for approximately 36% of the ICMS revenue. Companies such as [[Peugeot]], [[Renault]], [[Ford Motor Company|Ford]], [[Volkswagen]], [[Fiat|FIAT]], [[Toyota]], [[Hyundai]], [[Nissan]], [[Chery]], [[Mitsubishi]], [[Chevrolet]], and [[Jeep]] serve the entire region.<ref name="comarca" /> The city also hosts retail chains such as [[Lojas Americanas]], Ricardo Eletro, [[Magazine Luiza]], [[Casas Bahia]], Pernambucanas, Drogasil, Pague Menos, and the Bretas supermarket chain.

According to the Itumbiara Tax Office in 1997, the municipality had {{formatnum:1098}} active commercial establishments, {{formatnum:1322}} suspended commercial establishments, {{formatnum:542}} service providers, {{formatnum:45}} road transport companies for cargo and passengers, {{formatnum:36}} dry storage and warehousing facilities for grains, {{formatnum:9}} banking institutions, and {{formatnum:12}} hotels with over {{formatnum:530}} beds. In 2018, it hosts one of the largest beverage companies, the Polish multinational CanPack, which operates 11 plants worldwide. Its Itumbiara facility produces over 3 million aluminum cans daily.<ref name="comarca" />

== Infrastructure ==
[[File:Ponte Affonso Pena, Itumbiara (GO).jpg|left|thumb|200px|Affonso Penna Bridge.]]

In 2000, Itumbiara had {{formatnum:24216}} [[household]]s, including [[apartment]]s, [[house]]s, and rooms. Of these, {{formatnum:15245}} were owned properties, with {{formatnum:13841}} fully paid (57.16%), {{formatnum:1404}} under acquisition (5.80%), and {{formatnum:5487}} rented (22.66%). Additionally, {{formatnum:3407}} properties were provided, with {{formatnum:1123}} supplied by employers (4.64%), {{formatnum:2284}} provided otherwise (9.43%), and {{formatnum:77}} in other forms (0.32%).<ref name="CNM 2">{{Cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/infra/mu_infra_domicilio.asp |title=Infra-Estrutura - Domicílios Particulares Permanentes e Moradores (Itumbiara) |publisher=Confederação Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |date=2010 |access-date=24 February 2011 }}</ref>

The [[water supply]] is managed by the state-owned Companhia Saneamento de Goiás S/A (SANEAGO), which oversees sanitation in 223 Goiás municipalities. In the districts, water is distributed via [[artesian well]]s, while the municipal seat relies primarily on a water treatment plant.<ref name="MP2">{{Cite web |url=http://www.mp.go.gov.br/portalweb/hp/4/docs/tac_saneago_eliminador_ar.pdf |title=SANEAGO |author=Ministério Público do Estado de Goiás |date=2005 |access-date=24 February 2011 }}</ref>

Under the [[Constitution of Brazil|1988 Brazilian Constitution]], Itumbiara's Municipal Guard, alongside the Goiás Military Police, is tasked with protecting public assets, services, and facilities. In the public interest and exercising its police powers, it works to prevent and suppress certain crimes, particularly against public property and services, and can arrest offenders ''[[in flagrante delicto]]'' and present them to a police chief, as stipulated by criminal procedure law.<ref name="segurança">{{Cite web |url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm |publisher=Planalto |title=Constituição Federal do Brasil |date=14 September 2010 |access-date= }}</ref>

=== Healthcare ===
Itumbiara has 51 healthcare facilities, including 27 private and 24 public municipal facilities, such as hospitals, [[emergency department]]s, [[Community health center|health centers]], and [[dentistry]] services. The city has 232 beds for [[hospitalization]]. There are three general hospitals, two public and one private.<ref name="saúde">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1 |title=Serviços de Saúde |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística |access-date=24 February 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120430164910/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/topwindow.htm?1 |archive-date=30 April 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The city employs 52 surgical doctors, 108 clinical doctors, 54 obstetricians, and 54 pediatricians, totaling 268. In 2008, {{formatnum:1318}} live births were recorded, with 7.4% premature, 47.6% via cesarean section, and 25.1% to mothers aged 10 to 19 (0.5% aged 10 to 14). The crude birth rate is 14.14.<ref name="Datasus_SaúdeIUB">{{Cite web |url=http://tabnet.datasus.gov.br/tabdata/cadernos/GO/GO_Itumbiara_Geral.xls |title=Caderno de Informações de Saúde - Informações Gerais |author=DATASUS |publisher=Ministério da Saúde |format=[[Microsoft Excel|xls]] |access-date=24 February 2011 |archive-date=18 May 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060518160535/http://tabnet.datasus.gov.br/tabdata/cadernos/GO/GO_Itumbiara_Geral.xls |url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Services and communications ===
[[File:RioParanaiba-Furnas-RepresadeItumbiara-2007.JPG|thumb|right|[[Itumbiara Dam]].]]
The municipality is powered by the [[Itumbiara Hydroelectric Plant]] (UHI), operational since 1981, located on the [[Paranaíba River]] between Itumbiara and [[Araporã]]. It supplies much of [[Goiás]], [[Minas Gerais]], and parts of [[Mato Grosso do Sul]]. With six operational units and a total installed capacity of 2,082 MW, it is the largest plant in the Brazilian Furnas System. Construction began in November 1974.<ref name="Energia">{{Cite web |url=http://www.furnas.com.br/hotsites/sistemafurnas/usina_hidr_itumbiara.asp |title=Usina Hidrelétrica de Furnas |author=Furnas |access-date=2 March 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180308034428/http://www.furnas.com.br/hotsites/sistemafurnas/usina_hidr_itumbiara.asp |archive-date=2018-03-08 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Itumbiara's first newspaper, owned by José Flávio Soares in 1919, was titled ''O Comércio''.<ref name="Artes em Itumbiara 1" /> The city receives [[free-to-air television]] signals from various Brazilian [[television channel|broadcasters]]. Regional broadcasters airing local programs include Rede Anhanguera, which broadcasts TV Anhanguera, affiliated with [[Rede Globo]]; [[RedeTV! Itumbiara]], affiliated with [[RedeTV!]]; [[TV Serra Dourada]], affiliated with [[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]; [[Record Goiás|TV Record Goiás]], affiliated with [[Rede Record]]; and [[TV Brasil Central]], affiliated with [[TV Cultura]].

== Transportation ==
[[File:Aeroporto de Itumbiara.jpg|thumb|right|[[Itumbiara Airport]].]]
The municipality has a strong tradition in road transportation. Located along one of Brazil's busiest corridors, Itumbiara is served by highways and roads that connect it to major urban centers such as [[Goiânia]], [[Brasília]], [[Uberlândia]], and [[São Paulo]]. The distance between the municipal seat and the state capital, Goiânia, is approximately 206 kilometers.<ref name="Distância Itumbiara - Goiânia">{{cite web |url= http://viajeaqui.abril.com.br/tracar-rota?passo=finalizar&origin_city=Itumbiara%2C+GO&o_lat=-18.400271&o_lng=-49.203098&origin=&destination_city=Goi%C3%A2nia%2C+GO&d_lat=-16.677715&d_lng=-49.26763&destination=&price-per-liter=&tank-capacity=&average-consumption=&road-option=0 |title= Traçar rota - Trace sua rota |publisher= Viaje Aqui |access-date= January 24, 2015 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20150128114717/http://viajeaqui.abril.com.br/tracar-rota?passo=finalizar&origin_city=Itumbiara%2C+GO&o_lat=-18.400271&o_lng=-49.203098&origin=&destination_city=Goi%C3%A2nia%2C+GO&d_lat=-16.677715&d_lng=-49.26763&destination=&price-per-liter=&tank-capacity=&average-consumption=&road-option=0 |archive-date= January 28, 2015 |url-status= dead }}</ref> The distance to the federal capital, Brasília, is 404 kilometers.<ref name="Distância Itumbiara - Brasília">{{cite web |url= http://viajeaqui.abril.com.br/tracar-rota?passo=finalizar&origin_city=Itumbiara%2C+GO&o_lat=-18.400271&o_lng=-49.203098&origin=&destination_city=Bras%C3%ADlia%2C+DF&d_lat=-15.780148&d_lng=-47.92917&destination=&price-per-liter=&tank-capacity=&average-consumption=&road-option=0 |title= Calcular Distância e Rota entre Cidades |publisher= Distância entre Cidades |access-date= January 24, 2015 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20150128115558/http://viajeaqui.abril.com.br/tracar-rota?passo=finalizar&origin_city=Itumbiara%2C+GO&o_lat=-18.400271&o_lng=-49.203098&origin=&destination_city=Bras%C3%ADlia%2C+DF&d_lat=-15.780148&d_lng=-47.92917&destination=&price-per-liter=&tank-capacity=&average-consumption=&road-option=0 |archive-date= January 28, 2015 |url-status= dead }}</ref>

The three main highways in Itumbiara are [[BR-153 (Brazil highway)|BR-153]], also known as the Transbrasiliana Highway, which provides access to the capitals Goiânia and Brasília; [[BR-452 (Brazil highway)|BR-452]], which connects the city to [[Rio Verde, Goiás|Rio Verde]]; and GO-419, which links to [[Buriti Alegre]].<ref name="Transporte em Itumbiara 1">{{cite web |url= http://www.brasilchannel.com.br/municipios/mostrar_municipio.asp?nome=Itumbiara&uf=GO&tipo=informacoes |title= Itumbiara (GO) - Transportes |publisher= Brasil Channel |access-date= March 2, 2011 |archive-date= January 10, 2023 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20230110181442/https://www.brasilchannel.com.br/municipios/mostrar_municipio.asp?nome=Itumbiara&uf=GO&tipo=informacoes |url-status= dead }}</ref>

Itumbiara has limited tradition in river transportation, largely due to the absence of navigable rivers within its territory. However, the [[Paranaíba River]] is extensively used for river transport, particularly for ecotourism activities.<ref name="Transporte em Itumbiara 1" /> The [[Itumbiara Airport]]—officially named Itumbiara-Francisco Vilela do Amaral Airport ([[International Civil Aviation Organization|ICAO]]: SBIT)—is operated by the municipal government. It has an asphalt runway of 2,700 meters, suitable for both day and night flights, and can accommodate large aircraft.<ref name="Transporte em Itumbiara 1" />

== Culture and society ==
=== Tourism ===
[[Ecotourism]] is the primary driver of tourism in Itumbiara, serving as a key pathway for the city's development. This is characterized by the competitive advantages of the region in this sector, with a particular emphasis on the large lake of the [[Itumbiara Dam]], which has a depth of 521.20 meters, a flooded area of 778&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>, and a total volume of 17 billion m<sup>3</sup>. This is equivalent to two [[Guanabara Bay]]s.<ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 1">{{cite web |url= http://ecoviagem.uol.com.br/brasil/goias/itumbiara/ |publisher= Eco Viagem |title= Turismo - Itumbiara |access-date= March 3, 2011}}</ref>
[[File:Vista da Beira Rio.JPG|thumb|200px|left|Night view of Itumbiara, seen from the [[Minas Gerais]] municipality of [[Araporã]].]]
In recent years, the lake area of the Paranaíba River Dam has become a popular venue for gatherings of fishermen and fishing teams engaging in sport fishing, a phenomenon highlighted in specialized magazines that rank Itumbiara as the second-best location in Brazil for sport fishing of [[peacock bass]]. The [[Paranaíba River]] also hosts various water sports.<ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 1" />

The Dr. Henrique Santillo International Kartodrome, located in the west-central part of the city, hosts regional, national, and international events. It has a capacity for {{formatnum:5000}} people.<ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 1" />

Among the municipality's main natural tourist attractions are the [[Paranaíba River]], which acts as a vast mirror reflecting the well-constructed buildings along its banks on one side and the state of [[Minas Gerais]] on the other; and the Salitre Waterfall, located in the rural area near the border with Buriti Alegre, offering trails and climbing opportunities.<ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 2">{{cite web |url= http://www.ferias.tur.br/informacoes/2204/itumbiara-go.html |publisher= Ferias Tur |title= Turismo - Principais Pontos Turísticos |access-date= March 3, 2011 }}</ref><ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 3">{{cite web |url= http://viagenspassagensaereas.com.br/pontosturisticos.asp?idtour=6181 |publisher= Viagens & Passagens Aéreas |title= Itumbiara (GO) - Cachoeira Salitre |access-date= January 25, 2015 |archive-date= March 3, 2016 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20160303165428/http://viagenspassagensaereas.com.br/pontosturisticos.asp?idtour=6181 }}</ref>

Historical tourist attractions include the Main Square (Praça Matriz), established by municipal decree No. 538 in April 1934, authored by Sidney Pereira de Almeida. At the time of its construction, it was the city's first public garden. The project was executed by engineer Eduardo Figueiredo Mendes and opened to the public on June 23, 1935. It is now called Republic Square (Praça da República); the Affonso Penna suspension bridge, built to meet the significant need for development in the southwest of Goiás and to improve connections with major centers such as [[São Paulo]] and [[Rio de Janeiro]]; and the Cyro Gomes de Almeida Bridge, constructed over the Paranaíba River in 1958 to replace the old Affonso Penna Bridge, during the period when Brazil's capital was transferred from Rio de Janeiro to Brasília in 1960. The bridge is named in honor of its builder, Cyro Gomes de Almeida.<ref name="Turismo em Itumbiara 2" />

=== Arts ===
Several theater groups operate in the municipality. In 1919, the first theater group was established in Itumbiara, making it one of the pioneers in this field in southern Goiás. It was founded and conceptualized by José Flávio Soares, a journalist from the region. That same year, the first theatrical play in the municipality, also authored by José Flávio Soares, was performed.<ref name="Artes em Itumbiara 1">{{cite web |url= http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=225 |publisher= Prefeitura Municipal de Itumbiara |title= Dados sobre os píoneiros do município |access-date= March 11, 2011 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120111111803/http://www.itumbiara.go.gov.br/v3/site/index.php?p=materias_ver&id=225 |archive-date= January 11, 2012 |url-status= dead }}</ref>

The first cinema in Itumbiara, named Cine Brasil, was owned by Sidney Pereira de Almeida. The movie "The Ace of the West", a cowboy movie, was shown there. It was the first movie shown in the city.<ref name="Artes em Itumbiara 1" />

=== Events ===
Itumbiara hosts numerous events annually, some of which are well known among the local population. The city is home to significant religious and cultural events. Notable among them are the feast of [[Saint Sebastian]], held on January 20; the feast of [[Rita of Cascia|Saint Rita of Cascia]], in May; and the Christ the King Fair, held between May 1 and May 10.<ref name="Transporte em Itumbiara 1" /> The fluvial procession of Our Lady of Grace, held in August, has been celebrated for over 50 years.<ref name="Eventos em Itumbiara 2">{{cite web |url= http://www.radioparanaiba.com.br/noticias/noticias-itumbiara/festa-de-nossa-senhor-das-gra-as-tem-encerramento-com-prociss-o-fluvial-e-missa-campal.html |publisher= Rádio Paranaíba |title= Festa de Nossa Senhor das Graças tem encerramento com procissão fluvial e missa campal |date= August 19, 2014 |access-date= January 25, 2015 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20150603015945/http://www.radioparanaiba.com.br/noticias/noticias-itumbiara/festa-de-nossa-senhor-das-gra-as-tem-encerramento-com-prociss-o-fluvial-e-missa-campal.html |archive-date= June 3, 2015 |url-status= dead }}</ref><ref name="Eventos em Itumbiara 3">{{cite web |url= http://globotv.globo.com/tv-anhanguera-go/bom-dia-go/v/fieis-participam-da-procissao-fluvial-de-nossa-senhora-das-gracas-em-itumbiara/2097694/ |publisher= Bom Dia Goiás - TV Globo |title= Fiéis participam da procissão fluvial de Nossa Senhora das Graças, em Itumbiara |date= August 19, 2014 |access-date= January 25, 2015 }}</ref> The city's agricultural fair, held in September, is another cultural highlight. Additional events include the Ecological Week in June, the South Goiás Industry and Commerce Fair (Expossul), hosted in the city, and the municipality's anniversary, celebrated on October 12.<ref name="Transporte em Itumbiara 1" />

=== Football ===
[[File:Itumbiara X Corinthians 2009.jpg|thumb|200px|[[Itumbiara Esporte Clube]] against [[Sport Club Corinthians Paulista|Corinthians]] in the [[2009 Copa do Brasil]].]]
In football, the most important club in Itumbiara is [[Itumbiara Esporte Clube]], founded on March 9, 1970. Its headquarters are located on Goiânia Street in the Historic Center of Itumbiara. The club won its first state title in 2003, becoming the Goiás Second Division Champion. Since 2004, it has competed in the First Division of the Goiás Football Championship. In 1979, it played its first official match in the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|Campeonato Brasileiro]], finishing in 64th place. The club also participated in the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série B|Taça de Prata]] in 1980, 1983, and 1984; the [[1981 Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Taça de Bronze]] in 1981; and the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Third Division]] in 1987, 1994, 1995, 1997, and 1998.<ref name="Futebol em Itumbiara 2">{{cite web |url= http://www.arquivodeclubes.com/go/itumbiara.htm |publisher= Arquivos de Clubes |title= Itumbiara Esporte Clube |access-date= February 22, 2011 |archive-url= https://archive.today/20121205231641/http://www.arquivodeclubes.com/go/itumbiara.htm |archive-date= December 5, 2012 |url-status= dead }}</ref> It won the [[Campeonato Goiano|Goiás Football Championship]] in 2008, earning the right to compete in the [[2009 Copa do Brasil]], where it faced [[Sport Club Corinthians Paulista|Corinthians]].

The football club plays its matches at the [[Estádio JK|Juscelino Kubitschek Municipal Stadium]], located in the Planalto neighborhood. The stadium has a capacity of {{formatnum:33000}} people. It was inaugurated on October 10, 1976, against [[Club de Regatas Vasco da Gama]]. Its record attendance was in 2008, when Itumbiara faced [[Goiás Esporte Clube]], witnessed by {{formatnum:31000}} spectators.<ref name="Futebol em Itumbiara 3">{{cite web |url= http://mavalem.sites.uol.com.br/go/Itumbiara.htm |publisher= Templos do Futebol |title= Estádio Municipal Juscelino Kubitschek |access-date= February 22, 2011 }}</ref>
