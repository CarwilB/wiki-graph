'''Jesús''', also called Barrio Jesús, is a [[Districts of Costa Rica|district]] of the [[Santa Bárbara (canton)|Santa Bárbara]] canton, in the [[Heredia Province|Heredia]] province of [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019 |archive-date=24 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240224063957/https://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |lang=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The district consists of several large neighborhoods: Altagracia, Birrí, Catalina, Común, Cuesta Colorada, La Máquina, Guachipelines, Guaracha, and Ulises.

==History==
Like the rest of the canton, before the Spanish settlers came, Jesús was originally occupied by the Huetares, an indigenous tribe. The Huetare King, Cacique Garabito, dominated the area.<ref name=RHC>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|accessdate=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es|archive-date=22 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522195138/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In early 1663, settler Joseph de Sandoval Ocampo arrived in the region of Jesús in order to set up a cattle farm.<ref name=RHSBH>{{cite news|last1=Hernández Vargas|first1=Rose Marie|title=Reseña histórica de Santa Bárbara de Heredia|url=http://www.uned.ac.cr/extension/index.php/extension-en-accion/identidad-cultural/105-resena-historica-de-santa-barbara-de-heredia|accessdate=5 June 2014|agency=Universidad Estatal a Distancia|publisher=Extensión en acción|date=6 November 2013|language=Es}}</ref> [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]], [[Barva]], and [[Alajuela (city)|Alajuela]], three neighboring cities, were populated and settled in the late 1700s. As trade increased between the three cities, the canton developed.<ref name=centenario>{{cite book|title=Centenario del cantón Santa Bárbara de Heredia|year=1982|url=http://santabarbara.co.cr/modules/notas/seo.php/item.49/centenario-del-canton-santa-barbara-heredia.html}}{{Dead link|date=March 2023 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

Jesús officially became a district in the canton of Santa Bárbara on 7 December 1848.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|url=http://pnud.or.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1192%3Asanta-barbara&catid=65&Itemid=106|accessdate=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=25 March 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140325003616/http://www.pnud.or.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1192%3Asanta-barbara&catid=65&Itemid=106|url-status=dead}}</ref> This official recognition from the national government came when Santa Bárbara was declared the fourth canton of [[Heredia Province]], thanks to the efforts of Gregorio Salazar.<ref name=RHC />

By 1852, resident Horacio Morales had successfully lobbied Jesúseños to construct a small chapel in the district.<ref name=centenario /> By 1885, there were two public schools for children in Jesús.<ref name=centenario /> One was for boys and one was for girls.<ref name=centenario /> Jesús was one of the last of Santa Bárbara's districts to receive running water, sometime after 1911.<ref name=RHC /> It was electrified in 1937.<ref name=centenario />

==Geography and wildlife==
Jesús has an area of {{formatnum:11.18}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020 |archive-date=24 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024225203/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:1160}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

Major neighborhoods include Calle Solís, Mitad Sur de la Cuesta Colorada (Lower Red Hill), Quebrada la Cruz (Cruz River), Urbanización Cifuentes, Calle de Trapiche, Rosa Blanca (White Rose), La Teofila, and Central Jesús.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> In the northwest of the district are the neighborhoods of Birrí, La Catalina, La Cuesta Colorada (Red Hill), and San José de Altagracia.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

Like much of Costa Rica, the Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos (National Risk Prevention Commission) puts Jesús in a high-risk zone.<ref name=Emergencias>{{cite web|title=AMENAZAS NATURALES CANTON DE SANTA BARBARA|url=http://www.cne.go.cr/Atlas%20de%20Amenazas/STABARBARA.htm|website=Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos y Atención de Emergencias|publisher=Comisión Nacional de Prevención de Riesgos y Atención de Emergencias|accessdate=5 June 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es|archive-date=2 December 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131202062932/http://www.cne.go.cr/Atlas%20de%20Amenazas/STABARBARA.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> This is due to the district's mountainous geography, which can lead to landslides and flooding.<ref name=Emergencias /> Several large rivers run through Jesús. These include Quebrada Burros, Quebrada Birrí, Río Porrosati, and Quebrada Cruz.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

Over 60 different species of birds have been observed in Jesús.<ref name=OTNCRC>{{cite web|title=Birdwatching Villas in Costa Rica|url=http://www.orbitcostarica.com/bwvilla.htm|website=Orbit Costa Rica|publisher=Orbit Travel Network Costa Rica|accessdate=5 June 2014|year=1997}}</ref>

==Economy==
[[File:La Maquina Jesús de Santa Bárbara de Heredia.jpg|thumb|Fruit stand in La Maquina Neighborhood of Jesús de Santa Bárbara (de Heredia)]]
The backbone of Jesús' economy is agricultural production, particularly [[Coffee production in Costa Rica|coffee]].<ref name=centenario /> In 1973, 1,810 kilograms of coffee were produced in Jesús. In addition, Jesús is a major producer of [[sugarcane]].<ref name=centenario /> There is an active recycling group in the district.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

==Education and public services==
An EBAIS, [[Costa Rican Department of Social Security|Equipo Básico de Atención Integral en Salud (Basic Medical Care Center)]], is located in Jesús, serving residents and non-residents of the district.<ref name="sbd" /> The [[Catholic Church]] serves the region through the Archdiocese of Alajuela.<ref>{{cite web|title=Parroquia Santa Bárbara (Santa Bárbara de Heredia)|url=http://www.diocesisdealajuela.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=109&Itemid=508|website=Diócesis de Alajuela|accessdate=5 June 2014|location=Alajuela, Costa Rica|date=17 October 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221213211/http://www.diocesisdealajuela.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=109&Itemid=508|archive-date=21 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

There are now three elementary schools in the district: Alfredo Volio Jiménez, located in Birrí, Alfredo González Flores, located on highway 126, and Escuela Jesús in central Jesús. Students from the district usually attend Colegio Santa Bárbara High School,<ref name="MEPSB">Ministerio de Educación Pública: [http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara Santa Bárbara | Ministerio de Educación Pública] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140520215916/http://www.mep.go.cr/centros-educativos/santa-barbara |date=2014-05-20 }}, accessdate: 27 May 2014</ref> but may also go to the high schools in [[Barva]] or [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]]. Jesús is home to a private language school: The Amistad Institute. The Amistad Institute has a five-acre campus close to the center of Jesús.<ref>{{cite web|title=Amistad Institute, Santa Bárbara de Heredia, Costa Rica |url=http://www.spanish-direct.com/spanish-schools/Costa%20Rica/Heredia/Santa%20B%E1rbara%20de%20Heredia/435/?PHPSESSID=8ae2454382d1ef4d011206b2d85b9197 |publisher=Spanish-Direct.com |accessdate=5 June 2014 |format=Directory website |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150323054815/http://www.blayney.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/329/3h%20-%20Hobbys%20Yards%20Heritage%20Items.pdf.aspx |archivedate=23 March 2015 }}</ref>
