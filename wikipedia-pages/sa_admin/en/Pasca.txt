'''Pasca''' is a town and municipality in the [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]] department of [[Colombia]] located in the [[Andes]]. It belongs to the [[Sumapaz Province]]. Pasca is situated on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]] at a distance of {{convert|71|km|mi}} from the capital [[Bogotá]]. It borders [[Fusagasugá]], [[Sibaté]] and [[Soacha]] in the north, Bogotá D.C. in the north and east, [[Arbeláez]] in the south and Fusagasugá in the west. Is the entrance to the Páramo del Sumapaz, the biggest ecosystem in its genre in the world. The urban center is located at an altitude of {{convert|2180|m|ft}} and the altitude ranges from {{convert|2000|m|ft}} to {{convert|3500|m|ft}}.<ref name=websitePasca>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.pasca-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Pasca] {{Webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20150522003332/http://www.pasca-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml |date=2015-05-22 }}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Pasca in the [[Chibcha language]] means "father's enclosure", according to Acosta Ortegón.<ref name=EspejoOlaya>{{in lang|es}} {{aut|Espejo Olaya, Maria Bernarda}} (1999) [http://cvc.cervantes.es/lengua/thesaurus/pdf/54/TH_54_003_424_0.pdf Notas sobre toponimia en algunas coplas colombianas] Thesaurus. Tomo LIV, Núm. 3. page 1122.</ref>

== History ==
Pasca in the time before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]] was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]], organized in their [[Muisca Confederation]]. The southern Muisca territories were ruled from [[Bacatá|Muyquytá]], the current capital. On April 6, 1536 [[conquistador]]s [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] and [[Hernán Pérez de Quesada|his brother]] started the strenuous march into the inner highlands of Colombia. With 209 men he arrived on March 12, 1537, in [[Guachetá]]. From there he led his army to conquer the villages of the Muisca on the [[Bogotá savanna]].<ref name=websitePasca/>

One of his captains, [[Juan de Céspedes Ruiz|Juan de Céspedes]], reached Pasca in July 1537, founding modern Pasca on July 15.<ref name=websitePasca/> It was the last village of the Muisca to be conquered before heading south into the domain of the [[Sutagao people|Sutagao]].<ref name=EspejoOlaya/>

== Economy ==
Main economical activities in Pasca are [[livestock]] farming and [[agriculture]], predominantly [[Solanum phureja|papa criolla]], other [[potato]]es, [[pea]]s, [[onion]]s, [[Allium fistulosum|bunching onions]], [[tree tomato]]es, [[bean]]s, [[carrot]]s, [[cabbage]], [[lettuce]], [[tomato]]es, [[maize|corn]], [[blackberry|blackberries]], [[coriander]] and the Colombian fruits [[gulupa]] and [[curuba]].<ref name=websitePasca/>

== Archeology ==
The famous [[Muisca raft]], traditionally interpreted as representing the ritual of ''[[El Dorado]]'', was found in Pasca in 1969.<ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://banrep.org/museo/esp/expo_bogota4.htm Banco de la Republica: Exposición del Museo del Oro del Banco de la República en Bogotá]</ref><ref>{{in lang|es}} [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcXgRApLxfM Explanation of the metallurgy of the Muisca raft by Eduardo Londoño] - [[Gold Museum, Bogotá|Museo del Oro]]</ref> The raft is now part of the [[Gold Museum, Bogotá|Gold Museum]] collection in [[Bogotá]].

The town contains an [[archaeology|archaeological]] [[museum]] and a natural history museum.
