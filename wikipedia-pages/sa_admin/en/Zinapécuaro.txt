'''Zinapécuaro''' is a [[Municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Michoacán]], located {{convert|50|km}} northeast of the state capital [[Morelia]].<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Geography==
The municipality of Zinapécuaro is located in northeast Michoacán on the border with [[Guanajuato]]. In Michoacán it borders the municipalities of [[Álvaro Obregón Municipality|Álvaro Obregón]] to the west, [[Indaparapeo]] and [[Queréndaro]] to the southwest, [[Ciudad Hidalgo, Michoacán|Hidalgo]] to the southeast, and [[Maravatío]] to the east. To the north it borders the municipality of [[Acámbaro]] in Guanajuato.<ref name=infocard/> Zinapécuaro covers an area of {{convert|598.179|km2}} and comprises 1.0% of the state's area.<ref name=survey/> 

The flat western part of the municipality lies in the [[Lake Cuitzeo]] basin. Along the basin's eastern edge are a series of hills and ridges where the municipal seat is located. The Ucareo Valley in the eastern part of the municipality comprises part of an ancient caldera, and is over {{convert|700|m}} higher in elevation than Lake Cuitzeo. It is an agricultural area flanked by forested hills and ridges.<ref name=obsidian>{{cite journal |last1=Healan |first=Dan M. |date=1997 |title=Pre-Hispanic Quarrying in the Ucareo-Zinapecuaro Obsidian Source Area |doi=10.1017/S0956536100001590 |journal=Ancient Mesoamerica |volume=8 |issue=1 |pages=77–100 }}</ref><ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Zinapécuaro, Michoacán de Ocampo |language=es |publisher=INEGI |access-date=3 February 2018 |archive-date=4 August 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110804015215/http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Zinapécuaro's climate is temperate with summer rains.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average annual precipitation in the municipality ranges between 700 and 1600 millimetres.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
Two [[Purépecha language|Purépecha]] words have been suggested as possible sources of the place name Zinapécuaro: ''tzinapo'' "[[obsidian]]" or ''tzinápecua'' "healing".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.zinapecuaro.gob.mx/tu-municipio.html |title=Toponimia |language=es |publisher=Government of Zinapécuaro |access-date=3 February 2018}}</ref> The obsidian quarries near Ucareo include one of the largest known pre-Hispanic quarries in [[Mesoamerica]] and were already [[Obsidian use in Mesoamerica|exploited]] in the early [[Mesoamerican chronology#Preclassic Era or Formative Period|Formative period]]. In the late Classic period Ucareo was the principal source of obsidian at [[Tula (Mesoamerican site)|Tula]] and [[Xochicalco]], and Ucareo obsidian was distributed throughout central Mexico, [[Oaxaca]] and the northern [[Yucatán Peninsula|Yucatán]], being found as far away as [[Chichen Itza]].<ref name=obsidian/> In the Postclassic period the area was controlled by the [[Tarascan state|Tarascans]], who built a temple there to worship {{Interlanguage link multi|Cuerauáperi|qid=Q5793844}}, the [[mother goddess]] of [[Purépecha deities|Purépecha mythology]].<ref name=encyclopedia/>

Around 1530, a Spanish settlement was founded at Zinapécuaro by the [[conquistador]] Don Luis Montañez.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Zinapécuaro was first incorporated on 15 March 1825 as a ''partido'' in the department of Oriente in Michoacán. It became a free municipality on 5 February 1918.<ref>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Michoacán de Ocampo. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |pages=59, 232–233 |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |isbn=970-13-1501-4 |archive-date=2019-04-12 |access-date=2018-02-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190412021950/http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and ten trustees (''regidores''), six elected by [[relative majority]] and four by [[proportional representation]]. The current president of the municipality is Alejandro Correa.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Zinapécuaro recorded a population of 46,666 inhabitants living in 11,608 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=16&mun=110 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Zinapécuaro |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |access-date=3 February 2018 |archive-date=25 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200125232353/http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave |url-status=dead }}</ref> It recorded a population of 47,327 inhabitants in the 2015 Intercensal Survey.<ref name=survey/> 

There are 97 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> four of which are classified as urban:
*Zinapécuaro de Figueroa, the seat of the municipality located in its west-central part, which recorded a population of 15,875 inhabitants in the 2010 Census;
*Jeráhuaro, a town located {{convert|30|km}} east of the municipal seat in the Ucareo Valley, which had 2822 inhabitants in 2010;
*Ucareo, a town located {{convert|27|km}} east of the municipal seat, which had 2284 inhabitants in 2010;
*Bocaneo, a town located {{convert|3|km}} south of the municipal seat, which had 2082 inhabitants in 2010.<ref name=catalog/><ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Economy==
Zinapécuaro's economy is largely dependent on foreign remittances.<ref>{{cite news |last=Paz |first=Fátima |url=http://www.cambiodemichoacan.com.mx/nota-239759 |title=70% de la economía de Zinapécuaro depende del envío de remesas familiares: alcalde |language=es |newspaper=Cambio de Michoacán |date=11 November 2014 |access-date=3 February 2018 |archive-date=3 February 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180203123959/http://www.cambiodemichoacan.com.mx/nota-239759 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Beauregard |first=Luis Pablo |url=https://elpais.com/internacional/2016/11/15/mexico/1479175712_638788.html |title=México teme por el futuro de sus remesas tras el triunfo de Trump |language=es |newspaper=[[El País]] |date=15 November 2016 |access-date=3 February 2018}}</ref> Fruit is produced in the area around Jeráhuaro and Ucareo in the eastern part of the municipality.<ref name=encyclopedia/>
