''' Tecolotlán''' ({{langx|nah|Tecolotlán}}: "town of owls")<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> is a town and [[Municipalities of Jalisco|municipality]] in the [[Administrative divisions of Mexico|State]] of [[Jalisco]], central-western [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 765.1&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

As of 2005, the municipality had a total population of 14,984.<ref name="Enciclopedia">{{cite web|title=Tecolotlán |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14088a.htm |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |access-date=April 16, 2009 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070626042748/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/jalisco/mpios/14088a.htm |archive-date=June 26, 2007 }}</ref>

== History ==
{{Main|History of Jalisco|Mexican War of Independence}}
{{Further|New Spain|Second Federal Republic of Mexico|Spanish colonization of the Americas}}

The name of Tecolotlán derives from the words ''tecolote'' ("owl") and ''tlán'' ("town"), meaning "town of owls".<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> The current coat of arms, formally approved of by the municipal council on 27 April 1999 ordinary, designed by Ernesto García de Alba Cruz, has a figure of an owl on it with outstretched wings representing the municipality. 

At the base of the shield is the name of the municipality and its founding date of 1524 when the [[Spaniards|Spanish]] ''[[conquistador]]'' Francisco Cortes invaded the area of modern-day [[San Buenaventura, Jalisco|San Buenaventura]]. The area was placed under the command of his trustees, Pedro Gómez and Martín Monje.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

The first efforts to [[Catholic Church and colonialism|convert the native population to Roman Catholicism]] took place shortly after, between 1525 and 1526 under the direction of [[Franciscans|Franciscan friar]] [[Juan de Padilla]]. Construction of the Sanctuaria de la Purisima to this end was started in 1526. In 1599, the [[convent]] of San Agustin de Tecolotlán was founded.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

In 1825, Tecolotlán included the towns of [[Xuchitl]] and [[Ayotitlan]], as well as farmers and ranchers from San Juan Buenavista, Quila, Tenextitlán, Agua Caliente, Santa Maria, San Jose, and Santa Rita.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

A decree of July 9, 1835 formally established the municipality of Tecolotlán. However at this time the place was barely more than a small village and only 2,600 homes were recorded in 1843.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

On June 23, 1844, the town hall was renovated in Tecolotlán in compliance with the decree No. 5 of the State Congress passed on April 8 of the same year. 

Local legend states that during the [[Second Franco-Mexican War]], the Free Battalion of Tecolotlán took part in the 1866 ambush of Imperialist forces under the command of French Captain [[Alfredo Berthelin]]. The French were defeated and Berthelin killed.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

== The town ==
[[File:Museo Comunitario de Tecolotlán.JPG|thumb|right|The {{ill|Museo Comunitario de Tecolotlán|es|}}]]

The town is fairly modern, and most of its residents have access to drinking water, sewage, and public restrooms. As of 2005, 89.6% of people have drinking water, 77.8% have proper sanitation and 97.5% have electricity.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

The streets, markets, parking lots and sports centers in Tecolotlan are electrically lit. There are also gardens, cemeteries, and public parks. The town has a post office, telegraph office, telephones, fax, radio station, and television station.

The La Purísima Sanctuary is dedicated to the Virgin of Tecolotlán. It was built between 1821 and 1869.<ref name="perfil">{{Cite report |title=Perfil Turistico del Municipio de Tecolotlan, Jalisco Potencialidad Turistica |url=http://visita.jalisco.gob.mx/espanol/dependencia/docs/estudios/tecolotlan.pdf |author= Secretaria de Turismo de Jalisco |date= 2007-07-15 |publisher=State of Jalisco |access-date=March 23, 2010 }}</ref>

The current Temple of La Santisima Trinidad dates from the 19th century, and has a three-foiled cusped arch with star and plant decorated pediment above. It was originally built as the Parish of San Agustin by the [[Franciscans]] in 1599, but when the church was rebuilt, it was dedicated to the Holy Trinity.<ref name="perfil"/>

The Tecolotlan Cultural Center building was constructed in the mid 19th century by Serapio Pérez. Architectural elements include arches with Corinthian style capitols. The center hosts art exhibitions as well as exhibitions of fossils and other antiquities.<ref name="perfil"/>

[[Carnaval]] is celebrated in Tecolotlan with music and dancing in the main square of town.
Men traditionally serenade women, invite them to dance, and confetti-filled eggshells called ''[[cascarones]]'' are thrown. The festival lasts ten days and also includes [[Cockfighting|cockfights]], fireworks, and the crowning of a Carnaval Queen.<ref name="perfil"/>

The patron saint festivities are celebrated from August 20–30, to worship the patron saints of Tecolotlán: St. Augustine and Santa Rosa de Lima.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> Festivities also include carnival rides such as a ferris wheel, etcetera.

== The municipality ==
Tecolotlán is located in the midwest of the state at an altitude of 1285 meters above sea level. 

The municipality, which covers an area of 795.55 square kilometres is bordered on the north by the municipalities of [[Atengo]], [[Ameca, Jalisco|Ameca]], and [[San Martín de Hidalgo]], to the east by the municipalities of [[San Martín de Hidalgo]], [[Cocula, Jalisco|Cocula]], [[Atemajac de Brizuela]], and [[Chiquilistlan]], to the south by the municipalities of [[Chiquilistlan]], [[Juchitlán]], and [[Tenamaxtlán]], and to the west by the municipalities of [[Tenamaxtlán]] and [[Atengo]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

The municipality contains 47 localities, the most important being Tecolotlán (the capital), [[Tamazulita, Jalisco|Tamazulita]], [[Quila, Jalisco|Quila]], [[Ayotitlán]], and [[Cofradía de Duendes]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

=== Topography ===
The municipal area is in a valley between hills to the north and southeast, ranging from 1,200 to 2,400 meters.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> The hills include El Huehuentón (at 2000 metres), El Pichacho (at 1,700 metres), and others such as Cerro del Colotepec, Salto Colorado, El Tecolote, El Carrizal, La Coronilla, La Ventana. Cuchillos, Prieto, and Picachitos.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

=== Hydrography ===
A seasonally dry segment of the Tecolotlán river flows through the town of Tecolotlán. The largest river in the district is the [[Ferrería River]], which has a number of tributary streams such as El Jabalí, Gallinero, Tamazula, Tecolotlán, Las Canoas, Colorado, Cofradía, Sauz, and the Amarillo.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

The [[San Pedro Dam]] is located to the south of the municipality. Other water features include the El Pochote dam, Tecolotán waterfall, Salto de Santa Rosa, Salto de La Campana, Salto del Venado, Salto Seco, and Salto de La Disciplina.

=== Flora and fauna ===
The municipality is covered with 30,900 hectares of pine and oak forests with some fruit trees. In the Sierra de Quila near the towns of Tenamaxtlan, Ameca, Atengo, and San Martin Hidalgo, there is a tree named the Arbol de la Lira (Lyre Tree) which is approximately 600 years old. It is protected by the state environmental agency. The forest that surrounds the tree covers about 15,000 hectares and is guarded 24 hours a day. 

This forest is also home to the Cienega spring, which is surrounded by vegetation and has a monitoring station nearby.<ref name="perfil"/> Animals that inhabit this region are [[deer]], [[badger]], [[raccoon]], [[wolves]], [[foxes]], [[coyotes]], [[rabbit]]s, and some small [[List of reptiles of Mexico|reptiles]] and a variety of [[List of birds of Mexico|birds]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

=== Protected Natural Areas ===
[[Protected natural areas of Mexico|Protected natural areas in the municipality]] include La Ciénega spring, Las Juntas, Las Piedras de Quila, and Sierra de Quila. There is also a notable [[Palaeontology|palaeontological site]] at [[Gliptodonte]]; many unearthed artifacts are located within the "Museo Comunitario" in the main town.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/>

=== Climate ===
The [[Climate of Mexico|climate is moderately warm and semi-dry]], and dry in the autumn and winter with an average annual temperature is 23&nbsp;°C., with a maximum of 31&nbsp;°C. and minimum of 15&nbsp;°C.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> 

The rainfall falls heaviest in June and July, and a total of 773.1&nbsp;mm is received annually on average. Prevailing winds approach from the south.

=== Transportation ===
The main road in the municipality for transportation is via the [[Guadalajara]]–[[Barra de Navidad]], 107 kilometres from the state capital. Private vehicles are the primary method of travel in the region. 

=== Attractions ===
The area does not have a highly developed [[Tourism in Mexico|tourist industry]], but opportunities for [[Ecotourism in Mexico|ecotourism activities]] such as [[mountain biking]], [[horseback riding]], and [[camping]] are available. The Sierra de Quila and other natural features provide scenic landscapes and views. 

There are two principle dams with [[artificial lake]]s, the Presa del Ahogado and the Presa del Pochote. Both have facilities for [[camping]] and [[picnicking]], as well as docks for [[boating]] and [[fishing]]. The Presa del Pochote is particularly popular with residents of the nearby city of Guadalajara.<ref name="perfil"/>

=== Religion ===
The main religion is [[Roman Catholicism]],<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> and notable [[Catholic Church in Mexico|Roman Catholic chapels and parishes]] within the municipality include the Parroquia del Sagrado Corazón, Santuario de la Purísima, Capilla de San José, Ermita de San Genaro, Capilla de la Cruz Verde, Capilla del Señor del Socorro, Capilla de San José María Robles, and the Parroquia de la Virgencita.

== Economy ==
{{Main|Economy of Jalisco}}

The economy is largely based in agriculture and ranching. Livestock reared include [[beef]], [[goats]], [[horses]], [[pigs]], and [[honeybees]]. Some of the regions' livestock produce [[dairy products]], such as [[Cheeses of Mexico|cheese]] and [[cream]].<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> Fishermen catch [[carp]] and [[Bass (fish)|bass]] in the rivers and lakes. 

Crops grown in the region include [[Maize|corn]], [[chickpeas]], [[alfalfa]], [[peaches]], [[avocado]], [[mango]], and [[pitaya]]s.<ref name="Enciclopedia"/> As of 2005 there were approximately 30,900 hectares of forest. Some segments of these forests are logged for [[Oak wood|oak]] and [[Pine wood|pine]] wood.

The municipality produces basketry, pottery, clay pots, and wooden furniture. There is also some [[Manufacturing in Mexico|manufacturing]] and [[Mining in Mexico|mining]] activity, with lime and cement factories and mining of [[barite]], [[Lime (material)|lime]], [[limestone]], [[marble]], and [[quartz]].

==Notable people==
{{more citations needed|section|date=April 2018}}

* [[:es:Juan Salvador Agraz|Juan Salvador Agraz]] – Chemical engineer, founder of the National School of Chemical Science (now, Faculty of Chemistry in the [[National Autonomous University of Mexico]] (UNAM).
* [[:es:Gabriel Agraz García de Alba|Gabriel Agraz García de Alba]] – Historian.
* [[:es:Ruperto García de Alba|Ruperto García de Alba]] – General and acting governor of Jalisco, 1930-1931.
* Esteban García de Alba Larios – Attorney at Law, politician, president of the Mexican Senate during the government of [[Manuel Ávila Camacho]]. Founder of Pensiones del Estado de Jalisco. He built several multi-family homes, some of them in Tlatelolco. Ambassador representing Mexico before the [[League of Nations]].
* [[Ana Bertha Lepe]] – actress and [[Miss Mexico]] in 1954.
