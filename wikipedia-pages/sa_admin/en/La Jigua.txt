'''La Jigua''' is a [[Municipalities of Honduras|municipality]] in the [[Departments of Honduras|department]] of [[Copán Department|Copán]] in western Honduras, located approximately {{convert|30|km}} north of the departmental capital, [[Santa Rosa de Copán]]. Its name ultimately derives from [[Nahuatl]] ''Xihuacan'', "place that has turquoise."<ref name=secoff>{{cite web |last=Secoff |first=Mario |url=https://www.angelfire.com/ca5/mas/dpmapas/cop/jig/jig.html |title=Departamento de Copán: Municipio de La Jigua |language=es |work=Honduras Universal |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

==Geography==
The municipality of La Jigua straddles the [[Chamelecón River]] valley at its point of confluence with the [[Tepemechín River]]. It borders the municipalities of [[Florida, Copán|Florida]] to the west and north, [[Nueva Arcadia]] to the east, and [[San Nicolás, Copán|San Nicolás]] to the south. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|114|km2}}.<ref name=ine/>

The terrain is a mixture of pastureland, farmland, and conifer and broadleaf forest.<ref name=plan/>

La Jigua has a temperate climate with average daily temperatures that range from {{convert|18|to|30|C}} and an average annual relative humidity of 80%. The wettest months are September and October and the driest months are March and April.<ref name=plan/>

==History==
The place name Santiago de los Caballeros de Xiguat is attested as early as 1729, while the current name of La Jigua first appears in historical records from 1791. La Jigua was part of the municipality of Florida when it was established in 1875. On 13 July 1965, La Jigua was separated from Florida as an independent municipality.<ref name=secoff/>

==Government==
The current mayor of La Jigua is Germán Guerra Echeverría.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://portalunico.iaip.gob.hn/portal/index.php?portal=79 |title=Alcaldía Municipal de La Jigua |language=es |website=Portal Único de Transparencia |publisher=Instituto de Acceso a la Información Pública |date=11 November 2020 |accessdate=14 November 2020}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2013 Honduran Census, La Jigua recorded a total population of 9288 inhabitants. There are no urban areas in the municipality.<ref name=ine/> The inhabitants are predominantly of Spanish and [[Chʼortiʼ people|Chʼortiʼ]] ''[[mestizo]]'' descent.<ref name=plan/>

==Culture==
[[File:Pirámide del Parque el Puente de Honduras.png|thumb|A pyramid at [[El Puente (Maya site)|El Puente]].]] 
The Mayan archaeological site of [[El Puente (Maya site)|El Puente]] is located in the municipality, on the Chinamito River on the north side of the Chamelecón valley. It was established in January 1994 as the second archaeological park in the country after [[Copán]].<ref>{{cite conference |last1=Nakamura |first1=Seiichi |last2=Cruz Torres |first2=Daniel |url=http://www.asociaciontikal.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/45.93-Nakamura-y-Torres.pdf |title=Investigaciones arqueológicas y trabajos de restauración en el sitio arqueológico El Puente, Copan, Honduras |language=es |publisher=[[Museo Nacional de Arqueología y Etnología]] |book-title=VII Simposio de Investigaciones Arqueológicas en Guatemala, 1993 |pages=518–526 |location=Guatemala |accessdate=14 November 2020}}</ref>

[[James the Great|Saint James]] is the patron saint of the municipality.<ref name=secoff/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
According to the 2013 Census, 69% of the population in La Jigua works in the [[primary sector]].<ref name=ine/> The main crops grown are corn, beans, and coffee. Cattle are the main livestock raised.<ref name=plan/> 

Central American Highway 11 runs through the municipality, connecting La Jigua with [[Copán Ruinas]] and the Guatemalan border to the west, and the nearby town of [[La Entrada]] to the east.<ref name=plan/> Another road runs north from Highway 11, passing by the site of El Puente and connecting to the village of [[La Ruidosa]].
