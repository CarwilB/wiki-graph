'''Guarapari''' is a coastal town of [[Espírito Santo]], Brazil, a popular tourist destination. Its beach is famous for the high natural radioactivity level of its sand.

==Geography==
===Location===
Guarapari is a part of Greater Vitoria, 47&nbsp;km south of the state capital [[Vitória, Espírito Santo|Vitória]]. Its population is 126,701 (2020) and its area is 592&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.
It is a well-known tourist destination, known for its curving white sand beaches backed by commercial development, which extend southward to Nova Guarapari and [[Meaípe]]. With its heavily built-up coastline like [[Vila Velha]] and Vitória, it caters heavily to seasonal tourists, and consequently has quite a dramatic seasonal population fluctuation.

The municipality contains the {{convert|953|ha|adj=on}} [[Concha d'Ostra Sustainable Development Reserve]], established in 2003 to protect the mangroves of the Bay of Guarapari.<ref>{{cite web |title=RDS de Concha D'Ostra |language=pt |publisher=IEMA: Instituto Estadual de Meio Ambiente e Recursos Hídricos |url=http://www.meioambiente.es.gov.br/default.asp?pagina=16701 |access-date=2016-10-27 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161028083720/http://www.meioambiente.es.gov.br/default.asp?pagina=16701 |archive-date=2016-10-28}}</ref> It also contains the {{convert|1500|ha|adj=on}} [[Paulo César Vinha State Park]], which protects an area of dunes, lagoons and marshes along the Atlantic shore.<ref>{{cite web |title=Parque Estadual Paulo César Vinha |language=pt |publisher=IEMA: Instituto Estadual de Meio Ambiente e Recursos Hídricos |url=http://www.meioambiente.es.gov.br/default.asp?pagina=16712 |access-date=2016-10-26 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161026232344/http://www.meioambiente.es.gov.br/default.asp?pagina=16712 |archive-date=2016-10-26}}</ref> Formerly called the Setiba nature reserve, it is a pristine example of a coastal ecosystem and important for local turtle and bird populations.

==History==
Around the year 1000, the [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|Indigenous people]] who occupied the southern coast of what is now the state of Espírito Santo were driven inland by the invasion of [[Tupi people]]s from the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]]. In the 16th century, when the first European explorers arrived in the region, it was inhabited by one of these Tupi peoples: the Temiminós.<ref>{{Cite web |last=guarapari.net.br |title=guarapari.net.br {{!}} Guia de empresas e serviços em Guarapari |url=https://guarapari.net.br/ |access-date=2025-09-08 |website=guarapari.net.br |language=pt}}</ref>

In 1585, the [[Jesuits|Jesuit]] priest [[Joseph of Anchieta|José de Anchieta]] founded a [[Reductions|Jesuit mission]] to [[Catechism|catechize]] the Indigenous people of the region: the village of Rio Verde, or the village of Santa Maria de Guaraparim. The village had a convent and a church dedicated to [[Saint Anne]]. For its inauguration, Anchieta composed the [[Autos sacramentales|Auto]] [[Tupi language|Tupi]]. In 1677, the church of Our Lady of the Conception was built. In 1679, the village of Guaraparim was elevated to the status of a town. In 1835, the district of Guarapari was created. In 1860, the district received a visit from the [[List of monarchs of Brazil|Brazilian Emperor]] [[Pedro II of Brazil|Dom Pedro II]]. In 1878, it became a municipality. In 1891, it acquired city status.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Informação |first=AlphaTec Tecnologia da |title=História e Geografia – PREFEITURA MUNICIPAL DE GUARAPARI – ES |url=https://www.guarapari.es.gov.br/pagina/ler/2293/ |access-date=2025-09-08 |website=www.guarapari.es.gov.br |language=pt-br}}</ref>

At the end of the 19th century, European settlers (mostly Italians who landed on the [[Benevente River]]) settled in the municipality's interior, founding the towns of Todos os Santos and Rio Calçado, among others. These families' main economic activity was coffee, in addition to the cultivation they cultivated for their own subsistence. In 1948, its own town hall was established. In the mid-1960s and 1970s, Guarapari became nationally famous due to the purported medicinal properties of its [[monazite sand]]s. As a result, there was a growing tourist wave around the city.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Informação |first=AlphaTec Tecnologia da |title=História e Geografia – PREFEITURA MUNICIPAL DE GUARAPARI – ES |url=https://www.guarapari.es.gov.br/pagina/ler/2293/ |access-date=2025-09-08 |website=www.guarapari.es.gov.br |language=pt-br}}</ref>

==Transportation==
The city is served by [[Guarapari Airport]].

==Radioactivity==
Along a roughly 500-mile (800&nbsp;km) portion of Brazil's Atlantic coast that runs from north of [[Rio de Janeiro]] up to the region south of [[Bahia]], the sands of old beaches are naturally radioactive. Sea waves pound coastal mountains rich in [[monazite]], a phosphate of rare earth metals containing uranium and thorium.<ref>{{cite web |publisher=Industrias Nucleares do Brasil |url=http://www.inb.gov.br/eng/WebForms/Imprimir.aspx?campo=252&secao_id=106 |title=Heavy Minerals |access-date=2012-08-06 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121127045933/http://www.inb.gov.br/eng/WebForms/Imprimir.aspx?campo=252&secao_id=106 |archive-date=2012-11-27 }}</ref><ref name="Japan">{{cite web |publisher=Health Research Foundation |location=Japan |url=http://www.taishitsu.or.jp/radiation/guarapari-e.html |title=High natural background radiation areas – Guarapari, Brazil}}</ref> The [[background radiation]] level on some spots on the Guarapari beach read 175&nbsp;[[mSv]] per year (20μSv/h);<ref>{{Cite journal| issn = 0001-3765| volume = 53| issue = 4| pages = 683–691| last1 = Pfeiffer| first1 = W. C.| last2 = Penna-Franca| first2 = E.| last3 = Ribeiro| first3 = C. C.| last4 = Nogueira| first4 = A. R.| last5 = Londres| first5 = H.| last6 = Oliveira| first6 = A. E.| title = Measurements of environmental radiation exposure dose rates at selected sites in Brazil| journal = Anais da Academia Brasileira de Ciências| date = December 1981| pmid = 7345962}}</ref> Some other spots can reach dosages of up to 55&nbsp;μSv/h.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RvgAx1yIKjg&t=48s  |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/RvgAx1yIKjg |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live|type=Video |title=Brazil 2012: sunbathing on radioactive beaches |at=at 50s |via=YouTube}}{{cbignore}}</ref> The average exposure level across the United States is 0.34&nbsp;μSv/h<ref>{{cite web |title=Relative Radioactivity Levels |url=https://radwatch.berkeley.edu/dosenet/levels |website=Berkeley RadWatch |publisher=[[UC Berkeley]] |access-date=18 October 2018}}</ref> while a [[chest x-ray]] is a one time exposure of 0.1&nbsp;mSv, and an abdominal and pelvic CT scan with and without contrast is 20–30&nbsp;mSv.<ref>{{cite web |title=Radiation Sources and Doses |url=https://www.epa.gov/radiation/radiation-sources-and-doses |website=Radiation Protection |publisher=EPA |language=en}}</ref>

In the Guarapari city, radiation levels are far lower: a study among 320 inhabitants showed an average received dose of 0.6&nbsp;μSv/h, corresponding to 5.2&nbsp;mSv per year.<ref>{{cite web |author1=N.
