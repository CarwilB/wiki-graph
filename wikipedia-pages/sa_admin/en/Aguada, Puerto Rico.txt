'''Aguada''' ({{IPAc-en|ə|ˈ|ɡ|w|ɑː|d|ə}}; {{IPA|es|aˈɣwaða|lang}}, {{IPA|es|aˈwaða|local}}), originally '''San Francisco de Asís de la Aguada''', is a [[Aguada barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]], located in the northwestern coastal valley region bordering the Atlantic Ocean, east of [[Rincón, Puerto Rico|Rincón]], south of [[Aguadilla, Puerto Rico|Aguadilla]], west of [[Moca, Puerto Rico|Moca]]; and north of [[Añasco, Puerto Rico|Añasco]] and [[Mayagüez, Puerto Rico|Mayagüez]]. It is part of the [[Aguadilla-Isabela-San Sebastián Metropolitan Statistical Area]]. Aguada's population is spread over 17 [[Barrios of Puerto Rico|barrios]] and [[Aguada barrio-pueblo|Aguada Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city).

== Etymology and nicknames ==
The name ''Aguada'' is a shortening of the town's original name ''San Francisco de Asís de la Aguada.'' The word ''aguada'' literally translates to "watery" or "watered down" from Spanish, possibly a reference to the town's strategic importance as a port in the [[Mona Passage]] and the Atlantic Ocean.

The municipality has many nicknames: ''La Villa de Sotomayor'' ("Sotomayor's Villa") is a reference to one of the town's Spanish founders, Cristóbal de Sotomayor; ''La Ciudad del Descubrimiento'' ("City of the Discovery") is a reference to the possibility that [[Christopher Columbus]] first landed in Puerto Rico at what is today the municipality's territory; ''El Pueblo Playero'' translates to "the beach town" and ''La Ciudad del Vaticano'' ("the [[Vatican City|Vatican]]'s City") which references the town's early importance for [[Catholic Church|Catholicism]] in the island.<ref>{{Cite web|date=November 26, 2016|title=Aguada, Puerto Rico – La Villa de Sotomayor|url=https://boricuaonline.com/aguada-pr/|access-date=2021-10-07|website=BoricuaOnline.com|language=es-ES|archive-date=October 7, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211007043500/https://boricuaonline.com/aguada-pr/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==History==
A [[Taíno people|Taíno]] settlement called ''Aymamón'' was located close to the [[Culebrinas River]].<ref name="History on aguada gov">{{cite web |title=AGUADA – ¡LA VILLA DE SOTOMAYOR! |url=http://aguada.gov.pr/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/HISTORIA-DE-AGUADA.pdf |website=aguada.gov.pr |publisher=PR Government |access-date=June 16, 2020 |language=es |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180921081342/http://aguada.gov.pr/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/HISTORIA-DE-AGUADA.pdf |archive-date=September 21, 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>[http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/caciques.html Caciques y Yucayeques de Puerto Rico] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107001412/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/caciques.html |date=November 7, 2012 }} on Proyecto Salon Hogar</ref>

''Bahía de Aguada (Aguada Bay)'' in Aguada has historically been recognized as the location where [[Christopher Columbus]] first landed in [[Puerto Rico]] during his [[Voyages of Christopher Columbus#Lesser Antilles and Puerto Rico|second voyage]] on 19 November 1493. However, it is believed by most historians that ''Bahía de Añasco (Añasco Bay)'' in the neighboring town of [[Añasco, Puerto Rico|Añasco]] was the place of Columbus’ first landing and stay of two days in Puerto Rico before continuing to [[La Navidad]], first European settlement in the [[Americas]], in [[Haiti]] in [[Hispaniola]].<ref>{{cite book|last= Van Middeldyk|first=R.A.|title=The history of Puerto Rico, from the Spanish discovery to the American occupation |year=1903|publisher=D. Appleton and Co.|location=NY|url=https://www.loc.gov/resource/lhbpr.08353/?sp=35&st=image |page=13-16}}</ref><ref name="Monson1986">{{cite book |last1=Morison |first1=Samuel Eliot |title=The Great Explorers: The European Discovery of America |date=1986 |publisher=Oxford University Press |isbn=978-0-19-504222-1 |pages=440, 448, 449 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JnotvLHX80gC&pg=PA448}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Controversia en Puerto Rico por el punto donde Colón llegó a la isla caribeña | website=Diario Libre| date=December 6, 2019| url= https://www.diariolibre.com/actualidad/internacional/controversia-en-puerto-rico-por-el-punto-donde-colon-llego-a-la-isla-caribena-IB15723931 | language=es | access-date=January 19, 2024}}</ref> 

In July 1510, Cristóbal de Sotomayor received control of the area from [[Juan Ponce de León]] and renamed the town ''Villa de Sotomayor (Sotomayor Village)''.<ref>{{cite book | last = Fuson | first = Robert | title = Juan Ponce De Leon and the Spanish Discovery of Puerto Rico and Florida | publisher = McDonald & Woodward | location = Blacksburg, Virginia | year = 2000 | page = [https://archive.org/details/juanponcedeleons00fuso/page/79 79] | isbn = 0-939923-82-3 | url-access = registration | url = https://archive.org/details/juanponcedeleons00fuso/page/79 }}</ref> However, in 1511 the settlement was attacked and burned by the local [[Taíno|Taínos]]. That same year, the Spanish crown ordered a [[monastery]] be established in Puerto Rico, and the ''Ermita de Espinar'' was founded. The name of the area was then changed to ''San Francisco de Asís de la Aguada'', since the [[friar]]s were [[Franciscan]]. The monastery was finished in 1516. 

In 1526, [[Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor|King Charles I of Spain]] officially founded the [[Aguada barrio-pueblo|Aguada]] settlement. However, in 1529, Taínos attacked the monastery killing the friars and burning the settlement.<ref name="aguadapsh">[http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/aguada.html Aguada, la Villa del Sotomayor] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140717092734/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/aguada.html |date=July 17, 2014 }} on Proyecto Salon Hogar</ref> The [[Taínos]], indigenous people of Puerto Rico, were believed to have practiced [[polytheism]]. As the Spanish began to colonize the [[Caribbean]] area, they wanted to convert the natives to [[Catholicism]].<ref>Menhinick, Kevin, [http://www.avirtualdominica.com/caribs3.htm "The Caribs in Dominica"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120303082342/http://www.avirtualdominica.com/caribs3.htm|date=March 3, 2012}}</ref> The Caribs destroyed a church of [[Franciscans]] in Aguada and killed five of its members in 1579.<ref name="Puerto Rico Office of Historian 19492">{{cite book|author=Puerto Rico. Office of Historian|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA238|title=Tesauro de datos historicos: indice compendioso de la literatura histórica de Puerto Rico, incluyendo algunos datos inéditos, periodísticos y cartográficos|publisher=Impr. del Gobierno de Puerto Rico|year=1949|page=238|language=es|access-date=January 4, 2020|issue=v. 2|archive-date=August 14, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230814161145/https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA238|url-status=live}}</ref>

Still, Aguada resurfaced and became a stopover point for ships on their way to Spain from South America.<ref name="Mari Mut">{{cite web | title=Los pueblos de Puerto Rico y las iglesias de sus plazas |first= José A. |last= Mari Mut |via=archive.org| date=August 28, 2013 | url=https://archive.org/details/pueblospr/page/n13/mode/2up| access-date=June 23, 2020|language=es}}</ref> On September 17, 1692, King [[Charles II of Spain]] emitted a Royal Decree declaring Aguada a "village" and assigning Captain Juan López de Segura as War Lieutenant and Ordinary Mayor of the ''Villa de San Francisco de Asís de la Aguada.''<ref name="aguadapsh"/>

In 1737, [[Philip V of Spain|Philip V]], [[King of Spain]], declared that all mail en route to [[Venezuela]] and other South American countries from Puerto Rico should exit from Aguada's ports, leading to the area's economic growth. Also, an increase in population has been attributed to possible desertions from foreign merchant ships.<ref name="PREncyclopedia">{{cite web |title=Aguada Municipality |url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aguada-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14 |website=Enciclopedia PR |access-date=February 14, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214115638/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aguada-municipality/#1465331233922-61915a4b-ef14 |archive-date=February 14, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Puerto Rico was ceded by Spain in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became an unincorporated territory of the United States. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted its first census of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Aguada was 10,581.

In the early years of the 20th century, two disasters affected the town of Aguada. First, a huge fire in 1912 destroyed most of the town buildings, including the old [[city hall]], which contained all the city archives. On October 11, 1918, at {{tooltip|10:14:42 local time|14:14:42 UTC}} an earthquake known as the [[1918 San Fermín earthquake|San Fermín earthquake]] destroyed the church and other structures.<ref>[http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09033001&page=2 Aguada: Fundación e historia] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140701040955/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09033001&page=2 |date=July 1, 2014 }} on Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico</ref> At [[Culebrinas River|Rio Culebrinas]], 1000&nbsp;kg blocks of limestone from the wrecked Columbus monument were carried inland to distances of {{convert|46|–|76|m|abbr=off|sp=us}} by waves {{convert|4.0|m|abbr=on}} high.<ref>[ftp://ftp.ngdc.noaa.gov/hazards/publications/Ref0537_lander.pdf Tsunamis in the Caribbean Sea – NOAA]{{dead link|date=May 2025|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>

[[Hurricane Maria|Hurricane María]] on September 20, 2017, triggered numerous landslides in Aguada, with its strong winds and heavy rain.<ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides map">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref> Infrastructure and an estimated 8,000 homes in Aguada were damaged or destroyed. Two police officers died when they were caught in the flooded [[Culebrinas River]].<ref>{{cite web | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar – Aguada | trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget – Aguada | website=El Nuevo Día | date=June 13, 2019 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/aguada/ | language=es | access-date=2021-08-24 | archive-date=August 24, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210824182347/https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/aguada/ | url-status=live }}</ref>

On July 16, 2023 a [[tornado]] touched down in Aguada, producing [[Enhanced Fujita scale|EF1]] damage which included tearing a roof from a house and damage to power lines.<ref>{{Cite web |last=PR • • |first=Por TELEMUNDO |date=2023-07-17 |title=Vientos de 110mph: catalogan como EF-1 el tornado en Aguada |url=https://www.telemundopr.com/noticias/puerto-rico/vientos-de-110mph-catalogan-como-ef-1-el-tornado-en-aguada/2508411/ |access-date=2024-01-19 |website=Telemundo Puerto Rico |language=es}}</ref> The city is notorious for stormy weather. 

===Folklore===
[[File:La Cruz de Colón en Aguada, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Cross marking spot where [[Christopher Columbus]] is said to have disembarked in Aguada]]
{{lang|es|El Matador de Tiburones}} (The Aguada Shark Killer) is [[folklore]] that comes out of Aguada and was written around 1640. It tells of a young man who was accustomed to fighting sharks but was without his [[Milagros (religious charms)|religious charm]]. When asked to demonstrate his shark-fighting capabilities to visiting Spanish dignitaries, he hesitated. All day and night he pondered whether he could fight a shark without his religious charm. Even though the Spaniards had increased their offer to 3 bars of gold the matador was hesitant. In the morning, as the shark came into the bay, the spectators who were gathered on the beach yelled in anticipation, and {{lang|es|El Matador de Tiburones}}, as the young man was called, was unable to stop himself. He jumped into the open sea pursuing the shark and fought it with his bare hands. He was nearly killed and after receiving his prize of gold, vowed to never again fight a shark.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ufdc.ufl.edu/UF00078480/00001/25|language=es|title=Selección de Leyendas puertorriqueñas – El Matador de Tiburones (1640)|page=27|website=ufdc|access-date=2019-03-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190306043810/http://ufdc.ufl.edu/UF00078480/00001/25|archive-date=2019-03-06|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Geography==
Aguada is located in the west coast of the island of [[Puerto Rico]]. It borders the Atlantic Ocean and [[Aguadilla, Puerto Rico|Aguadilla]] on the north, [[Moca, Puerto Rico|Moca]] on the east, [[Añasco, Puerto Rico|Añasco]] on the south, and [[Rincón, Puerto Rico|Rincón]] on the west. Aguada is part of the Coastal Plains of the West, which features alluvial and fertile terrain. Although the terrain is mostly plain, there are some mountains to the south and southeast.<ref name="enciclopedia">[http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09033001 Aguada] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120617125522/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09033001 |date=June 17, 2012 }} on Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico</ref><ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info">{{cite web|title=Aguada Municipality General Info (Location, Square Miles, Economy and Geography)|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aguada-municipality/|website=enciclopediapr|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|language=English|access-date=February 14, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214115638/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aguada-municipality/|archive-date=February 14, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

Among the mountains located in Aguada are the Atalaya Peak, located within the limits of Aguada and Rincón. Also, San Francisco Mountain, which is popularly known as the birth point of the [[Cordillera Central, Puerto Rico|Cordillera Central]], and Cerro Gordo, peaking at {{convert|853|ft|m|abbr=off|sp=us}}.<ref>[http://www.salonhogar.com/est_soc/pr/pueblos/aguada/ Aguada] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111008095548/http://www.salonhogar.com/est_soc/pr/pueblos/aguada/|date=October 8, 2011}} on SalonHogar.com</ref>

===Water features===
Aguada's hydrographic system is composed of the [[Río Culebrinas]], [[Río Grande (Aguada, Puerto Rico)|Río Grande]], [[Río Cañas (Aguada, Puerto Rico)|Río Cañas]], [[Río Culebra (Aguada, Puerto Rico)|Río Culebra]], [[Río Guayabo]], and [[Río Ingenio]]. All of these rivers flow into the [[Mona Passage]].<ref name="enciclopedia"/> According to a news article by [[Primera Hora (Puerto Rico)|Primera Hora]], there are ten beaches in Aguada.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/las1200playasdepuertorico-1216285/|title=Las 1,200 playas de Puerto Rico [The 1200 beaches of Puerto Rico]|date=April 14, 2017|website=Primera Hora|language=es|access-date=December 12, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191212200804/https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/las1200playasdepuertorico-1216285/|archive-date=December 12, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Aguada has an area of 45.55 square miles and 14.62% are water bodies.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www3.epa.gov/region2/water/npdes/permits/pdfs/noi_aguada.pdf |title=EPA Municipio Aguada Discharges |access-date=2018-08-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180805083251/https://www3.epa.gov/region2/water/npdes/permits/pdfs/noi_aguada.pdf |archive-date=2018-08-05 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Special Communities ===
{{See also|Puerto Rico Office for Socioeconomic and Community Development}}
{{lang|es|Comunidades Especiales de Puerto Rico}} (Special Communities of Puerto Rico) are marginalized communities whose citizens are experiencing a certain amount of [[social exclusion]]. A map shows these communities occur in nearly every municipality of the commonwealth. Of the 742 places that were on the list in 2014, the following barrios, communities, sectors, or neighborhoods were in Aguada: {{lang|es|Parcelas Noboa Vieja}} and {{lang|es|Parcelas Noboa Nuevas}} in Guanaquilla barrio, {{lang|es|Calle San Francisco, Calle San José, Comunidad Las Flores, Sector Las Bimbas}} in {{lang|es|Luyando comunidad, Parcelas Matías, Parcelas Nieves}}, and {{lang|es|Sector García}}.<ref>{{Citation|author1=Rivera Quintero, Marcia|title=El vuelo de la esperanza:Proyecto de las Comunidades Especiales Puerto Rico, 1997–2004|publication-date=2014| publisher=San Juan, Puerto Rico Fundación Sila M. In 2020, the population of Aguada decreased by 9.1% to 38,136. In 2010, the population of Aguada was 41,959, which represented a small decrease from the 42,042 registered in the 2000 Census.<ref>[http://209.68.12.238/censo2000/barrios.php?municipio=AG Censo 2000: Población de Puerto Rico por Municipios y Barrios] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140702192954/http://209.68.12.238/censo2000/barrios.php?municipio=AG |date=July 2, 2014 }} on CEEPUR</ref> Until its decrease in 2010, Aguada's population had been increasing steadily.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ceepur.org/censo2000/1930-2000.html |title=Población de Puerto Rico por Municipios: 1930–2000 |work=CEEPUR |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130321155203/http://ceepur.org/censo2000/1930-2000.html |archivedate=2013-03-21 }}</ref>

According to the 2020 Census, 22.7% of the population identifies themselves as White, and 10.6% as Black. Also, according to the census, the population is equally divided by gender (49.1% are males, while 50.1% are females). Finally, 23.7% of the population is under 18 years old. The next biggest percentage of population (20.8%) is between 35 and 49 years old.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130727083106/http://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|url-status=dead|archive-date=2013-07-27|title=2010 Census Interactive Population Search: Aguada, Puerto Rico|work=US Census 2010}}</ref>

==Economy==
[[File:Coloso Refinery.jpg|thumb|Coloso Sugar Cane Refinery]]
Historically, the economy of Aguada was mostly based on the processing of [[sugarcane]]. The [[Central Coloso]], located in the [[Guanábano, Aguada, Puerto Rico|Guanábano]] barrio of Aguada, was one of the most important refineries in the island. It was also the last one to cease operations, officially closing in 2003.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" /><ref>[http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=10031703 Central Coloso] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120708075352/http://enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=10031703 |date=July 8, 2012 }} on Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico</ref> Aside from sugar mills, there was also a cattle and wood industry. 
[[File:La Villa de Sotomayor Store at Aguada, PR.jpg|thumb|La Villa de Sotomayor Department Store by Rivera & Rivera, Inc.]]
The commercial economy in Aguada reached a great boom in the 1950s. There were many commercial stores selling merchandise, grocery, hardware, and food products of all categories. “''La Villa de Sotomayor''” by Rivera & Rivera, Inc., founded around 1912 by Remigio Rivera Bonet, was the biggest and most important business in Aguada with an estimated income that exceeded the million dollars annually at that time.<ref>{{Cite book |last=González González |first=Eugenio |title=Memoria Histórica de Aguada |publisher=Imprenta America, Inc. |year=1962 |edition=1st |location=Rio Piedras, PR |page=125 |language=Spanish}}</ref> Rivera & Rivera, Inc. had department stores, hardware stores, bakeries, travel agencies and LPG gas distribution, among other business in Aguada and the region. As of 2012, the Aguada economy relies mostly on small businesses and manufacturing.<ref name="enciclopedia"/>

In late 2014, the government announced a $172&nbsp;million deal with private investors to restart sugar production in Puerto Rico for the purpose of supplying the island rum producers with up to 56% of the molasses needed. The plan involved building a new processing plant on the grounds of the old Coloso Sugar Cane factory in Aguada.<ref>{{cite web | title=De camino el contrato para el proyecto de caña | website=El Nuevo Dia | date=January 1, 2015 | url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/decaminoelcontratoparaelproyectodecana-1919121.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150101172557/http://www.elnuevodia.com/decaminoelcontratoparaelproyectodecana-1919121.html | archive-date=January 1, 2015 | url-status=dead | access-date=January 21, 2022}}</ref>

===Tourism===
Aguada is part of the [[Porta del Sol]] touristic region in Puerto Rico. The Porta del Sol website highlights Aguada's town square and beaches as its most notable touristic attractions.

===Landmarks and places of interest===
To stimulate local tourism during the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Puerto Rico]], the [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]] launched the ''Voy Turistiendo'' (I'm Touring) campaign in 2021. The campaign featured a passport book with a page for each municipality. The {{lang|es|Voy Turisteando}} Aguada passport page lists the {{lang|es|Ermita Espinar}} which are ruins, {{lang|es|Jeanmarie Chocolat}}, which was the first commercial chocolate farm in Puerto Rico, and {{lang|es|Pirámide}}, the only pyramid in Puerto Rico, as places of interest.<ref>{{cite book | title= Pasaporte: Voy Turisteando | url= https://voyturisteando.com/78-destinos/|publisher=Compañia de Turismo de Puerto Rico | year=2021| language=es}}</ref>

===Public health===
Although there are no hospitals in Aguada, the town does have a small emergency medical center located near the town center. Most important emergency procedures are carried out in [[Mayagüez, Puerto Rico|Mayagüez]] and [[Aguadilla, Puerto Rico]] local hospitals.

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Aguada celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in October. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Francisco de Asis}} is a religious and cultural celebration in honor of [[Francis of Assisi|Saint Francis of Assisi]] that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="History on aguada gov" /><ref>{{cite web | title=Puerto Rico Festivales, Eventos y Actividades en Puerto Rico | website=Puerto Rico Hoteles y Paradores | url=https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | language=es | access-date=2020-07-17 | archive-date=February 26, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226163727/https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/link%20p.r/www.linktopr.com/aguada.html|title=LinkToPuertoRico.com – Aguada|publisher=Proyecto Salon Hogar|language=es|access-date=2017-12-27|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140717092734/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/aguada.html|archive-date=2014-07-17|url-status=live}}</ref> The festival has featured live performances by well-known artists such as [[Kany García]], [[Tito Rojas]], [[Andy Montañez]], and [[Cano Estremera]].<ref name="Fiestas">{{cite web |title=Fiestas Patronales de Aguada 2013 en honor a Santo San Francisco de Asis |url=https://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/09/560011_10151899366990450_1356659465_n.jpg |website=sondeaquiprnet |publisher=El Gobierno Municipal de Aguada |access-date=14 August 2023 |archive-date=July 3, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220703074923/https://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/09/560011_10151899366990450_1356659465_n.jpg |url-status=live }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Aguada include:

* {{lang|es|Festival de Reyes}} ([[Epiphany (holiday)|Three Kings Festival]]) which is celebrated in January at {{lang|es|Paraíso de los Niños}} park. It is an activity dedicated to children of Aguada and is celebrated with gifts, music, clowns, and raffles.<ref name="History on aguada gov" />
* {{lang|es|Festival de Playa}} (Beach Festival) is celebrated on June 24, the day of {{lang|es|San Juan Bautista}} ([[John the Baptist|Saint John the Baptist]]), at the {{lang|es|Pico de Piedra}} Beach.
* {{lang|es|Festival y Feria de Artesanías}} (Arts & Craft Fair) takes place from November 17–20 at the {{lang|es|Plaza Cristóbal Colón}}.<ref name="History on aguada gov" />
* Every year in November, a parade called {{lang|es|La Parada del Descubrimento}} takes place to remember the [[discovery of Puerto Rico]] by [[Christopher Columbus]]. In this parade the people walk from the Catholic church in the town square to the {{lang|es|Santurario Histórico a Colón}} (Cross of Columbus) next to the beach in [[Guaniquilla, Aguada, Puerto Rico|Guaniquilla]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/Xstatic/endi/template/imprimir.aspx?id=821748&t=3 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130122084216/http://www.elnuevodia.com/Xstatic/endi/template/imprimir.aspx?id=821748&t=3 |url-status=dead |language=es|archive-date=January 22, 2013 |title=Aguada celebra sus 500 años |work=[[El Nuevo Día]] |date=November 21, 2010 }}</ref>
* {{lang|es|Festival de Cabras de Raza}} is held on the last weekend of May in the [[Atalaya, Aguada, Puerto Rico|Atalaya]] barrio, with exhibitions of different goat breeds and competitions.<ref name="History on aguada gov" />
* {{lang|es|Parranda del Octavón}} is a one-day annual, cultural activity that takes place in January at the {{lang|es|Plaza Cristóbal Colón}}, with local music.<ref name="History on aguada gov" />
* {{lang|es|Encuentro de Talladores}} (Carvers' Meeting) is a one-day annual celebration that takes place the first weekend of July at the {{lang|es|Plaza Cristóbal Colón}}, to commemorate the birthday of Don Zoilo Cajigas a carver of wooden saints. Carved wooden saints are on exhibition and for sale.<ref name="History on aguada gov" />

===Flag===
[[File:Flag_of_Aguada_(PR).svg|thumb|100px|right|Aguada flag]]
Aguada's flag was designed by Pedro Vélez Adróvar. It features three main colors: white, red, and yellow. White represents purity and the waters of [[Culebrinas River]]. Over the white field, a blue triangle with a white dove is featured. The dove is the symbol of peace that unites the towns. Red symbolizes the [[martyrdom]] of [[Franciscan friars]] from Espinal. Over the red field, there's a black cross which represents the birth of Christianity in Puerto Rico. The name of "Aguada" is also above the cross. The yellow field represents happiness and hospitality of the residents. Over the yellow field, there's a white star which symbolizes the hope of the town for more development and progress.<ref>[http://aguada.gov.pr/historiayturismo/historiadeaguada/bandera-de-aguada/ Bandera de Aguada] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120106055051/http://aguada.gov.pr/historiayturismo/historiadeaguada/bandera-de-aguada/ |date=January 6, 2012 }} on Aguada Official website</ref><ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=AGUADA | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=February 19, 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/AGUADA.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219000330/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/AGUADA.html | archive-date=February 19, 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=September 16, 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of Arms===
[[File:Escudo de Aguada, Puerto Rico.svg|thumb|80px|right|Aguada coat of arms]]
The coat of arms is divided into two main fields. The upper field features a cross, with the interlaced arms of Christ and Father Saint Francis. It is taken from the badge of the [[Order of Friars Minor]]. It represents "Pax et Bonum", a Latin phrase which means "peace and good will between man and the Redeemer". The sun below the cross symbolizes the light that brightens the world. The lower part of the shield consists of five ships that symbolize the [[Christopher Columbus#Second voyage (1493–1496)|second voyage of Christopher Columbus]], who allegedly arrived at the western "Guaniquilla" coast on November 19, 1493, to gather water. Although the precise location is disputed, the Aguada wells ({{lang|es|Puerto de los pozos de la Aguada de Colón}}) is a plausible site for the actual event.<ref name="History by Aguada.gov.pr">{{cite web |title=AGUADA – ¡LA VILLA DE SOTOMAYOR! |url=http://aguada.gov.pr/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/HISTORIA-DE-AGUADA.pdf |website=aguada.gov.pr |publisher=Government of Aguada, Puerto Rico |access-date=January 21, 2022 |archive-date=September 21, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180921081342/http://aguada.gov.pr/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/HISTORIA-DE-AGUADA.pdf |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="PH 2021">{{cite web | title=Aguada: un manantial de historia | website=Primera Hora | date=October 5, 2021 | url=https://www.primerahora.com/estilos-de-vida/ph-mas-pa-aprender/notas/aguada-un-manantial-de-historia/ | language=es | access-date=January 21, 2022 | archive-date=January 21, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220121132225/https://www.primerahora.com/estilos-de-vida/ph-mas-pa-aprender/notas/aguada-un-manantial-de-historia/ | url-status=live }}</ref>

The mural crown in the upper part of the shield signifies the title of village, that was given to this town by [[Charles III of Spain|King Charles III]] in 1778. The official colors of the shield are red, which stands for the fraternal love in Aguada; gold, for the Spanish royalty in Puerto Rico; green, for the island's hope and fertility; black, for the wooden beam of the cross; blue, for the sky and the kingship of God; and white, for Christ's purity and the purity of the people of the town toward the cultural patrimony.<ref>"Banderas y escudos municipales" (Flags and Municipal Shields) by Anisa Productions Inc.</ref><ref>[http://aguada.gov.pr/historiayturismo/historiadeaguada/escudo-de-aguada/ Escudo de Aguada] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151004165830/http://aguada.gov.pr/historiayturismo/historiadeaguada/escudo-de-aguada/ |date=October 4, 2015 }} on Aguada Official website</ref>

===Nicknames===
Aguada has various nicknames, most of them pertaining to its origins. One is ''"La Villa del Sotomayor"'' ("Sotomayor Village"), which was the name originally given to it by [[Cristóbal de Sotomayor]] during its colonization in 1510. It is also called ''"Villa de San Francisco de Asís de la Aguada"'', which was the name given to the region when the Franciscan friars took control of it. Aguada is also called ''"La Ciudad del Descubrimiento"'' ("City of the Discovery") in reference to it being one of the possible places where [[Christopher Columbus]] entered the island. Other nicknames are ''"El Pueblo Playero"'' ("The Beach Town") for its many beaches, and ''"La Ciudad del Vaticano"'' ("The Vatican City") for being considered the "capital of [[Catholicism]]" in the island.<ref>[http://www.filirealty.com/Pueblos/Aguada/Datos.htm Aguada] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090416030343/http://www.filirealty.com/Pueblos/Aguada/Datos.htm |date=April 16, 2009 }} on FiliRealty</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://pr.gov/Directorios/Pages/DirectoriodeMunicipios.aspx|title=Directorio de Municipios de Puerto Rico|website=pr.gov|access-date=2019-03-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190315093910/http://www2.pr.gov/directorios/pages/directoriodemunicipios.aspx|archive-date=2019-03-15|url-status=live}}</ref>
