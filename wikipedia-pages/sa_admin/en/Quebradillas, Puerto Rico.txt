'''Quebradillas''' ({{IPA|es|keβɾaˈðiʝas}}, {{IPA|es|ˌkeβɾaˈðiʝaʔ|local}}) is a [[Quebradillas barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] in [[Puerto Rico]] located in the northern shore bordering the Atlantic Ocean, north of [[San Sebastián, Puerto Rico|San Sebastián]]; east of [[Isabela, Puerto Rico|Isabela]]; and west of [[Camuy, Puerto Rico|Camuy]]. Quebradillas is spread over seven barrios and [[Quebradillas barrio-pueblo|Quebradillas Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan–Bayamón–Caguas metropolitan area]].

Quebradillas is called "''La Guarida del Pirata''" (The Pirate's Hideout). A well-known beach in the area, [[Puerto Hermina]], is home to an old structure known to have been a hiding place for pirates and their contraband.

==History==
The town was founded in 1823 by Felipe Ruiz.  There were many factors that cause the inhabitants of this territory of Puerto Rico to be independent from the towns that formed the Hato de la Tuna resulting in the formation of a new municipality. The town lacked chapels or churches, religious services nor priests, a place to bury the dead, a post office, and quality roads to transport. They relied on the neighboring municipalities of [[Camuy, Puerto Rico|Camuy]], [[Hatillo, Puerto Rico|Hatillo]] and [[Isabela, Puerto Rico|Isabela]]. This would however be a problem during the rainy seasons when the [[Camuy River|Río Camuy]] and [[Guajataca River|Río Guajataca]] water level rose resulting in closed off passage to the other municipalities and would sometimes take up to a week for the water level to return to normal. This led the residents of the area to organize a corporation that would look after the political, economic and administrative interests of ''"Las Quebradillas"'' and its jurisdiction.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Historia|url=https://www.quebradillas.pr.gov/historia/|access-date=March 22, 2021|website=Gobierno Municipal de Quebradillas|language=en|archive-date=April 18, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210418142554/https://www.quebradillas.pr.gov/historia/|url-status=dead}}</ref> This town derives its name from the large number of streams flowing through it. Quebradillas means "small streams".

Puerto Rico was ceded by Spain in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the United States.  In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Quebradillas was 7,432.

On September 20, 2017 [[Hurricane Maria]] struck the island of Puerto Rico.  In Quebradillas, 800 concrete barriers had to be built to stabilize the [[Guajataca Lake|Guajataca reservoir]] which suffered a structural failure and was causing a "life-threatening situation".<ref>{{cite web|title=Guajataca Dam fails in Puerto Rico, prompting "extremely dangerous" situation|url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/guajataca-dam-fails-puerto-rico-towns-evacuated-hurricane-maria/|work=[[CBS News]]|access-date=September 22, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title= Puerto Rico dam failure "imminent" after Hurricane Maria|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-41368478|website=[[BBC World News]]|date=September 22, 2017 |publisher=[[BBC News]]|access-date=September 22, 2017}}</ref><ref name=guardian1722>{{cite web|title= Puerto Rico evacuates 70,000 after dam fails in Hurricane Maria's wake|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2017/sep/22/puerto-rico-hurricane-maria-dam|website=[[The Guardian]]|date=22 September 2017|access-date=September 22, 2017}}</ref> "It’s a structural failure. I don’t have any more details," Governor [[Ricardo Rosselló]] stated. "We’re trying to evacuate as many people as possible."<ref name=guardian1722/> Rosselló ordered the [[Puerto Rico National Guard]] and the Police to help assist in the evacuation effort downstream.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Talmazan|first1=Yuliya|title=Puerto Rico's Failing Guajataca Dam Endangers Thousands|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/news/weather/puerto-rico-s-failing-guajataca-dam-endangers-thousands-n804096|work=[[NBC News]]|date=September 23, 2017 |access-date=September 23, 2017}}</ref> The dam lies across the Guajataca River to form a reservoir that can hold roughly 11 billion gallons of water.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://weather.com/storms/hurricane/news/hurricane-maria-puerto-rico-us-virgin-islands-dominica-caribbean-turks|title=Damage Assessments, Power Restoration Efforts Begin in Puerto Rico, Virgin Islands|work=[[The Weather Channel]]|access-date=2017-09-25|language=en-US}}</ref>

In Quebradillas, infrastructure and about 2200 homes were damaged or completely destroyed.<ref>{{cite news| title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. ==Geography==
[[File:Guajatacatunel.jpg|thumb|The coastline and [[Guajataca Tunnel]] at Guajataca Beach]]
Quebradillas, located in the [[Northern Karst (Puerto Rico)|Northern Karst]], borders on the Atlantic Ocean, is north of [[San Sebastián, Puerto Rico|San Sebastián]]; east of [[Isabela, Puerto Rico|Isabela]], and west of [[Camuy, Puerto Rico|Camuy]]. Quebradillas is home to one of the 20 designated forest preserves in Puerto Rico, the [[Guajataca State Forest]]. The forest serves as a great example of an unusual topography known as [[karst|''karst country'']]. Karst is characterized by dissolved limestone formations such as [[sinkholes]] and haystack-shaped hills known as [[mogote|"mogotes"]]. It is also home to the beautiful man-made reservoir, [[Guajataca Lake]] ({{convert|2.5|mi|abbr=on|disp=or}} long), where you can fish for [[largemouth bass]], [[peacock bass]], [[tilapia]] and [[bluegill]] (in Spanish known as "chopa"). You can also go [[hiking]] or [[camping]]. The [[Scouting in Puerto Rico|Puerto Rico Council]] of the [[Boy Scouts of America]] maintains a campground on the lake known as [[Camp Guajataka]]. The name Guajataca comes from the name ''Wahataka'', a [[Taíno people|Taíno]] chief who lived in this area. This Indian chief also gives his name to [[Guajataca Beach]] to the north where [[Río Guajataca]] flowing from [[Guajataca Lake]] meets the Atlantic Ocean. Guajataca Beach is popular with [[Surfing|surfers]] and is known for its white sands and wild waves. This beach is ideal for sunning and collecting seashells.<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/quebradillas-municipality/|title=Quebradillas Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=March 20, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190618160420/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/quebradillas-municipality/|archive-date=June 18, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Economy==
===Business===
A small shopping center called Quebradillas Plaza is located in this municipality.

An event which negatively affected the region in the decade of the 1990s was the disappearance of tax exemptions to the private corporations Section 936 of the code of the US [[Internal Revenue Service]], which at the time was the resulted in the closure of one of the greater manufacturers of textiles in the northwest area of the island. This decline of the industry of the needle occurred similarly in the bordering cities like [[Isabela, Puerto Rico|Isabela]] and [[Camuy]], generating a regional economic decline as the locals greatly depended on these jobs. In nearby towns like [[Hatillo, Puerto Rico|Hatillo]], [[Mayagüez]] and [[Aguadilla]], the arrival of [[megastores|mega stores]] and new shopping centers attracted the jobs that used to be in Quebradillas.

The island lacks mass public transportation, people must resort to private cars. The only road to access the important cities, the [[Puerto Rico Highway 2]] suffers from congestion. The town possesses one tunnel that at the beginning of the 20th century was used by steam driven trains that traveled throughout the Island. This absence aggravates the problem of mass transit and the dependence on privately owned vehicles as these are the only method of transportation.<ref name="OS_scenic">{{cite news |last1=Oliver |first1=Lance |title=The Prettiest Face Of Puerto Rico |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2000/04/16/the-prettiest-face-of-puerto-rico/ |access-date=February 18, 2019 |work=Orlando Sentinel |date=April 16, 2000 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190218201919/http://articles.orlandosentinel.com/2000-04-16/travel/0004120377_1_puerto-rico-northwest-puerto-ponce |archive-date=February 18, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The economy, entering into the 21st century, is based on retail sales. Many small businesses are located along the two main thoroughfares that cross Quebradillas. These consist mainly of light hardware, bakeries, pharmacies, seafood restaurants, American fast food chains and automobile car repair shops.

The town has bank branches for [[Popular, Inc.|Banco Popular]] and local savings and credit [[cooperative banking|Unions]]. These credit unions are most noted for their involvement in common and cultural activities, sponsoring and participating in festivals of typical music such as "Festival of the [[Gourd]] in December", sports and educational activities.

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Quebradillas celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in October. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de San Rafael Arcangel}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" /><ref>{{cite web | title=Puerto Rico Festivales, Eventos y Actividades en Puerto Rico | website=Puerto Rico Hoteles y Paradores | url=https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | language=es | access-date=July 17, 2020 | archive-date=February 26, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226163727/https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | url-status=dead }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Quebradillas include:
* [[Epiphany (holiday)|Wake of the Three Kings]] – January
* Kite Festival – February
* Guajataca [[Carnival]] – January/February
* [[Mother's Day (United States)|Serenade to Mothers]] – May
* [[Father's Day|Serenade to Fathers]] – June
* Quebradillas Cultural Festival – December

==Government==
The town's government is a small unit with few powers it can execute. Elections are hosted in specific schools every four years to choose a town mayor.

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=June 15, 2021}}</ref>

===Flag===
It has two red quarters. The red stands for struggle, effort and sacrifice. The other two-quarters have each five green and white wavy stripes, similar to those that appear in the shield.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=QUEBRADILLAS | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=February 19, 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/QUEBRADILLAS.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219000144/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/QUEBRADILLAS.html | archive-date=February 19, 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=September 17, 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The three waved stripes represent the ''quebradillas'' (Spanish for creeks or streams), over the green background of the vegetation. The fish and the walking stick (distinctive of the traveler), are insignias of the angel [[Raphael (archangel)|Saint Raphael]], by allusion from the episodes narrated in the [[Book of Tobit|Book of Tobias]] in the [[Old Testament]].<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

==Education==
The town features various public and private schools. There is also a public electronic library near ''Los Chorritos Pirata'' water park. In 2021 the municipal government opened a child daycare center.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2021|title=Inauguran moderno centro de cuido de niños en Quebradillas|url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/notas/inauguran-moderno-centro-de-cuido-de-ninos-en-quebradillas/|access-date=May 25, 2021|website=Primera Hora|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=elvocero.com|first=Redacción|title=Inauguran un centro de cuido de niños en Quebradillas|url=https://www.elvocero.com/gobierno/inauguran-un-centro-de-cuido-de-ni-os-en-quebradillas/article_518fcd1e-b0bd-11eb-a295-27310587b614.html|access-date=May 25, 2021|website=El Vocero de Puerto Rico|date=May 9, 2021 |language=es}}</ref> Some schools have been remodeled as a result of a government initiative to have "21st century" schools.
