'''Camajuaní''' is a municipality and town in the [[Villa Clara Province]] of [[Cuba]].

==History==
[[Image:StationCamajuani.JPG|thumb|left|The old train station]]
In 1819 was decided the construction of a new port near [[Caibarién]] to replace an old one located in [[Tesisco]]. From that moment, settlers established in the surroundings creating a new village. In 1832 blueprints for the new town of Caibarien were presented, and finally in 1841 this city was founded. Consequently, in 1864 an extension of the railway was planned in order to bring the sugar from the Camajuani valley to Caibarien and a new settlement began near the train station.<ref name=guije>{{Cite web|url=http://www.guije.com/pueblo/municipios/vcamajuani/index.htm|title=Camajuaní|author=Guije.com|accessdate=2007-10-09|language=es}}</ref> This is the starting point and birth of Camajuaní village.

[[File:Plano de Camajuaní.jpg|thumb|left|Planned map of Camajuaní in 1877]]
January 1, 1871 Camajuani was declared a municipality<ref name="History" /> and on August 1, 1879 the city hall was established. The first mayor of the villa was [[Hipólito Escobar Martelo]]. 
{{clear|left}}

In the 1907 census [[Zulueta (Remedios)|Zulueta]], which was a barrio at the time was switched from being a part of the municipality of Camajuaní to [[Remedios, Cuba|Remedios]].<ref>{{Cite book |title=Population |publisher=[[census.gov]] |year=1907 |location=Cuba |pages=6}}</ref> In 1909 Camajuaní was split into the barrios of Egido (main city of Camajuaní), Santa Fe, Sabana, Santa Clarita (including [[Falcón (Placetas)|Falcón]]), Guadalupe, and Salamanca (including [[José María Pérez, Cuba|José María Pérez]]).

In 1976, the former municipal territories of San Antonio de las Vueltas was given to the municipality of Camajuaní.<ref name="History" />

==Geography==
[[Image:ValleyCamajuani.JPG|thumb|left|Camajuaní Valley]]
Camajuaní is located in a valley, surrounded by an old range. Predominantly the city lies in a flat terrain. With the same name, the Camajuani river passes near the city, and Sagua la Chica river also passes through the region, playing both an important role in the fertility of the municipality.

The municipality is divided into 9 ''[[consejo popular (Cuba)|consejos populares]]'' (i.e. "popular councils"): the main town of Camajuaní (municipal seat, divided in [[Camajuaní I|C. I]] and [[Camajuaní II|C. II]]), and the villages of [[Aguada de Moya, Cuba|Aguada de Moya]], [[José María Pérez, Cuba|José María Pérez]], [[Luis Arcos Bergnes, Cuba|Luis Arcos Bergnes]] (or Carmita), [[San Antonio de las Vueltas]] (or Vueltas), [[Taguayabón]], [[Vega Alta, Cuba|Vega Alta]], and [[Batalla de Santa Clara]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nacional |first=Equipo de Producción |date=2020-03-18 |title=Portal del Ciudadano en Camajuaní - Sobre nosotros |url=https://soycamajuani.gob.cu/es/nuestra-region/region-camajuani/sobre-nosotros |access-date=2023-05-18 |website=Portal del Ciudadano en Camajuaní |language=es-es}}</ref> {{r|g=nb|r=The Camajuani official website says 9, but other sources say 13. ex.{{r|r={{cite web|url=https://fpalmareal.com/villa-clara/|title=VILLA CLARA|website=Fundacion Palma Real|access-date=2023-05-18|archive-date=2023-05-18|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230518171040/https://fpalmareal.com/villa-clara/|url-status=dead}}}}}}

==Demographics==
In 2004, the municipality of Camajuaní had a population of 63,544.<ref name=atenas>{{Cite web |url=http://www.atenas.inf.cu/todo/Estadisticas/TABLA%20No_3balance.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060714001016/http://www.atenas.inf.cu/todo/Estadisticas/TABLA%20No_3balance.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=2006-07-14 |title=2004 Population trends, by Province and Municipality |author=Atenas.cu |year=2004 |accessdate=2007-10-09 |language=es }}</ref> With a total area of {{convert|614|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}},<ref name=statoids>{{Cite web|url=http://www.statoids.com/ycu.html|title=Municipios of Cuba|author=Statoids|date=July 2003| accessdate=2007-10-09}}</ref> it has a population density of {{convert|103.5|/km2|/sqmi|abbr=on}}.

==Economy==
[[File:CCS “Raúl Torres Acosta” sign.jpg|thumb|left|[[CCS Raúl Torres Acosta]] in [[Vega Alta, Cuba|Vega Alta]]]]
[[File:SugarCamajuani.JPG|thumb|right|Sugarcane truck passing through Camajuaní]]
[[Image:PradoCamajuani.JPG|thumb|right|Camajuaní's "[[Prado]]" avenue]]
Main economic activity is agricultural in nature, [[sugarcane]] and high quality [[tobacco]] have been key.

The foundation of the town was a natural consequence of the establishment of train station constructed by "Ferrocarriles Unidos de Caibarién", (United Railways of [[Caibarién]]) in 1864, this node connected the valley where the city now is with the port in Caibarien at the North, [[Santa Clara, Cuba|Santa Clara]] on the South, Vega Alta and [[Placetas]] west and east respectively. The valley soon gain agricultural importance with important productions of Tobacco and Sugar. Most of the biggest sugar mills in the island were located in the Camajuani region, and its productions were exported to the rest of the country and the world using this port located in the near coastal city of Caibarien.

Camajuaní’s cooperatives include 17 [[UBPC]], 33 [[CCS (cooperative)|CCS]], and 15 [[CPA (agriculture)|CPA]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Portal del Ciudadano en Camajuaní - Sobre nosotros |url=https://www.soycamajuani.gob.cu/es/nuestra-region/region-camajuani/sobre-nosotros/amp |access-date=2023-05-29 |website=www.soycamajuani.gob.cu}}</ref> Major cooperatives include [[CPA Benito Ramirez]] in [[Consejo popular|CP]] Vega Alta, [[UBPC Crescencio Valdés]] in CP Batalla de Santa Clara,  [[CPA Roberto Rodríguez]] in CP Batalla de Santa Clara, [[CCS Julio Antonio Mella (Camajuaní)|CCS Julio Antonio Mella]] in [[Guajén, Cuba|Guajén]], CP Aguada de Moya, and much more.

==Culture==
[[File:Bienvenidos a Camajuaní (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|Welcome sign in Camajuaní, with the Sapos y Chivas (Toads and Goats), which represents the [[parrandas]] of Camajuaní]]
Camajuaní along with Remedios, Vueltas, Taguayabón and Caibarien, all located in the same region and just few kilometers from one each other are famous due to the [[parrandas]], a carnival like party with origins date back to the 18th century. Every Parranda has its own difference in every town, but they share some similarities as well; like division of the population in two neighborhoods that compete each year in best decorative flat, plaza works or "Wall of lights" (a structure meant to support a display of lights or fireworks), traditional dance and fireworks display. In the case of Camajuani the city is divided in Chivos (Goats) and Sapos (Toads).

==Transportation==
Camajuaní has 3 total state highways, being [[Highway 4–321 (Cuba)|Highway 4–321]], from Crucero Carmita–Camajuaní–Taguayabón–Palenque, [[Highway 4–401 (Cuba)|Highway 4–401]], from Camajuaní–José María Pérez, and the [[Highway 4–I–3 (Cuba)|Highway 4–I–3]], from Pavón–Vueltas–Taguayabón–Palenque, with other paved and unpaved roads linking other towns.
