==The municipality==
[[File:SraGuadCamotepec03.JPG|thumb|Parish church in Camotepec]]
The [[Zacatlán|city of Zacatlán]] is the seat of government for itself and other communities, forming a municipality which covers {{cvt|512.82|km2}}.  This municipality borders the municipalities of [[Chiconcuautla (municipality)|Chiconcuautla]], [[Huauchinango]], [[Aquixtla]], [[Chignahuapan (municipality)|Chignahuapan]], [[Ahuacatlán, Puebla|Ahuacatlán]], [[Tepetzintla, Puebla|Tepetzintla]], [[Tetela de Ocampo]] and [[Ahuazotepec]] with the state of [[Hidalgo (state)|Hidalgo]] to the west. The municipal government is formed by a municipal president, an officer called a síndico, and eight representatives called regidors. It has commissions related to governing, taxes, health, education, industry and commerce, public works, environment and agriculture.<ref name="encmuc">{{cite web |url= http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/puebla/Mpios/21208a.htm |title= Zacatlán |year= 2009 |work= Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México Puebla |publisher= Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal  and Gobierno del Estado de Puebla |location= Mexico |language= es |access-date= March 9, 2012 |archive-date= July 24, 2011 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110724164512/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/puebla/Mpios/21208a.htm |url-status= dead }}</ref>

The population of the municipality has grown from 62,788 in 1995 to 76,296 in 2010. About ninety percent of the population is Catholic with the rest Protestant or Evangelist.<ref name="encmuc"/> The main indigenous ethnicities in the area are [[Nahua peoples|Nahua]] and [[Totonac]], with 7,450 people who spoke an indigenous language as of 2005.<ref name="encmuc"/> Most of the indigenous population lives outside of the city of Zacatlán, especially in San Miguel Tenango.<ref name="festejo">{{cite news |title= Festejo a la fertilidad |author=Carmen González |newspaper=Mural |location=Guadalajara, Mexico |date=August 6, 2010 |page=4 |language=es |trans-title=Celebrating fertility }}</ref><ref name="lenguanah">{{cite web |url= http://www.sil.org/Mexico/nahuatl/zacatlan/00e-NahuatlZacatlan-nhi.htm |title= Náhuatl de Zacatlán, Ahuacatlán y Tepetzintla |author=Pedro Hernández Hernández |publisher= Instituto Lingüistico de Verano de México AC |location=Mexico |language=es |access-date=March 9, 2012}}</ref> Nahuatl is the dominant indigenous language, locally called Mehcanohtlahtol. It is estimated that there are nearly 17,000 Nahuatl speakers in Zacatlán and the neighboring municipalities of Ahuacatlán and Tepetzintla. The Nahuatl dialect spoken here is closest to that spoken in [[Zacapoaxtla]] and Huauchinango.<ref name="lenguanah"/>

[[File:Nahuas en Zacatlán.jpg|thumb|Nahua men in the municipality]]
Traditional dress for women includes short sleeved blouses with embroidery around the neck (tlahmach cmaisahtli), a white poncho called a [[quezquémetl]], also embroidered and a black skirt or wrap with stripes (ilpicatl), which is often accompanies by large numbers of necklaces, ribbons in the hair, blue [[huarache (shoe)|huarache sandals]], large earrings and sometimes with a wrap for carrying small children. For men, it consists of pants and shirt of undyed cotton, a hat of palm fronds, huaraches and a black or brown wool overcoat.<ref name="encmuc"/> Variations in the clothing style and embroidery generally indicates the wearers' origin.<ref name="lenguanah"/>

Traditional dishes in the municipality include "pinchón del campesino" [[mixote]] made with chicken, rabbit or beef, [[Sope (food)|sope]]s, [[chalupa]]s, [[tlacoyo]]s and eggs with chili pepper. Traditional drinks include hard apple cider and other fruit wines.<ref name="encmuc"/>

The municipality has 178 education centers from preschool to high school along with vocational training sites. There are 61 preschools, 69 primary schools, 37 middle schools, eight high schools/vocational school and two adult education centers. There are also thirteen bilingual/bicultural schools at the primary school level.<ref name="encmuc"/> There is also a technological college called the [[Instituto Tecnológico Superior de la Sierra Norte de Puebla]].<ref name="itssnp">{{cite web |url= http://www.itssnp.edu.mx/ |title= Instituto Tecnológico Superior de la Sierra Norte de Puebla |location=Zacatlán, Puebla |language=es |access-date=March 9, 2012}}</ref>

Federal Highway 119 passes through the municipality connecting the set with Ahuazotepec, Huauchinango, [[Juan Galindo (municipality)|Juan Galindo]], [[Xicotepec (municipality)|Xicotepec]], [[Jalpan, Puebla|Jalpan]], Venustiano Carranza onto the cities of [[Poza Rica]] and [[Tlaxcala, Tlaxcala|Tlaxcala]]. A smaller highway connects the seat with Zacapoaxtla.<ref name="encmuc"/> There is bus service to Tlaxcala, [[Tulancingo]] and [[Mexico City]].<ref name="finsemana">{{cite news |title= Fin de Semana: Zacatlan de las Manzanas |author=Edgar Anaya |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |date=September 29, 2002 |page=2 |language=es |trans-title=Weekend: Zacatlan de las Manzanas }}</ref>

Major towns or settlements located close to the city (and municipal seat) of Zacatlán include Atzingo (estimated population around 2,600), Jicolapa (population 1,500), San Pedro Atmatla (population 1,500), Tlatempa (population  1,700), and {{ill|Tomatlán, Puebla|lt=Tomatlán|es|Tomatlán (Puebla)}} (population 1,700). In order of increasing distance from Zacatlán are the towns or settlements of Xoxoonacatla (population 1,700), Xilotzingo (population 1,200), San Miguel Tenango (population 1,350), and Tepeixco (population 1,300) with the town of Camotepec located near the Piedras Encimadas Valley with a population of about 1,800. Agriculture and livestock are the dominant economic activities across all population centers within the municipality,<ref name="encmuc"/> however the economy of the city Zacatlán is more diversified, and is noted for [[Relojes Centenario]], the first clock factory located in Latin America.<ref name="biografia">{{cite web |title=Biografía Alberto Olvera Hernández |trans-title=Alberto Olvera Hernández biography |url=http://www.centenario.com.mx/ |accessdate=March 15, 2012 |publisher=Relojes Centenario |language=Spanish |location=Zacatlán, Puebla}}</ref><ref name="artesania">{{cite news |author=Harry Miller |date=January 9, 2005 |title=Mexico Channel / Artesania que mide el tiempo: [1] |trans-title=Mexico Channel/Handcrafts that measure time |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |page=13 |language=Spanish}}</ref><ref name="mexdes">{{cite web |author=Leonor López |title=Relojes Centenario. La magia de la exactitud |trans-title=Centenario Clocks. The magic of exactness |url=http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/relojes-centenario.-la-magia-de-la-exactitud.html |accessdate=March 15, 2012 |publisher=Mexico Desconocido magazine |language=Spanish |location=Mexico City}}</ref>

The Zacatlán municipality is particularly noted for its production of apples which have been grown in the area since 1600, with the city of Zacatlán surrounded by orchards containing an estimated 700,000 apple trees.<ref name="entrada">{{cite news |author=Francisco Ortiz |date=July 26, 2003 |title=Zacatlan: Entrada a pasajes de la historia |trans-title=Zacatlan: Entrance to the passages of history |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |page=14 |language=es}}</ref><ref name="reto">{{cite news |author=Maryell Ortiz de Zarate |author2=Ricardo Diazmunoz |date=August 8, 1999 |title=Zacatlan: Reto a la imaginacion |trans-title=Zacatlan: Challenge to the imagination |newspaper=Reforma |location=Mexico City |page=12 |language=es}}</ref> Other soft fruits and nuts grown include plums (with an estimated 300,000 trees), peaches, pears (150,000 trees) and also walnuts.<ref name="reto" /> Livestock mostly consists of cattle and sheep for wool, with some mules and domestic fowl, while fishing and fish farming are also important economic activities, especially in the towns of Xoxanacatla and Tepeixco which produce [[trout]] and [[carp]]. There is also some beekeeping as well.<ref name="encmuc2">{{cite web |year=2009 |title=Zacatlán |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/puebla/Mpios/21208a.htm |access-date=March 9, 2012 |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México Puebla |publisher=Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal  and Gobierno del Estado de Puebla |language=es |location=Mexico |archive-date=July 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110724164512/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/puebla/Mpios/21208a.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref>
