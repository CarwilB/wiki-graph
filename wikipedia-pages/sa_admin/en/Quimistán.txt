'''Quimistán''' is a [[Municipalities of Honduras|municipality]] in [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara Department]] of [[Honduras]]. It covers an area of {{cvt|745.3|km2}} and had a population of 47,993 inhabitants according to the 2013 national census. It is the most populous municipality in Santa Bárbara department.

==History==
The region was part of the region conquered by the conquistador [[Pedro de Alvarado]] in the early 16th century CE. In 1791, it is mentioned as part of the curato (ecclesiastical jurisdiction) of Petoa. Later, it was part of the San Pedro partido, which later became [[San Pedro Sula]]. The word Quimistan is from Mexican roots and means "Place of mice or spies".<ref name="Edu">{{cite web|title=Municipio de Quimistan, Santa Bárbara|url=https://edu-honduras.info/Los-Departamentos/16-Santa-Barbara/Quimist%C3%A1n.html|work=Edu-Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026|lang=es}}</ref><ref name="Red">{{cite web|url=https://redhonduras.hn/en/geography/municipality-Quimistan/|title=Municipality of Quimistan|work=Red Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref>

== Geography ==
Quimistan is located in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] in Honduras.<ref name="Red"/><ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/|title=Administrative divisions of Honduras|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Petoa]] and [[San Marcos, Santa Bárbara|San Marcos]] to the south, [[Azacualpa]] and [[Macuelizo]] to the west, San Pedro Sula to the west, and [[Omoa]] to the north.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/> The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|745.3|km2}}.<ref name="Edu"/>

== Demographics ==
At the time of the 2013 census, Quimistán had a total population of 47,993 inhabitants, of which 24,158 (50.3%) were male and 23,835 (49.7%) were female.<ref>{{cite web|url=
https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/santa_b%C3%A1rbara/1618__quimist%C3%A1n/|title=Quimistan|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref><ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=20}} It is the most populous municipality in Santa Bárbara department.<ref name="CP"/> Of the total population, 14,445 (30.1%) was classified as urban and 33,549 (69.9%) lived in rural.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=20}}

Of the total population, 6.16% were classified as [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and rest as non-indigenous.<ref name="CP"/> By broad age group, 37.6% of the population were aged 0–14, 57.6% were aged 15–64, and 4.8% were aged 65 and over. The median age was 20.3 years and the mean age was 24.8 years. Among the population aged 15 and over (29,951 persons), the illiteracy rate was recorded as 17.6% as against 22.2% for the department.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}} The municipality had 10,778 occupied private dwellings,<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=34}} with an average household size of 4.3 persons.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}}

==Culture==
The municipality celebrates its annual festival in June in honour of its patron [[John the Baptist|San Juan Bautista]].<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/>
