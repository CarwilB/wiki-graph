'''Santa Bárbara''' is a [[Cantons of Costa Rica|canton]] in the [[Heredia Province|Heredia]] province of [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The head city is in [[Santa Bárbara District|Santa Bárbara]] district.

== History ==
Santa Bárbara was created on 29 September 1882 by decree 21.<ref name="crevoterritorial" />

<!-- History section translated from Spanish Wikipedia -->
Santa Bárbara was originally populated by the Huetares, who lived in the Barva region.<ref name=RHC>{{cite news|title=Reseña Histórica del Cantón|url=http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|access-date=23 May 2014|newspaper=Municipalidad de Santa Bárbara|date=2010|language=es|archive-date=22 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522195138/http://www.santabarbara.go.cr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=105&Itemid=133|url-status=dead}}</ref> When the Spanish settled, the Huetares were forced to work on Spanish haciendas where many crops were produced, including corn, beans, legumes, cotton, sugarcane, and sarsaparilla.<ref name=RHC />

While looking for an outlet to the [[Caribbean Sea]], Joaquín Mora Fernández (not to be confused with the future president), conducted the first documented European explorations of the area.<ref name=RMHV>{{cite news|last=Hernández Vargas|first=Rose Marie|title=Reseña histórica de Santa Bárbara de Heredia|url=http://www.uned.ac.cr/extension/index.php/extension-en-accion/identidad-cultural/105-resena-historica-de-santa-barbara-de-heredia|access-date=28 May 2014|newspaper=UNED: Extensión en acción|date=6 November 2013|language=Es}}</ref> Mora Fernández established a base within the general area of the canton.<ref name=RMHV />

The first haciendas in the area are dated to January 19, 1663, when Joseph Sandoval Ocampo, among others, was given a farming and pasture concession from the capital city of [[Cartago, Costa Rica|Cartago]].<ref name=RMHV />

By the time [[Alajuela]] was founded in 1782, the present district of San Pedro, which were then collectively called Targuás or Targuases, are mentioned. At the time, there were seventy-six men and sixteen women in Targuás.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

The name of the San Pedro district name is mentioned in 1819, according to a government document that states Juan Pablo Lara purchased land in the district on which to build a home and a farm.<ref name=RHC />

Two years later, Churruca (also known as Surruco) is mentioned in documents from 1821. Churruca was the name of the Santa Bárbara canton before it took the current name.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />
[[File:Costa Rica WDL11321.png|thumb|Santa Bárbara on an 1890 map of Costa Rica]]
According to a local legend, Santa Bárbara was chosen for the region's patron saint because a resident bought an image of [[Saint Barbara]] from a Franciscan and made an altar in her home. During a terrible rainy season in Santa Bárbara, Saint Barbara heard the prayers of the resident. Little by little, her neighbors became convinced of that Saint Barbara was answering her prayers.

The canton was established by a government resolution of October 3, 1855.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

Some of the early residents of Santa Bárbara include Ambrose Arias, Juan Cortez, Andres Murillo, Gregory Cuadra, Salvador Sanchez, Jose Maria Palma, Juan Bastos, Victorio Cortes, Joaquin Gutierrez, Jose Angel Sanchez, Jose Dolores Gonzalez and Diego Murillo.

It is unknown when [[Coffee production in Costa Rica|coffee production]] began in Santa Bárbara. Shortly after 1842, resident Víctor Aguilar started the first coffee farm in the canton at "El Paso del Guayabo" ("Guayabo Steps"), followed by José Zamora  in the Porrosati River lowlands. The first harvests were processed in San Joaquín, and it soon became the most important crop of the canton.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo 28">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|page=28|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|access-date=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

By 1846, there were four major districts of Santa Bárbara.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />  There were 1,500 residents, with 1,000 living in San Pedro. In 1854, the site of the first church was chosen because it was in the center of the four major areas. The first church is presumed to have been located two blocks south of where the current Santa Bárbara Church is.

The most infamous resident of Santa Bárbara, where he was born and later died, was [[Beltrán Cortés|Beltrán Dalay Cortés Carvajal]]. Cortés Carvajal murdered two doctors who had performed a surgery on him ten years before.

== Geography ==
Santa Bárbara has an area of {{formatnum:53.21}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |access-date=26 September 2020 |archive-date=16 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200916045223/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and a mean elevation of {{formatnum:1177}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

The elongated canton begins midway between the cities of [[Alajuela]] and [[Heredia, Costa Rica|Heredia]], where the Segundo River marks its southern boundary. It climbs through foothills northward to the Desengaño River high in the [[Cordillera Central (Costa Rica)|Cordillera Central]] (Central Mountain Range).

===Geology===
The Santa Bárbara cantón is constructed largely of volcanic rock from the Holocene Period of the [[Cenozoic]] Era. The fluvial system of Santa Bárbara generally flows toward the [[Pacific Ocean]] via the [[Grande de Tárcoles River]] river basin. The Santa Bárbara cantón forms part of the larger Origen Volcánico geomorphic region, including the sub-units of [[Barva Volcano]] and the Central Valley's Relleno Volcánico.

For the [[Costa Rica 2011 Census|2011 census]], Santa Bárbara had a population of {{formatnum:36243}} inhabitants.<ref name="censo2011">{{cite web |language=es |title=Censo. 2011. Población total por zona y sexo, según provincia, cantón y distrito |url=https://www.inec.cr/censos/censos-2011 |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |access-date=26 September 2020}}</ref>

Jesús was the most populous district with 8,791 residents.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> 58% of the population lived in areas marked as rural, while 42% lived in urban areas.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> 15% of residents seeking employment were unemployed,<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" /> while 68% of residents worked in agriculture.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo 28" />

Population growth has placed strains on infrastructure, such as the quality of roads.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />{{rp|17}} Should its population growth continue, the canton is expected to have a population of 41,725 in 2030.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo" />

The canton has one of the highest standards of living in country as determined by the [[Human Development Index]].<ref name=oportunidades>{{cite news|last=Vindes|first=Leticia|title=Heredia concentra las mejores oportunidades, pero falla en seguridad|url=http://wvw.elfinancierocr.com/ef_archivo/2011/octubre/30/economia2957816.html|access-date=23 May 2014|newspaper=El Financiero|date=27 October 2011|location=San Jose|language=es|archive-date=24 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140524004138/http://wvw.elfinancierocr.com/ef_archivo/2011/octubre/30/economia2957816.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> The same index, however, ranks Santa Bárbara as one of the twenty least secure cantons.

According to the United Nations, Santa Bárbara has a literacy rate of 98.4.<ref name=atlas>{{cite book|title=Atlas del desarrollo humano cantonal de Costa Rica 2011|date=2011|publisher=Programa de las Naciones Unidas para el Desarrollo PNUD|location=San José|isbn=978-9968-794-58-9|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/atlas.pdf|access-date=23 May 2014|language=es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002827/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/atlas.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> 100% of elementary-age children and 74.8% of secondary-age children are enrolled in school.

==Education and community organizations==
Within the canton of Santa Bárbara, there are many different community service organizations. Such groups include a [[Red Cross]] committee, nine [[Alcoholics Anonymous]] groups, six sports committees, twelve local health committees, nine local church committees, a [[Lions Clubs International]] branch, a public library, and organizations for senior citizens.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo 47">{{cite book|title=Plan de Desarrollo Humano Local 2010-2020, Cantón Santa Bárbara|date=August 2009|publisher=Proyecto de Fortalecimiento Municipal y Descentralización|page=17|url=http://pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|access-date=27 May 2014|language=Es|archive-date=5 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140605002755/http://www.pnud.or.cr/mapa-cantonal/index.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> In addition, the Municipality and Ministry of Health maintain community outreach programs.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo 47" />

Within the canton, there are fifteen public and three private elementary schools. There are three high schools, including Liceo de Santa Bárbara, and Liceo el Roble.<ref name="Plan Desarrollo 47" />

Like most of Costa Rica, the church with the largest following is the [[Catholic Church]]. There are numerous churches within the district, organized by Eucharistic communities, reading groups, youth groups, and so forth. In addition, there are several [[Protestant Christianity|Protestant]] churches, including two churches for [[Jehovah's Witnesses]].

==Sports==

===Athletics===
The municipality recently funded a professional athletics team led by Edrey Campos with over 300 active students and formal equipment. The team has qualified to National Selection. {{citation needed|date=February 2021}}

===Soccer===
Santa Bárbara has an active sporting community. Beginning in 1943, Club Sport Barbareño represented Santa Bárbara in the first division of the national soccer league. The team changed its name to Asociación Deportiva Barbareña (Bárbara Sports Association) in 1984. The team plays within the national [[Costa Rican Primera División|Costa Rican soccer hierarchy]]. Between 1962 and 1975, it was a third division team. Between 1975 and 1981, as well as 1993 and 1997, it was a second division team. Between 1997 and 2004, it was a first division team. It currently plays in the third division at the Carlos Alvarado Villalobos Stadium in Santa Bárbara, which holds 1,500 spectators.{{citation needed|date=February 2021}}

Another team from [[San Pedro de Santa Bárbara]], Asociación Deportiva Fraternidad de Santa Bárbara, played within the third and second divisions beginning in 1947 until its disintegration in the late 1990s.{{citation needed|date=February 2021}}

===Volleyball===
The municipality is also home to the Asociación Deportiva Barbareña de Voleibol (ASBAVOL) (Santa Bárbara Volleyball Sports Association), one of the country's most successful volleyball clubs.<ref>{{cite news|last=Luis Rodríguez C.|first=Jose|title=Atenas será el rival de Santa Bárbara en la final del voleibol femenino|url=http://www.nacion.com/deportes/otros-deportes/Atenas-Santa-Barbara-voleibol-femenino_0_1400860055.html|access-date=16 April 2014|newspaper=La Nacion (Costa Rica)|date=7 March 2014|location=San Jose|language=Es}}</ref> The clube began in 1992,<ref name="ASBAVOL Historia">ASBAVOL.org: [http://www.asbavol.org/historia Historia] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140522155527/http://www.asbavol.org/historia |date=2014-05-22 }}, Retrieved: 22 May 2014</ref> winning numerous championships and tournaments in different categories.<ref name="ASBAVOL Historia" /><ref>{{cite news|last=Araya N.|first=José Fernando|title=Santa Bárbara alcanzó el tricampeonato del voleibol femenino al derrotar a Atenas|url=http://www.nacion.com/deportes/otros-deportes/Santa-Barbara-tricampeonato-derrotar-Atenas_0_1401059981.html|access-date=22 May 2014|newspaper=La Nacion|date=8 March 2014|location=San Jose|language=es}}</ref> ASBAVOL players regularly join the [[Costa Rica women's national volleyball team]].
