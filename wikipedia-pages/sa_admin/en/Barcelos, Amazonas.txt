'''Barcelos''' (also '''Barcellos'''), formerly '''Mariuá''', is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], [[northern Brazil]]. Its population was 27,638 (2020) and its area is {{convert|122,476|km2|mi2}}.<ref>[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics|IBGE]] - {{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php |title=Archived copy |accessdate=August 3, 2014 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070109005631/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php |archivedate=January 9, 2007 }}</ref> It is the second largest municipality in [[Brazil]] (behind [[Altamira, Pará]]), equivalent in size to [[New York (state)|New York]] state in the United States and slightly larger than [[North Korea]].

Since 1994, Barcelos has been the host of an annual festival celebrating [[Fishkeeping|ornamental fish]],<ref>{{Cite web|url = http://www.tfhmagazine.com/details/articles/hope-along-the-rio-negro-part-1-the-view-from-manaus.htm|title = Hope Along the Rio Negro Part 1: The View from Manaus|date = March 2007|accessdate = 9 August 2015|website = Tropical Fish Magazine|publisher = |last = Carey|first = Randy}}</ref> which is a significant source of income for the region. [[Project Piaba|Project ''Piaba'']] uses Barcelos as the starting point for their annual research expedition into the [[Rio Negro (Amazon)|Rio Negro]] area.<ref>{{Cite web|url = http://projectpiaba.org/what-we-do/brazilamazon/|title = Project Piaba|date = |accessdate = 9 August 2015|website = Project Piaba|publisher = |last = |first = }}</ref> The region is the biggest exporter of this particular fish in Brazil with over 19 million specimens exported into [[Europe]], [[Asia]] and the [[United States|USA]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Visit the city of Barcelos in Amazonas Brazil {{!}} Brol.com |url=https://www.brol.com/visit-the-city-of-barcelos-in-amazonas-brazil/ |access-date=2024-10-07 |language=en-US}}</ref> Barcelos is known for [[Ecotourism]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barcelos |url=https://www.amazonastur.am.gov.br/barcelos/ |access-date=2024-10-07 |language=pt-BR}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:Vila de Barcelos, Rio Negro.jpg|left|thumb|287x287px|Barcelos, 1783–1792.]]
Barcelos was originally named Mariuá, village of the Manaus Indians. When the captaincy of São José do Rio Negro was formed in 1755, [[Francisco Xavier de Mendonça Furtado]], Governor General of [[State of Grão-Pará and Maranhão|state of Grao Para e Maranhao]] chose the ''aldeia'' de São José do Javari, a village founded by Carmelite Friar Matias São Boaventuras in 1728 near the mouth of the [[Javari River]], as its capital. It was the capital of the captaincy of São José do Rio Negro from 1755 to 1791, and again from 1799 to 1808.

==Geography==
The municipality area is 122,476 square kilometres (47,288 sq mi), making it the second largest municipality in Brazil (behind Altamira, Pará), equivalent in size to New York state in the United States and slightly larger than North Korea.
The municipality contains part of the [[Amanã Sustainable Development Reserve]].<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|RDS Amanã – ISA}}|language=Portuguese |title=RDS Amanã|publisher=ISA: Instituto Socioambiental |url=https://uc.socioambiental.org/uc/337|accessdate=2016-05-05}}</ref>
It also contains part of the [[Amazonas National Forest]].<ref>{{citation|language=Portuguese |title=Unidade de Conservação: Floresta Nacional do Amazonas|publisher=MMA: Ministério do Meio Ambiente |url=http://sistemas.mma.gov.br/cnuc/index.php?ido=relatorioparametrizado.exibeRelatorio&relatorioPadrao=true&idUc=114|accessdate=2016-05-19}}</ref>
It contains the {{convert|833352|ha|adj=on}} [[Rio Unini Extractive Reserve]], created in 2006.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|RESEX Rio Unini – ISA}}|language=Portuguese
