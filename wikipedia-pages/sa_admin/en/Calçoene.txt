'''Calçoene''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|kawsoˈeni}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the east of the state of [[Amapá]], [[Brazil]]. It is located on the [[Atlantic Ocean]] in the [[Amazon jungle]] basin near [[French Guiana]]. Calçoene covers {{convert|14,269|km2|mi2}} and has a population is 11,306.<ref>[https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/ap/calcoene/panorama IBGE 2020]</ref> The name Calçoene is a corruption of "Calço N" (North Wedge, one of four mining zones defined by the Brazilian Government at the beginning of the 20th century).<ref>Amapá Digital: [http://www.amapadigital.net/calcoene.php Calçoene]</ref>

The city has the highest rainfall of any in Brazil, with an annual average of {{convert|4165|mm|in|}}. Calçoene is noted for its [[Amazon Stonehenge|ancient megalithic observatory]], often referred to as the "Amazon Stonehenge".<ref name="terra">{{cite news|url=http://noticias.terra.com.br/ciencia/interna/0,,OI1324506-EI299,00.html|title=Embrapa identifica cidade mais chuvosa do Brasil|date=2014-12-05 |publisher=[[Terra Networks|Notícias Terra]] |language=Portuguese |accessdate=2009-01-31}}</ref>

== History ==
The borders between French Guiana and Brazil were not clear, and in the 19th century, it was decided that the area between the [[Amazon River|Amazon]] and the [[Oyapock River]] was a neutral territory. Paul Quartier had a meeting with the village chiefs of [[Cunani, Calçoene|Cunani]] and Carsewenne (nowadays: Calçoene) in 1885.<ref name="conflict">{{cite journal|url=https://www.persee.fr/doc/outre_1631-0438_2011_num_98_372_4577 |title=Le Contesté franco-brésilien : enjeux et conséquences d'un conflit oublié entre la France et le Brésil|author=Stéphane Granger|year=2011|journal=Outre-Mers. Revue d'histoire |language=fr|pages=162-163}}</ref><ref name="king"/> In 1886, the [[Republic of Independent Guiana]]<ref name="king">{{cite web|url=https://www.une-saison-en-guyane.com/bk03/la-republique-de-counani-lhomme-qui-voulut-etre-roi/|title=La république de Counani: L’homme qui voulut être roi|website=Une Saison en Guyane|access-date=30 March 2021|language=fr|url-access=limited|archive-date=6 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210606072617/https://www.une-saison-en-guyane.com/bk03/la-republique-de-counani-lhomme-qui-voulut-etre-roi/|url-status=dead}}</ref> was founded by a group of French adventurers and two village chiefs with Cunani as the capital. The unrecognised republic lasted until 1891.<ref name="times">{{cite news|url=https://opinionator.blogs.nytimes.com/2012/01/16/the-loneliness-of-the-guyanas/ |title=The Loneliness of the Guyanas|website=New York Times|access-date=30 March 2021}}</ref> In 1900, the territory was awarded to Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://international.loc.gov/intldl/brhtml/br-1/br-1-1.html |title=Timeline Brazil|website=Library of Congress|access-date=31 March 2021}}</ref> Calçoene became a municipality in 1956.<ref name="municipality">{{cite web|url=https://calcoene.portal.ap.gov.br/conteudo/municipio/historia|title=História|website=Municipality of Calçoene|access-date=31 March 2021|language=pt|archive-date=15 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210515080709/https://calcoene.portal.ap.gov.br/conteudo/municipio/historia|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Geography ==
Calçoene is bordered on the north and east by the [[Atlantic Ocean]], to the south by the municipalities of [[Amapá]] and [[Pracuúba]], and to the west by the municipalities of [[Oiapoque]] and [[Serra do Navio]]. Calçoene is {{Convert|272|km|mi}} from the state capital of [[Macapá]].<ref name="g"/>
The municipality contains 23.23% of the {{convert|2369400|ha|adj=on}} [[Amapá State Forest]], a sustainable use conservation unit established in 2006.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|FES do Amapá – ISA}}|language=Portuguese

== Tourism ==
One of the most popular tourist attraction is the Goiabal beach on the [[Atlantic Ocean]], which is located 14 kilometers from the town of Calçoene.<ref name="municipality"/> The beach is 4 kilometres long and the water is dark due to the influence of the Amazon.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.estadosecapitaisdobrasil.com/amapa/praias-do-amapa/ |title=Praia de Goiabal |website=Estados e Capitais Do Brasil|access-date=31 March 2021|language=pt}}</ref>

=== Calçoene megalithic observatory ===
[[File:Calçoene, Stonehenge brasileira, Amapá.jpg|thumb|left]]
{{main|Parque Arqueológico do Solstício}}
In May 2006, archeologists announced they had found a pre-colonial [[Amazon Stonehenge|astronomical observatory]], possibly 500 to 2,000 years old, near Calçoene. The age is based on pottery sherds on site that have been dated to 2,000 years old. The site is on a hill and has 127 large stones blocks of granite, each {{Convert|3|m|ft}} high, and dug firmly into the ground. Archaeologist Mariana Petry Cabral of the [[Amapa Institute of Scientific and Technological Research]] (IEPA) said that the observatory has been designed to align with the winter [[solstice]]. Other agricultural societies also developed sophisticated ways to track important times in the solar calendar. At one time, researchers did not think any cultures in the Amazon Basin had developed such complexity as to build such a site. This has altered their thinking.<ref name="bbc">{{cite web | url = http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/americas/4767717.stm |last=Wolford |first=Ben | title = Brazilian Stonehenge discovered | year = 2006 | publisher = BBC | location = London | accessdate = 2014-11-18}}</ref>
