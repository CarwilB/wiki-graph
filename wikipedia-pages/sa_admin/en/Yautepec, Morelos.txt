'''Yautepec''' is a [[municipalities of Mexico|municipality]] located in the north-central part of the [[Mexican state]] of [[Morelos]]. The municipal seat is the city of '''Yautepec de Zaragoza'''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/cuautla/noticias/64280-propone-historiador-cambio-de-nombre-de-yautepec-de-zaragoza,-a-yautepec-de-altamirano.html |title=Propone historiador cambio de nombre de Yautepec de Zaragoza, a Yautepec de Altamirano |publisher=La Union de Morelos |last=Olivares |first=Job |date=19 Dec 2014 |access-date=Jan 8, 2019 |language=es|trans-title=Historian proposes changing the name Yautepec of Zaragoza to Yautepec of Altamirano}}</ref> It stands at {{coord|18|53|N|99|04|W|}}.

The city serves as the municipal seat for the surrounding municipality of Yautepec. In the 2020 census the municipality had a population of 105,780,<ref name="censo2020">{{cite web |title=¿Cuántos habitantes tiene... - Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/cpv/2020/resultadosrapidos/default.html?texto=Yautepec |website=inegi.org.mx |publisher=INEGI |access-date=February 23, 2021}}</ref> the fifth-largest community in the state in population (after [[Cuernavaca]], [[Jiutepec]], [[Cuautla, Morelos|Cuautla]], and [[Temixco]]). The municipality, which has an area of {{convert|203|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} reported 102,690 inhabitants in the 2015 census.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/|title=Número de habitantes|publisher=INEGI Informacion de Mexico para Niños|access-date=Dec 13, 2018|language=es|trans-title=Number of inhabitants|archive-date=August 4, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220804023637/https://cuentame.inegi.org.mx/monografias/informacion/mor/poblacion/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Yautepec de Zaragoza had 44,353	inhabitants in 2020.<ref name="censo2020"/>

Other large towns in the municipality are La Joya (population 14,126), [[Cocoyoc]] (population 10,178), [[Oaxtepec]] (population 7,097), Los Arcos (San Carlos) (population 5,736), Oacalco (population 2,543), Lázaro Cárdenas (El Empalme) (population 1,503), Itzamatitlán (population 1,366), Corral Grande (population 1,189), San Isidro (population 1,153), Ignacio Bastida (Santa Catarina Tlayca) (population 1,119), and La Nopalera (population 1,005).<ref name="censo2020"/>

Yautepec, has its etymological roots derived from ''Yautli'': Bright-colored plant with an aniseed flavor and yellow flowers in bouquets that indigenous people used for cleansing baths; ''Tepe-tl'': (hill) and "k" contraction of ''Ko'': (adverb of place); it means "On the hill of the pericón flower" in English.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html|title=Morelos - Yautepec|access-date=2019-01-08|archive-date=2019-03-07|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190307155829/http://inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> President Benito Juarez changed the name from ''La villa de Yautepec de [[Gómez Farías]]'' to ''Yautepec de Zaragoza'' (of Zaragoza) in 1869 in honor of General [[Ignacio Zaragoza]],<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/cuautla/noticias/64280-propone-historiador-cambio-de-nombre-de-yautepec-de-zaragoza,-a-yautepec-de-altamirano.html |title=Propone historiador cambio de nombre de Yautepec de Zaragoza, a Yautepec de Altamirano |website=www.launion.com.mx |access-date=Jan 8, 2019}}</ref> the general who had led the [[Cinco de Mayo]], 1862, victory over the invading French forces in Puebla.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.inside-mexico.com/gen-ignacio-zaragoza/ |title=General Ignacio Zaragoza: Cinco de Mayo Hero &#124; Inside Mexico |website=www.inside-mexico.com |access-date=Jan 8, 2019}}</ref>

==History==
The mythical love story of [[Popocatépetl]] and [[Iztaccihuatl]] is well-known. What is less known is that the princess's father preferred she date the King of Yautepec, which she did on at least one occasion.<ref name="siglo.inafed.gob.mx">http://siglo.inafed.gob.mx/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181108052453/http://siglo.inafed.gob.mx/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html |date=2018-11-08 }} (retrieved Dec 12, 2018)</ref>

===Prehispanic history===
Francisco Plancarte y Navarrete, Cuernavaca's second bishop (1898–1911), wrote ''Tamoanchan—El Estado de Morelos y El Principio de la Civilizacion en Mexico'' in 1911. In it, he proposes that the first agriculturally based settlements in Mexico appeared around 1500 [[BCE]] in a place called ''Tamoanchan'' which he associates with Morelos.<ref name="morelos37">Secretaria, p. 37</ref><ref name="gobhistoria">{{cite web |url=http://www.e-morelos.gob.mx/index2.php?action=view&art_id=475 |title=Nuestra Historia |publisher=Government of Morelos |location=Morelos |language=es |trans-title=Our History |access-date=September 9, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100618084545/http://www.e-morelos.gob.mx/index2.php?action=view&art_id=475 |archive-date=June 18, 2010 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all}}</ref> While later archaeologists do not mention Tamoanchan, the remains of an elephant-like animal dating back 6,000 years, have been found near Yautepec. These remains can be seen at the ''Museo Regional Cuauhnahuac'' ([[Palace of Cortés, Cuernavaca|Palace of Cortes]]) in Cuernavaca.

The earliest findings are on the hill of ''Atlihuayán''; the first settlers in the region were the [[Olmec]]. The Olmec were nomads, living from hunting, fishing, as well as gathering roots and wild fruit. Archaeologists Valentín López González and Ramón Piña Chan, have found an Atlihuayan figurine, now exhibited in the [[National Museum of Anthropology and History]] in Mexico City. Also, nine pyramids and a ball court have been found in ''Itzamatitlán''.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.municipios.mx/morelos/yautepec/ |title=Municipio de Yautepec de Zaragoza en Morelos |website=www.municipios.mx |access-date=Jan 8, 2019 |archive-date=January 9, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190109062834/http://www.municipios.mx/morelos/yautepec/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Yautepec and the entire Morelos Valley had a subordinate role in Mesoamerican history. From the Classic to the Postclassic the local populations developed under the influence of [[Teotihuacanos]], [[Toltecas]] and [[Nahuas]]. It is with these last ones that the Valley had a greater relevance in the regional dynamics, since it was occupied by Tlahuica population (Nahuatl language speakers), taking Cuauhnahuac  (Cuernavaca) as its capital and expanding its dominion to other zones, including Yautepec.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://inah.gob.mx/zonas/11-zona-arqueologica-de-yautepec |title=Zona Arqueológica de Yautepec |website=inah.gob.mx |access-date=Jan 8, 2019}}</ref> In 1389, the inhabitants of Yautepec, Tetlama, and [[Jiutepec]], fought the Tlahuicas.<ref name="siglo.inafed.gob.mx"/>

After the establishment of the Triple Alliance in 1438, the Tlahuica lordship was conquered and forced to pay tribute to the [[Mexicas]]. When [[Moctezuma I]] ascended the throne, he set about conquering Yautepec, forcing them to submit to the Lord of Tenochtitlan. Moctezuma built a botanical garden in ''Huaxtepec'' ([[Oaxtepec]]) in (1440–1469).<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.oaxtepec.com.mx/historia-de-oaxtepec/ |title=Historia de Oaxtepec |access-date=8 January 2019 |archive-date=16 February 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140216171020/http://www.oaxtepec.com.mx/historia-de-oaxtepec/ |url-status=bot: unknown }}</ref>

===Conquest & colonization===
The conquest of Morelos by the conquistadors under [[Hernán Cortés]] was part of the strategy to achieve the ultimate goal: the fall of the Great [[Tenochtitlán]].

Upon the arrival of the Spaniards, the Indigenous groups of the region were divided into two ''cacicazgos'': Cuauhnahuac and Huaxtepec. The conquistador sent an expedition to take the town of [[Ocuituco]]; later [[Gonzalo de Sandoval]] was sent to [[Yecapixtla]]. A year later, 1521, Cortes explored the lands submitted by Sandoval, then went to Tlalmanalco and Huaxtepec.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://morelos.gob.mx/?q=historial |title=Historia |website=morelos.gob.mx |access-date=March 6, 2023}}</ref> After some bloody fights and some peaceful deliveries, including Yautepec,<ref name="Smith2016">{{cite book|author=Michael Smith|title=At Home with the Aztecs: An Archaeologist Uncovers Their Daily Life|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=nF6aCwAAQBAJ&pg=PA105|date=22 February 2016|publisher=Routledge|isbn=978-1-317-32825-4|page=105}}</ref> Morelos fell into Spanish hands. Cortes made peace with the ''[[Cacique]]s'' in this area prior to his [[Siege of Tenochtitlan]].<ref name=Diaz>Diaz, B., 1963, The Conquest of New Spain, London: Penguin Books, {{ISBN|0140441239}}</ref>{{rp|334–337,338}}

Once the military conquest was complete, catechization was carried out by [[Dominican Order|Dominican]] friars, who began the construction of the convent of the Ascension of the Lord in 1567.<ref name="Yautepec">{{cite web |url=http://morelos.gob.mx/?q=Yautepec |title=Yautepec |website=morelos.gob.mx |access-date=March 6, 2023}}</ref> It stands out for its simplicity and austerity. This large building is unlike many others, simple in its spatial solutions and form in façade, nave of the temple, tower, and convent cloister. The church is rectangular, long, tall and rather narrow, interrupted in its length by two lateral chapels. The building has a popular Renaissance character. The choral window on the façade is rectangular and possibly the largest one of any 16th-century churches in the state. There is a large but simple bell tower in the southwest corner. The convent emphasizes the austerity and simplicity of the home or abode of the friars. There are no luxuries, no ornamentation and here lies the beauty of this set. On the southeast side is the large open chapel, perhaps the largest of the Indian chapels in the state. This chapel is beautiful although it is semi-destroyed and abandoned.<ref>Moro, Agustín. ''Catálogo gráfico de los Conventos del S. XVI en Morelos, Tomo I''. Cuernavaca: Publi Jvs, 1993, p. 41.</ref>

The [[Dominican Order|Dominican]] convent of ''Santo Domingo'' in Oaxtepec was founded by Fray Vicente de Sta. Ma. and Fray Domingo in 1533; it was the first built by that order in Morelos. This church does not have an atrium, and it was built on top of a prehispanic base some three or four meters above the level of the plaza.<ref>Moro, Agustín. ''Catálogo gráfico de los Conventos del S. XVI en Morelos, Tomo I''. Cuernavaca: Publi Jvs, 1993, p. 46</ref> Both of these monasteries were recognized by the UNESCO as [[Monasteries on the slopes of Popocatépetl|World Heritage Sites]] in 1994, and are included in the ''Ruta de los Conventos''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/por-la-ruta-de-los-conventos-de-morelos.html |title=Por la ruta de los conventos de Morelos |website=www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx |date= 27 August 2010|access-date=Jan 9, 2019}}</ref>

While cotton was the most important crop in the pre-conquest era, sugar replaced cotton after the conquest. The sugar cane haciendas of [[Cocoyoc]] and Atlihuayán were built in the 17th century. Cocoyoc is a Nahuatl word that means "place of coyotes". The city of Cocoyoc was founded in the 11th century by the Tlahuicas. After the conquest, Hernán Cortés was named Marquis of Oaxaca in recognition of his services to the Spanish crown. Cortés also married Isabel, daughter of [[Moctezuma II]]. In 1614, the Hacienda received a license from the government to establish a sugar factory pulled by horses, in order to grind and process sugarcane. Elviro Ruiz, a descendant of Isabel, sold part of her inheritance to the peasants as farmland, but the hacienda later increased its lands through purchases, negotiations, and marriages. In 1698, the Hacienda consisted of three hundred and sixty-six hectares of irrigated land, a house, a chapel, the ''trapiche'' (mill) and other buildings necessary for the operation of a sugar plantation. However, it was later reduced by forced sales by fines of tax evasion and other debts, and on more than one occasion the entire property was auctioned off. During the 18th century, Hacienda Cocoyoc was one of the twelve most important sugar mills in the country and by then it had already had numerous owners.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://es.hcocoyoc.com/historia.html |title=Historia |access-date=9 January 2019 |archive-date=29 March 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130329160544/http://es.hcocoyoc.com/historia.html |url-status=bot: unknown }}</ref>

===Independence & 19th century===
The haciendas established at Oacalco, Apanquetzalco, Atlihuayán, San Carlos Borromeo, Cocoyoc and Xochimancas generally prospered into the 19th century,<ref name="inafed.gob.mx">{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html |title=Morelos - Yautepec |website=www.inafed.gob.mx |access-date=Jan 9, 2019 |archive-date=March 7, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190307155829/http://inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/municipios/17029a.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> and the [[Mexican War of Independence]] (1810–1821) had little direct effect on the lives of the people of Yautepec.

Once Mexico achieved its independence, Yautepec became a community in the district of ''Cuautla de las Ampilpas'' in the State of Mexico. Over the next 40+ years, the country suffered through power struggles between conservatives (who favored a strong central government) and liberals (who favored a federated government) as well as several foreign invasions.<ref>http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/historia.html {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190129034834/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/historia.html |date=2019-01-29 }} accessed Jan 9. 2019</ref> In 1854 this led to the ''Plan de Cuernavaca'' which led to the ouster of dictator [[Antonio Santa Anna]] and in 1857 the [[Plan de Ayutla]] and [[Reform War|civil war]]. Yautepec in 1861-1862 is the fictional setting of ''El Zarco,'' a novel about bandits called ''Los Plateados'' written by Ignacio Manuel Altamirano and published after his death in 1901.<ref>{{Cite web |title=''El Zarco (Episodios de la vida mexicana en 1861-63)'': Detalle de la obra |website=[[Enciclopedia de la literatura en México]] |publisher=FLM – CONACULTA |url=http://www.elem.mx/obra/datos/5609 |access-date=2024-10-17}}</ref> The reality relating to the bandits who thrived during this time of anarchy, however, may not have been as thrilling and romantic as Altamirano suggests.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.moreloshabla.com/morelos/mas-municipios/la-leyenda-de-el-plateado-una-historia-de-terror-del-canon-de-lobos/ |title=Morelos Habla |website=www.moreloshabla.com |access-date=Jan 9, 2019}}</ref>

The town was briefly called, ''la Villa de Yautepec de Gomez Farias'' in honor of one of the signers of the Plan de Cuernavaca. After the French invasion and the Republic was restored, Morelos became a state in 1869 and Yautepec became a municipality. The city of Yautepec changed its name to ''Yautepec de Zaragoza'' in honor of general [[Ignacio Zaragoza]], hero of the [[Battle of Puebla]]. There is a bust of Zaragoza in ''La Plazuela de Leyva'' in the town center. The plaza itself is named for ''Francisco Leyva'', first [[Governor of Morelos]] as Yautepec became the first capital of the state (April 17, 1869).<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=2024-09-15 |title=Yautepec |url=https://www.diariodemorelos.com/noticias/tags/yautepec |access-date=2024-10-17 |website=www.diariodemorelos.com |language=es}}</ref> Leyva created the Literary and Scientific Institute of the State of Morelos with preparatory studies in agriculture and veterinary science; trade and administration; Arts and crafts; normal for teachers; and law.<ref name="ReferenceA">{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/historia.html |title=Historia - Morelos |website=www.inafed.gob.mx |access-date=Jan 9, 2019 |archive-date=January 29, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190129034834/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM17morelos/historia.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> A telegraph line between Cuernavaca and Yautepec was laid in 1877 during the administration of [[Governor Carlos Pacheco]].<ref name="ReferenceA"/>

Peace was restored under the dictatorship of [[Porfirio Diaz]], bringing prosperity to large landowners such as the Escandon family, who had belonged to the court of Emperor Maximilian.<ref name="Yautepec"/>

The actress Virginia Fábregas García was born in the Hacienda de Oacalco in the municipality of Yautepec on December 17, 1871. She was the daughter of Ricardo Fábregas of Spanish origin and of Mrs. Ursula García de Figueroa. Her vocation for the theater was manifested from adolescence when the owner of the Hacienda de Apanquetzalco built a theater in the municipal seat which was a silent witness to the first artistic steps of the talented Virginia.<ref name="inafed.gob.mx"/> As for the hacienda, it went bankrupt and its lands were bought by the Escandon family.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.goapp.mx/que-hacer-hacienda-de-apanquetzalco-965 |title=Haciendas Azucareras de Morelos |access-date=9 January 2019 |archive-date=13 February 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180213095730/https://www.goapp.mx/que-hacer-hacienda-de-apanquetzalco-965 |url-status=bot: unknown }}</ref>

===20th & 21st centuries===

One person died and the church was damaged during the [[2017 Puebla earthquake|September 19, 2017 earthquake]].<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2024-10-17 |title=Reportes rápidos después del sismo de 7.1 |url=https://www.launion.com.mx/morelos/avances/noticias/113372-reportes-rapidos-despues-del-sismo-de-7-1.html |access-date=2024-10-17 |website=La Unión de Morelos |language=es-es}}</ref>

==In popular culture==

===Movies===
Numerous national and international movies have been filmed in Yautepec. About 70 movies were filmed there during the [[Golden Age of Mexican cinema]] (1933–1964); Oacalco, Oaxtepec, and Cocoyoc were the most popular locations within the municipality.<ref name=oacalco_movies>{{cite web |last1=Hernández |first1=Rosaura |title=Oacalco, locación de Hollywood |url=https://www.elsoldecuernavaca.com.mx/local/oacalco-locacion-de-hollywood-3351175.html |website=El Sol de Cuernavaca |access-date=May 9, 2020 |language=es}}</ref>

Scenes from ''[[The Magnificent Seven]]'' (1960), directed by [[John Sturges]] and starring [[Yul Brynner]], Eli Wallach, Steve McQueen, Charles Bronson, Robert Vaughn, Brad Dexter, James Coburn, and Horst Buchholz were filmed in Oacalco.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Magnificent Seven (1960) - Notes - TCM.com |url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/15857/the-magnificent-seven#notes |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121215060649/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/15857/The-Magnificent-Seven/#notes |url-status=dead |archive-date=December 15, 2012 |website=Turner Classic Movies |access-date=May 9, 2020 |language=en}}</ref>

Parts of ''[[Under the Volcano (1984 film)]]'' (1984–1985), directed by [[John Huston]] and starring [[Albert Finney]], Jacqueline Bisset, Anthony Andrews, and Katy Jurado, based on the 1947 novel by [[Malcolm Lowry]] were filmed in Oacalco.<ref name=oacalco_movies />
