'''Lagoa Santa''' (''Holy Lagoon'') is a [[Brazil|Brazilian]] [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] and region in the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]]. It is located 37&nbsp;km north-northeast from [[Belo Horizonte]] and belongs to the mesoregion Metropolitana de Belo Horizonte and to the microregion of Belo Horizonte. In 2025 the estimated population was 81,299.<ref name="IBGE" />

==Cradle of Brazilian paleontology==
The Danish palaeontologist [[Peter Wilhelm Lund]], known as the father of Brazilian paleontology, discovered a cave filled with human bones (15 skeletons) and [[megafauna]] (very large mammals) dating to the [[Pleistocene]] era. [[Eugenius Warming|Eugen Warming]] assisted Lund 1863–1866, and described the flora of the area and the adaptations of the plants to the hazards of [[cerrado]] – drought and fire – in a work that still stands as a paradigm of ecological study ([[Eugenius Warming#Lagoa Santa|'Lagoa Santa']]). The tomb of illustrator [[Peter Andreas Brandt]], also an assistant of Lund, is located in the town.<ref>{{Cite web

The municipality contains 56% of the {{convert|2004|ha}} [[Sumidouro State Park]], created in 1980, which protects the cave where Lund made his discovery of the "Lagoa Santa Man".<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|Parque Estadual do Sumidouro – Descubra Minas}}|language=pt|title=Parque Estadual do Sumidouro|work=Descubra Minas|publisher=SENAC Minas|url=http://www.descubraminas.com.br/Turismo/ParqueApresentacao.aspx?cod_destino=839|access-date=2016-12-12|archive-date=2016-12-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161220183651/http://www.descubraminas.com.br/Turismo/ParqueApresentacao.aspx?cod_destino=839|url-status=dead}}</ref>

A century later, in the 1970s, French archeologist [[Annette Laming-Emperaire]] carried out excavations in the area and discovered the oldest human fossil in Brazil, over 11 thousand years old, given the nickname [[Luzia Woman|Luzia]].
