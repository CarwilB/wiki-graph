'''Abangares''' is a [[Cantons of Costa Rica|canton]] in the [[Guanacaste Province|Guanacaste]] province of [[Costa Rica]].<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |language=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |access-date=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |language=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref> The head city is in [[Las Juntas District|Las Juntas]] district.

== History ==
Abangares was created on 4 June, 1915 by decree 13.<ref name="crevoterritorial" />

== Geography ==
Abangares has an area of {{formatnum:675.76}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |language=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |access-date=26 September 2020 |archive-date=24 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024225203/https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |url-status=dead }}</ref> and a mean elevation of {{formatnum:154}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />
The canton begins on the coastline of the [[Gulf of Nicoya]] north of the mouth of the [[Abangares River]]. It widens between the Lajas River on the northwest and [[Lagarto River]] on the east as it reaches up into the [[Cordillera de Tilarán]].

== Gold mines ==
The Abangares canton is home to the oldest [[gold mining]] tradition in Costa Rica, dating back over a century.<ref name="glitters">{{Cite news

One of the canton's major modern producers of gold, with silver as a by-product, has been the Tres Hermanos mine, which for many years was operated by El Valiente Ascari S.A., a subsidiary of Ariel Resources Ltd. of [[Vancouver]], [[British Columbia]], [[Canada]].<ref name="geo">{{cite conference
| first =David B.
| last =Doan

| url =https://minerals.usgs.gov/minerals/pubs/country/1996/9508096.pdf
| access-date =2007-09-08}}</ref> That company filed for insolvency in 2001 and abandoned the operation while still owing its workers two months worth of wages.<ref name="glitters" /> More than 300 families continue to earn a meager living working through an independent local collective that now operates the mine.<ref name="glitters" />

A mining museum at La Sierra de Abangares with artifacts from the boom times of Costa Rican gold mining is a major tourist attraction of the canton.<ref>{{cite book
