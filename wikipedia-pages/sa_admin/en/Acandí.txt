'''Acandí''' is a town in [[Colombia]] at the northern extremity of the department of [[Chocó Department|Chocó]] in the northwest of Colombia, bordering [[Panama]] and the [[Caribbean Sea]]. It is {{convert|366|km|abbr=on}} from the department's capital, [[Quibdó]]. Its average temperature is {{convert|28|C|abbr=out}}. It was founded around 1887, and it became a municipality in 1905, previously being part of [[Turbo, Colombia|Turbo]]. The name "Acandí" is a corruption of the indigenous word "Acanti", which means "River of Stone".

It is one of the constituent territories of the [[region of Darién]], together with the towns of [[Unguía]], [[Juradó]] and the municipality of [[Riosucio, Chocó|Riosucio]] west of the [[Atrato River]]. About 87% of the population are Afro-Colombians, Blacks and Mulato, and the poverty index was estimated at 51 in 2018.<ref name="stats1"/>

Acandí has no road connection with the outside world. It is only about 5 miles (8&nbsp;km.) from the Panama border. There is a ferry connecting it with [[Necoclí]], some {{convert|35|miles}} east across the [[Gulf of Urabá]], from which there is a paved road south. This is the route for everything in Acandí that does not arrive by air. In the years leading up to the [[Haitian crisis (2018–present)|Haitian migrant crisis of 2021]], these ferries, running at capacity, were primarily transporting Haitians, who were about to enter the Panamanian [[Darién Gap]] en route to the United States.<ref>{{cite news

==History==
The name Acandí is derived from the Acandí tribe which means "River of Stones". The first the town of Acandí was founded in c. 1890 by Spanish settlers Concepción Gómez, Fermín Ávila, José Piestán, José Garrido and others with the name of San Nicolás de Titumate and was located at the western shore of the Acandí River but was constantly flooded and devastated by this river. The inhabitants moved the village to its current safer location in 1896.

The municipality of Acandí was created on August 5, 1908, by an ordinance of the same year.

==Geography==
Its urban area is by the [[Caribbean Sea]] in the northeastern part of the [[Gulf of Urabá]], some {{convert|558|km|abbr=on}} away from the capital [[Quibdó]]. The municipality has an area of {{convert|869|sqkm|abbr=on}} and borders to the north with the Caribbean Sea, to the south with the municipality of [[Unguía]] and to the west with the [[Republic of Panama]].

==Economy==
The main economic activities of Acandí inters of Added value  are Mining (41%), agriculture, livestock, hunting, forestry and fishing  (35%), manufacturing (15%) and commerce repairs and hospitality (5%).<ref name="stats1"/> The main agricultural products are [[maize]], [[Plantain (cooking)|plantain]] and [[Cassava|yuca]]. The municipality also covers the tourism hub [[Capurganá]], located near the border with [[Panama]].

==Transportation==
Acandí has a small seaport on the Caribbean Sea mainly utilizing it to communicate with other nearby ports in Colombia and Panama. The [[Atrato River]] is also used for transportation and a way to reach the Departmental capital [[Quibdó]]. The town also has a small airport, [[Alcides Fernández Airport]] (Airport class G. 1,200 m long, 10 m wide) that can receive light planes and is used primarily to fly to Quibdó or towns in the [[Antioquia Department]]. It is owned by the Colombian government agency INCORA.

Inhabitants also use the nearby airfields in Capurganá:

*Capurganá Airport – Airport class F, in the police inspection of Capurganá. 852 m long, 12 m wide. Operated by the municipality.
*El Gilcal Airport – Airport class G, 500 m long, 15 m wide. Owned by the municipality.{{citation needed|date=November 2012}}
