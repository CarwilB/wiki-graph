'''Benjamin Constant''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the [[Federative units of Brazil|Brazilian state]] of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]]. Its population was 43,935 (2020)<ref>[https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/am/benjamin-constant/panorama IBGE 2020]</ref> and its area is 8,793&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> (5,495&nbsp;mi<sup>2</sup>).<ref>[http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php IBGE website] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070109005631/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php |date=January 9, 2007 }}</ref>

==History==
The area is inhabited by [[Ticuna Indians]].  The Magüta Museum in the town is devoted to their culture and language.  In 1988 the town was the scene of the murder of 14 Ticunas in a dispute between them and logging interests.<ref>{{cite book| last = Woods | first = Sarah| title = Colombia | year = 2008| publisher = Bradt Travel Guides| page = [https://archive.org/details/colombiabradttra00wood/page/257 257] |url = https://archive.org/details/colombiabradttra00wood| url-access = registration }}</ref>

The town was named after the Brazilian revolutionary [[Benjamin Constant (military)|Benjamin Constant]] (1836–1891).

==Geography==
The town is located by the confluence of the [[Javary River]] and the [[Amazon River|Amazon]], close to the border with [[Peru]].  However, there are no customs or immigration facilities in Benjamin Constant, and entry and exit formalities take place at [[Tabatinga]] on the opposite bank of the Amazon.<ref>{{cite book| title = [[South American Handbook]] | year = 2009 |page = 644 |ISBN = 978-1-906098-36-0}}</ref>  There are no roads into Benjamin Constant and the only access is by river boat. By fast boat it is about 31 hours from [[Manaus]] (about 7 days by slow boat).  The nearest airport is at Tabatinga, 2 hours by boat.
