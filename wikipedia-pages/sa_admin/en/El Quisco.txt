'''El Quisco''' is a [[Chile]]an city and commune in [[San Antonio Province]], [[Valparaíso Region]]. Located in the country's central coast, it serves as a popular summer resort for the population of [[Santiago]] and forms part of the [[Coast of Poets]], a cultural space named after four Chilean poets: [[Pablo Neruda]], [[Vicente Huidobro]], [[Violeta Parra]] and [[Nicanor Parra]]. El Quisco is home to [[Casa de Isla Negra|La Casa de Isla Negra]], the former house of Chilean poet Pablo Neruda, which is now a museum and Neruda's burial site.

==Etymology==
The word "quisco" refers to the [[Echinopsis chiloensis]], a species of cactus native to the central coast of Chile. It is derived from the [[Quechuan languages|Quechua]] word ''khishka'', meaning "[[Thorns, spines, and prickles|spine]]."<ref>{{Cite web|date=2012-08-16|title=Echinopsis chiloensis - QUISCO|url=http://www.florachilena.cl/Niv_tax/Angiospermas/Ordenes/Caryophyllales/Cactaceae/Echinopsis%20chiloensis/Echinopsis%20chiloensis.htm|access-date=2021-01-18|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120816061039/http://www.florachilena.cl/Niv_tax/Angiospermas/Ordenes/Caryophyllales/Cactaceae/Echinopsis%20chiloensis/Echinopsis%20chiloensis.htm|archive-date=2012-08-16}}</ref>

==History==

[[File:Tralca Point Beach from Peninsula El Quisco.jpg|thumb|180px |El Quisco]]

=== Early history ===
There have been important archaeological findings from the [[Andean preceramic|pre-ceramic period]] and the late ceramic period along the coast of El Quisco. El Quisco was a centre of the {{Interlanguage link|El Bato|es|Tradición Bato}} and {{Interlanguage link|Llolleo cultures|es|Cultura Llolleo}}, among others, and their presence coincided with the dates of the [[El Molle culture]] further north.<ref>http://www.puc.cl/sw_educ/alfareria/areascult/areas232.htm, ''El Bato y Llolleo'', [[Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile]]</ref>

The Bato culture was established between 400 and 300 BC, and shared cultural traits with the Molle culture. Typical of this culture was the use of a [[tembetá]] (a metal or stone rod used in lower-lip piercings). In addition, [[Mortar and pestle|mortars]], stones used to grind vegetables and minerals, have been found. Evidence of this group has been found in Trebulco and in La Manresa, located near [[Lonquén]]. Its peak development came in 400 to 500 AD, both on the coast and in the interior, and the culture persisted in the Preandean zone until around 900 AD.

The El Molle culture was established in Chile around 300 AD among the [[Transverse Valleys]] of the rivers [[Copiapó River|Copiapó]] and [[Choapa river|Choapa]], with large migrations to the country from [[Brazil]], from the tropical [[jungle]]s of the [[Gran Chaco|Chaco]], [[Argentina|Argentine pampas]], and from [[Peru]].<ref>Cornelly, F.L., 1956: Cultura Diaguita Chilena y Cultura de El Molle. Santiago: Editorial del Pacifico</ref><ref>[http://html.rincondelvago.com/culturas-precolombinas_4.html Historia de América. Pueblos indígenas chilenos. Cultura Molle y Diaguita. Cerámica. Artesanía]</ref> The first settlements were in the interior of the country, in El Molle (in the [[Valle de Elqui]]), where the first evidence was found. They mixed with existing populations and adopted some cultural elements and physical features from them. They were the predecessors of the [[Diaguita]] culture, and by the time the Spanish arrived (1492) they had already disappeared. This culture was located in what would become regions III ([[Atacama Region|Atacama]]) and IV ([[Coquimbo Region|Coquimbo]]) of modern Chile.

The [[Aconcagua culture]] spread along the coast, near the present-day resorts of [[Algarrobo, Chile|Algarrobo]], El Quisco, [[Llolleo]], [[El Tabo]], Las Cruces, [[Rocas de Santo Domingo]], and [[Cartagena, Chile|Cartagena]].

The ceramics of these cultures are commonly called the ''Aconcagua Salmon Type'', for their orange colour with decorative designs in black paint, found in the Late ''Agroalfarero'' ("agro-potter") period of the region, between 800 and 1470, principally in pieces known as ''pucos,'' or bowls. The outer face is often a decoration representing the figure of a ''"Trinaurio",'' or whirlpool with three arms.

=== Mapuche period ===
{{Main|Picunche}} 
[[File:Playa de Los Corsarios, El Quisco.jpg|thumb|left| Quisco beach]]
More recently [[Picunche]] people lived between two important rivers: the [[Choapa river|Choapa]] and the [[Itata River|Itata]]. They were [[farmer]]s and thus sedentary, growing [[gourd]]s, [[maize]], [[bean]]s and [[chili pepper]]. Water was abundant where they lived, and the climate was hot. They raised animals, especially  [[llama]]s and [[guanaco]]s, which provided meat for food and wool for clothes. They lived in small villages, in groups of approximately 300 people. Their houses were made of mud, with roofs of [[Totora (plant)|totora]]. About 30 people lived in each house: a father, wives, children, and other close relatives. Men gave the orders and directed the work: the highest authority was the father and after him the oldest son. Only in emergencies, especially in wars,  was there a chief as a leader. Nevertheless, the villages were peaceful.<ref>Larrain, Etnogeografía, Geography of Chile, Geographic Military Institute, Santiago, 1987; XVI: 147. In this article Professor Larrain made an extensive investigation of the Picunche population, classifying them as northern and southern Picunches, the [[Promaucae]].</ref>

===Inca period===
{{Main|Qullasuyu|Inca road system}}
[[File:Banner of the Qulla Suyu (1979).svg|thumb|right|Banner of the Qulla Suyu]]

The area was part of the [[Inca Empire]], specifically of [[Qullasuyu]]. To assure their domination and introduce Inca customs, Inca sovereigns established numerous colonies of [[mitimae#Origin of the name|mitimae]]s, farmers and peaceful craftsmen brought from diverse parts of the empire: [[Aymara people|Aymara]], [[Quechua people|Quechua]] and [[Atacameño]]. These foreign settlers founded numerous villages with names that recall their places of origin: [[Limache]] (people of [[Rímac District|Rímac]] or [[Lima]]), Collahue (place of the [[Qulla people|Qulla]] people or [[Aymara people|Aymara]] people), [[Pomaire]] and different people of Atacameño origin.
<!--This section was going between [[mitimae]]s of [[Quillota]] and [[Talagante]]. The zone of Casablanca and El Quisco was close and was a step of the coastal route of them. In Casablanca, the section of the [[Inca road system]] that crosses the tideland in [[Las Dichas]], has retained the name of  ''Way of the Chicken farmers (polleros)'' (the name came from Inca soldiers taking the ''Pulla''  as a uniform), which intersects the [[Casablanca, Chile|Casablanca]] - [[Algarrobo, Chile]] road on the current road to [[Lagunillas, Chile|Lagunillas]] and [[Lo Abarca.]] {{coord|33|23|22.64|S|71|26|12.07|W |}}.-->

The clothes of Inca soldiers were made from ''Pulla Pulla'' ([[Zephyranthes]] párvula Killip), an annual plant of the family [[Amaryllidaceae]], native to the highland Andean provinces, with beautiful flowers that bloom in spring. This bloom is considered an omen of whether the agricultural year would be good or bad. The route from [[Limache]] to Las Dichas passed through Lo Orozco, continuing to Portezuelo de Ibacache and finishing at [[Talagante]], where other Mitimae existed. In 1430, [[Túpac Inca Yupanqui]] initiated a great military campaign that culminated in the establishment of a border at the [[Maule river]]. Ilabe, an Inca [[Nobility|noble]] who commanded the invading forces, established himself in the [[Picunche]]  Llollehue valley, between the [[Maipo river]] and the [[Mapocho river]]. He decided to found a colony and a [[pucará]], a task he entrusted to his son, [[Tala Canta Ilabe]].

He had the authority to name ''[[kuraka]]s'' or governors, derived from that of the Inca in [[Cusco]]. His mandate allowed him to establish the social, political and economic system characteristic of the [[Inca Empire]], to distribute land and community property, and initiate public works for the common good, like roads, granaries and housing. Agricultural and cattle production was sufficiently developed to allow food exports to the ''[[pukara]]s'' of the north.

===Spanish settlement===

In 1541, the captain [[Pedro de Valdivia]], his soldiers, ''[[yanakuna]]'' and Indians of service, entered the basin of the [[Mapocho river]] for the [[Inca road system|Inca way]] by the route that today is called [[Independencia Avenue (Santiago de Chile)|Independence Avenue]] in [[Santiago de Chile|Santiago]], crossed the Mapocho river, advanced five blocks and took possession of [[Tambo (Incan structure)|the principal buildings of this administrative center]]. Within one year, founded a Spanish city bigger than Inca that existed there with a [[Plaza de Armas]] initiating with this the [[Conquest of Chile|Spanish Conquest]]. This ''Plaza de Armas'' was designed in a standard military fashion based on a [[grid pattern]], taken from the [[Ancient Rome|Roman]] '''[[Castrum]]''', of which one of the blocks would be left vacant to form the Plaza de Armas.  It is often surrounded by governmental buildings, churches, and other structures of cultural or political significance. The name derives from the fact that this area would be a refuge in case of an attack upon the city, from which arms would be supplied to the defenders.
 
In the time of Valdivia, the presence of Indian fishermen was recorded in the Central Zone.

{{Quote|" [[Valparaíso|Aliamapu (Valparaíso)]] was inhabited ... for that special race of aborigines that preserves still its type, its name and even its humble navigation... the former [[Changos]] " " | [[Benjamín Vicuña Mackenna]], 1936}}

The term "chango" appears not only in the toponymy, but also in the documents of the colonial era,  alluding to the Indian fishermen. According to the historian [[Joaquín Santa Cruz]] (1926), they were living fundamentally by fishing and hunting seafood and marine mammals.

Towards 1562 a great number of Changos of the central coast had already disappeared already because they had been moved, chased off or exterminated.

{{Quote|''"Vicuna Mackenna affirms that the colonists of Santiago had news of a ship that had smashed against the coasts of [[Topocalma]] and the few survivors, among whom was a black, they were cruelly murdered for the Indians who there were living, for such a motive " .. they happened in avenging them killing hundred for one, as it was the eye for an eye rule in such cases"'' |Szmulewicz, 1984}}-->
The first indications of Spanish colonization in the El Quisco area date to 1570, when the Spanish family of [[Alonso de Córdoba]] took control. Previously it was named ''Huallilemu'' ''([[Mapudungun]]: forest of oakwood)'', and then ''El Totoral'' after the many [[totora (plant)|totora]]s there.

==== Pirates ====
{{Main|Piracy}}
[[File:Brixham.hind.750pix.jpg|thumb|left|A replica of Golden Hind]]
After 1578, El Quisco and nearby places became bolt holes for pirates such as the Englishmen [[Francis Drake]], [[Thomas Cavendish]], and [[Richard Hawkins]] and Dutchmen like [[Oliver Van Noort]] and [[Joris van Spilbergen]]. Francis Drake probably moored his [[galleon]] the ''[[Golden Hind]]'' in the bay in December 1578. He travelled with two people known only by their Christian names, Christopher and Cosmas, the first [[Japanese people|Japanese explorers]] to circumnavigate the globe.

===Today===
[[File:Tralca Point Beach in Peninsula El Quisco.jpg|thumb|180px|View north, towards the Peñablanca cape at El Canelo from El Quisco.]] 
The commune of El Quisco was incorporated on August 30, 1956, under the presidency of [[Carlos Ibáñez del Campo]] under Statutory order Nº 12110.

==Demographics==
[[File:Patrullera Clase Defender atracando en El Quisco.JPG|thumb|300px|[[Defender class boat]] docking in a dock at El Quisco, [[Chile]] ]]
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], El Quisco spans an area of {{convert|50.7|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 9,467 inhabitants (4,815 men and 4,652 women). Of these, 8,931 (94.3%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 536 (5.7%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 55.3% (3,370 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, El Quisco is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2008-2012 alcalde is Natalia Carrasco Pizarro.<ref name="Official"/>
* Cristofer Ceballos Lira ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]])
* Juan Andrés Berrios Olmedo ([[Party for Democracy (Chile)|PPD]])
* José Moraga Lira ([[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]])
* Maria Magdalena Bianchi Berroeta ([[Christian Democratic Party|PDC]])
* Guillermo Romo Díaz ([[Juntos PODEMOS Más|JPM]])
* Guillermo Vidal Devia (UDI)

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], El Quisco is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by María José Hoffmann (UDI) and Víctor Torres (PDC) as part of the 15th electoral district, together with [[San Antonio, Chile|San Antonio]], [[Santo Domingo, Chile|Santo Domingo]], [[Cartagena, Chile|Cartagena]], [[El Tabo]], [[Algarrobo, Chile|Algarrobo]] and [[Casablanca, Chile|Casablanca]]. The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Francisco Chahuán Chahuán (RN) and [[Ricardo Lagos Weber]] (PPD) as part of the 6th senatorial constituency (Valparaíso-Coast).

== Tourism ==
The area is a favourite tourist destination in central Chile. One of its advantages is its short distance from Santiago. There are many residences and hotels for lodging,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.elquisco.cl/elquisco.htm |title=Ilustre Municipalidad de el Quisco |access-date=2009-01-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080221222331/http://www.elquisco.cl/elquisco.htm |archive-date=2008-02-21 |url-status=dead }}</ref> as well as many restaurants and inns for enjoying [[Chilean cuisine|typical Chilean food]]. Marine sports are available, including [[scuba diving]] and [[human swimming|swimming]].
