'''Malacatancito''' ({{IPA|es|malakatanˈsito}}) is a mostly rural [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Guatemala]]n [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Huehuetenango Department|Huehuetenango]], spanning 312.4 square kilometers with an estimated population of 23,359 as of 2023. Its residents are primarily of [[Ladino people|Ladino]] and [[Maya peoples|Maya]] descent, speaking [[Spanish language|Spanish]] and [[Kʼicheʼ language|Kʼicheʼ]] as their primary mother tongues. The municipality features a warm, low-humidity [[Oceanic climate#Subtropical highland variety (Cfb, Cwb)|subtropical highland climate]] marked by extreme seasonal variations in [[precipitation]] and [[cloud cover]] between its distinct [[wet season|wet]] and [[dry season]]s. The municipal government is led by Mayor Irma Elizabeth Ávila Alvarado of the [[Vamos (Guatemala)|Vamos]] party, who has served consecutive terms since her election in 2019.

==Demographics==
According to data from the [[National Institute of Statistics (Guatemala)|Guatemalan National Institute of Statistics]], the population of the Malacatancito municipality has shown steady growth over recent decades. In mid-2008, the population was estimated at 19,177 residents, which increased to an estimated 20,846 by June 2018. Population projections for mid-2023 indicated a further increase to 23,359 inhabitants, representing an [[annual growth rate]] of 2.3%. Across the municipality's total area of 312.4 square kilometers, this population size yields a [[population density]] of 74.77 people per square kilometer.<ref name="CityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/admin/huehuetenango/1303__malacatancito/|title=Malacatancito (Municipality in Guatemala)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=6 September 2023|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>

The 2023 projections reveal a nearly equal [[gender]] distribution within the municipality, consisting of 11,731 males (50.2%) and 11,628 females (49.8%). The population structure is notably young. [[Children]] aged 0 to 14 years account for 34.1% of the population (7,972 residents), while the [[working age]] demographic between 15 and 64 years represents the majority at 58.7% (13,710 residents). [[Old age|Elders]] aged 65 and older make up the remaining 7.2% (1,677 residents).<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Data from the 2018 census highlights that Malacatancito is almost entirely [[rural]], with 95.6% of the population (18,320 people) living in rural areas compared to just 4.4% (835 people) residing in [[urban centers]]. The census also reflects a very localized population regarding birthplace, as 84.2% of residents (15,967 people) were born in Malacatancito. An additional 15.7% (2,972 people) migrated from other municipalities of [[Guatemala]], while only 31 residents were born in another country.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

In terms of ethnic composition, the 2018 census recorded [[Ladino people|Ladinos]] as the largest demographic group, comprising 69.8% of the population (13,362 people). The [[Maya peoples|Maya]] population represents the second-largest group at 30% (5,752 people). Other ethnic populations registered in the municipality include 17 [[Garifuna]], 11 [[Afro-Guatemalans]], 1 [[Xinca people|Xinca]], and 12 foreign residents.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Linguistic data from 2018 closely mirrors the ethnic breakdown, with [[Spanish language|Spanish]] being the predominant mother tongue spoken by 76.7% of the population (13,342 people). Among the [[Mayan languages]] spoken in the municipality, [[Kʼicheʼ language|Kʼicheʼ]] is the most prevalent, serving as the mother tongue for 18.7% of residents (3,258 people), followed by [[Mam language|Mam]] at 3.3% (582 people). Smaller linguistic groups include 11 [[Kaqchikel language|Kaqchikel]] speakers, 5 [[Qʼeqchiʼ language|Qʼeqchiʼ]] speakers, and 188 people (1.1%) who speak other languages. Educational metrics from the same census indicate a [[literacy rate]] of 83.7% among residents aged seven and older, representing 13,358 literate individuals, while 16.3% (2,601 people) were recorded as illiterate.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

==Climate==
The climate of Malacatancito is warm year-round, with temperatures typically ranging from 46°F to 82°F and rarely falling below 40°F or exceeding 86°F. The municipality features a brief warm season from 11 March to 10 May, peaking in May with an average high of 79°F, and a cooler season from 16 September to 19 January, with January averaging a low of 47°F. Due to its comfortable, [[humidity]]-free profile, which is highly comparable to the climates of [[Mbeya]], Tanzania and [[Bonga]], Ethiopia, the ideal time for outdoor [[tourism]] and warm-weather activities is from mid-February to late April.<ref name="WeatherSpark">{{cite web|url=https://weatherspark.com/y/11263/Average-Weather-in-Malacatancito-Guatemala-Year-Round|title= Climate and Average Weather Year Round in Malacatancito (Guatemala)|website=Weather Spark|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>

[[Precipitation]] and [[cloud cover]] vary drastically by season, though perceived humidity remains at a constant 0% all year. The 5.6-month [[wet season]] runs from 10 May to 30 October, peaking in September with an average of 23.2 rainy days and 10.0 inches of accumulated [[rainfall]], under skies that are 95% [[overcast]] in June. Conversely, the [[dry season]] spans from late October to May, with January serving as both the clearest month (74% clear or partly cloudy) and the driest, averaging just 0.2 inches of rain. Mild wind speeds peak in December at 4.2 miles per hour, while [[daylight]] ranges from roughly 11 to 13 hours without the observation of [[daylight saving time]].<ref name="WeatherSpark"/>

==Government==
The current mayor of Malacatancito is Irma Elizabeth Ávila Alvarado, who was first elected in 2019.<ref>{{cite journal|url=https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/liminar/v20n1/1665-8027-liminar-20-01-e893.pdf|title=El perfil profesional y político de las mujeres electas como alcaldesas en los municipios de Centroamérica entre 2017 y 2020|trans-title=The professional and political profile of women elected as mayors in the municipalities of Central America from 2017 to 2020|lang=es|first=Rafael Cedillo|last=Delgado|journal=LiminaR: Estudios Sociales y Humanísticos|date=January–June 2022|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref> After representing the [[National Unity of Hope]] (UNE) party in her first term, she switched affiliation to the [[Vamos (Guatemala)|Vamos]] party and was re-elected in 2023.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.agenciaocote.com/blog/2023/08/23/solo-el-3-5-de-las-municipalidades-seran-dirigidas-por-mujeres/|title=Sólo el 3.5 % de las municipalidades serán dirigidas por mujeres|trans-title=Only 3.5% of municipalities will be led by women.|lang=es|first=María José Longo|last=Bautista|website=[[Agencia Ocote]]|date=23 August 2023|access-date=26 May 2026}}</ref>
