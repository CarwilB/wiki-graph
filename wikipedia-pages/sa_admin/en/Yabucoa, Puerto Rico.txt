'''Yabucoa''' ({{IPA|es|ʝaβuˈkoa}}) is a [[Yabucoa barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] in [[Puerto Rico]] located in the eastern region, north of [[Maunabo, Puerto Rico|Maunabo]]; south of [[San Lorenzo, Puerto Rico|San Lorenzo]], [[Las Piedras, Puerto Rico|Las Piedras]] and [[Humacao, Puerto Rico|Humacao]]; and east of [[Patillas, Puerto Rico|Patillas]]. Yabucoa is spread over 9 barrios and [[Yabucoa barrio-pueblo|Yabucoa Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

== Etymology and nicknames ==
The name ''Yabucoa'' is said to come from the [[Taíno language|Taíno]] name ''Guaroca'', both a toponymic and personal name meaning "where water [is found]". However this is considered a [[folk etymology]] and other linguists suggest the name is most likely a [[Spanish language|Spanish]] interpretation of the Taíno word ''yaucoa'' (similar to the name [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]]) which means "[[cassava]] plantation".<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=Yabucoa|url=http://nuestropuertorico.com/municipios/yabucoa/|access-date=2021-10-11|language=es|archive-date=October 11, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211011002632/http://nuestropuertorico.com/municipios/yabucoa/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Some nicknames of the city are "Sugar City", after the numerous sugarcane plantations of the [[Yabucoa Valley]], and ''Pueblo de Yuca'' ("Yuca Town") which references the Taíno origin of the name of the town. The locals are also known as the ''Bebe Leches'' ("milk drinkers") after the numerous dairy farms located in the region.<ref name=":0" />

==History==
The region of what is now Yabucoa belonged to the [[Taíno people|Taíno]] region of Guayaney, which covered a portion of the southeast region of Puerto Rico.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.webring.org/l/rd?ring=taino;id=2;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.taino-tribe.org%2Fjatiboni-s.html|title=Gobierno Tribal del Pueblo Jatibonicu Taíno de Puerto Rico|access-date=February 3, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005010944/http://www.webring.org/l/rd?ring=taino;id=2;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.taino-tribe.org%2Fjatiboni-s.html|archive-date=October 5, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref> The region was led by cacique Guaraca. After the [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish colonization]], the region of Yabucoa belonged to [[Humacao, Puerto Rico|Humacao]], and its territory was mostly used for [[cattle]] and farming. Yabucoa, as a town, was founded on October 3, 1793, when Don Manuel Colón de Bonilla and his wife, Doña Catalina Morales Pacheco, donated the lands to the people.<ref name="enciclopedia">{{cite web|url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09052002&page=2|title=Municipios: Yabucoa|work=Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico|access-date=May 7, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005010504/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09052002&page=2|archive-date=October 5, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Delgado1878">{{cite book|author=Manuel Ubeda y Delgado|title=Isla de Puerto Rico: estudio histórico, geográfico y estadístico de la misma|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Gl8zAQAAMAAJ&pg=PA271|year=1878|language=es|publisher=Academia Puertorriqueńa de la Historia|pages=275–}}</ref>

Many more deaths were occurring than expected. An entire new section to the cemetery was built following the hurricane and the deaths that followed.<ref name="CPI" />

He stated there were 4,000 residences with between 12,000 and 15,000 residents without electrical power, of 36,000 residents. Large swaths of Yabucoa municipality including [[Guayabota]], [[Tejas, Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Tejas]], [[Juan Martín, Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Juan Martín]], [[Calabazas, Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Calabazas]], [[Limones, Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Limones]] and [[Aguacate, Yabucoa, Puerto Rico|Aguacate]] barrios, and 100% of [[Jácanas]] were without electrical power for nine months, some since [[Hurricane Irma]] had hit a week prior to Hurricane Maria.<ref name="CPI">{{cite web |title=YABUCOA: Enfermos y viejos sin servicio eléctrico |url=http://periodismoinvestigativo.com/2018/06/vidas-de-damnificados-por-maria-aun-penden-de-un-hilo/ |website=Periodísmo Investigativo |publisher=CPI |language=es}}</ref><ref name="Jervis 2018">{{cite news | last=Jervis | first=Rick | title='This city has been ignored': Yabucoa, ground zero for Hurricane Maria in Puerto Rico, still reeling | newspaper=USA TODAY | date=11 March 2018 | url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/2018/03/11/yabucoa-puerto-rico-ground-zero-hurricane-maria-long-road-ahead-recovery/393118002/ | access-date=4 July 2020}}</ref>

==Geography==
The municipality of Yabucoa is located in the south-eastern coast of Puerto Rico. The [[Yabucoa Valley|Valley of Yabucoa]] is surrounded by the hills of the [[San Lorenzo Batholith]] on three sides and by the [[Caribbean Sea]] on the fourth. The hills surrounding the Yabucoa Valley as well as the bedrock underlying the alluvium in the valley are composed of the San Lorenzo Batholith, a large, igneous intrusive body emplaced during the [[Late Cretaceous]] (Rogers, 1977; Rogers and others, 1979). The San Lorenzo Batholith is a composite body that is composed of [[gabbro]] (Kd), [[diorite]], [[tonalite]], [[granodiorite]], and [[quartz monzonite]]. The [[Sierra Pandura|Cuchillas de Panduras]], a fork of the [[Sierra de Cayey]] in the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]] runs through its south. Santa Elena is one of its most prominent peaks with an altitude of {{convert|1,870|ft|m|abbr=off|sp=us}}. Santa Elena is located in Juan Martin ward. Pandura Peak rises {{convert|1,693|ft|m|abbr=off|sp=us}} above sea level. Pandura is located in the Calabazas ward. The altitude of the hills surrounding the Valley of Yabucoa reaches a maximum of about {{convert|2,130|ft|m|abbr=off|sp=us}} at the head of the [[Río Guayanés]] basin. The land surface in the Yabucoa Valley slopes gently from an altitude of about {{convert|98|ft|m|abbr=off|sp=us}} above mean sea level, at the western edge of the valley, to sea level where the valley meets the Caribbean Sea.<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/yabucoa-municipality/|title=Yabucoa Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191105214537/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/yabucoa-municipality/|archive-date=2019-11-05|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Economy==
===Agriculture===
[[File:Olein Recovery Corp. Yabucoa, PR 05.jpg|thumb|Olein Recovery Corp. in Yabucoa in 2006]]
Yabucoa is known for its agricultural prowess because of the surrounding fertile [[Yabucoa Valley|valley]] that produces most of the island's [[Plantain (cooking)|plantains]] and [[banana]]s. Yabucoeños are known as "sugar people" because most of the valley was used for [[sugar cane]] growth. In addition, one of the most visible landmarks when entering the municipality is the old Hacienda Roig sugar mill, one of the last mills that produced sugar in Puerto Rico.<ref name ="PhotoRoig2011">{{Cite web |url=http://robertospuertorico.com/remains-of-central-roig-sugar-mill-in-yabucoa/ |title=Remains of Central Roig Sugar Mill in Yabucoa |access-date=2012-01-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111123231353/http://robertospuertorico.com/remains-of-central-roig-sugar-mill-in-yabucoa/ |archive-date=2011-11-23 |url-status=live }}</ref>

There's an oil recycling company, the only one in the Caribbean, called Olein Recovery Corp. operating in Yabucoa.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.oleinrefinery.com/about-oleinrefinery.html|title=About Olein Refinery & Lubricants|website=www.oleinrefinery.com|access-date=22 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190622222008/http://www.oleinrefinery.com/about-oleinrefinery.html|archive-date=22 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://laprensafl.com/2017/05/03/crece-el-interes-por-expansion-comercial-entre-puerto-rico-y-orlando/|title=Crece el interés por expansión comercial entre Puerto Rico y Orlando|first=Cecilia|last=Figueroa|publisher=La Prensa|language=es|access-date=22 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190622222401/https://laprensafl.com/2017/05/03/crece-el-interes-por-expansion-comercial-entre-puerto-rico-y-orlando/|archive-date=22 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> During the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Puerto Rico|COVID-19 pandemic]] Olein began manufacturing hand sanitizer.<ref>{{cite web | title=Olein Refinery se transforma para producir 'hand sanitizer' | website=El Nuevo Dia | date=6 April 2020 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/negocios/empresas/nota/oleinrefinerysetransformaparaproducirhandsanitizer-2559064/ | language=es | access-date=26 June 2020}}</ref>

==Culture==
[[File:Concha Acústica Toño Márquez en Yabucoa, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|{{lang|es|Concha Acústica Toño Márquez}} in Yabucoa, where community events and concerts are held]]

===Festivals and events===
Yabucoa celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in September / October. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de los Santos Angeles Custodios}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref>{{cite web | title=Puerto Rico Festivales, Eventos y Actividades en Puerto Rico | website=Puerto Rico Hoteles y Paradores | url=https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | language=es | access-date=2020-07-17 | archive-date=February 26, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226163727/https://www.puertoricohotelesparadores.com/festivales-eventos-actividades | url-status=dead }}</ref>

Other festivals and events celebrated in Yabucoa include:
* Sugar Cane Festival - May
* Beach Festival - May
* {{lang|es|Festival del Carmen}} – July
* {{lang|es|Campesino Festival}} – October
* {{lang|es|Jíbaro de Martorell Festival}} – December

Community events are held at the {{lang|es|Concha Acústica Toño Márquez}}.<ref>{{cite web | title=Suministros para sectores de Yabucoa y pasadía para niños pacientes de cáncer | website=Walo Radio 1240 AM | date=2018-07-19 | url=https://waloradio.com/suministro-de-alimentos-a-sectores-de-yabucoa-y-pasadia-para-ninos-pacientes-de-cancer/ | language=es | access-date=2020-07-08}}</ref>

===Sports===
[[File:Estadio Félix "Nacho" Millán in Yabucoa, Puerto Rico destroyed by Hurricane Maria.jpg|thumb|The {{lang|es|Estadio Félix "Nacho" Millán}} stadium, where the Azucareros played their home games, sustained significant damage from Hurricane Maria in 2017.<ref name="ENDI Hurricane Stadium">{{cite web | title=Los Azucareros de Yabucoa no se escapan del huracán María | website=El Nuevo Día | date=2019-09-20 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/deportes/beisbol/notas/los-azucareros-de-yabucoa-no-se-escapan-del-huracan-maria/ | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18}}</ref>]]
{{lang|es|Azucareros de Yabucoa}} are the [[Federación de Béisbol Aficionado de Puerto Rico|Doble A]] team of Yabucoa and have won 4 championships. They won the 1994 championship playing against the {{lang|es|Toritos de Cayey}}. In 1995 they won against Cidra and in 1996 they won against Cabo Rojo.<ref>{{cite web | title=Una serie final para la historia en el Béisbol Doble A | website=El Nuevo Día | date=2018-10-29 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/deportes/beisbol/notas/una-serie-final-para-la-historia-en-el-beisbol-doble-a/ | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18}}</ref> In 2020, {{lang|es|Azucareros de Yabucoa}} was the eighth Puerto Rico Double-A baseball team to have won 900 games.<ref>{{cite web | title=Los Azucareros de Yabucoa consiguen la victoria número 900 | website=El Nuevo Día | date=2020-02-24 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/deportes/beisbol/notas/los-azucareros-de-yabucoa-consiguen-la-victoria-numero-900/ | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Ruina del estadio Félix Millán deja un vacío deportivo en Yabucoa | website=El Nuevo Día | date=2018-04-12 | url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/deportes/beisbol/notas/ruina-del-estadio-felix-millan-deja-un-vacio-deportivo-en-yabucoa/ | language=es | access-date=2020-07-18}}</ref>

The {{lang|es|Estadio Félix "Nacho" Millán}} where the Azucareros played sustained significant damage from Hurricane Maria in 2017 and as of 2020 the stadium remains in disrepair.<ref name="ENDI Hurricane Stadium" />

==Government==
{{Main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
Like all municipalities in [[Puerto Rico]], Yabucoa is administered by a mayor. The current mayor is [[Rafael Surillo|Rafael Surillo Ruiz]], from the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] (PPD). Surillo was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2012|2012 general election]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VII]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Wanda Soto Tolentino]] and [[Luis Daniel Colón La Santa]] were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Humacao_VII.xmll Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241115225812/https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Humacao_VII.xmll |date=November 15, 2024 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
The design of the flag of Yabucoa is abstract, inspired by the colors of the municipal shield; green, white and violet.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=Yabocoa | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/YABUCOA.HTML | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001007/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/YABUCOA.HTML | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
In the shield appear two angels the Santos Angeles Custodios, patron saints of Yabucoa. The color purple (violet) field of the shield represents the highest dignity of the angels. The walking sticks are attributes of the traveller, and refer to the holy office of the Angels as guides and companions in man's journey in his earthly life. The canes are adorned with guajana flowers, representing the wealth of the sugar cane. The green land where the angels stand symbolizes the fertile [[Yabucoa Valley|valley]] in which Yabucoa is located.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />

==Transportation==
One of the main roads to Yabucoa is the [[Puerto Rico Highway 3|PR-3]], which borders the east side of the island. Distance from the capital is approximately 1 hour.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/yabucoa.html|title=Yabucoa... La Ciudad del Azúcar|work=Proyecto Salón Hogar|access-date=May 9, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141122201726/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/yabucoa.html|archive-date=November 22, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2008, a tunnel connecting the town of Yabucoa with the town of [[Maunabo, Puerto Rico|Maunabo]] was completed.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/diario/noticia/puertoricohoy/noticias/se_acorta_la_distancia_en_el_sureste/356165|title=Se acorta la distancia en el sureste|work=[[El Nuevo Día]]|author=Morales, Sandra|date=February 2, 2008|access-date=May 10, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080206105022/http://www.elnuevodia.com/diario/noticia/puertoricohoy/noticias/se_acorta_la_distancia_en_el_sureste/356165|archive-date=February 6, 2008|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sice.com/documentos/Noticias/Trafico/TUNEL_MAUNABO.pdf|title=Un túnel, dos pueblos|work=[[El Nuevo Día]]|author=Del Valle, Sara and David Toucet|access-date=May 9, 2013}}{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> It is currently the longest on the island.
