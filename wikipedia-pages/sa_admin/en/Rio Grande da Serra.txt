'''Rio Grande da Serra''' (''Great River of the Mountain Chain'') is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the state of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]] in Brazil. It is located approximately {{convert|49|km}} by road southeast of the centre of the city of [[São Paulo]]. It is part of the [[Metropolitan Region of São Paulo]].<ref>[http://governo-sp.jusbrasil.com.br/legislacao/1028148/lei-complementar-1139-11 Lei Complementar nº 1.139, de 16 de junho de 2011]</ref> The population is 51,436 (2020 est.) in an area of 36.34&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name=ibge>[http://cod.ibge.gov.br/I45 Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística]</ref>

==History==

===Early period===
The earliest date recorded in the history of Rio Grande da Serra is May 26, 1560 when land was allotted in Jeribatiba, the third village developed by the [[Jesuits]]. To meet local needs, salt had to be brought from the port of [[São Vicente, São Paulo|São Vicente]] up to the Alto da Serra highlands via the village now known as [[Paranapiacaba]]. In 1640, the Marquês de Alegretti officially renamed Jeribatiba "Rio Grande". The mode of transport was donkeys during that period and [[Mogi das Cruzes]], founded in 1640, rapidly became the largest settlement. Salt was transported along the so-called ''Caminho do Mar'' (sea road) to Alto da Serra, also locally known as Zanzaláh. One of the Jesuits who died on this route was buried with a wooden cross which in 1611 was converted into a chapel, now known as the ''Capela de São Sebastião''.<ref name=History>{{Cite web|url=http://www.riograndedaserra.sp.gov.br/historia.html|title=História|language=pt|access-date=26 January 2013|publisher=Official web site of Government of Brazil|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130213191108/http://www.riograndedaserra.sp.gov.br/historia.html|archive-date=13 February 2013}}</ref><ref name="Guidade">{{cite web|url=http://www.guiadedireitos.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=624%3Ario-grande-da-serra&Itemid=173|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304030619/http://www.guiadedireitos.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=624:rio-grande-da-serra&Itemid=173|url-status=usurped|archive-date=March 4, 2016|title=Rio Grande da Serra|publisher=Guiadedireitos.org|access-date=26 January 2013|language=pt}}</ref>

===Nineteenth century===
In 1840, General [[Antônio Manuel de Melo]] built a ranch on the road from Mogi das Cruzes to São Paulo. In 1855, a [[cholera]] epidemic caused devastation in the region.<ref name=History/>Until the nineteenth century, the inhabitants had been engaged in pastoral activities. On February 16, 1867, the [[São Paulo Railway]] reached Rio Grande, enabling coffee beans and timber to be transported to São Paulo. As a result, the development of Rio Grande boomed, However. it also resulted in the influx of expatriates mostly from England who established a new town at Paranapiacaba, a few miles away.<ref name="Guidade"/><ref name="LouisChandler2010">{{cite book|author1=Regis St Louis|author2=Gary Chandler|title=Brazil|url=https://archive.org/details/isbn_9781741791631|url-access=registration|access-date=3 January 2013|date=1 December 2010|publisher=Lonely Planet|isbn=978-1-74179-163-1|pages=[https://archive.org/details/isbn_9781741791631/page/304 304]–}}</ref> The railroad encouraged the development of coal mining from 1880 and by 1895, that of manganese and graphite extraction and as well as mineral water. Expatriates from Italy, Germany and Switzerland came to work at Rio Grande from 1899 onwards.<ref name=History/>

===History===
Work on the canalization of the Rio Grande river was initiated in 1906. In 1918, graphite industry was established. 1920 marked the first telephone connection. In 1927, São Paulo municipality acquired Pedreira, a suburb of Rio Grande, bringing new factories with opportunities for employment. In 1928, an electric power station was built. Between 1930 and 1954 many small scale industries, extensive housing facilities, marketing centers and factories manufacturing all types of goods were set up. Schools, sports centers, musical events, carnivals and religious festivals emerged. After all these developments the town, Rio Grande da Serra, was given the status of municipality in 1964.<ref name=History/><ref name="Guidade"/> Population growth in Rio Grande da Serra was slow until the mid-1970s, when there was an influx of migrants, mostly miners which has lasted until today, although the increase is now falling.{{cn|date=January 2026}}

==Geography==
[[File:Rio Grande da Serra Paranapiacaba Sao Paolo map.png|thumb|Map]]
[[File:Rio do Cloro.jpg|thumb|Rio do Cloro]]
Rio Grande da Serra is one of the 39 municipalities (Prefeituras) in the state of São Paulo under the federal government of Brazil.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.metropolis.org/sites/default/files/metropolitan_regions/387_eng-004_sao_paulo.pdf|format=pdf|title=004 Sao Paulo (Brazil)|access-date=26 January 2013|publisher=Metropolis organization|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120516174558/http://www.metropolis.org/sites/default/files/metropolitan_regions/387_eng-004_sao_paulo.pdf|archive-date=16 May 2012}}</ref>
The municipality is located approximately {{convert|49|km}} by road to the southeast of the centre of the [[São Paulo|city of São Paulo]] according to [[Google Maps]], {{convert|55|km}} according to the municipal authorities.<ref name=grande>{{cite web|url=http://www.riograndedaserra.sp.gov.br/|title=Sobre o Município|publisher=Government of Rio Grande da Serra|access-date=26 January 2013|language=pt}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Pop_2007">{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/contagem2007/popmunic2007layoutTCU14112007.xls|title=Estimativas - Contagem da População 2007|publisher = IBGE|access-date=11 July 2008}}</ref>
<ref>{{Google maps | url =https://maps.google.com/maps?ll=-23.743889,-46.397778&spn=0.03,0.03&t=h&q=-23.743889,-46.397778| accessdate = 26 January 2013 }}</ref> Under the Greater [[ABC region]], its neighboring municipalities are Santo André, Ribeirão Pires and Suzano.<ref name=grande/> It covers an area of {{Convert|36.7|km2}}. With the city of São Paulo and the municipalities of [[Diadema, São Paulo|Diadema]], [[Mauá]], [[Ribeirão Pires]], [[Santo André, São Paulo|Santo André]], [[São Bernardo do Campo]] and [[São Caetano do Sul]], it forms part of the [[Microregion of São Paulo]]. The principal road is [[SP-122]] (Rodovia Deputado Antônio Adib Chammas), which links to [[Ribeirão Pires]] and [[Paranapiacaba]]. Paranapiacaba is a historical site approached by road (15 minutes drive) from Rio Grande da Serra, which has train link with [[Luz Station]] (Estaco da Luz) in São Paulo.  The nearest international airport is the [[Cumbica]] at [[Guarulhos]] which is quite far from the city of São Paulo.<ref name="RobinsonRobinson2011">{{cite book|author1=Alex Robinson|author2=Gardenia Robinson|title=Brazil Handbook|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=-u6a1Etx5vkC&pg=PA179|access-date=3 January 2013|date=17 May 2011|publisher=Footprint Travel Guides|isbn=978-1-907263-26-2|pages=179–}}</ref>

[[File:Billings 2008051704.jpg|thumb|left|[[Billings Reservoir]]]]
The [[Rio Grande (Paraná River)|Rio Grande River]] Basin, with its tributaries, form part of the municipality, with the "Rio Grande arm" of the [[Billings Reservoir]] and Dam which is its most significant geographical feature, covering a total of {{Convert|127|km2}}. It is named after [[Asa White Kenney Billings]], the United States [[hydroelectricity|hydroelectric]] engineer who was instrumental in building it. The reservoir supplies about 1.8 million people, and also is used for fishing and swimming. In addition to Rio Grande da Serra and São Paulo, the Billings sub-basin includes Diadema, Ribeirao Pires, Santo Andre, and Sao Bernardo do Campo.<ref name="BilibioHensel2012">{{cite book|last1=Bilibio|first1=Carolina|last2=Hensel|first2=Oliver|last3=Selbach|first3=Jeferson Francisco|title=Sustainable water management in the tropics and subtropics - and case studies in Brazil|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=WktzAliHowMC&pg=PA834|access-date=26 January 2013|volume=4|year=2012|publisher=Jeferson Selbach|isbn=978-85-63337-23-8|pages=834–}}</ref> Besides the Rio Grande and Billings Reservoir and dam, the municipality has a lot of streams and creeks, such as the streams of Figueira, Cloro, and Piolzinho.{{cn|date=January 2026}}

Within the city limits, the forests areas and wetlands are stated to be well conserved, including wild animals and birds. Its rainforests are also protected under suitable environmental laws.<ref name=grande/>

===Climate===
The climate of the city, and across the Metropolitan Region of São Paulo, is subtropical. Summers are hot and rainy, winters are mild, with a constant haze between May and October. The average annual temperature is around 18&nbsp;°C, with the coldest month being July (average 14&nbsp;°C) and the hottest in February (average 22&nbsp;°C). Annual rainfall is around 1,400&nbsp;mm.

==Demography==
The population of the municipal town is 44,000, which over an area of {{Convert|36.7|km2}} gives a population density of 1,198 inhabitants /km<sup>2</sup>.{{Citation needed|date=January 2013}} Its Human Development growth Index (HDI) is reported to be 0.764. The rate of Infant Mortality is 18.38 per thousand inhabitants and life expectancy is 74 years. The urbanization rate is reported to be 10%.

==Economy==
The city's economy is dependent on [[ecotourism]], family farming, [[intensive farming]], cooperatives and small non-polluting industries.<ref name=grande/> Fishing is practiced on the Billings Reservoir. However, the issue of disposal of city waste water and sewerage directly into the reservoir from the city is still an issue needing a solution.<ref name=Reservoir>{{Cite web|url=http://www.ipen.br/biblioteca/2011/17389.pdf|format=pdf|title= Assessment of metals and trace elements in sediments from Rio Grande Reservoir, Brazil, by neutron activation analysis|date= 21 June 2011|access-date=26 January 2013|publisher=Springers}}</ref>

Economic activities in the form of small-scale craft industries in wood craft, dehydration of leaves, among others, sculptures, fabric dolls, decorated boxes, ornaments, jewelry, paintings, artifacts from recycled material have also provided job opportunities to some artisans.<ref name=Tourism>{{Cite web|url=http://www.riograndedaserra.sp.gov.br/turismo.pdf|format=pdf|title=Toursimo|language=pt|access-date=26 January 2013|publisher=Official web site of Government of Brazil|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130226160401/http://www.riograndedaserra.sp.gov.br/turismo.pdf|archive-date=26 February 2013}}</ref> The southeast on the road to [[Paranapiacaba]] is a large industrial complex. A quarry began operations in 1927; the railway communications enabled fast shipment of mined material.<ref name=Tourism/> In 1931, the city of São Paulo signed an agreement with Light & Power to provide energy and extend the operation of the quarry. The quarry operations were terminated in the 1970s but it is still reputed to be the largest quarry in Latin America at more than {{convert|640|m}} long and {{convert|70|m}} high and it is used as a climbing venue.<ref name=Tourism/>

==Notable landmarks==
Capela de São Sebastião was established in 1611. It is a white-washed structure in the colonial style. In 1906, a sculpture of Saint Sebastian was added to the church.<ref name=Tourism/> The distinctive light blue and white Our Lady of Grace Chapel (Igreja Nossa Senhora das Graças), accessed via a steep flight of steps, was established in 1949.<ref name=Tourism/> The church has its feast day on November 27. Estação Ferroviária, the railway station was inaugurated on 18 February 1867. Casa dos Castelucci and Casa família Rago are notable houses in the town.<ref name=Tourism/>

== Media ==
In telecommunications, the city was served by [[:pt:Companhia Telefônica da Borda do Campo|Companhia Telefônica da Borda do Campo]]. In July 1998, this company was acquired by Telefónica, which adopted the [[Vivo (telecommunications company)|Vivo]] brand in 2012. The company is currently an operator of cell phones, fixed lines, internet (fiber optics/4G) and television (satellite and cable).<ref name=":vivo">{{Cite web|url=https://ri.telefonica.com.br/en/the-company/our-history/|title=Our History - Telefônica RI|access-date=2024-06-14|website=Telefônica}}</ref>
