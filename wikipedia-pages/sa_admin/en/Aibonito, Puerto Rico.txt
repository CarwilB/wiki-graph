'''Aibonito''' ({{IPA|es|ajβoˈnito}}) is a small mountain [[Aibonito barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] in [[Puerto Rico]] located in the [[Sierra de Cayey]] mountain range, north of [[Salinas, Puerto Rico|Salinas]]; south of [[Barranquitas, Puerto Rico|Barranquitas]] and [[Comerío, Puerto Rico|Comerío]]; east of [[Coamo, Puerto Rico|Coamo]]; and west of [[Cidra, Puerto Rico|Cidra]], and [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]]. Aibonito is spread over 8 ''barrios'' and [[Aibonito barrio-pueblo|Aibonito Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

Aibonito is located at a relatively high elevation (its main square is the highest in the island at {{convert|2,401|ft|order=flip}} above sea level), which makes its climate cooler than most of [[Puerto Rico]]'s towns.

== Etymology and nicknames ==
The name ''Aibonito'' is possibly a combination of [[Spanish language|Spanish]] and Taíno from ''Hatibon'' or ''Jatibon'', which the native name of the [[Río de Aibonito|Aibonito River]]. ''Jatibon'' most likely translates to "black river" or "night river", the indigenous name of the river which was later turned into a [[diminutive]] in Spanish (''Jatibon-ito''). Another possibility is that it comes from the Taino ''Haitibon'' or ''Jaitibon'', similarly to the origins of [[Artibonite River|Artibonite]] in Hispaniola, meaning 'high river'.<ref>{{cite book
  |last=Las Casas|first=Bartolomé de|title=Apología Histórica Sumaria, Chapter V|publisher=UNAM|date=1967|location=Mexico|language=Spanish}}</ref> It is common to have native place names in Puerto Rico be historically adopted and modified into the Spanish language such as ''Mucarabones'' for example, which is a plural of ''Mucarabon'', meaning "[[Puerto Rican owl|owl]] river" in Taíno.<ref>Faraclas, Nicholas; Severing, Ronald; Echteld, Elisabeth; Delgado, Sally; Rutgers, Wim (eds.). ''Caribbean Cosmopolitanisms and Caribbean Sciences: Inclusive Approaches to the Study of the Languages, Literatures and Cultures of the Greater Caribbean and Beyond''. University of Curaçao, 2022.</ref>

There's also a legend that tells of a Spanish soldier called Diego Alvarez who on May 17, 1615, reached one of the highest peaks in the area and upon watching the view, exclaimed "''¡Ay, qué bonito!''" ("Oh, how pretty!") which eventually was turned into the name of the region. If this is the case, it would be considered [[folk etymology]].<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-01-08|title=Aibonito|url=https://prmeencanta.com/2021/01/08/aibonito/|access-date=2021-10-07|website=Puerto Rico Me Encanta|language=es|archive-date=7 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211007055024/https://prmeencanta.com/2021/01/08/aibonito/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Some of the municipality's nicknames include ''Ciudad de las Flores'' ("City of Flowers") and ''Jardín de Puerto Rico'' ("Puerto Rico's Garden") after the region's [[floral industry]] and [[Aibonito Festival of Flowers|annual festival]], and ''Ciudad Fría'' ("Cold City") and ''Nevera de Puerto Rico'' ("Puerto Rico's [[refrigerator]]") after the fact that the municipality has recorded some of the island's lowest temperatures.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2016-11-27|title=Aibonito, Puerto Rico – City Of Flowers|url=https://boricuaonline.com/en/aibonito-puerto-rico/|access-date=2021-10-07|website=BoricuaOnline.com|language=en-US|archive-date=7 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211007055023/https://boricuaonline.com/en/aibonito-puerto-rico/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==History==
Before the [[Spanish colonization of the Americas]], it is believed that there were [[Taíno people|Taíno]] settlements in the region that belonged to [[Cacique]] [[Orocobix]] domain.

After the Spanish arrived, it is believed that a ranch was established in the region by Pedro Zorascoechea in 1630, which led the development of a [[Hamlet (place)|hamlet]]. However, it wasn't until 1822 when Don Manuel Veléz presented himself before the government, representing the inhabitants of the area, to ask for Aibonito to be officially declared a town. This was authorized on March 13, 1824, by Governor Don [[Miguel de la Torre]]. The first Catholic church in Aibonito was built in 1825. The building was replaced by the current church, which was started in 1887 and completed in 1897. After the town was officially constituted, barrios started developing in the area.

On the [[Spanish–American War]] of 1898, around 800 [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]] and [[Puerto Ricans|Puerto Rican]] soldiers were able to defeat the invading [[United States Armed Forces|American troops]] due to their strategic placement in [[Asomante Mountain]]. This scrimmage came to an end when the Spanish government surrendered on August 12, 1898. The Spanish and Puerto Rican forces at Asomante never surrendered and would have held their position indefinitely if not for the buckling of the Spanish government in [[Madrid]].

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the United States conducted its first [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Aibonito was 8,596.

== Geography ==
Aibonito is located in the [[Sierra de Cayey]], part of the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]] in [[Puerto Rico]].<ref>{{cite web | title=Aibonito | website=Discover Puerto Rico | url=https://www.discoverpuertorico.com/regions/central-mountains/aibonito | access-date=1 August 2020 | archive-date=1 September 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200901033454/https://www.discoverpuertorico.com/regions/central-mountains/aibonito | url-status=live }}</ref> Aibonito is the town with the highest elevation in [[Puerto Rico]], located at {{convert|2,401|ft|order=flip}} above sea level. Some of its mountains are La Sierra ({{convert|2,394|ft|order=flip}}), Asomante ({{convert|2,042|ft|order=flip}}) and Buena Vista ({{convert|2,042|ft|order=flip}}).<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info">{{cite web |title=Aibonito Municipality|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aibonito-municipality/#1463492689874-dd12c211-136e |website=enciclopediapr.org |publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH) |language=en |access-date=14 February 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190214233415/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/aibonito-municipality/#1463492689874-dd12c211-136e |archive-date=February 14, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Climate===
Aibonito features a rather mild [[tropical rainforest climate]], owing to its elevation and location, close but still slightly removed from the equator, as well as being directly exposed to the wind and moisture of the [[Trade Winds]]. It borders on a [[subtropical highland climate]], and also a [[humid subtropical climate]], though like the former, summers are not much hotter than the warm to cool winters, as it is typical of higher elevations near the equator. As the trade winds coming from the northeast go through [[orographic lift]], rainfall is both greater and more consistent throughout the year than in coastal areas (particularly the south coast only minutes away), with fog being a common occurrence. Aibonito holds the record for the lowest temperature in Puerto Rico. That is {{convert|40|°F|°C|abbr=on}} on March 9, 1911.<ref name="NOAA">[[National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration]] [http://www.srh.noaa.gov/sju/?n=climo_barranquitas] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130809232427/http://www.srh.noaa.gov/sju/?n=climo_barranquitas|date=August 9, 2013}}</ref> The highest temperature record is {{convert|98|°F|°C|abbr=on}} recorded on September 29, 1920.<ref name="NOAA"/> Aibonito is among Puerto Rico's coolest towns.

== Tourism ==

===Landmarks and places of interest===
Two of the older landmarks of Aibonito are the {{lang|es|Old Encanto Theater}}, and {{lang|es|Moscoso Pharmacy}}. Their building facades reflect early 20th century architecture. The Mirador Overlook allows for panoramic views of the [[Río de la Plata (Puerto Rico)|Plata Valley]]. The Degetau Stone (in {{langx|es|La Piedra de Degetau}}) was where Federico Degetau, a writer, found his inspiration. {{lang|es|Casa Museo Federico Degetau}} is the restored, house museum of the writer. [[Church San José of Aibonito|San José Church]], which was designed by architect Pedro Cobreros between 1887 and 1897, was built in 1898 is located in the central plaza. There is a plaque commemoration the 100th anniversary of the Spanish–American War at Asomante Memorial, a place with a view of the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Central Mountain Range]]. The [[La Trinchera de Asomante|Asomante Trench]] in {{langx|es|La Trinchera de Asomante}} is where [[Battle of Aibonito Pass|part]] of the [[Spanish–American War]] took place. The main square is named after [[Segundo Ruiz Belvis|Segundo Ruíz Belvis]], a national [[Abolitionism|abolitionist]] hero. The [[San Cristóbal Canyon]], a nine-kilometers-long [[canyon]] with [[Waterfall|waterfalls]] is located between Aibonito and Barranquitas. In [[Robles, Aibonito, Puerto Rico|Robles]] barrio there is an iron bridge with [[PR-176]] and was built in 1892. The annual [[Aibonito Festival of Flowers|Festival of Flowers]] is celebrated each June in Aibonito.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" />

To stimulate local tourism during the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Puerto Rico]], the [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]] launched the ''Voy Turistiendo'' (I'm Touring) campaign in 2021. The campaign featured a passport book with a page for each municipality. The {{lang|es|Voy Turisteando}} Aibonito passport page lists {{lang|es|Mirador Piedra Degetau}}, ''[[Federico Degetau House and Museum|Casa Museo Federico Degetau]]'', Meyer's Nurseries for [[agritourism]], and several travel routes through Aibonito including the {{lang|es|Ruta de Las Flores}}, {{lang|es|Ruta del Pollo}}, and {{lang|es|Ruta Cultural}} as places of interest.<ref>{{cite book| title=Pasaporte: Voy Turisteando| url=https://voyturisteando.com/78-destinos/| publisher=Compañia de Turismo de Puerto Rico| year=2021| language=es| access-date=24 August 2021| archive-date=24 August 2021| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210824215951/https://voyturisteando.com/78-destinos/| url-status=live}}</ref>

== Culture ==
=== Sports ===
Aibonito had a [[Baloncesto Superior Nacional|BSN]] basketball franchise called the [[Polluelos de Aibonito]].<ref name="Isla_News_PR">{{cite news |title=El Béisbol Recuerda A Héctor Ferrer |url=http://www.islanewspr.com/2018/11/06/el-beisbol-recuerda-a-hector-ferrer/ |access-date=1 February 2019 |work=Isla News PR |date=November 6, 2018 |language=es |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190202100821/http://www.islanewspr.com/2018/11/06/el-beisbol-recuerda-a-hector-ferrer/ |archive-date=2 February 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In 1986 they beat the defending champions, [[Atléticos de San Germán]], in seven games to win their only championship. In 1987, the Polluelos reached the finals once again, but that time, they lost in seven games to the [[Titanes de Morovis]]. Recently, the Polluelos have not been able to see action on the BSN's tournaments because of economic and team ownership problems.
Also Aibonito had a Double AA baseball. The franchise is also Polluelos de Aibonito. In 1966 they won their only baseball championship.

=== Festivals and events ===
Aibonito celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in late July or early August. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de Santiago Apostol}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" /> The festival has featured live performances by well-known artists such as [[Ismael Miranda]], and [[Tommy Torres]].<ref name="Fiestas 2014">{{cite web |title=Fiestas Tradicionales Aibonito, 2013 |url=https://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/07/1005293_568368146535874_1769584052_n.jpg |website=sondeaquiprnet |publisher=El Gobierno Municipal de Aibonito |access-date=14 August 2023 |archive-date=13 July 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140713082356/http://sondeaquiprnet.files.wordpress.com/2013/07/1005293_568368146535874_1769584052_n.jpg |url-status=live }}</ref>

Aibonito celebrates the [[Aibonito Festival of Flowers]] between late June and early July.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-06-20 |title=Regresa el Festival de las Flores de Aibonito con 32 jardines en exhibición |trans-title=Festival of flowers of Aibonito returns with 32 gardens on exhibit |url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/notas/regresa-el-festival-de-las-flores-de-aibonito-con-32-jardines-en-exhibicion/ |url-status=live |access-date=2022-06-21 |website=[[El Nuevo Día]] |language=es |archive-date=21 June 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220621131838/https://www.elnuevodia.com/entretenimiento/cultura/notas/regresa-el-festival-de-las-flores-de-aibonito-con-32-jardines-en-exhibicion/ }}</ref> During this event, many visitors from other towns and countries come to Aibonito to see a huge display of various flowers and others. Aibonito also celebrates {{lang|es|Festival de la Montaña}} in November.

== Economy ==
=== Agriculture ===
Aibonito has several plantations of [[tobacco]] and [[coffee]]. They are also known for their flower plantations. Also, a popular chicken producer in the island, To-Ricos, operates from Aibonito.<ref name="PR_Ency_General_Info" />

===Business===
[[Baxter International]] has one of its factories located in Aibonito. They produce medical tools for the hospitals and other medical uses.

===Industrial===
Other industries located in Aibonito are clothing, furniture and [[tapestry]] factories, as well as [[pottery]] and ceramics.

==Current projects==
In 2014, [[William Alicea Pérez]], the mayor of Aibonito stressed that many improvements had been made to the town and municipality of Aibonito and to its urban center. Several infrastructure projects were completed in 2014, including the inauguration of an assisted living center, and a stadium and he announced future plans for a gym, progress on another stadium and other projects that were under way for the benefit of Aibonito. Pérez indicated that its urban center had been transformed and would continue to be improved upon. The construction of a gym for boxing and martial arts is underway with the help of [[Miguel Cotto]], a Puerto Rican boxing champion.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lacordillera.net/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=7378:rinde-cuentas-el-alcalde-de-aibonito&catid=42:noticias&Itemid=109|title=Rinde cuentas el Alcalde de Aibonito|newspaper=La Cordillera|access-date=24 June 2019|archive-date=1 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200901033456/http://www.lacordillera.net/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=7378%3Arinde-cuentas-el-alcalde-de-aibonito&catid=42%3Anoticias&Itemid=109|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Government ==
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}

All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a mayor, elected every four years. The current mayor of Aibonito is [[William Alicea Pérez]], of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP). He was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2008|2008 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VI]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Rafael Santos Ortiz]] and [[Wilmer Reyes Berríos]] were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Guayama_VI.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241115225812/https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Guayama_VI.xml |date=15 November 2024 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

== Symbols ==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15 | archive-date=20 January 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210120152958/http://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | url-status=live }}</ref>
===Flag===
The flag of Aibonito features four equal horizontal bands of blue, white, red, and yellow; a green isosceles triangle based on the hoist side bears the town's coat of arms.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=AIBONITO | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/AIBONITO.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219001206/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/AIBONITO.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=16 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
The coat of arms features a four-paneled shield. The upper left corner features the [[Asomante Mountain]] with a sword in front, representing the last battles of the [[Spanish–American War]] that took place there. The upper right corner features a white band on a blue field, symbolizing the fog that covers the region in winter. To each side of the band, a purple color flower and a golden lily. The lower left corner features the gold tower of [[Casa Manresa]], to symbolize the spiritual value of the institution. In the lower right corner, a divided green mountain which represents the [[San Cristóbal Canyon]] with a seashell above it that symbolizes Apostle James.
