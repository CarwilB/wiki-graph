'''Ráquira''', is a municipality and town in [[Boyacá Department]], [[Colombia]], part of the subregion of the [[Ricaurte Province]]. Ráquira is situated on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]] and the urban center at an altitude of {{convert|2150|m|ft}}. It borders [[Tinjacá]] and [[Sutamarchan|Sutamarchán]] in the north, [[Guachetá]], [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]] in the south, in the east [[Sáchica]] and [[Samacá]] and in the west [[San Miguel de Sema]] and [[Lake Fúquene]].<ref name=websiteRaquira>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.raquira-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Ráquira]</ref>

== Etymology ==
In [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]], Ráquira means "Village of the pans".<ref name=excelsio>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.excelsio.net/2011/11/sabe-el-origen-del-nombre-de-su.html Etymology Ráquira] - Excelsio.net</ref>

== History ==
The area of Ráquira was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]] in the centuries before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]] of the central highlands of the Colombian [[Andes]]. Already in those times Ráquira was famous for its [[Muisca art|ceramics]] due to the clay of the area.<ref>{{in lang|es}} [https://letras-uruguay.espaciolatino.com/aaa/bolivar_grimaldos_rafael/los_muiscas_a_la_llegada.htm Muisca history and ceramics in Ráquira]</ref><ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.galeon.com/culturasamerica/Muiscas.htm Ceramics of Ráquira because of the clay present there]</ref>

In March 1537 [[conquistador]] [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] crossed the valley around Ráquira. Modern Ráquira was founded on October 18, 1580 by [[friar]] Francisco de Orejuela.<ref name=websiteRaquira/>

== Economy ==
About three quarters of the economy of Ráquira is centered on the handcrafts. Other economical activities are [[agriculture]], [[livestock]] farming and [[mining]].<ref name=websiteRaquira/>

== Tourism ==
Ráquira is famous in Colombia for its colony of artisans, who produce traditional northern Andean pottery & hand-woven goods. The Sunday market is especially a popular time to visit. Ráquira is also known for its colorful houses that contrast with the famous nearby town of [[Villa de Leyva]].<ref>[http://www.vivatravelguides.com/south-america/colombia/eastern-colombia/villa-de-leyva/near-villa-de-leyva/ Crafts in Ráquira]</ref>
