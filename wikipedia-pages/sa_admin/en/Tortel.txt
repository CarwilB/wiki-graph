'''Tortel''' is a [[Chile]]an [[Communes of Chile|commune]] located at the outflow of the [[Baker River, Chile|Baker River]] and [[Pascua River]] to the Pacific Ocean. It lies between the [[Northern Patagonian Ice Field|Northern]] and [[Southern Patagonian Ice Field]] in the [[Capitán Prat Province]] in the [[Aysén Region]]. The commune is administered by the municipality in [[Caleta Tortel]], the principal settlement.

According to the 2002 [[census]] of the Chilean [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Tortel spans an area of {{convert|19930.6|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 507 inhabitants (322 men and 185 women), making the commune an entirely [[rural area]]. The population grew by 13% (59 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Tortel is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2008–2012 alcalde is Bernardo López Sierra ([[Party for Democracy (Chile)|PPD]]).<ref name="Munitel"/><ref name="Official"/>

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Tortel is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Iván Fuentes Castillo (''[[Nueva Mayoría]]'') and David Sandoval ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]]) as part of the 59th electoral district, which includes the entire [[Aisén Region]]. The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Antonio Horvath Kiss ([[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]]) and Patricio Walker Prieto (PDC) as part of the 18th senatorial constituency (Aisén Region).

==Landmarks and main features==
Tortel commune is one of the most remote in southern Chile and its unspoiled landscape is one of its principal attractions. Features include:
* [[Caleta Tortel]] is a lumber town with an intricate walkway system built by the town's inhabitants which run several kilometres around the cove (“caleta”). Without these walkways, the only way to communicate and travel between houses would be by boat, given the density of the vegetation and the steepness of the hills around the cove.<ref>[http://www.livingatlaschile.com/?s=tortel Tortel walkways] Atlas vivo de chile, retrieved December 09, 2013</ref> The walkway system has become part of the town's culture and a local tourist attraction.
* [[Laguna San Rafael National Park]] is a park located on the [[Pacific]] coast of southern [[Chile]]. The park is named for the [[San Rafael Lagoon]] formed by the retreat of the [[San Rafael Glacier]]. Created in 1959, it covers an area of {{convert|17420|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and includes the [[Northern Patagonian Ice Field]]. A [[fjord]] of more than {{convert|16|km|0|abbr=on}} in length is one of the park's principal attractions.
* The [[Southern Patagonian Ice Field]] ({{langx|es|Hielos Continentales or Campo de Hielo Sur}}), located in the Southern [[Patagonia]]n [[Andes]] between [[Argentina]] and [[Chile]], is the world's second largest contiguous extrapolar [[ice field]].<ref>At about 16,800 square kilometers, it is second only to southeastern Alaska's approximately 25,000 square kilometer [[Kluane / Wrangell–St. Elias / Glacier Bay / Tatshenshini-Alsek Ice Field]].</ref> It is the bigger of two remnant parts of the [[Patagonian Ice Sheet]], which covered all of southern [[Chile]] during the [[last glacial period]], locally called the [[Llanquihue glaciation]].
* [[Katalalixar National Reserve]] is a natural reserve located in an archipelago between the [[Southern Patagonian Ice Field]] and the [[Northern Patagonian Ice Field]] in the [[Aysén del General Carlos Ibáñez del Campo Region|Aysén Region]] of [[Chile]]. The reserve was created in 1983 and has no infrastructure. It covers an area of {{convert|6245|km2|abbr=on}} within the [[Magellanic subpolar forests]] [[ecoregion]] and exhibits more biodiversity than other areas of [[Zona Austral, Chile|southern Chile]]. This may appear contradictory, as the area is supposed to have been covered by the [[Patagonian Ice Sheet]] during the last glacial maximum.
* [[Bernardo O'Higgins National Park]] ({{IPA|es|beɾˈnaɾðo oˈxiɣins}}) is the largest of the [[protected area]]s in [[Chile]], covering an area of {{convert|35259|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} in both the [[Aysén del General Carlos Ibáñez del Campo Region|Aysén]] and [[Magallanes and Antartica Chilena Region|Magallanes and Antártica Chilena]] regions.<ref name="LetsGoChile">{{cite web| url= http://www.letsgochile.com/locations/patagonial/aysen-xi/bernardo-ohiggins-national-park |title= Bernardo O’Higgins National Park | accessdate= 18 February 2010}}</ref> Management of this and other national parks in Chile is entrusted to the ''[[National Forest Corporation (Chile)|Corporación Nacional Forestal]]'' (CONAF).<ref name="conaf">{{cite web | title =Parque Nacional Bernardo O'Higgins | publisher =Corporacion Nacional Forestal | url =http://www.conaf.cl/parques/ficha-parque_nacional_bernardo_ohiggins_-12.html | accessdate =2012-03-20 | url-status =dead | archiveurl =https://web.archive.org/web/20120807032131/http://www.conaf.cl/parques/ficha-parque_nacional_bernardo_ohiggins_-12.html | archivedate =2012-08-07 }}</ref> The park is named after General [[Bernardo O'Higgins]], the first head of state of the Republic of Chile. [[Los Glaciares National Park]] ([[Argentina]]) and [[Torres del Paine National Park]] (Chile) are its neighbours to the east, [[Laguna San Rafael National Park]] is located to the north, the [[Alacalufes National Reserve]] to the southwest and the [[Katalalixar National Reserve]] to the northwest.
