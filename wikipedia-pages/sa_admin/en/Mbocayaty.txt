'''Mbocayaty''' ''([[Paraguayan Guaraní|Guaraní]]: Mbokajaty)'' is a [[List of cities and towns in Paraguay|town]] in the [[Guairá Department]] of [[Paraguay]]. Its located 7&nbsp;km to the north of [[Villarrica, Paraguay|Villarrica]] and 166&nbsp;km to the east of [[Asunción|Asuncion]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Mbocayaty del Guaira |url=https://www.municipios.gov.py/mbocayaty/ |access-date=September 19, 2024 |website=Municipios Paraguay}}</ref>

Originally known as ''Capilla Duarte'' ([[Spanish language|Spanish]] ''Duarte Chapel)'' due to the Catholic chapel built in the 1800s by a family named Duarte. Its current name comes from the [[Guarani language]] expression ''Mbokaja + ty'', meaning ¨''land with many [[Acrocomia aculeata|macaw palms]]''"<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |last=Barreto |first=Antonio Ramon |date=November 27, 2022 |title=Mbocayaty, jurisdiccion de Villarrica, antiguamente llamada Capilla Duarte |trans-title=Mbocayaty, territory of Villarrica, previously known as Duarte Chapel |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/cultural/2022/11/27/mbocayaty-jurisdiccion-de-villa-rica-antiguamente-llamada-capilla-duarte-1/ |access-date=September 19, 2024 |work=ABC Digital |language=spanish}}</ref>

== History and geography ==
It was founded due to a land donation by a [[Priesthood in the Catholic Church|Catholic priest]] named Jose Mariano Duarte. Jose Mariano, the first priest and possibly the first school teacher in the area, had inherited the lands from his father, General Carlos Duarte. The Duarte family was from [[Villarrica, Paraguay|Villarrica]].<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite news |last=Barreto |first=Antonio Ramon |date=December 18, 2022 |title=Mbocayaty, jurisdiccion de Villarrica, antiguamente llamada Capilla Duarte |trans-title=Mbocayaty, territory of Villarrica, previously known as Duarte Chapel |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/cultural/2022/12/18/mbocayaty-jurisdiccion-de-villa-rica-antiguamente-llamada-capilla-duarte-final/ |access-date=September 19, 2024 |work=ABC Digital |language=spanish}}</ref>

In 1903, it was upgraded to municipality disengaged from Villarrica. The first president of its gubernatorial board was Juan de la Cruz Talavera.<ref name=":0" />

During the [[Paraguayan War]], many female residents of what is now Mbocayaty donated their jewelry to help the war effort, and several local families donated part of their livestock to feed the soldiers.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Barreto |first=Antonio Ramon |date=December 11, 2022 |title=Mbocayaty, jurisdiccion de Villarrica, antiguamente llamada Capilla Duarte (3) |trans-title=Mbocayaty, territory of Villarrica, previously known as Duarte Chapel (3) |url=https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/cultural/2022/12/11/mbocayaty-jurisdiccion-de-villa-rica-antiguamente-llamada-capilla-duarte-3/ |access-date=September 19, 2024 |work=ABC Digital |language=spanish}}</ref> Since the XX century, the original territory of Mbocayaty started to be divided to lay the foundations of new towns like [[Natalicio Talavera, Paraguay|Natalicio]] in 1931, [[Colonia Independencia|Independencia]] in 1955 and [[Doctor Botrell|Bottrell]] in 1983.<ref name=":0" />

Mbocayaty is irrigated by the [[Tebicuarymí River|Tebicuarymi River]] and its tributary streams such as Ita, Yaguarete and Gervasia. It borders Bottrell to the North, separated by the Tebicuarymi River, Villarrica to the South, through a stream named Bobo, the towns of Natalicio and Independencia to the east, and the town of [[Yataity]] to the west.
