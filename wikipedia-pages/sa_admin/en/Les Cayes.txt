'''Les Cayes''' ({{IPAc-en|l|eɪ|_|ˈ|k|eɪ|,_|l|eɪ|_|ˈ|k|aɪ}} {{respell|lay|_|K(A)Y}}, {{IPA|fr|le kaj|lang}}), often referred to as '''Aux Cayes''' ({{IPA|fr|o kaj|lang}}; {{langx|ht|Okay}}), is a [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] and [[seaport]] in the [[Les Cayes Arrondissement]], in the [[Sud (department)|Sud]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Haiti]], with a population of 151,696. Due to its isolation from the political turmoil of the capital, [[Port-au-Prince]], it is one of Haiti's major ports, with export trade concentrating on mostly coffee and [[sugarcane]]. As the world's largest supplier of [[Chrysopogon zizanioides|vetiver]], it exports 250 tons annually of this ingredient of perfume and fragrance manufacturing.<ref>[http://www.tradeforum.org/Frager-Haiti-shortening-the-perfume-chain-to-become-world-number-one/ Frager, Haiti: shortening the perfume chain to become world number one] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220912133256/https://www.tradeforum.org/Frager-Haiti-shortening-the-perfume-chain-to-become-world-number-one/ |date=12 September 2022 }}, International Trade Centre, International Trade Forum - Issue 3/2001</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/business/2014/mar/04/chanel-perfume-legislation-guerlain-loreal |title=Perfume manufacturers must cope with the scarcity of precious supplies |newspaper=The Guardian |date=4 March 2014 |editor=The Guardian |access-date=12 April 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fida-pch.org/index.php?p=stories.View&story=11 |title=FEATURE-Perfumers promote fair trade for Haiti's 'super-crop' |author=Adams, David |date=24 April 2014 |publisher=Reuters |access-date=12 April 2015 |archive-date=12 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220912111603/https://fida-pch.org/?p=stories.View&story=11 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fida-pch.org/index.php?p=stories.View&story=11 |title=Did you know? Haiti is one of the World's Leading Vetiver Producers |editor=FIDA |year=2014 |access-date=12 April 2015 |archive-date=12 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220912111603/https://fida-pch.org/?p=stories.View&story=11 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Minor exports include bananas and timber.

==History==
===European settlement===
The island of what was known by the Spanish as [[Hispaniola]] was inhabited for thousands of years by [[indigenous people]]s.  The first European settlement in the southwest area was the town of ''Salvatierra de la Sabana'', founded by the Spanish explorer [[Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar]]<ref name="Floyd">{{cite book|last1=Floyd|first1=Troy|title=The Columbus Dynasty in the Caribbean, 1492-1526|date=1973|publisher=University of New Mexico Press|location=Albuquerque|pages=63}}</ref> in 1504. [[Vasco Núñez de Balboa]] was a co-founder of this town and lived there for several years trying to raise pigs as a business. Balboa gave up that enterprise and left the town hiding in a barrel of a Spanish expedition going to explore the Gulf of Uraba, Panama. [[Vasco Núñez de Balboa]], later on 25 September 1513 would discover the South Sea, today known as the Pacific Ocean. This settlement was abandoned in 1540.

The area was uninhabited until the French colonial administration founded the town of ''Aux Cayes'' ("On the [[caye]]s"), so named due to its proximity to [[Île-à-Vache]]. The town was destroyed twice by [[hurricane]]s in 1781 and 1788. In July 1793, the whites in Les Cayes were massacred.<ref name="KnightPalmer1989">{{cite book|author1=Franklin W. Knight|author2=Colin A. Palmer|title=The Modern Caribbean|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=w6zg5sYCHpAC&pg=PA32|access-date=2013-07-27|year=1989|publisher=UNC Press Books|isbn=978-0-8078-4240-9|page=32}}</ref>

===Asylum for independent insurgencies===
[[Simón Bolívar]] arrived in Les Cayes on 24 December 1815,{{sfnm|1a1=Slatta|1a2=de Grummond|1y=2003|1p=129|Langley|2009|2p=59|Arana|2013|3p=178}} and on 2 January 1816 was introduced to [[Alexandre Pétion]], President of the [[Republic of Haiti (1806–1820)|Republic of Haiti]] by a mutual friend{{sfnm|Masur|1969|1p=192|2a1=Slatta|2a2=de Grummond|2y=2003|2p=130|Lynch|2006|3p=97|Langley|2009|4p=59|Arana|2013|5pp=178–179}} seeking assistance for his insurgency against the Spanish colonial government in [[Venezuela]].<ref name="Aristide1996">{{cite book|author=Jean-Bertrand Aristide|title=Dignity|url=https://archive.org/details/dignity00aris|url-access=registration|year=1996|publisher=University of Virginia Press|isbn=978-0-8139-1674-3|page=[https://archive.org/details/dignity00aris/page/191 191]}}</ref> Bolívar and Pétion impressed and befriended each other and,{{sfnm|Masur|1969|1pp=192–193|2a1=Slatta|2a2=de Grummond|2y=2003|2p=130|Arana|2013|3p=179}} after Bolívar pledged to free every slave in the areas he occupied, Pétion gave him money and military supplies.{{sfnm|Masur|1969|1p=193|2a1=Slatta|2a2=de Grummond|2y=2003|2p=130|Lynch|2006|3p=97|Arana|2013|4p=179}}{{sfn|McFarlane|2014|p=313}}

Mexican nationalists, [[Martín Francisco Javier Mina y Larrea|Francisco Javier Mina]] and [[José Joaquín de Herrera]] took asylum in Les Cayes and were welcomed by Pétion during the [[Mexican War of Independence]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Antecedentes |url=https://embamex.sre.gob.mx/haiti/index.php/embajada/antecedentes-historicos|access-date=2020-09-17|website=embamex.sre.gob.mx}}</ref> 

===United States occupation of Haiti===
{{main|United States occupation of Haiti|Les Cayes massacre}}
[[File:In Haiti, the Americans Let Gunpowder Do the Talking.jpg|thumb|upright|''[[Le Petit Journal (newspaper)|Le Petit Journal]]'' illustration of the [[Les Cayes massacre]]]]

On 5 December 5 1919, American planes bombed Les Cayes in a possible act of intimidation. American pilots were investigated for their actions, though none were condemned. These actions were described by anthropologist Jean-Philippe Belleau as possibly "the first ever carried out by air on civilian populations".<ref name="Belleau-2016">{{cite web|last=Belleau|first=Jean-Philippe|date=January 25, 2016|title=Massacres perpetrated in the 20th Century in Haiti|url=https://www.sciencespo.fr/mass-violence-war-massacre-resistance/en/document/massacres-perpetrated-20th-century-haiti.html|url-status=live|access-date=May 28, 2021|website=[[Sciences Po]]|language=en|archive-date=June 2, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210602213715/https://www.sciencespo.fr/mass-violence-war-massacre-resistance/en/document/massacres-perpetrated-20th-century-haiti.html}}</ref>

President [[Herbert Hoover]] had become increasingly pressured about the effects of occupying Haiti at the time and began inquiring about a withdrawal strategy.<ref name="Munro-1969" /> By 1929, Haitians had grown angered with the Borno-Russell government and American occupation, with demands for [[direct elections]] increasing.<ref name="Munro-1969">{{cite journal|last=Munro|first=Dana December 1929, protests against the American occupation began at the Service Technique de l’Agriculture et de l’Enseignement Professionnel's main school.<ref name="Munro-1969" /> On December 6, 1929, about 1,500 Haitians peacefully protesting local economic conditions in Les Cayes were fired upon by U.S. Marines, with the massacre resulting in 12 to 22 Haitians dead and 51 injured.<ref ===Natural disasters===
In the wake of the [[2010 Haiti earthquake|12 January 2010 earthquake]], the Cuban military set up a [[field hospital]] in the region.<ref>Caribbean Net News, [http://www.caribbeannetnews.com/news-21303--2-2--.html "Cuba to open fifth field hospital in Haiti"] {{dead link|date=July 2016 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}, 5 February 2010. Accessed 5 February 2010</ref>

On 4 October 2016, [[Hurricane Matthew]] made landfall in Les Cayes causing severe damage.<ref name=relief-sos>{{cite report |title=Haïti: Premier bilan après le passage de l'ouragan Matthew  |url=http://reliefweb.int/report/haiti/ha-ti-premier-bilan-apr-s-le-passage-de-l-ouragan-matthew |work=SOS Children's Villages International |publisher=ReliefWeb |access-date=7 October 2016 |date=7 October 2016 |language=fr}}</ref>

On 14 August 2021, a [[2021 Haiti earthquake|magnitude 7.2 earthquake]] occurred near the city. It was the largest earthquake to strike Haiti in modern history, even stronger than the [[2010 Haiti earthquake|2010 earthquake]] near the Haitian capital.<ref name="USGS_DYFI_RESPONSES">{{cite web| url = https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/at00qxtxcn/dyfi/responses| title = undefined}}</ref> The earthquake killed over 2,200 people and injured around 12,700 others, most of them in Les Cayes and its surrounding areas.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2000560|title=Death toll from Haiti earthquake rises to 2,248|date=9 July 2021|website=BERNAMA|access-date=7 September 2021|archive-date=7 September 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210907185343/https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2000560|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Tourism ==
Les Cayes plays a significant role in the still under-developed Haitian tourism industry, with pleasant sights such as:
'''Gelée Beach''': one of the longest and most visited beaches in Haiti. The white sand beach of Gelée beach is very popular in Haiti, not only for its restaurants which serve typical southern Haiti dishes such as tonm-tonm, grilled-conch, grilled-fish and lobster, but also for hosting an annual music festival around mid-August which usually features some of the best Compas music bands. Many visitors both from within Haiti and neighboring USA will come spend the weekend in Les Cayes. As the number of tourists continues to grow, several new hotels and restaurants continue to pop up.

'''The Botanical Garden of Les Cayes'''  (Jardin Botanique des Cayes, in French) is located in Bergeau, at the northern entrance of the city. The site occupies an area of eight (8) hectares.
Other places of interest to tourists are the nearby [[Île à Vache]], Pic Macaya, Saut-Mathurine falls and Kounoubois cave in [[Camp-Perrin]], Pointe-de-Sable beach in [[Port-Salut]], Marie-Jeanne cave in [[Port-a-Piment]] and   [[Aquin Arrondissement|Arrondissement Aquin]] where Fort des Oliviers, Fort Anglais and Bonnet Carré can be found in the town of [[Saint-Louis-du-Sud|Saint Louis du Sud]].

==Facilities==
===Airport=== 
Les Cayes has an airport, [[Antoine-Simon Airport]]. In 2013, the first stone on the expansion project of the Antoine-Simon Airport in Les Cayes was laid. The project to make Antoine-Simon a viable international airport is part of broader efforts aiming at ramping up infrastructure development in the south.<ref>Caribbean Journal [http://www.caribjournal.com/2013/02/01/haiti-lays-first-stone-on-les-cayes-airport-expansion "Haiti Lays First Stone on Expansion of Airport in Les Cayes "]</ref>

The airport projected was completed in 2025, increasing the length of the runway to 1,850 meters (6,069 feet), increasing aircraft capacity, adding new lighting, a weather station, a control tower, and space for an Anti-Drug Trafficking Brigade.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Octave |first=Juhakenson Blaise, Fritznel D. |date=2025-03-06 |title=Les Cayes airport upgrade sparks hope—but is it enough for Haiti’s Great South? |url=https://haitiantimes.com/2025/03/06/les-cayes-airport-upgrade-for-international-flights/ |access-date=2025-12-03 |website=The Haitian Times |language=en-US}}</ref> The overall cost of the project was $26.44 million.<ref name=":0" /> At the time that the project began, Haitian officials Prime Minister [[Laurent Lamothe]] and Tourism Minister [[Stéphanie Villedrouin]] suggested that the airport would open up completely the Southern Region as the country saw tourism as one of the promising sectors capable of creating thousands of new jobs in the region.<ref>Caribbean Journal [http://www.caribjournal.com/2013/02/01/haiti-lays-first-stone-on-les-cayes-airport-expansion "Haiti Lays First Stone on Expansion of Airport in Les Cayes "]</ref> In the context of [[Haitian crisis (2018–present)|Haiti's ongoing crisis]], infrastructure in the south of Haiti has gained new importance, as the highways connecting the region to the rest of the country are under gang control.<ref name=":0" /> 

The expanded airport received its first international passenger flight on November 10, 2025, an [[IBC Airways]] flight from Miami.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Verdieu |first=Jordany Junior |date=2025-11-12 |title=IBC Airways lands first Miami-Les Cayes flight {{!}} Photos |url=https://haitiantimes.com/2025/11/12/ibc-airways-launches-miami-les-cayes-flights/ |access-date=2025-12-03 |website=The Haitian Times |language=en-US}}</ref>

Another airport project is also planned for the neighbouring island of Île-à-Vache.<ref>Caribbean Journal [http://www.caribjournal.com/2013/02/01/haiti-lays-first-stone-on-les-cayes-airport-expansion "Haiti Lays First Stone on Expansion of Airport in Les Cayes "]</ref>

===University===
Les Cayes has some training centers of which The [[American University of the Caribbean, Haiti]]; The Public University of The South in Les Cayes, (UPSAC); and 
The Law and Economics School of Les Cayes (EDSEC).

===Hospital===
Hôpital Immaculée Conception (Immaculate Conception Hospital, also referred to as HIC-Cayes), is the public hospital for Les Cayes and the South Department. Its facilities include an emergency department, maternity ward, and a dental clinic.

==Sport==

Les Cayes is home to professional football clubs, [[America des Cayes]] and [[FC Juventus des Cayes]].
