'''Mapastepec''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Chiapas|municipality]] in the [[List of states in Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Chiapas]] in southern [[Mexico]]. Its name derives from the place name ''mapachtepec'', "Hill of the [[Raccoon]]", a compound of the [[Nahuatl]] words ''mapachi'' ("raccoon") and ''tepetl'' ("mountain").<ref name="Starr">{{cite book |last=Starr |first=Frederick |date=1920 |publisher=Privately printed by the author |title=Aztec Place Names, Their Meaning And Mode Of Composition |edition=2nd |url=https://archive.org/stream/AztecPlaceNames#page/n13/search/mapach |access-date=September 22, 2018}} </ref>

Mapestepec is on the [[Pacific Ocean]], with roughly half of its territory on the [[Pacific Coastal Plain]] and half in the [[Sierra Madre de Chiapas]] mountain range. It is partly within two of Mexico's [[Biosphere Reserves]], featuring a number of important species, including the [[horned guan]] (''Oreophasis derbianus''), [[Baird's tapir]] (''Tapirus bairdii''), the [[Jaguar]] (''Panthera once'') and rare cloud forest and mangrove habitat.

The primary sector makes up over half of the local economy. Key products include cheese and dairy products and the local [[Ataulfo (mango)|Ataulfo mango]].

==History==

Mapastepec was founded in 1486 as an [[Aztecs|Aztec]] [[tributary state]], under the name of Mapachtépec.<ref name="Villalobos Velázquez">{{cite thesis |last=Villalobos Velázquez |first=Amílcar |date=September 2004 |title=Métodos para la Medición de la Pobreza : Caso, la Región del Soconusco, Chiapas |type=MSc |page=141 |publisher=Instituto Politécnico Nacional, Centro de Investigaciones Económicas, Administrativas y Sociales |language=es |trans-title=Methods for the Measurement of Poverty: Case Study, the Region of Soconusco, Chiapas |url=https://tesis.ipn.mx/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/14205/2005%20AMILCAR%20VILLALOBOS%20VELAZQUEZ.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y |access-date=September 22, 2018}}</ref> In 1611, the first census conducted by the colonial authorities recorded Mapastepec's population as 265.<ref name=SEDESOL /> Mapastepec was upgraded to the status of [[town]] (''villa'') by a decree issued by Ephraim A. Osorio, then governor of the State of [[Chiapas]], on July 5, 1955.<ref name=SEDESOL />

==Demographics==

===Population===

The total population of the municipality was 47,932 at the time of the 2015 [[Intercensal estimate#Intercensal survey|Intercensal Survey]], conducted by the [[National Institute of Statistics and Geography]] (''Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía'' - INEGI).<ref name="INEGI">{{cite web |title=Mapastepec, Chiapas (07051) |url=http://www.beta.inegi.org.mx/app/areasgeograficas/?ag=07# |work=México en Cifras |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía]] (INEGI) |access-date=23 September 2018}} </ref> This represented an increase from 43,913 in 2010,<ref name="SEDESOL">{{cite web |title=Mapastepec |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=07&mun=051 |work=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[Secretaría de Desarrollo Social]] (SEDESOL)|access-date=23 April 2014}}</ref> and 39,055 in 2005.<ref name="Enciclopedia">{{cite web |title=Mapastepec |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/chiapas/municipios/07051a.htm |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal Gobierno del Estado de Chiapas |access-date=July 25, 2008 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927205515/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/chiapas/municipios/07051a.htm |archive-date=September 27, 2007}}</ref>

In 2015, women made up 50.9% of the inhabitants of the municipality and men 49.1%, with an average age of 24. The population represented 0.9% of the state of Chiapas.<ref name="INEGI"/>

The population of the town of Mapastepec was 17,931 in 2010, followed by Sesecapa (2,143) and Nuevo Milenio Valdivia (1,789).<ref name=SEDESOL/> In 2015, 52 of the 118 localities in the municipality had more than 100 inhabitants. Isolation and the poor condition of roads, particularly at higher altitudes, makes access to public services difficult for many of the smaller settlements.<ref name=PMD />

===Indigenous languages===

The 2010 Census of Population and Housing (''Censo de Población y Vivienda 2010'') recorded 131 speakers of an indigenous language in Mapastepec, representing 0.33 percent of its population.<ref name="INEGI"/> The most widely spoken of these languages were [[Zapotec languages|Zapotec]] (41), [[Mam people|Mame]] (13), [[Tzotzil language|Tzotzil]] (13) and [[Tzeltal language|Tzeltal]] (12), with another twelve languages represented. An additional 34 respondents did not specify which indigenous language they spoke.<ref name=PMD />

===Religion===

As of 2005, 65.68% of the population was Catholic, higher than the figure for the region of Soconusco (58.98%) and slightly above the national figure of 63.83%. 14.77% of the population was Protestant, 4.81% non-evangelical biblical, and 13.07% professed no religion.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

==Geography==

===Location===

The municipality of Mapastepec is situated in the southern part of the state of [[Chiapas]]. It is bounded by the municipalities of [[La Concordia, Chiapas|La Concordia]], [[Ángel Albino Corzo]] and [[Montecristo de Guerrero]] to the north, [[Siltepec]], [[Acacoyagua Municipality|Acacoyagua]] and [[Acapetahua]] to the east, the [[Pacific Ocean]] to the south, and  [[Pijijiapan]] to the west.<ref name="Prontuario">{{cite web |title=Mapastepec, Chiapas (07051) |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/07/07051.pdf |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos - Mapastepec, Chiapas |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía]] (INEGI) |language=es |trans-title=Handbook of Municipal Geographic Information of the United Mexican States - Mapastepec, Chiapas |access-date=September 24, 2018}} </ref>

The municipality covers an area of {{convert|1085|km2|sqmi|0}}, representing 19.81% of the Soconusco region and 1.44% of the state of Chiapas. It stretches from the Pacific Ocean to the [[Sierra Madre de Chiapas]] mountain range, with a maximum altitude of {{cvt|2,700|m|ft}} [[meters above sea level|above sea level]] (a.s.l.). The highest peaks are La Cumbre, La Bandera and El Pujido. Mountainous and hilly terrain makes up approximately 50% of the area of the municipality, with low foothills accounting for another 10%. 40% of its territory lies on the Pacific coastal plain, and some 6% of this area is alluvial floodplain and saltmarsh. Alluvial soils predominate.<ref name=PMD /><ref name=Enciclopedia />

The town of Mapestepec, the municipal capital, is at {{convert|40|m|ft}} a.s.l.<ref name=PMD />

The main rivers of Mapastepec are the Río Novillero, Río San Nicolás, Río Gobierno and Río Las Flores. Smaller [[perennial stream]]s include the Arroyo Sesecapa, Arroyo Tablasón and Arroyo Cuilapa. A number of [[stream#Intermittent stream|intermittent streams]] run through the municipality. The principal [[bodies of water]] are the Chantuto, Pampa Buenavista and Pampa Castaña lagoons, and the El Coco estuary.<ref name="PMD">{{cite web |url=http://ayuntamientomapastepec.com.mx/index_htm_files/PLAN%20MUNICIPAL%20DE%20DESARROLLO%20MAPASTEPEC%202015-2018.pdf |title=Plan Municipal de Desarrollo Mapastepec 2015-2018 |last=Gobierno Municipal de Mapastepec, Chiapas, Administración 2015-2018 |date=2015 |language=es |trans-title=Municipal Development Plan for Mapastepec, Chiapas, 2015-2018 Administration |website=ayuntamientomapastepec.com.mx |access-date=September 23, 2018}}</ref>

===Climate===
Hot and humid from January to September and a milder coastal climate from October to December. Its average annual temperature is {{cvt|26.5|C|F}}, with an average annual rainfall of {{cvt|2,500|mm|in}}. The heaviest rainfall occurs from June to September.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

===Protected areas===
More than half of the territory of Mapastepec (51.85%) is situated in protected and conservation areas, totalling {{cvt|63,205.6|ha}}.<ref name=PMD />

The Huizapa-Sesecapa Reforestation Area (''Zona de Reforestación Huizapa-Sesecapa'') lies on the Pacific coastal plain. Created by decree of October 8, 1936, it was the first protected natural area in the state of Chiapas. The Pico El Loro-Paxtal ecological conservation zone was created by decree of November 22, 2000 to protect cloud forest in the Sierra Madre de Chiapas foothills.<ref name=Enciclopedia /> 

Parts of Mapastepec are within two important natural reserves in the [[World Network of Biosphere Reserves]] designated by the [[UNESCO]] [[Man and the Biosphere Programme]].

The El Triunfo reserve, created by decree of March 13, 1990, was inscribed on the list of [[World Network of Biosphere Reserves in Latin America and the Caribbean#Mexico|Mexican Biosphere Reserves]] in 1993. It contains two of the most endangered ecosystems in Mexico: the Soconusco tropical rainforest, and cloud forest growing to over 2,000 meters (6,600 ft) above sea level.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

[[La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserve]], established as a reserve by decree on June 6, 1995,<ref name=Enciclopedia /> was inscribed on the list of Biosphere Reserves in 2006. La Encrucijada is also protected under the [[Ramsar Convention]], figuring on the [[List of Ramsar wetlands of international importance]]. It was inscribed on the [[list of Ramsar sites in Mexico]] on March 20, 1996. Located to the south of the town of Mapastepec, La Encrucijada contains one of the most significant wetland and coastal lagoon networks in Mexico, with mangroves among the tallest of the Pacific coast of the Americas.<ref name="PAOT">{{cite web |url=http://www.paot.mx/centro/ine-semarnat/anp/AN09.pdf |title=Programa de Manejo Reserva de la Biosfera La Encrucijada, México |last=Instituto Nacional de Ecología (SEMARNAP) |date=1999 |language=es |trans-title=Management Program for La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserve, Mexico |website=www.paot.mx |access-date=September 23, 2018}}</ref><ref name=Enciclopedia />

Both the El Triunfo and La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserves are under the administration of the [[Mexican Protected Natural Areas|National Commission of Protected Natural Areas]] (''Comisión Nacional de Áreas Naturales Protegidas'', CONANP), an agency of the [[Federal government of Mexico|Federal government]]. CONANP has a water management office for El Triunfo Biosphere Reserve in the town of Mapastepec.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://agua.org.mx/directorio-del-agua/el-triunfo// |title=Directorio del Agua – El Triunfo |access-date=September 24, 2018 |language=es |trans-title=Water Board – El Triunfo}}</ref>

===Flora===
[[File:Kapok tree Honolulu.jpg|250px|thumb|[[Ceiba pentandra]]]] 

The vegetation of Mapastepec varies from the lowland jungle and mangroves of the Pacific coastal plain to oak–pine forest in the high mountains of the [[Sierra Madre de Chiapas]]. <ref name=Enciclopedia /> Emblematic mangrove and lowland species in La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserve include the red mangrove (''[[Rhizophora mangle]]''), white mangrove (''[[Laguncularia racemosa]]''), black mangrove (''[[Avicennia germinans]]''), buttonwood (''[[Conocarpus erectus]]''), gumbo-limbo, also known as copperwood, or chaca (''[[Bursera simaruba]]''), Mexican palmetto (''[[Sabal mexicana]]''), (''[[Cyperus|Cyperus spp.]]''),  and Malabar chestnut, or saba nut (''[[Pachira aquatica]]''). Other species growing in the lowland areas and adjacent highlands include ''([[Pithecellobium dulce]]''), and pinguin (''[[Bromelia pinguin]]'').<ref name="SIMEC-Encrucijada">{{cite web |url=https://simec.conanp.gob.mx/ficha.php?anp=163&reg=11 |title=El Encrucijada |last=Sistema de Información, Monitoreo y Evaluación para la Conservación (SIMEC), Comisión Nacional de Áreas Naturales Protegidas (CONANP) |date=September 14, 2018 |website=www.simec.conanp.gob.mx |access-date=September 23, 2018}}</ref>

The cloud forest of El Triunfo Biosphere Reserve also contains a rich diversity of [[orchids]], [[cycads]] and tree ferns ([[Cyatheales]]).<ref name="CONANP-Triunfo">{{cite web |url=https://www.gob.mx/conanp/prensa/la-reserva-de-la-biosfera-el-triunfo-ejemplo-de-conservacion-y-sustentabilidad-23304 |title=La Reserva de la Biosfera El Triunfo, Ejemplo de Conservación y Sustentabilidad |last=Comisión Nacional de Áreas Naturales Protegidas (CONANP) |date=March 14, 2016 |website=www.gob.mx |access-date=September 23, 2018}}</ref>

Among other species are the hardwood Ecuador laurel or salmwood (''[[Cordia alliodora]]'') in the high forests, the guanacaste (''[[Enterolobium cyclocarpum]]''), the ceiba or kapok (''[[Ceiba pentandra]]''), the lowland evergreen sapodilla (''[[Manilkara zapota]]''), ''[[Calycophyllum candidissimum]]'', and the yellow-wood (''[[Terminalia oblongata]]'').

Fruits include avocados, mamey, mango, custard apple, soursop, papausa, cuajinicuil, and caspirol.

===Fauna===
The endangered [[horned guan]] (''pavón cornudo''; ''Oreophasis derbianus''), considered the state bird, is found in El Triunfo Biosphere Reserve in the high mountains of Mapastepec and neighboring municipalities.<ref>{{cite thesis |last=González-García |first=D. Fernando |date=July 15, 2017 |title=Ecología e Historia Natural del Pavón Oreophasis derbianus : Un Crácido de Montaña |type=PhD |chapter=Introducción Sección 1.1 |publisher=Universidad de Alicante |chapter-url=http://docplayer.es/77835948-Ecologia-e-historia-natural-del-pavon-oreophasis-derbianus-un-cracido-de-montana-fernando-gonzalez-garcia.html |access-date=September 23, 2018}}</ref> Also present is the emblem of the reserve, the [[Resplendent quetzal]] (''Pharomachrus mocinno''). Other key species include [[Azure-rumped tanager]] (''Poecilostreptus cabanisi''), the [[king vulture]] (''Sarcoramphus papa''), [[Baird's tapir]] (''Tapirus bairdii''); the [[Jaguar]] (''Panthera once''), the [[oncilla]] (''Leopardus tigrinus'') and the [[otter]] (''Lutrinae'').<ref name="CONANP-Triunfo"/>

Also found are hummingbirds, the harpy eagle, hawk, parrot, owl, skylark, ''chupamiel'', mockingbird, rook, buzzard, pigeons, doves, yellow-throated toucans and many other bird species. Mammals and reptiles include the spider monkey, raccoon, porcupine, anteater, skunk, deer, puma, bobcat, agouti, wild boar, opossum, rabbit, rattlesnake, coral snake, false coral snake, ''nahuyaca'', and ''Chichicua''. There is high insect diversity.

Emblematic species of La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserve on the coastal plain include the [[northern tamandua]] (''Tamandua mexicana''), [[orange-chinned parakeet]] (''Brotogeris jugularis''), [[American crocodile]] (''Crocodylus acutus''), [[spectacled caiman]] (''Caiman crocodilus''), [[green iguana]] (''Iguana iguana''), [[wood stork]] (''Mycteria americana''), [[Eupsittula canicularis|orange-fronted parakeet]] (''Aratinga canicularis''), [[nine-banded armadillo]] (''Dasypus novemcinctus''), [[gray fox]] (''Urocyon cinereoargenteus''), [[boa constrictor]] (''Boa constrictor''), black spiny-tailed iguana (''[[Ctenosaura similis]]''), [[olive ridley sea turtle]] (''Lepidochelys olivacea''), and [[scorpion mud turtle]] (''Kinosternum scorpioides'').<ref name="SIMEC-Encrucijada"/> 

Also endemic to the area are the [[cinnamon-tailed sparrow]] (''Peucaea sumichrasti''), [[berylline hummingbird]] (''Amazilia berillyna''), [[green-fronted hummingbird]] (''Amazilia viridifrons''), [[giant wren]] (''Campylorhynchus chiapensis''), [[Mexican hairy dwarf porcupine]] (''Coendou mexicanus''), Mexican spiny-tailed iguana (''[[Ctenosaura pectinata]]''), [[flammulated flycatcher]] (''Deltarhynchus flammulatus''), [[Red-breasted chat]] (''Granatellus venustus''), [[West Mexican chachalaca]] (''Ortalis poliocephala''), [[rose-bellied bunting]] (''Passerina rositae''), [[Spiny lizard|longtail spiny lizard]] (''Sceloporus siniferus''), [[citreoline trogon]] (''Trogon citreolus''), [[Sonora mud turtle]] (''Kinosternon sonoriense''), and the [[cinnamon myotis]] bat (''Myotis fortidens'').<ref name="SIMEC-Encrucijada"/>

===Environmental issues===
Chiapas has a wide variety of natural resources. Heavy exploitation over many years, in particular of timber, has led to significant deforestation in the municipality, causing species loss and environmental degradation. Logging and forest fires are also associated with the [[Leaching (agriculture)|leaching]] of nutrients from the soil and with soil erosion, aggravated by heavy rainfall. Pollution of local waterways is related to agricultural activities including fertilizer use. These environmental issues are the focus of municipal government ecological management, conservation and sustainability planning.<ref name="PMD"/>

==Cultural and tourist attractions==

===Festivals===

The most important [[feast days]] celebrated in Mapastepec are the feast day of the ''Señor de Esquipulas'', honoring the [[Black Christ of Esquipulas]] on January 15, and the [[Feast of Saints Peter and Paul]] on June 29.<ref name=Enciclopedia /> [[Paul the Apostle]] (''San Pedro Apostol'') is considered the patron saint of the town of Mapastepec. The town also honors the [[Our Lady of Guadalupe|Virgin of Guadalupe]]. 

Easter, the [[Day of the Dead]] ({{langx|es|Día de Muertos}}<!-- Please do not change "Día de Muertos&nbsp;— it is correct, it does not need the word "los". -->), Christmas and the New Year are also celebrated.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

In March, a trade and agriculture fair is held in the town of Mapastepec, featuring associated cultural events and musical performances. Since 2016 it has been promoted as the Cheese Fair (''Feria del Queso''), a showcase for local dairy produce.<ref>{{cite news |date=March 19, 2016 |title=Realizan en Mapastepec la feria del queso |trans-title=Cheese Festival Held in Mapastepec |url=http://www.diariodechiapas.com/landing/realizan-en-mapastepec-la-feria-del-queso/ |language=es |work=Diario de Chiapas |location=Chiapas, Mexico |access-date=September 24, 2018}}</ref><ref name="Cheese Fair">{{cite news |date=March 27, 2018 |title=Impresionante cierre de la feria del queso en Mapastepec |trans-title=Impressive Close to the Cheese Festival in Mapastepec |url=http://www.asich.com/index.php?itemid=46581 |language=es |work=Agencia de Servicios Informativos de Chiapas |location=Chiapas, Mexico |access-date=September 24, 2018}}</ref>

===Crafts===

Handcrafted woodcuts are made in the municipality.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

===Gastronomy===

Typical dishes of the municipality are iguana tamales, armadillo stew, baked chicken, marshmallow candies, ''nuegados chilacayote,'' coconut water, beer, cocoa, and white pozol.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

===Sports===

The four main football fields in Mapastepec are the November 20 field, Santa Cruz, Olimpia and Anahuac.  

In addition to other sports, the traditional sport of [[Charreada|Charrería]] is also popular.<ref>{{cite news |last=Guizar Solis |first=Oscar Armando |date=February 18, 2014 |title=Informativo - ¡¡¡¡¡ Charrería en Chiapas, Unión de Charros Trabajando !!!!! |trans-title=Bulletin – Charreria in Chiapas, Charros at Work!!!!! |url=http://www.decharros.com/noticias2014/marimba/oscarguizar_feb17.htm |language=es |location=Chiapas, Mexico |access-date=September 25, 2018}}</ref>

==Economy==

The economy of the municipality is based on agriculture, beef and dairy cattle and other livestock farming, fishing and aquaculture, forestry and construction, tourism, trade and services.<ref name=PMD/><ref name=Enciclopedia/>
The dairy industry, celebrated in the annual Cheese Fair<ref name="Cheese Fair" /> is a significant sector, producing some 20 major cheeses including cream cheese, mediacrema cheese, cheese snacks, cheese, Cotija cheese, and cream.

===Agriculture and livestock===

The [[Primary sector of the economy|primary sector]] occupies more than half of the working population of the municipality (52.82% in 2000). The agricultural sector is the largest economic sector.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

The principal crops grown are [[avocado]], [[banana]], [[cocoa beans]], [[coffee beans]], [[fodder]], green [[chili peppers]], [[green beans]], [[maize]], [[mango]], [[orange (fruit)|orange]]s, [[African oil palm]], [[parlour palm]] (''Chamaedorea'' spp.), [[rice]], [[sesame]], [[sorghum]] and [[watermelon]].<ref name=PMD /><ref name=Enciclopedia /> Approximately half of the municipality lies on the Pacific coastal plain, facilitating extensive agriculture and allowing for significant mechanization. Agricultural tractors are widely used. 

Mapastepec grows many kinds of mango. Varieties include the [[Ataulfo (mango)|Ataulfo mango]], a protected [[designation of origin]] cultivar originating locally from the Soconusco region of Chiapas.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=692301&fecha=27/08/2003/ |title=Declaración general de protección de la denominación de origen Mango Ataulfo del Soconusco Chiapas |last=Diario Oficial de la Federación |publisher=Secretaría de Gobernación |date=August 27, 2003 |website=www.dof.gob.mx |access-date=September 24, 2018 |language=es |trans-title=General Declaration of Protection of the Designation of Origin Mango Ataulfo of Soconusco Chiapas}}</ref> Also grown are the cultivars known in Mexico as ''Criollo'', ''Jobo'', ''Manila'' and ''Manililla'', and the [[Tommy Atkins (mango)|Tommy Atkins]].

The livestock sector includes beef and dairy cattle and poultry,<ref name=Enciclopedia /> as well as sheep, pigs and horses.

===Tourism===

The beaches of the Pacific coast are one of the main draws for tourists in the municipality.<ref name=Enciclopedia /> 

Ecological reserves constitute another point of interest. They include the El Triunfo Biosphere Reserve, with its cloud forest and tropical rainforest habitats, and the wetlands, coastal lagoons and mangroves of La Encrucijada Biosphere Reserve, which is also a Ramsar wetland site.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

According to information from the Ministry of Tourism, in the year 2000 the town of Mapastepec had five hotels with 53 rooms.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

===Services===

Most of the services available in the municipality are found in the town of Mapastepec. These include public transportation, gas and service stations, hotels and restaurants, garages and repair shops, a hospital, clinics, and medical professionals.<ref name=Enciclopedia />

The town Mapastepec has a Rural Hospital with a number of medical units run by the [[Mexican Social Security Institute]] (''Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social, IMSS'')<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.imss.gob.mx/directorio// |title=Directorio de instalaciones del IMSS |last=Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social (IMSS) |access-date=September 25, 2018}}</ref> under its IMSS Prospera program<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.imss.gob.mx/imss-prospera// |title=IMSS-Prospera |last=Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social (IMSS) |access-date=September 25, 2018}}</ref> Health services provided include an emergency department, surgical facilities, a labor ward, a pediatrics department, a general hospital and laboratory services.<ref>{{cite news |last=Editorial staff |date=February 19, 2018 |title=Invierte IMSS en nuevos aires acondicionados para hospitales de Chiapas |trans-title=IMSS Invests in New Air-Conditioning for Chiapas Hospitals |url=http://www.elsoldechiapas.com |language=es |work=El Sol de Chiapas |location=Tapachula, Chiapas, México |access-date=September 25, 2018}}</ref>

===Railway===

There were two unsuccessful attempts to build a railway along the coast of Chiapas in the 19th century before an Anglo-American company, The Mexican Pacific Railroad Limited, built the Pan American Railway (''Ferrocarril Panamericano'') in the early 20th century. 

Work began in 1902 with initial repairs to a short existing rail segment from the port town of [[Puerto Arista]] on the northern coast of Chiapas in 1902. In 1903, construction began in San Jerónimo Doctor (now [[Ixtepec, Oaxaca|Ixtepec]]) in the state of Oaxaca, building south towards the Guatemalan border. Work was completed as far as [[Tonalá, Chiapas| Tonalá]] in Chiapas by December of that year. The railway reached the municipalities of Pijijiapan and Mapastepec in 1906, arriving at [[Suchiate]] on the border with [[Guatemala]] on July 1, 1908. The national rail operator [[Ferrocarriles Nacionales de México]] took over the line in 1914. It was privatized in 1999 under a 30-year concession to [[Genesee & Wyoming]] subsidiary [[Ferrocarriles Chiapas-Mayab]].<ref name="Mireles Gavito">{{cite news |last=Mireles Gavito |first=Sofia |title=Construccion del Ferrocarril Panamericano |trans-title=Construction of the Pan American Railway |url=http://www.lavozdelnorte.com.mx/2012/09/30/construccion-del-ferrocarril-panamericano/ |language=es |work=La voz del norte |location=Sinaloa, Mexico |access-date=September 24, 2018}}</ref>

Ferrocarriles Chiapas-Mayab gave up its concession in 2007 after [[Hurricane Stan]] badly damaged the line in October 2005, rendering it inoperable. State-owned company [[Tren Interoceánico|Ferrocarril Transístmico]] took over the concession in 2008, and the line has since been known as the [[Isthmus of Tehuantepec]] Railway (''Ferrocarril del Istmo de Tehuantepec''). Work was done to repair a number of bridges but the line remained out of commission. In 2015, a feasibility study was conducted into repairing and rehabilitating the line.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ballinas |first=Miguel |date=November 23, 2015 |title=Iniciará reactivación del Tren Chiapas-Mayab |trans-title=Reactivation of Chiapas-Mayab Railway to Begin |url=http://www.cuartopoder.mx/iniciarareactivaciondeltrenchiapasmayab-139812.html |language=es |work=Cuarto Poder |location=Tuxtla Gutiérrez, Chiapas, México |access-date=September 24, 2018}}</ref> In 2018 it remains out of service.<ref>{{cite news |date=August 28, 2018 |title=Plan Frontera Sur se ahogó en el Suchiate; el tren ya no opera en la costa |trans-title=[[ Guatemala–Mexico border# Programa Frontera Sur|Southern Border Plan]] Bogged Down in Suciate; Trains No Longer Run on the Coast |url=https://www.elsoldemexico.com.mx/republica/politica/plan-frontera-sur-se-ahogo-en-el-suchiate-el-tren-ya-no-opera-en-la-costa-1949324.html |language=es |work=El Sol de México |location=Mexico City, Mexico |access-date= September 24, 2018}}</ref>
