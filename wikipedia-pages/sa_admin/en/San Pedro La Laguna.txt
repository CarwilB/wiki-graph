'''San Pedro La Laguna''' ({{IPA|es|sam ˈpeðɾo la laˈɣuna}}) is a Guatemalan town on the southwest shore of [[Lake Atitlán]]. For centuries, San Pedro La Laguna has been inhabited by the [[Tz'utujil people]], and in recent years it has also become a [[tourist destination]] for its Spanish [[language school]]s, nightlife, and proximity to the lake and volcanoes, particularly [[Volcán San Pedro]], at whose base San Pedro La Laguna is located.

Local crops include corn, beans, coffee, and avocado. Some women make belts, shawls, and skirts with a [[back strap loom]]. San Pedro graduates large numbers of teachers who work the Atitlán area.

== Geography ==
San Pedro La Laguna is located on the southwestern shore of [[Lago de Atitlán]]. The town is located beneath [[Volcan San Pedro]]. This volcano towers over the lake at 9,905 feet and is an attraction for climbers and hikers. The town of San Pedro La Laguna sits on a plateau and has roads running down either side of a steep ridge towards docks on the lakeshore. The dock on the west side of the plateau is called ''muelle municipal'' and has boats arriving from and departing to the town of [[Panajachel]]. The east side has a dock named ''muelle santiago'' which has boats arriving from and departing to [[Santiago Atitlan]]. The east side is also home to ''la playa'' (the beach) where people enjoy snorkeling, canoeing, and kayaking.<ref>Greenspan, Eliot. Frommer's Guatemala. 3rd ed. Hoboken: Wiley Publishing, 2011. Print. p.153.</ref>

San Pedro La Laguna can be reached by regular boats across Lake Atitlán from [[Panajachel]] (45 min) and [[Santiago Atitlán]] (30 min), or by direct bus from [[Guatemala City]] (4 hrs), [[Quetzaltenango|Xela]] (3 hrs), and [[Mazatenango, Suchitepéquez|Mazatenango]]. The town is located one hour off the [[Inter-American Highway|Interamericana Highway]] (CA-1) at km 148. Buses for San Pedro depart hourly from zone 8, near the Trébol interchange, alongside Calzada Roosvelt in Guatemala City or, in Xela, from the main bus terminal in zone 3.

== Demography ==

San Pedro La Laguna is a small town with a population of approximately 13,000 people.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hubbard|first=Kristen|title=San Pedro La Laguna -- Lake Atitlan, Guatemala|url=http://gocentralamerica.about.com/od/panajachellakeatitlan/a/SanPedroLaguna.htm|work=About.com|accessdate=26 March 2012|archive-date=20 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111020020145/http://gocentralamerica.about.com/od/panajachellakeatitlan/a/SanPedroLaguna.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> The inhabitants of San Pedro are primarily Tz'utujil Maya, with a small, but growing, expatriate community. The population is over 90 percent indigenous.<ref>{{cite web|last=McGraw|first=John J.|title=Basic Facts|url=http://www.tzununya.com/|work=Tz'unun Ya'.com|publisher=John J. McGraw|accessdate=26 March 2012}}</ref>  The expatriate community that is forming within San Pedro La Laguna is composed of Americans and Europeans.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hubbard|first=Kristen|title=San Pedro La Laguna -- Lake Atitlan, Guatemala|url=http://gocentralamerica.about.com/od/panajachellakeatitlan/a/SanPedroLaguna.htm|work=About.com|accessdate=26 March 2012|archive-date=20 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111020020145/http://gocentralamerica.about.com/od/panajachellakeatitlan/a/SanPedroLaguna.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Religion ===

[[Catholic Church|Catholicism]] is the religion that is the most dominant in San Pedro La Laguna. It has been the most dominant religion since the 16th century, when [[Spanish Empire|Spain]] colonized [[Central America]].<ref>{{cite book|last=Morrison|first=Marion|title=Guatemala|url=https://archive.org/details/guatemala0000morr|url-access=registration|year=2005|publisher=Children's Press|location=New York|isbn=0-516-23674-1|pages=[https://archive.org/details/guatemala0000morr/page/89 89]}}</ref>

Although the Catholic religion has been dominant for centuries, about half of the population still practices traditional [[Maya religion|Maya rituals]] either separately, [[Religious syncretism|syncretically]], or alongside another religion.<ref>{{cite book|last=Morrison|first=Marion|title=Guatemala|url=https://archive.org/details/guatemala0000morr|url-access=registration|year=2005|publisher=Children's Press|location=New York|isbn=0-516-23674-1}}</ref> [[Xocomil (weather)|Xocomil]], a wind coming from the south that "carries away sin", usually visits the [[Lake Atitlán]] area around midday. If the wind is coming from the north, the Maya claim, the lake's spirit is getting rid of a person who has drowned after claiming his or her soul.<ref>{{cite book|last=Stewart|first=Lain|title=The rough Guide to Guatemala|year=2009|publisher=Rough Guides|location=New York|isbn=978-1-84836-017-4|pages=150|edition=4th}}</ref>{{better source needed|date=April 2025}}

In 2010, due to an influx of [[Jews|Jewish]] tourists and immigrants from Israel and North America, a [[synagogue]] and [[kosher]] restaurant were opened by an Israeli organization known as [[Chabad]] of San Pedro, Guatemala.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://chabadpedro.com/en/about-us/|title = About Us|website=[[Chabad House]] Pedro|url-status=dead|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201030155104/https://chabadpedro.com/en/about-us/|archive-date=October 30, 2020}}</ref> Today, many Israeli businesses in the town have [[Hebrew language]] signs and advertising.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://religionunplugged.com/news/2020/2/25/israels-guatemalan-outpost|title = Inside Israel's Guatemalan Outpost| date=9 March 2020 }}</ref>

== Government ==

San Pedro La Laguna is in the [[Sololá Department|Sololá department]] of [[Guatemala]]. San Pedro La Laguna is separated into four cantons: Pacucha, Chuacante, Chuasanahi, and Tzanjay.<ref>{{cite web|last=McGraw|first=John J.|title=Tz'unun Ya'.com|url=http://www.tzununya.com/|publisher=John J. McGraw|accessdate=23 March 2012}}</ref>

San Pedro La Laguna passed a town ordinance in October of 2016 to ban the use of plastic, which includes straws and plastic bags. The citizens have opted to utilize readily made textiles, baskets, etc. for daily use during their shopping trips.<ref>{{Citation|title=Guatemala: un pueblo sin plástico {{!}} Reporteros en el mundo| date=27 January 2018 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nC9lxoRzxrE|language=en|access-date=2019-10-30}}</ref> Residents have complained of contamination in the lake, caused by pollution, increased population, tourism, and [[cyanotoxin]], a bacterium which has substantially increased since 2009.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://truthout.org/articles/contamination-of-sacred-lake-underscores-environmental-racism-in-guatemala/|title=Contamination of Sacred Lake Underscores Environmental Racism in Guatemala|last=Abbott|first=Jeff|website=Truthout|date=28 May 2016 |language=en-US|access-date=2019-10-30}}</ref>

== Customs ==
Museo Tz'unun 'Ya is a museum documenting local history and customs, including the formation of the lake and colorized photos of pre-Civil War San Pedro La Laguna.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lonelyplanet.com/guatemala/san-pedro-la-laguna/attractions/museo-tzunun-ya/a/poi-sig/484052/358420|title=Museo Tz'unun 'Ya {{!}} San Pedro La Laguna, Guatemala Attractions|website=www.lonelyplanet.com|language=en|access-date=2019-10-30}}</ref> Indigenous medical traditions are still commonly practiced by healers, known as ''[[curandero]]s''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/despite-centuries-mayan-healers-are-still-curing-caring-ancient-sacred-n824936|title='Maya healers' captures centuries-old cures in Guatemala's sacred spaces|website=NBC News|date=11 December 2017 |language=en|access-date=2019-10-30}}</ref>

== History ==

===Civil War===
The [[Guatemalan Civil War]] spanned the years from 1960 to 1996. During this time San Pedro La Laguna remained relatively unaffected and unharmed by the surrounding violence. However, from the years of 1980 to 1982 there was a series of kidnappings and murders in the community. It became known that the local military commissioners were behind these actions. It was alleged that villagers were blacklisted for many different activities including promoting adult education in the local [[Mayan languages|Mayan language]] ([[Tzʼutujil language|Tzʼutujil]]), and celebrating the anniversary of the victory of the [[Sandinista National Liberation Front]] in [[Nicaragua]]. Local commissioners in collaboration with the [[Guatemalan Army]] painted [[Organization of People in Arms]] and other [[guerrilla warfare|guerrilla]] signs on walls, and disseminated leaflets around the village at night to support allegations and feign a guerrilla presence in the village. This continued for nearly two years until August 1982, when higher military officials intervened.<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://archive.org/details/harvestofviolenc0000unse|title=Harvest of violence the Maya indians and the Guatemalan crisis|last=Carmack|first=Robert M|date=1992|publisher=University of Oklahoma Press|isbn=9780806121321|location=Norman, Oklahoma|pages=119–127|oclc=902437712|url-access=registration}}</ref>
