'''Tapanahoni''' ({{langx|djk|Tapamawoni}} or {{lang|djk|Tapanawoni}}),<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://suriname-languages.sil.org/Aukan/English/AukanEngDictIndex.html|title=Aukan–English Dictionary|publisher=[[SIL Global]]}}</ref> is a [[resorts of Suriname|resort]] in [[Suriname]], located in the [[Sipaliwini District]].  Its population at the 2012 census was 13,808.<ref>[http://www.statoids.com/ysr.html Statoids.com]</ref> Tapanahoni is a part of Sipaliwini which has no capital, but is directly governed from [[Paramaribo]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.surinameview.com/districten/|title=Districten|access-date=21 May 2020|language=nl|website=Suriname View}}</ref> Tapanahony is an enormous resort which encompasses a quarter of the country of Suriname.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.geo-ref.net/ph/sur.htm|title=Country map - Administrative structure - Population density of Suriname|website=Geo Ref|access-date=21 May 2020}}</ref> The most important town is [[Diitabiki]] (old name: Drietabbetje) which is the residence of the [[granman]] of the [[Ndyuka people]] since 1950, and the location of the [[oracle]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/kemp009gesc02_01/kemp009gesc02_01_0015.php|title=Een geschiedenis van de Surinaamse literatuur. Deel 2|website=Digital Library for Dutch Literature|access-date=21 May 2020|date=2002|language=nl}}</ref>

The disputed area of south-east Suriname between the [[Malani (river)|Malani]] (the eastern tributary river) and the [[Litani River (South America)|Litani]] rivers belongs to the Tapanahoni resort.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.starnieuws.com/index.php/welcome/index/nieuwsitem/51615|title=Goudwinning met toestemming Suriname en Frankrijk |website=Star Nieuws|access-date=21 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>

==History==
The Ndyuka people are of [[Africa]]n descent, and were shipped as slaves to Suriname in the 17-18th century to work on [[Netherlands|Dutch]]-owned colonial plantations. The escaped slaves moved into the [[rainforest]], and banded together.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mpm.edu/research-collections/anthropology/online-collections-research/ndyuka|title=Suriname and the Maroons|website=Milwaukee Public Museum|access-date=23 May 2020}}</ref> There were frequent clashes between the colonists and the Ndyuka, however in 1760, a peace treaty was signed granting the Ndyuka autonomy.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.culturalsurvival.org/publications/cultural-survival-quarterly/ndyuka-treaty-1760-conversation-granman-gazon|title=The Ndyuka Treaty Of 1760: A Conversation with Granman Gazon|website=Cultural Survival|access-date=23 May 2020}}</ref> From 1761, the Ndyuka gradually moved southwards in order to protect themselves from the [[Surinam (Dutch colony)|colonists]], and started to build camps on the [[Tapanahoni River]] dispelling the indigenous [[Tiriyó people|Tiriyó]]. Slaves who had recently fled from Armina{{efn|Post Armina was a guard post on the [[Marowijne River]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.suriname.nu/175alg/marowijne01.html|title=Disktrikt Marowijne 2|access-date=22 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>}} and [[Sarakreek|Boven Commewijne]] were stationed at [[Poeloegoedoe]] near the confluence of the Tapanahoni and [[Lawa River (South America)|Lawa River]] to guard against attacks by the [[Aluku]].<ref name="encycl">{{Cite web|title=Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch West-Indië - Page 154 - Boschnegers|website=[[Digital Library for Dutch Literature]]|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/benj004ency01_01/benj004ency01_01.pdf|date=1916|access-date=22 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref> In December 1791, Philip Stoelman founded a [[Outpost (military)|military outpost]] on [[Stoelmanseiland]], thus establishing a militarised border between the Ndyuka held territory and the Colony of Suriname.<ref>{{cite magazine|author=Silvia de Groot|title=Rebellie der Zwarte Jagers. De nasleep van de Bonni-oorlogen 1788-1809|work=De Gids|language=nl|date=1970}}</ref> In 1857, the granman of the Ndyuka was given a yearly allowance of ƒ1,000 (2018: €10,233<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.iisg.nl/hpw/calculate-nl.php|website=Internationaal Instituut voor Sociale Geschiedenis|title=De waarde van de gulden / euro|access-date=23 May 2020|language=nl}}</ref>) by the Government of Suriname.<ref name="encycl"/> 

The area was an unknown area for the white settlers, and was not explored until the beginning of the 20th century.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.suriname.nu/175alg/sipaliwini02.html|title=Distrikt Sipaliwini|access-date=22 May 2020|language=nl|website=Suriname.nu}}</ref> Still the explorers first had to request an audience with the granman. [[Gerard Versteeg]], leader of a 1904 expedition, expressed his frustration in his diary about having to wait four days before being granted permission to continue his journey.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_els001190501_01/_els001190501_01_0084.php|title=Elsevier's Geïllustreerd Maandschrift. Jaargang 15|date=1905|access-date=22 May 2020|language=nl|website=Digital Library for Dutch Literature}}</ref>

In 1983, the [[Sipaliwini District]] was created.<ref name="suriname_nu">{{cite web|url=https://www.suriname.nu/175alg/sipaliwini01.html|title=Distrikt Sipaliwini|access-date=23 May 2020|language=nl|website=Suriname.nu}}</ref> On 11 September 2019,<ref name="gov_sr">{{cite web|url=http://regionaldevelopment.gov.sr/actueel/2019/paamaka-en-ndyuka-leggen-grens-vast/|title=Paamaka en Ndyuka leggen grens vast|website=Regional Development.gov.sr|access-date=23 May 2020|language=nl|archive-date=28 January 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220128031825/http://regionaldevelopment.gov.sr/actueel/2019/paamaka-en-ndyuka-leggen-grens-vast/|url-status=dead}}</ref> a new resort was created out of Tapanahony, and is called [[Pamacca]]. The Pamacca resort is the northern part of Tapanahony, and mainly inhabited by the [[Paramaccan people]].<ref name="suriname_nu"/><ref name="gov_sr"/>

==Villages==
The resort of Tapanahony consists of about 49 Ndyuka villages and 14 [[indigenous people|indigenous]] villages.<ref name="planning"/>
