'''Chinchero District''' is one of seven [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of the [[Urubamba Province]] in [[Peru]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} [[Instituto Nacional de Estadística e Informática]]. ''[http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ Banco de Información Distrital] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080423164200/http://desa.inei.gob.pe/mapas/bid/ |date=April 23, 2008 }}''. Retrieved April 11, 2008.</ref> The town of Chinchero is the capital of the district. It is the location for the proposed [[Chinchero International Airport]], which would serve travelers to the [[Cusco Region]].<ref>{{cite web|title=International Airport of Chinchero - Cusco (AICC) :: Proinversión|url=http://www.proyectosapp.pe/modulos/JER/PlantillaProyecto.aspx?ARE=1&PFL=2&JER=5897|website=www.proyectosapp.pe|accessdate=19 April 2017}}</ref>

== Ethnic groups ==
The people that live in the district are mainly [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Quechua language|Quechua]] is the language which the majority of the population (81.49%) learnt to speak in childhood, 17.95% of the residents started speaking using the [[Spanish language|Spanish]] language ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe]  {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=January 27, 2013 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007, Frequencias: Preguntas de Población: Idioma o lengua con el que aprendió hablar (in Spanish)</ref>

==Climate==

Chinchero has a dry-winter [[Oceanic climate|subpolar oceanic climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification]]: '''Cwc'''), that borders very closely on both a [[tundra climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification]]: '''ET'''), and a [[Semi-arid climate|cold semi-arid climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification]]: '''BSk''').

==Notable residents==
The anthropologists Ed and Chris Franquemont lived among the Chinchero people during the 1970s, studying traditional textile production techniques. Their daughter [[Abby Franquemont]], having spent her childhood within a spinning culture, later became a revivalist of [[hand spinning]] with the [[spindle (textiles)|spindle]].<ref name="franquemont 2009" >Abby Franquemont, ''Respect the Spindle, spin infinite yarns with one amazing tool'', Interweave (2009) {{ISBN|9781596681552}}</ref>
