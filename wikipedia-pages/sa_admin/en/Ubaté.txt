'''Ubaté''' (originally '''Villa San Diego de Ubaté''') is a town and municipality in the [[Ubaté Province]], part of [[Cundinamarca Department]], [[Colombia]]. Ubaté is the capital of the province with the same name and situated in the [[Ubaté–Chiquinquirá Valley]] of the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]]. The urban centre is located at an altitude of {{convert|2556|m|ft}} and {{convert|97|km|mi}} from the capital [[Bogotá]]. The province borders the department of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]] in the north, the [[Rionegro Province]] in the west, the [[Central Savanna Province]] in the south and in the east and southeast the [[Almeidas Province]].<ref name=websiteUbate>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.ubate-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Ubaté] {{Webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20140310085028/http://www.ubate-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml |date=2014-03-10 }}</ref>

== Etymology ==
The name Ubaté comes from the native name "Ebate" meaning "Bloodied land" or "Sower of the mouth".<ref name=websiteUbate/>

== History ==
The area of Ubaté before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]] was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]], organised in a loose confederation of [[Muisca rulers|rulers]] called the [[Muisca Confederation]]. Initially loyal to the ''[[zaque]]'' of [[Tunja|Hunza]], Ubaté changed rule around 1490 when it was submitted by ''[[zipa]]'' [[Saguamanchica]].<ref>Restrepo Arcila, 2002, p.179</ref>

Modern Ubaté was founded on April 12, 1592, by Bernardo de Albornoz.<ref name=websiteUbate/>

It is known as the "Milk Capital of Colombia", for a [[Gothic architecture|Gothic style]] cathedral and the [[San Luis Convent]].

== Economy ==
The main activities of Ubaté are [[agriculture]] and [[livestock]] farming.<ref name=websiteUbate/>
