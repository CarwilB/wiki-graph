'''Tekantó Municipality''' (In the [[Yucatec Maya]] Language: “Place where there is yellow flint”)<ref name= "Maya Missions" /> is a small (47.25&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>)<ref name="municipios"/> [[Municipalities of Yucatán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Yucatán]]. The municipality was formed in 1900<ref name="municipios"/> and its municipal seat is the homonymous locality of Tekantó, at the end of highway 80, 54&nbsp;km east of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Merida]]. 
[[Image:Tekanto Shop.jpg|thumb|left|A typical shop in Tekantó, these buildings date back to the 1920s]]

==History==
* '''Before the [[Spanish conquest of Yucatán]]''' [[commons:file:Roys Provinces.png|the region of Tekantó]] was part of the chieftainship of Ah Kin Chel (1441–1543).
* '''1546:'''  The Spanish conquest of Yucatán that started in 1526 is considered complete.
* '''1567:''' Franciscan Mission founded in Tekantó.<ref name= "Maya Missions" />
* '''1581:''' Cristobal Sanchez inherits both Tekantó and Tepakan under the [[encomienda]] system from his father, the famous conquistador Don Diego Sanchez.<ref>Loveland Roys, Ralph  ''The political geography of the Yucatan Maya'' Page 86. Carnegie Institution of Washington (1957). ASIN B0012KNZKY</ref>
* '''1688:''' Construction of the convent and mission of San Augustin Tekantó is completed.<ref name= "Maya Missions" />
* '''1700:''' Antonia Pacheco and Juan N. Calderón are responsible for Tekantó under the [[encomienda]] system.<ref>''Los Municipios de Yucatán'' p.396. Secretaría de Gobernación, 1988 {{in lang|es}}</ref>
* '''1720:''' The legality of the [[Encomienda]] system is ended and is succeeded by the [[Hacienda system]] in which laborers were directly employed by the hacienda owners (Sanlatah is a Hacienda in Tekantó municipality).<ref>[[Alfred J. Andrea]], James H. Overfield. ''The Human Record: Since 1500'' p.75. Houghton Mifflin, 1998</ref>
* '''1821:''' Yucatán declares itself independent of Spain.<ref>John Macgregor ''Commercial statistics: A digest of the productive resources, commercial'' p.1217. Whittaker and co., 1850</ref>
* '''1825:''' During this time Tekantó was in the partido of La Costa, which had [[Izamal]] as its capital. (At this time [[Yucatán (state)|Yucatán]] was divided up onto 15 partidos or subdelegaciones, and Yucatán then included what is now [[Campeche]]).<ref>Thompson, Philip C. ''Tekantó, a Maya Town in Colonial Yucatan'', page 14. Middle American Research Institute, 2000. {{ISBN|0-939238-97-7}}</ref>
* '''1848:''' On May 28 during the [[Caste War of Yucatán]] after a 6-day siege Colonel Bello led his force of 1,000 men out of [[Izamal]] taking a back trail to Tekantó. From Tekantó he watched Izamal burn.<ref>Reed, Nelson ''The caste war of Yucatán'', page 95. Stanford Univ Press (June 1964). {{ISBN|0-8047-0164-4}}</ref>
* '''1900:''' Tekantó Municipality is created and Tekantó becomes its own municipality seat.<ref name="municipios"/>

==Coat of arms==
In 1990 the Mexican federal government asked all the municipalities to have their own coat of arms.
The City council then presided over by C. Manuel González Towers, decided to hold a contest to produce the armorial design. The design was adapted from submissions by several people. Some notable features of the shield are as follows. A sun with 13 rays that indicate the Mayan supraworld. An illustration of the Mayan chief Ah Kin Chel on a green background. Gold background with [[henequen]] that has 9 blades, which means the Mayan infraworld, the color gold has economic importance. At either side two branches interlaced by a plant called Kantó and that some say gave rise to the name of the town (this would make the arms "[[Canting arms|canting]]"). At the bottom of the shield in front of the branches is a ribbon of silver with the words Siyan Kán Tekantó, this means the illustrious or famous Tekantó.<ref name="Shields">{{Cite web | title = Galería de Escudos Mesoamericanos | url = http://www.heraldicamesoamericana.com.mx/galeria.htm#tekanto | access-date = 2009-10-22 | url-status = dead | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20110722223739/http://www.heraldicamesoamericana.com.mx/galeria.htm#tekanto | archive-date = 2011-07-22 }}</ref>

==Population==
The population of Tekantó is approximately 4,000.<ref name="municipios" /> In the year 2000 the census record showed that 1,655 people spoke the indigenous language, [[Yucatec Maya]].<ref name="INEGI year 2000 General Census of Population and Housing">{{Cite web| title = De acuerdo al XII Censo General de Población y Vivienda 2000 efectuado por el INEGI | url = http://www.inegi.org.mx/inegi/default.aspx?s=est&c=11448&e=&i= | access-date = 2009-10-22 }}</ref> This is consistent with the state average which was 30% in 2000.

==Communities==
The municipality is made up from four principle communities, the municipal seat Tekantó along with its neighboring communities Tixkochoh {{Coord|  21.0138|-89.0806|type:city|name=Tixkochoh}}, San Francisco Dzon {{Coord| 20.9835|-89.1433|type:city|name=San Francisco Dzon}} and Sanlatah {{Coord|  21.0426|-89.1466|type:city|name=Sanlatah}}). Their populations are as follows: Tekantó 3224, Tixkochoh 455, San Francisco Dzon 127, Sanlatah 83; the census also included 53 people living outside of these communities,<ref name="municipios"/> either in isolated rural homes or small communities like the hamlet of San Diego Rodriguez {{Coord|  20.9923|-89.1143|type:city|name=San Diego Rodriguez}}. In 2005 there were 942 houses in the municipality.<ref name="Enciclopedia de Municipios de México">{{Cite web | title = Enciclopedia de Municipios de México | url = http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/yucatan/ | access-date = 2009-10-22 | url-status = dead | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20061205022959/http://e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/yucatan/ | archive-date = 2006-12-05 }}</ref>

==Geography==
[[Image:Tekanto Flora.jpg|thumb|left|Road leading into the Tekantó locality]]

===Borders===
The municipalities that surround Tekantó Municipality are to the north: Suma and Teya, to the south: [[Izamal]], to the east: Tepakán and to the west: Bokobá.

===Nearby cities===
The distances from the municipal seat to nearby cities are as follows:
* [[Izamal]], [[Yucatán (state)|Yucatán]] {{·}}{{convert|13|km}} southeast {{·}} 14,954 people.
* [[Motul de Carrillo Puerto|Motul]], Yucatán {{·}} {{convert|21|km}} west-northwest {{·}} 21,109 people.
* [[Merida, Yucatán|Merida]], Yucatán {{·}} {{convert|54|km}} west {{·}} 717,175 people.

Tekantó's rail station is on the (now disused) rail line that runs between [[Mérida, Yucatán|Mérida]] and [[Valladolid, Yucatán|Valladolid]] via [[Izamal]].

===Climate===
Warm, with rains during the early summer followed by droughts during the late summer. The annual average temperature is of 26.5&nbsp;°C and the annual average rainfall is 50 millimeters. The winds are predominantly from the northeast. Relative humidity Annual Average, March 66% - December 89%. The Rainy Season is May through July.<ref name="municipios"/>

===Flora and fauna===
Small portions of non-evergreen low forest with secondary vegetation in the ends northeast and the northwest. Including: poppy, bojom, ceiba, chehem, chichibé, pochote and flamboyán.
The common animal species that are found in the area include: rabbit, raccoon, deer, armadillo, various snakes, iguanas and turtles; birds: dove, tzutzuy, quail and tórtola.<ref name="municipios"/>

==Places of interest and tourism==

===Architectural===
San Augustin Tekantó is an impressive building and one of the largest churches in the Yucatán outside of [[Mérida, Yucatán|Merida]]; it was an important early [[Franciscan]] Convent in this region.

[[File:Santo Christo de Citilcún.jpg|right|thumb|Santo Christo de Citilcún]] [[File:San Augustin Tekanto from south.JPG|left|thumb|San Augustin Tekantó, as seen from the south]]

The original mission dates from 1567. According to an inscription on the facade, the church was completed in 1688, over a hundred years after the mission's founding.<ref name= "Maya Missions" />
Near to Tekantó are the hamlets of the ex-properties: Sanlatah and San Francisco Dzon.

===Popular celebrations===
From 26 to 28 August an annual fiesta is held in honor of the patron saint of Tekantó, San Augustin.

Between 15 and 26 November, an annual fair is held to celebrate Santo Christo de Citilcún. On November 15 each year a procession brings Santo Christo de Citilcún to Tekantó, after the procession there are firework displays and food for sale in the town square. Towards the end of the month there are various festivities including [[Folk dance|folk dancing]], hosted at the town hall; an impromptu [[bullfighting]] ring is set up to host bull fights and other events.

===Surrounding area===
The ruins of [[Ake]] are 20&nbsp;km to the south west of the locality of Tekanto, and [[Izamal]] with its world-famous monastery, Victorian era carriages, and [[mesoamerican pyramids]] is only 13&nbsp;km to the south east.

==Economy==
[[File:Tekanto traditional house.JPG|left|thumb|Traditional House in Tekantó]] [[File:Tekanto Railway Station.JPG|right|thumb|Railway Station in Tekantó]]
Tekantó is a municipality that is located in the North central zone of Yucatán that is part of the denominated [[henequen]] zone, where the main agricultural crop is agave. Along with the surrounding municipalities the major productive activity for many years, until the end of the 20th Century, was the [[henequen]] industry.

With the decline of the [[henequen]] industry, a process of economic diversification occurred in Tekantó. Today in this area maize is main crop, along with kidney beans, various vegetables, watermelon and some varieties of chili peppers. In addition to these crops, pigs and poultry are farmed. Beekeeping is also common in the area.

'''Spelling of Mayan Names:'''

The spelling of Mayan names (family names, place names and others) will often vary from document to document,<ref>Thompson, Philip C. ''Tekanto, a Maya Town in Colonial Yucatan'', page 9. Middle American Research Institute, 2000. {{ISBN|0-939238-97-7}}</ref> for this page the spellings used on the municipality web site have been adopted as the standard. Another recommended standard for place names is the [[Secretaría de Comunicaciones y Transportes|SCT]] (Secretaria de Comunicaciones y Transportes) spelling, as this would then match any road signs. As there are more references to the municipality web site, the [[Secretaría de Comunicaciones y Transportes|SCT]] spellings are not used throughout the page, but included below for reference.

San Francisco Dzon is also known as  San Francisco Tzon ([[INEGI]]) and San Francisco Izun ([[Secretaría de Comunicaciones y Transportes|SCT]])<ref name="SCT Map of Yucatan">{{Cite web | title = SCT Map of Yucatan | url = http://dgp.sct.gob.mx/fileadmin/Atlas/yucatan.pdf | format = pdf | access-date = 2009-10-23 | url-status = dead | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090126152518/http://dgp.sct.gob.mx/fileadmin/Atlas/yucatan.pdf | archive-date = 2009-01-26 }}</ref>
