'''San Salvador Huixcolotla''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Puebla|municipality]] in the [[List of states of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Puebla]] that may be best known as the birthplace of [[papel picado]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/puebla/|title=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México|publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]]|access-date=4 January 2010}}</ref><ref name="copalPicado">{{Cite news|url=https://www.mexican-folk-art-guide.com/papel-picado.html#.W-x4RvZFxdh|title=Papel Picado|work=Copal, Mexican Folk Art at its best Online.|access-date=14 November 2018|archive-date=18 April 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230418113444/https://www.mexican-folk-art-guide.com/papel-picado.html#.W-x4RvZFxdh|url-status=dead}}</ref> ''San Salvador'' is of [[Spanish language|Spanish]] origin and translates to "Holy Savior" and ''Huixcolotla'' is [[Nahuatl]] for "place of the curved spines".<ref name="gobHuixcolotla">{{cite web |title=Gobierno Municipal de San Salvador Huixcolotla |url=https://www.huixcolotla.gob.mx/#municipio |website=huixcolotla.gob.mx |access-date=31 May 2021}}</ref>

==History==
The original inhabitants were [[Popoloca languages|Popoloca]] speakers, under the [[Aztec Empire|Aztec Triple Alliance]].<ref name="gobHuixcolotla" /> Friar Juan de Rivas founded a small congregation in 1539 as part of the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|Spanish colonization]] and in 1750, construction began on ''Iglesia del Divino Salvador'' (Church of the Divine Savior).<ref name="gobHuixcolotla" /> In 1779 it became a town, and on 15 April 1930, it was declared a municipality under the governorship of Leonides Andrew Almazán.<ref name="gobHuixcolotla" /><ref name="enciclopediaMunicipos">{{cite web |title=San Salvador Huixcolotla |url=http://inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM21puebla/municipios/21144a.html |website=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México (Mexican Encyclopedia of Municipalities and Delegations) |access-date=31 May 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

==Geography==
The total area of San Salvador Huixcolotla is {{convert|23.909|km2|sqmi}}, of which 79% is devoted to agriculture and the remaining is 21% developed.<ref name="planMunicipal" /><ref name="prontuarioSanSalvadorHuixcolotla" />{{rp|2}} The four ''barrios'' are El Calvario, San Antonio, San Martín, and La Candelaria, and the three ''colonias'' are San Isidro, Dolores, and Benito Juárez.<ref name="planMunicipal" /> It is surrounded to the north by the municipalities of [[Los Reyes de Juárez]] and [[Acatzingo]]; to the east by Acatzingo and [[Tecamachalco, Puebla|Tecamachalco]]; to the south by Tecamachalco, [[Tochtepec]], and [[Cuapiaxtla de Madero]]; and to the west by Cuapiaxtla de Madero and Los Reyes de Juárez.<ref name="prontuarioSanSalvadorHuixcolotla">{{cite book |title=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos (Mexican Municipal Geographic Information Handbook), San Salvador Huixcolotla, Puebla |date=2009 |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/21/21144.pdf |access-date=31 May 2021 |language=es}}</ref>

===Climate===

==Demographics==
78.12% of the population lives in poverty.<ref name="planMunicipal" />{{rp|15}} As of 2015, electricity and sanitary systems are universal, but only 50.10% of households have piped water inside their homes.<ref name="planMunicipal" />{{rp|17}} 90.25% of the population between age 6 and 14 can read and write.<ref name="planMunicipal" />{{rp|18}}

==Papel picado==
{{Main|Papel picado}}
Papel Picado ("perforated paper," "pecked paper") is a decorative Mexican folk art made by cutting elaborate designs into sheets of tissue paper that were popularized in San Salvador Huixcolotla. It is thought to have originated from the pre-Hispanic practice of making religious offerings with [[Amate|amate bark paper]].<ref name="HernándezSol" /> Among the first makers were Juan Hernandez, Cristóbal Flores, Santiago Vivanco R., and Lauro Pérez Macías.<ref name="enciclopediaMunicipos" /> By the late 1920s, it had spread outside Puebla to [[Tlaxcala]] and is now used around the world in observations of ''Día de Muertos'' (Day of the Dead).<ref name="arteDeJugar">{{cite book |last1=Álvarez Cordero |first1=María del Carmen |title=El arte de jugar y crear Tijereteando |date=2008 |publisher=LXIII Legislatura de la H. Cámara de Diputados |page=82 |url=http://biblioteca.diputados.gob.mx/janium/bv/ce/lxiii/art_jug_cre_lxiii.pdf |access-date=31 May 2021}}</ref><ref name="copalPicado" /> In addition to ''Día de Muertos'', known locally as ''Todos Santos'', papel picado is also commonly made to celebrate [[Holy Week in Mexico|Semana Santa]] (the Holy Week of Easter), [[Mexican War of Independence|Mexican independence]], and Christmas.<ref name="harvey" /><ref name="HernándezSol">{{cite news |last1=Hernández |first1=Maricarmen |title=Huixcolotla, cuna del papel picado ("Huixcolotla, cradle of papel picado") |url=https://www.elsoldepuebla.com.mx/local/huixcolotla-cuna-del-papel-picado-puebla-cultura-dia-de-muertos-manualidades-oficios-creatividad-san-salvador-huixcolotla-papel-china-artesanias-manualidades-patino-papel-picado-4391506.html |access-date=1 June 2021 |work=El Sol de Puebla |publisher=Organización Editorial Mexicana |date=31 October 2019 |language=es}}</ref>

In 1998 the governor of Puebla declared the town, in which 35% of the residents participate in this craft, a cultural heritage of the state.<ref name="HernándezSol" /><ref name="milenioPicado">{{cite web |last1=Palacios |first1=Karina |title=Pueblo de papel picado |url=https://www.milenio.com/cultura/pueblo-de-papel-picado |website=milenio.com |access-date=31 May 2021 |language=es-MX|date=25 October 2015}}</ref> Papel Picado from Huixcolotla is exported around the world via the [[Museo Nacional de Arte]].<ref name="harvey">{{cite book |last1=Harvey |first1=Marian |title=Mexican crafts and craftspeople: The papel picado of Huixcolotla |date=1987 |publisher=Art Alliance Press |location=Philadelphia |isbn=9780879825126 |pages=50–58 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=n2EWryQkeXYC |chapter=4}}</ref>

==Other culture==
An annual fair celebrates the town's patron of San Salvador and runs from 6–14 August.<ref name="enciclopediaMunicipos" /> A typical dish is the eponymous [[Mole (sauce)#Puebla|mole poblana]] of the region.<ref name="enciclopediaMunicipos" />
