'''Cojumatlán de Régules''' is a [[Municipalities of Michoacán|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Michoacán]]. It is located {{convert|180|km}} west of the state capital of [[Morelia]].

==Geography==
The municipality of Cojumatlán de Régules is located in the [[Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt]] in northwest Michoacán at an altitude between {{convert|1600|and(-)|2400|m}}. It borders the Michoacano municipalities of [[Venustiano Carranza, Michoacán|Venustiano Carranza]] to the east, [[Sahuayo]] to the southeast, [[Jiquilpan, Michoacán|Jiquilpan]] to the south, [[Marcos Castellanos]] to the southwest. It also borders [[Tizapan El Alto]] in [[Jalisco]] to the west, while its northern border runs along the southeastern shore of [[Lake Chapala]], which is administered by the Jaliscan municipality of [[Poncitlán]].<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Cojumatlán de Régules, Michoacán de Ocampo |language=es |publisher=INEGI |accessdate=2 May 2018}}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|131.48|km2}}<ref name=infocard/> and comprises 0.2% of the state's area.<ref name=survey/> 

Cojumatlán's climate is temperate with rain in the summer.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|16|and(-)|20|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|700|and(-)|1000|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
The settlement of Cojumatlán was founded in 1531. Its name means "place of weasels", from the [[Nahuatl]] word ''cutzamalí'', "weasel".<ref name=encyclopedia/> It was incorporated as the municipality of Régules in the district of Jiquilpan on 2 April 1910, taking its name after Nicolás de Régules, a general who fought against the [[Second French intervention in Mexico|French intervention in Mexico]]. In 1985 the municipality changed its name to Cojumatlán de Régules.<ref>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Michoacán de Ocampo. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |pages=86, 185 |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/historicos/2104/702825222420/702825222420_5.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |isbn=970-13-1501-4}}</ref>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and seven trustees (''regidores''), four elected by [[relative majority]] and three by [[proportional representation]].<ref name=encyclopedia/> The current president of the municipality is Enrique Mújica Sánchez.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Cojumatlán de Régules recorded a population of 9980 inhabitants living in 2638 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=16&mun=074 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Cojumatlán de Régules |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=SEDESOL |accessdate=2 May 2018}}</ref> The 2015 Intercensal Survey estimated a population of 10,450 inhabitants in Cojumatlán.<ref name=survey/>

There are 14 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> of which only the municipal seat, also known as Cojumatlán de Régules, is classified as urban. It recorded a population of 6763 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=catalog/>

==Economy==
[[File:White pelicans staging at Squaw Creek NWR (6366886293).jpg|thumb|A flock of American white pelicans]]
The main economic activities in Cojumatlán de Régules are agriculture, fishing, and livestock production.<ref name=plan2012>{{cite news |author=Cojumatlán de Régules Municipal Council |url=http://201.159.134.38/obtenerpdf.php?path=/Documentos/ESTADO/MICHOACAN/TODOS%20LOS%20MUNICIPIOS/o2744441.pdf&nombreclave=o2744441.pdf |title=Plan de Desarrollo Municipal 2012–2015 |language=es |pages=21–23 |newspaper=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Michoacán |publisher=Government of Michoacán |date=11 July 2012 |accessdate=2 May 2018}}</ref> Main crops grown include corn, tomatoes, alfalfa, and other vegetables.<ref>{{cite news |author=Cojumatlán de Régules Municipal Council |url=http://transparencia.congresomich.gob.mx/media/documentos/periodicos/seg-3816.pdf |title=Plan de Desarrollo Municipal 2015–2018 |language=es |page=9 |newspaper=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Michoacán |publisher=Government of Michoacán |date=4 April 2016 |accessdate=2 May 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180503111350/http://transparencia.congresomich.gob.mx/media/documentos/periodicos/seg-3816.pdf |archive-date=3 May 2018 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The main fish caught are carp and tilapia, which are filleted in small local [[maquiladora]]s.<ref name=plan2012/>

The arrival of thousands of [[American white pelican]]s from November to March at the fishing village of Petatán has become a tourist attraction. The pelicans are dubbed ''borregones'' by the locals, in reference to the sheep-like flocks created by these large white birds. A mass feeding of fish scraps from the local filleting operations occurs in the afternoon of every day except Sundays.<ref>{{cite news |last=Pint |first=John |title=Some Chapala spots well worth a visit |url=https://mexiconewsdaily.com/mexicolife/some-chapala-spots-well-worth-a-visit/ |newspaper=Mexico News Daily |date=6 September 2017 |accessdate=2 May 2018}}</ref>
