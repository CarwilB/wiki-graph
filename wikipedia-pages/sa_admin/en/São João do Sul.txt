'''São João do Sul''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the [[states of Brazil|state]] of [[Santa Catarina (state)|Santa Catarina]] in the [[South Region, Brazil|South region]] of Brazil.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2008">{{cite web |url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2008/dtb_2008.zip |title=Divisão Territorial do Brasil |date=July 1, 2008 |publisher=Divisão Territorial do Brasil e Limites Territoriais, Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=December 17, 2009|language=pt}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Pop_2009">{{cite web | url = http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/populacao/estimativa2009/POP2009_DOU.pdf | title = Estimativas da população para 1º de julho de 2009| publisher = Estimativas de População, Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) | date = August 14, 2009 | access-date=December 17, 2009 | format = [[PDF]]|language=pt }}</ref><ref name="PNUD_IDH_2000">{{cite web |url=http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/IDH-M%2091%2000%20Ranking%20decrescente%20(pelos%20dados%20de%202000).htm |title=Ranking decrescente do IDH-M dos municípios do Brasil |year=2000 |publisher=Atlas do Desenvolvimento Humano, Programa das Nações Unidas para o Desenvolvimento (PNUD) |access-date=December 17, 2009|language=pt}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_PIB_2005">{{cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/estatistica/economia/pibmunicipios/2005/tab01.pdf |title=Produto Interno Bruto dos Municípios 2002–2005 |date=December 19, 2007 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=December 17, 2009|language=pt}}</ref>

== History ==
By 1860, a small settlement had been established at a site known as ''Passo do Sertão'', situated near a river crossing requiring ferry or [[Canoe|canoe transport.]] The name reflected its location at a "step of the wilderness."<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=History of Sao Joao do Sul |url=https://sensacaotermica.com/sao-joao-do-sul-sc/historia-da-cidade}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=ClimaOnline |title=📕 História de SAO JOAO DO SUL – Santa Catarina (SC) |url=https://climaonline.com.br/sao-joao-do-sul-sc/historia-da-cidade |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=ClimaOnline.com.br |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=São João do Sul, Santa Catarina {{!}} Brasil |url=https://www.ferias.tur.br/cidade/8698/sao-joao-do-sul-sc.html |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=www.ferias.tur.br}}</ref>

Colonization advanced gradually through successive waves of European settlers. [[German diaspora|German pioneers]] arrived early. One of the first groups settled in the Glória (now [[Glorinha]]) community around 1826, when the [[Schaeffer|Schaeffer family]] relocated from the [[Torres, Rio Grande do Sul|Torres colony]] in Rio Grande do Sul. Subsequent arrivals included João Evalt (in 1856), Jacó May (1860), Caetano Lummertz, and Antônio Miguel Magnus (1888), some bringing the "Magnus" surname still prevalent today.<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Jr |first=Luiz Antonio |title=História da cidade de São João do Sul – SC |url=https://prefeituras.info/ |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=Prefeituras.info |language=pt-BR}}</ref><ref name=":2">{{Cite web |title=Sao Joao do Sul |url=https://www.procep.com.br/sc/sao-joao-do-sul}}</ref>

In 1870, families from the [[Azores]], including [[Martins (surname)|Martins]], [[Rocha (surname)|Rocha]], [[Cardoso (surname)|Cardoso]], [[Rodrigues (surname)|Rodrigues]], [[Aguiar]], [[Borba (surname)|Borba]], [[Mendonça]], [[Luz (name)|Luz]], [[Reis (surname)|Reis]], and [[Homem]], began settling broadly across the area.<ref name=":2" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Jr |first=Luiz Antonio |title=História da cidade de São João do Sul – SC |url=https://prefeituras.info/ |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=Prefeituras.info |language=pt-BR}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Cite web |title=Tudo sobre a cidade de São João do Sul Estado de Santa Catarina |url=https://www.cidadesdomeubrasil.com.br/sc/sao_joao_do_sul |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=Cidades do meu Brasil |language=pt-BR}}</ref> [[Italian diaspora|Italian settlers]] followed in the early 20th century, particularly in Timbopeba. Families like Scandolara, [[Piazza (surname)|Piazza]], and [[Bianchini]] (originating from [[Meleiro]] and [[Cocal do Sul]]) helped foster early trade with the nearby Serra region.<ref name=":2" /><ref name=":3" /> By 1900, these immigrant families; [[Emerich]], Lummertz, [[Borba (surname)|Borba]], and [[Abel (surname)|Abel]], had firmly established the agricultural and cultural foundation of the community.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2" /><ref name=":3" />

On February 3, 1891, the settlement of Passo do Sertão was elevated to district status within the municipality of [[Araranguá]] by decree no. 45.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" /> Later, on March 31, 1938, State Decree-Law no. 86 raised it to the "Vila Passo do Sertão." Finally, on December 20, 1961, Law no. 801 officially separated the district from Araranguá and created the municipality of [[São João do Sul]], named in honor of its patron saint, [[John the Baptist]].<ref name=":1" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=Sao Joao do Sul – Santa Catarina, History |url=https://www.guiadoturista.net/america-do-sul/brasil/santa-catarina/sao-joao-do-sul}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Machado |first=Fernanda |date=December 12, 2022 |title=All about São João do Sul – SC |url=https://wenttrip.com.br/en/brazil/brasil-sul/santa-catarina/sao-joao-do-sul/all-about-sao-joao-do-sul-sc/ |access-date=July 16, 2025 |website=WentTrip |language=en-US}}</ref>
