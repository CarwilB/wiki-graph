'''Caracaraí''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|kaɾɐkaɾaˈi}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the state of [[Roraima]] in [[Brazil]]. Its population is 22,283 and its area is {{convert|47411|km2}}, making it the largest municipality in Roraima state. Caracaraí is located on the [[Branco River]].

== Overview ==
Caracaraí was founded by [[cattle rancher]]s from [[Rolim de Moura|Moura]]. The town was originally known as Cidade Porto and was the capital of the Rio Branco Territory which was later renamed Roraima.<ref name="hist">{{cite web|url=https://www.caracarai.rr.gov.br/historia-de-caracarai |title=História de Caracaraí|website=Municipality of Caracaraí|access-date=14 April 2021|language=pt}}</ref>

The word ''caracaraí'' means "little hawk", a very common bird in the region.<ref name="ESECCaracaraí"/> Its territory cuts Roraima's in two and keeps boundaries with both the Brazilian state of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]] and the country of [[Guyana]], though the Guyana portion it borders is disputed with [[Venezuela]]
It is not the only municipality in the state to do so; [[Caroebe]] also borders both Amazonas and Guyana, and has boundaries with [[Pará]] as well. 

Caracaraí is located near the intersection of the [[BR-174]] and [[BR-210]] highways.<ref name="wwf"/> Caracaraí became an independent municipality in 1955. Between 1960 and 1982, the municipality contained the districts of [[Santa Maria do Boiaçu]] and [[São José de Anauá]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rr/caracarai/historico |title=Caracaraí |website=[[IBGE]]|access-date=14 April 2021|language=pt}}</ref> Santa Maria do Boiaçu became part of the municipality of [[Rorainópolis]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://saude.rr.gov.br/2018/index.php/2017-04-30-22-46-19/noticias-2/10-noticias-da-sesau/240/santa-maria-do-boiacu-unidade-recebe-primeira-grande-reforma-e-ampliacao-na-estrutura |title=SANTA MARIA DO BOIAÇU - Unidade recebe primeira grande reforma e ampliação na estrutura|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191016150948/https://saude.rr.gov.br/2018/index.php/2017-04-30-22-46-19/noticias-2/10-noticias-da-sesau/240/santa-maria-do-boiacu-unidade-recebe-primeira-grande-reforma-e-ampliacao-na-estrutura|access-date=14 April 2021|archive-date=2019-10-16|language=pt}}</ref>

The municipality contains the [[Caracaraí Ecological Station]] and the [[Niquiá Ecological Station]].<ref name="ESECCaracaraí">{{citation|language=Portuguese|title=Estação Ecológica de Caracaraí|publisher=Via Rural|url=http://br.viarural.com/servicos/turismo/estacoes-ecologicas/estacao-ecologica-de-caracarai/default.htm|accessdate=2016-04-23|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161220065943/http://br.viarural.com/servicos/turismo/estacoes-ecologicas/estacao-ecologica-de-caracarai/default.htm|archive-date=2016-12-20|url-status=usurped}}</ref><ref>{{citation|url=http://ambientes.ambientebrasil.com.br/unidades_de_conservacao/estacao_ecologica/estacao_ecologica_de_niquia.html
 |title=Estação Ecológica de Níquia|work=Ambiente Brasil|date=23 June 2010|accessdate=2016-04-16}}</ref>

The economy used to be based on cattle ranching. The cattle used to be shipped to [[Manaus]]. Caracaraí developed as a supply centre for the region,<ref name="hist"/> because it is located on the [[Branco River]] south of the waterfalls.<ref name="wwf">{{cite web|url=https://wwf.panda.org/wwf_news/?148741/In-search-of-reptiles-and-amphibians |title=Caracaraí: port for cattle shipping |website=Panda.org|access-date=14 April 2021}}</ref> It is the largest producer of fish in the state.<ref name="hist"/>
