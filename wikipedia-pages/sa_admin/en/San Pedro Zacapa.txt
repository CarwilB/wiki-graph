'''San Pedro Zacapa''' ({{IPA|es|sam ˈpeðɾo saˈkapa}}) is a [[Municipality (Honduras)|municipality]] in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]], Honduras. It covers an area of {{cvt|227.8|km2}} and had a population of 10,535 inhabitants according to the 2013 national census. The first indigenous settlers arrived from Guatemala in 1780, and it  later became a municipality in the Santa Bárbara District.

== History ==
The name originated from the [[Mesoamerican language|Mesoamerican]] word "Cacat Atl" , meaning "between rivers", indicating its location between the Zacapa, Zunzucuapa and Guajiniquil rivers. It was named so by the first indigenous settlers who arrived from Guatemala in 1780. During the 1801 census, it was listed as part of Tencoa, and it was listed as a municipality in the Santa Bárbara District as per the Political Territorial Division of Honduras in 1889.<ref name="Edu">{{cite web|title=Municipio de San Pedro Zacapa, Santa Bárbara|url=https://edu-honduras.info/Los-Departamentos/16-Santa-Barbara/San-Pedro-Zacapa.html|work=Edu-Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026|lang=es}}</ref><ref name="Red">{{cite web|url=https://redhonduras.hn/en/geography/municipality-san-pedro-zacapa/|title=Municipality of San Pedro Zacapa|work=Red Honduras|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref>

== Geography ==
San Pedro Zacapa is located in the department of [[Santa Bárbara Department, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] in Honduras.<ref name="Red"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/|title=Administrative divisions of Honduras|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Intibucá]] and [[Jesús de Otoro]] to the south, [[San José de Comayagua]] and [[Santa Cruz de Yojoa]] to the east, [[Santa Bárbara, Honduras|Santa Bárbara]] and Santa Cruz de Yojoa to the north, and [[Concepción del Sur]] and [[San Francisco de Ojuera]] to the west.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/> The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|227.8|km2}} and has an average elevation of {{cvt|385|m}} above sea level.<ref name="Edu"/> Several rivers including the [[Ulua River|Ulua]], Jaiti, Zacapa River, Cacao, Limón, and Jimilares pass through the municipality.<ref name="Edu"/><ref name="Red"/> Cerro Cargamón is an inactive [[volcano]] in the department. The 
Cacao in La Boquita, Salto in Agua Zarca, and Salto de Cangel in Mojarras are major waterfalls in the municipality. The Calichon caves are located in the El Zapote village.<ref name="Red"/>

== Administrative divisions ==

== Demographics ==
As per the 2013 census, San Marcos had a total population of 10,535 inhabitants, of whom 5,378 were male and 5,378 were female. Of the total population, 14.3% was classified as urban and 85.7% lived in the rural areas.<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/honduras/admin/santa_b%C3%A1rbara/1623__san_pedro_zacapa/|title=San Marcos|work=Citypopulation.de|access-date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}}

Of the total population, 11%  was classified as [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indigenous]] and rest as non-indigenous.<ref name="CP"/> By broad age group, 36.7% of the population were aged 0–14, 56.8% were aged 15–64, and 6.5% were aged 65 and over. The median age was 20.6 years and the mean age was 26.4 years. Among the population aged 15 and over, the illiteracy rate was 17.4%, lower than the departmental average of 22.2%.<ref name="INE2013"/>{{rp|page=19}}
