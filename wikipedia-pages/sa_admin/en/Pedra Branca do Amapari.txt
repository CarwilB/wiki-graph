'''Pedra Branca do Amapari''' ({{IPA|pt-BR|ˈpɛdɾɐ ˈbɾɐ̃kɐ du ɐmapaˈɾi|lang}}; 'White Stone of Amapari'), also known simply as '''Amapari''', is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] located in the midwest of the state of [[Amapá]], [[Brazil]].<ref name="a"/> Its population is 17,067,<ref>[https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/ap/pedra-branca-do-amapari/panorama IBGE 2020]</ref> with an area of {{convert|9,495|km2|mi2}}. The municipality has a population density of 1.13/km2, and the population remains evenly divided between rural and village areas.<ref name="a"/><ref name="s"/>

==Geography==

Pedra Branca do Amapari is bordered by the municipalities of [[Oiapoque]] to the north, [[Serra do Navio]] to the east, [[Porto Grande]] to the southeast, [[Mazagão]] to the south, and [[Laranjal do Jari]] to the west. The town is rich in rivers and streams (igarapé). The [[Amapari River]] and its tributaries, the Mururé, Tucumpi and Cupixi, all cross through the municipality. The Amapari River feeds in to the [[Araguari River (Amapá)|Araguari River]] in the southwest of the town.<ref name="a"/>
The municipality contains 12% of the {{convert|806184|ha|adj=on}} [[Rio Iratapuru Sustainable Development Reserve]], created in 1997.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|RDS do Rio Iratapuru – ISA}}|language=pt
 |title=RDS do Rio Iratapuru|publisher=ISA: Instituto Socioambiental
 |url=https://uc.socioambiental.org/uc/5379|accessdate=2016-11-05}}</ref>
It contains 6.39% of the {{convert|2369400|ha|adj=on}} [[Amapá State Forest]], a sustainable use conservation unit established in 2006.<ref>{{citation|ref={{harvid|FES do Amapá – ISA}}|language=Portuguese

==History==
The municipality of Amapari was originally explored by members of the [[Saramaka]], a [[Maroon people|Maroon]] group from [[Suriname]], who searched for gold in the area.<ref name="s"/> Manganese ore was discovered in the region in 1953. Travel to Pedra Branca do Amapari was possible only by airplane, and pilots used a huge white rock (''pedra branca'') in the Amapari River as a reference point. Thereafter the town was known as Pedra Branca do Amapari.<ref name="a"/> The town can nowadays be accessed via the [[BR-210]] highway.<ref name="amapa">{{cite web|url=https://www.portal.ap.gov.br/conheca/pedra-branca-do-amapari |title=Pedra Branca do Amapari|website=Government of Amapá|access-date=2 April 2021|language=pt}}</ref>

==Economy==

Amapari supports the small-scale production of [[rice]], [[maize|corn]], [[bean]]s, and [[cassava]] for the domestic market. [[Cupuaçu]], [[pineapple]]s, [[orange (fruit)|orange]]s, [[banana]]s, and [[melon]]s are grown for local consumption.<ref name="a"/> Per capital income in Amapari is [[Brazilian real|R$]] 24,782 (US$9629), as compared to the Brazilian national average of R$20,988 (US$12,536).<ref name="s"/><ref name="imf"/> 

{{anchor|Gold}}
[[Gold mining]] has become a major part of the economy, and resulted in a steep population growth.<ref name="amapa"/> The biggest concession is the Tucano mine which is operated by [[Great Panther Mining]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.greatpanther.com/operations/producing-mines/tucano-mine/ |title=Tucano mine|website=Great Panther|access-date=2 April 2021}}</ref>
