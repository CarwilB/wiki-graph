'''Dibulla''' (Sometimes spelled '''Dibuya''') is a town and municipality located in the [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[La Guajira Department|La Guajira]], [[Colombia]] by the [[Caribbean Sea]] and the [[Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta]] mountains on the [[Guajira Peninsula]]. It was proclaimed municipality in 1995.

The town is mostly recognized for being the birthplace of renown [[vallenato]] composer [[Carlos Huertas (vallenato composer)|Carlos Huertas]] and his song "''[[El Cantor de Fonseca]]''".

==Geography==

Dibulla limits to the west with the [[Magdalena Department]]; to the north with the [[Caribbean Sea]] by the steps of the [[Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta]] mountains (northeastern side). Located 74&nbsp;km away from La Guajira's capital [[Riohacha]] en route to the city of [[Santa Marta]], it is crossed by the river of the same name, the [[Dibulla River]] and also the [[Jérez River]].

The municipality has a total area of 1,847&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> and its only 2 m above sea level. The average temperature is of 28&nbsp;°C. Its main attraction relies on a recreational center called [[Maziruma]], a beach resort.<ref>[http://www.laguajira.gov.co/municipios/dibulla.htm {{in lang|es}} Government of La Guajira: Geography of Dibuya] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070813150123/http://www.laguajira.gov.co/municipios/dibulla.htm |date=2007-08-13 }}</ref>

==History==

[[Image:Provincia Guanebucan en la Gobernacion de Santa Marta 1500.png|thumb|left|250px|Province of Guanebucanes (Green), part of the Government of Santa Marta created in the year 1524.]]

The area of present-day municipality of Dibuya was inhabited predominantly during the [[Pre-Columbian]] era by indigenous tribes pertaining to the [[Koguis]] and [[Guanebucanes]] ethnic groups descendants of the [[Tairona]] civilization with a direct influence from the [[Wayuu]] ethnic group. During the [[Spanish colonization of the Americas]], the indigenous village of Yaharo was first seen by Spanish explorers upon reaching the coasts of the [[Guajira Peninsula]] in 1502. In 1525, Spanish explorer [[Rodrigo de Bastidas]] visited the Yaharo Town and registered an account of the village.<ref>[http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m {{in lang|es}} Dibuya: History] {{webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20120715115333/http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m |date=2012-07-15 }}</ref> A year before, in 1524 Bastidas had created the Government of Santa Marta which covered an area from [[Cabo de la Vela]] to the mouth of the [[Magdalena River]].<ref>{{in lang|es}} Juan Friede, “La conquista del territorio y el poblamiento”, Manual de Historia de
Colombia, 3 Vols., Vol. 1, Bogotá, [[Instituto Colombiano de Cultura]], 1982, pp.116-222, p.131.</ref>

Between 1609 and 1640, the Spanish colonizers imported some 800 or more African slaves. Most of these later escaped and formed [[Palenques]]. In 1679 the Government of Santa Marta offered these palenques their freedom in exchange of protecting the territory from English pirates.<ref>[http://www.universia.net.co/view-document/documento-283.html {{in lang|es}} La guerra de los Cárdenas y los Valdeblánquez (1970-1989) Estudio de un conflicto mestizo en La Guajira by Simón Uribe Martínez and Nicolás Cárdenas Angel] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150122090906/http://www.universia.net.co/view-document/documento-283.html |date=2015-01-22 }}</ref>

In 1846 then [[President of Colombia]], Gen. [[Tomas Cipriano de Mosquera]] ordered the reestablishment of the government after the [[War of the Supremes]] civil war. Yaharo was renamed to Dibuya which translates from the [[Guanebucan language]] to "lagoon by the sea", lagoon which is located in the region of the Ramada.<ref>[http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m {{in lang|es}} Dibuya: History] {{webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20120715115333/http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m |date=2012-07-15 }}</ref>

In 1872 Dibuya became a municipality seat or district by law 216 of this same year and was part of the [[Department of Magdalena]]. In 1886 this category was removed by interim governor of the department Luis Cotes by Decree 377 of February 17, 1886 when the [[United States of Colombia]] was dissolved. The Council of Riohacha formally created the Corregimiento of Dibuya on April 1, 1887.<ref>[http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m {{in lang|es}} Dibuya: History] {{webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20120715115333/http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m |date=2012-07-15 }}</ref>

From the 1970s and until approximately 1989 two families from the [[Department of La Guajira]]; the Cárdenas and the Valdeblánquez engaged in a war for the control of the [[marijuana]]. The confrontation between the two families gained national attention and directly affected those family members living in Dibuya, as well as those living from [[Barranquilla]] and [[Santa Marta]] to [[Riohacha]], [[Maicao]] and [[Valledupar]] in the [[Caribbean Region of Colombia]].<ref>[http://www.universia.net.co/view-document/documento-283.html {{in lang|es}} La guerra de los Cárdenas y los Valdeblánquez (1970-1989) Estudio de un conflicto mestizo en La Guajira by Simón Uribe Martínez and Nicolás Cárdenas Angel] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150122090906/http://www.universia.net.co/view-document/documento-283.html |date=2015-01-22 }}</ref>

On December 5, 1995 the [[Department Assembly of La Guajira]] created the Municipality of Dibuya by Ordinance 030 of that same year. [[Cristian Montero Córdoba]] became its first appointed mayor by Decree 350 of December 28, 1995.<ref>[http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m {{in lang|es}} Dibuya: History] {{webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20120715115333/http://dibulla-laguajira.gov.co/nuestromunicipio.shtml?apc=m-I1--&m=f&s=m |date=2012-07-15 }}</ref>
