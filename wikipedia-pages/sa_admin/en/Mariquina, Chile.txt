'''Mariquina''' is a [[Communes of Chile|commune]] in southern [[Chile]], [[Valdivia Province]], [[Los Ríos Region]]. It is located about 40&nbsp;km northeast of [[Valdivia, Chile|Valdivia]], close to [[Cruces River]]. The capital is the city of '''San José de la Mariquina'''. The commune's main economic activities are agriculture, cattle farming and wood pulp manufacturing.

According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Mariquina spans an area of {{convert|1320.5|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 18,223 inhabitants (9,361 men and 8,862 women). Of these, 8,925 (49%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 9,298 (51%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 1.5% (271 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Mariquina is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The current alcalde is Ronaldo Mitre Gatica.

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Mariquina is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Alfonso De Urresti ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]]) and Roberto Delmastro ([[National Renewal (Chile)|RN]]) as part of the 53rd electoral district, together with [[Valdivia, Chile|Valdivia]], [[Lanco, Chile|Lanco]], [[Máfil]] and [[Corral, Chile|Corral]]. The commune is represented in the {{Current senators (16th senatorial constituency of Chile)}} as part of the 16th senatorial constituency (Los Ríos Region).

==History==
In the mid-17th century local [[cacique]] [[Juan Manqueante]] ruled the lands of Mariquina. He presented himself to the [[Dutch expedition to Valdivia|Dutch who had arrived in Valdivia]] in 1643 as a friend.<ref name=Arana285>Barros Arana 2000, p. 285.</ref> When the Spanish returned in 1645 he allied to them for about ten years until the [[Mapuche uprising of 1655]].<ref name=cultura>{{Cite book|title=Culturas de Chile|last=Alonqueo|first=Martín|publisher=Editorial Andrés Bello|year=1996|isbn=956-13-1437-1|pages=232|editor-last=Hidalgo L.|editor-first=Jorge|volume=2|editor-last2=Schiappacasse F.|editor-first2=Virgilio|editor-last3=Niemeyer F.|editor-first3=Hans|editor-last4=Aldunate del S.|editor-first4=Carlos|editor-last5=Mege R.|editor-first5=Pedro|language=es}}</ref> Local lore consider him the most notable person ever born in the lands of Mariquina.<ref name=cultura/> Manqueante was catholic according to contemporary chronicler [[Diego de Rosales]].<ref name=cultura/>

According to [[Tomás Guevara]], by the 18th century the Mapuche of Mariquina were among the last to raise [[chilihueque]]s, a now extinct llama-like animal.<ref name=Torrejonetal2004>{{cite journal |last1=Torrejón |first1=Fernando |last2=Cisternas |first2=Marco|last3=Araneda |first3=Alberto |date=2004 |title=Efectos ambientales de la colonización española desde el río Maullín al archipiélago de Chiloé, sur de Chile |trans-title=Environmental effects of the spanish colonization from de Maullín river to the Chiloé archipelago, southern Chile |url=http://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0716-078X2004000400009&script=sci_arttext&tlng=en |journal=[[Revista Chilena de Historia Natural]] |volume=77 |pages=661–677 |language=es }}</ref>

During the [[Mapuche uprising of 1881]], most women of San José de la Mariquina were sent to the city of [[Valdivia]] as men prepared for hostilities. Chileans and [[German colonization of Valdivia, Osorno and Llanquihue|German settlers]] dug defensive trenches around the town.<ref name=Vergara>{{cite journal |last=Vergara |first=Jorge Iván |last2=Gundermann |first2=Hans |date=2012 |title=Constitution and internal dynamics of the regional identitary in Tarapacá and Los Lagos, Chile |url=http://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0717-73562012000100009&script=sci_arttext |journal=[[Chungara (journal)|Chungara]] |language=es |publisher=[[University of Tarapacá]] |volume=44 |issue=1 |pages=115–134 |doi= 10.4067/s0717-73562012000100009|access-date=25 December 2013|doi-access=free }}</ref> At the moment of the uprising it was even thought that rebels could reach Valdivia in the south if they succeeded in penetrating San José de la Mariquina.<ref name=Vergara/>
