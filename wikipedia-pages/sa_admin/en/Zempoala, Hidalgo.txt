'''Zempoala ''' is a town and one of the 84 [[municipalities of Hidalgo]], in central-eastern [[Mexico]]. The municipality covers an area of 319.78&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=Hidalgo - Zempoala|url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM13hidalgo/municipios/13083a.html|access-date=2021-05-25|website=www.inafed.gob.mx|archive-date=2017-04-10|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170410092554/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM13hidalgo/municipios/13083a.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> The name Zempoala derives from the [[Nahuatl]] roots "Cempoalli", meaning "twenty" and "tlan", meaning "place"; giving Zempoala the meaning of "place of twenties", referring to the market held every twenty days.<ref name=":0" />

As of 2020, the municipality had a total population of 57,906.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Zempoala (Municipality, Mexico) - Population Statistics, Charts, Map and Location|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/mexico/admin/hidalgo/13083__zempoala/|access-date=2022-02-25|website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref>

== History ==
Zempoala was an important place in the prehispanic culture, inhabited by [[Toltec]]s and [[Chichimeca|Chichimecs]], the latter arriving in the year 1120, guided by [[King Xolotl]]. By 1167, Zempoala was head of the province, being ruled by King Xolotl's son, [[Nopaltzin]]. In 1430, the province fell to [[Tetzcoco (altepetl)|Texcoco]].<ref name=":0" />

In 1540, the Spanish arrived to the area. Juan Pérez de Gama was the first ''[[encomendero]]'' of Zempoala. Between 1553 and 1570, the [[Aqueduct of Padre Tembleque]] was built between Zempoala and [[Otumba, State of Mexico|Otumba]],<ref>{{Cite web|date=2009-04-15|title=Aqueduct of Padre Tembleque - UNESCO World Heritage Centre|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1592/|access-date=2021-05-25|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090415052950/https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1592/|archive-date=2009-04-15}}</ref> which would be designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Sites in Japan, Turkey, Mexico, Uruguay inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List, extension of Spanish site approved|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1317/|access-date=2021-05-25|website=UNESCO World Heritage Centre|language=en}}</ref>

On 1 December 2020, the city of Zempoala was declared a ''[[Pueblos Mágicos|Pueblo Mágico]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|title=¿Qué visitar en Zempoala? El nuevo Pueblo Mágico de Hidalgo|url=https://www.milenio.com/cultura/que-visitar-en-zempoala-el-nuevo-pueblo-magico-de-hidalgo|access-date=2021-05-25|website=www.milenio.com|date=12 January 2020 |language=es}}</ref>
