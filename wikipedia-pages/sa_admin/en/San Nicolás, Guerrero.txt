'''San Nicolás''' is a [[Municipalities of Guerrero|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[List of states of Mexico|state]] of [[Guerrero]]. It is located {{convert|165|km}} southeast of the state capital of [[Chilpancingo]]. It is named after its patron saint, [[Nicholas of Tolentino]].<ref>{{cite journal |last=Lewis |first=Laura A. |date=February 2001 |title=Of Ships and Saints: History, Memory, and Place in the Making of Moreno Mexican Identity |journal=[[Cultural Anthropology (journal)|Cultural Anthropology]] |volume=16 |issue=1 |pages=62–82 |jstor=656602}}</ref> Its creation from the municipality of [[Cuajinicuilapa]] was approved in 2021 and went into force on 21 May 2022.

==Geography==
The municipality of San Nicolás is located in the [[Costa Chica of Guerrero|Costa Chica]] region of southeastern Guerrero. It borders the municipality of Cuajinicuilapa in Guerrero to the west, north, and east, the [[Oaxaca]]n municipality of [[Santiago Tapextla]] to the southeast, and the [[Pacific Ocean]] to the southwest. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|167.22|km2}}.<ref name=creation/> The municipality's Pacific coast serves as a critical nesting area for the [[leatherback turtle]] and is protected as the [[Ramsar site]] [[Playa Tortuguera Tierra Colorada]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://rsis.ramsar.org/ris/1327 |title=Playa Tortuguera Tierra Colorada |website=Ramsar Sites Information Service |publisher=[[Ramsar Convention]] |date=27 November 2003 |access-date=24 November 2023}}</ref>

San Nicolás has a [[subhumid temperate climate]] with rain in the summer. Average temperatures range between {{convert|24|and(-)|28|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|1100|and(-)|1300|mm}}.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/12/12023.pdf |title=Cuajinicuilapa, Guerrero |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos |language=es |publisher=INEGI |year=2009 |access-date=24 November 2023}}</ref>

==History==
The municipality of San Nicolás is located on territory that was once part of the province of [[Quahuitlán]], whose inhabitants spoke Quahteca or Cuahuiteca, which was probably a [[Mixtec languages|Mixtec dialect]]. Quahuitlán which was controlled by [[Tututepec]] until around 1497, when the area was conquered by the [[Mexica Empire]]. [[Pedro de Alvarado]] conquered the area for Spain in 1522, and in 1548 the area was made an ''[[encomienda]]'' of [[Tristán de Luna y Arellano]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://enciclopediagro.mx/cultura-general/quahuitlan-provincia-de/ |title=Quahuitlán, Provincia de |website=Enciclopedia Guerrerense |date=11 March 2020 |access-date=24 November 2023}}</ref> In the late 16th century, Spanish cattle ranchers brought free and enslaved blacks and [[mulatto]]es to the area, from whom most of San Nicolás's present-day inhabitants are descended.<ref name=lewisbook>{{cite book |last=Lewis |first=Laura A. |title=Chocolate and Corn Flour: History, Race, and Place in the Making of "Black" Mexico |pages=2–4 |publisher=[[Duke University Press]] |year=2012 |isbn=978-0-8223-9477-8 |doi=10.1215/9780822394778}}</ref>

In 2004, inhabitants of San Nicolás and nearby communities began an initiative to separate from Cuajinicuilapa and form a new municipality, citing a lack of resources for development in their area.<ref>{{cite news |last=Contreras Lara |first=Juan José |date=30 July 2014 |title=Promueven la creación de un nuevo municipio en Costa Chica |url=https://guerrero.quadratin.com.mx/Promueven-la-creacion-de-un-nuevo-municipio-en-Costa-Chica/ |language=es |newspaper=Quadratín Guerrero |access-date=25 November 2023}}</ref> On 31 August 2021 the [[Congress of Guerrero|Guerrero state legislature]] approved the formation of the municipality of San Nicolás comprising ten localities previously belonging to Cuajinicuilapa.<ref name=creation/> The state constitutional amendment establishing the municipality was passed on 13 January 2022 and went into force on 21 May 2022.<ref name=decree>{{cite news |date=20 May 2022 |title=DECRETO NÚMERO 161 POR EL QUE SE APRUEBA LA ADICIÓN DE LOS NUEVOS MUNICIPIOS DE SANTA CRUZ DEL RINCÓN, SAN NICOLÁS, ÑUU SAVI Y LAS VIGAS AL ARTÍCULO 27 DE LA CONSTITUCIÓN POLÍTICA DEL ESTADO LIBRE Y SOBERANO DE GUERRERO. EN EL LUGAR QUE CONFORME AL ORDEN ALFABÉTICO LES CORRESPONDE. |url=https://periodicooficial.guerrero.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/P.O-40-20-MAYO-20221.pdf |language=es |work=Periódico Oficial del Estado de Guerrero |pages=9–24 |access-date=21 November 2023}}</ref>

==Administration==
San Nicolás held its first elections as an independent municipality in 2024. The municipal government of San Nicolás comprises a [[municipal president]], a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''), and six trustees (''regidores'').<ref>{{cite news |last=Chávez |first=Lourdes |date=16 November 2023 |url=https://suracapulco.mx/impreso/2/aprueba-iepc-composicion-de-ayuntamientos-incluidos-los-cuatro-nuevos-municipios/ |title=Aprueba IEPC composición de ayuntamientos, incluidos los cuatro nuevos municipios |language=es |newspaper=El Sur |location=Chilpancingo |accessdate=24 November 2023}}</ref>

==Demographics and culture==
[[File:PuntaMaldonado18.JPG|thumb|Girls in [[Punta Maldonado]].]]
In the 2020 [[INEGI]] Census, the localities that now comprise the municipality of San Nicolás recorded a population of 6984 inhabitants.<ref name=creation/> The municipal seat, also named San Nicolás, recorded a population of 3249 inhabitants in the 2020 Census.<ref name=creation/> The next most populous localities are {{ill|El Pitahayo|es}} and [[Punta Maldonado]], which recorded populations of 1583 and 848 inhabitants respectively in the 2020 Census.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/areasgeograficas/?ag=120230036 |title=El Pitahayo, Cuajinicuilapa, Guerrero (120230036) |language=es |website=México en Cifras |publisher=[[INEGI]] |accessdate=24 November 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/areasgeograficas/?ag=120230022 |title=Punta Maldonado (El Faro), Cuajinicuilapa, Guerrero (120230022) |language=es |website=México en Cifras |publisher=[[INEGI]] |accessdate=24 November 2023}}</ref>

Like the neighbouring Cuajinicuilapa, San Nicolás has been identified by cultural authorities, academics, and journalists as a centre of [[Afro-Mexican]] culture in Guerrero's Costa Chica. However, residents of San Nicolás self-identify as ''moreno'' (black Indians) and reject labels such as "black", "Afro-Mexican" and "Afromestizo". Since the late 1990s, a significant community of expatriates from San Nicolás has lived in the cities of [[Winston-Salem, North Carolina|Winston-Salem]], [[Greensboro, North Carolina|Greensboro]] and [[Charlotte, North Carolina|Charlotte]] in the U.S. state of [[North Carolina]].<ref name=lewisbook/>

==Economy==
The economy of San Nicolás is based on [[smallholder]] farming: farmers grow corn for their own consumption, and sesame and fruit (e.g., mango, papaya and watermelon) for sale to regional markets. Seaside communities depend heavily on fishing.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Lewis |first=Laura A. |date=November 2000 |title=Blacks, Black Indians, Afromexicans: The Dynamics of Race, Nation, and Identity in a Mexican "moreno" Community (Guerrero) |journal=[[American Ethnologist]] |volume=27 |issue=4 |pages=898–926 |jstor=647400}}</ref>
