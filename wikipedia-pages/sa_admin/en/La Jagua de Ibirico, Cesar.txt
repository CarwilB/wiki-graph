'''La Jagua de Ibirico''' (({{IPA|es|la ˈxaɣwa ðejβiˈɾiko}}), literally "The [[Genipa americana|Jagua]] of [[Juan Ramon de Ibirico|Ibirico]]") is a town and municipality in the [[Department of Cesar]], [[Colombia]]. This region of La Jagua is rich in [[coal]] which is exploited extensively and is second only to [[Cerrejon|El Cerrejon]] in [[La Guajira]]. The area is also known for being a hot spot in the [[Colombian armed conflict (1960s–present)|Colombian conflict]].

==Geography==

The municipality of La Jagua de Ibirico borders to the north with the municipality of [[Becerril]]; to the east with [[Venezuela]]; to the south with the municipality of [[Chiriguana]] and to the west with the municipality of [[El Paso, Cesar|El Paso]].

==History==

The first inhabitants in the region were the [[Chiriguanás]]. During the [[Spanish conquest of the Americas]] period, some remnants of indigenous tribes pertaining to the [[Arhuacos]] and [[Yucos]] established in the area. The village of La Jagua was founded in 1771 by the Spanish [[Juan Ramon de Ibirico]].<ref>[http://www.sinic.gov.co/SINIC/ColombiaCultural/ColCulturalBusca.aspx?AREID=3&SECID=8&IdDep=20&COLTEM=213 {{in lang|es}} National System for Cultural Information: La Jagua de Ibirico]</ref>

La Jagua de Ibirico became a municipality by ordinance 005 of 1979. In 1985 the rudimentary exploitation of [[coal]] began but with little results due to inexperience. In 1990 the government sponsored Carbocol gave concessions to mining companies.<ref>[http://www.sinic.gov.co/SINIC/ColombiaCultural/ColCulturalBusca.aspx?AREID=3&SECID=8&IdDep=20&COLTEM=213 {{in lang|es}} National System for Cultural Information: La Jagua de Ibirico]</ref>
