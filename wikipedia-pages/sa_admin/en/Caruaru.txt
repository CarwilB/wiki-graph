'''Caruaru''' ({{IPA|pt|caɾua'ɾu}} [[File:Loudspeaker.svg|10px]] <small>[//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dd/CaruaruPronunce.ogg listen]</small>) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Pernambuco]], located in the [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast region]] of the country. It is part of the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|Caruaru Intermediate Geographic Region]]. According to the 2025 census,<ref>[https://www.ibge.gov.br/cidades-e-estados/pe/caruaru.html 2025 census]</ref> its population is 405,408 inhabitants, making it the second most populous municipality in the interior of Pernambuco and the fourth most populous in the Northeast countryside, surpassed only by [[Feira de Santana]], [[Campina Grande]], and [[Petrolina]]. The municipality is situated to the [[west]] of the state capital, [[Recife]], approximately {{convert|130|km|mi}} away. It covers an area of {{convert|923.150|km2|sqmi}}, of which {{convert|59.51|km2|sqmi}} is urban.<ref name="IBGE_Caruaru">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru|title=Caruaru|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)|access-date=30 April 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230430235014/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/panorama|archive-date=30 April 2023}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Áreaurbananizada2019">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/panorama|title=Environment - Urbanized Area (2019)|publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE)|access-date=2 May 2025}}</ref>

Founded on 18 May 1857, one account of its origin suggests that the municipality began to take shape in 1681 when the then-governor of the captaincy granted the Rodrigues de Sá family a ''sesmaria'' spanning thirty [[League (unit)|leagues]], aimed at developing agriculture and cattle ranching in the region. However, a more widely accepted account considers a ''sesmaria'' charter granted in 1661 by Governor Fernão de Souza Coutinho to Captain Bernardo Vieira de Mello, a nobleman and knight of the Royal Household, who likely held lands that included Caruaru. A 1758 document recording an investigation into abuses committed by Bernardo’s son, Antônio Vieira de Mello, mentions "…in these my lands a site called Caruru, which my father settled eighty years ago…" (verbatim), dating the establishment of Caruru around 1678, when the area was demarcated and organized as a farm.

The name Caruru likely refers to the region and gave its name to a farm at the heart of what is now the city’s central landmark. Its strategic location and the entrepreneurial spirit of its inhabitants led to significant growth and rapid population increase, necessitating the construction of a [[chapel]] in 1782, dedicated to Our Lady of Conception. This chapel fostered a sense of community and visibility for the residents of the village and surrounding areas, eventually giving rise to the city.<ref name="História">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/historico|title=History of the Municipality of Caruaru|publisher=IBGE|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140225032859/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/historico|archive-date=25 February 2014}}</ref> The chapel’s builder, José Rodrigues de Jesus, was not a native of the area but came from Cabo de Santo Agostinho, son of Plácido Rodrigues de Jesus and Lourença do Vale Pereira. He was married to Maria do Rosário, a native of Vitória de Santo Antão, and they had eleven children. Although it is claimed that the Rodrigues de Sá family is related to the Rodrigues de Jesus, no documentary evidence supports this.

According to the IBGE, Caruaru is a [[Urban hierarchy in Brazil|regional capital]] classified as category B, playing a significant centralizing role in the [[Agreste]] and countryside of Pernambuco. It is a major hub for [[hospital|medical-hospital services]], [[university|academic institutions]], [[culture]], and [[tourism]] in the Agreste.<ref name="Prefeitura">{{cite web|url=https://caruaru.pe.gov.br/historia/|title=About Caruaru|publisher=Caruaru City Hall|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230328082308/https://caruaru.pe.gov.br/historia/|archive-date=28 March 2023}}</ref> The municipality is also renowned for its grand [[Festa Junina|June Festivals]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://penosaojoao.ne10.uol.com.br/cidades/16/caruaru|title=Discover the Charms of Caruaru's São João|publisher=NE10|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222003519/http://penosaojoao.ne10.uol.com.br/cidades/16/caruaru|archive-date=22 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> It hosts the Feira de Caruaru, recognized as the world’s largest open-air market and designated an intangible cultural heritage of Brazil by the [[National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage]] (IPHAN).<ref name="IPHAN">{{cite web|url=http://portal.iphan.gov.br/portal/montarDetalheConteudo.do?id=13457&sigla=Noticia&retorno=detalheNoticia|title=IPHAN Designates Elevador Lacerda, Santos Stock Exchange Building, and Registers the Feira de Caruaru|date=7 December 2006|access-date=9 February 2014|publisher=IPHAN|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070622183510/http://portal.iphan.gov.br/portal/montarDetalheConteudo.do?id=13457&sigla=Noticia&retorno=detalheNoticia|archive-date=22 June 2007}}</ref> Its [[handicraft|clay craftsmanship]] gained worldwide recognition through the work of Vitalino Pereira dos Santos, known as Mestre Vitalino, who represented Pernambuco at the 1955 Brazilian Primitive and Modern Art Exhibition in [[Neuchâtel]], [[Switzerland]]. His works are displayed at the [[Louvre Museum]] in [[Paris]] and at his former residence in the Alto do Moura neighborhood of Caruaru.<ref name="Vitalino">{{cite web|url=http://www.itaucultural.org.br/aplicexternas/enciclopedia_ic/index.cfm?fuseaction=artistas_biografia&cd_verbete=4457|title=Mestre Vitalino (1909–1963)|publisher=Itaú Cultural|access-date=9 February 2014|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114131203/http://www.itaucultural.org.br/aplicexternas/enciclopedia_ic/index.cfm?fuseaction=artistas_biografia&cd_verbete=4457|archive-date=14 November 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://revistadehistoria.com.br/secao/artigos-revista/todos-amam-vitalino|title=Everyone Loves Vitalino|author=Angela Mascelani|work=Revista de História|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120308084411/http://revistadehistoria.com.br/secao/artigos-revista/todos-amam-vitalino|archive-date=8 March 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> Mestre Vitalino’s followers have made Caruaru the largest center of figurative art in the [[Americas]], according to [[UNESCO]].<ref name="UNESCO">{{cite web|title=Caruaru - Capital do Artesanato|url=http://www.unesco.org/|access-date=21 February 2014|publisher=UNESCO|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305061401/http://www.unesco.org/|archive-date=5 March 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Several hypotheses explain the origin of the [[toponym]] Caruaru. One widely accepted theory suggests it derives from the dialect of the [[Cariri people]], who inhabited the region during the 16th-century exploration. In their language, "caru" can mean "food" or "abundance," and "aru aru" can mean "plenty." Thus, Caruaru could translate to "land of abundance."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-caruaru.shtml|title=Caruaru|author=Carlos Ferreira|publisher=Folha Online|access-date=11 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140720235614/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/turismo/americadosul/brasil-caruaru.shtml|archive-date=20 July 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The IBGE’s "Brazilian Territorial Documentation" notes various meanings linked to pathological conditions but adds another definition: the name may come from a plant commonly known as ''caruru'' (a type of amaranth) that once covered a [[well]] on the banks of the [[Ipojuca River]], leading to the area being called Sítio do Caruru. Over time, an additional vowel transformed it into Caruaru. The [[Houaiss Dictionary of the Portuguese Language|Houaiss Dictionary]] lists two meanings: 1) synonymous with ''curuaí'' (from [[Tupi language|Tupi]] ''curuá’i''), the plant ''Orbignya sabulosa'', a type of palm common in the region; 2) synonymous with ''jacuraru'' (from Tupi ''yakurua’u''), a common lizard known as [[tegu]], also prevalent in the area.<ref>{{harvnb|Fonseca|2018|pp=129–130}}</ref>

== History ==

Due to its advantageous geographic position in the heart of the [[Agreste]], a mandatory passage for cattle transport from the ''[[Sertão]]'' to the coast, numerous agro-pastoral properties were soon established. The lands where Caruaru now stands were owned by the Vidal de Souza and Almeida Pereira families. Land sale records and parish documents confirm that the Vidal de Souza family held large tracts along the Ipojuca River, including the Caruru area. José Rodrigues’ wife was a granddaughter of pioneer João Alvares Vidal, mentioned in 1758 as a cattle ranch owner whose farm was attacked by Antônio Vieira de Mello’s henchmen. No documentation clarifies how the Caruru farm passed to José Rodrigues, whether by purchase or inheritance. Baptism records and inventory transcriptions show that José Rodrigues de Jesus was already married by 1774, the year of the earliest recorded birth of one of his children, indicating he was married well before the 1782 inauguration of the Conceição Chapel. With permission from the Olinda Bishopric in 1781, he built the Nossa Senhora da Conceição Chapel,<ref name=":7">{{harvnb|Ferreira|2021|pp=115–118}}</ref> which spurred the creation of a weekly market and became a focal point for new settlers, increasing the population in the central area. Documents from 1794 confirm the existence of a village with a "considerable number of houses," already known by its current name.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=915&Cod=1|title=Municipal History - Caruaru|publisher=State Database - Government of Pernambuco|year=2006|page=2 (C)|access-date=8 April 2023|archive-date=26 September 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220926163828/http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=915&Cod=1|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 1834, Caruaru was listed as the 7th district of [[Bonito, Pernambuco|Bonito]], as noted in a letter dated 8 November of that year from Bonito’s [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]] to the Pernambuco Government Council. Provincial Law No. 133 of 6 May 1844 (some records cite 2 May) created the São Caetano da Raposa district, annexed to the municipality of Caruaru. In 1846, Capuchin missionary Friar Euzébio de Sales from Penha began constructing the main church, now the cathedral. Rebuilt twice, the last time in 1883, the church received a bell that year, still in place today, the largest votive offering in the region. It was a promise by Francisco Gomes de Miranda Leão, who transported the offering on animal backs from Tapera to Caruaru, where it was enthusiastically received by the population. On 16 August 1848, Provincial Law No. 212 elevated Caruaru to the status of village, with territory detached from Bonito. This law transferred the [[Freguesia|parish]] seat from São Caetano da Raposa to Nossa Senhora das Dores in Caruaru, along with the Bonito judicial district seat. Article 3 of the same law divided the judicial district into two municipalities: the first comprising the parishes of Caruaru, [[Bezerros]], and [[Altinho, Pernambuco|Altinho]], and the second including Bonito and [[Panelas]].<ref name=":0" />

The municipal chamber was established on 16 September 1849, as reported in a letter to the province president, by Francisco Xavier de Lima, president of Bonito’s council. The first [[vicar]] of the parish was Father Antonio Jorge Guerra, who established it on 28 September of the same year. On 18 May 1857, Provincial Law No. 416 elevated Caruaru’s village to city and municipal seat status. On 20 May 1867, Provincial Law No. 720 created the Caruaru judicial district, classified as 1st instance by Decree No. 3,978 of 12 October of the same year; the first judge was Antonio Buarque de Lima. On 13 November 1872, Decree No. 5,139 reclassified it as 2nd instance.<ref name=":0" />

Caruaru became a [[municipality]] on 1 March 1893, under Article 2 of the general provisions of State Law No. 52 (Municipal Organic Law) of 3 August 1892. The first elected mayor was [[Major (rank)|Major]] João Salvador dos Santos. A report attached to a 26 May 1893 letter from the mayor to the Government Secretary stated that the municipality was divided into three administrative districts: Caruaru (municipal seat), Carapotós, and [[São Caetano, Pernambuco|São Caetano da Raposa]].<ref name=":0" /><ref name="Formação">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/pernambuco/caruaru.pdf|title=History of the Municipality of Caruaru|publisher=IBGE|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070724160737/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/pernambuco/caruaru.pdf|archive-date=24 July 2007|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Caruaru was established as the second municipality in the Pernambuco Agreste through project No. 20, proposed by provincial deputy Francisco de Paula Batista (1811–1881),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www3.ufpe.br/memoriafdr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=329%3Abiografias-francisco-de-paula-batista&catid=1%3Aprograma&Itemid=243|title=Francisco de Paula Batista (1811–1881)|publisher=Academic Memory Project of the Recife Law School|access-date=7 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190209124329/https://www3.ufpe.br/memoriafdr/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=329%3Abiografias-francisco-de-paula-batista&catid=1%3Aprograma&Itemid=243|archive-date=9 February 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref> debated on 3 April 1857 and enacted without further debate on 18 May 1857 through Provincial Law No. 416, signed by the then vice-president of Pernambuco, Joaquim Pires Machado Portela.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cartaforense.com.br/conteudo/colunas/o-processualista-francisco-de-paula/9343|title=The Proceduralist Francisco de Paula|author=Alessandro Hirata|work=Jornal Carta Forense|access-date=1 March 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120919224043/http://www.cartaforense.com.br/conteudo/colunas/o-processualista-francisco-de-paula/9343|archive-date=19 September 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> Over the decades, the city developed, and the former Caruru Village is now known by various titles such as "Capital of the Agreste," "Capital of Forró," and "Princess of the Agreste," reflecting its political and economic significance in Pernambuco.<ref name="Prefeitura"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/_1998/0604/ec0504i.htm|title=BR-232 Expansion Begins|work=JC Online|access-date=1 March 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010904213308/http://www2.uol.com.br/JC/_1998/0604/ec0504i.htm|archive-date=4 September 2001|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The municipality’s development peaked from 1896 with the construction of the ''Great Western'', a railway connecting the city to the Pernambuco capital. Through its tracks, agricultural products and goods from its traditional market were transported.<ref name="Ferrovia"/> Initiated in 2001 by the Pernambuco government, the expansion of the main highway accessing the municipality, [[BR-232 (Brazil highway)|BR-232]], was pivotal for industrializing its economy and boosting the service sector, as it reduced travel time and increased safety, attracting more tourists during certain periods. The first section, from Recife to Caruaru, began in 2001 and was completed in 2003, followed by the Caruaru-São Caetano section.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jconlineblogs.ne10.uol.com.br/deolhonotransito/tag/br-232/|title=BR-232, Between Recife and Caruaru, Is Visibly Deteriorating|work=JC Online|access-date=1 March 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140426175021/http://jconlineblogs.ne10.uol.com.br/deolhonotransito/tag/br-232/|archive-date=26 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Geography ==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]], the municipal territory spans {{convert|923.150|km2|sqmi}},<ref name="IBGE_Caruaru"/> with {{convert|80.561|km2|sqmi}} classified as [[urban area]]. It is located at 08°17'00" [[south latitude]] and 35°58'34" [[west longitude]],<ref name="geografos.com.br">{{cite web|url=http://www.geografos.com.br/cidades-pernambuco/caruaru.php|title=Coordinates of the Municipality of Caruaru|publisher=Geógrafos|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221212345/http://www.geografos.com.br/cidades-pernambuco/caruaru.php|archive-date=21 February 2014}}</ref> approximately {{convert|130|km|mi}} from the [[Recife|state capital]]. Its neighboring municipalities are [[Brejo da Madre de Deus]] and [[São Caetano, Pernambuco|São Caetano]] to the west; [[Taquaritinga do Norte]], [[Toritama]], [[Vertentes]], and [[Frei Miguelinho]] to the north; [[Riacho das Almas]] and [[Bezerros]] to the east; and [[Altinho, Pernambuco|Altinho]] and [[Agrestina]] to the south.<ref name="Diagnóstico">{{cite web|url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/rehi/atlas/pernambuco/relatorios/CARU043.pdf|title=Environmental Diagnosis of the Municipality of Caruaru|publisher=Brazilian Geological Service (CPRM)|page=2|access-date=9 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090824101002/http://www.cprm.gov.br/rehi/atlas/pernambuco/relatorios/CARU043.pdf|archive-date=24 August 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The municipality lies within the Borborema Province geo-environmental unit, characterized by high massifs and hills with altitudes ranging from 600 to 1,000 meters.<ref name="Diagnóstico"/> The [[terrain|relief]] is predominantly rugged, with deep and dissected valleys, and an average [[elevation]] of 554 meters above [[sea level]].<ref name="Diagnóstico"/> Located on the [[Borborema Plateau]], its highest point is Monte Bom Jesus, at 630 meters above sea level.<ref name="geografos.com.br"/>

The municipal territory is crossed by [[perennial river]]s with low flow and limited groundwater potential. It is part of the [[Ipojuca River]] and [[Capibaribe River]] basins,<ref name="Diagnóstico"/> with major watercourses including the Tabocas, Caiçara, Borba, da Onça, Olho d’água, Mandacaru do Norte, Caparatós, São Bento, Curtume, and Taquara streams. Its main water reservoirs are the Eng°. Gercino de Pontes (13,600,000 m<sup>3</sup>), Taquara (1,100,000 m<sup>3</sup>), Guilherme (786,000 m<sup>3</sup>), Serra dos Cavalos (761,000 m<sup>3</sup>), and Jaime Nejaim (100,000 m<sup>3</sup>) dams.<ref name="Diagnóstico"/>

=== Climate ===
According to the [[Köppen climate classification]], Caruaru's climate is [[Semi-arid climate|semi-arid]] (''BSh''), featuring hot, dry [[summers]] and mild, relatively rainy [[winter]]s.<ref name="SobreCaruaruPE">{{cite web|url=http://www.encontracaruaru.com.br/caruaru/|title=About Caruaru|access-date=5 March 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120921085431/http://www.encontracaruaru.com.br/caruaru/|archive-date=21 September 2012}}</ref> This is due to [[orographic precipitation]] in the [[Borborema Plateau]], between the municipalities of [[Gravatá]] and [[Pombos]], which acts as a barrier, limiting heavier rainfall in Caruaru.<ref name="SobreCaruaruPE"/>

[[Wind]]s are constant year-round, but the arrival of weather systems and the formation of high-level [[Upper tropospheric cyclonic vortex|cyclonic vortices]] off the Northeast coast can cause strong winds, leading to damage such as roof tiles being blown off, fallen trees, and power outages.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/01/ventania-provoca-estragos-e-deixa-uma-pessoa-ferida-no-agreste.html|title=Windstorm Causes Damage and Injures One Person in the Agreste|work=G1 PE|date=25 January 2014 |access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222145520/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/01/ventania-provoca-estragos-e-deixa-uma-pessoa-ferida-no-agreste.html|archive-date=22 February 2014}}</ref> According to the Pernambuco Water and Climate Agency (Apac), which began measurements in March 2010, the lowest recorded temperature in Caruaru was 11.7&nbsp;°C on 21 July 2016,<ref>{{cite web|last=Carmo|first=Emerson Freitas do|url=http://radioculturadonordeste.com.br/caruaru-registra-temperatura-de-135oc/|title=Caruaru Records Temperature of 13.5°C|date=15 August 2017|access-date=13 April 2023|website=Rádio Cultura do Nordeste}}</ref> and the highest was 35.9&nbsp;°C in November 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/canal/cidades/geral/noticia/2019/12/10/veja-quais-sao-os-recordes-de-temperatura-nas-cidades-de-pernambuco-394693.php|title=See the Temperature Records in Pernambuco Cities|date=10 December 2019|access-date=13 April 2023|website=JC Online}}</ref>

=== Ecology and environment ===
Until early 2013, the municipal government lacked a dedicated environmental department, with environmental policies managed solely by the Infrastructure Secretariat. However, following the re-election of Mayor José Queiroz de Lima in 2013, a municipal reform created the Special Secretariat for the Environment, linked directly to the mayor’s office.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.portaldecaruaru.com/conteudo/8254/Caruaru%20vai%20contar%20com%20Secretaria%20Especial%20de%20Meio%20Ambiente#.Uw0affldVZk|title=Caruaru to Have a Special Secretariat for the Environment|publisher=Portal Caruaru|access-date=25 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140301181235/http://www.portaldecaruaru.com/conteudo/8254/Caruaru%20vai%20contar%20com%20Secretaria%20Especial%20de%20Meio%20Ambiente#.Uw0affldVZk|archive-date=1 March 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The predominant [[vegetation]] consists of plants typical of the [[caatinga]] biome, with remnants of [[Atlantic Forest]] in high-altitude wetlands.<ref name="Diagnóstico"/> The municipality has only one conservation unit, the Professor João Vasconcelos Sobrinho Municipal Natural Park, commonly known as Serra dos Cavalos Park, located on the border with [[Altinho, Pernambuco|Altinho]], covering 359 [[hectares]] of protected area.<ref name="wikimapia.org">{{cite web|url=http://wikimapia.org/15296967/reserva-florestal-da-serra-dos-cavalos-Caruaru|title=Serra dos Cavalos Forest Reserve|work=Wikimapia|access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101213013358/http://wikimapia.org/15296967/reserva-florestal-da-serra-dos-cavalos-Caruaru|archive-date=13 December 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The caatinga features hyperxerophytic species, with twisted-branched shrubs and deep-rooted plants. Common species include [[Cactus|cacti]], [[Neoglaziovia|caroá]], [[aroeira]], [[Anadenanthera colubrina|angico]], [[Sarcomphalus|juazeiro]], [[mandacaru]], and [[Pilosocereus polygonus|dildo cactus]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/caatinga.htm|title=Vegetation - Caatinga|work=Sua Pesquisa|access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221006211335/http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/caatinga.htm|archive-date=6 October 2022}}</ref> The Atlantic Forest, typical of the [[Caatinga moist-forest enclaves]] in the southern part of the municipality, comprises medium to large trees, forming a dense, closed forest. Rich in biodiversity, large trees create a microclimate with shade and high humidity. Common species include [[palm trees]], [[bromeliads]], [[begonias]], [[orchids]], [[vines]], [[bryophyte]]s, [[Caesalpinia echinata|Brazilwood]], [[jacaranda]], [[Aspidosperma spruceanum|peroba]], [[Cariniana legalis|jequitibá-rosa]], [[Pinaceae|cedar]], [[andira]], [[pineapple]], and [[fig trees]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/mata_atlantica.htm|title=Atlantic Forest Vegetation|publisher=Sua Pesquisa|access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220930191652/http://www.suapesquisa.com/geografia/vegetacao/mata_atlantica.htm|archive-date=30 September 2022}}</ref>

According to the 2010 IBGE census, Caruaru had {{formatnum:314912}} inhabitants, with a majority being female, totaling {{formatnum:165759}} women (52.5% of the population), and {{formatnum:149153}} men (47.4%). Additionally, {{formatnum:279589}} residents (approximately 88.78%) lived in the [[urban area]], while {{formatnum:35323}} (about 11.2%) resided in the [[rural area]].<ref name="PNUD_Atlas">{{cite web|url=http://atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/perfil_print/caruaru_pe|title=Profile of the Municipality of Caruaru|publisher=PNUD|access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406125630/http://atlasbrasil.org.br/2013/perfil_print/caruaru_pe|archive-date=6 April 2014}}</ref> The [[urbanization]] rate was 88.78%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

Of the total population in 2010, {{formatnum:78066}} residents (24.79%) were under 15 years old, {{formatnum:214848}} (68.22%) were aged 15 to 64, and {{formatnum:21998}} (6.99%) were over 65, with a [[life expectancy]] at birth of 73.0 years and a total [[fertility rate]] of 2.0 children per woman.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> Caruaru’s [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) is 0.677, considered medium by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP),<ref name="IDH">{{cite web|url=http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-Municipios-2010.aspx|title=Municipal HDI Ranking - 2010|work=PNUD|access-date=11 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130921053801/http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-Municipios-2010.aspx|archive-date=21 September 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref> ranking eleventh in the state and above Pernambuco’s average of 0.673.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-UF-2010.aspx|title=State HDI Ranking|work=PNUD|access-date=7 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130921212826/http://www.pnud.org.br/atlas/ranking/Ranking-IDHM-UF-2010.aspx|archive-date=21 September 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref> Despite its state prominence, its HDI is below the national average, ranking 2503rd among Brazil’s {{formatnum:5570}} municipalities.<ref name="IDH" />

=== Poverty and inequality ===
From 2000 to 2010, the number of people with a per capita household income of up to half the minimum wage decreased by 41.6%. In 2010, 82.5% of the population lived above the [[poverty line]], 10.5% were at the poverty line, and 6.9% were below it.<ref name="ODM_1">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|title=1 - End Hunger and Poverty|work=Portal ODM|year=2012|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406130940/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|archive-date=6 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.542, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|title=Municipal Profile|work=Portal ODM|year=2012|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406131050/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|archive-date=6 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The richest 20% of the population accounted for 57.6% of the city’s total income, 16.1 times higher than the 3.84% share of the poorest 20%.<ref name="ODM_1"/>

Christianity, particularly Catholicism, has been significant in Caruaru’s history since at least 1781, when the Our Lady of Conception Chapel was built, marking the institutional beginning of Catholicism in the city.<ref name=":7" /> The [[Diocese of Caruaru]] was established on 7 August 1948 by [[Pope Pius XII]] through the bull ''Quae Maiori Christifidelium''. It is part of the [[Archdiocese of Olinda and Recife]].<ref>{{harvnb|Marques|Torres|2022|pp=438–439, 441}}</ref> The diocese’s [[episcopal see]] is the Our Lady of Sorrows Cathedral, with 29 parishes divided into three pastoral regions: central, northern, and southern.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://teste2cnbbne2.blogspot.com/2011/01/diocese-de-caruaru.html|title=Diocese of Caruaru|date=1 January 2011|access-date=21 April 2023|website=Diocese of Caruaru ~ CNBB Northeast 2}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://diocesedecaruaru2.blogspot.com/2008/07/parquias-da-diocese.html|title=Diocese of Caruaru: Parishes of the Diocese|access-date=21 April 2023|website=Diocese of Caruaru}}</ref> The current bishop, since 2019, is José Ruy Gonçalves Lopes.<ref>{{cite web|last=Azevedo|first=Antônio Marcos de|url=https://www.cbncaruaru.com/artigo/para-bispo-de-caruaru-dom-ruy-o-grande-desafio-para-a-igreja-catolica-e-consolidar-a-fe|title=For Bishop of Caruaru, Dom Ruy, the Great Challenge for the Catholic Church is to Strengthen Faith|date=26 September 2019|access-date=21 April 2023}}</ref> The patron saint of Caruaru is [[Our Lady of Sorrows]], with an annual festival held in her honor.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2022/09/05/diocese-divulga-programacao-da-festa-de-nossa-senhora-das-dores-padroeira-de-caruaru.ghtml|title=Diocese Announces Schedule for the Our Lady of Sorrows Festival, Patron Saint of Caruaru|date=5 September 2022|access-date=21 April 2023|website=G1}}</ref><ref>{{harvnb|CNBB|2000|p=969}}</ref>

The main religious educational institutions in the city are the Catholic schools Sagrado Coração and Diocesano. Sagrado Coração, founded on 8 September 1920 under the initiative of Canon Osvaldo Brasileiro with permission from the Olinda archdiocese, is managed by the Benedictine Missionary Sisters of Tutzing and was the first Catholic school built in Pernambuco’s countryside.<ref>{{harvnb|Marques|Torres|2022|pp=444–447, 449–453}}</ref> The Diocesano, previously called Caruaru Gymnasium and School, was founded on 2 February 1927 by Father Júlio Cabral de Medeiros, José Florêncio de Souza Leão, and Luiz Pessoa da Silva, and is one of the most important schools in Pernambuco’s countryside.<ref>{{harvnb|Rodrigues|2016|p=34}}</ref>

The most influential evangelical denominations in the city are the [[Assemblies of God]], House of Blessing, [[Adventism|Adventists]], and [[Baptist Church|Baptists]]. Less prominent denominations include [[Jehovah’s Witnesses]], representing just over 0.7% of the population, and members of [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]], accounting for about 0.1%.<ref name="Religião2" />

The 2010 IBGE census showed that most Caruaru residents declared themselves Catholic (64.6%), though [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]] (2.0%) and various evangelical denominations (22.1%) are also common. Other religions include African religions such as [[Umbanda]] and [[Candomblé]] (0.3% combined), and Eastern religions such as [[Hinduism]], [[Judaism]], and [[Buddhism]], with the latter having a minimally significant number of adherents (0.1%). Practitioners of [[Western esotericism|esoteric traditions]] are few (0.04%). Those who declared no religion accounted for 8.0%, and atheists 0.3%.<ref name="Religião2">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/pesquisa/23/22107?detalhes=true|title=2010 Census: Sample Results - Religion|work=IBGE|access-date=13 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230416190941/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/pesquisa/23/22107?detalhes=true|archive-date=16 April 2023}}</ref>

== Politics and subdivisions ==
[[File:Prefeitura de Caruaru.jpg|thumb|right|350px|Caruaru City Hall with the Jobson Figueiredo obelisk]]

The municipality is governed by the [[executive power]] and the [[legislative power]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074|title=The Municipal Public Authority and the Constitutional Obligation to Create an Internal Control System|author=Flávio Henrique M. Lima|date=8 February 2006|publisher=JusVi|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090304120846/http://jusvi.com/artigos/20074|archive-date=4 March 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2020, candidate [[Raquel Lyra]] of the [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party]] (PSDB) was re-elected in the first round with 114,466 votes, equivalent to 66.86% of valid votes (excluding blank and null votes). In second place was Delegado Lessa ([[Progressistas|PP]]), with 32,910 votes (19.22%).<ref>{{cite news|date=8 December 2020|title=Raquel Lyra, from PSDB, Re-elected Mayor of Caruaru in the First Round|newspaper=G1|url=https://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/eleicoes/2020/resultado-das-apuracoes/caruaru.ghtml}}</ref> Raquel Lyra is the first woman to be elected mayor of Caruaru.<ref>{{cite news|title=Raquel Lyra Is the First Woman Elected Mayor of Caruaru|url=http://jconline.ne10.uol.com.br/canal/politica/pernambuco/noticia/2016/10/30/raquel-lyra-e-a-primeira-mulher-eleita-prefeita-de-caruaru-258756.php|work=Jornal do Commercio}}</ref>

The municipal legislative power is exercised by the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of 23 councilors elected for four-year terms (per Article 29 of the [[Constitution of Brazil|Constitution]]) and is structured as follows:<ref name="Vereador"/> seven seats for [[Brazilian Social Democracy Party|PSDB]], three for [[Cidadania|CID]], two for [[Brazilian Labour Party (1981)|PTB]], two for [[Progressistas|PP]], two for [[Brazilian Democratic Movement|PMDB]], one for [[Democratic Labour Party (Brazil)|PDT]], one for [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|PSD]], one for [[Democrats (Brazil)|DEM]], one for the [[Republicans (Brazil)|Republicans]], one for [[Podemos (Brazil)|PODE]], one for [[Social Liberal Party (Brazil)|PSL]], and one for [[Republican Party of the Social Order|PROS]].<ref>{{cite news|date=19 December 2016|title=Understanding the Shuffle of Seats in Caruaru's Council After the Party Window|url=https://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/entenda-como-ficou-a-danca-das-cadeiras-na-camara-de-caruaru-apos-a-janela-partidaria/|newspaper=Blog do Mário Flávio}}</ref> The council is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for administration and the executive, particularly the participatory budget (Budget Guidelines Law).<ref name="Vereador">{{cite web|url=http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-caruaru-pe/|title=Councilor to Mayor of Caruaru|date=14 February 2014|publisher=2012 Elections|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121011022945/http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-caruaru-pe/|archive-date=11 October 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The municipality operates under an organic law, promulgated on 5 April 1990 and effective from the same date,<ref name="Vereador2">{{cite web|url=https://www.leismunicipais.com.br/lei-organica/caruaru-pe/2792|title=Organic Law of the Municipality of Caruaru|date=14 February 2014|publisher=Leis Municipais|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140214183358/https://www.leismunicipais.com.br/lei-organica/caruaru-pe/2792|archive-date=14 February 2014}}</ref> and serves as the seat of the Caruaru Judicial District.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://tj-pe.justica.inf.br/noticia/2013/8/comarca-caruaru-realiza-mutirao-vara-tribunal-juri-julga-68-processos|title=Caruaru Judicial District Holds Task Force in Jury Court, Judging 68 Cases|date=14 February 2014|publisher=Pernambuco Court of Justice|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222020229/http://tj-pe.justica.inf.br/noticia/2013/8/comarca-caruaru-realiza-mutirao-vara-tribunal-juri-julga-68-processos|archive-date=22 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> According to the Pernambuco Regional Electoral Court, in 2020, the municipality had 225,164 voters, making it the fourth largest electoral college in [[Pernambuco]] and the largest in the [[Agreste]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tre-pe.jus.br/eleicoes/estatisticas-de-eleitorado/estatistica-do-eleitorado-municipio|title=Voters by Municipality|publisher=TRE-PE|date=4 July 2020|access-date=4 June 2020}}</ref>

Carapotós was created by State Decree-Law No. 3 on 15 November 1896. Lajedo do Cedro was established by State Decree-Law No. 271 on 15 October 1953, and Gonçalves Ferreira by State Decree-Law No. 289 on 30 December 1953.<ref name="História"/> In 2010, the IBGE recorded 24 neighborhoods, with Salgado being the most populous, with {{formatnum:51503}} residents.<ref name="IBGE_BairrosPop">{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela202.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/202/n102/2604106002,2604106003,2604106004,2604106005,2604106006,2604106007,2604106008,2604106009,2604106010,2604106011,2604106012,2604106013,2604106014,2604106015,2604106016,2604106017,2604106018,2604106019,2604106020,2604106021,2604106022,2604106023,2604106024,2604106025/v/allxp/p/last%201/c2/all/c1/0/l/v,p%2Bc2,t%2Bc1|title=Table 202 - == Economy ==
[[File:Tree Map-Atividades Economicas em Caruaru (2017).png|thumb|left|231x231px|Economic activities in Caruaru by number of employees (2017)<ref>{{cite web|url=http://dataviva.info/pt/location/2pe000506/wages|title=Economic Activities in Caruaru by Employees (2017)|access-date=12 April 2023|website=Plataforma DataViva|year=2018|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230412023227/http://dataviva.info/pt/location/2pe000506/wages|archive-date=12 April 2023}}</ref>]]

Caruaru's [[gross domestic product]] is the 181st largest in Brazil and the 5th largest in Pernambuco. According to the 2011 Regional Accounts, the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics calculated the gross GDP at R$3,407,458,000, with R$468,871,000 in taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices.<ref name="PIB"/> The [[GDP per capita]] was R$10,662.30.<ref name="PIB">{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities - 2011|work=IBGE|publisher=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities - 2011|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131219004456/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|archive-date=19 December 2013}}</ref>

In 2010, 68.04% of the population over 18 was [[Workforce|economically active]], with an unemployment rate of 6.70%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> In 2011, there were approximately {{formatnum:8424}} local units and {{formatnum:7857}} active companies and commercial establishments. A total of {{formatnum:74665}} workers were employed, with {{formatnum:65001}} as salaried employees. Salaries and other remunerations totaled R${{formatnum:763186|thousands}}, with an average monthly wage of 1.7 [[minimum wages]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=115&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cestatisticas-do-cadastro-central-de-empresas-2011|title=Central Business Register Statistics - 2011|work=IBGE|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140304192842/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=115&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cestatisticas-do-cadastro-central-de-empresas-2011|archive-date=4 March 2014}}</ref> According to the IBGE, 68.61% of households lived on less than one minimum wage per person monthly, 21.54% earned between one and three minimum wages per person, 2.95% received between three and five minimum wages, 2.02% had incomes above five minimum wages, and 4.88% had no income.<ref name="SIDRA_Rendimento">{{cite web|url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=4&i=P&c=3261|title=Permanent Private Households by Nominal Monthly Per Capita Household Income Classes|publisher=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação de Dados Automática (SIDRA)|year=2010|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406132213/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3261&i=P&opc386=2&nome=on&qtu8=137&digt102=&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=3261&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&qtu11=658&nivt=0&orp=4&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&orc386=3&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&qtu2=5&opn10=0&qtu15=3&sev=96&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&qtu102=14208&opn13=0&poc386=2&sep=26272&orn=1&digt10=&opn11=0&qtu7=36&sec386=9680&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Caruaru&opn102=0&OpcCara=44&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&qtu10=10282&digt11=&opn7=0&decm=99|archive-date=6 April 2014}}</ref>

== Primary sector ==
The primary sector is the least significant in Caruaru's economy. Of the total wealth generated in the municipality, only {{BRL|19699}} come from agriculture and livestock activities,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|title=Produto interno bruto dos municípios - 2011|work=IBGE|accessdate=14 February 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20131219004456/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=125&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cproduto-interno-bruto-dos-municipios-2011|archivedate=19 December 2013}}</ref> while in 2010, 5.86% of the municipality's economically active population was employed in this sector.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> According to the livestock census, in 2012, the municipality had approximately {{formatnum:23250}} cattle, 150 buffalo, {{formatnum:5500}} goats, 460 donkeys, {{formatnum:1850}} horses, 820 mules, and {{formatnum:4200}} sheep. It also had {{formatnum:344810}} poultry (roosters, hens, chickens, and chicks), {{formatnum:276888}} laying hens, producing {{formatnum:6142}} eggs. {{formatnum:2950}} cows were milked, yielding {{formatnum:2360}} liters of milk. {{formatnum:3250}} [[kilogram]]s of honey were produced. There were also {{formatnum:2950}} pigs.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=121&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cpecuaria-2012|title=Pecuária - 2012|work=IBGE|accessdate=14 February 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140304085933/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=121&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cpecuaria-2012|archivedate=4 March 2014}}</ref>

In terms of temporary agriculture, the main crops were [[cassava]] (700 [[tonne]]s and 100 [[hectare]]s cultivated), [[tomato]] (250 tonnes produced and 8 hectares cultivated), [[sweet potato]] (130 tonnes and 20 hectares planted), [[sugarcane]] (23,333 kilograms per hectare and 3 hectares cultivated), [[maize]] (10 tonnes, with 950 hectares planted and 250 harvested), and [[bean]]s (6 tonnes, with 620 hectares planted and 120 harvested).<ref name=":9">{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=123&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Clavoura-temporaria-2012|title=Lavoura temporária - 2012|work=IBGE|accessdate=14 February 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305012054/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=123&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Clavoura-temporaria-2012|archivedate=5 March 2014}}</ref> In terms of permanent agriculture, the following results were obtained: [[banana]] (360 tonnes and 120 hectares cultivated), [[avocado]] (12 tonnes and 4 hectares planted), [[Orange (fruit)|orange]] (8 tonnes and 3 hectares planted), [[mango]] (30 tonnes and 8 hectares cultivated), [[papaya]] (9 tonnes and 3 hectares), [[coconut]] (15,000 fruits and 5 hectares), and [[cashew nut]] (4 tonnes and 30 hectares).<ref name=":9" />

== Secondary sector ==
In 2011, industry was the second largest economic activity in Caruaru. Approximately {{BRL|497573}} of the gross domestic product came from the gross value added of the secondary sector,<ref name="PIB"/> and in 2010, 0.11% of the municipality's workers were employed in the extractive industry, while 22.51% worked in the manufacturing industry.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> The city is renowned for its [[textile industry]], hosting around 12,000 factories of this type, 30,000 points of sale, and generating 140,000 direct and indirect jobs.<ref name="Permanente2">{{cite web|url=http://acic-caruaru.com.br/conheca-caruaru/|title=Conheça Caruaru|work=Associação Comercial e Empresarial de Caruaru|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111031010059/http://acic-caruaru.com.br/conheca-caruaru/|archive-date=31 October 2011}}</ref>

Since the 1980s, Caruaru has been part of the clothing manufacturing hub of the Pernambuco Agreste, the largest of its kind in the Northeast, producing garments sold throughout Brazil and abroad.<ref>{{cite web|last=Esteves|first=Eduarda|url=http://especiais.leiaja.com/descosturandoacrise|title=Descosturando a crise|access-date=19 April 2023|website=LeiaJá|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419000000/http://especiais.leiaja.com/descosturandoacrise|archive-date=19 April 2023}}</ref> Since 2004, the city has had the Caruaru Industrial Park, located between Caruaru and [[Toritama]], consisting of four color-coded modules. In addition to shops and kiosks, the park is home to the local campus of the [[University of Pernambuco]] (UPE).<ref name=":5">{{Harvtxt|Tavares|2016|pp=19–20}}</ref><ref>{{Harvtxt|Silva|2022|p=34}}</ref> It temporarily housed the Caruaru campus of the [[Federal University of Pernambuco]] (UFPE) from 2006 to 2010, the year the Academic Center of the Agreste (CAA) was inaugurated, and the medical course of the same campus until 2020.<ref name=":5" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/blogs/jamildo/2010/08/25/campus-do-agreste-da-ufpe-que-lula-inaugura-sexta-feira-ja-funciona-desde-2006-com-3-mil-alunos/index.html|title=Campus do Agreste da UFPE que Lula inaugura sexta-feira já funciona desde 2006 com 3 mil alunos|date=25 August 2010|access-date=19 April 2023|website=JC|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419000000/https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/blogs/jamildo/2010/08/25/campus-do-agreste-da-ufpe-que-lula-inaugura-sexta-feira-ja-funciona-desde-2006-com-3-mil-alunos/index.html|archive-date=19 April 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ufpe.br/caa/noticias-do-caa/-/asset_publisher/8TgQ0vpyChuQ/content/novo-predio-do-curso-de-medicina-do-campus-do-agreste-sera-inaugurado-na-quinta-feira-10-/40687|title=Novo prédio do curso de Medicina do Campus do Agreste será inaugurado na quinta-feira (10)|date=4 December 2020|access-date=19 April 2023|website=UFPE|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419000000/https://www.ufpe.br/caa/noticias-do-caa/-/asset_publisher/8TgQ0vpyChuQ/content/novo-predio-do-curso-de-medicina-do-campus-do-agreste-sera-inaugurado-na-quinta-feira-10-/40687|archive-date=19 April 2023}}</ref>

Caruaru is home to the second unit of [[Porto Digital]] in the state, known as the "Armazém da Criatividade" (Creativity Warehouse). The unit's construction was announced in May 2014 and inaugurated in October 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/05/porto-digital-sera-estendido-caruaru-com-o-armazem-da-criatividade.html|title=Porto Digital será estendido a Caruaru com o 'Armazém da Criatividade'|date=29 May 2014|access-date=29 May 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140530000803/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/05/porto-digital-sera-estendido-caruaru-com-o-armazem-da-criatividade.html|archive-date=30 May 2014}}</ref> Located in the Nova Caruaru neighborhood, the hub features creation, prototyping, and editorial centers, including 3D printing, graphic design labs, and photography and video studios. The technological complex is part of Porto Digital's decentralization policy and encompasses six areas: entrepreneurship, experimentation, exhibition, education, coworking, and credit.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2015/10/armazem-da-criatividade-do-porto-digital-comeca-funcionar-em-caruaru.html|title='Armazém da Criatividade' do Porto Digital começa a funcionar em Caruaru|date=16 October 2015|access-date=19 April 2023|website=G1|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419000000/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2015/10/armazem-da-criatividade-do-porto-digital-comeca-funcionar-em-caruaru.html|archive-date=19 April 2023}}</ref>

== Tertiary sector ==
[[File:Caruaru Shopping em 2019.jpg|thumb|left|294px|Caruaru Shopping, the first shopping center in the Pernambuco Agreste]]

In 2010, 5.98% of the employed population worked in construction, 0.89% in public utilities, 23.04% in commerce, and 35.74% in services.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> In 2011, {{BRL|2421315}} of the municipal GDP came from the gross value added of the tertiary sector, making it the most significant contributor to Caruaru's economy,<ref name="PIB"/> accounting for approximately 82.4% of the annual wealth produced.<ref name="Perfil"/> The city hosts the largest open-air market in Brazil, the Feira de Caruaru. The market offers a wide variety of products, including [[fruit]]s, [[vegetable]]s, cereals, medicinal herbs, meats, as well as manufactured goods such as [[clothing]], [[footwear]], [[handbag]]s, [[cookware]], furniture, animals, hardware, small items, radios, electronics, and imported goods.<ref name="IPHAN"/> According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics, approximately {{formatnum:21918}} people work in the city's commerce, and about {{formatnum:20181}} are employed in service activities.<ref name="Perfil"/>

The city has two [[shopping center]]s: Caruaru Shopping and Shopping Difusora. The former, previously known as Shopping Center Caruaru (1997–2009) and North Shopping (2009–2015),<ref>{{cite web|last=Marques|first=Juliana|url=http://m.leiaja.com/noticias/2015/07/31/north-shopping-ganha-novo-nome-e-visual|title=North Shopping ganha novo nome e visual|date=31 July 2015|access-date=18 April 2023|website=LeiaJá|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230418000000/http://m.leiaja.com/noticias/2015/07/31/north-shopping-ganha-novo-nome-e-visual|archive-date=18 April 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Harvtxt|Costa|2012|pp=25–26}}</ref> was inaugurated in 1997 and is the first shopping center in the Pernambuco Agreste. Its construction significantly impacted the city's infrastructure, leading to the [[verticalization]] of the Indianapolis neighborhood.<ref name=":4">{{Harvtxt|Almeida|2022|p=467}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Harvtxt|Maia|Marafon|2020|p=69}}</ref> The shopping center is near the Ipojuca Valley University Center, a private higher education institution owned by the [[YDUQS|Wyden Educacional]] group.<ref name=":3" /><ref>{{Harvtxt|Souza|2022|pp=78, 114}}</ref> Shopping Difusora, opened in 2009, was built on the site of a former radio station of the same name that operated there from 1951 to the 1990s.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ipatrimonio.org/caruaru-radio-difusora-de-caruaru/|title=Caruaru – Rádio Difusora|access-date=18 April 2023|website=ipatrimônio|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230418000000/https://www.ipatrimonio.org/caruaru-radio-difusora-de-caruaru/|archive-date=18 April 2023}}</ref> The construction of Shopping Difusora triggered a similar phenomenon along Agamenon Magalhães Avenue, with the establishment of shops, restaurants, and banks.<ref name=":4" /><ref>{{Harvtxt|Silva|Castro|2015|p=3}}</ref>

=== Healthcare ===
[[File:Shopping Difusora - Caruaru - Pernambuco - Brasil.jpg|thumb|right|Shopping and business complex Difusora, the second largest shopping center in Caruaru, hosting several clinics]]

In 2009, the municipality had 176 healthcare facilities, including hospitals, [[emergency department]]s, [[health center]]s, and [[dentistry|dental services]], with 90 private and 86 public establishments.<ref name="Saúde">{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=5&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cservicos-de-saude-2009|title=Serviços de Saúde 2009|work=IBGE|access-date=15 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305061401/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=5&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cservicos-de-saude-2009|archive-date=5 March 2014}}</ref> These facilities provided 627 hospital beds, with 207 in public establishments and 585 in private ones.<ref name="Saúde"/> In 2012, 98.5% of children under one year old were up to date with their vaccinations.<ref name="ODM_4">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/4-reduzir-a-mortalidade-infantil/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|title=4 - reduzir a mortalidade infantil|work=Portal ODM|year=2012|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406133617/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/4-reduzir-a-mortalidade-infantil/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|archive-date=6 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2011, there were {{formatnum:5458}} live births, with an [[infant mortality]] rate of 16.1 deaths per 1,000 live births for children under five.<ref name="ODM_4"/> In 2010, 7.92% of women aged 10 to 17 gave birth, with 0.18% of them aged 10 to 14, and the activity rate in this age group was 7.59%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> In 2012, {{formatnum:67796}} children were weighed by the [[Programa Saúde da Família|Family Health Program]], with 1.2% found to be malnourished.<ref name="ODM_1"/> In 2010, Caruaru's Human Development Index (HDI) for longevity was 0.799.<ref name="IDH" /> According to the [[Ministry of Health (Brazil)|Ministry of Health]], 665 cases of [[AIDS]] were recorded in Caruaru between 1990 and 2012, and between 2001 and 2011, there were {{formatnum:13002}} cases of [[Dengue fever|dengue]], 105 cases of [[leishmaniasis]], and five cases of [[malaria]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/6-combater-a-aids-a-malaria-e-outras-doencas/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|title=6 - In 2013, Caruaru's municipal health network included approximately 46 Family Health Units (USF), with 15 located in rural areas across all districts and 28 in urban areas. The city had six health centers, all in the main district, three regional hospitals, one polyclinic, two hospitals, a SAMU emergency service center, a 24-hour emergency care unit (UPA), and four private hospitals.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.saudecaruaru.pe.gov.br/smscaruaru2013/index.php/mnu-rede-atencao-saude|title=Localização Geográfica dos Estabelecimentos de Saúde|work=Secretaria de Saúde de Caruaru|access-date=15 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223133800/http://www.saudecaruaru.pe.gov.br/smscaruaru2013/index.php/mnu-rede-atencao-saude|archive-date=23 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Caruaru Health Department, in collaboration with the [[Sistema Único de Saúde|SUS]] and other state and federal agencies, is responsible for planning and implementing the municipal public health policy.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.saudecaruaru.pe.gov.br/smscaruaru2013/index.php/homepage|title=Apresentação|author=Secretaria de Saúde de Caruaru|access-date=15 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223133757/http://www.saudecaruaru.pe.gov.br/smscaruaru2013/index.php/homepage|archive-date=23 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Education ===
[[File:UFPE CAA.jpg|thumb|left|UFPE Academic Center of the Agreste]]

==== Indicators ====
In the field of education, the [[Basic Education Development Index]] (IDEB) average for Caruaru's public schools in 2011 was 3.7 (on a scale from 1 to 10), with fifth-grade students scoring 4.3 and ninth-grade students scoring 3.2, compared to a national public school average of 4.0.<ref name="ODM_2">{{cite web|url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/2-educacao-basica-de-qualidade-para-todos/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|title=2 - educação básica de qualidade para todos|work=Portal ODM|year=2012|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140406134902/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/2-educacao-basica-de-qualidade-para-todos/BRA002026045/caruaru---pe|archive-date=6 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Human Development Index (HDI) for education in 2010 was 0.569.<ref name="IDH"/>

In 2010, 5.09% of children aged six to fourteen were not attending [[primary education]].<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> The completion rate for youths aged 15 to 17 was 38.0%, and the literacy rate for youths and adolescents aged 15 to 24 was 95.0%. The age-grade distortion in primary education, i.e., students older than the recommended age, was 21.3% for the early years and 32.7% for the later years, while in secondary education, the distortion reached 37.6%.<ref name="ODM_2"/> Among residents aged 18 or older, 46.81% had completed primary education, 30.94% had completed [[secondary education]], and the population had an average of 8.89 expected years of schooling.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

In 2010, according to census sample data, {{formatnum:93954}} inhabitants attended [[daycare]]s and/or schools. Of these, {{formatnum:2645}} were in daycares, {{formatnum:9195}} in early childhood education, {{formatnum:5320}} in literacy classes, {{formatnum:1073}} in adult literacy programs, {{formatnum:47759}} in primary education, {{formatnum:12632}} in secondary education, {{formatnum:3973}} in adult primary education, {{formatnum:3221}} in adult secondary education, 498 in postgraduate specialization, {{formatnum:7515}} in undergraduate programs, 114 in [[master's degree]] programs, and nine in [[doctoral degree]] programs. A total of {{formatnum:220958}} people did not attend schools, with {{formatnum:37170}} never having attended and {{formatnum:183788}} having attended at some point.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=105&codmun=260410|title=Censo Demográfico 2010: Resultados da Amostra - Educação|author=Cidades@ - IBGE|year=2010|access-date=6 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140407080408/http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=105&codmun=260410|archive-date=7 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2021, the municipality had {{formatnum:73960}} enrollments in educational institutions, with 198 schools offering primary education, including 8 state public schools, at least 106 municipal schools, and a minimum of 75 private schools.<ref name="Educação">{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/pesquisa/13/78117?indicador=77881|title=Censo escolar - sinopse|access-date=6 April 2014|website=IBGE|year=2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230416163042/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/pe/caruaru/pesquisa/13/78117?indicador=77881|archive-date=16 April 2023}}</ref>{{Refn|When cataloging schools offering primary education, IBGE distinguishes between those offering early and later years. Since some schools offer both levels and IBGE does not specify how many, only a minimum number of schools is provided.}} Caruaru has two public libraries, the Aleixo Leite Filho Municipal Library and the Álvaro Lins Municipal Library.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/noticia/13/02/2014/bibliotecas-municipais-passarao-por-organizacao-.html|title=Bibliotecas Municipais passarão por organização e controle patrimonial|access-date=16 August 2016|work=Prefeitura Municipal de Caruaru|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160915172612/http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/noticia/13/02/2014/bibliotecas-municipais-passarao-por-organizacao-.html|archive-date=15 September 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==== Higher education ====
[[File:FACITEC-UPE.jpg|thumb|right|University of Pernambuco (UPE) - Faculty of Science and Technology of Caruaru (FACITEC)]]

Caruaru is home to campuses of three major state universities: the [[University of Pernambuco]] (UPE),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://caruaru.upe.br/|title=Campus Caruaru|work=UPE Caruaru|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220803144519/https://caruaru.upe.br/|archive-date=3 August 2022}}</ref> offering degrees in Information Systems and Business Administration (with a focus on fashion marketing); the [[Federal University of Pernambuco]] (UFPE), providing undergraduate programs in areas such as [[Pedagogy]], [[Business Administration]], [[Design]] (with emphases in graphic, fashion, and product design), [[Civil Engineering]], [[Economics]], [[Medicine]], [[Communication studies|Communication Studies]] (with emphases in digital media and cultural production), [[Production Engineering]], and teaching degrees in [[Physics]], [[Chemistry]], and [[Mathematics]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ufpe.br/proacad/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=198&Itemid=138|title=Campus Caruaru|author=UFPE Caruaru|access-date=14 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100210111545/http://www.ufpe.br/proacad/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=198&Itemid=138|archive-date=10 February 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> and the [[Federal Institute of Pernambuco]] (IFPE), offering a degree in [[Mechanical Engineering]]. These higher education institutions make Caruaru a regional educational hub, attracting numerous students from neighboring cities and states, either relocating to the municipality or commuting daily.<ref name="Prefeitura"/>

=== Public safety and crime ===
As is common throughout much of Pernambuco, violence is a significant issue in Caruaru.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/03/04/secretaria-de-defesa-social-registra-alto-indice-de-violencia-em-caruaru/|title=Secretaria de Defesa Social registra alto índice de violência em Caruaru|work=NE10|date=4 March 2013|access-date=15 February 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140216033832/http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/03/04/secretaria-de-defesa-social-registra-alto-indice-de-violencia-em-caruaru/|archive-date=16 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2012, the rate of violent crime per {{formatnum:100000}} inhabitants was 39.60. By 2021, Caruaru's rate of violent crime had decreased to 32.09.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1028&Cod=3|title=Número de vítimas e taxa de criminalidade violenta letal e intencional|access-date=15 February 2014|author=Agência Condepe/Fidem|year=2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223193258/http://www.bde.pe.gov.br/visualizacao/Visualizacao_formato2.aspx?CodInformacao=1028&Cod=3|archive-date=23 February 2014}}</ref> In 2008, there were 27 recorded suicides, resulting in a rate of 9.2 suicides per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking 427th nationally and 11th in the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls|title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de suicídio nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais|work=Sangari|year=2011|access-date=15 February 2014|format=xls|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110301222951/http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/STN.xls|archive-date=1 March 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> In the same year, there were approximately 108 workplace accidents, with a rate of 36.7 per 100,000 inhabitants, placing Caruaru 313th nationally and 3rd in the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls|title=Número e taxas (em 100 mil) de Óbitos Ac.Transporte nos municípios com 10.000 habitantes ou mais|access-date=15 February 2014|work=Sangari|year=2011|format=xls|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110301223018/http://www.sangari.com/mapadaviolencia/tabelas2011/TTN.xls|archive-date=1 March 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The Pacto pela Vida program, launched by the Pernambuco state government in 2007, addressed the state's high violent crime rate of 55.0 per 100,000 inhabitants at the time.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Sobral|Porto|Morais|Lima|2017|pp=5, 7–8}}</ref> By April 2013, the violent crime rate had fallen to 35.0, a reduction of 20.0 points. In Caruaru, the homicide rate per 100,000 inhabitants was 15.9 in 2007,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-12029.pdf|title=Boletim Trimestral da Conjuntura Criminal em Pernambuco - 1° trimestre de 2008|access-date=16 February 2014|website=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|year=2013|page=15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223133845/http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-12029.pdf|archive-date=23 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> dropping to 8.38 in the second quarter of 2013,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-46001.pdf|title=Boletim Trimestral da Conjuntura Criminal em Pernambuco - 2° semestre de 2013|access-date=16 February 2014|website=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|year=2013|page=12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223140857/http://www.condepefidem.pe.gov.br/c/document_library/get_file?p_l_id=20018&folderId=141973&name=DLFE-46001.pdf|archive-date=23 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> and further to 6.12 in the third quarter of 2022.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sds.pe.gov.br/images/media/1671474647_BOLETIM%203%20TRIMESTRE%202022.pdf|title=Boletim Trimestral da Conjuntura Criminal em Pernambuco - 3º Trimestre de 2022|access-date=18 April 2023|website=Secretaria de Defesa Social de Pernambuco|year=2022|page=12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230418221749/https://www.sds.pe.gov.br/images/media/1671474647_BOLETIM%203%20TRIMESTRE%202022.pdf|archive-date=18 April 2023}}</ref>

Caruaru is home to the Juiz Plácido de Souza Penitentiary, located in the Vassoural neighborhood in the southern zone, part of Pernambuco's prison system, which includes 17 other penitentiaries across the state.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.seres.pe.gov.br/unidade/24/penitenciaria-juiz-placido-de-souza|title=Penitenciária Juiz Plácido de Souza|author=Secretaria de Ressocialização|year=2013|access-date=16 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130211082829/http://www.seres.pe.gov.br/unidade/24/penitenciaria-juiz-placido-de-souza|archive-date=11 February 2013}}</ref> With a capacity of 350 inmates, it housed {{formatnum:1498}} prisoners as of August 2013, exceeding its capacity by ten times.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/tag/penitenciaria-juiz-placido-de-souza/|title=Depois de visita à PJPS, OAB aponta melhorias e aposta em mutirão para diminuir superlotação|access-date=16 February 2014|website=Blog do Mário Flávio|year=2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222162029/http://blogdomarioflavio.com.br/vs1/tag/penitenciaria-juiz-placido-de-souza/|archive-date=22 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The penitentiary offers a rehabilitation program for 60% of its inmates, including literacy courses, vocational training, supplementary education, handicraft production, clothing manufacturing, and sports activities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cnj.jus.br/atos-administrativos/atos-da-presidencia/resolucoespresidencia/96-noticias/8491-ressocializacao-atende-60-dos-detentos-em-presidio-de-caruaru-pe|title=Ressocialização atende 60% dos detentos em presídio de Caruaru (PE)|author=CNJ-PE|year=2013|access-date=16 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221144529/http://www.cnj.jus.br/atos-administrativos/atos-da-presidencia/resolucoespresidencia/96-noticias/8491-ressocializacao-atende-60-dos-detentos-em-presidio-de-caruaru-pe|archive-date=21 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In March 2017, the British magazine ''[[The Economist]]'' ranked Caruaru as the third most violent city in Brazil and one of the most violent in the world.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.economist.com/blogs/graphicdetail/2017/03/daily-chart-23|title=The world's most dangerous cities|access-date=4 April 2017|newspaper=The Economist|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170404000000/http://www.economist.com/blogs/graphicdetail/2017/03/daily-chart-23|archive-date=4 April 2017}}</ref> In the same year, the municipal program "Juntos pela Segurança" was launched, resulting in a 50% reduction in homicides and a 70% reduction in violent property crimes between 2017 and 2021.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cbnrecife.com/blogdoelielson/artigo/raquel-lyra-promete-implantar-programa-juntos-pela-seguranca-no-estado|title=Raquel Lyra promete implantar programa Juntos pela Segurança no estado|date=7 July 2022|access-date=11 March 2023|website=CBN Recife|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230311000000/https://www.cbnrecife.com/blogdoelielson/artigo/raquel-lyra-promete-implantar-programa-juntos-pela-seguranca-no-estado|archive-date=11 March 2023}}</ref> During this period, the violent crime rate in the municipality decreased from 57.06 to 33.79.<ref>{{cite web|last=Tenório|first=Augusto|url=https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/colunas/jamildo/2022/05/15004650-a-seguranca-nas-cidades-dos-tres-ex-prefeitos-que-concorrem-ao-governo-de-pernambuco.html|title=A violência nas cidades dos três ex-prefeitos que concorrem ao Governo de Pernambuco|date=8 May 2022|access-date=18 April 2023|website=JC|language=pt-br|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230418000000/https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/colunas/jamildo/2022/05/15004650-a-seguranca-nas-cidades-dos-tres-ex-prefeitos-que-concorrem-ao-governo-de-pernambuco.html|archive-date=18 April 2023}}</ref>

=== Housing, services, and communications ===
In 2010, according to IBGE, Caruaru had {{formatnum:96304}} permanent private households, including {{formatnum:86795}} houses, {{formatnum:8728}} apartments, 397 houses in gated communities or condominiums, and 384 tenement houses. Of these, {{formatnum:66524}} were owned, with {{formatnum:64103}} fully paid, {{formatnum:2421}} under acquisition, and {{formatnum:24973}} [[Renting|rented]]. Additionally, 548 households were provided by employers, and {{formatnum:3984}} were occupied in other ways. Another 275 residences were occupied differently. Most of the municipality has access to treated water, [[municipal solid waste|waste collection]], sewage, [[electricity]], [[landline telephone]], and [[mobile phone]] services. In the same year, {{formatnum:85522}} households were supplied with [[drinking water]] from the general network, {{formatnum:95964}} had direct electricity access, {{formatnum:91993}} received waste collection services, and {{formatnum:92289}} had exclusive bathrooms.<ref name="População_característica">{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=67&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Ccenso-demografico-2010:-resultados-do-universo-caracteristicas-da-populacao-e-dos-domicilios-|title=Censo demográfico 2010: resultados do universo - características da população e dos domicílios|work=IBGE|year=2013|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305130928/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=67&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Ccenso-demografico-2010:-resultados-do-universo-caracteristicas-da-populacao-e-dos-domicilios-|archive-date=5 March 2014}}</ref>

The Pernambuco Sanitation Company (Compesa) is responsible for sewage collection and treatment, as well as water supply for Caruaru and all of Pernambuco. In late 2012, the municipalities of the [[Ipojuca River]] valley received a $200 million investment from a loan by the [[Inter-American Development Bank]] (IDB) to the Pernambuco government. Eighty percent of the funds were allocated to sanitation works, as sewage discharge is the main source of river pollution, with the remaining 20% for educational campaigns. In 2013, according to a study by Instituto Trata Brasil, Caruaru had one of the worst sewage collection and treatment rates in the state, collecting only 40% of wastewater and treating 20%. However, with the state investment, it is expected that the city will approach 100% coverage by 2019 upon completion of the Ipojuca environmental sanitation works, as the river is currently the third most polluted in Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/10/14/caruaru-esta-entre-as-cidades-com-piores-indices-de-saneamento-basico-no-pais/|title=Caruaru está entre as cidades com piores índices de saneamento básico no país|author=Rádio Jornal|date=14 October 2013|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222015202/http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2013/10/14/caruaru-esta-entre-as-cidades-com-piores-indices-de-saneamento-basico-no-pais/|archive-date=22 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://blogs.diariodepernambuco.com.br/meioambiente/tag/rio-ipojuca/|title=Arquivos da Tag: Rio Ipojuca|work=Diário de Pernambuco|date=19 October 2011|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120719215849/http://blogs.diariodepernambuco.com.br/meioambiente/tag/rio-ipojuca/|archive-date=19 July 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Pernambuco Energy Company (Celpe) supplies electricity to Caruaru and all 184 municipalities of Pernambuco, as well as the municipality of [[Pedras de Fogo]] in [[Paraíba]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://servicos.neoenergiapernambuco.com.br/a-celpe/Paginas/Quem%20Somos/Quem-Somos.aspx|title=Quem Somos|author=Companhia Energética de Pernambuco|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210923033037/https://servicos.neoenergiapernambuco.com.br/a-celpe/Paginas/Quem%20Somos/Quem-Somos.aspx|archive-date=23 September 2021}}</ref> Approximately 99.7% of Caruaru's households receive electricity services.<ref name="População_característica"/>

Several television channels operate in the [[Very High Frequency|VHF]] and [[Ultra High Frequency|UHF]] bands, with some affiliates based in the city, such as [[TV Jornal Interior]] (affiliated with [[Sistema Brasileiro de Televisão|SBT]]), founded in 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://portalbsd.com.br/novo/terrestres_channels.php?channels=169|title=Listando canais de Caruaru - PE|work=Portal BSD|date=17 March 2013|access-date=9 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140422024115/http://portalbsd.com.br/novo/terrestres_channels.php?channels=169|archive-date=22 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> Caruaru also is home to [[TV Asa Branca]], affiliated with [[Rede Globo]] in the Pernambuco Agreste, established on 1 August 1991, with its signal reaching much of the state's countryside.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sucom.redeglobo.com.br/atlas2004/mapas/php/con_emissora.php?pexib=CRR&puf=PE|title=Cobertura do sinal da TV Asa Branca|work=TV Globo|year=2014|access-date=24 February 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130624064746/http://sucom.redeglobo.com.br/atlas2004/mapas/php/con_emissora.php?pexib=CRR&puf=PE|archive-date=24 June 2013}}</ref> The city has several circulating newspapers, including traditional ones such as "Diário de Pernambuco," "Jornal Extra de Pernambuco," "Jornal do Commercio," "Tribuna de Pernambuco," and "Vanguarda."<ref name="netpapers">{{cite web|url=http://www.netpapers.com/jornais-por-cidade/caruaru|title=Jornais de Caruaru|work=Netpapers|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221153440/http://www.netpapers.com/jornais-por-cidade/caruaru|archive-date=21 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> Caruaru also is home to traditional radio stations such as "Liberdade" AM and FM, "Jornal," and "Cultura do Nordeste."

=== Transportation ===

==== Air ====
The nearest airport is the [[Recife/Guararapes–Gilberto Freyre International Airport]], also known as Guararapes or Gilberto Freyre, located in the Imbiribeira neighborhood in southern Recife, approximately 138&nbsp;km from downtown Caruaru, accessible primarily via the [[BR-232 (Brazil highway)|BR-232]] highway. This airport, serving most municipalities in eastern Pernambuco, including the Pernambuco Forest and Agreste Pernambucano regions, has the best infrastructure, the longest runway, the largest physical space, and the most advanced technology in the [[North Region, Brazil|North]]/[[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeast]] of Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.aeroportorecife.net/|title=Aeroporto de Recife|work=Aeroporto Recife|access-date=17 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230407151416/https://www.aeroportorecife.net/|archive-date=7 April 2023}}</ref> It is considered the most efficient airport in Brazil and the second most efficient in [[South America]], behind [[José Joaquín de Olmedo International Airport]] in [[Guayaquil]], [[Ecuador]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.worldairportawards.com/awards_2012/rgnal_samerica.htm|title=Airline travellers rate Guayaquil Airport as the Best Regional Airport in South America|work=World Airport Awards|year=2012|access-date=18 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130318085235/http://www.worldairportawards.com/awards_2012/rgnal_samerica.htm|archive-date=18 March 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Caruaru also has its own airport, [[Caruaru Airport|Oscar Laranjeira]]. Inaugurated in 1944, it primarily operated executive flights for most of its history. On 25 February 2002, its first commercial flight was scheduled, but the plane got stuck in the runway asphalt, which collapsed. After renovations, the airport operated commercial flights between 2006–2007 and 2010–2011, but these were discontinued due to low demand.<ref name=":6">{{Harvtxt|Nascimento|2019|pp=19–21}}</ref> In July 2018, the airport was closed by the [[National Civil Aviation Agency of Brazil|ANAC]]. However, on 11 November 2020, commercial operations resumed at Oscar Laranjeira with [[Azul Brazilian Airlines]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodepernambuco.com.br/noticia/vidaurbana/2022/11/aeroportos-de-caruaru-e-serra-talhada-completam-dois-anos-de-operacao.html|title=Aeroportos de Caruaru e Serra Talhada completam dois anos de operação comercial|date=11 November 2022|access-date=19 April 2023|website=Diario de Pernambuco|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419000000/https://www.diariodepernambuco.com.br/noticia/vidaurbana/2022/11/aeroportos-de-caruaru-e-serra-talhada-completam-dois-anos-de-operacao.html|archive-date=19 April 2023}}</ref> The airport is managed by the state government, which has invested in its infrastructure.<ref name=":6" /><ref>{{Harvtxt|Santos|2018|p=28}}</ref>

==== Rail and metro ====
There were plans for the Transnordestina railway to pass through Caruaru, connecting the [[Suape Port]] in [[Ipojuca]] (southern coast of Pernambuco) to [[Eliseu Martins]] in the [[Piauí]] [[cerrado]], with another line linking [[Salgueiro]] (central ''[[sertão]]'') to the [[Port of Pecém]] in [[São Gonçalo do Amarante, Ceará|São Gonçalo do Amarante]], [[Ceará]]. However, a new route map presented in 2009 excluded Caruaru, passing instead through part of the Mata Sul Pernambucana region.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.carlosbritto.com/roteiro-da-transnordestina-prejudica-pernambuco/|title=Roteiro da Transnordestina prejudica Pernambuco|author=Carlos Britto|date=30 September 2009|access-date=18 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091214155400/http://www.carlosbritto.com/roteiro-da-transnordestina-prejudica-pernambuco/|archive-date=14 December 2009}}</ref> The city retains the railway line of the ''Great Western'', built by the British in 1896, primarily to transport goods such as beans, leather, cotton, and cheese to Recife, and to support one of the region's largest cattle fairs.<ref name="Ferrovia">{{cite web|url=http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar./index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=268&Itemid=186|title=Great Western|work=Fundação Joaquim Nabuco|access-date=20 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305061401/http://basilio.fundaj.gov.br/pesquisaescolar./index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=268&Itemid=186|archive-date=5 March 2014}}</ref>

==== Road ====
[[File:Rodoviária de Caruaru, Pernambuco.jpg|thumb|left|Buses parked at the city's bus terminal]]

The city's bus terminal, located in the Caiucá neighborhood, is partially adapted for people with disabilities. It features basic amenities, including male and female restrooms (the latter with a changing room), a 24-hour taxi service, seven public telephones at the entrance and near the restrooms, as well as snack bars and convenience stores. Major bus companies operating at the terminal include Borborema, Caruaruense, Coletivo Turismo, Viação Cruzeiro, Guanabara, Itapemirim, Progresso, Expresso São Luiz, and Gontijo.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.socicam.com.br/terminais/terminais_rodoviarios.php?ID=318|title=Pernambuco - Terminal Rodoviário Caruaru|work=Socicam|access-date=25 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130811013605/http://www.socicam.com.br/terminais/terminais_rodoviarios.php?ID=318|archive-date=11 August 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Caruaru is well-connected by highways to neighboring cities, the state capital, and other [[Northeast Region, Brazil|Northeastern]] capitals. The main highways are [[BR-104 (Brazil highway)|BR-104]] and [[BR-232 (Brazil highway)|BR-232]]. The former starts in [[Macau, Rio Grande do Norte|Macau]], [[Rio Grande do Norte]], and ends in [[Maceió]], [[Alagoas]], with an 86-km dual carriageway section between [[Agrestina]] and [[Toritama]]. The latter begins in central Recife and ends in [[Parnamirim, Pernambuco|Parnamirim]] in the Pernambuco ''[[sertão]]'', with a dual carriageway section from Recife to [[São Caetano, Pernambuco|São Caetano]]. Additionally, the PE-095 connects to [[Limoeiro]], and the PE-145 links to [[Brejo da Madre de Deus]].<ref name="der.pe.gov.br">{{cite web|url=http://www.der.pe.gov.br/web/der/rodovias-do-estado;jsessionid=10EC1B50C4E4EF0B6FAF305D11B890E3.jvm2i1|title=Rodovias do Estado|work=Departamento de Estradas de Rodagem de Pernambuco|access-date=20 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140228122030/http://www.der.pe.gov.br/web/der/rodovias-do-estado;jsessionid=10EC1B50C4E4EF0B6FAF305D11B890E3.jvm2i1|archive-date=28 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==== Urban ====
The Destra, the municipal authority for public safety, traffic, and transportation, is responsible for planning, regulating, controlling, and overseeing traffic, as well as managing public transportation services, citizen and municipal property safety, and permanent civil defense actions against natural, human-caused, and mixed disasters.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/secretaria/destra-autarquia-municipal-de-defesa-social-transito-e-transportes|title=DESTRA – Autarquia Municipal de Defesa Social, Trânsito e Transportes|work=Prefeitura Municipal de Caruaru|access-date=12 June 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140525034406/http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/secretaria/destra-autarquia-municipal-de-defesa-social-transito-e-transportes|archive-date=25 May 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> Several bus companies, including Tabosa, Bahia, Coletivo, Caruaruense, and Capital do Agreste, transport thousands of passengers daily.

In early 2014, Caruaru's city council approved a [[Bus Rapid Transit|BRT]] project to improve urban mobility, particularly in the city center.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/01/vereadores-aprovam-projeto-que-inclui-o-brt-em-caruaru-no-agreste.html|title=Vereadores aprovam projeto que inclui o BRT, em Caruaru, no Agreste|work=G1|date=23 January 2014|access-date=20 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140130071011/http://g1.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2014/01/vereadores-aprovam-projeto-que-inclui-o-brt-em-caruaru-no-agreste.html|archive-date=30 January 2014}}</ref> The project includes an exclusive bus corridor, similar to a metro system, with accessibility for the elderly and disabled, air-conditioned environments, and a less polluting system. This initiative aims to enhance urban mobility with a public transport corridor from the Rendeiras neighborhood to Alto do Moura, accompanied by a bike lane and 500 paved and sanitized streets.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://portalmidiaurbana.com/entenda-como-vai-funcionar-o-projeto-brt-e-as-consequencias-para-caruaru/|title=Entenda como vai funcionar o projeto BRT e as consequências para Caruaru|author=Portal Mídia Urbana|date=2013|access-date=20 February 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140228214856/http://portalmidiaurbana.com/entenda-como-vai-funcionar-o-projeto-brt-e-as-consequencias-para-caruaru/|archive-date=28 February 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 2022, the municipal vehicle fleet consisted of {{formatnum:196896}} vehicles, including {{formatnum:80617}} cars, {{formatnum:5521}} trucks, 812 tractor-trucks, {{formatnum:11858}} pickups, {{formatnum:4739}} vans, 907 minibuses, {{formatnum:66338}} motorcycles, {{formatnum:16989}} scooters, 771 buses, and {{formatnum:8344}} other vehicle types.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=110&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cfrota-2012|title=Frota - 2022|access-date=16 April 2023|work=IBGE|year=2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230416193329/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=260410&idtema=110&search=pernambuco%7Ccaruaru%7Cfrota-2012|archive-date=16 April 2023}}</ref>

== Performing arts and events ==
Theater in Caruaru emerged under religious influence, as priests were the first to promote this art form in the city during the transition from the 19th to the 20th century. At that time, the Church used theatrical plays to engage with the population. In the first half of the 20th century, cine-theaters were established, which were cinemas that also hosted theatrical performances. The main venues were the Cine Theatro Rio Branco (founded in 1922) and the Cine Theatro Santa Rosa (1947). With Caruaru's economic growth from the 1990s onward, most of these cinemas closed, and today, the only cinemas in the city are commercial ones located in shopping malls.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Junior |first=Frank |title=A brief history of Caruaru's cinemas |url=https://medium.com/a-ponte/um-breve-hist%C3%B3rico-dos-cinemas-de-caruaru-2b369a42ba48 |access-date=2023-05-01 |website=A Ponte |date=2018-09-27 |language=en |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230501000001/https://medium.com/a-ponte/um-breve-hist%C3%B3rico-dos-cinemas-de-caruaru-2b369a42ba48 |archive-date=2023-05-01}}</ref> However, the city currently has three theaters: Teatro João Lyra Filho, Teatro Rui Limeira Rosal, and Teatro Difusora.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Guerra|2015|pp=57–63}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |title=Curso de Comunicação Social (Bacharelado) - Campus Caruaru |journal=UFPE |year=2018 |page=18 |url=https://www.ufpe.br/documents/501736/2335017/Ata_RO+07.18+-+12.09.18.pdf/d0f8c584-a32d-4839-913f-5cd4e218493a |ref={{harvid|UFPE|2018}}}}</ref>

Since 1981,<ref>{{Harvtxt|Vilela|2018|p=27}}</ref> Caruaru has hosted the FETEAG – Agreste Theater Festival, an event independently organized by the Teatro Experimental de Arte (TEA) group, with support from Funcultura, the [[Serviço Social do Comércio|Social Service of Commerce]] (Sesc), and the Culture Foundations of Recife and Caruaru. The festival, which began modestly, is now held in both Recife and Caruaru, having presented over a thousand performances and attracted a total audience of more than 200,000 people.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bento |first=Em thatuel |title=31st Agreste Theater Festival celebrates artists with a program of 20 shows; check it out |url=https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/cultura/2022/09/15079152-31-festival-de-teatro-do-agreste-celebra-artistas.html |access-date=2023-04-20 |website=JC |date=2022-09-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230420000001/https://jc.ne10.uol.com.br/cultura/2022/09/15079152-31-festival-de-teatro-do-agreste-celebra-artistas.html |archive-date=2023-04-20}}</ref> It features both a student and a professional exhibition and is a highlight of Caruaru's artistic scene.<ref>{{Cite journal |title=Recife e Caruaru recebem festival de teatro |journal=Revista Continente Multicultural |year=2022 |issue=261 |pages=39–40 |url={{Google books|plainurl=y|id=pXeHEAAAQBAJ|page=39}} |ref={{harvid|Continente Multicultural|2022}}}}</ref><ref name=":8">{{Harvtxt|Mélo|Rêgo|Santos|Silva|2019|p=4}}</ref> In addition to FETEAG, the city hosts the Caruaru Film Festival, an annual event since 2013 aimed at promoting independent audiovisual production and developing the local market.<ref name=":8" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=From Caruaru to the screens of the world |url=https://verborevista.wordpress.com/2017/12/03/de-caruaru-para-as-telas-do-mundo/ |access-date=2023-05-01 |website=Revista Verbo |date=2017-12-03 |first1=Kadu|last1= Ferraz|first2= Adson|last2= Emanuel|first3= Caio|last3= César|first4= Alex|last4= Vinícius |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230501000002/https://verborevista.wordpress.com/2017/12/03/de-caruaru-para-as-telas-do-mundo/ |archive-date=2023-05-01}}</ref>

Although not a major strength of the city, Caruaru has several [[carnival block]]s that parade through its central streets as a preview of the [[Recife]] and [[Olinda]] carnivals. The main carnival block is organized by Byron Lasserre, owner of Bar Confraria do Sucata, on João Condé Street in the city center.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Caruaru also gets into the Carnival spirit |url=http://www.folhape.com.br/cms/opencms/folhape/pt/edicaoimpressa/arquivos/2012/Fevereiro/08_02_2012/0123.html |access-date=2014-05-11 |website=Folha de Pernambuco |date=2014-02-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120211060040/http://www.folhape.com.br/cms/opencms/folhape/pt/edicaoimpressa/arquivos/2012/Fevereiro/08_02_2012/0123.html |archive-date=2012-02-11 |url-status=dead}}</ref> On 15 September, the city celebrates the feast of its patron saint, [[Our Lady of Sorrows]], with masses, processions, and religious and artistic performances. The celebration takes place at the Matrix Church, dedicated to the saint.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Feast of the patron saint of Caruaru, Agreste, will feature masses, a procession, and shows |url=http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2012/09/14/festa-da-padroeira-de-caruaru-agreste-tera-missas-procissao-e-shows/ |access-date=2014-02-22 |website=Radio Jornal |date=2012-09-14 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230409233754/http://radiojornal.ne10.uol.com.br/2012/09/14/festa-da-padroeira-de-caruaru-agreste-tera-missas-procissao-e-shows/ |archive-date=2023-04-09}}</ref>

Alto do Moura is a neighborhood located 7&nbsp;km from downtown Caruaru, known as the home of numerous internationally recognized artisans. It remains one of the most significant centers of figurative arts in the Americas. Visitors can explore museums, workshops, regional cuisine restaurants, and bars, as well as cultural attractions based on the regional event calendar. The neighborhood is also home to a distinguished resident, Manuel Eudócio, who was awarded the title of Living Heritage of Pernambuco by Fundarpe.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions">{{Cite web |title=Tourist Attractions |url=http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/?pg=pontosturisticos |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Caruaru City Hall |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140224005402/http://www.caruaru.pe.gov.br/?pg=pontosturisticos |archive-date=2014-02-24 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Caruaru boasts several museums that narrate the city's history and showcase the works of its most notable figures, who have established the region as a significant cultural hub. One of the most famous, the Museu do Barro Espaço Zé Caboclo, adjacent to the Tancredo Neves Cultural Space, offers a cultural showcase with thematic rooms dedicated to [[Luiz Gonzaga]]; Saint John and Caruaru artists; [[Elba Ramalho]]; Mestre Vitalino and his family; Alto do Moura artisans, featuring exhibitions of figurative, decorative, and utilitarian-decorative clay artifacts; Abelardo Rodrigues, a collection of clay art; and the Luísa Maciel Gallery, displaying paintings that highlight aspects of popular culture. The museum also includes a temporary exhibition room.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Museu do Barro in Caruaru - Espaço Zé Caboclo |url=http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/museu6_caruaru.html |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Secretariat of Culture of the State of Pernambuco |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010902145106/http://www.cultura.pe.gov.br/museu6_caruaru.html |archive-date=2001-09-02 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Also attached to the city's cultural space is the Caroá Factory Museum, which highlights one of the industries that boosted Caruaru's economy. The museum features panels tracing its history from its founding in 1935 by José Vasconcelos to its full operation. The collection includes machinery used in fiber production, including a carding machine, a spinning machine, a thread strength tester, shuttles, bobbins, and leather storage containers for reels. The museum also displays old furniture from the presidential office, a time clock, a telephone, and a typewriter, as well as samples of the caroa plant and its fiber.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Caroá Factory Museum |url=http://www.caruarumaiscriativa.com.br/?pagina=canais&idc=21 |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Caruaru Mais Criativa |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140224005407/http://www.caruarumaiscriativa.com.br/?pagina=canais&idc=21 |archive-date=2014-02-24 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

In the city center, the Caruaru City Memorial was originally built to serve as a flour market. However, when the city's market was relocated to Parque 18 de Maio, the building lost its original purpose. In 1992, it became the Fair Memorial, and in 2009, it was restored, restructured, and reopened with an expanded collection that now includes panels showcasing the political, social, cultural, religious, economic, and sporting history of the municipality.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Caruaru - Historical Sites |url=http://viagem.uol.com.br/guia/cidade/caruaru---lugares-historicos.jhtm |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=UOL |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140301183141/http://viagem.uol.com.br/guia/cidade/caruaru---lugares-historicos.jhtm |archive-date=2014-03-01 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The Mestre Vitalino House-Museum, located in Alto do Moura, was the last residence of Caruaru's most famous artisan. Visitors can view replicas of his main works, household items belonging to his family, and photographs highlighting key milestones in his artistic life. The space is managed by one of his sons, Severino Vitalino, who, like his father, earns a living through clay craftsmanship. The museum also offers insights into the stages of craft production.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Mestre Vitalino House-Museum |url=http://altodomoura.com/Paginas.php?pg=30 |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Alto do Moura |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110405171312/http://altodomoura.com/Paginas.php?pg=30 |archive-date=2011-04-05 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Also in the same neighborhood, the Mestre Galdino Museum houses many of his original pieces and poems, along with photographs and texts detailing the personal life of this contemporary of Mestre Vitalino, known for his surrealist works.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Mestre Galdino Memorial |url=http://altodomoura.com/Paginas.php?pg=32 |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Alto do Moura |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110405171324/http://altodomoura.com/Paginas.php?pg=32 |archive-date=2011-04-05 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Caruaru Fair ===
[[File:Pórtico de Entrada da Feira de Caruaru, Pernambuco.jpg|thumb|Entrance gate to the Caruaru Fair|left]]

Located in the Nossa Senhora das Dores neighborhood, in the economic heart of the city, the Caruaru Fair is one of the largest open-air markets of significant economic and cultural importance in Northeast Brazil. Operating for over 200 years, its origins are closely tied to the city's history. The site was a stopping point for cowboys driving cattle from the interior to the coast and for peddlers traveling in the opposite direction. The fair takes place on Wednesdays and Saturdays, with setup beginning the previous afternoon as merchants arrive with their goods. They use various forms of transportation, including donkeys, carts, old trucks, vans, bicycles, ox carts, and cars. Due to its diversity, the fair keeps the city bustling nearly every day of the week.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions"/><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Gaspar |first=Lúcia |title=Caruaru Fair (Pernambuco) |url=https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/feira-de-caruaru-pe/ |access-date=2023-04-11 |website=Pesquisa Escolar |publisher=Fundação Joaquim Nabuco |year=2003 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230411000001/https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/feira-de-caruaru-pe/ |archive-date=2023-04-11}}</ref>

In 1992, the fair was relocated from Largo da Igreja da Conceição to Parque 18 de Maio, also in the city center. Thousands of colorful stalls stretch over two kilometers of city streets, offering a wide variety of products, particularly popular crafts such as straw, leather, and fabric hats, baskets, clay and ceramic objects, traditional toys, and bird cages. The fair includes sections for fruits, vegetables, grains, medicinal herbs, and meats, as well as clothing, shoes, bags, kitchen utensils, furniture, animals, hardware, radios, and imported electronics. A unique "swap" section allows bartering of items such as bicycles, radios, watches, clothes, wallets, and musical instruments. Musicians play the accordion and guitar, while vendors of [[cordel literature]] advertise through loudspeakers.<ref name="IPHAN"/><ref name=":1" />

The fair also features musical groups and [[Fife (instrument)|fife]] bands. Blending commerce, festivity, and popular art, anonymous artists showcase Northeastern culture. It is a meeting point for artists, poets, bohemians, and tourists from Brazil and abroad who join the local population and crowd the stalls, contributing significantly to the city's economy. [[Luiz Gonzaga]]'s rendition of Onildo Almeida's song ''Feira de Caruaru'' is a well-known representation of the essence of the Caruaru Fair.<ref name=":1" /><ref>{{Cite news |last=Romero |first=Pedro |title=Caruaru Fair is Brazilian heritage |url=http://thacker.diraol.eng.br/mirrors/www.cultura.gov.br/site/index.html%3Fp=5867 |access-date=2023-04-11 |work=Jornal do Commercio |publisher=Ministério da Cultura |date=2006-12-27 |page=1 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230411203153/http://thacker.diraol.eng.br/mirrors/www.cultura.gov.br/site/index.html%3Fp=5867 |archive-date=2023-04-11}}</ref> In 2006, the fair was designated a Brazilian Intangible Cultural Heritage by the [[National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage]].<ref name="IPHAN"/>

Caruaru hosts the world's largest ''[[Festa Junina]]'', attracting over 1.5 million visitors. Due to its massive attendance, it holds the [[Guinness World Records]] title as the largest open-air regional country festival.<ref name="São_João">{{Cite web |title=At the world's largest São João festival, attendance reaches 1.5 million people |url=http://diariocatarinense.clicrbs.com.br/sc/noticia/2011/06/na-maior-festa-de-sao-joao-do-mundo-publico-chega-a-1-5-milhao-de-pessoas-3363619.html |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Diário Catarinense |date=2011-06-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160629124148/http://diariocatarinense.clicrbs.com.br/sc/noticia/2011/06/na-maior-festa-de-sao-joao-do-mundo-publico-chega-a-1-5-milhao-de-pessoas-3363619.html |archive-date=2016-06-29 |url-status=dead}}</ref> This title should not be confused with the theme of the São João festival in [[Campina Grande]], known as "The Greatest São João in the World," though the official title belongs to Caruaru.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The tradition of São João festivals through the centuries |url=http://www.hotlink.com.br/noticias_show.php?id=15590 |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Hotlink |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140227154747/http://www.hotlink.com.br/noticias_show.php?id=15590 |archive-date=2014-02-27 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Since the mid-19th century, Caruaru's São João festivals have drawn visitors from surrounding areas and even Recife. Initially organized on private rural estates, they featured [[bonfires]], [[balloons]], fireworks, ''quadrilha'' dances, and stalls offering [[curau]], [[pamonha]], [[corn]], and lively entertainment. In the 1950s, a fair featuring a wide variety of fireworks typical of Northeast Brazil was a popular attraction for people of all ages. Fireworks are now a staple of the festivities. They were introduced to Brazil by Portuguese and Spanish immigrants who had learned about them from Chinese and Arab traders.<ref name="Tourist_Attractions" /><ref name=":2" />

With a long tradition of São João celebrations, the festival has been held since 1994 at the Luiz Gonzaga Events Courtyard, a 41,500 m<sup>2</sup> complex housing the Caruaru Culture Foundation, the Museu do Barro and Museu do Forró, an exhibition pavilion, the Municipal Tourism Secretariat, a stage for performances, and the Vila do Forró, a replica of a typical rural village with a small church, town hall, grocery store, bank, and post office, all built in masonry.<ref name=":2" /><ref>{{Harvtxt|Farias|2005|p=16}}</ref>

The vibrant and grand festivities attract over 1.5 million tourists. Visitors can enjoy performances by ''bacamarteiros'' and fife bands, as well as shows by artists such as [[Alceu Valença]], [[Dominguinhos]], [[Elba Ramalho]], [[Gilberto Gil]], [[Zé Ramalho]], and Nando Cordel. They can also sample regional cuisine and dance to traditional Northeastern [[forró pé-de-serra]] music.<ref name=":2" />

Food and drinks are also major attractions and are offered on designated days. Options include [[hot chocolate]], ''[[quentão]]'', [[popcorn]], ''[[pamonha]]'', [[couscous]], [[corn cake]], ''[[Pé de moleque|pé]]'' ''[[Pé de moleque|de moleque]]'', [[rice pudding]], ''[[Canjica (dish)|canjica]]'', [[cassava cake]], ''[[xarém]]'', and [[stew]]. On June 28, a massive bonfire made of eco-friendly wood is lit in front of the Convent Church.<ref name=":2" />

Inspired by traditional ''[[Quadrille|quadrilhas]]'', the ''Drilhas'', carnival-like blocks similar to Bahia's [[Trio elétrico|electric trios]], emerged in Caruaru's São João in 1989. To preserve the festival's traditions, these trios perform only in the afternoon on Agamenon Magalhães Avenue. The oldest and most traditional are the Gaydrilha, featuring men dressed as rural characters, and the Sapadrilha, with women dressed as men, both established in 1989. Other ''drilhas'' include Piradrilha, Diversãodrilha, Turisdrilha, Trokadrilha, Brinkadrilha, and Nova Drilha. In 2009, the São João de Caruaru honored the centennial of Mestre Vitalino, the city's famous ceramist, and was registered as Pernambuco's Intangible Heritage by the [[Legislative Assembly of Pernambuco]].<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Gaspar |first=Lúcia |title=São João in Caruaru |url=https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/sao-joao-em-caruaru/ |access-date=2023-04-11 |website=Pesquisa Escolar |publisher=Fundação Joaquim Nabuco |year=2011 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230411000002/https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/sao-joao-em-caruaru/ |archive-date=2023-04-11}}</ref>

== Sports ==

Each year, [[TV Asa Branca]] and partners organize the Copa TV Asa Branca de Futsal. The [[Serviço Social do Comércio|SESC]] gymnasium, near the Sulanca Fair courtyard, hosts matches involving futsal teams from major cities in the Forest Zone, Agreste, and Sertão regions, such as [[Palmares, Pernambuco|Palmares]], [[Catende]], [[Bezerros]], [[Santa Cruz do Capibaribe]], [[Belo Jardim]], [[Garanhuns]], [[Lajedo, Pernambuco|Lajedo]], [[Arcoverde]], and [[Serra Talhada]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Arcoverde crowned two-time champion of the 8th Copa TV Asa Branca de Futsal 2013 |url=http://globoesporte.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/11/arcoverde-sagrou-se-bicampeao-da-8-copa-tv-asa-branca-de-futsal-2013.html |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Globo Esporte |date=2013-11-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131204011423/http://globoesporte.globo.com/pe/caruaru-regiao/noticia/2013/11/arcoverde-sagrou-se-bicampeao-da-8-copa-tv-asa-branca-de-futsal-2013.html |archive-date=2013-12-04}}</ref>

Another major sports venue in Caruaru is the [[Autódromo Internacional Ayrton Senna (Caruaru)|Ayrton Senna International Racetrack]], which hosts an annual stage of the [[Fórmula Truck]] and various other competitions. It is Pernambuco's main racetrack, inaugurated on December 13, 1992, with a 3,180-meter track and widths ranging from nine to sixteen meters.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ayrton Senna International Racetrack, Caruaru-PE |url=http://www.fabianomachado.com.br/noticias/item/autdromo-internacional-ayrton-senna-caruaru-pe |access-date=2014-02-23 |website=Blog do Fabiano Machado |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140303060331/http://www.fabianomachado.com.br/noticias/item/autdromo-internacional-ayrton-senna-caruaru-pe |archive-date=2014-03-03 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Holidays ==
According to the Municipal Official Gazette, Caruaru observes five municipal holidays in 2025: [[Good Friday]], on 18 April; the anniversary of the municipality's political emancipation, on 18 May; [[Saint John the Baptist|Saint John's Day]], on 24 June; [[Saint Peter|Saint Peter's Day]], on 29 June; and the day of [[Our Lady of Sorrows]], the municipal patron saint, on 15 September. In addition to these holidays, there are optional holidays during [[Carnival]] week, from 3 March to 5 March (the latter until 12 PM), and on Civil Servants' Day, 28 October.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Caruaru will have only five municipal holidays in 2014 |url=http://blogs.recifecbn.com.br/inaldosampaio/2014/01/caruaru-tera-apenas-cinco-feriados-municipais-em-2014/ |access-date=2014-02-24 |website=CBN Recife |date=2014-01-27 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140308194743/http://blogs.recifecbn.com.br/inaldosampaio/2014/01/caruaru-tera-apenas-cinco-feriados-municipais-em-2014/ |archive-date=2014-03-08 |url-status=dead}}</ref> {{clear}}
