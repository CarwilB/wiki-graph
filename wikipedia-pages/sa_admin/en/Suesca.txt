'''Suesca''' is a town and municipality in the [[Almeidas Province]], part of the department of [[Cundinamarca Department|Cundinamarca]], [[Colombia]]. It is located on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], {{convert|59|km|mi}} north of the capital [[Bogotá]]. Suesca forms the northern edge of the [[Bogotá savanna]] and is a scenic countryside town which is well known because its landscape attracts devotees of rock climbing, trekking, and rafting. It is surrounded by dairy farms and flower plantations. The municipality borders [[Cucunubá]] and [[Lenguazaque]] in the north, [[Sesquilé]] and [[Gachancipá]] in the south, [[Chocontá]] in the east and [[Nemocón]] in the west.<ref name=websiteSuesca>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.suesca-cundinamarca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Suesca]</ref>

== Etymology ==
The name Suesca is derived from the [[Chibcha language|Chibcha]] word ''Suejica'', ''Sueica''<ref name=Wills_p163>Wills et al., 2001, Ch.43 - Rocas de Suesca - paraíso de escaladores, p.163</ref> or ''Suesuca'',<ref>Espejo Oyala, 1999, p.1130</ref> which means "Rock of the birds" or "Tail of the macaw".<ref name=websiteSuesca/><ref name=DeQuesadaBanco>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.banrepcultural.org/blaavirtual/historia/ilustre/ilus14a.htm Etymology Suesca] - [[Banco de la República]]</ref>

== History ==
Suesca was inhabited early in the [[Template:Timeline of inhabitation of the Altiplano Cundiboyacense|history of inhabitation]] of the Altiplano and the rock shelters formed the site for semi-nomadic [[hunter-gatherer]]s of the [[Herrera Period|Herrera]] and [[Andean preceramic|preceramic]] periods.

Before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]], Suesca was part of the [[Muisca Confederation]]. The ''[[cacique]]'' of [[Guatavita]] ruled over Suesca,<ref>Gamboa Mendoza, 2008, p.141</ref> the village close to the [[Sacred lake|sacred]] [[Lake Guatavita]] and containing [[Lake Suesca]] where rituals were held.<ref>Casilimas & López, 1987, p.135</ref> Suesca formed an important centre on the trade [[Muisca architecture|road]] to [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]], ruled by the ''[[zaque|hoa]]'' of [[Tunja|Hunza]]. The merchants took [[coal]] and [[halite|salt]] on their way to the north.<ref>Bohórquez Caldera, 2008, p.162</ref> Every four days a [[Muisca economy|market]] was held in Suesca.<ref>Gamboa Mendoza, 2008, p.83</ref> Suesca was also an important settlement for the [[Muisca art|pottery]] made by the Muisca.<ref>Francis, 1993, p.39</ref>

When [[conquistador]] [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada]] arrived in Suesca in March 1537, he founded the modern town.<ref name=websiteSuesca/> In Suesca he sentenced a soldier of his army to death for stealing mantles from the [[Muisca people|Muisca]].<ref name=SuescaTiempo>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/MAM-197988 Suesca en la cuerda] - [[El Tiempo (Colombia)|El Tiempo]]</ref> After submitting the ''hoa'' of Hunza, [[Quemuenchatocha|Eucaneme]], the [[Muisca rulers|Muisca ruler]] was taken prisoner to Suesca in an attempt to get him to reveal the location of his treasures.<ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://pueblosoriginarios.com/biografias/quemen.html Biography Quemuenchatocha] - Pueblos Originarios</ref> When the ''[[zipa|psihipqua]]'' of Muyquytá, [[Tisquesusa|Bogotá]], heard about the Spanish presence in Suesca, he sent a spy to the town to gain information about their strength. The Muisca, unfamiliar with horses and horseback riders, thought that the horse and the rider were one. When a horse died in Suesca, they found out this was not the case.<ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.banrepcultural.org/blaavirtual/biografias/tisquesusa Biography Tisquesusa] - [[Banco de la República]]</ref>

In 1602, in a cave in Suesca, 150 [[Muisca mummification|Muisca mummies]] were discovered. The mummies were organised in a circle around the mummy of the ''cacique'' of the town.<ref>Martínez & Martínez, 2012, p.69</ref> [[Rock art]] has also been found in Suesca.<ref>Muñoz, 2006, p.11</ref> The [[pictograph]]s of Suesca are among the most extensive of Cundinamarca, but at the same time the most vandalised.<ref>Martínez & Botiva, 2004, p.32</ref>

== Tourism ==
* Handicrafts - Wool clothes
* Church of Nuestra Señora del Rosario
* [[Lake Suesca]] - a natural lake that is located at {{convert|3000|m|ft}} above sea level. It is found {{convert|10|km|mi}} from Suesca town.
* Suesca Monoliths - wind erosion of the [[sandstone]] has produced natural [[monolith]]s<ref>{{in lang|es}} [http://sendanativa.com/es/actividades/caminatas/item/51-monolitos-de-suesca#.V2sp0KKWmBU Suesca monoliths]</ref>
* Rocas de Suesca - natural cliffs approximately {{convert|4|km|mi}} long that are located {{convert|1|km|mi}} before arriving in Suesca town. A beautiful landscape, the rocks are ideal for people who love trekking and climbing, with many guided tours taking place at the cliffs. The rocks of Suesca are considered the birthplace of Colombian rock climbing, and feature over 400 routes<ref name=Wills_p163/> on excellent quality sandstone up to several pitches in length, with a majority of routes being one pitch. The area is best known for traditional climbing, but there are many sport (bolted) routes as well. The [[Bogotá River]] flows along the Rocas de Suesca. To get to the cliffs, one walks from the town along the train tracks for about 10 minutes. The train only runs very early in the morning, so there is little danger. Camping is available very close to cliffs or there is reasonably priced lodging close to Rocas de Suesca.
* Getting to Suesca: by car or bus:
** By car from Bogotá, one should take [[Autopista Norte (Bogotá)|Autopista Norte]] that goes to [[Tunja]], passing a toll bridge in Autopista Norte; then one will pass [[Briceño, Boyacá|Briceño]], the town of [[Tocancipá]] and a second toll bridge; then you will find yourself on the main road to Tunja, where you will reach a crossroads: right leads to Sesquilé and left to Suesca. Suesca is approximately {{convert|15|km|mi}} from the main road. It is about {{convert|59|km|mi}} from Bogotá to Suesca, or around an hour and a half travel time.<ref name=Wills_p162>Wills et al., 2001, Ch.43 - Rocas de Suesca - paraíso de escaladores, p.162</ref>
