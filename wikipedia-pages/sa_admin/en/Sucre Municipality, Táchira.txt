'''Sucre Municipality''' is one of the 29 municipalities in [[Táchira (state)|Táchira]], [[Venezuela]]. Its capital is [[Queniquea]]. Based on the [[2011 Venezuelan census]], the population was 7,494. The mayor is Carlos Rodríguez Vera of the [[United Socialist Party of Venezuela|PSUV]] party since July 2025.

==Name==
The municipality is one of several in Venezuela named "[[Sucre Municipality (disambiguation)|Sucre Municipality]]" in honour of Venezuelan independence hero [[Antonio José de Sucre]].<ref>{{cite journal|url=https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/200/20023433004.pdf|title=Arte e historia de antiguas estatuas conmemorativas del estado Táchira, Venezuela|trans-title=Art and History of Ancient Commemorative Statues in the State of Táchira, Venezuela|lang=es|first=Samir|last=Sánchez|journal=Journal of History and Social Sciences|date=July–December 2012|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the [[2011 Venezuelan census]], The population of the Sucre Municipality was 7,494 people, accounting for 0.6% of the total population of the state of [[Táchira]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/20__t%C3%A1chira/|title=Táchira (State in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref> Less than half (40.7%) of the municipality's population is in the town of [[Queniquea]], the [[municipal seat]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/tachira/2026__sucre/|title=Urban Localities in Sucre (Táchira, Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=25 June 2017|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref>

By June 2019, official projections from the [[Statistics National Institute (Venezuela)|Venezuelan Statistics National Institute]] estimated the population of Sucre as 7,912 people, representing an [[annual growth rate]] of 0.68% since 2011 and showing a population density of 19.44 inhabitants/km². However, these projections do not account for the impact of emigration linked to the country's recent economic and political circumstances.<ref name="CityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/venezuela/admin/t%C3%A1chira/2026__sucre/|title=Sucre (Municipality in Venezuela)|website=CityPopulation.de|date=11 May 2019|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref>

The [[gender]] distribution of the population was 53.4% men (3,907) and 46.6% women (3,413). The [[age distribution]] showed that the largest segment of the population was aged 15 to 64, comprising 64.3% of the people. Younger people aged 0 to 14 made up 28% of the population, while those aged 65 and older accounted for 7.7%. The municipality is more rural than urbanized, with 59.3% of inhabitants (4,341) living in [[rural areas]] compared to 40.7% (2,979) in [[urban centers]].<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

Ethnically, the municipality identified as predominantly [[White people]] (56.3%) and [[Mestizo]] (42%). Minority groups included 0.5% [[Afro-Venezuelans]] and 1.2% belonging to other ethnic groups. The [[literacy rate]] was 86.7%, with 809 inhabitants of Sucre not able to read or write.<ref name="CityPopulation.de"/>

==Government==
The mayor of the Sucre Municipality is Carlos Rodríguez Vera of the [[United Socialist Party of Venezuela]]. He won the election in July 2025.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://lanacionweb.com/politica/oficialismo-gana-21-alcaldias-en-tachira-oposicion-obtiene-8/|title=Oficialismo gana 21 alcaldías en Táchira, oposición obtiene 8|trans-title=Ruling party wins 21 mayoralties in Táchira, opposition wins 8|lang=es|newspaper=[[La Nación]]|first=Maryory|last=Bustamante|date=30 July 2025|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref> The municipality is divided into three [[parish]]es; Eleazar López Contreras, San Pablo, and Sucre.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://callejero-venezuela.openalfa.com/municipio-sucre_tachira|title= Callejero de Municipio Sucre, Estado Táchira |trans-title=Street map of Sucre Municipality, Táchira State|lang=es|website=Callejero Venezuela|access-date=19 May 2026}}</ref>
