'''Lares''' ({{IPA|es|ˈlaɾes}}, {{IPA|es|ˈlaɾeʔ|local}}) is a mountain [[Lares barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]]'s central-western area. Lares is located north of [[Maricao, Puerto Rico|Maricao]] and [[Yauco, Puerto Rico|Yauco]]; south of [[Camuy, Puerto Rico|Camuy]], east of [[San Sebastián, Puerto Rico|San Sebastián]] and [[Las Marias, Puerto Rico|Las Marias]]; and west of [[Hatillo, Puerto Rico|Hatillo]], [[Utuado, Puerto Rico|Utuado]] and [[Adjuntas, Puerto Rico|Adjuntas]]. Lares is spread over 10 barrios and [[Lares barrio-pueblo|Lares Pueblo]] (Downtown Lares). It is part of the [[Aguadilla-Isabela-San Sebastián Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

A city adorned with [[Spain|Spanish]]-era colonial-style churches and small downtown stores, Lares is located on a mountainous, breezy area that is about 1.5 hours from the capital [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] by car.

In 1868, Lares was the site of the ''[[Grito de Lares]]'' (literally, ''The Cry of Lares'', or Lares Revolt), an uprising brought on by pro-independence rebels and members of the [[Revolutionary Committee of Puerto Rico]] striving for [[Independence movement in Puerto Rico|Puerto Rican independence]] from [[Spanish Empire|Spain]]. Even though it was short-lived, it remains an iconic historical event in the [[History of Puerto Rico|history of the island]]. The flag of the revolt, known as the ''[[Grito de Lares flag|Bandera del Grito de Lares (Grito de Lares flag)]]'', is the official flag of Lares.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://progresoweekly.us/cry-lares/|title=The Cry of Lares|date=22 September 2013|publisher=Progreso Weekly Inc.|access-date=April 9, 2019|archive-date=August 14, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230814153757/https://progresoweekly.us/cry-lares/|url-status=live}}</ref>

== History ==
[[File:Lares, Puerto Rico. In an apothecary store.jpg|thumb|An apothecary store in Lares in 1942]]

Lares was founded on April 26, 1827, by Francisco de Sotomayor and Pedro Vélez Borrero, who named the town after [[Amador de Lariz]], a [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish]] nobleman and one of its settlers.<ref>
{{cite web |url=http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/fundacion_lares_01.htm |title=History and foundation of Lares |publisher=Rootsweb.ancestry.com |access-date=2008-11-09 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081110104501/http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/fundacion_lares_01.htm |archive-date=2008-11-10 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Nueva Isla">
{{cite web |title=Lares: Ciudad de cielos abiertos |url=https://nuevaisla.com/lares.html |website=nuevaisla.com |publisher=SG Communications |access-date=8 February 2019 |language=es |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190209124540/https://nuevaisla.com/lares.html |archive-date=2019-02-09 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Lares was the location of the ''[[Grito de Lares]]'' ''(Cry of Lares)'' revolt against [[Captaincy General of Puerto Rico|Spanish rule]] in Puerto Rico on September 23, 1868. The revolt is recognized as the most important symbol of the [[Independence movement in Puerto Rico|Puerto Rican struggle for independence]].<ref name="The Library of Congress">{{cite web | title=Historia de la insurrección de Lares, precedida de una reseña de los trabajos separatistas que se vienen haciendo en la isla de Puerto-Rico desde la emancipación de las demás posesiones hispano-ultramarinas, y seguida de todos los documentos á ella referentes; | website=The Library of Congress | url=https://www.loc.gov/item/03002727/ | access-date=2020-10-22 | archive-date=October 23, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201023061556/https://www.loc.gov/item/03002727/ | url-status=live }}</ref> Historian [[Fernando Picó]] described it thus:

{{Boxquote|This revolution was the biggest anti-Spanish manifestation in the [[History of Puerto Rico|history of the island]] and articulated the economic frustrations of nearly ruined landowners, the political project of the creoles, the rejection of coerced labor by the ''jornaleros'' ([[day labor]]ers), and the emancipation hopes of the [[Slavery in Puerto Rico|enslaved population]].|[[Fernando Picó]]|The Absent State<ref>{{cite book |last1=Picó |first1=Fernando |author-link1=Fernando Picó |editor-last1=Negrón-Muntaner |editor-first1=Frances |editor-link1=Frances Negrón-Muntaner |chapter=The Absent State |title=None of the Above:Puerto Ricans in the Global Era |url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131416481 |url-status=live |url-access=subscription |series=New Directions in Latino American Cultures Ser. |publisher= |publication-date=2008-03-13 |page=23 |isbn=9781403962454 |access-date=2022-09-22 |archive-date=September 28, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220928112841/https://www.proquest.com/docview/2131416481 |id={{ProQuest|2131416481}} }}</ref>}}

Lares adopted the [[Grito de Lares flag|flag of the revolt]] as its official flag. The flag was proclaimed the national flag of a [[Sovereign state|sovereign]] "Republic of Puerto Rico" by the [[Revolutionary Committee of Puerto Rico|revolting revolutionaries]] in 1868, making it the first flag of Puerto Rico. In 1895, the exiled [[Revolutionary Committee of Puerto Rico|revolutionaries]] replaced it with the [[Flag of Puerto Rico|current flag of Puerto Rico]] as the new revolutionary flag to represent an independent Puerto Rico. As the flag of the revolt, the flag of Lares is used by the [[independence movement in Puerto Rico]] to show support for the independence of the [[Geography of Puerto Rico|island]] from the [[United States]].

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Lares was 20,883.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del Gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n251 160]|language=es|access-date=2019-02-14}}</ref>

[[Hurricane Maria]] on September 20, 2017, triggered numerous landslides in Lares. In many areas of Lares there were more than 25 landslides per square mile due to the significant amount of rainfall.<ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides map">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref> {{lang|es|Puerto Rico se levanta}} (Puerto Rico will stand up) became the slogan used across the island to communicate the island would rise again.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2018/09/hurricane-maria-anniversary-puerto-rico-trump/570928/|title=The Situation in Puerto Rico Is Untenable|first=Vann R.|last=Newkirk II|date=20 September 2018|website=The Atlantic|access-date=3 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190603133624/https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2018/09/hurricane-maria-anniversary-puerto-rico-trump/570928/|archive-date=3 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Farmland in Barrio La Torre, Lares, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Cultivating in La Torre barrio in Lares in 2019]]

Lares is a mountainous municipality located in the central western part of the island of Puerto Rico. According to the 2010 [[U.S. Census Bureau]], the municipality has a total area of {{convert|61.64|sqmi}}, of which {{convert|61.45|sqmi}} is land and {{convert|.09|sqmi}} is water.<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/lares-municipality/|title=Lares Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190603183527/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/lares-municipality/|archive-date=2019-06-03|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Special Communities ===
{{See also|Puerto Rico Office for Socioeconomic and Community Development}}

{{lang|es|Comunidades Especiales de Puerto Rico}} (Special Communities of Puerto Rico) are marginalized communities whose citizens are experiencing a certain amount of [[social exclusion]]. == Sports ==
Lares has a professional volleyball team called [[Patriotas de Lares]] (Lares Patriots) that has international players. The Patriotas won 3 championships, in 1981, 1983 and 2002. In 1981 and 1983 they beat Corozal in the finals and in 2002 they beat Naranjito.<ref>{{cite web |title=Patriotas de Lares |url=https://larespatriotasclub.wixsite.com/voleibol-pr/diapositivas-c1f5z |website=voleibol-pr |access-date=5 June 2026 |language=es}}</ref>

== Economy ==
[[File:Heladeria de Lares.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Heladería de Lares]] (Lares Ice Cream Shop), July 2007]]

Lares' economy is primarily agricultural. Harvested products include [[banana]]s, [[coffee]], [[Orange (fruit)|oranges]], and [[tomato]]es.

[[Tourism in Puerto Rico|Tourism]] also plays a significant role in the municipality's economy. The ''[[Heladeria de Lares]]'' (Lares Ice Cream Shop) is well known around Puerto Rico for its unorthodox selection of ice cream including; rice and beans-flavored ice cream.<ref name="Heladeria">{{cite web|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/negocios/consumo/nota/historicaheladeriadelaresreabrirasuspuertas-2286820/|title=Histórica Heladería de Lares reabrirá sus puertas|date=1 February 2017|website=El Nuevo Dia|access-date=8 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190508024758/https://www.elnuevodia.com/negocios/consumo/nota/historicaheladeriadelaresreabrirasuspuertas-2286820/|archive-date=8 May 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

There was a large population exodus, out of Lares, after September 20, 2017, when [[Hurricane Maria]] struck the island.<ref name="NYT_Exodus">{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2017/07/16/us/puerto-rico-lares.html|title=Exodus From a Historic Puerto Rican Town, With No End in Sight|first=Frances|last=Robles|date=16 July 2017|publisher=NYTimes|access-date=8 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190508030908/https://www.nytimes.com/2017/07/16/us/puerto-rico-lares.html|archive-date=8 May 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2016, Rural Opportunities Puerto Rico Inc. (ROPRI) in conjunction with the [[United States Department of Agriculture]] (USDA) completed the building of 24 (one-bedroom, two-bedroom and three-bedroom) units<ref>{{cite web|url=https://housingapartments.org/rental_detail/28573|title=Alturas De Castaner – Castaner PR Multi-Family Housing Rental|website=housingapartments.org|access-date=17 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190617002311/https://housingapartments.org/rental_detail/28573|archive-date=17 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> in Lares, specifically for farmers (in {{langx|es|agricultores}}), and their families, to live and work. It is called {{lang|es|Alturas de Castañer}} (Castañer Heights) and there the families work to grow coffee, bananas and other crops which are sold to markets, and restaurants nearby.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/community-agricultores-puerto-rico|title=A Community for Agricultores in Puerto Rico|website=www.usda.gov|access-date=17 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190617002122/https://www.usda.gov/media/blog/2016/01/28/community-agricultores-puerto-rico|archive-date=17 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

Like most of Puerto Rico, Lares population originated with the [[Taíno|Taino Indians]] and then many immigrants from Spain settled the central highland, most prominently the [[Andalusians|Andalusian]], [[Canary Islanders|Canarian]] and [[Extremadurans|Extremaduran]] Spanish migration who formed the bulk of the ''Jibaro'' or white peasant stock of the island.<ref>
{{cite web|url=http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/passengers_01.htm#Migration|title=Brief History of the Canarian Migration to Spanish America|last=Hernández|first=Miguel|access-date=2014-12-09|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418105438/http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~prhgs/passengers_01.htm#Migration|archive-date=2015-04-18|url-status=live}}
</ref> The Andalusian, Canarian and [[Extremadurans|Extremaduran]] Spaniards also influenced much of the Puerto Rican culture which explains the use of [[Puerto Rican Spanish|Spanish]] and the Spanish architecture that can be found in the city.

=== Flag ===
{{Main article| Grito de Lares flag}}

The origins of the [[Grito de Lares flag|flag of Lares]] can be traced back to the failed ''[[Grito de Lares]] (Cry of Lares)'' revolt of 1868 against [[Captaincy General of Puerto Rico|Spanish rule]]. The flag is modeled after the [[First Dominican Republic|flag of the First Dominican Republic]] and the [[flag of Cuba]], reflecting the revolutionaries idea to unite the Spanish-speaking [[Greater Antilles]] of [[Puerto Rico]], [[Cuba]], and the [[Dominican Republic]] in [[Hispaniola]] into an [[Antillean Confederation]]. The flag was first knitted by [[Mariana Bracetti]] at the behest of [[Ramón Emeterio Betances]], leader of the [[Revolutionary Committee of Puerto Rico]].

Th flag consists of a white cross in the center that extends to its four sides, with four equal rectangles, two located on top and two below the arms of the cross. The superior (top) ones are blue and the inferior (bottom) ones red. A [[Five-pointed star |five-pointed]] white star is located in the center of the upper left rectangle.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=Lares | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=6 August 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/PUEBLOS/pueblos_files/lares.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200806201547/http://www.lexjuris.com/PUEBLOS/pueblos_files/lares.html | archive-date=6 August 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

=== Coat of arms ===
A silver cross is centered on and extends across the shield from side to side and top to bottom; it has blue top quadrants and red bottom quadrants; it has a five pointed silver star in the upper left quadrant. A chain surrounds the shield. The seal is same coat of arms with a scroll and a ribbon in a semicircle with the words ''Lares Ciudad del Grito'' (Lares City of the Cry).<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />
