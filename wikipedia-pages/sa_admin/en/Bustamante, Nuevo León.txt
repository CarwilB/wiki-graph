'''Bustamante''' is a municipality and town of [[Mexico]] in the state of [[Nuevo León]]. The full name is San Miguel de Bustamante. The municipality has an area of {{cvt|558|km2}}.  It is bordered on the north by [[Lampazos de Naranjo]] and the state of [[Coahuila]], on the south by [[Mina, Nuevo León|Mina]] and [[Villaldama, Nuevo León]], on the east by Villaldama, and on the west by Mina. In 2010 the population of the municipality was 3,773, of which nearly all lived in the town of Bustamante.<ref>''Instituto Nacional de Estadistica Geographica y informatica, Mexico''http://www.citypopulation.de/php/mexico-nuevoleon.php, accessed 15 Jan 2013</ref>

==Geography==
Bustamante is located in an irrigated valley at the mouth of a well-watered canyon in the headwaters of the [[Sabinas River]], a tributary of the [[Rio Salado (Mexico)|Salado River]]. The Sierra de Gomas to the west of the town rise to an elevation of 5,591 feet (1,704&nbsp;m) at the Cabeza de Leon four miles south-southwest of Bustamante. East of town is the Sierra de Lampazos which rise to an elevation of 3,816 feet (1,163&nbsp;m)<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.mapcarta.com/20348160 |title=Sierra Lampazos |website=www.mapcarta.com |access-date=16 Jan 2013}}</ref>

The climate is semi-arid with about 20 inches (500&nbsp;mm) of precipitation annually, mostly falling in summer. Summers are hot and winters are mild, with the lowest annual temperature usually about 23 °F (−5 °C).<ref>[http://www.slideshare.net/biogger/climate-in-nuevo-len slideshare.net], "Climate in Nuevo Leon", accessed 16 Jan 2013</ref>

==History==
The Spanish recruited more than 400 [[Tlaxcaltec|Tlaxcalan]] families in 1591 to settle in northern Mexico to help pacify and integrate the northern Indian tribes, collectively called the [[Chichimeca]]. This was one of the measures taken to resolve the long-running [[Chichimeca War]]. Sixteen Tlaxcalan families among the ancestors of the Tlaxcalans who were settled near [[Saltillo]] moved north in 1686 to found Bustamante, which was originally called San Miguel de Aguayo de la Nueva Tlaxcala. The [[Franciscans]] established a mission nearby to convert the Alazapas, a local band of nomadic Indians, to Christianity.<ref>''Encyclopedia de los Municipios de Mexico'' http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/nuevoleon/ {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930020154/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/nuevoleon/# |date=2007-09-30 }}, accessed 15 Jan 2013; Adams, David B. Ëmbattled Borderland: Northern Nuevo León and the Idios Bárabaros, 1686-1870" ''The Southwestern Historical Quarterly'', Vol. 95, No. 2 (1991), p- 205-220</ref>

In 1690, silver mines were established about five miles (8&nbsp;km) from Bustamante and the town of Villaldama was founded. Bustamante depended upon agriculture and provided labor and food for the mines. The Alazapa resisted the Spanish and Tlaxcalan settlements. A few [[Alazapa]] survived into the 19th century, but were expelled to [[Monterrey, Nuevo Leon|Monterrey, Nuevo León]] in 1860 and absorbed into the Hispanic population.<ref>"Consejo de la Historia y Cultura Sabinas Hidalgo" http://www.consejodehistoria.org/index2.php?option=com_content&do_pdf-1&id=10 {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304030120/http://www.consejodehistoria.org/index2.php?option=com_content&do_pdf-1&id=10 |date=2016-03-04 }}, accessed 16 Jan 2013; Adams, p. 206</ref> Bustamante received its present name in 1832. It was named after [[Anastasio Bustamante]], a President of newly independent Mexico.

On October 5, 1840, the area was raided by a band of [[Comanche]] Indians estimated to number 300 to 400. In Bustamante, the Comanche killed 17 people, abducted more than a dozen, and stole eight hundred head of livestock. This marked the onset of three decades of Comanche raids which devastated communities all over northern Mexico and, from 1848 to 1860, caused at least 71 deaths and 18 abductions in Bustamante. The disorganized central government of Mexico was of little help to the beleaguered northerners. A militia force of about 100 Tlaxcalans and Alazapa guarded mountain passes Comanche raiders were likely to use as part of a mobile force of 2,000 cavalry which patrolled northern and western Nuevo León.<ref>Adams, pp. 209-219</ref> (See [[Comanche-Mexico Wars]])

Adding to the misery were epidemics of disease. [[Cholera]] cost 197 lives in the Bustamante region in 1849 and smallpox in 1856-1857 killed 429 in a wider area. The epidemics also took a heavy toll on the Indian raiders and the number of raids dropped substantially after the late 1850s, culminating in one last Comanche raid in 1870.<ref>Adams, pp. 219-220</ref>

==Attractions==
The main tourist attractions are the [[Grutas de Bustamante|Bustamante Caves]] about two miles southwest of the town.
