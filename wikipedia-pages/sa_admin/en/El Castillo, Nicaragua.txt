'''El Castillo''' is a [[Municipalities of Nicaragua|municipality]] located on the [[San Juan River (Nicaragua)|Río San Juan]] (San Juan River) in the [[Río San Juan Department]] of [[Nicaragua]]. The settlement of what would eventually become the municipality of El Castillo began in 1673, when construction of the ''Fortaleza de la Limpia Pura e Inmaculada Concepción'', (now known as the [[Fortress of the Immaculate Conception|Castillo de la Inmaculada Concepción]], or Fortress of the Immaculate Conception) was begun. The [[village]] of [[El Castillo (village)|El Castillo]] eventually grew around the fortress, which continued to be strategically important to the [[Captaincy General of Guatemala]] until the late 18th century.

The administrative capital of the municipality of El Castillo is the village of [[Boca de Sábalos]], which translates from [[Spanish language|Spanish]] to "mouth of the tarpon" (''[[Atlantic tarpon|Megalops atlanticus]]''). Boca de Sábalos is located 350 kilometers from the national capital of [[Managua]], 55 kilometers downriver from the departmental capital of [[San Carlos, Nicaragua|San Carlos]] and several kilometers upriver from the village of [[El Castillo (village)|El Castillo]].

==History==
{{See|Fortress of the Immaculate Conception|Battle for the Río San Juan de Nicaragua|Rafaela Herrera}}
[[File:El Castillo-Fortaleza.jpg|thumb|left|alt=Color photograph of the Fortress of the Immaculate Conception in Nicaragua, taken in February 2011|[[Fortress of the Immaculate Conception]]]]

The municipality of El Castillo began with the construction of the Fortress of the Immaculate Conception on the southern bank of the San Juan River, approximately 6 kilometers from the [[Costa Rica–Nicaragua border|present-day border]] with [[Costa Rica]]. This was part of a series of [[fortification]]s built along the San Juan River in order to defend against [[Piracy|pirate]] attacks upon the city of [[Granada, Nicaragua|Granada]] (which can be reached by navigating upstream from the [[Caribbean Sea]] along the San Juan River into [[Lake Nicaragua]]).<ref name=UNESCO>{{cite web|title=Fortress of the immaculate Conception|author=United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization|authorlink=UNESCO|publisher= UNESCO World Heritage Centre|location=Paris|year=2011|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/484/|accessdate=2011-04-23}}</ref> Construction of the fortress, initially named ''Fortaleza de la Limpia Pura e Inmaculada Concepción'' (now known as the [[Fortress of the Immaculate Conception|''Castillo de la Inmaculada Concepción'']], or Fortress of the Immaculate Conception), was begun on March 10, 1673, and completed in 1675.<ref name=Webre2007>{{cite journal|author=Stephen Webre|title=Defense, Economy, and Politics in Seventeenth-Century Nicaragua: Don Fernando Francisco de Escobedo and the Fortification of the San Juan River, 1672–1673|journal=Jahrbuch für Geschichte Lateinamerikas|volume=44|pages=93–110|year=2007|issn=1438-4752|url=http://www-gewi.uni-graz.at/jbla/JBLA_Band_44-2007/jbla07_115-132.pdf|accessdate=2011-05-01|doi=10.7767/jbla.2007.44.1.93|s2cid=129880025}}</ref> The chosen site was the site of the present-day village of El Castillo. Despite the construction of the fortress, [[buccaneer]] [[William Dampier]] plundered the city and set it on fire on April 8, 1685.

The fortress was attacked several times during the 18th century, sometimes by [[Miskito people|miskito]] or [[zambo]] forces and sometimes by the British. On the morning of July 26, 1762, a combined British and Miskito force laid [[siege]] to the fortress in what would later be called the [[Battle for the Río San Juan de Nicaragua]]. This expedition, which had originally sailed from a base in [[Jamaica]], consisted of seven large boats and several canoes. The garrison commander, Don [[José de Herrera y Sotomayor]], had died unexpectedly 11 days earlier. Inspired by acts of heroism displayed by Herrera's 19-year-old daughter [[Rafaela Herrera|Rafaela]] (including killing the British commander),<ref name=Olsen1994>{{cite book|title=Chronology of women's history|page=[https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780313288036/page/90 90]|author=Kirstin Olsen|publisher=Greenwood Press|location=Westport, Connecticut|year=1994|isbn=0-313-28803-8|url=https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780313288036|url-access=registration|quote=Rafaela Herrera 1762.|accessdate=2011-04-23}}</ref> ''[[pro tempore]]'' garrison commander Lieutenant Juan de Aguilar y Santa Cruz led the defenders to victory in a battle that lasted six days.<ref name=Marley2008/><ref name=Archivo>{{cite web|title=Rafaela Herrera: File#201, Record#1651 (subscription required)|publisher=Archivo General de Centroamerica|location=Guatemala City, Guatemala|language=Spanish|year=2009|url=http://www.archivogeneraldecentroamerica.com/index.php?searchword=%22Rafaela+Herrera%22&option=com_search&Itemid=|accessdate=2011-04-24|archive-date=2011-09-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110904072035/http://www.archivogeneraldecentroamerica.com/index.php?searchword=%22Rafaela+Herrera%22&option=com_search&Itemid=|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name=Viscasillas2009>{{cite web|title=La Fortaleza de la Inmaculada Concepción de María|author=Carlos Viscasillas|publisher=Agencia Española de Cooperación Internacional para el Desarrollo (AECID)|location=Managua, Nicaragua|language=Spanish|year=2009|url=http://www.aecid.org.ni/files/doc/folleto_fortaleza_inmaculada_concepcion.pdf|accessdate=2011-04-24|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20120323043523/http://www.aecid.org.ni/files/doc/folleto_fortaleza_inmaculada_concepcion.pdf|archivedate=2012-03-23}}</ref> The British finally lifted their siege and retreated on August 3, 1762.<ref name=Marley2008>{{cite book|title=Wars of the Americas: a chronology of armed conflict in the Western Hemisphere, 1492 to the present|edition=2nd|volume=1|page=441|author=David F. Marley|publisher=ABC-CLIO, Inc.|location=Santa Barbara, California|year=2008|isbn=978-1-59884-100-8|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DkgGVTOr2EsC&dq=%22Rafaela+Herrera%22+1762&pg=PA441|accessdate=2011-04-23}}</ref> In 1781, King [[Charles III of Spain]] issued a royal [[decree]] granting Rafaela Herrera a [[pension]] for life, as a reward for her heroic defense during that battle.<ref name=Dario2005>{{cite book|title=Selected Writings: Rubén Dario|page=[https://archive.org/details/selectedwritings00dari/page/535 535]|author=Rubén Darío|authorlink=Rubén Darío|editor=Ilan Stavans|publisher=Penguin Books Ltd.|location=London|year=2005|isbn=0-14-303936-9|url=https://archive.org/details/selectedwritings00dari|url-access=registration|quote=Rafaela.|accessdate=2011-04-23}}</ref>

After Spain entered the [[American Revolutionary War]] in 1779, Major General [[Sir John Dalling, 1st Baronet|John Dalling]], the [[List of Governors of Jamaica|British governor]] and commander-in-chief of [[Jamaica]], proposed a second naval [[expeditionary warfare|expedition]] to Nicaragua. The goal was to sail up the San Juan River to Lake Nicaragua and capture the town of Granada, which would effectively cut [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish America]] in half as well as provide potential access to the [[Pacific Ocean]]. During this expedition, which took place in 1780 and later became known as the [[San Juan Expedition (1780)|San Juan Expedition]], Colonel [[John Polson (British Army officer)|John Polson]] and Captain [[Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson|Horatio Nelson]] led a British attack against the fortress.<ref name=Peary1889/> At the time, the Spanish garrison consisted of 228 men under the command of [[Juan de Ayssa]]<ref name=Squier>{{cite book|title=Nicaragua: its people, scenery, monuments, and the proposed interoceanic canal|volume=I|pages=110–1|author=Ephraim George Squier|authorlink=E. G. Squier|publisher=Longman, Brown, Green and Longmans|location=London|year=1852|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ALF6AAAAMAAJ&dq=%22Diego+Machuca%22+nicaragua&pg=PA193|accessdate=2011-04-23}}</ref> (who later served as Governor of Nicaragua from 1783 to 1787). The expeditionary force succeeded in capturing the fortress on April 29, 1780,<ref name=Squier/> despite the fact that it consisted of only 200 men.<ref name=Peary1889>{{cite journal|author=Robert Edwin Peary|authorlink=Robert Peary|title=The Río San Juan de Nicaragua|journal=Journal of the American Geographical Society of New York|volume=21|pages=57–86|year=1889|issn=1949-8535|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=WB5fcmR-mR8C&dq=Gallardillo+pirate+granada&pg=PA61|accessdate=2011-04-23|doi=10.2307/196656|jstor=196656|url-access=subscription}}</ref> The 22-year-old Nelson, in command of {{HMS|Hinchinbrook|1778|6}}, was responsible for leading his men through dense jungle to attack the fortress from a hill in the rear. The British captured the fortress and occupied it for nine months, finally abandoning it in January 1781.<ref name=Squier/> The hill is named ''Lomas de Nelson'' to this day.

==Geography==
The municipality of El Castillo is remote from major population centers, with dense vegetation and adverse weather conditions. The [[topography|topographic features]] include hilly soil, with good conditions for agriculture and livestock. The village is situated in a [[tropical rainforest climate]], with wet weather conditions. The average temperature is about 25°C, and annual rainfall ranges from 2,800 to 4,000&nbsp;mm.<ref name=INIFOM>{{cite web|title=El Castillo: Ficha municipal |author=Nicaraguan Institute of Municipal Development |publisher=Instituto Nicaragüense de Fomento Municipal |location=Managua |language=Spanish |year=2011 |url=http://www.inifom.gob.ni/municipios/documentos/RIO%20SAN%20JUAN/el_castillo.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110928183702/http://www.inifom.gob.ni/municipios/documentos/RIO%20SAN%20JUAN/el_castillo.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-date=2011-09-28 |accessdate=2011-04-22 }}</ref> To the north of El Castillo municipality is the municipality of [[Bluefields]]. To the south is the Republic of Costa Rica, to the east is the municipality of [[San Juan de Nicaragua]] and to the west is the municipality of [[San Carlos, Río San Juan|San Carlos]].<ref name=INIFOM/> The [[county seat]] is located 350&nbsp;km from the city of [[Managua]]. The total area is 1,656 km2 (639.4 sq mi).<ref name=INIFOM/>

==Economy==
Historically the municipality has been engaged in [[forestry]], [[logging]] of tropical [[hardwood]]s, extraction of other [[forest product]]s such as [[Botánica|medicinal herbs]] and other [[non-timber forest product]]s.<ref name=INIFOM/> From 1948 to 1951 and then from 1968 to 1970, the municipality of El Castillo was a major producer of bananas. The bananas were transported to [[Limón|Puerto Limón]] in Costa Rica, where they were sold by U.S. companies. Cultivation later declined as a result of [[black sigatoka]] (a disease caused by ''Mycosphaerella fijiensis'', a [[fungus]] of the [[Ascomycota]] phylum).<ref name=Manfut/>

The main economic activity continues to be [[agriculture]], most notably corn and bananas.<ref name=INIFOM/> There are also a number of small businesses that serve the needs of the local and tourist population. These include 25 small markets, 9 bakeries, 2 butcher shops, 7 restaurants, 20 bars (6 just for tourists), 4 hotels, 4 sport shops, 5 carpenters shops, 2 gas stations for boats, and 1 mill.<ref name=Manfut/>

==Political subdivisions==
There are 27 [[comarca]]s in the municipality of El Castillo: [[El Castillo (village)|El Castillo]], [[Boca de Sábalos]] (where the municipality administration is located), Santa Cruz, Sábalo, San Antonio, Mauricio Gutiérrez, Laureano Mairena, Marlon Zelaya, Marcelo, Buena Vista, Gordiano, Guasimo, La Quesada, Las Colinas, Che Guevara, Boca de Escalara, Las Maravillas, Chanchon, Libertad Sandino, El Bosque, Laguna Blanca, La Ñoca, Sompopera, El Roble, Monico, Bartola, Rivera del Río San Juan and Río Cariño.<ref name=INIFOM/>

==Education==
Of roughly 5,000 citizens of school age in the municipality,<ref name=Censo2005/> only roughly 1,800 attend school. This is largely due to the great distances between home and school facilities, lack of roads and suitable vehicles, and the difficult economic situations of many families of the community.<ref name=Manfut>{{cite web|title=El Castillo del Río San Juan |language=Spanish |url=http://www.manfut.org/juan/castillo.html|accessdate=2011-04-22}}</ref> At the preschool level, there are 179 students enrolled, 4 schools, and 9 teachers. At the primary school level, there are 1015 students enrolled, 10 schools, and 32 teachers. At the secondary school level, there are 178 students enrolled, 1 school, and 5 teachers.<ref name=Manfut/>
