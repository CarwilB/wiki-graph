'''Santa Juana''' is a [[List of cities in Chile|city]] and [[Communes of Chile|commune]] of the  [[Concepción Province, Chile|Concepción Province]]  in the [[Bío Bío Region]] of [[Chile]]. It lies south and west of the [[Biobío River]] in the valley of [[Catiray|Catirai]] and is 48 kilometers from [[Concepción, Chile]].

== History ==
The [[Mapuche]] originally named the valley where Santa Juana is now located the ''Valley of [[Catirai]]'', and the inhabitants ''Catirayen''.<ref>Montero de Tortora, Lo que fue Catirai y es Santa Juana de Guadalcazar. pg. 6,7</ref>  This town originated with a fort established in March 1626 by Governor [[Luis Fernández de Córdoba y Arce]] and was named ''Santa Juana de Guadalcázar'' in memory of the wife of the [[viceroy of Peru]], [[Diego Fernández de Córdoba, Marquis of Guadalcázar]].  Evacuated in 1641, it was repaired and expanded in 1648 by the governor [[Martín de Mujica y Buitrón]].  It was destroyed by the Mapuches in 1722, but restored two years later.

In 1739 Governor [[José Antonio Manso de Velasco]] made important adjustments, improving and properly supplying the fortress and constructing between the river and the small Laguna de Rayenantú behind it, deep ditches, that turned the enclosure into an island, and increasing its population to serve as its garrison for the defense of the region and the wooded valleys of the southern mountainous area. Governor [[Antonio de Guill y Gonzaga]] obtained the title of "Villa" for the town in 1765,<ref name=santajuanafuertehistoria>I. Municipalidad de Santa Juana, 2007. Antecedentes Históricos. Estudios realizados por la Universidad de Concepción en los empalzamientos del Fuerte Santa Juana de Gualdalcázar. Disponible en http://www.santajuana.cl/Fuerte%20Historico.asp {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080417214132/http://www.santajuana.cl/Fuerte%20Historico.asp |date=2008-04-17 }}</ref> but the wars with the Indians did not allow it to make any progress even though it constituted the refuge of the extinguished towns of Coya, Monterey and San Jerónimo. It was also one of the towns burned in 1821 at the order of [[Vicente Benavides]] the rebellious royalist [[guerrilla]].  It was later ruined by the [[earthquake]] on February 20, 1835.  As a result of this last disaster the town was six years later moved to its present more suitable location.

Santa Juana was head of the [[Department of Lautaro]] between 1841<ref name=santajuanaanexo>I. Municipalidad de Santa Juana, 2007. Anexos: Datos Históricos de Nuestra Comuna. Disponible en {{cite web |url=http://www.santajuana.cl/anexos.asp |title=::::www.santajuana.cl:::: |accessdate=2008-09-19 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20080402050611/http://www.santajuana.cl/anexos.asp |archivedate=2008-04-02 }}</ref> and 1865.<ref name="coroneldesarrollo">I. Municipalidad de Coronel, 2007. Historia de Coronel. Coronel: Su desarrollo urbano y poblacional. Disponible en [https://web.archive.org/web/20070607170107/http://www.coronel.cl/historia/desarrollo.htm]</ref> On May 30, 1865, [[Coronel, Chile|Coronel]] became the new capital of the department.<ref name="coroneldesarrollo"/>  With the Law of Independent Communes, December 22, 1891, the Decree of Creation of Municipalities was promulgated,<ref name="santajuanaanexo"/> with which created the Municipality of Santa Juana, in which Santa Juana administers Santa Juana, Santo Domingo and San Jerónimo, whose boundaries were determined by the decrees of November 13, 1885, March 29 and December 1, 1886.

==Demographics==
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Santa Juana spans an area of {{convert|731.2|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 12,713 inhabitants (6,357 men and 6,356 women). Of these, 7,095 (55.8%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 5,618 (44.2%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 6.3% (756 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

==Administration==
As a commune, Santa Juana is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an [[alcalde]] who is directly elected every four years. The 2008-2012 alcalde is Angel Castro Medina ([[Christian Democratic Party (Chile)|PDC]]).<ref name="Munitel"/><ref name="Official"/>

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Santa Juana is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Sergio Bobadilla ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]]) and Clemira Pacheco ([[Socialist Party of Chile|PS]]) as part of the 45th electoral district, (together with [[Tomé]], [[Penco]], [[Florida, Chile|Florida]], [[Hualqui]], [[Coronel, Chile|Coronel]]). The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Alejandro Navarro Brain ([[Broad Social Movement|MAS]]) and Hosain Sabag Castillo (PDC) as part of the 12th senatorial constituency (Biobío-Cordillera).
