'''Santiago Yucuyachi''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Oaxaca|municipality]] located in the western part of the [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[Administrative divisions of Mexico|state]] of [[Oaxaca]]. It is part of the [[Silacayoápam District]] in the north of the [[Mixteca Region|Mixteca region]]. The name is formed from the union of ''<span lang="es" dir="ltr">Santiago</span>'', referring to [[James the Great|Saint James the Apostle]], with <span lang="es" dir="ltr">''Yucuyachi''</span> (from [[Mixtec languages|Mixtec]]: ''<span lang="mix" dir="ltr">yucu</span>'', "mountain", and ''<span lang="mix" dir="ltr">yachi</span>'', interpreted as "thorn" or "brief/short"). Literally, Mountain of Thorns, referring to the local vegetation, while the alternative meaning relates to the region's warm climate and fertile lands, which allow crops to mature more quickly than in the surrounding areas.

== Geography ==
Santiago Yucuyachi serves as the [[municipal seat]] of the Santiago Yucuyachi municipality, which encompasses an area of 90.311 km<sup>2</sup> (34.87 sq mi) and as of the 2020 [[Censo General de Población y Vivienda|census]] a population of 998.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Santiago Yucuyachi: Economy, employment, equity, quality of life, education, health and public safety |url=https://www.economia.gob.mx/datamexico/en/profile/geo/santiago-yucuyachi |access-date=2026-03-28 |website=Data México |language=en}}</ref> It is located in a rugged and mountainous area of Oaxaca.

The municipality is bordered to the north by [[Santiago Tamazola]], to the northwest by [[San Lorenzo Victoria]], to the west by Santa Rosa de Juárez, and to the south by [[San Martín del Estado|San Martin del Estado]].

== Demographics ==
A large proportion of the population is of [[Mixtec]] descent, and the Mixtec language is widely spoken. The region experiences a high rate of [[Mixtec transnational migration]] to the [[United States]] and [[Remittance|remittances]] are important to the local [[Economy of Mexico|economy]]. 15% of the population is in extreme [[Poverty in Mexico|poverty]] and 53.3% is in moderate poverty.<ref name=":0" />

== Infrastructure ==

=== Telecommunications ===
Santiago Yucuyachi has limited [[Mass media in Mexico|telecommunications]] infrastructure. [[3G|Third-generation (3G)]] cellular service is available through [[Telcel]] via a [[cellular repeater]] installed by the municipality. The [[Comisión Federal de Electricidad|Federal Electricity Commission (CFE)]] has also installed [[Red Compartida]] antennas in Santiago Yucuyachi (October 2022) and Santa Rosa (September 2025) to expand [[Cellular network|mobile coverage]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Mapa Torres BTS |url=https://mapabts.cfeteit.mx/ |access-date=2026-03-27 |website=CFE - Telecomunicaciones e Internet para Todos}}</ref>
[[File:Santiago Yucuyachi InternetParaTodos sign 2024.jpg|thumb|Sign in town center with instructions on using community network]]
The municipality lacks traditional fixed-line [[Internet in Mexico|internet service providers]]. [[Internet access]] is instead provided through locally operated [[Wireless network|wireless networks]], including InternetParaTodos, a community network developed by Kelvin Orduna.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Philanthropy – Oaxaca Internet Connectivity Project |url=https://kelvinorduna.com/philanthropy/oaxaca-internet-connectivity-project |access-date=2026-03-28 |website=kelvinorduna.com |language=en}}</ref> The network delivers connectivity to individual users and government institutions using [[Wireless Internet service provider|wireless internet service provider (WISP)]] infrastructure. Public access is also available at [[Wi-Fi hotspot|Wi-Fi hotspots]] throughout the municipality via voucher-based (Spanish: ''<span lang="es" dir="ltr">ficha</span>'') [[Captive portal|systems]] that provide time-limited connectivity. Smaller-scale providers also operate within the municipality, typically offering single-network coverage.

The municipality maintains infrastructure for local [[Terrestrial television|over-the-air television]] signals. [[Multichannel television|Subscription television]] services are also available through providers such as [[Sky México|Sky]] and VeTV.

=== Transportation ===
The municipality is connected by [[State highway#Mexico|regional roads]] linking it to nearby municipalities, including [[Huajuapan de León]] and Santiago Tamazola. These roads form part of the 167 km (103.8 mi) Huajuapan–[[Mariscala de Juárez|Mariscala]]–Tamazola–[[Silacayoápam]] highway undergoing rehabilitation as part of the Lázaro Cárdenas del Río federal infrastructure program, with approximately 668 million pesos allocated to this highway.<ref>{{Cite web |title=BNamericas - Mexico launches US$250mn highway program for... |url=https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/mexico-launches-us250-highway-program-for-oaxaca-guerrero-states |access-date=2026-03-28 |website=BNamericas.com |language=en}}</ref>

There are no formal [[Taxis of Mexico|taxi]] services; instead ''[[Motorcycle taxi|mototaxis]]'' serve as the primary means of local [[Transportation in Mexico|transport]], including connections to Santiago Tamazola.

Travel time to Huajuapan de León is approximately 2.5 to 3 hours, depending on road and weather conditions..

=== Public Works ===
During the 2023-2025 [[Ayuntamiento|municipal administration]], public works projects have included the paving of Avenida Lázaro Cárdenas in the municipal seat using [[Cement|hydraulic concrete]]. The project covered approximately 970 square meters and was funded through the federal Contribution Fund for Municipal Social Infrastructure and Territorial Divisions of the Federal District (FAISMUN) program, with an allocation of approximately 1.1 million pesos.

== Healthcare ==
[[File:Santiago Yucuyachi Centro de Salud 2025.jpg|thumb|Health care center with municipal ambulance parked on the right]]
Santiago Yucuyachi has a basic [[Healthcare in Mexico|health care center]] provider [[primary care]] services staffed by a physician and a nurse.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=Plan Municipal de Desarrollo con Enfoque Participativo 2023-2025 – Municipio de Santiago Yucuyachi, Silacayoápam, Oaxaca |url=https://sisplade.oaxaca.gob.mx/bm_sim_services/PlanesMunicipales/2023_2025_/501.pdf |access-date=2026-03-27 |website=Instituto de Planeación para el Bienestar}}</ref> According to census data, most residents are affiliated with [[Universal health care|public healthcare systems]], including INSABI, ISSTE, and the [[Mexican Social Security Institute|Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS)]]. 

Medical attention is restricted in scope and availability. The health care center operates limited hours and visiting medical personnel from the hospital in Santiago Tamazola provide additional services on a periodic basis. In cases requiring [[Individualized medicine|specialized care]] or [[Medical emergency|emergencies]], patients must travel to Santiago Tamazola or Huajuapan de León for treatment.

== Education ==
Santiago Yucuyachi has a relatively high rate of [[Literacy|illiteracy]] (19.7%) in comparison with national and state statistics.<ref name=":0" /> Litearcy rates vary significantly by age group, with 97.6% literacy among individuals aged 15-24, compared to 77% among those aged 25 and older.<ref name=":1" /> According to municipal statistics, nearly 60% of the population has completed [[primary education]], while only 8.8-10.5% have completed [[secondary education]].<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /> Approximately 3.1% of residents have attained [[higher education]].<ref name=":1" />
[[File:Salon de escuela en Santiago Yucuyachi 2024.jpeg|thumb|360x360px|School building used for storage]]
Basic [[Education in Mexico|education]] is provided through six public institutions located in the municipal seat and the locality of Santa Rosa de Juárez. These include two [[Preschool|preschools]], two [[Primary school|primary schools]], and two [[telesecundaria]] ([[Education in Mexico#Junior high school|lower secondary]]) schools.<ref name=":1" />

The municipality does not have institutions offering upper secondary or higher education. As a result, students must travel to nearby municipalities such as Santiago Tamazola, Silacayoápam, Huajuapan de León, or Oaxaca de Juárez to continue their studies. This requirement contributes to increased household costs which attribute to migration patterns among youth.

Municipal planning documents identify deficiencies in educational infrastructure, noting that many school facilities are in deteriorated conditions and do not meet adequate standards for effective educational use.<ref name=":1" />
