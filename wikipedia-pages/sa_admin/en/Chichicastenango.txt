'''Chichicastenango''', also known as '''Santo Tomás Chichicastenango''', is a [[town]], with a population of 71,394 (2018 census),<ref>[https://www.citypopulation.de/en/guatemala/cities/ Citypopulation.de] Population of cities & towns in Guatemala</ref> and the [[municipal seat]] for the surrounding [[municipality]] of the same name in the [[El Quiché]] [[Departments of Guatemala|department of Guatemala]]. It is located in a mountainous region about {{convert|140|km|mi|abbr=on}} northwest of [[Guatemala City]], at an altitude of 1,965 m (6,447&nbsp;ft).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://ojoconmipisto.com/chichicastenango/|title=Chichicastenango, Quiché |date=11 October 2013 |publisher=Ojoconmipisto.com|access-date=12 October 2017}}</ref> The [[Spanish Empire|Spanish]] [[conquistador]]s gave the town its name from the [[Nahuatl]] name used by their allied soldiers from [[Tlaxcala]]: Tzitzicaztenanco, or ''City of Nettles''. Its original name was ''Chaviar''.

Chichicastenango is a [[K'iche' people|K'iche' Maya]] cultural centre. According to the 2012 census, 98.5% of the municipality's population is indigenous Mayan K'iche. Of the population, 21% speak only K'iche, 71% speak both K'iche and Spanish, and the remaining 8% speak only Spanish.<ref name="auto"/>

==Market==
[[File:Chichicastenango market.jpg|left|thumb|Chichicastenango Market]]
Chichicastenango hosts market days on Thursdays and Sundays where vendors sell handicrafts, food, flowers, pottery, wooden boxes, condiments, medicinal plants, candles, pom and [[copal]] ([[Incense|traditional incense]]), [[Lime (mineral)|cal]] ([[limestone|lime stones]] for preparing tortillas), grindstones, pigs and chickens, [[machetes]], and other tools.

Among the items sold are textiles, particularly women's blouses. Masks used by dancers in traditional dances, such as the [[Baile de la Conquista|Dance of the Conquest]], are also manufactured in Chichicastenango.

===Church of Santo Tomás===
{{main|Iglesia de Santo Tomás}}
Next to the market is the 400-year-old church of Santo Tomás. It is built atop a [[Pre-Columbian]] temple platform, and the steps originally leading to a temple of the pre-Hispanic [[Maya civilization]] remain venerated.  K'iche' [[Maya priesthood|Maya priests]] still use the church for their rituals, burning [[incense]] and candles. In special cases, they burn a chicken for the gods. Each of the 20 stairs that lead up to the church stands for one month of the [[Maya calendar]] year. Another key element of Chichicastenango is the Cofradia of [[Pascual Abaj]], which is an ancient carved stone venerated nearby and the Maya priests perform several rituals there. Writing on the stone records the doings of a king named [[Tohil]] (Fate).<ref>[http://www.epigraphy.org/volume_17.htm Epigraphic Society Occasional Publications, Vol. 17, 1988 ''A Decipherment of the Chichicastenango Stone'' (22 pp) John S. Carroll -p 31 ]</ref>

The [[Chichicastenango Regional Museum]] lies in its grounds.

==Geography==
Chichicastenango is composed of the municipal seat and 81 rural communities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.inforpressca.com/chichicastenango/plandesarrollo.pdf |title=Santo Tomás Chichicastenango - Plan de Desarrollo Municipal|access-date=1 August 2008 |year=2002 |publisher=Municipalidad de Chichicastenango }}</ref> Nearby village communities include [[Paquixic]] (1.0&nbsp;nm), [[Chucam]] (1.0&nbsp;nm), [[Chujupen]] (1.4&nbsp;nm), [[Camanibal]] (2.2&nbsp;nm), [[Chontala]] (2.2&nbsp;nm) and [[Chucojom]] (1.0&nbsp;nm).
