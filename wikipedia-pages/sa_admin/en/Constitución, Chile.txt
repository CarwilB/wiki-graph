'''Constitución''' ({{IPA|es|konstituˈsjon}}) is a city and commune of [[Talca Province]], [[Maule Region]], [[Chile]]. It was historically a popular [[seaside resort]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Tourism|url=http://www.intendenciamaule.gov.cl/info_turismo.html|website=Government of Chile|access-date=2016-05-26|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150607173515/http://www.intendenciamaule.gov.cl/info_turismo.html|archive-date=2015-06-07|url-status=dead}}</ref> However, following the growth of the industrial sector ([[paper]] and [[Wood pulp|pulp]]) tourism has since declined. Constitución is a minor port in [[Chile]].

== History ==
Before the arrival of the Spanish, the area that would later be the city of Constitución was inhabited by the [[Chango people]] and the [[Mapuche]] tribe.  Both indigenous groups used the area for fishing and seasonal habitation.  When the Spaniards came to the region, the European explorers, and those who followed, used the area as a port for their [[galleons]] and merchant ships on their voyages to South American and other [[Pacific Ocean|Pacific]] ports.{{citation needed|date=December 2014}}

=== ''Nueva Bilbao'' ===

While numerous attempts were made to establish a permanent settlement in the area, Santiago Oñederra was the first successful colonizing pioneer, circa 1791.  The founding of the settlement was proposed by Oñederra to the Chilean government, and authorized by Governor [[Ambrosio O'Higgins]] in 1794.  The settlement was originally named New Bilbao ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''Nueva [[Bilbao]]'') after the [[Basques|Basque]] city in Northern [[Spain]].<ref name=cambionombre />

=== Renaming to ''Constitución'' ===

In 1828, New Bilbao was renamed ''Constitución''.  On 4 August 1828, Congress approved the designation of Constitución as a major Chilean port.  Four days later, Vice President Jose Antonio Pinto signed the decree.<ref name=cambionombre>Aravena, Luz K., et al.; 2007. [https://web.archive.org/web/20080412091635/http://www.luz-katherine.com/constitucion/historia/nombre.htm ''Cambio de Nombre'' ("Change of name")]. From: "History", Constitución in Internet Microsite. (In Spanish)</ref>

Beginning in 1828, Constitución was designated as part of [[Maule Region]] and later included in the [[Talca Province]].<ref name="deptoconstitución">Aravena, Luz K., et al.; 2007. [https://web.archive.org/web/20090111120309/http://www.luz-katherine.com/constitucion/historia/finaldesconocido.htm ''Un final desconocido'' ("An unknown destination")]. From: "History", Constitución in Internet Microsite. == Geography ==

The town is dominated both economically<ref name="Contaminacion en el rio">{{cite web|title=Toxic contamination|url=http://www.derechoalagua.cl/mapa-de-conflictos/contaminacion-toxica-de-celco-licancel-en-el-rio-mataquito/|website=Water rights}}</ref> and geographically by the large cellulose plant of [[Celulosa Arauco y Constitución]]. Celulosa Arauco y Constitución has had numerous legal cases with regards to contamination<ref>{{cite web|title=Pollution of the Sea|url=http://www.olca.cl/oca/chile/region07/celumaule02.htm|website=OCLA}}</ref> of the local environment. The artificial forests of eucalyptus and pinewood dominate the local landscape. Critics argue that this destroys the natural habitat for local wildlife and leaves the soil acidic and infertile.

== Demographics ==
According to the 2002 [[census]] of the [[National Statistics Institute (Chile)|National Statistics Institute]], Constitución spans an area of {{convert|1343.6|sqkm|0|abbr=on}} and has 46,081 inhabitants (23,389 men and 22,692 women). Of these, 37,202 (80.7%) lived in [[urban area]]s and 8,879 (19.3%) in [[rural area]]s. The population grew by 14.2% (5,741 persons) between the 1992 and 2002 censuses.<ref name= "INE"/>

== Administration ==
As a commune, Constitución is a third-level [[administrative division of Chile]] administered by a [[municipal council]], headed by an ''[[alcalde]]'' (mayor) who is directly elected every four years. In 2008-2012 the ''alcalde'' was Hugo Tilleria Torres, who was replaced by Carlos Valenzuela Gajardo.<ref name="Official"/>

Within the [[electoral divisions of Chile]], Constitución is represented in the [[Chamber of Deputies of Chile|Chamber of Deputies]] by Pablo Lorenzini ([[Christian Democratic Party|PDC]]) and Pedro Pablo Alvarez-Salamanca ([[Independent Democratic Union|UDI]]) as part of the 38th electoral district, together with [[Curepto]], [[Empedrado, Chile|Empedrado]], [[Pencahue]], [[Maule, Chile|Maule]], [[San Clemente, Chile|San Clemente]], [[Pelarco]], [[Río Claro, Chile|Río Claro]] and [[San Rafael, Chile|San Rafael]]. The commune is represented in the [[Senate of Chile|Senate]] by Juan Antonio Coloma Correa (UDI) and [[Andrés Zaldívar|Andrés Zaldívar Larraín]] (PDC) as part of the 10th senatorial constituency (Maule-North).

[[Celulosa Arauco y Constitución]] actively funds social and economic regeneration projects in constitucion, providing much needed funding for the municipality. Critics argue this dependence prevents the administration from being able to question issues regarding pollution with Arauco.

== Economy ==
{{See also|Maulino forest}}
The Maule region of which Constitucion is part has the second lowest income per capita in Chile, with only Aysen receiving less income per capita. The residents of Maule receive per capita less than 25% of the national average income and around 10% of the income per capita in Antofagasta. Illiteracy is currently 7% in Maule versus the 3% country wide average. The main employment sources are the primary sectors, fishing, farming, forestry and low level artesanal production.

One of the city's main employers is [[Celulosa Arauco y Constitución]], a wood pulp, engineered wood, and forestry company.  Until his 2007 death, the company was owned and controlled by Italian-born [[Santiago]] billionaire [[Anacleto Angelini]] and has since been taken over by his nephew, Roberto Angelini Rossi, current chairman of Angelini's holding company, [[AntarChile]]. Despite lawsuits and protests, AntarChile was given permission in 2006 to discharge pollutants from the plant into the sea.<ref>{{cite web|title=''Corema dio luz verde para que Celco Constitución bote residuos al mar'' (Corema gives green light for Celco to discharge residues into the sea)|url=http://www.olca.cl/oca/chile/region07/celumaule02.htm|publisher=OLCA (Observatorio Latinoamericano de Conflictos Ambientales)}}</ref> An earthquake and tsunami in 2010 caused the plant's closure for three months due to power outages and damage.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Belchior|first1=Fernanda|title=Arauco's Constitución mill to restart Thursday in Chile|url=http://woodbiomass.com/news/pulpandpaper/news/Araucou2019s-Constitucion-mill-to-restart-tomorrow-in-Chile.html|accessdate=31 December 2014|publisher=RISI Wood Biomass Markets|date=19 May 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141231190126/http://woodbiomass.com/news/pulpandpaper/news/Araucou2019s-Constitucion-mill-to-restart-tomorrow-in-Chile.html|archive-date=31 December 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

== Transportation ==

Railway expansion first came to the Maule Region in mid-1889 when rail-line construction began by the North and South American Company.  Service began on August 13, 1892, when lines were opened between [[Talca]] and Curtiduría. The next railway portion to be completed was to Pichamán on November 1, 1894. Rails were laid along the northern bank of the [[Maule River]], opposite Constitución.  Constitución's first station was established in 1902.<ref name=Buscarril>{{cite web|title=Buscarril|url=http://www.tmsa.cl/link.cgi/servicios/buscarril/historia.act|website=Chilean Train operator|access-date=2014-12-31|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141230090201/http://www.tmsa.cl/link.cgi/servicios/buscarril/historia.act|archive-date=2014-12-30|url-status=dead}}</ref> and remained in use until 1915 when a new station was constructed.<ref name="Buscarril"/>

Constitución is home for the end-of-the-line station of the ''Ramal de Maule'' also known as the [[Ramal Talca-Constitución]], a rural train that runs an 80 kilometer (50 mile) rail line from [[Talca, Chile|Talca]].  The scenic train affords views of the Andean foothills as well as the Pacific Ocean.<ref name="the last ramal">{{cite web|url=http://www.lan.com/onlyinsouthamerica/tag/ramal/|publisher=Only in South America|title=Nostalgic Train Ride: From Talca to Constitución|author=Eileen Smith|website=LAN}}</ref> Currently, the Ramal is facing shutdown due to lack of use.  From the late 1800s on, rail travel had been Chile's main form of transportation. In the 21st century, however, Chileans have largely changed their transportation   preference to buses and private cars.  As a result of this switch, passenger rail travel has become obsolete.<ref name="Chile is yours">{{cite web|publisher=Chile es tuyo, Chilean national tourist service|title=Ramal Talca Constitución (in Spanish)|url=http://www.chileestuyo.cl/regiones/region-del-maule/valle-del-maule/lugar/ramal-talca-constitucion.html|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150108023906/http://www.chileestuyo.cl/regiones/region-del-maule/valle-del-maule/lugar/ramal-talca-constitucion.html|archivedate=2015-01-08}}</ref>

The city is served by [[Quivolgo International Airport]].
