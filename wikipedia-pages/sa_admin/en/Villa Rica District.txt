'''Villa Rica District''' is one of eight [[Districts of Peru|districts]] of [[Oxapampa Province]] in [[Pasco Department]], [[Peru]]. The district has an area of {{convert|896|sqkm|sqmi}} and a population of 18,673 in 2017.<ref>{{cite web |title=Villa Rica (District) |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/peru/pasco/admin/oxapampa/190307__villa_rica/ |website=City Population |access-date=23 June 2020}}</ref> The capital of the district is the town of Villa Rica which had a population of 11,900 in 2017.<ref>{{cite web |title=Villa Rica |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/php/peru-pasco.php?cityid=1903070001 |website=City Population |access-date=23 June 2020}}</ref> The district is the location of the [[Cerro de la Sal]] or Salt Mountain, an important gathering place for [[indigenous people of Peru|indigenous people]] of the region and a center of [[Franciscans|Franciscan]] missionary activity in the 17th and 18th centuries. The town was founded in 1928 by settlers of German and Austrian origin from the town of [[Pozuzo]].<ref>{{cite book|author=Dilwyn Jenkins|title=The Rough Guide to Peru|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Aj8VzlKRlL4C&pg=PA499|date=2009-07-01|publisher=Rough Guides Limited|isbn=978-1-84836-053-2|pages=487}}</ref>

The District of Villa Rica is on the eastern slopes of the [[Andes]] mountains in an area of abundant rainfall and mild temperatures. It is located in the ecological zone of the [[Peruvian Yungas]], or [[:es:Selva Alta|Ceja de Selva]] ("eyebrow of the jungle"), the transition zone between the low jungles of the [[Amazon Basin]] and the [[Andes]] highlands.  Elevations in the district range from about {{convert|800|m|ft}} to about {{convert|2400|m|ft}}.  Where not cleared for agriculture, the vegetation is tropical and sub-tropical forests.<ref>{{cite book |last1=The Nature Conservancy |title=Planificacion para la Conservacion Ecoregional de las Yungas Peruanas |date=2006 |publisher=Facultad de Ciencias Forestales, Universidad Nacional Agraria La Molina |location=Peru}}</ref>

==Cerro de la Sal==
[[File:Puente de paucartambo - panoramio.jpg|thumb|right|250px|The Paucartambo River is the southern border of Villa Rica District.]]
The Cerro de la Sal (Mountain of Salt) is located about {{convert|7|km|mile}} north of the town of Villa Rica at {{coord|10.683|S|75.267|W}}.<ref>Google Earth</ref> In pre-Columbian and colonial Peru the indigenous people exploited the salt and transported it downriver by raft to a large number of peoples in the [[Amazon Basin]]. The Cerro de la Sal was in the territory of the [[Asháninka]] and was also a source of salt for the [[Amuesha people|Amuesha]] and other peoples in the [[Amazon Basin]].<ref name="Tibesar">{{cite journal |last1=Tibesar |first1=Antonine S. |title=The Salt Trade among the Montana Indians of the Tarma Area of Eastern Peru |journal=Primitive Man |date=October 1950 |volume=23 |issue=4 |page=103}}. Downloaded from JSTOR.</ref>

The Spanish realized the strategic importance of the Cerro de la Sal early in the 17th century.<ref name="Varese">{{cite book |last1=Varese |first1=Stefano |title=Salt of the Mountain |date=2002 |publisher=University of Oklahoma Press |location=Norman |isbn=0806134461 |pages=62, 70}}</ref> [[Roman Catholic]] [[Franciscan]] missionaries were attracted to the Cerro de la Sal because of the seasonal congregation of large numbers of indigenous people there. A large number of Christian missions collectively called the Cerro de la Sal missions were established in the region. In 1742, a messianic movement headed by [[Juan Santos Atahualpa]] emptied the missions and the Spanish were expelled from Cerro de la Sal for more than 100 years.{{sfn|Brown and Fernandez|1991|pages=25-53}} In the late 19th and early 20th century settlers of European and Andean origin dispossessed the indigenous people.<ref name="OAS">{{cite web |title=Chapter Eight: Human Occupation of the Central Selva of Peru |url=http://www.oas.org/usde/publications/Unit/oea37e/ch11.htm |website=Organization of American States |access-date=25 June 2020}}</ref>

==Climate==
Villa Rica has a Af (tropical, humid, no pronounced dry season) climate under the [[Köppen Classification]] system. However, the climate borders on Aw (tropical savannah with a dry season) and Cfb (subtropical mountain climate, "eternal spring.")  Measured by the [[Trewartha climate classification]] system the climate is Arbb (tropical rainforest with warm summers and winters). Villa Rica's climate is modified by its altitude which results in lower average temperatures year than low-elevation tropics. The monthly deviation in average temperatures between the hottest and coldest months is only 1.8 degrees C (3 degrees F). The austral winter months of June and July are fairly dry, but precipitation is abundant during the remainder of the year.
