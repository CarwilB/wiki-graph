'''Mayarí''' is a municipality and town in the [[Holguín Province]] of [[Cuba]].

==History==
The origins of the city date back to 1757 in [[Spanish Cuba]],<ref name=guije/> when the first farms were established here by immigrant colonists. On 19 January 1879 the city became the seat of Mayarí Municipality.

==Geography==
The municipality is divided into the [[barrio]]s and hamlets of Barajagua, Cabonico, Cajimaya, Chavaleta Norte, Chavaleta Sur, Guayabo, Juan Vicente, Mateo Sánchez, Punta de Tabaco, Río Frío, [[Cayo Saetía|Sae-Tía]], San Gregario Norte, San Gregorio Sur and Santa Isabel.<ref name=guije>{{Cite web|url=http://www.guije.com/pueblo/municipios/omayari/index.htm|title=Mayarí|author=Guije.com|accessdate=2007-10-09|language=es}}</ref> [[Birán]], the birthplace of [[Fidel Castro|Fidel]] and [[Raúl Castro]], was part of Mayarí until the 1976 reform, when it became part of the neighboring [[Cueto, Cuba|Cueto]] municipality.<ref>{{OSM|r|5904273|Cueto}}</ref><ref>{{OSM|n|1336109283|Birán}}</ref>

Among other barrios or neighborhoods in this municipality are: Felton (on Cajimaya Bay, once the seaport for the Bethlehem Cuba Iron Mines Company -Bethlehem Steel-), [[Guaro (Mayarí)|Guaro]], [[Guatemala, Cuba|Guatemala]] (on [[Nipe Bay]], previously named Preston and a central sugar mill operated by the [[United Fruit Company]]), [[Nicaro-Levisa]] (on Levisa Bay, originally named ''Lengua de Pajaro'' which translated into English means bird's tongue -as viewed from an air ride-), El Purio, Santa Rita, La Italiana, Mandinga. The last two being on the road to the open mines named Mina Martí where nickel and cobalt exploitation started in the beginnings of the twentieth century by Bethlehem Cuba Iron Mines Company (Bethlehem Steel) and Nicaro Nickel Company.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/nicaro_nickel_company Infos on archive.org]</ref>

==Demographics==
In 2022, the municipality of Mayarí had a population of 92,792.<ref name="citypopulation">{{Cite web |title=Cuba: Administrative Division (Provinces and Municipalities) - Population Statistics, Charts and Map |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/cuba/admin/ |access-date=2024-02-03 |website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref> With a total area of {{convert|1307|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}},<ref name=statoids>{{Cite web|url=http://www.statoids.com/ycu.html|title=Municipios of Cuba|author=Statoids|date=July 2003| accessdate=2007-10-09}}</ref> it has a population density of {{convert|71|/km2|/sqmi|abbr=on}}.

==Main sights==
The [[Sierra Cristal National Park]] ({{langx|es|link=no|Parque Nacional Sierra Cristal}}) is partly located in the Mayarí Municipality and partly in neighboring [[Sagua de Tánamo, Cuba|Sagua de Tánamo Municipality]].<ref name=rhc>{{Cite web|url=http://www.radiohc.cu/espanol/medioambiente/parques/sierracristal1.htm |title=Parque Nacional Sierra Cristal |author=Radio Habana |accessdate=2007-10-09 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20071004222518/http://www.radiohc.cu/espanol/medioambiente/parques/sierracristal1.htm |archivedate=October 4, 2007 |language=es |url-status=dead }}</ref> Other attractions include [[Cayo Saetía]], a scenic [[cay]] with beaches located within the municipality on the [[Nipe Bay|Bay of Nipe]], north of the Mayarí city.

==Popular culture==
The city's name Mayarí is in the 1940s song "''Voy para Mayarí''" ("I'm going to Mayarí"), by [[Compay Segundo]], and in his song "[[Chan Chan (song)|Chan Chan]]" on the [[Buena Vista Social Club (album)|Buena Vista Social Club album]].

It also appears in the song “''Las Mujeres de Mayarí''” (“The women of Mayarí”) in the same album.
