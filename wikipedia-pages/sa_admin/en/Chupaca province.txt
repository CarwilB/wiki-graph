'''Chupaca''', located in central [[Peru]], is one of the nine provinces that compose the [[Junín Region]], bordering to the north with the province of [[Concepción Province, Peru|Concepción]], to the east with the province of [[Huancayo Province|Huancayo]], to the south with the [[Huancavelica Region]], and to the west with the [[Lima Region]]. The province has a population of approximately 57,000 inhabitants, and the capital is [[Chupaca]].

== Geography ==
Chupaca is located {{convert|297|km|0|sp=us|abbr=~}} from Lima and has a temperate and dry climate. Located within the province is the lake [[Ñawinpukyu (Junín)|Ñawinpukyu]] {{convert|15|km|1|abbr=on}} west of the city [[Huancayo]], with surface water spanning {{convert|7|ha|0}}, where the breeding of trout, mule trips, and boating occur.

== Places of interest and importance ==
* Huayao - {{convert|12|km|1|abbr=on}} from [[Huancayo]]; facilities including a [[Geophysical]] Observatory, where meteorological studies, nuclear physics, and other topics are studied.
* Ñawinpukyu - The name of the lake comes from a [[Quechua languages|Quechua]] word meaning "eye spring". It is 5 minutes from the Ahuac and {{convert|11|km|0|sp=us}} from Huancayo. To the flank of the lake, there is a hill with the archaeological remains of the Arwaturu.

== History ==
Furthest back in the known history of the province of Chupaca the Arwaturu tribe located alongside the lake named Ñawinpukyu constructed buildings from stone and mud that aligned to the north and to the south in such a way that they received all the rays from the sun.

Low the Chongos; a town {{convert|20|km|0|sp=us}} south of [[Huancayo]] has a church dating back to 1540 with altars carved in [[baroque]] style.
