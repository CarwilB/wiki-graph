'''Mossoró''' ({{IPA|pt|mosoˈɾɔ}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] in the interior of the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Rio Grande do Norte]], recognized as the capital of the Brazilian semi-arid region.<ref>{{cite law|jurisdiction=|url=https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2017/lei-13568-21-dezembro-2017-785991-publicacaooriginal-154598-pl.html|title=Federal Law|number=13,568|date=21 December 2017}}</ref> Covering an area of approximately {{convert|2100|km2}}, it is the largest municipality in the state by area, located 281 kilometers from the state capital, [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]]. With a population of 264,577 inhabitants according to the [[2022 Brazilian census|2022 demographic census]], it ranks as the second most populous city in Rio Grande do Norte, after the capital, the most populous in the state's interior, and the [[List of cities in Brazil by population|108th in Brazil]].

Situated between two state capitals, [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]] and [[Fortaleza]], both connected by the [[BR-304 (Brazil highway)|BR-304]] highway that bypasses the municipality, Mossoró is one of the main cities in the Northeast's interior and is experiencing significant economic and infrastructural growth.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_mossoro-35391|publisher=Correiodatarde.com.br|title=Mossoró's Growth Highlighted by Isto É|date=15 October 2008|access-date=15 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62SOVy2RG?url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_mossoro-35391|archive-date=15 October 2011|url-status=live}}</ref> It is one of the most attractive cities in Brazil for investments.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-35536|publisher=Correiodatarde.com.br|title=Mossoró's Growth Gains National Attention|date=18 October 2008|access-date=15 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62SOdOEnQ?url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-35536|archive-date=15 October 2011|url-status=live}}</ref> The municipality is one of Brazil's largest producers of onshore oil, and its economy is notably driven by irrigated horticulture, largely oriented toward export.

Emancipated from [[Assu, Rio Grande do Norte|Assu]] in 1852, the municipality's history is marked by significant events, including the abolition of [[slavery]] in 1883, five years before the ''[[Lei Áurea]]'', the [[Celina Guimarães Viana|first female vote]] in Brazil, and the historic resistance against the bandit group led by [[Virgulino Ferreira da Silva|Lampião]] in 1927. A cultural hub of Rio Grande do Norte, Mossoró is also prominent in [[business tourism]]. Annual festivities attract numerous tourists, such as the Mossoró Cidade Junina, one of Brazil's largest [[Festa Junina|June festivals]], and the Auto da Liberdade, the country's largest open-air theatrical performance.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.defato.com/08_04_2011/ubiratan.php|title=Mossoró - RN|author=SALDANHA, Ubiratan|date=8 April 2011|publisher=Jornal de Fato|access-date=28 March 2012|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/66VGFAmDF?url=http://www.defato.com/08_04_2011/ubiratan.php|archive-date=28 March 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
The origin of the name "Mossoró" remains uncertain, though several theories exist. One suggests it derives from "Monxoró," the name attributed to the indigenous people who first inhabited the region. Another theory posits that it comes from "Mororó," a resilient and flexible tree.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/190406/conteudo/cotidiano.htm|title=Indigenous Artifacts Influence Architecture and Decoration|author=AMORIM, Mayara|date=19 April 2006|publisher=O Mossoroense|access-date=15 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62SN0XF5V?url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/190406/conteudo/cotidiano.htm|archive-date=15 October 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

According to current [[Portuguese orthography]] rules, the correct spelling is ''Moçoró'', as the letter "[[ç]]" is used for words of [[Tupi language|Tupi]] origin. The name, which comes from Tupi, means ''erosion, cut, or rupture''. Over time, the spelling evolved from ''mo-so-'roka'' to ''mossoró'' and finally to ''moçoró''. The term ''moçoroense'', referring to the municipality's residents, derives from the same root.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://emdiv.com.br/pt/brasil/obrasil/2429-mocoro-rio-grande-do-norte.html|publisher=Emdiv.com.br|title=Moçoró - Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=15 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62SMp0K2L?url=http://emdiv.com.br/pt/brasil/obrasil/2429-mocoro-rio-grande-do-norte.html|archive-date=15 October 2011|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.emdiv.com.br/brasil/obrasil.html?start=40&format=html&lang=pt|publisher=Emdiv.com.br|title=The Brazil that Brazil Doesn't Know|access-date=15 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62SMyEsVQ?url=http://www.emdiv.com.br/brasil/obrasil.html?start=40&format=html&lang=pt|archive-date=15 October 2011}}</ref>

== History ==
=== Origins ===
Around 1600, references to salt pans in the region suggest the initial settlement of what is now Mossoró. According to [[Luís da Câmara Cascudo]], a historian from Rio Grande do Norte, Dutchmen Gedeon Morris de Jonge and Elbert Smiente extracted salt from the area until around 1644.<ref name="Formação administrativa de Mossoró">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/riograndedonorte/mossoro.pdf|title=History - Mossoró/RN|publisher=IBGE Library|access-date=October 10, 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62KyGf8Vf?url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/riograndedonorte/mossoro.pdf|archive-date=October 10, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 1701, Fernando Martins Mascarenhas, governor of [[Pernambuco]], granted lands in Paneminha to the Carmo Convent in Recife, including ''sesmarias'' that remain part of Mossoró today. Additional lands were gradually allocated to [[Brazilians]] and [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]].<ref name="Formação administrativa de Mossoró"/> During the 18th century, farms were established along a river by landowners from other regions. The population was primarily composed of cowboys, breeders, and farm managers, as landowners typically resided elsewhere, such as in Natal or neighboring provinces such as [[Paraíba]] and [[Ceará]]. The first permanent settlers are believed to have been the Gamboa, Guilherme, and Ausentes families, who lived along the [[Apodi River|Mossoró River]] and gradually spread to areas as far as [[Apodi, Rio Grande do Norte|Apodi]].<ref name="Surgimento de Mossoró">{{cite web|url=http://juliao.eymard.sites.uol.com.br/hist.html|publisher=Juliao.eymard.sites.uol.com.br|title=How the City of Mossoró Emerged|access-date=October 14, 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62QXwlXPV?url=http://juliao.eymard.sites.uol.com.br/hist.html|archive-date=October 14, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the mid-18th century, around 1760, Portuguese sergeant-major Antônio de Souza Machado and his family relocated to the area, aiming to populate it. He owned the Santa Luzia farm and ordered the construction of the Santa Luzia chapel, a foundational milestone for Mossoró, officially established on August 5, 1772.<ref name="Formação administrativa de Mossoró"/><ref name="Surgimento de Mossoró"/>

=== 19th Century ===
On October 27, 1842, by provincial resolution no. 87, the district of Mossoró was created as part of the municipality of [[Assu]], and the parish of Santa Luzia was separated from Apodi, with the chapel elevated to the status of parish church. Until the appointment of its first vicar, Priest Antônio Joaquim Rodrigues, in 1844, the new parish was led on an interim basis by Father José Antônio Lopes da Silveira. On March 15, 1852, provincial law no. 246 separated the district and elevated Mossoró to the category of a village, making it a new municipality of Rio Grande do Norte. On March 23 of the same year, the district of São Sebastião was created, today the municipality of Governador Dix-Sept Rosado.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 39-40.</ref> Still that year, the first elections for the Municipal Council were held, which was formally inaugurated on January 24, 1853, presided over by Father Antônio Freire de Carvalho.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 37.</ref> In 1855, Priest Antônio Joaquim, parish vicar of Mossoró, created the Brotherhood of Santa Luzia, and starting in 1858, the chapel was demolished and rebuilt over the next ten years.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 43.</ref>

By provincial law no. 499 of May 23, 1861, the District Court of Mossoró was created, separated from the Court of Assu.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 47-48.</ref> Finally, on November 9, 1870, provincial law no. 620 elevated the village of Mossoró to the status of a city.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 54</ref> In 1872, the first demographic census of Brazil was conducted, and Mossoró ranked fourteenth among the 22 municipalities of the province, with exactly 8,000 inhabitants, of whom 293 were enslaved people.<ref name="geoftp.ibge.gov.br">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/evolucao_da_divisao_territorial_do_brasil/evolucao_da_divisao_territorial_do_brasil_1872_2010/evolucao_da_populacao_segundo_os_municipios.pdf|title=Evolução da população, segundo os municípios|author=IBGE|access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> On October 17 of that same year, the journalist Jeremias da Rocha Nogueira founded the newspaper ''O Mossoroense'',<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 147.</ref> and on December 5, the district of Areia Branca was created<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 55.</ref> (today corresponding to the territories of Areia Branca, Grossos, and Tibau), but it was extinguished on December 19, 1876.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 62.</ref> On August 18, 1873, the Municipal Council authorized the construction of the city’s first cemetery, now known as São Sebastião Cemetery. In that same year, the Masonic lodge of Mossoró was founded.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 57.</ref>

On August 30, 1875, at the headquarters of ''O Mossoroense'', around three hundred women, led by Anna Floriano, took to the streets in protest against the mandatory military draft. They held the district clerk hostage and publicly tore up the recruitment book and papers that forced Mossoró’s men to join the armed forces to fight in the Paraguayan War. Between 1877 and 1879, the Northeast suffered a severe drought that caused a mass exodus from the city to coastal areas—including most of the enslaved population—and in 1878, the city’s public jail was built.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 66.</ref>

In 1882, an abolitionist movement began with the goal of ending slavery in Mossoró, resulting in the founding of the Mossoroense Liberation Society on January 6, 1883.<ref>{{cite web |lang=pt-br |url=https://www.omossoroense.com.br/sociedade-libertadora-mossoroense/ |title=Sociedade Libertadora Mossoroense |author=Maia, Geraldo |date=January 11, 2024 |access-date=June 12, 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250612190406/https://www.omossoroense.com.br/sociedade-libertadora-mossoroense/ |archive-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> That year, only 86 enslaved people remained, forty of whom were freed by manumission in June, at the Masonic lodge. Effective abolition took place on September 30, 1883, and the city began to receive many fugitive enslaved people from other towns.<ref name="História de Mossoró"/>

On June 1, 1886, the district of São Sebastião was extinguished and then reinstated on April 2, 1887, the same year the first postal and telegraph agency was created.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 71.</ref> After the fall of the monarchy and the Proclamation of the Republic on November 15, 1889, the government of Rio Grande do Norte issued a decree dissolving the Mossoró City Council and creating a municipal intendency, which took effect on January 18, 1890.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 73.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 77.</ref> On November 22 of the same year, the district of Areia Branca was reinstated by decree,<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 75.</ref> becoming a municipality on February 16, 1892.

=== 1900 to 1926 ===
After the creation of Mossoró’s first secondary school on September 7, 1900, the bishop of the [[Diocese of Paraíba]], Adauto Aurélio de Miranda Henriques, founded the Santa Luzia Gymnasium (now Santa Luzia Diocesan College) on March 2, 1901, appointing Canon Estevam Dantas, the parish vicar, as its first director.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 79.</ref> The school was closed by the diocese in 1908 and reopened only in 1912, when Rio Grande do Norte came under the newly created Diocese of Natal (December 1909).<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 160.</ref>

On September 30, 1904, during the celebration of 21 years since abolition in Mossoró, the Monument of Liberty was inaugurated in what is today Redemption Square.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 81.</ref> On November 15, 1908, the state government created the September 30 School Group, opened on May 12 of the following year.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 82.</ref> Earlier, on December 12, 1908, Mossoró gained its first movie theater, the Almeida Castro Cinema, which operated inside the Grande Hotel.<ref name="defato.com"/>

[[File:Primeira locomotiva da Estrada de Ferro Mossoró-Porto Franco.jpg|thumb|left|First locomotive of the Mossoró-Porto Franco Railroad, in 1915]]
In 1912, another cinema — Politeama — arrived in the city, one year after opening in [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]],<ref name="defato.com"/> and the Franciscan sisters founded the Sacred Heart of Mary School on August 2.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 84-85.</ref> On September 13, 1913, nearly thirty years after abolition, the Mossoró intendency declared September 30 a municipal holiday. In 1914, the new public slaughterhouse was inaugurated<ref name="Rosado, p. 87"/> and the city’s first urban plan was drafted by engineer Henrique de Novaes.<ref name="Rocha, p. 42"/> In 1915, during yet another great [[drought]], the Mossoró–Porto Franco Railway (today Areia Branca) began operating,<ref name="Rosado, p. 87">[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 87.</ref> and on December 19, 1916, the Mossoró Electric Power and Improvements Company (''Empresa Força e Luz e Melhoramentos de Mossoró'') was founded,<ref name="Rocha, p. 42">[[#Bibliography|ROCHA]], 2005, p. 42.</ref> bringing electric lighting to the entire city,<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 87.</ref>  which replaced kerosene lamps.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 85.</ref> After its cornerstone was laid in October 1915, the Church of São Vicente was inaugurated in July 1919.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://ruraldemossoro.com.br/2023/07/20/capela-historica-de-sao-vicente-completa-104-anos/ |title=Capela Histórica de São Vicente completa 104 anos |date=July 20, 2023 |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref>

On January 19, 1922, by state decree, the Mossoró Primary Normal School was created (today Escola Estadual Jerônimo Rosado), inaugurated a month and a half later, on March 2. On September 7 of the same year, during the celebration of Brazil’s first centennial of independence, the Market Square was renamed Independence Square, and an obelisk was placed at its center.<ref>[[#Bibliography|CASCUDO]], 2001, p. 191.</ref> On July 23, 1926, the diocesan bishop of Natal, Dom José Pereira Alves, created the Parish of the Sacred Heart of Jesus, officially installed on August 1. The chapel, later elevated to parish church, had been built between 1904 and 1907.<ref>[[#Bibliography|ROSADO]], 2014, p. 140.</ref>

=== Resistance to Lampião's gang and the first female vote ===
[[File:Memorial da Resistência, Mossoró (RN).jpg|thumb|left|Main building of the Memorial to the Resistance of Mossoró, a museum featuring exhibitions on the 1927 invasion by Lampião's gang<ref name="vivaviver"/>]]

Another significant act of resistance in Mossoró occurred in 1927, when the city stood against the gang of the Northeast's most infamous ''[[cangaceiro]]'', [[Virgulino Ferreira da Silva]], popularly known as "Lampião." That year, Mossoró was experiencing notable economic growth in commerce and industry. The gang entered [[Rio Grande do Norte]] through the municipality of [[Luís Gomes]] between June 9 and 10, passing through several cities in western Rio Grande do Norte before reaching Mossoró, where Lampião suffered his only defeat since becoming a ''cangaceiro''.<ref name="História de Mossoró"/><ref name="Invasão de Lampião a Mossoró"/>

On June 12, the ''cangaceiro'' and his companions arrived in the district of São Sebastião, now the municipality of [[Governador Dix-Sept Rosado]], then a district of Mossoró. From there, he sent a telegram warning the population of an impending attack, causing widespread panic. Mayor Rodolfo Fernandes organized an exodus, setting up trenches to repel the invaders.<ref name="História de Mossoró">{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro_historia.php|title=History|publisher=Mossoró City Hall - RN|access-date=October 14, 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62QXRTqeT?url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro_historia.php|archive-date=October 14, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Invasão de Lampião a Mossoró">{{cite web|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090212161052/https://www.nominuto.com/vida/cultura/ha-80-anos-lampiao-chegava-a-mossoro/33/|title=Há 80 anos, Lampião chegava a Mossoró|language=pt-br|author=SOUZA, Alex de|date=June 17, 2007|access-date=October 14, 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62Qg3GLtR?url=http://www.nominuto.com/vida/cultura/ha-80-anos-lampiao-chegava-a-mossoro/33/|archive-date=October 14, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

On June 13, Lampião and his gang reached Sítio Saco, where he sent a note demanding 400 [[Brazilian real (old)|réis]] in money to spare the city, a demand rejected by the mayor. The attack on Mossoró began at 4 p.m., with the gang leader dividing his ''cangaceiros'' into three groups, each targeting a different location: the first attacked the mayor's house; the second invaded and attacked the Mossoró railway station; and the third targeted what is now the São Sebastião Cemetery. An hour later, the gang retreated, leaving behind Colchete (killed during the confrontation) and Jararaca, who was wounded, captured, and killed days later.<ref>[http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/140602/geraldo.htm José Leite de Santana "O Jararaca"] O Mossoroense Column by Geraldo Maia</ref> Jararaca was buried in the same cemetery attacked by the gang and later became a figure of local reverence among the people of Mossoró.<ref name="História de Mossoró"/><ref name="Invasão de Lampião a Mossoró"/>

The following year, Mossoró made history again as the site of Brazil's first female vote, a globally significant event, as most countries still prohibited women from voting. At the time, the [[Brazilian Constitution of 1891|Brazilian Constitution]], enacted in 1891, granted voting rights only to the wealthy. Women, slaves, the illiterate, and the poor were excluded. In 1928, [[Celina Guimarães Viana]], a teacher and football referee, obtained the first female voter's card and cast the first female vote in Brazil. This inspired women in Rio Grande do Norte and nine other Brazilian states to organize street protests demanding voting rights, which had already been secured in Rio Grande do Norte. Women nationwide gained the right to vote six years later, in 1934, under President [[Getúlio Vargas]].<ref name="História de Mossoró"/>

=== From 1929 to the 1970s ===
On January 1, 1929, Rafael Fernandes Gurjão took office as Mossoró’s first constitutional mayor, elected on September 2 the previous year. He held office for only three months, until April 2, when his vice-mayor, Vicente Carlos Saboia Filho, took over.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/secretaria_extraordinaria_de_cultura/DOC/DOC000000000109214.PDF|title=Rafael Fernandes Gurjão|date=Mar 18, 2016|author=José Augusto Foundation|access-date=October 29, 2021}}</ref> He was deposed on October 8, 1930, when all mayors in the state were removed by decree.

On July 28, 1934, by the [[papal bull]] ''Pro Eclesiarum Omnium'' issued by [[Pope Pius XI]] the [[Diocese of Mossoró]] was created, separated from the [[Archdiocese of Natal|Diocese of Natal]] and subordinate to the [[Archdiocese of Paraíba]]. However, the news only reached Mossoró on September 14, when the parish vicar of Saint Lucy, Priest Luiz Ferreira da Cunha Mota, received a telegram from the bishop of Natal, Dom Marcolino Esmeraldo de Souza Dantas, who had been appointed apostolic administrator of the new diocese. The diocese was officially installed on November 18, 1934.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/padre-motta-mestre-de-mossora-ii/303553|title=Padre Motta, mestre de Mossoró (II)|author=Femenick, Tomislav R.|date=January 18, 2015|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref>

[[File:Estátua de Dix-Sept Rosado na Praça Vigário Antônio Joaquim, Mossoró (RN).jpg|thumb|left|Statue of Dix-Sept Rosado in Vigário Antônio Joaquim Square, in the center of Mossoró]] 
On January 23, 1943, Mossoró inaugurated Cine Pax, located across from Rodolfo Fernandes Square, operating on the upper floor of the home of its owner, businessman Jorge Pinto. The cinema opened with the screening of [[Belle Starr (1941 film)|Belle Starr]]. Another cinema, Cine Déa, opened in 1944.<ref name="defato.com">{{cite web|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211029201936/https://defato.com/cultura/86160/reprter-luiza-gurgel-faz-um-passeio-no-tempo-de-mossor-cidade-dos-cinemas|title=Repórter Luiza Gurgel faz um passeio no tempo de Mossoró, cidade dos cinemas|author=GURGEL, Luiza|date=January 5, 2020|access-date=October 29, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220702222930/https://reticenciasculturais.com.br/2022/07/de-1908-a-2008-um-passeio-pelo-passado-do-cinema-no-pais-de-mossoro/ |title=De 1908 a 2008: um passeio pelo passado do cinema no país de Mossoró |author=Gurgel, Luiza |date=July 2, 2022 |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> By the 1960s, at least seven cinemas had opened in the city, including Cine Cid, across from the Vicar Antonio Joaquim Square, inaugurated on July 22, 1964, with the film [[Rome Adventure]].<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211030182130/http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/dei/DOC/DOC000000000052485.PDF|title=Belas artes nas telas dos cinemas|date=2011|access-date=October 30, 2021}}</ref>

In 1948, Mossoró held direct elections for mayor again, with the candidate Jerônimo Dix-Sept Rosado Maia winning. He remained in office until 1950, when he was elected governor of Rio Grande do Norte, taking office in 1951. He governed the state until July 12, 1951, when, as a result of an air disaster, he died in [[Aracaju]], the capital of [[Sergipe]]. In his honor, on the 25th of that month, a municipal law changed the name of the district of Sebastianópolis, formerly the village of São Sebastião, to [[Governador Dix-Sept Rosado]], which became a municipality on April 4, 1963, through state law 2878.

[[File:Escola Superior de Agricultura de Mossoró, Mossoró (RN), em 1974. Atualmente integra a Universidade Federal Rural do Semi-Árido (UFERSA).jpg|thumb|Mossoró Higher School of Agriculture (ESAM) in the year 1974]]
On March 29, 1964, the diocesan bishop Dom Gentil Diniz Barreto dissolved the Parish of the Sacred Heart of Jesus and elevated the São Manoel Church to parish status. The Sacred Heart Church became a sanctuary of perpetual adoration and was incorporated into the Santa Luzia parish.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180117040819/http://www.diocesedemossoro.com/p/a-catedral.html |title=A Catedral |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> On April 18, 1967, Mayor Raimundo Soares de Souza created the Mossoró Higher School of Agriculture (ESAM), which was inaugurated eight months later, on December 22. Its first admissions exam was held the following year, and its first agronomy class graduated in 1971.<ref>{{cite web|access-date=May 18, 2021 |date=April 18, 2017 |lang=pt-br |title=Decreto de criação da ESAM completa 50 anos |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210518141140/https://assecom.ufersa.edu.br/2017/04/18/decreto-de-criacao-da-esam-completa-50-anos/}}<!-- auto-translated from Portuguese by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

In 1970, when the municipality had a population of 97,245 inhabitants,<ref name="geoftp.ibge.gov.br"/> Mossoró’s industrial district was established, and in 1975 the Abolição neighborhood emerged,<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 143.</ref> along with the city’s first master plan, which defined its first urban boundary.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 101.</ref> Three years later, the first oil well was discovered in Mossoró, at the site of the current Thermas Hotel.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 180.</ref> In 1980, the city expanded further with the creation of new neighborhoods,<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 172.</ref> and the population reached 145,981.<ref name="geoftp.ibge.gov.br"/>

=== 1980s to the present ===
On December 15, 1981, the district of Baraúna was separated and became a new municipality.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200412193518/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/barauna/historico|title=Brasil / Rio Grande do Norte / Baraúna / Histórico|author=IBGE|access-date=October 30, 2021}}</ref> During that decade, Mossoró became a hub of irrigated fruit production, particularly melons.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 184.</ref><ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 225.</ref> Urban expansion and population growth continued, though at a slower pace than in previous decades. Meanwhile, railway transport declined, leading to the deactivation of the Mossoró–Sousa Railway and, in 1995, the dissolution of the Mossoró Railway Company.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 179.</ref>

In 1984, the Diocese of Mossoró celebrated its fiftieth anniversary, and [[Pope John Paul II]] proclaimed [[Saint Lucy]] the patron saint of the diocese.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151024161301/http://www.diocesedemossoro.com/2009/11/diocese-de-mossoro-completa-75-anos.html|title=Diocese de Mossoró completa 75 anos|date=November 18, 2009|access-date=October 24, 2015}}</ref> In 1985, the city was hit by heavy rains, and the Mossoró River recorded the largest flood in its history, inundating downtown and riverside neighborhoods.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211030141543/https://mossoroonline.com.br/1985-a-maior-enchente-da-historia-de-mossoro/|title=1985, a maior enchente da história de Mossoró|author=FAUSTINO, Lindomarcos|access-date=October 30, 2021}}</ref> In 1986, more petroleum was discovered in the municipality, this time in the Canto do Amaro area along BR-110, making Mossoró the largest onshore petroleum producer in Brazil.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 181.</ref> In the 1988 elections, the city elected its first female mayor, Rosalba Ciarlini, who served until 1992.<ref name="agorarn.com.br">{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250612192419/https://agorarn.com.br/ultimas/mossoro-elegeu-quatro-prefeitas-nos-ultimos-24-anos/ |title=Mossoró elegeu quatro prefeitas nos últimos 24 anos, destaca jornal |date=September 26, 2024 |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> In 1990, five new neighborhoods were created,<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 189.</ref> and the 1991 census recorded a population of 192,267 — growth that continued slowing compared to 1960–1980 rates.<ref>[[#Bibliography|PINHEIRO]], 2007, p. 203.</ref>

Rosalba was succeeded by Dix-Huit Rosado, who had previously served two mayoral terms. On December 29, 1994, Mossoró inaugurated the Mossoró Decentralized Teaching Unit (ETFRN/UNED), which became the Federal Center for Technological Education (CEFET-RN) in 1999 and, in 2008, the Federal Institute of Rio Grande do Norte (IFRN).<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140810114136/https://portal.ifrn.edu.br/campus/reitoria/noticias/campus-mossoro-comemora-amanha-20-anos-de-atuacao/ |title=Campus Mossoró comemora amanhã 20 anos de atuação |date=May 13, 2014 |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170922014602/https://portal.ifrn.edu.br/campus/reitoria/noticias/campus-mossoro-comemora-22-anos-de-historia/ |title=Campus Mossoró comemora 22 anos de história |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref> On October 22, 1996, Mayor Dix-Huit Rosado died in office and was succeeded by Sandra Rosado. In that year’s election, Rosalba was once again elected mayor.<ref name="agorarn.com.br"/> On September 24, 1999, the old Mossoró railway station was reopened as the Estação das Artes Elizeu Ventania, named after a local poet and repentista.<ref>{{cite web|lang=pt-br|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250120065142/https://tcmnoticia.com.br/mossoro/estacao-das-artes-completa-25-anos-de-sua-transformacao-em-centro-cultural/ |title=Estação das Artes completa 25 anos de sua transformação em centro cultural |author=Rodrigues, Yuri |date=September 25, 2024 |access-date=June 12, 2025}}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Mossoró.svg|thumb|left|280px|Map of Mossoró and surrounding areas, highlighting neighboring municipalities and geographic features.]]

According to the regional division established by the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) in 2017,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Divisão Regional do Brasil |author=IBGE |date=2017 |accessdate=19 September 2017 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170919225405/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archivedate=19 September 2017}}</ref> the municipality of Mossoró is part of both the intermediate and immediate geographic regions of Mossoró.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{cite web |url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Base de dados por municípios das Regiões Geográficas Imediatas e Intermediárias do Brasil |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=25 September 2017}}</ref> Prior to this, under the previous division into [[Microregions of Brazil|microregions]] and [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregions]], Mossoró was part of the Mossoró microregion, which was included in the Oeste Potiguar mesoregion.<ref>{{cite journal |author=IBGE |year=1990 |title=Divisão regional do Brasil em mesorregiões e microrregiões geográficas |journal=Biblioteca IBGE |pages=44–45 |volume=1 |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv2269_1.pdf |accessdate=19 September 2017 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170919225416/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv2269_1.pdf |archivedate=19 September 2017}}</ref> The municipality is located 281 kilometers from [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]], the state capital,<ref name="DistNatal-Mossoró">{{cite web|url=http://www.distanciaentreascidades.com.br/distancia-de-natal-rio-grande-do-norte-brasil-ate-mossoro-rn-brasil|title=Distância entre Natal - Rio Grande do Norte, Brasil e Mossoró - Rio Grande do Norte, Brasil|accessdate=15 June 2014}}</ref> 237 kilometers from [[Fortaleza]], [[Ceará]] (the closest state capital),<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.distanciaentreascidades.com.br/distancia-de-mossoro-rio-grande-do-norte-brasil-ate-fortaleza-ce-brasil|title=Distância entre Mossoró - Rio Grande do Norte, Brasil e Fortaleza - Ceará, Brasil|accessdate=15 June 2014}}</ref> and 1,977 kilometers from [[Brasília]], the federal capital.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.distanciaentreascidades.com.br/distancia-de-mossoro-rio-grande-do-norte-brasil-ate-brasilia-df-brasil|title=Distância entre Mossoró - Rio Grande do Norte, Brasil e Brasília - Distrito Federal, Brasil|accessdate=15 June 2014}}</ref> Covering an area of 2,099.36&nbsp;km²,<ref name="IBGE-Mossoró-RN"/> (3.9753% of the state's surface), Mossoró is the largest municipality in Rio Grande do Norte by territorial extent.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia">{{cite web|url=http://prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro/geografia/|title=Geografia|publisher=Prefeitura de Mossoró|accessdate=16 January 2014|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20140331142026/http://prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro/geografia/|archivedate=31 March 2014}}</ref> It borders the municipalities of [[Aracati]] ([[Ceará]]), [[Tibau]], and [[Grossos]] to the north; [[Governador Dix-Sept Rosado]] and [[Upanema]] to the south; [[Areia Branca, Rio Grande do Norte|Areia Branca]], [[Serra do Mel]], and [[Assu, Rio Grande do Norte|Assu]] to the east; and [[Baraúna, Rio Grande do Norte|Baraúna]] to the west.<ref name="Mossoró"/>

The [[Relief (geography)|terrain]] of the municipality, with predominant [[altitude]]s below 100 meters, comprises the [[Chapada do Apodi]] (encompassing areas intersected by the Apodi-Mossoró and Piranhas-Açu rivers with a slightly elevated tendency), the Sertaneja-São Francisco Depression (areas between the Chapada do Apodi and the [[Borborema Plateau]]), the sublittoral depression (transitional areas between coastal tablelands and the Borborema Plateau), and fluvial plains (located along riverbanks).<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/><ref name="Mossoró"/> The highest point in the municipality is Serra Mossoró, located 16 kilometers from the city,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://dcv.ufersa.edu.br/historico/|title=Departamento de Ciências Vegetais - DCV|accessdate=24 October 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150316104754/http://dcv.ufersa.edu.br/historico/|archivedate=16 March 2015}}</ref> with a peak at an altitude of 268 meters.<ref>DIAS, Gutemberg Henrique et al. ''Extração de Atributos Morfométricos da Serra Mossoró (Mossoró-RN) a Partir do Modelo de Elevação Digital (MDE)''. ''Revista Brasileira de Geografia Física'', v. 12, n. 06, p. 2239-2248, 2019.</ref>

The entire territory of the municipality lies within the Apodi/Mossoró River Hydrographic Basin. The main rivers flowing through the municipality are the [[Apodi River|Apodi/Mossoró]] and Carmo rivers. The main streams include Bonsucesso, Cabelo Negro, São Raimundo, and Pai Antônio. The largest reservoirs, with capacities equal to or greater than 100,000 cubic meters of water (m³), are the Açude Favela (500,000 m³), Lagoa de Paus Dam (264,000 m³), Baixo Dam (250,000 m³), Mossoró Dam, and Santana dos Pintos Dam (both with a capacity of 100,000 m³).<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/><ref name="Mossoró"/>

The [[biodiversity]] of Mossoró is evident in both its flora and fauna, with the latter being particularly notable due to the discovery of the species ''Hypsolebias bonita'', an annual fish ([[killifish]]) locally known as the "cloud fish." This species was named in honor of [[Maria Bonita (bandit)|Maria Bonita]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://noticias.uol.com.br/colunas/carlos-madeiro/2024/01/01/pesquisadores-encontra-novas-especies-de-peixes-das-nuvens-no-nordeste.htm|title=Dois peixes que 'caem do céu' são descobertos por pesquisadores na caatinga|author=Carlos Madeiro|publisher=[[Universo Online|UOL]]|date=2024-01-01|accessdate=2024-01-01}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal|url=https://mapress.com/zt/article/view/zootaxa.5389.5.2|title=Molecular delimitation of the seasonal killifishes of the Hypsolebias antenori species group (Cyprinodontiformes, Rivulidae), with description of two new species from the Caatinga coastal basins, northeastern Brazil|author=Yuri Abrantes|journal=[[Zootaxa]]|date=2023-12-22|volume=5389 |issue=5 |pages=545–562 |accessdate=2024-01-01|doi=10.11646/ZOOTAXA.5389.5.2|pmid=38221004 | issn = 1175-5326 |url-access=subscription}}</ref>

[[File:Rio-Mossoró.jpg|thumb|[[Apodi River|Mossoró River]], with the [[vegetation]] of the [[Caatinga|hyperxerophilous caatinga]] along its banks.]]

The predominant [[Soil|soil types]] in the municipality are eutrophic [[Cambisol]], which has high fertility, clay texture, and good to moderate drainage; [[rendzina]], similar to Cambisol but with moderate to imperfect drainage; and [[Latosol]], specifically the red-yellow eutrophic type, with medium to high fertility, medium texture, and good to excellent drainage. Other soil types include eutrophic equivalent red-yellow [[Podzol|podzolic soils]], ''[[solonchak]]'', and [[Vertisol]].<ref name="Mossoró"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.uep.cnps.embrapa.br/solos/rn/mossoro.pdf|title=Mapa Exploratório-Reconhecimento de solos do município de Mossoró, RN|author=[[Brazilian Agricultural Research Corporation]] (EMBRAPA)|accessdate=15 June 2014|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/6QM9uqXMZ?url=http://www.uep.cnps.embrapa.br/solos/rn/mossoro.pdf|archivedate=2014-06-15}}</ref>

These soils are covered by [[Caatinga|hyperxerophilous caatinga]], a [[vegetation]] type consisting of low-growing species adapted to [[drought]], such as the ''[[Cnidoscolus quercifolius|faveleiro]]'', [[Mimosa tenuiflora|black jurema]], and ''[[Combretum leprosum|mofumbo]]'', as well as halophytic vegetation with species adapted to high [[salinity]] levels (including [[Amaranthaceae|amaranth]] and ''pirrixiu''), and carnaúba groves, where the [[Copernicia prunifera|''carnaubeira'']] and [[Palm tree|palm]] are predominant species.<ref name="Prefeitura_Geografia"/><ref name="Mossoró"/> Part of Mossoró’s territory lies within the [[Furna Feia National Park]], an environmental preservation area spanning 8,494 hectares (mostly in the municipality of Baraúna, with the remainder in Mossoró). The park was established by presidential decree on 5 June 2012, during [[World Environment Day]] celebrations, to preserve the local [[caatinga]] biome.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_Ato2011-2014/2012/Dsn/Dsn13320.htm|title=DECRETO DE 5 DE JUNHO DE 2012: Dispõe sobre a criação do Parque Nacional da Furna Feia, nos Municípios de Baraúna e Mossoró, Estado do Rio Grande do Norte|date=May 6, 2012|publisher=Portal da legislação|accessdate=January 17, 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428232946/http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_Ato2011-2014/2012/Dsn/Dsn13320.htm|archivedate=28 April 2014}}</ref>

Mossoró, with its [[semi-arid climate]] classified as [[Köppen climate classification|''Bsh'']], is one of the hottest municipalities in Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/040307/conteudo/regional.htm|title=Mossoró, Pau dos Ferros e Caicó são as cidades mais quentes do RN|author=COSTA, Márcio|date=4 March 2007|publisher=O Mossoroense|accessdate=February 13, 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418232239/http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/040307/conteudo/regional.htm|archivedate=18 April 2015}}</ref> Temperatures can reach 38&nbsp;°C on some occasions, with the [[apparent temperature]] occasionally exceeding 40&nbsp;°C. [[Precipitation]] is concentrated in the first half of the year and occurs primarily as [[rain]], with rare instances of [[hail]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/149091/chuva-forte-sobre-o-nordeste-2|title=Chuva forte sobre o Nordeste|author=PEGORIM, Josélia|date=April 18, 2013|publisher=Climatempo|accessdate=June 17, 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130524043530/http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/149091/chuva-forte-sobre-o-nordeste-2|archivedate=24 May 2013}}</ref> Rainfall may also be accompanied by [[lightning]] and [[thunderstorm]]s, sometimes with significant intensity.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/212681/chuva-forte-raios-e-trovoadas-em-mossoro-rn/|title=Chuva forte, raios e trovoadas em Mossoró (RN)|author=PINHEIRO, Marcelo|work=Climatempo |date=12 March 2015|publisher=[[Climatempo]]|accessdate=October 24, 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304214506/http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/212681/chuva-forte-raios-e-trovoadas-em-mossoro-rn/|archivedate=4 March 2016}}</ref> During the afternoon, particularly in the [[dry season]], [[Humidity|relative humidity]] often drops below 30%, well below the 60% considered ideal by the [[World Health Organization]] (WHO).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/12298/mossororn-forte-calor-e-baixa-umidade-do-ar/|title=Mossoró (RN) - Forte calor e baixa umidade do ar|author=WEYKAMP, Fabiana|work=Climatempo |date=11 October 2010|accessdate=November 7, 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303180441/http://www.climatempo.com.br/noticias/12298/mossororn-forte-calor-e-baixa-umidade-do-ar/|archivedate=3 March 2016}}</ref> According to data from the [[National Institute of Meteorology]] (INMET), from 1970 to 2008, and the Rio Grande do Norte Agricultural Research Company (EMPARN), from March 2021 onward, the lowest temperature recorded in Mossoró was 16&nbsp;°C on 7 June 1996, and the highest was 39.6&nbsp;°C on 14 October 1979, both recorded by INMET.<ref name="bdmep.inmet.gov.br"/> The highest single-day precipitation accumulation was 203.5&nbsp;mm on 22 April 2013. The record for monthly precipitation belongs to April 1985, with 677.2&nbsp;mm accumulated.<ref name="meteorologia.emparn.rn.gov.br"/>

With 264,577 inhabitants according to the [[2022 Brazilian Census|latest demographic census]], Mossoró is the second most populous municipality in the state of Rio Grande do Norte, with a [[population density]] of 126.03 inhabitants per km². According to the same census, 52.18% of the population were women and 47.82% were men,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9514#/n6/2408003/v/all/p/all/c2/all/c287/100362/c286/113635/d/v1000093%202/l/t+v,p+c2+c287,c286/resultado |title=Tabela 9514 - População residente, por sexo, idade e forma de declaração da idade |author=IBGE |accessdate=January 7, 2025}}</ref> resulting in a [[sex ratio]] of 91.63.<ref name="Tema: população">{{cite web |url=https://censo2022.ibge.gov.br/panorama/indicadores.html?localidade=2408003&tema=1 |title=Tema: população |accessdate=January 7, 2025}}</ref> Regarding [[Race and ethnicity in Brazil|color or race]], 58.39% of inhabitants identified as [[Pardo|mixed race]], 25.5% as [[White people|white]], 14.63% as [[Black people|black]], 1.25% as [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous]], and only 0.23% as [[Asian people|Asian]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/9605#/n6/2408003/v/all/p/last%201/c86/all/d/v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t/resultado |title=Tabela 9605 - População residente, por cor ou raça, nos Censos Demográficos|author=IBGE|accessdate=January 7, 2025}}</ref> Between the 2010 and 2022 censuses, Mossoró’s population grew at a geometric rate of 0.15% per year.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/4709#/n6/2408003/v/all/p/all/d/v10605%202/l/v,p,t/resultado |title=Tabela 4709 - População residente Variação absoluta de população residente e Taxa de crescimento geométrico |author=IBGE |accessdate=January 7, 2025}}</ref>

=== Socioeconomic Indicators ===
The [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) of the municipality is considered high by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP). According to data from the 2010 report, released in 2013, its value was 0.720, making it the third highest in Rio Grande do Norte, behind only [[Parnamirim]] (1st) and Natal (2nd), and the 1,301st in Brazil. When considering specific components, the longevity index is 0.811, the income index is 0.694, and the education index is 0.663.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010" />

From 2000 to 2010, the [[Gini coefficient|Gini index]] decreased from 0.57 to 0.52, and the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to R$140 dropped by 60.6%, from 35.4% to 14%. In 2010, 86.05% of Mossoró’s population lived above the [[poverty line]], 9.09% were between the poverty and indigence lines, and 4.86% were below the indigence line. In the same year, the Gini index was 0.52, and the share of the richest 20% of the population in the municipality’s total income was 57.6%, nearly fifteen times higher than that of the poorest 20%, which was 3.85%.<ref name="ODS-01">{{cite web|url=http://rd.portalods.com.br/relatorios/9/erradicacao-da-pobreza/BRA002024087/mossoro---rn |title=ODS 01 Erradicação da pobreza |accessdate=December 15, 2020}}</ref>

=== Religion ===
[[File:Igreja Mórmon, Mossoró - RN.JPG|thumb|left|Chapel of [[the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] in Mossoró]]

In the 2010 census, the most recent with published data on religion, [[Catholic Church|Roman Catholicism]] was the predominant religion in Mossoró, with 70.69% of residents identifying as Catholic. This was followed by [[Protestantism|Evangelicals]] (18.46%), [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (0.62%), [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (0.47%), and [[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints|Latter-day Saints]] (0.24%); other denominations accounted for 0.92%. Additionally, 8.6% of the population [[Irreligion|had no religion]], including [[Atheism|atheists]] (0.19%) and [[Agnosticism|agnostics]] (0.02%); 0.12% had no specific religion or multiple affiliations, and 0.02% did not know.<ref name="SIDRA-2094">{{cite web|url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/tabela/2094#/n6/2408003/v/all/p/last%201/c86/0/c133/all/d/v93%200,v1000093%202/l/v,p+c86,t+c133/resultado|title=Tabela 2094 - População residente por cor ou raça e religião|author=IBGE|accessdate=December 15, 2020}}</ref>

Within the [[Catholic Church]], Mossoró is the seat of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Mossoró|Diocese of Santa Luzia]], a suffragan of the [[Archdiocese of Natal]], [[Canonical erection of a house of religious in the Catholic Church|canonically erected]] by [[Pope Pius XI]] on 28 July 1934 and formally established on 18 November of the same year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/padre-motta-mestre-de-mossora-ii/303553|title=Padre Motta, mestre de Mossoró (II)|date=18 January 2015|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|accessdate=25 October 2015|archiveurl=https://archive.today/20150925150349/http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/padre-motta-mestre-de-mossora-ii/303553|archivedate=25 September 2015}}</ref> The diocese covers an area of 18,847&nbsp;km²<ref name="Statistics">{{cite web|url=http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/diocese/dmoss.html|title=Statistics|publisher=Catholic Hierarchy|language=en|accessdate=24 October 2015|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150925124645/http://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/diocese/dmoss.html|archivedate=2015-09-25}}</ref> and has as its [[episcopal see]] the Santa Luzia Cathedral, constructed in the 18th century between 1772 and 1773, rebuilt in the 19th century, inaugurated in 1830, and elevated to parish church status in 1842. It became the diocesan cathedral in 1934 upon the creation of the diocese.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/280304/nhistoria.htm|title=A Catedral de Santa Luzia|author=MAIA, Geraldo|publisher=UOL|accessdate=8 October 2011|archiveurl=https://www.webcitation.org/62HWpbPXE?url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/280304/nhistoria.htm|archivedate=2011-10-08}}</ref>

Mossoró is also home to a variety of Protestant or Reformed denominations, with the five largest being: [[Assembleias de Deus|Assemblies of God]] (7.82% of residents), [[Baptist Church]] (1.97%), [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]] (0.67%), [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]] (0.57%), and [[Presbyterian Church of Brazil]] (0.39%). Other Protestant denominations account for 0.61%.<ref name="SIDRA-2094"/>

== Politics ==
[[File:Vice-prefeitura de Mossoró (RN).jpg|thumb|Headquarters of the Vice-Mayor's Office in Mossoró]]

Municipal administration in Mossoró is conducted through the [[Executive (government)|executive]] and [[Legislature|legislative]] branches. The executive branch is led by the [[Mayors in Brazil|mayor]], supported by a cabinet of secretaries.<ref name="Lei Orgânica de Mossoró"/> The first constitutional mayor of Mossoró was Father Antônio Freire de Carvalho, who served in 1853.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://omossoroense.uol.com.br/index.php/universo/geraldo-maia/52639-primeiro-codigo-de-postura-da-vila-de-mossoro-07-07-2013|title=First Code of Conduct of the Village of Mossoró - 07-07-2013|date=7 July 2013|publisher=O Mossoroense|access-date=17 January 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222012955/http://omossoroense.uol.com.br/index.php/universo/geraldo-maia/52639-primeiro-codigo-de-postura-da-vila-de-mossoro-07-07-2013|archive-date=22 February 2014}}</ref> The current mayor is Allyson Leandro Bezerra Silva of the [[Solidarity (Brazil)|Solidarity]] (SD) party. Bezerra, who served as a [[state deputy]] from 2019 to 2020, was elected mayor in 2020 with 47.52% of the valid votes and was sworn in on 1 January 2021. The deputy mayor is João Fernandes de Melo Neto of the [[Social Democratic Party (Brazil, 2011)|Social Democratic Party]] (PSD).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://g1.globo.com/rn/rio-grande-do-norte/noticia/2020/11/15/alyson-bezerra-do-solidariedade-e-eleito-prefeito-de-mossoro.ghtml|title=Allyson Bezerra, from Solidarity, is elected mayor of Mossoró|date=15 November 2020|publisher=G1|access-date=2 January 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2 January 2021 |title=Allyson Bezerra upon taking office: 'The people have arrived at the City Hall' |url=https://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/allyson-bezerra-ao-assumir-mandato-o-povo-chega-a-prefeitura/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210124095345/https://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/allyson-bezerra-ao-assumir-mandato-o-povo-chega-a-prefeitura/ |archive-date=2021-01-24 |access-date=2 January 2021 |work=Prefeitura Municipal de Mossoró |publisher=Municipality of Mossoró}}</ref> The legislative branch is represented by the [[Câmara municipal|Municipal Chamber]],<ref name="Lei Orgânica de Mossoró"/> located at the Rodolfo Fernandes Palace, which comprises 23 councilors.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mossoro.rn.leg.br/institucional/noticias/mossoro-tera-23-vereadores-a-partir-de-2021|title=Mossoró will have 23 councilors starting in 2021|author=Regy Carte|date=14 September 2020|publisher=Mossoró Municipal Chamber|access-date=2 January 2021}}</ref> The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for the administration and the executive, particularly the municipal budget (known as the Budget Guidelines Law).<ref name="Lei Orgânica de Mossoró"/>

== Economy ==
The [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) of Mossoró is the second largest in Rio Grande do Norte and the largest in the state's western region. According to 2013 data from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), the municipality's GDP was R$6,538,346,000, with R$2,648,585,000 from the [[service sector]], R$1,988,062,000 from industry, R$1,028,608,000 from municipal administration (excluding tax revenue), R$731,678,000 from [[tax]]es, and R$141,413,000 from [[agriculture]]. The per capita GDP was R$23,325.08.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=152|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities - 2013|author=IBGE|access-date=2 December 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161202123030/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=152|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref>

[[File:Cavalo mecanico Mossoró.jpg|thumb|left|Oil extraction in the territory of Mossoró. The municipality is the largest onshore oil producer in Brazil.]]

[[File:Partage Shopping, Mossoró (RN).jpg|thumb|left|Partage Shopping Mossoró, formerly ''Mossoró West Shopping'', one of the main shopping malls in Rio Grande do Norte<ref name="landis"/>]]

In 2010, among the municipal population aged 18 or older, 64.1% were [[Workforce|economically active and employed]], 25.8% were economically inactive, and 10.1% were economically active but unemployed. In the same year, among the employed economically active population in this age group, 43.71% worked in the service sector, 19.72% in [[commerce]], 9.78% in [[construction]], 8.52% in [[manufacturing industries]], 5.67% in [[agriculture]], 4.5% in [[extractive industries]], and only 0.76% in public utilities.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/> According to the 2014 Business Registry Statistics, Mossoró had 5,891 local units (businesses), of which 5,577 were active.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=155|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Central Business Registry Statistics - 2014|author=IBGE|access-date=2 December 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161202123242/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=155|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref>

In the [[primary sector]], irrigated horticulture stands out. Mossoró, together with the neighboring municipalities of [[Assu, Rio Grande do Norte|Assu]] and [[Baraúna, Rio Grande do Norte|Baraúna]], forms the Mossoró/Baraúna/Assu Hub, the largest [[melon]] producer in [[Brazil]], followed by the Médio Jaguaribe region in the neighboring state of Ceará. In 2007, the hub produced approximately 254,000 tons of melons, with 204,000 tons exported to international markets.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nippo.com.br/campo/historia/historia478.php|title=Japanese Pioneering in Mossoró's Fruit Farming|author=Susy Murakami|date=2009|publisher=Nippo-Brasil Newspaper|access-date=10 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731185805/http://www.nippo.com.br/campo/historia/historia478.php|archive-date=31 July 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://docs.google.com/viewer?a=v&q=cache:wNlc0sAL6zcJ:www.cepea.esalq.usp.br/pdf/Melao_Sober.doc+Mossor%C3%B3+%C3%A9+o+maior+produtor+de+mel%C3%A3o+do+RN&hl=pt-BR&gl=br&pid=bl&srcid=ADGEESjMQhVBX_hD0UavWFs2lLLz3gDIwvcEb5AH2D3DmHkqZUiGF0guWUS8PwhPjOiwhGoXC-MR5EXGZiN_5AQY9XMFs_UGOJgebJMZUT8AH_Vwc8rdYxbjFDBsL5NedrdHAnScrYW0&sig=AHIEtbTDjDYTkZMDt35Ue1PEUNfDelOk1Q|title=Melon Agroindustrial System: Mossoró versus Juazeiro|access-date=10 October 2011|archive-url=https://archive.today/20161202123801/http://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:ju1Wt_jCMSAJ:cepea.esalq.usp.br/pdf/Melao_Sober.doc+&cd=1&hl=pt-BR&ct=clnk&gl=br|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref> Since 1990, the region centered around Mossoró has been recognized by the [[Ministry of Agriculture (Brazil)|Ministry of Agriculture]] as a "Fruit Fly" or "''[[Anastrepha grandis|Anastrepha Grandis]]'' pest-free area," facilitating the entry of Mossoró's products into consumer markets such as the United States, Japan, and the European Union.<ref name="Economia mossoroense">{{cite web|url=http://www.achetudoeregiao.com.br/rn/mossoro/localizacao.htm|title=Location and General Data of Mossoró RN|publisher=Ache Tudo e Região|access-date=10 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62KbTSInA?url=http://www.achetudoeregiao.com.br/rn/mossoro/localizacao.htm|archive-date=10 October 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

According to IBGE data from 2015, the municipality produced, in temporary crops, 187,600 tons of melon, 56,000 tons of [[watermelon]], 264 tons of [[maize]], 240 tons of [[cassava]], 190 tons of [[bean]]s, 1,170 tons of [[onion]]s, and 19 tons of [[sorghum]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=158|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Municipal Agricultural Production - Temporary Crops - 2015|author=IBGE|access-date=2 December 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161202124105/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=158|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref> In permanent crops, production included 54,000 [[coconut]]s, 3,900 tons of [[papaya]], 1,100 tons of [[banana]]s, 1,000 tons of [[mango]]es, 829 tons of [[cashew nut]]s, 54 tons of [[Orange (fruit)|oranges]], and 38 tons of [[Passion fruit (fruit)|passion fruit]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=157|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Municipal Agricultural Production - Permanent Crops - 2015|author=IBGE|access-date=2 December 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161202123943/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=157|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref> In [[livestock]], the municipality had a herd of 846,621 [[poultry]], 35,912 [[sheep]], 28,100 [[quail]], 24,108 [[goat]]s, 19,102 [[cattle]], 18,900 [[pig]]s, and 3,812 [[horse]]s, while also producing 23,800 dozen eggs, 7,750,000 liters of milk, 6,800,000 dozen quail eggs, and 5,680 kilograms of honey.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=159|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Livestock - 2015|author=IBGE|access-date=2 December 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161202124204/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=159|archive-date=2 December 2016}}</ref>

In industry, Mossoró is the largest national producer of both salt and onshore oil, with a daily production of 47,000 barrels and over 3,500 wells. The production of [[cement]] and ceramics is also significant, with several branches of large companies operating in the area. Mossoró is one of the main industrial hubs in Rio Grande do Norte, alongside Natal, hosting a significant concentration of textile, clothing, and tourism-related industries.<ref name="Economia mossoroense"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.portalbrasil.net/estados_rn.htm|title=Rio Grande do Norte|publisher=Portal Brasil|access-date=10 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110724023446/http://www.portalbrasil.net/estados_rn.htm|archive-date=24 July 2011}}</ref> In recent years, construction has also gained prominence in Mossoró's economy.<ref name="Economia mossoroense"/>

=== Healthcare ===
[[File:Hospital Wilson Rosado, Mossoró (RN).jpg|300px|left|thumb|Wilson Rosado Hospital]]

In 2009, Mossoró had 115 health facilities, 68 private and 47 public (43 state-run and four municipal), including hospitals, [[emergency department]]s, [[Community health center|health centers]], and [[dentistry]] services, with a total of 664 inpatient beds.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?lang=&codmun=240800&idtema=5|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Health Services - 2009|author=IBGE|access-date=10 October 2011}}</ref> In April 2010, the municipality's professional healthcare workforce consisted of 977 [[physician]]s, 338 [[nursing assistant]]s, 209 [[nurse]]s, 196 [[dentist]]s, 146 nursing technicians, 101 [[social worker]]s, 95 [[pharmacist]]s, 48 [[physiotherapist]]s, 30 [[psychologist]]s, 27 [[nutritionist]]s, and 22 [[speech therapist]]s, totaling 2,189 professionals.<ref>{{Cite FTP |url=ftp://ftp.datasus.gov.br/caderno/geral/rn/RN_Mossoro_Geral.xls|server=DATASUS|title=Municipality: Mossoró - RN|access-date=10 October 2011}}</ref>

In 2010, Mossoró had an expected number of years of schooling of 9.97 years, surpassing the state average of 9.54 years. The percentage of children aged five to six in school was 96.6%, and those aged 11 to 13 attending primary education was 87.18%. Among youths, 57.96% of those aged 15 to 17 had completed primary education, and 45.68% of those aged 18 to 20 had completed secondary education. Among the population aged 25 or older, 51.55% had completed primary education, 37.84% had completed secondary education, 17.33% were [[illiterate]], and 9.89% had completed [[higher education]].<ref name="Atlas_PNUD_Mossoró"/>

In 2015, the lag in primary education (students older than the recommended age) was 11.4% for early years and 30.6% for final years, with a lag of 35.5% in secondary education.<ref name="ODS-04"/> In the same year, the municipality had a network of 164 [[primary school]]s (with 1,832 teachers), 123 preschools (385 teachers), and 37 secondary schools (594 teachers).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?codmun=240940&idtema=156|title=Rio Grande do Norte » Mossoró » Education - Enrollments, Teachers, and School Network - 2015|author=IBGE|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123140917/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/temas.php?codmun=240940&idtema=156|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref>

=== Crime and public safety ===
According to the 2014 Violence Map, based on 2012 data from the Sangari Institute, among municipalities with over 20,000 inhabitants, Mossoró's [[homicide]] rate was 60.0 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking fifth in the state and 162nd nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Homicidio_total.xlsx|title=Table HT. Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Homicides in Municipalities with Over 20,000 Inhabitants. Total Population. Brazil. 2008/2012.|publisher=Instituto Sangari|access-date=23 January 2017|format=xls|archive-date=8 July 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140708065758/http://www.mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Homicidio_total.xlsx|url-status=dead}}</ref> The [[suicide]] rate that year was 9.4 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking eighth in the state and 289th nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Suicidio_total.xlsx|title=Table ST. Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Suicides in Municipalities with Over 20,000 Inhabitants. Total Population. Brazil. 2008/2012.|publisher=Instituto Sangari|access-date=23 January 2017|format=xls|archive-date=3 March 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303225729/http://www.mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Suicidio_total.xlsx|url-status=dead}}</ref> The rate of deaths from traffic accidents was 43.5 per 100,000 inhabitants, ranking first in the state and 203rd nationally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Transp_total.xlsx|title=Table TT. Number and Rates (per 100,000) of Deaths in Traffic Accidents in Municipalities with Over 20,000 Inhabitants. Total Population. Brazil. 2008/2012.|publisher=Instituto Sangari|date=2015|access-date=23 January 2017|format=xls|archive-date=22 October 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171022122151/http://mapadaviolencia.org.br/tabelas2014/2014_Transp_total.xlsx|url-status=dead}}</ref>

More recent data from the Rio Grande do Norte Secretariat of Public Security and Social Defense (SESED/RN) indicate that 2016 was the most violent year in Mossoró's history, with 217 homicides,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2017/01/2016-foi-considerado-o-ano-mais.html|title=2016 Was Considered the Most Violent Year in Mossoró's History|author=Alyson Oliveira|work=Mossoró Notícias |date=3 January 2017|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160411002138/http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2017/01/2016-foi-considerado-o-ano-mais.html|archive-date=11 April 2016}}</ref> with the Santo Antônio neighborhood being the most violent in the city.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2015/12/bairro-mais-violento-de-mossoro-tem.html|title=Most Violent Neighborhood in Mossoró Sees 36.7% Drop in Homicides Compared to 2014|work=Mossoró Notícias |date=31 December 2015|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123155924/http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2015/12/bairro-mais-violento-de-mossoro-tem.html|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref> According to the 2016 Violence Atlas by the [[Institute of Applied Economic Research]] (IPEA), Mossoró had a homicide rate of 71.5 per 100,000 inhabitants, making it the ninth most violent municipality in Brazil and the second in the state, after [[Macaíba]] in the [[Greater Natal|Natal Metropolitan Region]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.omossoroense.com.br/mossoro-e-a-9a-cidade-mais-violenta-do-brasil/|title=Mossoró is the 9th Most Violent City in Brazil|author=Ana Paula Cardoso|date=22 March 2016|publisher=O Mossoroense|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123151810/http://www.omossoroense.com.br/mossoro-e-a-9a-cidade-mais-violenta-do-brasil/|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref>

To address these crime rates, the 2nd and 12th military police battalions of Rio Grande do Norte, based in Mossoró, work alongside public authorities to implement [[public safety]] measures.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://agorarn.com.br/regionais/forcas-de-seguranca-se-reunem-para-reduzir-criminalidade-em-mossoro/|title=Security Forces Meet to Reduce Crime in Mossoró|date=3 February 2016|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123160722/http://agorarn.com.br/regionais/forcas-de-seguranca-se-reunem-para-reduzir-criminalidade-em-mossoro/|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref> The city is home to a federal penitentiary, inaugurated in 2009,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dnonline.com.br/app/noticia/cotidiano/2009/07/03/interna_cotidiano,12533/index.shtml|title=Mossoró Federal Penitentiary Inaugurated and Expected to Receive 50 Prisoners by July|date=3 July 2009|publisher=Diário de Natal|access-date=11 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120121122045/http://www.dnonline.com.br/app/noticia/cotidiano/2009/07/03/interna_cotidiano,12533/index.shtml|archive-date=21 January 2012}}</ref> and has a municipal guard, established by Complementary Law 37 on 14 December 2009, to contribute to public safety.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/jom/jom47b.pdf|title=OFFICIAL JOURNAL OF MOSSORÓ|date=22 December 2009|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140719080309/http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/jom/jom47b.pdf|archive-date=19 July 2014}}</ref>

=== Transportation ===
[[File:BR-304 e RN-013, Mossoró - RN.JPG|thumb|left|300px|Intersection of [[BR-304 (Brazil highway)|BR-304]] (curve) with RN-013 (in the background), connecting Mossoró to Natal/[[Fortaleza]] and [[Tibau]], respectively]]

In 2018, the municipal vehicle fleet totaled 162,639 vehicles, including 59,267 [[car]]s, 51,782 [[motorcycle]]s, 16,810 [[moped]]s, 10,661 [[pickup truck]]s, 9,160 [[light truck]]s, 4,196 [[truck]]s, 3,075 utility vehicles, 2,549 [[SUV]]s, 1,883 utility vehicles, 1,543 semi-trailers, 907 tractor-trailers, 387 [[bus]]es, 293 [[minibus]]es, 96 [[tricycle]]s, 20 [[tractor]]s, three [[sidecar]]s, one platform chassis, and ten in other categories.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/rn/mossoro/pesquisa/22/28120|title=Vehicle Fleet|author=IBGE|access-date=15 December 2020}}</ref> Since 2009, Mossoró's [[traffic]] has been municipalized<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mossoro.rn.leg.br/institucional/noticias/mossoro-modernizara-rede-de-semaforos|title=Mossoró to Modernize Traffic Light Network|date=16 July 2020|access-date=15 December 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201215193149/https://www.mossoro.rn.leg.br/institucional/noticias/mossoro-modernizara-rede-de-semaforos|archive-date=15 December 2020}}</ref> and is managed by the Municipal Secretariat of Public Safety, Civil Defense, Urban Mobility, and Traffic.

The municipality is crossed by the following federal highways: [[BR-405 (Brazil highway)|BR-405]], starting in Mossoró and extending through the western Rio Grande do Norte region to [[Marizópolis]], in the Paraíba backlands; [[BR-304 (Brazil highway)|BR-304]], connecting [[Natal, Rio Grande do Norte|Natal]] and [[Fortaleza]]; and [[BR-110 (Brazil highway)|BR-110]], starting in Areia Branca, passing through the municipal seat, and extending to [[Catu]], in [[Bahia]]. State highways crossing Mossoró include RN-013, recently expanded, linking Mossoró to [[Tibau]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=49878&ACT=null&PAGE=null&PARM=null&LBL=NOT%C3%8DCIA|title=Governor Inaugurates Sunrise Route: Duplicated RN Between Mossoró and Tibau|date=27 December 2014|publisher=Government of Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=19 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170119211820/http://rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=49878&ACT=null&PAGE=null&PARM=null&LBL=NOT%C3%8DCIA|archive-date=19 January 2017}}</ref> RN-015, connecting Mossoró to [[Baraúna, Rio Grande do Norte|Baraúna]];<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.defato.com/noticias/31277/urgente-moradores-de-juremal-fecham-rn-que-liga-mossoro-a-barauna|title=Juremal Residents Close RN Linking Mossoró to Baraúna for Safety|date=3 February 2014|publisher=De Fato Newspaper|access-date=19 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123142039/http://www.defato.com/noticias/31277/urgente-moradores-de-juremal-fecham-rn-que-liga-mossoro-a-barauna|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref> and RN-117, connecting Mossoró to various municipalities in the western Rio Grande do Norte region.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://noticias.r7.com/transito/noticias/rodovias-do-rio-grande-do-norte-20090926.html|title=Rio Grande do Norte Highways|date=26 September 2009|publisher=Notícias R7|access-date=20 August 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130428031157/http://noticias.r7.com/transito/noticias/rodovias-do-rio-grande-do-norte-20090926.html|archive-date=28 April 2013}}</ref>

[[File:Primeira locomotiva da Estrada de Ferro Mossoró-Porto Franco.jpg|thumb|First locomotive of the Mossoró-Porto Franco Railway, in 1915]]

In [[air transport]], Mossoró is served by the [[Dix-Sept Rosado Airport]], located in the Aeroporto neighborhood and managed by the Department of Highways of Rio Grande do Norte (DER-RN).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cofemac.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=2622&Itemid=|title=Government Wants Airport for Pau dos Ferros|author=Fábio Barbosa|date=29 September 2011|publisher=Diário de Natal|access-date=13 October 2011|archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62PBzGsjB?url=http://www.cofemac.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=2622&Itemid=|archive-date=13 October 2011}}</ref> Another form of urban transportation is the bicycle, although there are few kilometers of [[bike lane]]s. Projects are underway to build more bike lanes and dedicated bicycle parking to improve [[urban mobility]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.defato.com/noticias/46361/por-mobilidade-mossora-adere-a-construa-a-o-de-ciclovias|title=For Mobility, Mossoró Adopts Bicycle Lane Construction|author=Marcos Garcia|date=21 March 2015|publisher=De Fato Newspaper|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123143526/http://www.defato.com/noticias/46361/por-mobilidade-mossora-adere-a-construa-a-o-de-ciclovias|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.omossoroense.com.br/ruas-de-mossoro-ganharao-quatro-ciclovias-e-estacionamento-para-bicicletas/|title=Mossoró to Gain Four Bike Lanes and Bicycle Parking|author=Márcio Gomes|date=8 March 2016|publisher=O Mossoroense|access-date=23 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123144042/http://www.omossoroense.com.br/ruas-de-mossoro-ganharao-quatro-ciclovias-e-estacionamento-para-bicicletas/|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref>

Mossoró was once crossed by the Mossoró-Sousa Railway, designed in the 19th century and inaugurated on 15 March 1915, initially connecting the municipality to Porto Franco (now [[Areia Branca, Rio Grande do Norte|Areia Branca]]). Over time, the railway expanded, reaching [[Sousa, Paraíba|Sousa]] in the Paraíba backlands by 1950. Since the 1970s, the railway has been deactivated, though passenger trains continued operating on the Mossoró branch until 1991. Today, parts of the tracks no longer exist, and the old railway station has been transformed into the current Elizeu Ventania Arts Station.<ref name="estacoesferroviarias.com.br">{{cite web|url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rgn/mossoro.htm|title=Mossoró -- Railway Stations of Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=28 November 2020}}</ref>

== Culture ==
Despite being a cultural hub, Mossoró has yet to establish a defined historic center. The NGO Salv'Art - Institute of Service and Support for Art, Culture, Citizenship, and Environment - has been seeking support from public authorities to designate the former Redenção Square, now Dorian Jorge Freire Square, as the historic center, as it retains much of the city's original architecture intact.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/131105/conteudo/especial.htm|title=MOSSORÓ YESTERDAY, TODAY... AND TOMORROW?|date=13 November 2005|access-date=6 August 2011|publisher=O Mossoroense|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428232730/http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/131105/conteudo/especial.htm|archive-date=28 April 2014}}</ref>

Municipal holidays include 30 September, commemorating the abolition of [[slavery]] in the municipality, and 13 December, the feast day of the patron saint [[Saint Lucy]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mp.rn.gov.br/intranet/cpj/files/resolucao/ANEXO%20III%20-%20FERIADOS%20MUNICIPAIS%202010..pdf|title=MUNICIPAL HOLIDAYS|publisher=Public Prosecutor's Office of Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=1 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170123031718/http://www.mp.rn.gov.br/intranet/cpj/files/resolucao/ANEXO%20III%20-%20FERIADOS%20MUNICIPAIS%202010..pdf|archive-date=23 January 2017}}</ref> The day Mossoró was elevated to city status, 9 November, is considered an optional holiday.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-58869|title=Legislative Holds Solemn Session for Mossoró's 140 Years of Political Emancipation|author=Fabiano Souza|date=8 November 2010|publisher=Correio da Tarde|access-date=6 August 2011|archive-date=20 January 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120120034522/http://www.correiodatarde.com.br/editorias/correio_politico-58869|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Cultural spaces and tourist attractions ===
Mossoró features several cultural spaces, including museums and theaters. The city has two theaters, the oldest being the Lauro Monte Filho Theater, located in the city center with a capacity of 483 people. The theater operates in the same building that once housed the ''Cine Cid'' cinema, inaugurated on 22 July 1964. It later served as a temple for the Universal Church of the Kingdom of God and was converted into a theater in 1999 after being purchased by the state government. It was closed in 2008 due to structural issues and reopened in 2018.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://medium.com/hiperlab/um-passeio-no-tempo-da-mossor%C3%B3-cidade-dos-cinemas-c51e41db4cda|title=A Journey Through Time in Mossoró, the City of Cinemas|author=Luiza Gurgel|date=24 December 2019|access-date=15 December 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2011 |title=Fine Arts on the Cinema Screens |url=http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/dei/DOC/DOC000000000052485.PDF |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211030182130/http://adcon.rn.gov.br/ACERVO/dei/DOC/DOC000000000052485.PDF |archive-date=2021-10-30 |access-date=15 December 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tribunadonorte.com.br/noticia/teatro-lauro-monte-reabre-dia-18/432841|title=Lauro Monte Theater Reopens on the 18th|date=12 December 2018|publisher=Tribuna do Norte|access-date=15 December 2020}}</ref>

[[File:Estação das Artes Elizeu Ventania, Mossoró (RN).JPG|400px|thumb|Elizeu Ventania Arts Station, formerly the railway station, now home to the Petroleum Museum and host of Mossoró's main cultural events<ref name="estacoesferroviarias.com.br"/>]]

The Dix-Huit Rosado Municipal Theater, the city's main theater, was inaugurated in 2003, built by the municipality in partnership with [[Petrobras]], with a capacity of 740 seats. It hosts various cultural performances, including folk dances, theatrical plays, assemblies, and other events.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nataltrip.com/passeios/teatro-municipal-dixhuit-rosado-maia/39|title=Dix-Huit Rosado Municipal Theater|publisher=Natal Trip|access-date=6 August 2011|archive-date=28 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428231715/http://www.nataltrip.com/passeios/teatro-municipal-dixhuit-rosado-maia/39|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/2008/teatro.php|title=Dix-Huit Rosado Municipal Theater|publisher=Municipality of Mossoró|access-date=6 August 2011}}</ref> Among the museums, the Mossoró Resistance Memorial features exhibitions highlighting the ''[[cangaço]]'' and the city's resistance to Lampião's gang during its 1927 invasion.<ref name="vivaviver">{{cite web|url=http://www.vivaviver.com.br/viagens/memorial_da_resistencia_mossoro_reconta_historia_da_expulsao_de_lampiao/1084/|title=Mossoró Resistance Memorial: Mossoró Retells the Story of Lampião's Expulsion|author=Samysia Almeida|date=28 March 2011|publisher=Viva Viver.com|access-date=6 August 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/?pagina=noticias&idcat=2&idnot=7195|title=Inauguration of the Resistance Memorial is on the 4th|publisher=Mossoró Notícias|access-date=6 August 2011}}</ref>

The Elizeu Ventania Arts Station, formerly the railway station, houses the Petroleum Museum, which displays a diverse collection of materials on the history of petroleum in Mossoró and Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/2008/estacao_museu_petroleo.htm|title=Petroleum Museum|publisher=Municipality of Mossoró|access-date=6 August 2011}}</ref> The Journalist Lauro Escóssia Municipal Museum, once a public jail, was established in 1948 and is now a key part of the city's cultural center, showcasing exhibitions on Mossoró's history and historical documents.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nataltrip.com/passeios/museu-municipal-jornalista-lauro-da-escossia/35|title=Journalist Lauro da Escóssia Municipal Museum|publisher=Natal Trip|access-date=6 August 2011|archive-date=28 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140428231932/http://www.nataltrip.com/passeios/museu-municipal-jornalista-lauro-da-escossia/35|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Vingt-Un Rosado Paleontology Museum houses fossil specimens from the former ESAM (Superior School of Agriculture of Mossoró), now UFERSA.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://acd.ufrj.br/geologia/sbp/esam.htm|title=Paleontology Museum of the Superior School of Agriculture of Mossoró - ESAM|publisher=Acd.ufrj.br|access-date=6 August 2011}}</ref>

As the main city of the Costa Branca Hub, Mossoró is one of the top tourist destinations in Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=12096&ACT=&PAGE=0&PARM=&LBL=Conhe%E7a+o+RN|title=Tourism|publisher=Government of Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=16 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170107181023/http://www.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=12096&ACT=&PAGE=0&PARM=&LBL=Conhe%E7a+o+RN|archive-date=7 January 2017}}</ref> Notable tourist attractions include the São Vicente Chapel, the Santa Luzia Cathedral, the São Sebastião Cemetery, the Public Market, the Goat Market, as well as the museums, the Dix-Huit Rosado Theater, and the Arts Station.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.pontosturisticosbrasil.org/fotos-e-pontos-turisticos-de-mossoro.html|title=PHOTOS AND TOURIST ATTRACTIONS OF MOSSORÓ|date=11 March 2015|access-date=16 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170116211153/http://www.pontosturisticosbrasil.org/fotos-e-pontos-turisticos-de-mossoro.html|archive-date=16 January 2017}}</ref>

=== Events ===
[[File:Chuva de Bala no país de Mossoró.jpg|thumb|left|"Bullet Rain in the Land of Mossoró" performance, held during Mossoró Cidade Junina]]

The Mossoró Cidade Junina, one of the largest [[Festa Junina|June festivals]] in Northeast Brazil, attracting over one million tourists,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.saude.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=118390&ACT=null&PAGE=null&PARM=null&LBL=Materia|title=Government and Municipality Define Security Plan for Mossoró Cidade Junina|date=1 June 2016|publisher=Government of Rio Grande do Norte|access-date=13 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170113182938/http://www.saude.rn.gov.br/Conteudo.asp?TRAN=ITEM&TARG=118390&ACT=null&PAGE=null&PARM=null&LBL=Materia|archive-date=13 January 2017}}</ref> has been held since 1996 at the Arts Station throughout June. It features ''[[Quadrille|quadrilha]]'' performances, musical shows, food stalls with typical dishes, and cultural projects.<ref name="MCJ">{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossorocidadejunina/evento.html|title=Over 14 Years, the Event Has Established Itself as One of the Largest June Festivals in the Country|publisher=Municipality of Mossoró|access-date=13 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141213020250/http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossorocidadejunina/evento.html|archive-date=13 December 2014}}</ref> During the event, the theatrical performance ''Bullet Rain in the Land of Mossoró'' recounts the story of [[Lampião]] and his gang's 1927 invasion, performed since 2003 in front of the São Vicente Chapel, the site of the clashes between the gang and the local population.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossorocidadejunina/chuvadebala.html|title=Bullet Rain|publisher=Municipality of Mossoró|access-date=13 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141213103806/http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossorocidadejunina/chuvadebala.html|archive-date=13 December 2014}}</ref>

The Feast of Saint Lucy is one of the main religious events in Rio Grande do Norte, held in December in front of the cathedral. It begins on 3 December with an opening mass, followed by nine nights of [[novena]]s. During the festival, the Saint Lucy Oratory, a theatrical performance depicting the life of [[Saint Lucy]], is staged after the novenas. Other events include the Saint Lucy Cavalcade, the Light Pedalling, the Light Motorcycle Pilgrimage, and musical performances.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2016/12/comeca-hoje-oficialmente-festa-de-santa.html|title=The 2016 Saint Lucy Festival Officially Begins Today|author=Caio Vale|date=3 December 2016|publisher=Mossoró Notícias|access-date=13 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170113181509/http://www.mossoronoticias.com.br/2016/12/comeca-hoje-oficialmente-festa-de-santa.html|archive-date=13 January 2017}}</ref> The festivities conclude on 13 December with masses and a traditional procession, attracting up to 100,000 devotees from Mossoró and beyond. Through state law 10,114, enacted on 7 October 2016, the festival was recognized as a cultural, historical, and intangible heritage of Rio Grande do Norte.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.al.rn.gov.br/portal/_ups/legislacao/2016/11/07/bbc9222640afadc4ad4533168d9da157.pdf|title=LAW No. 10,114, OF 7 OCTOBER 2016|publisher=Rio Grande do Norte Legislative Assembly|access-date=13 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170113162624/http://www.al.rn.gov.br/portal/_ups/legislacao/2016/11/07/bbc9222640afadc4ad4533168d9da157.pdf|archive-date=13 January 2017}}</ref>

Other significant events in Mossoró's cultural calendar include:<ref name="Prefeitura-turismo">{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro/turismo/|title=Turismo|publisher=Prefeitura de Mossoró|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref> the Goat Festival, held at the Armando Buá Exhibition Park by the Rio Grande do Norte state government in partnership with the municipal government and local associations. Its program features exhibitions of cattle, goats, sheep, and pigs, as well as artisanal products, a gastronomic festival, artistic performances, and other attractions;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.defato.com/noticias/38461/aerobode-mossora-esta-inserido-na-programaa-a-o-da-festa-do-bode|title=Aerobode Mossoró está inserido na programação da Festa do Bode|date=7 August 2014|publisher=Jornal De Fato|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nominuto.com/noticias/cidades/confira-a-programacao-da-festa-do-bode-para-o-fim-de-semana/128856/|title=Confira a programação da Festa do Bode para o fim de semana|date=8 August 2015|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref> the Western Region Industrial and Commercial Fair (FICRO), which has been held since 1987 and is organized by the Mossoró Commercial and Industrial Association (ACIM);<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.calendariodoagronegocio.com.br/Evento/visualizar/portugues/1302|title=FICRO - Feira Industrial e Comercial da Região Oeste|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref> the Mossoró Book Fair, a reading promotion event held since 2005, featuring book exhibitions and a diverse program;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.feiradolivrodemossoro.com.br/afeira-apresentacao|title=Apresentação|date=2016|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref> the International Fair of Irrigated Tropical Horticulture (Expofruit), the main event for irrigated horticulture in Brazil, with participation from numerous national and international companies;<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.expofruit.com.br/aexpofruit|title=A Expofruit|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref> and the Freedom Act, the main theatrical performance held during the Freedom Festival at the end of September, which commemorates three of Mossoró's four libertarian acts: the Women's Revolt (1875), the abolition of slavery (1883), and the first female vote (1927), alongside parades and musical performances.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.defato.com/noticias/39613/programaa-a-o-da-festa-da-liberdade-a-apresentada|title=Programação da Festa da Liberdade é apresentada|date=5 September 2014|publisher=Jornal De Fato|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro/festa-da-liberdade/|title=Festa da Liberdade|publisher=Prefeitura de Mossoró|accessdate=13 January 2017}}</ref>

=== Sports ===
[[File:Nogueirão.jpg|thumb|A match between [[Associação Cultural Esporte Clube Baraúnas|Baraúnas de Mossoró]] and [[Campinense Clube|Campinense]] (from [[Campina Grande]], [[Paraíba]]) at the [[Nogueirão]] during the [[2012 Campeonato Brasileiro Série D]]. Baraúnas won 2–0.]]

The first football club in Mossoró was Humaitá Futebol Clube, founded on 14 October 1919.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www2.uol.com.br/omossoroense/150808/conteudo/artigos.htm|title=Mossoró e o futebol|author=MAIA, Geraldo|date=15 August 2008|publisher=O Mossoroense|accessdate=6 August 2011}}</ref> The city is home to the [[Nogueirão|Estádio Leonardo Nogueira]], popularly known as ''Nogueirão'', established on 25 January 1922,<ref name="Estádio">{{cite web|url=http://www.futeboldorn.com.br/historia2/index_10.htm|title=História do Futebol do Rio Grande do Norte|accessdate=18 May 2011|archive-date=24 August 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110824230216/http://www.futeboldorn.com.br/historia2/index_10.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> which serves as the home stadium for two traditional local football clubs: [[Associação Cultural e Desportiva Potiguar]] (Potiguar de Mossoró) and [[Associação Cultural Esporte Clube Baraúnas]] (Baraúnas). The former has won two titles (2004 and [[2013 Campeonato Potiguar|2013]]) and secured three runner-up positions (1997, 2006, and 2008) in the [[Campeonato Potiguar]], while the latter has one title (2006) and two runner-up positions (1981 and 1987).

Several sports events are held annually in Mossoró, including the Governador Dix-Sept Rosado Cycling Race, a cycling race held since 1949 across various categories, making it one of Brazil's oldest [[cycling]] events. Originally conducted on a route between Governador Dix-Sept Rosado (then a district, now a municipality) and Mossoró, it is now held solely within the city's urban streets.<ref name="prefeitura-esporte"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://mossorohoje.com.br/noticias/11989/67-prova-ciclistica-governador-dixsept-rosado-e-realizada-em-mossoro-confira-os-resultados|title=67ª Prova Ciclística Governador Dix-sept Rosado é realizada em Mossoró; confira os resultados|date=26 September 2016|publisher=Mossoró Hoje|accessdate=23 January 2017}}</ref> Another notable event is the Municipal School Games (JEM's), which brings together various sports and involves students from multiple schools in the municipality, aiming to promote engagement in sports.<ref name="prefeitura-esporte">{{cite web|url=http://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/mossoro_esporte.php|title=Esporte|publisher=Prefeitura de Mossoró|accessdate=6 August 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|author1=Rits |url=http://www.nominuto.com/noticias/esporte/jogos-escolares-de-mossoro-iniciam-hoje/125952/|title=Jogos Escolares de Mossoró iniciam hoje|date=26 May 2015|accessdate=23 January 2016}}</ref>

The municipal government also closes the ''Cultural Corridor'' on Sundays to facilitate sports activities such as [[walking]], cycling, [[inline skating]], and [[skateboarding]],<ref>{{Cite web |date=9 January 2021 |title=Projeto Viva Rio Branco retorna neste domingo |url=https://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/projeto-viva-rio-branco-retorna-neste-domingo/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210201050720/https://www.prefeiturademossoro.com.br/projeto-viva-rio-branco-retorna-neste-domingo/ |archive-date=2021-02-01 |work=Prefeitura Municipal de Mossoró}}</ref> and has adapted several public squares for sports, including the Praça do Inocoop<ref>{{Cite web |title=Praia do Inocoop |url=https://pt.foursquare.com/v/pra%C3%A7a-do-inocoop/532e056f498e05c8e7f76e1e}}</ref> and the Praça do [[Rotary International|Rotary Club]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Praça do Rotary |url=https://pt.foursquare.com/v/pra%C3%A7a-do-rotary/4db5c80acda1c57c82762777 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Foursquare |language=pt}}</ref>

The city also features the Engenheiro Pedro Ciarlini Neto Sports Gymnasium ([[sports complex]]),<ref>{{cite web |date=2012-06-02 |title=Ginásio poliesportivo é inaugurado |url=https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/regiao/ginasio-poliesportivo-e-inaugurado-1.593911 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Diário do Nordeste |publisher=Verdes Mares |language=pt}}</ref> the Sports Square (multi-sport square),<ref>{{cite web |title=Petras pede recuperação de praças esportivas |url=https://mossoro.rn.leg.br/institucional/noticias/petras-pede-recuperacao-de-pracas-esportivas |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Câmara Municipal de Mossoró |language=pt}}</ref> and the Skateboard Square,<ref>{{cite web |date=2015-01-27 |title=Skate Park em Mossoró/RN |url=http://skatemasters-jpa.blogspot.com/2015/01/skate-park-em-mossororn.html |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Skate Masters JPA |language=pt}}</ref> as well as a [[BMX]] track at the Praça Maria de Lourdes Fernandes Moreira in Ulrick Graff,<ref>{{cite web |title=Reforma da Praça Ulrick Graff é entregue à população |url=https://prefeiturademossoro.com.br/noticias/reforma-da-praca-ulrick-graff-e-entregue-a-populacao/1966 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Prefeitura de Mossoró |language=pt}}</ref> a [[motocross]] track in Alto da Pelonha,<ref>{{cite web |title=PM localiza moto roubada e alguns objetos da vítima na pista de motocross, região do Alto da Pelonha |url=https://fimdalinha.com.br/pm-localiza-moto-roubada-e-alguns-objetos-da-vitima-na-pista-de-motocross-regiao-do-alto-da-pelonha/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190715145103/https://fimdalinha.com.br/pm-localiza-moto-roubada-e-alguns-objetos-da-vitima-na-pista-de-motocross-regiao-do-alto-da-pelonha/ |archive-date=2019-07-15 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=Fim da Linha |language=pt}}</ref> and a [[go-kart]] track in the Cidade Nova neighborhood.<ref>{{cite web |date=2017-12-01 |title=Praça de Esportes Radicais - Mossoró/RN |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z30_EoIyQP0 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=YouTube |publisher=TV Cidade Oeste |language=pt}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tela Esportiva: Mossoró passa a contar com pista de kart |url=https://tcm10hd.com.br/jornalismo/tela-esportiva-mossoro-passa-a-conta-com-pista-de-kart/ |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=TCM 10 HD |language=pt}}</ref> Additionally, various facilities for [[seven-a-side football]] and [[health club]]s are spread throughout the city.<ref>{{cite web |date=2020-09-20 |title=Inauguração da pista de kart em Mossoró/RN |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=liu2FDaxUA0o |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=YouTube |publisher=TV Cidade Oeste |language=pt}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2020-07-14 |title=Academias de Mossoró reabrem nesta quarta-feira (15) |url=https://defato.com/mossoro/90229/academias-de-mossoro-reabrem-nesta-quarta-feira-15 |access-date=2025-05-19 |website=DeFato.com |language=pt}}</ref>
