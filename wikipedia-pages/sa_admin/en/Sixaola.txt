'''Sixaola''' is a [[Districts of Costa Rica|district]] of the [[Talamanca (canton)|Talamanca]] canton, in the [[Limón Province|Limón]] province of [[Costa Rica]]. It is a border town together with [[Guabito]], [[Panama]]<ref name="divadm">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Declara oficial para efectos administrativos, la aprobación de la División Territorial Administrativa de la República N°41548-MGP |url=http://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?param1=NRTC&nValor1=1&nValor2=88416&nValor3=115607&param2=1&strTipM=TC&lResultado=1&strSim=simp |website=Sistema Costarricense de Información Jurídica |accessdate=26 September 2020 |date=19 March 2019}}</ref><ref name="dta2017">{{cite book |lang=es |title=División Territorial Administrativa de la República de Costa Rica|date=8 March 2017|publisher=Editorial Digital de la Imprenta Nacional|isbn=978-9977-58-477-5|url=https://www.imprentanacional.go.cr/editorialdigital/libros/historiaygeografia/division_17.pdf}}</ref>  Sixaola is right across the [[Sixaola River]] from [[Guabito]], [[Panama]].<ref name=fodor>{{citation|title=Costa Rica 2008|year=2008|location=New York|publisher=Fodor's Travel|isbn=978-1-4000-1803-1}}</ref><ref name=moon>{{citation|title=Moon Panama|edition=2nd|date=Nov 2008|publisher=Avalon Travel|location=Berkeley, CA|isbn=978-1-59880-085-2|url-access=registration|url=https://archive.org/details/panama0000fria}}</ref>
== History ==
Sixaola was created on 19 February 1970 by Decreto Ejecutivo 13.<ref name="dta2017" />
== Geography ==
Sixaola has an area of {{formatnum:169.32}} km<sup>2</sup><ref name="inecarea">{{cite web |lang=es |title=Área en kilómetros cuadrados, según provincia, cantón y distrito administrativo |url=https://www.inec.cr/anuario-estadistico/anuario-estadistico-geografia |website=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos |accessdate=26 September 2020}}</ref> and an elevation of {{formatnum:10}} metres.<ref name="divadm" />

== Economy ==

===Tourism===
Tourists pass through Sixaola and Guabito along a road connecting destinations in [[Limón Province]], Costa Rica and [[Bocas del Toro Province]], Panama.<ref name=fodor /> The road is an old elevated railroad grade. A former railroad bridge crosses the [[Rio Sixaola]] at the border. Costa Rican customs is located at the west end of the bridge just down some stairs from the elevated railroad grade.<ref name=fodor /> When crossing the border in either direction, tourists must clear ''both'' Costa Rican ''and'' Panamanian customs.<ref name=moon /> Entry and exit visas are required.<ref name=fodor /><ref name=moon /> Panamanian customs is located alongside the elevated railroad grade right at the east end of the bridge.<ref name=moon/>

The border towns have no accommodations, restaurants, or services.<ref name=moon /> In Costa Rica, [[Puerto Viejo de Talamanca|Puerto Viejo]] offers the closest accommodations, restaurants, and services to the border.<ref name=fodor /> In Panama, [[Changuinola]] offers accommodations, restaurants, and services about {{convert|10|km|mi|0|abbr=on}} from the border.<ref name=moon /> Buses and taxis wait on both sides of the border.
