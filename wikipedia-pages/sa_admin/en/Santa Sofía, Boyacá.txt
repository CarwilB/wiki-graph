'''Santa Sofía''' is a town and municipality in the [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[Boyacá Department|Boyacá]], part of the subregion of the [[Ricaurte Province]], [[Colombia]]. The urban centre of Santa Sofía is located at an altitude of {{convert|2387|m|ft}} on the [[Altiplano Cundiboyacense]], with the highest elevated parts of the municipality, that have a [[páramo]] ecosystem, reaching {{convert|3000|m|ft}}. Santa Sofía borders [[Moniquirá]] in the north, [[Gachantivá]] in the west, [[Villa de Leyva]] in the southeast, [[Sutamarchán]] in the south, [[Saboyá]] in the southwest, and [[Puente Nacional, Santander|Puente Nacional]], [[Santander Department|Santander]] in the west.<ref name=websiteSantaSofia>{{in lang|es}} [http://www.santasofia-boyaca.gov.co/informacion_general.shtml Official website Santa Sofía]{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

== Etymology ==
Originally, Santa Sofía was called Guatoque, a name derived from [[Chibcha language|Muysccubun]], meaning "creek of the mountain". The name was changed in 1906 to Santa Sofía, because mail that was meant to go to [[Guateque]], in southern Boyacá, was accidentally delivered in Guatoque. The name Santa Sofía was given to the town honouring Sofía Ángulo de Reyes, spouse of the then [[list of presidents of Colombia|president of Colombia]] [[Rafael Reyes]].<ref name=websiteSantaSofia/>

== History ==
The area of Santa Sofía before the [[Spanish conquest of the Muisca|Spanish conquest]] was inhabited by the [[Muisca people|Muisca]], who used the area to cross the lands towards the northern parts of their [[Muisca Confederation]]. 

It was in nearby [[Muisca economy#Trade|Sorocotá]] that [[Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada|De Quesada]] and his men first discovered the potato in 1537<ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OA8lAQAAMAAJ&pg=PT5|title=Contributions from Texas Research Foundation|date=1962|publisher=Texas Research Foundation|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last=Pemberton|first=John|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JxAQHFL7qtQC&pg=PT148|title=Conquistadors: Searching for El Dorado: The Terrifying Spanish Conquest of the Aztec and Inca Empires|date=2011|publisher=Canary Press eBooks|isbn=978-1-907795-96-1|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last=Sokolov|first=Raymond|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=EH9LqhXuJ3QC&pg=PA125|title=Why We Eat What We Eat: How Columbus Changed the Way the World Eats|date=1993-04-05|publisher=Simon and Schuster|isbn=978-0-671-79791-1|language=en}}</ref> together with maize and beans<ref>{{Cite book|last=Brücher|first=Heinz|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ryjwCAAAQBAJ&pg=PA22|title=Useful Plants of Neotropical Origin: and Their Wild Relatives|date=2012-12-06|publisher=Springer Science & Business Media|isbn=978-3-642-73313-0|language=en}}</ref> alongside many aspects of local culture and customs as described in the ''History of the conquest of the New kingdom of Granada'' by [[Juan de Castellanos]].<ref>{{Cite book|last=Snodgrass|first=Mary Ellen|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=cWJgDwAAQBAJ&pg=PT747|title=World Food: An Encyclopedia of History, Culture and Social Influence from Hunter Gatherers to the Age of Globalization: An Encyclopedia of History, Culture and Social Influence from Hunter Gatherers to the Age of Globalization|date=2012-09-15|publisher=Routledge|isbn=978-1-317-45160-0|language=en}}</ref>

Within the boundaries of the municipalities is located the ''Hoyo de la Romera'', a [[sinkhole]] where unfaithful [[Women in Muisca society|Muisca women]] were punished.{{citation needed|date=October 2021}}

Santa Sofía, then as Guatoque, was founded by Spanish colonists ordered by Amar y Borbón on January 10, 1810, just before the independence of Colombia from the [[Spanish Empire]] was called.<ref name=websiteSantaSofia/>

==Climate==
Santa Sofía has a [[subtropical highland climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]]: ''Cfb'')<ref>{{Cite journal |date=23 October 2023 |title=Table 1 Overview of the Köppen-Geiger climate classes including the defining criteria. |url=https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02549-6/tables/1 |journal=Nature: Scientific Data |language=en}}</ref> with consistently mild temperatures.

== Economy ==
Main economical activities of Santa Sofía are livestock farming and agriculture, especially [[curuba]], [[tomato]]es and [[strawberry|strawberries]]. Other products cultivated are [[potato]]es, [[maize]], [[pea]]s, [[bean]]s, [[sugarcane]] and [[yuca]].<ref name=websiteSantaSofia/>

=== Tourism ===
Santa Sofía is a rural community that gives access to the [[Guatoque Páramo]] and the [[Paso del Ángel]], an ancient [[Muisca architecture#Roads|indigenous road]]. There are various caves and waterfalls located around Santa Sofía.<ref name=websiteSantaSofia/>
