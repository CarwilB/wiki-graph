'''Cuajimalpa de Morelos''' ({{IPA|es|kwaxiˈmalpa|lang|Cuajimalpa.ogg}}; more commonly known simply as Cuajimalpa) is a [[Boroughs of Mexico City|borough]] (''demarcación territorial'') in [[Mexico City]]. It is located on the west side of the city in the Sierra de las Cruces mountains which separate Mexico City from the [[State of Mexico]].  The borough is named after the former rural town of Cuajimalpa, which has since been absorbed by [[urban sprawl]]. The borough is home to the [[Desierto de los Leones National Park]], the first declared in Mexico as well as the second largest annual [[passion play]] in Mexico City.

==History==
The proper name of the borough is Cuajimalpa de Morelos. The borough was named after the prominent community and former municipality of San Pedro Cuajimalpa which remains the seat of local government. "Cuajimalpa" is derived from the [[Nahuatl]] "Cuauhximalpan" which meant place of [[sawmill]]s.<ref name=logotipo>{{cite web |url=http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=39&Itemid=587 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703033311/http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=39&Itemid=587 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 3, 2013 |title=Logotipo |publisher=Borough of Cuajimalpa |date=November 3, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Logo |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref><ref name=delegobmx>{{cite web  |url=http://www.df.gob.mx/index.php/delegaciones/78-delegaciones/82-cuajimalpa-de-morelos  |title=Delegación Cuajimalpa de Morelos  |publisher=Government of Mexico City  |location=Mexico City  |language=es  |trans-title=Borough of Cuajimalpa de Morelos  |access-date=April 14, 2013  |url-status=dead  |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703033248/http://www.df.gob.mx/index.php/delegaciones/78-delegaciones/82-cuajimalpa-de-morelos  |archive-date=July 3, 2013  }}</ref> The appendage of "de Morelos" was added in 1970 to honor [[José María Morelos]], a hero of the [[Mexican War of Independence]].<ref name=programa>{{cite web |url= http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/documentos/cuajimalpa.pdf |title= Programa Delegacional de Desarrollo Urbano de Cuajimalpa de Morelos |language= es |trans-title=Urban Development Program of the Borough of Cuajimalpa de Morelos |access-date= April 14, 2013 }}{{dead link|date=August 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

In 1342 the [[Tepanec]]as established themselves in the area, controlling the forests for about 100 years from their capital in [[Azcapotzalco (altepetl)|Azcapotzalco]]. When the Tepanecas were overthrown by the [[Aztec Triple Alliance]] in the mid 15th century, many fled to this rugged terrain.<ref name=delhis>{{cite web |url=http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=36&Itemid=585 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703033239/http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=36&Itemid=585 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 3, 2013 |title=Historia |date=November 3, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=History |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref><ref name=cuajides>{{cite web |url= http://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/cuajimalpa.html|title= Cuajimalpa  |date= 26 January 2012 |publisher= Mexico Desconocido magazine  |location=Mexico City |language=es  |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref> During the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|Spanish conquest]], [[Hernán Cortés]] subdued settlements in the area such as Santa Rosa, Santa Lucía, Cuauhximalpan, Chimalpa and Acopilco to secure the roads leading to the [[Toluca Valley]]. In 1534, Cortés took personal control of lands in the area, calling it San Pedro Cuauhximalpa and established towns such as San Lorenzo Acopilco, San Mateo Tlaltenango and San Pablo Chimalpa.<ref name=delhis/><ref name=barrmag>{{cite web |url= http://www.mexicocity.gob.mx/barriosmagicos/guiasBMT/Cuajimalpa.pdf |title= Cuajimalpa |work= Barrios Mágicos Turisticos |publisher= Government of Mexico City |location= Mexico City |language= es |access-date= April 14, 2013 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20130512170721/http://www.mexicocity.gob.mx/barriosmagicos/guiasBMT/Cuajimalpa.pdf |archive-date= May 12, 2013 |url-status= dead |df= mdy-all }}</ref>

In the 17th century the [[Carmelites]] founded a hermitage and monastery called Desierto de los Leones, today a museum and national park.<ref name=cuajides/> At this time, the indigenous population of the area recovered enough that there were efforts to reclaim lost territory and government. To this end, a type of codex called "techialoayan" was created to document the history of indigenous communities to make legal claims. One of the most important of this is the [[Techialoyan Codex of Cuajimalpa]]. It describes a solemn meeting of authorities to confirm the extension and political organization of the area. Written in Nahuatl, it remained a valid legal document until 1865, when then [[Maximilian I of Mexico|Emperor Maximilian I]] had it translated into Spanish. Today the original document is part of the [[Archivo General de la Nación (Mexico)|Mexican Federal Archives]]. In 1997, the document was named as part of the [[Memory of the World Programme|“Memory of the World” by UNESCO]] .<ref name=bibdig>{{cite web |url=http://bdmx.mx/detalle.php?id_cod=24 |title=Códice Techialoyan de Cuajimalpa |publisher=Archivo General de la Nación Biblioteca Digital Mexicana |location=Mexico |language=es |trans-title=Techialoyan Codex of Cuajimalpa |access-date=April 14, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005000617/http://bdmx.mx/detalle.php?id_cod=24 |archive-date=October 5, 2013 }}</ref>

From the colonial period into the 19th and early 20th centuries, the most common economic activities in the area were the harvesting of firewood and the making of charcoal, with some raising of crops and livestock for domestic consumption.  The area was also important as a way station, providing lodging and food to travelers between the [[Valley of Mexico]] and the Toluca Valley.<ref name=logotipo/><ref name=delegobmx/> In 1884, a rail line was constructed through the same area.<ref name=programa/>

In the early 19th century, the first insurgent army under [[Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla]] arrived at the area, with Hidalgo himself staying at the Mesón San Luisito.<ref name=cuajides/> The town of Cuajimalpa was made the seat of a municipality in 1862.  During the last decades of the 19th century and into the 20th modern services such as running water, paved roads and electric lighting were introduced. A number of industries related to construction supplies were also founded.<ref name=cuajides/>

During the [[Mexican Revolution]] there were clashes in the area between forces loyal to [[Venustiano Carranza]] and [[Emiliano Zapata]] in El Contadero and San Pedro. One story from the time says that the [[Liberation Army of the South|Zapatistas]] cut the ears of the inhabitants of the town of Cuajimalpa to distinguish them from those loyal to Carranza.<ref name=cuajides/>

In 1929, the area's status was changed from municipality to borough with the reorganization of the Federal District of Mexico City.<ref name=delhis/><ref name=barrmag/> Although it remained very rural until the 1930s, its main economic activities were in decline with many moving to Mexico City.<ref name=programa/>

Prior to the 1950s, urbanization of the area was limited because of its terrain.<ref name=programa/>  In the mid 20th century, the industrialization of the Valley of Mexico began to reach the borough of Cuajimalpa. The population of the area increased over 900% from 1950 to 1980, with the highest rate of growth in the 1970s.<ref name=programa/><ref name=delhis/> New residential construction fueled the growth, covering former forest and agricultural areas especially in areas such as San Lorenzo Acopilco, Las Lajas, La Pila, Las Maromas, Xalpa, Cola de Pato, Atliburros, Cruz Blanca, Moneruco, Chancocoyotl, Teopazulco, Tlapeaca, Texcalco and Pito Real. These include large residential subdivisions such as El Contadero and Lomas de Vista Hermosa, as well as unregulated settlements on the sides of hills and small canyons.<ref name=delhis/> The pace of growth remained high in the 1980s, in part due to the 1985 earthquake, which prompted many to move away from the soft soils of the city center into the more solid rock of the west and south of the valley. However, the rapid development began to cause environmental problems, including the building over former pit mines which had later been used as landfills. In the 1990s, efforts were begun to regulate growth and protect natural areas.<ref name=delhis/> Much of the territory has been urbanized and contains some of the city's most expensive residential and commercial real estate, with newer developments for upper classes pushing out lower income groups.<ref name=programa/>

In the 1990s and 2000s there were changes in the borough's borders due to the settlements of disputes between the Federal District and the State of Mexico.<ref name=geografia>{{cite web |url=http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=41&Itemid=588 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703050911/http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=41&Itemid=588 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 3, 2013 |title=Geografía |publisher=Borough of Cuajimalpa |date=November 3, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Geography |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

==Geography==
[[File:La casa de los patos.jpg|thumb|left|In the Desierto de los Leones National Park]]
[[File:Croquis suelo conservacion.jpg|thumb|Map showing area of conservation]]
The Cuajimalpa borough is located on the west side of the Federal District of Mexico City. To the west of it are the municipalities of [[Ocoyoacac]] and [[Huixquilucan de Degollado]] in the State of Mexico. It also borders the boroughs of [[Miguel Hidalgo, D.F.|Miguel Hidalgo]], [[Álvaro Obregón, D.F.|Alvaro Obregon]] and [[Magdalena Contreras]] in the Federal District.<ref name=delegobmx/><ref name=programa/> The borough covers an area of 8,905 hectares, just over five percent of the total of the Federal District.<ref name=programa/>

The borough is situated on the east side of the Sierra de las Cruces, a volcanic mountain range which separates the Federal District of Mexico City from the State of Mexico and has an average elevation of 2,750 meters above sea level.<ref name=programa/><ref name=orografia>{{cite web |url=http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=42&Itemid=589 |title=Orografía |publisher=Borough of Cuajimalpa |date=November 3, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Orography |access-date=April 14, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703040219/http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=42&Itemid=589 |archive-date=July 3, 2013 }}</ref> The terrain is very rugged  with mountain peaks mostly found in the center and south. These include La Palma (3,810 masl), San Miguel (3,800 masl), El Cochinito (3,760 masl) and El Ángel (3,330 masl).<ref name=orografia/> The land mostly consists of volcanic rock with small valleys and canyons that contain sedimentary deposits; however, there is no longer volcanic activity.<ref name=programa/>

The area contains many fissures, natural caverns and small canyons which are the result of tectonic forces and erosion. Most of these run from north-northeast to south-southwest.<ref name=orografia/> There are small flat areas suitable for agriculture such as around the towns of San Pablo Chimalpa and San Lorenzo Acopilco. The canyons are formed by the channeling of rainwater along fissures.<ref name=programa/> There are two types of canyons: stable ones with wide bottoms and less steep slopes (mostly in the northeast) and V-shaped ones with steep unstable slopes, which can be found in various parts of the borough. The largest canyons are the Santo Domingo, Agua Azul, Los Helechos and Hueyatlaco.<ref name=orografia/> The borough contains seven "mini" basins called Arroyo Agua de Leones, Arroyo Santo Desierto, Río Tacubaya, Río Becerra, Río San Joaquín, Río Barrilaco and Río Magalenas. These are currents which channel rainwater along the rugged terrain with most running southwest to northeast. Three have their beginnings in the Sierra de las Cruces with the rest in the center or north of the territory. These streams and rivers then run into several other sections of Mexico City and into the State of Mexico. Because of the many cracks in the soil and rock, the borough is an important aquifer recharge area as well as the source of water for freshwater springs.<ref name=hidrografia>{{cite web |url=http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=43&Itemid=590 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130703040402/http://www.cuajimalpa.df.gob.mx/index.php?option=com_k2&view=item&layout=item&id=43&Itemid=590 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 3, 2013 |title=Hidrografía |publisher=Borough of Cuajimalpa |date=November 3, 2012 |language=es |trans-title=Hydrography |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

The climate ranges from temperate to cold and its mostly humid. Average annual temperatures vary between 10C and 12C with average annual precipitation varying from between 1,200mm and 1,500mm.<ref name=programa/> The canyons have microclimates which tend to be moister because prevailing winds do not reach and dry them out.<ref name=peligra>{{cite news |author=Ivan Sosa |title= Peligra reserva en Cuajimalpa  |publisher= Reforma  |location=Mexico City |date=August 15, 2004 |page=4  |language=es  |trans-title=Reserve in danger in Cuajimalpa }}</ref>

About twenty percent of the borough is classified as conservation zone with the rest urbanized; however, thirty percent of the population lives in these areas.<ref name=programa/> The area contains a number of threatened species of animal in the Valley of Mexico such as carpenter birds, hummingbirds, [[cacomistle]]s and several species of lizard.<ref name=peligra/> The highest elevations, mostly encompassed by the [[Desierto de los Leones National Park]], are the best conserved. This forest is an important source of oxygenation for the Valley of Mexico.<ref name=programa/> The greatest environmental threat is the continuation of urban sprawl, which threatens forests and other protected areas.<ref name=peligra/> Areas which already have high levels of deforestation suffer significant erosion.<ref name=programa/> Although there are official efforts in place to control building, illegal settlements are a significant concern, especially those on the edges of the national park.<ref name=programa/><ref name=peligra/>

==San Pedro Cuajimalpa and other communities in the borough==
[[File:SanPedroChurchCuajimalpa02.JPG|thumb|left|Facade of the Parish of San Pedro]]
The former rural town of San Pedro Cuajimalpa still maintains a distinct identity although it has been fully incorporated into the urban sprawl of Mexico City. The town used the ''Techialoyan Codex of Cuajimalpa'', written in [[Classical Nahuatl language|Classical Nahuatl]] at the end of the 17th century, "to prove its rights to its lands, which it retained until 1865."<ref>{{Cite web
| title = Techialoyan Codex of Cuajimalpa
| work = [[World Digital Library]]
| access-date = 2013-12-29
| date = 2012-06-12
| url = http://www.wdl.org/en/item/9682/
}}</ref> Its main road is called Avenida Juárez, the oldest road in the borough, and connects the former town center to the old Mexico City-Toluca highway. While only very few of the old traditional houses remain (two on the corner of Avenida Juarez and Coahuila Street with a few next to Jardín Hidalgo), the historic center of the town still exists.<ref name=cuajides/> It was named a "Barrio Mágico" in 2011.<ref name="mexdes">{{cite journal |date=November 2011 |title=Mexico Desconocido Guia Especial:Barrios Mágicos |trans-title=Mexico Desconocido Special Guide:Magical Neighborhoods |journal=Mexico Desconocido |pages=5–6 |location=Mexico City |publisher=Impresiones Aereas SA de CV |editor1-first=Beatriz  |editor1-last=Quintanar Hinojosa |language=es |issn=1870-9400 }}</ref>

The historic center contains a [[Traditional fixed markets in Mexico|traditional Mexican market]], the main plaza and the borough hall and former municipal palace.<ref name=barrmag/>  The center also contains an open-air stage called the Foro Pedro Infante. It is named after [[Pedro Infante|a 20th-century film star]] who made part of a movie called [[Vuelven los García]] on the spot.<ref name=cuajides/><ref name=barrmag/> Jardín Hidalgo is next to the main plaza and the San Pedro Parish. It is a small space filled with trees and a kiosk.<ref name=cuajides/> The borough hall contains the Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla Museum. It contains copies of the Techialoyan Codex of Cuajimalpa among other items. Originally the museum was as at a former inn called the Mesón de San Luisito, where Miguel Hidalgo stayed after the [[Battle of Monte de las Cruces]].<ref name=barrmag/> One modern addition to the historic center was a public skate park behind the borough offices opened in 2013.<ref name=skatepark>{{cite news |url= http://df.milenio.com/cdb/doc/noticias2011/c07fa8ecd60022456af27d9758169c4c|author=Ana Karen Martinez  |title= Inauguran primer Skate Park en Cuajimalpa |publisher= Milenio  |location=Mexico City |date=February 2, 2013 |language=es  |trans-title=Inaugurate first skate park in Cuajimalpa |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

The historic center is still dominated by the Parish of San Pedro, the oldest church in the borough. It was founded in the 16th century but the current building was begun in 1628 and completely finished in 1925.<ref name=programa/><ref name=barrmag/> The atrium is paved and has only a few trees.  The portal is Neoclassic with a frieze formed by [[metope (architecture)|metope]]s and [[triglyph]]s from ancient Greek architecture, along with a thick [[cornice]]. The main entrance has a niche on each side.  A truncated arch is found above the cornice and the base has the papal seal which alludes to [[Saint Peter]], the patron saint.  The rest of the facade is covered in red [[tezontle]] stone and there are two bell towers. The interior has a single nave. On the main altar, there is an image of Saint Peter, the patron on the town, inside a cypress. The vaults and cupola of the ceiling have simple ornamentation, the side walls have various oil paintings and there are a number of images of various saints.<ref name=cuajides/><ref name=barrmag/>

San Pablo Chimalpa is one of the two oldest settlements of the borough. Its name comes from Nahuatl which means "place of the shield."  Oral tradition states it was founded in 1532 by a warrior named Chimalli and Viceroy [[Antonio de Mendoza]] when land was being redistributed.<ref name=programa/>

San Mateo Tlaltenango is located high in the Sierra de las Cruces next to the Desierto de los Leones.  The name Tlaltenango comes from Nahuatl and means "en the walls of the earth." The area has been occupied since the pre-Hispanic period. The current town was founded in 1532 by Hernán Cortés, with Viceroy Enrique de Almanza taking possession in 1571.<ref name=programa/>

San Lorenzo Acopilco dates to the pre Hispanic period, reorganized by the [[conquistador]]s.<ref name=programa/>

El Contadero dates to at least 1753 and is located on a hill on the west side of the Chapultepec forest. It began as a single house which some historians state was the main house of a hacienda despite its small size. In the 20th century, the house served as a store, inn, and cantina serving travelers on the Mexico City-Toluca road. Today the area is a small town with a few streets.<ref name=programa/>

==Landmarks==
[[File:DesiertoLeonesChapel1.JPG|thumb|Chapel of the former monastery]]
The major attraction of the borough is the [[Desierto de los Leones National Park|Desierto de los Leones]], which is home to a former monastery and retreat.<ref name=barrmag/> In 1606, the Carmelites constructed a hermitage and monastery high up in the mountains of the Sierra de las Cruces.<ref name=programa/> The name literally means :Desert of the Lions." "Desert" is what Carmelites called remote retreats. The "lions" are from the surname of two brothers over which the monks had a dispute over the land.<ref name=cuajides/> It remained an isolated retreat until 1814. Then the site became a hospital administered by the [[Company of Jesus]].<ref name=programa/> Today the buildings of the former monastery are home to three museums, one dedicated to the site, one to the [[Celebration of Mexican political anniversaries in 2010|bicentennial of Mexico's Independence]] and one to the [[Mexican Revolution]].<ref name=cuajides/><ref name=amplia>{{cite news |author=Jesus Alberto Hernandez |title= Amplia Cuajimalpa su museo de sitio  |publisher= Reforma  |location=Mexico City |date=February 1, 2005 |page=4  |language=es  |trans-title=Cuajimalpa expands its site museum }}</ref> The site is surrounded by the Desierto de los Leones National Park, the first such declared in Mexico in 1917 by Venustiano Carranza. The park extends over 1,500 hectares with mountain peaks and canyons forested with pine and [[Quercus agrifolia|holm oak]]. Services of the park include the Peteretes Ecological Center and an area with restaurants selling local specialties.<ref name=cuajides/><ref name=barrmag/> The Desierto receives over 300,000 visitors per year with the busiest times being [[Holy Week in Mexico|Holy Week]] and December.<ref name=amplia/>

The Rancho los Laureles Ecotourism Park is also in the borough. It has camping and areas for bonfires and picnics. It also has areas for mountain biking, paintball and a petting zoo with deer.<ref name=ecotourismo>{{cite web |url=http://www.eluniversaldf.mx/cuajimalpa/ecoturismo-una-aventura-para-vivir-dentro-del-df-.html |title= Ecoturismo, una aventura para vivir dentro del DF  |work= El Universal DF |publisher=El Universal  |location=Mexico City  |date=March 29, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Ecotourism, an adventure to experience in the Federal District |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

==Festivals and events==
[[File:Sem Santa 2010 viernes (339).jpg|thumb|Crucifixion scene on Good Friday in Cuajimalpa]]
The largest and most important annual event of the borough is the passion play and fair, the 100th edition of which was held in 2013.<ref name=barrmag/><ref name=asiste>{{cite news |url=http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/notas/913560.html |title=Asiste medio millón a Viacrucis de Cuajimalpa |publisher=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=March 29, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Half a million attend the Good Friday procession in Cuajimalpa |access-date=April 14, 2013 |archive-date=April 2, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130402053421/http://www.eluniversal.com.mx/notas/913560.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> For the entire event, between 1.5 and 2 million people visit the town with 500,000 people for [[Good Friday]] alone.<ref name=asiste/><ref name=mescobedo>{{cite news |url=http://www.wradio.com.mx/noticias/actualidad/la-pasion-de-cristo-segun-cuajimalpa/20130328/nota/1867048.aspx |title= La Pasión de Cristo, según Cuajimalpa  |publisher= W Radio  |location=Mexico City |date= March 28, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=The Passion of Christ according to Cuajimalpa |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref> The passion play is the second largest in Mexico City after [[Passion Play of Iztapalapa|that of Iztapalapa]].<ref name=cuajides/> The passion play involves over 200 people in various speaking and non-speaking roles and locals design and make the sets.<ref name=mescobedo/>  The fair occurs for the entire week, featuring local products such as handcrafts and food.<ref name=cuajides/> During Holy Week, the sale of alcohol is banned in the borough, including nightclubs and bars.<ref name=alcohol>{{cite news |url= http://www.excelsior.com.mx/comunidad/2013/03/20/889896 |title= Suspenderán venta de alcohol en Cuajimalpa por Semana Santa  |publisher= Excelsior  |location=Mexico City |date= March 20, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Will suspend sale of alcohol in Cuajimalpa for Holy Week |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

The reenactments include events related to Jesus's entrance to Jerusalem on [[Palm Sunday]], and the [[washing of feet]] and the [[Last Supper]] on [[Maundy Thursday]].<ref name=mescobedo/>  The two most important days are Good Friday and [[Holy Saturday]]. The Good Friday procession extends for two kilometers and ends with the scene of the crucifixion. The cross carried by the actor playing Jesus weighs about ninety kilos.<ref name=asiste/> The most distinctive part of Cuajimalpa's Holy Week is Holy Saturday. Four young men are chosen each year to play Judas, two over 18 and two under. They dress in red, wear a mask and carry a whip. Judas represents evil but those chosen to play the role are considered to have the greatest responsibility, watching over others in the passion play procession on Good Friday and help with coordination.<ref name=escenifican>{{cite news |url=http://www.eluniversaldf.mx/cuajimalpa/nota59661.html|author= Isamara Gonzalez |title= Escenifican tradición de los Judas en Cuajimalpa  |publisher=El Universal |location=Mexico City |date=March 30, 2013 |language=es |trans-title=Stage the Judas tradition in Cuajimalpa |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref> Their tasks begin at 8am when they "rob" various stands related to the traditional fair although in reality the items are donated by the vendors.  These usually include utensils and toys. Then those playing Roman soldiers go and look for Judas to arrest; however, locals then offer the Judas's their houses to hide and often to eat and drink. At about 2 pm the Judas's are corralled by the soldiers and brought to the parish church of San Pedro. At 3 pm the captured Judas's are "hung" from the bell tower but then people come to the atrium to donate money for their release, often receiving one of the "stolen" items from the fair in return. One last aspect is a flogging but only for the older men playing Judas.<ref name=cuajides/><ref name=escenifican/> The more traditional [[burning of Judas]] also occurs on this day,<ref name=asiste/> but in competition with Iztapalapa, the borough has also added a fireworks show along with monumentally sized Judas figures.<ref name=mescobedo/>

Other important events related to the religious calendar is [[Carnival in Mexico|Carnival]], held just before Lent,<ref name=cuajides/> and for the Christmas season, there is a "Christmas village" set up in the historic center, completely with ice rink and [[toboggan]] slide.<ref name=pistahielo>{{cite news |url= http://www.maspormas.com/noticias/df/cuajimalpa-ya-tiene-su-pista-de-hielo|title= Cuajimalpa ya tiene su pista de hielo |publisher= Mas por mas  |location=Mexico City |date=December 13, 2012 |language=es  |trans-title=Cuajimalpa now has its ice rink |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref><ref name="navideña">{{cite news |url= http://www.excelsior.com.mx/2012/12/11/comunidad/874410 |author=Kenya Ramirez |title= Construirán villa navideña en delegación Cuajimalpa |publisher= Excelsior  |location=Mexico City |date=December 11, 2012 |language=es  |trans-title=Will build a Christmas village in Cuajimalpa borough|access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

The borough also hosts an annual Feria del Hongo dedicated to the gastronomy of native mushrooms in late summer at the borough hall plaza and the Desierto de los Leones. This event has been promoted in various ways, including a series of tickets by the National Lottery.<ref name=hongos>{{cite news |author=Marichuy |title= Hara Cuajimalpa fiesta de los hongos  |publisher= Reforma  |location=Mexico City |date=August 16, 2002 |page=13  |language=es  |trans-title=Cuajimalpa will have a mushroom fair}}</ref>

==Education==
The borough has various public and private institutions of higher education including the Colegio de Empresarios Excelentia Fervic, the Instituto de Hematopatología, the Instituto Nacional de Administración Pública (INAP), the [[Universidad del Valle de México]], Campus Santa Fe and the Universidad Hebarica. In the 2000s, the [[Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana]] added a Cuajimalpa campus. It offers ten undergraduate degrees and seven postgraduate degrees in three academic divisions (Sciences of Communication and Design, Natural Sciences and Engineering and Social Sciences and Humanities).<ref name=uamsite>{{cite web |url= http://www.uam.mx/unidad_cuajimalpa.html |title= Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana Unidad Cuajimalpa  |publisher= UAM  |location=Mexico City |language=es  |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>

==Transportation==
Main roads include the toll federal road connecting Mexico City and Toluca, Prolongación Paseo de la Reforma, Avenida Vasco de Quiroga and the Chamapa-La Venta highway.<ref name=barrmag/> Because of urbanization and the thru traffic between Mexico City and Toluca, there is significant congestion. The most pressing traffic problems are those in the historic center of Cuajimalpa and traffic between the rest of Mexico City and Toluca particularly during rush hours.<ref name=proyecta>{{cite news |author=Alejandro Ramos |title= Proyecta Cuajimalpa crear un libramiento  |publisher= Reforma  |location=Mexico City |date=June 29, 2006 |page=8  |language=es  |trans-title=Cuajimalpa projects the creation of a free highway }}</ref>

The most important bus lines include routes 4, 5, 76 and 80 of the RTP system.<ref name=barrmag/>

The [[El Insurgente]] commuter rail has a stop in Santa Fe.

==Socioeconomics==
[[File:Torre Arcos Bosques.jpg|thumb|right|Torre Arcos 2]]
There are areas of great wealth near areas populated by lower classes.<ref name=cuajides/> Cuajimalpa is the most expensive place to buy a house or apartment in Mexico City with the average square meter costing 55% more than the average for the rest of the city. However, prices vary widely within the borough between developments for the most wealthy and areas which are still poor.<ref name=mascara>{{cite news |url= http://eleconomista.com.mx/distrito-federal/2012/11/04/cuajimalpa-delegacion-mas-cara-adquirir-departamento |author=Erick Ramirez |title= Cuajimalpa, la delegación más cara para adquirir un departamento |publisher= El Economista  |location=Mexico City |date=November 4, 2012 |language=es  |trans-title=Cuajimalpa, the most expensive borough to buy an apartment |access-date=April 14, 2013 }}</ref>
