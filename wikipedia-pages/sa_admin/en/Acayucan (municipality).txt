'''Acayucan''' is a [[municipalities of Veracruz|municipality]] in the [[List of states of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Veracruz]]. It is located in the state's southeast, in the [[Olmeca (region)|Olmeca]] region. The municipal seat is the city of [[Acayucan, Veracruz]]. It covers a total of 724.65&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> (279.79 sq mi).

At the 2005 INEGI Census, the municipality reported a population of 79,459, of whom 49,945 lived in the municipal seat.<ref>{{cite web|title=Acayucan |url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/veracruz/municipios/30003a.htm |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |access-date=2008-11-08 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070523100119/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/veracruz/municipios/30003a.htm |archive-date=2007-05-23 }}</ref>
Of the municipality's population, 3,655 (5.22%) spoke an [[Languages of Mexico|indigenous language]], primarily [[Popoluca]], followed in numbers by [[Zapotec language|Zapotec]].

In addition to the seat, the municipality's largest other communities are [[Corral Nuevo]] and [[Dehesa, Veracruz|Dehesa]].

==History==
Conquistadores [[Gonzalo de Sandoval]] and [[Hernan Cortés]] passed through Acayucan on the route from [[Tuxtepec]] to [[Tenochititlan]]. In 1580 it was part of the "Villa de Espíritu Santo" ([[Coatzacoalcos]]); when this was wiped out by pirates, Ayayucan became the seat of the ''[[Alcaldía mayor]].''<ref name="monografia">{{cite web |title=.:· Monografía de Acayucan ·:. |url=https://www.acayucan.com/documentos/monografiaacayucan.php |website=www.acayucan.com |access-date=May 30, 2020}}</ref>

The local indigenous population rose up in rebellion in 1787.<ref name="monografia" />

After the [[Mexican War of Independence]] in 1821, the Acayucan municipality was established in 1824. San Martin Acayucan became a ''villa'' in 1824 and a town on June 13, 1848. The community was destroyed by a hurricane in 1888.<ref name="monografia" />

[[Mexican Liberal Party|Hilario C. Salas]] [[Acayucan Rebellion|took the town of Acayucan]] in his fight against the Porfirian dictatorship in 1906. San Martin Acayucan became a city on September 26, 1910.<ref name="monografia" />

Electrification came to the city in 1946. The Catholic church was destroyed in the [[List of earthquakes in 1957|July 1957 earthquake]].<ref name="monografia" />

In 2005&ndash;07, the [[Municipal president (Mexico)|municipal president]] (mayor) of Acayucan was Fabiola Vázquez Saút of the [[PAN (Mexico)|PAN]], a daughter of the slain [[cacique]] (local [[baron]]) [[Cirilo Vázquez]].<ref>http://search.chron.com/chronicle/openDocument.do?docRef=11_23_2006_2_p1a23MEXBOSS&selectedPath={{dead link|date=October 2016 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

In May [[2020 in Mexico|2020]], Francisco Navarette Serna, presumed drug chieftain responsible for the February [[2016 in Mexico|2016]] murders of five young people from Acayucan between 16 and 27 years old, was killed at a party at the height of the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Ataque armado deja siete muertos y dos heridos en salón de fiestas en Veracruz |url=https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/estados/ataque-armado-deja-siete-muertos-y-dos-heridos-en-salon-de-fiestas-en-veracruz |website=El Universal |access-date=May 30, 2020 |language=es |date=30 May 2020}}</ref>
