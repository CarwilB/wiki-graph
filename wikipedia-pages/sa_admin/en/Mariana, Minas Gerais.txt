'''Mariana''' ({{IPA|pt|maɾiˈɐ̃nə|lang}}) is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeastern]] state of [[Minas Gerais]], Brazil. As of 2022, the city had a population of 61,387, estimated to be at around 64,058 in 2024, making it the 59th most populous city in its state.<ref name="IBGE_overview_mariana"/> Known for its characteristic [[Portuguese colonial architecture|colonial-style]] architecture, Mariana is the [[Colonial Brazil|oldest settlement]] in Minas Gerais,<ref name="IPHAN">{{Cite web 
|title= História – Mariana (MG)
|url= http://portal.iphan.gov.br/pagina/detalhes/1491/
|url-status=live 
|access-date=2025-08-27 
|publisher= IPHAN
|trans-title= History – Mariana, MG
|language=pt
}}</ref> as well as the first to be officially ordained with the status of "[[city]]",<ref name="IPHAN"/> in the mid-18th century.

Situated along the lower contours of a river valley and surrounded by many rocky escarpments, Mariana has both historical and contemporary ties to the [[Mining|mining industry]],<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal">{{Cite book 
|title=Enciclopédia dos Municípios Brasileiros 
|publisher=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] 
|year=1959
|pages=49–57
|language=pt 
|trans-title=Encyclopaedia of Brazilian Municipalities 
|url=https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv27295_26.pdf 
|chapter=Mariana 
|access-date=2025-08-27 
|url-status=live
}}</ref> having been founded on the very same spot where gold was first spotted by Portuguese explorers in the late 17th century.<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /> Although mining is largely responsible for the historical economic development of the region,<ref name="IPHAN" /> the city of Mariana has also been at the centre of an [[Mariana dam disaster|unprecedented disaster]] brought about by the mining industry, with the [[Mariana dam disaster|2015 Samarco levee rupture and floods]].

Due to its cultural legacy and the active conservation of its many historical sites,<ref name="Prefeitura-História">{{Cite web
|title= A cidade – Histórico

|website= Prefeitura de Mariana
|language= pt
}}</ref> Mariana is a popular [[Tourism in Brazil|tourist destination]], making tourism another important component of the local economy. As such, the city is part of the ''Caminho dos Diamantes'' ({{langnf|pt||Diamonds Path}}) section of the ''[[Estrada Real]]'', the largest [[Scenic route|tourist route]] in Brazil,{{efn

|For more information, see https://institutoestradareal.com.br/en/estrada-real}} featuring dozens of other historically significant destinations – such as the town of [[Ouro Preto]], only {{Convert|18|km|mi}} west of Mariana.

==History==

===Pre-colonial period===

[[File:E11 p9.jpg|thumb|left|bottom|upright|<small>[[Jean-Baptiste Debret]], 1834.</small>]]

Although the official historiography around the city is primarily focused on the [[Portuguese Empire|Portuguese colonial efforts]] that formally originated it,<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano">{{Cite encyclopedia

However, [[Anthropology|ethnic studies]] have shown that the indigenous population that contemporarily inhabits some of the regions around Mariana, known as the [[Aimoré|Krenak]] (also known as ''Aimoré''<ref name="Krenak" />), originally came from the coastal regions of southern [[Bahia]],<ref name="Krenak" /> being part of a larger family-group the Portuguese called Botocudos (<small>lit.</small> 'Labret Wearers') and which were gradually relocated – through coercion, displacement and extermination<ref name="Krenak" /> – to the valleys of the [[Doce River|Doce River Basin]], up until the early 20th century.

===Colonial period===
The modern city of Mariana was born out of the ''[[Bandeirantes|Bandeiras]]'' period of Brazil's colonial history:<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /> during the second half of the 17th century and up until the late 18th, many members of the [[Portuguese nobility|colonial aristocracy]] either financed or personally commanded expeditions into the interior of the colony, with the intent of exploring the regions still unknown to the Portuguese Crown and in hopes of finding new untapped riches such as gold and [[silver]].

Around the 16th of July, 1696, a group of ''bandeirantes'' {{efn|{{IPA|pt|bɐ̃deɪ̯ˈɾɐ̃tɪs|lang}}}} led by explorers Salvador Fernandes Furtado de Mendonça and Miguel Garcia da Cunha,<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> camped on the banks of a brooklet, which they named Ribeirão do Carmo (<small>lit.</small> 'Carmel Brook'), after [[Our Lady of Mount Carmel]].<ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Prefeitura-História" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> While resting, the Portuguese explorers found that the unremarkable stream of water sported rich [[Alluvium|auriferous alluviums]]<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> and promptly decided to establish a permanent camp in the area, giving rise to a small settlement that came to be homonymously known by the stream's name.<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" />

Attracting more and more settlers and gold prospectors, by 1711 the settlement came to be known as Vila Albuquerque<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> (<small>lit.</small>. 'Albuquerque Town') through a royal decree that established the [[Captaincy|São Paulo and Gold Mines Captaincy]], splitting the administrative region in and around Mariana from the São Vicente Captaincy<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> (today, the state of [[Rio de Janeiro (state)|Rio de Janeiro]]) and nominated Antônio Albuquerque Coelho de Carvalho as its first governor-general<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /> (giving the town its new namesake). Through this, Vila Albuquerque became the ''de facto'' capital of the captaincy, and although the administrative headquarters of the colony were still located in the much older town of [[São Paulo dos Campos de Piratininga|São Paulo of Piratininga]],<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /> Antônio Albuquerque – as well as both of his successors – decided to take up residence in the emerging township, mainly due to the Portuguese Crown's riveting interest in the area's burgeoning gold-mining capacities. Only a year later, on April 14, 1712, when news of the town's incorporation into the colonial administration reached the [[metropole]], the town's establishment was officially ratified and it was registered under its originating namesake brook: ''Vila de Nossa Senhora do Ribeirão do Carmo'' (<small>lit.</small> 'Town of Our Lady of Carmel Brook'), or simply, '''Vila do Carmo'''.<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" />

In 1720, a major rebellion broke out in the neighbouring municipality of Vila Rica (today, [[Ouro Preto]]), mainly sparked by the local elite's growing dissent of the colonial administration's [[tax]]ation of gold production<ref name="IPHAN" /> – one of many similarly motivated rebellions the region saw throughout the 18th century. After violently quelling the rebellion by employing his personal [[Dragoon|dragoon brigade]], the Third [[Count of Assumar]], the region's then governor-general, under the guise of the Crown, established the new Captaincy of Minas Gerais, severing administrative ties with São Paulo and, in order to closely surveil the region's rebellious tendencies,<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> moving the colonial capital from Vila do Carmo to Vila Rica (<small>lit.</small> 'Wealthy Town').<ref name="Atlas">{{Cite web

===Slavery and the gold rush===
{{Main|Brazilian Gold Rush}}

[[File:Rodolfo Amoedo - Ciclo do Ouro, Acervo do Museu Paulista da USP.jpg|thumb|left|top|upright|<small> ''O ciclo do ouro'' (lit. 'The gold cycle'), 1923 painting by [[Rodolfo Amoedo]].</small>]]

All throughout the 18th century and especially so during its first half, [[gold prospecting]], [[gold panning|panning]] and mining came to be the Mariana's primary economic activity,<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /><ref name="Batuques" /> giving rise to an affluent local elite and further strengthening the exploitation of [[Slavery in Brazil|slave labour]] in the region.<ref name="Batuques">{{Cite thesis 
|title=Os batuques do silêncio: patrimônio e educação quilombola no município de Mariana (Minas Gerais); uma análise transecular. 
|trans-title= Drums of silence: Quilombola education and heritage in the municipality of Mariana, MG; a transsecular analysis
|last=Martins 
|first=Vittor P. 
|date=2023-08-14 
|access-date=2025-08-27 
|degree= Thesis
|publisher=[[Federal University of Ouro Preto]] 
|url=https://www.monografias.ufop.br/handle/35400000/5829 
|language=pt  
|pages=23-32
}}</ref>

According to historical demographic research, by the mid-18th century, Mariana was one of the most prominent destinations in the [[atlantic slave trade|slave trade]] throughout all of the Americas,<ref name="Batuques" /> undergoing a rapid growth of its enslaved population in a short period of time: in 1716, the town had a population of 6,834 slaves; by 1718, that number jumped to 10,937, and by 1723, it became 15,828 – more than 1,200 additional slaves per year.<ref name="Batuques" /> The enslaved labour force of the town would peak in 1735, at more than 26,000 people, or roughly a third of all of the captaincy's slaves, mostly employed in gold-mining. For such, the town's gold output was estimated at half a metric ton (around 2.5 million [[Carat (mass)|carats]]) per year, starting in 1720.<ref name="Batuques" />

Under such economic incentives, the region soon began to transform into one of the [[New World|New World's]] most diverse societies in terms of [[African diaspora|coinhabiting African ethnicities]], estimated to be, up until 1723, at around 40 different ethnic groups.<ref name="Batuques" /> Consequently, the town's cultural legacy has been greatly influenced by [[black studies|black culture]]: the region encompassing Mariana and its neighbouring towns is second only to Salvador, Bahia, in harbouring Brazil's largest percentage of black people, most of which directly descend from either freed, indentured or, most notably, escaped slaves, who founded small, secluded settlements around the region known as ''[[quilombo]]s'',{{efn|{{IPA|pt|kiˈlõbʊ|lang}}}} where runaway slaves and other oppressed peoples could establish new lives in liberty.<ref name="Batuques" /> Additionally, Mariana's racial legacy is also illustrated in its current population, with around 73% of its people identifying as either 'black' or 'pardo' ({{langnf|pt||mixed-race}}).<ref>{{Cite web

}}</ref>

===The city of Mariana and the Catholic Church===

Alongside the Portuguese colonial efforts in the region, there was the ever-present authority of the [[Vatican City|Vatican]]; running hand-in-hand with state power, the Church and its symbols were a decisive assertion of dominium and consecration for Portuguese settlers.<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> Since Mariana's founding, in 1696, one the first buildings to ever be erected was precisely that of a Christian [[place of worship]]: the small chapel of [[Our Lady of Assumption]], built by the founding expedition's priest,<ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> and which, in 1701, was officially made into a [[Catholic parish]] by the [[Bishops in the Catholic Church|bishop]] of Rio de Janeiro.<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" />

[[File:Vista da nave da Catedral Basílica de Nossa Senhora da Assunção.jpg|thumb|right|upright=0.8|<small>Interior view of Mariana's mother church, locally known as '''Igreja da Sé'''.</small>]]

Having grown both in population and economic prestige by the middle of the 18th century, the town underwent several major urbanistic changes, with much of its civil infrastructure being built around the founding chapel, itself becoming the town's [[mother church|matrice]], in 1712.<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> Three decades later, in 1745 – the [[Brazilian Gold Rush|pinnacle of gold extraction]] in the region<ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /><ref name="Batuques" /> – the town was elevated to the status of city by [[John V of Portugal|King John V]], who assigned José Fernandes Alpoim as the lead-engineer responsible for designing and erecting a new [[Urban planning|urban plan]] for the city.<ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /> By the same royal decree, the town was officially renamed '''Mariana''' (at the time, spelled 'Marianna'), in honour of Portugal's queen at the time, [[Maria Anna of Austria]].<ref name="Prefeitura-História" /><ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /><ref name="Atlas" />

In the same year (though only taking effect in 1748<ref name="Arquidiocese">{{Cite web 
|title=História
|url=https://arqmariana.com.br/historia
|url-status=live 
|access-date=2025-08-27 
|website=Arquidiocese de Mariana
|trans-title=History
|language=pt
}}</ref>) the [[Holy See]] reorganized the local ecclesiastical subdivision into the [[Diocese|Diocese of Mariana]], making it, at the time, the sixth bishopric in Colonial Brazil (Bahia, 1551; Rio de Janeiro, 1676; [[Olinda]] 1676; [[Maranhão]], 1677; [[Belém|Belém do Pará]], 1720).<ref name="Arquidiocese" /> For such, [[Pope Benedict XIV]] assigned [[Monsignor|Mons.]] Manuel da Cruz as the diocese's first bishop,<ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Mariana Espaço Urbano" /><ref name="Arquidiocese" /> conferred with the [[prelate|religious stewardship]] of around forty local parishes,<ref name="Arquidiocese" /> whilst headed by the [[Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Assumption, Mariana|Basilica of Our Lady of Assumption]]. In 1906, [[Pope Pius X]] elevated its ecclesiastical status once again, to that of the [[Archdiocese of Mariana]], which it still holds to this day.<ref name="IPHAN" /><ref name="Arquidiocese" />

===Recent history===

[[File:Alphonsus Guimarães.jpg|thumb|right|upright|<small>Late 19th century quill drawing of '''[[Alphonsus de Guimaraens]]''', by Laudelino Freire.</small>]]

By late 19th century, long after the [[independence of Brazil]], Mariana and its surrounding region slowly began to lose its prominence amongst the urban centres of Minas Gerais; not only had the gold mines long been exhausted, no longer providing any substantial economic gains, but also, with the founding of the new state capital of [[Belo Horizonte]] in 1897 (a title previously held by Mariana's neighbouring city of Ouro Preto), the city would no longer be viewed as a major economic centre.

Nonetheless, the city still managed to claim a few instances of historical notoriety: in 1906, a newly appointed [[judge|municipal judge]] would set up residence in Mariana with his family, one that would later be widely known throughout much of the [[Portuguese-speaking world|Lusophony]] as the famous [[Symbolism (movement)|symbolistic]] poet [[Alphonsus de Guimaraens]]. In 1987, the city would inaugurate the Casa Alphonsus de Guimaraens Museum, situated in the poet's former home, dedicated to his life and [[Complete works|poetic oeuvre]].

In 1945, Brazil's president [[Getúlio Vargas]] would grant Mariana the title of [[National monument|National Monument]], for its significant role in the formation of the [[national identity]] of the Brazilian nation, both through its cultural, religious, and historical heritage and its active civic participation in the national sphere.<ref name="Prefeitura-História" />

Another relevant historical attribution the city would be remembered by can be found in its direct connection to the history of the [[Estrada de Ferro Central do Brasil|Brazilian Central Railway]]: by 1955, one could board a train in Mariana's main [[train station]] and travel to one of seven locally serviced destinations: [[Alvinópolis]], [[Barra Longa]], [[Guaraciaba, Minas Gerais|Guaraciaba]], Ouro Preto, [[Santa Bárbara, Minas Gerais|Santa Bárbara]], Belo Horizonte and Rio de Janeiro.<ref name="Enciclopédia Municipal" />

===2015 Catastrophe===
{{Main|Mariana dam disaster}}

[[File:(2015-11-10) Visita à Bento Rodrigues-MG 113 Romerito Pontes (23407404309).jpg|thumb|left|top|<small>A street in '''Bento Rodrigues''', a [[municipal district]] of Mariana, after the catastrophe.</small>]]

Mariana would rise to national notoriety once again in 2015, though, unfortunately, for disastrous reasons. On November 5, just {{Convert|35|km|mi}} from Mariana's city centre, a local [[tailings dam]] owned and maintained by the [[Samarco|Samarco mining corporation]] gave way after a major structural failure of the levee, unleashing an insurmountable slew of mud and [[Tailings|mining refuse]] throughout most of the [[Doce River Basin]], eventually reaching the [[Atlantic Ocean]].<ref name="MPMG">{{Cite web
