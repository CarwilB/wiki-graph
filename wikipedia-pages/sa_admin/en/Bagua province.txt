'''Bagua''' is a province of the [[Amazonas (Peruvian department)|Amazonas Region]] in [[Peru]]. It is located in the north and central part of the [[Amazonas (Peruvian department)|department of Amazonas]]. The region is known for its rugged terrain. It is also cut by deep [[Canyon|gorges]] that have been formed by the important rivers that cross this province, as well as their numerous [[Tributary|tributaries]].

Bagua's terrain is varied in height, from hills of 400 m. along the banks of the [[Marañón River|Marañón]] and [[Santiago River (Peru)|Santiago]] rivers, to up to 2,500 m. in the [[Mountain range|mountain zone]] of the south.

The [[Marañón River|Marañón river]] runs from south-west to north-east across the province and is one of the principal rivers in the Peruvian Amazon. The [[Chiriaco River|Chiriaco]] or [[Chiriaco River|Imaza river]] runs from the south to the northeast. The [[Nieva River|Nieva river]] runs from southeast to north, and the [[Utcubamba River|Utcubamba]] from the east to the northwest.

The city of Bagua is located on a natural plateau rising from the right shore of the [[Utcubamba River|Utcubamba river]]. All the houses of the western part of the city have a view of the river waters.

Numerous species of animals are typical of the zone: [[White-lipped Peccary|huangana]]s, [[cougar|puma]]s, [[Giant Anteater|anteater]]s, [[ocelot]]s, [[cashpaicuro]]s, and [[Andean hairy armadillo (Chaetophractus nationi)|armadillo]]s. There are also numerous snakes such as the [[macanche]], [[Blacktail cribo (Drymarchon corais melanurus)|colambo]], [[uyurechonte]], [[Rattlesnake|cascabel]], [[Lachesis (genus)|shushupe]], etc. The natives hunted them for food.

These species attract tourists for hunting, which is regulated to preserve the species. For collectors, the quantity and variety of [[Butterfly|butterflies]] is a major attraction principally in the wooded zone of the district.

=='El Baguazo'==
{{Main|2009 Peruvian political crisis}}
In August 2008 and June 2009, Bagua suffered social unrest and the occupation of road infrastructure by indigenous communities protesting against new laws that would allow oil and mining companies to enter indigenous territories without seeking the consent or consulting with local communities. Indigenous communities complained that some 70% of the [[Peruvian Amazon]] would be leased for oil and gas exploration, putting at risk their own lives and the environment. In other areas affected by oil drilling and the dumping of toxic waste, local communities in Peru have already experienced severe environmental and health damage.<ref>"Peru tribe battles oil giant over pollution", BBC News, 24 March 2008</ref> Congress responded in 2008 by repealing two of the decrees but took no action on others.

In 2009, President [[Alan García]] signed new decrees, claiming they were necessary to implement the recently concluded [[Free Trade Agreement]] with the United States of America. In April 2009, protesters established a roadblock in the region preventing access to an oil company site.  On 5 June, the government declared a state of emergency and ordered the police to break out of the 59-day roadblock. The police intervention on 6 June resulted in a serious armed clash between the parties, resulting in 5 civilians being killed, with 5 natives, 23 policemen, and another 89 people wounded.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.defensoria.gob.pe/modules/Downloads/informes/varios/2009/informe-adjuntia-006-2009-DP-DHPD.pdf |title=Informe de Adjuntia de Junio de 2009 |language=es |access-date=1 July 2011 |archive-date=3 September 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100903200104/http://www.defensoria.gob.pe/modules/Downloads/informes/varios/2009/informe-adjuntia-006-2009-DP-DHPD.pdf |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ipsnews.net/news.asp?idnews=50608 |title=Suspension of Mining Operation Merely a Placebo], IPS News |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100621005830/http://ipsnews.net/news.asp?idnews=50608 |archive-date=21 June 2010}}</ref>

The indigenous population in Bagua and some news reports dispute the government version, stating that the death toll was in the hundreds and that the military threw bodies of the indigenous people into the river in an attempt to hide them. Black vultures were reported seen around a corpse in the river. Local news correspondents confirmed that the government's version 'did not adjust to the truth'.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c7Kr_RmOjvQ&feature=channel_video_title |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/c7Kr_RmOjvQ |archive-date=21 December 2021 |url-status=live |title=Bagua: la matanza de nativos que oficialmente no ocurrió |website=YouTube |date=7 July 2009 |language=es}}{{cbignore}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EtRkYTLNRqk |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/EtRkYTLNRqk |archive-date=21 December 2021 |url-status=live |title=Peru Police Accused of Protester Death Cover Up |website=YouTube |date=12 June 2009}}{{cbignore}}</ref>{{citation needed|date=October 2014}}
