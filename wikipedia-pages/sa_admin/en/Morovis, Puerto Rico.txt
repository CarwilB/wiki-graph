'''Morovis''' ({{IPA|es|moˈɾoβis}}, {{IPA|es|moˈɾoβiʔ|local}}) is a [[Morovis barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]] located in the central region of the island, north of [[Orocovis]], south of [[Manatí]], [[Vega Baja]] and [[Vega Alta]]; east of [[Ciales]], and west of [[Corozal, Puerto Rico|Corozal]]. Morovis is spread over 13 barrios and [[Morovis barrio-pueblo|Morovis Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city).<ref name="Morovis Municipal Limits with Maps">{{cite web |title=MUNICIPIO AUTÓNOMO DE MOROVIS REVISIÓN PARCIAL AL PLAN TERRITORIAL |url=http://www.gobierno.pr/NR/rdonlyres/EADF3346-E4BC-4FF2-B9D1-7008AC79EE46/0/Anejo1PTRevisi%C3%B3nParcialSeptiembre2009.pdf |website=Gobierno PR |publisher=Junta de Comunidad |access-date=2018-12-02 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181202155230/http://www.gobierno.pr/NR/rdonlyres/EADF3346-E4BC-4FF2-B9D1-7008AC79EE46/0/Anejo1PTRevisi%C3%B3nParcialSeptiembre2009.pdf |archive-date=2018-12-02 |url-status=live }}</ref> It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

==History==

[[File:Don Juan Evangelista Rivera Morovis, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|left|Don Juan Evangelista Rivera bust in Morovis barrio-pueblo]]
In 1815, a group of residents, under the leadership of Don Juan José de la Torre, began the process to separate Morovis from [[Manatí]]. In 1817, the [[government of Puerto Rico]] approved the separation, but it was not until 1818 that the requirements of a population of one thousand residents and the construction of a church and several other public buildings was fulfilled, that the municipality of Morovis was officially founded. In 1822, the mayorship was constructed and in 1823, the new church was built, dedicated to ''Nuestra Señora del Carmen'' on lands donated by Don Juan Evangelista Rivera. Its first mayor was Don Juan José de la Torre, with its second mayor being Don Juan Evangelista Rivera.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://eldiariony.com/2018/06/07/morovis-abraza-la-esperanza/|title=Morovis abraza la esperanza|first=Carmen Molina|last=Tamacas|date=7 June 2018|publisher=El Diario NY|language=es|access-date=2019-06-01|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190601025834/https://eldiariony.com/2018/06/07/morovis-abraza-la-esperanza/|archive-date=2019-06-01|url-status=live}}</ref>
Morovis has a particular nickname. It was the only municipality in Puerto Rico that did not suffer from a [[cholera]] epidemic in 1853; as a result, a common phrase, ''"la isla menos Morovis"'' ("''(all of) the island but Morovis''") was coined. The phrase is believed by Puerto Ricans from outside of the town to have a negative connotation against ''moroveños'', while, in reality, it is considered a positive motto.''{{Citation needed|date=March 2025}}''

In 1875, Abino Casellas was mayor of Morovis and in 1879, Cruz Maria Castro was mayor.<ref name="Puerto Rico Office of Historian 1949 p. 252">{{cite book |title=Tesauro de datos historicos: indice compendioso de la literatura histórica de Puerto Rico, incluyendo algunos datos inéditos, periodísticos y cartográficos | publisher=Impr. del Gobierno de Puerto Rico | series=Tesauro de datos historicos: indice compendioso de la literatura histórica de Puerto Rico, incluyendo algunos datos inéditos, periodísticos y cartográficos | issue=v. 2 | year=1949 | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IVRnAAAAMAAJ&pg=PA252 | language=es | access-date=4 January 2020 | page=252}}</ref>

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the [[United States]]. In 1899, the [[United States Department of War]] conducted a [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Morovis was 11,309.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|author1=Joseph Prentiss Sanger|author2=Henry Gannett|author3=Walter Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n252 161]| language=es}}</ref>

Morovis is a mountainous municipality with an area of approximately {{convert|100.3|km2}}, located slightly north of the center of the island,<ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/morovis-municipality/|title=Morovis Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190621053230/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/morovis-municipality/|archive-date=2019-06-21|url-status=live}}</ref> which was hit particularly hard by [[Hurricane Maria]] on September 20, 2017. With a 2016 estimate of 31,603 residents at the time, 1500 to 2000 homes lost their roof and 500 wooden homes were completed destroyed. Roads and bridges were impacted by landslides and flooding, and residents were left without access to electrical power, telecommunication services and basic necessities for many months.<ref name="END Morovis">{{Cite news|url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/morovis/|title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. [Maria, a name we'll never forget.]|language=es|newspaper=El Nuevo Día}}</ref>

The community in [[San Lorenzo, Morovis, Puerto Rico|San Lorenzo]], a barrio of Morovis with about 1,000 people, was left isolated when the bridge into the barrio was washed away by the [[Morovis River]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metro.pr/pr/noticias/2018/08/07/municipio-anuncia-reparacion-puente-afectado-huracan.html|title=Municipio anuncia reparación de puente afectado por huracán|first=Lyanne Meléndez|last=García|website=Metro |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://cnnespanol.cnn.com/video/cnnee-vo-rec-ramos-una-cuerda-entre-la-vida-y-la-muerte-en-puerto-rico/|title=Con cuerda cruzan río peligroso en Puerto Rico|date=28 September 2017|publisher=CNN|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cnn.com/videos/world/2017/09/28/bridge-collapse-morovis-puerto-rico-watson-lok-cnni.cnn|title=Bridge collapse strands Puerto Ricans - CNN Video|via=edition.cnn.com}}</ref>

Then mayor of Morovis, Carmen Maldonado delivered food to residents, the municipal town hall became a food distribution center and she arranged for the purchase and delivery of a water truck to provide clean water daily to the residents who did not have access to clean water.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/fotogalerias/noticias/puerto-rico/loquemariasellevoenmorovis-1251915/|title=Lo que María se llevó en Morovis|website=Primera Hora|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.npr.org/2017/11/09/563028732/frustration-mounts-over-puerto-ricos-new-normal-as-federal-troops-leave-the-isla|title=Frustration Mounts Over Puerto Rico's 'New Normal' As Federal Troops Leave The Island|website=NPR.org}}</ref><ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar.Incomunicación y falta de agua aflige a Morovis| trans-title=Maria, a name we won't forget. No communication and no drinking water aflicts Morovis|newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/morovis/| language=es | access-date=2022-09-12}}</ref>

Maldonado spoke to [[Governor of New York|New York governor]] [[Andrew Cuomo]], requesting his help and Cuomo responded by contacting ''H.e.a.r.t. 9/11'', a non-profit organization. In early 2019, Maldonado received and thanked volunteers from ''H.e.a.r.t. 9/11'', mostly carpenters who came to Morovis to repair and rebuild homes damaged by the hurricane.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/llegandesdenuevayorkparareconstruircasasenmorovis-1319499/|title=Llegan desde Nueva York para reconstruir casas en Morovis|date=2 January 2019|website=Primera Hora|language=es}}</ref> In late 2019, ''H.e.a.r.t. 9/11'' was still rebuilding roofs in Morovis.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.facebook.com/plugins/post.php?href=https://www.facebook.com/HEART911/posts/10157762169484723&width=500 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/iarchive/facebook/290077484722/10157762169484723 |archive-date=2022-02-26 |url-access=limited|title=Facebook|website=www.facebook.com}}{{cbignore}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Hurricane Maria Puerto Rico Response | website=HEART 9/11 | date=September 6, 2017 | url=https://www.heart911.org/archive/puerto-rico | access-date=February 21, 2022}}</ref>

==Morovis National (Veterans) Cemetery==
[[File:Morovis National Cemetery construction in 2019, Morovis, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Morovis National Cemetery, construction Phase I]]
The [[Morovis National Cemetery]], a US veterans cemetery, was built in Morovis within a 247.5-acre parcel of land that can be accessed from [[Puerto Rico Highway 137|Highway 137]] at ''Km. 11.2''. It was built to replace the existing [[Puerto Rico National Cemetery]] located in [[Bayamón, Puerto Rico|Bayamón]] which reached capacity.<ref name="cemetery">{{cite web |title=Puerto Rico National Cemetery Replacement Morovis, Puerto Rico |url=https://www.cem.va.gov/CEM/docs/EA/Puerto_Rico_Draft_EA.pdf |website=U.S. Dept. of Veterans Affairs |publisher=U.S. Dept. of Veterans Affairs |access-date=2019-06-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170513213909/https://www.cem.va.gov/CEM/docs/EA/Puerto_Rico_Draft_EA.pdf |archive-date=2017-05-13 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.elnuevodia.com/english/english/nota/aclaimforbetterhealthservices-2496239/|title=A claim for better health services|date=28 May 2019|website=El Nuevo Dia|access-date=13 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190625005915/https://www.elnuevodia.com/english/english/nota/aclaimforbetterhealthservices-2496239/|archive-date=25 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Construction was underway in 2019<ref>{{cite web | title=Comienza construcción nuevo cementerio nacional de Puerto Rico en Morovis | website=www.efe.com | date=25 January 2019 | url=https://www.efe.com/efe/usa/puerto-rico/comienza-construccion-nuevo-cementerio-nacional-de-puerto-rico-en-morovis/50000110-3878614 | language=es | access-date=26 June 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Quality Control (QC) system management for Veterans Affairs (VA) | website=San Diego Project Management, PSC | date=2018-10-29 | url=https://www.sandiegopm.net/new-page-20 | access-date=2020-08-22}}</ref> with interments slated to begin in 2021.<ref>{{cite web | title=VA dedicates Morovis National Cemetery  |website=Office of Public and Intergovernmental Affairs | date=2016-09-01 | url=https://www.va.gov/opa/pressrel/pressrelease.cfm?id=5582 | access-date=2021-07-28}}</ref>

==Economy==
An Energy Consortium was signed in late February 2019 by the mayors of [[Villalba, Puerto Rico|Villalba]], [[Orocovis]], Morovis, [[Ciales]] and [[Barranquitas]] municipalities. The consortium is the first of its kind for Puerto Rico. It is intended to have those municipalities work together to safeguard and create resilient, and efficient energy networks, with backups for their communities. This is part of the hurricane preparedness plan of these municipalities, which were hit particularly hard by [[Hurricane Maria]] on September 20, 2017.<ref>{{cite web|last=Vazquez|first=Priscilla|title=Asociación de Industriales de Puerto Rico|url=http://industrialespr.org/communications/news/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190312030317/http://industrialespr.org/communications/news/|archive-date=2019-03-12|access-date=2019-03-16|publisher=Industriales Puerto Rico}}</ref>

==Government==
[[File:Town hall in Morovis barrio-pueblo.jpg|thumb|left|Town Hall in Morovis barrio-pueblo]]
All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a mayor, elected every four years. The current mayor of Morovis is [[Carmen Maldonado González]], of the [[Popular Democratic Party of Puerto Rico|Popular Democratic Party]] (PPD). She was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2016|2016 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district III]], which is represented by two Senators. In 2024, [[Brenda Pérez Soto]] and [[Gabriel Gonzáles]] were elected as District Senators.<ref>[https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Arecibo_III.xml Elecciones Generales 2024: Escrutinio General] {{webarchive|url= https://elecciones2024.ceepur.org/Escrutinio_General_121/index.html#es/default/SENADORES_POR_DISTRITO_Arecibo_III.xml |date=2024-12-30 }} on CEEPUR</ref>

==Symbols==
The {{lang|es|municipio}} has an official flag and coat of arms.<ref>{{cite web | title=Ley Núm. 70 de 2006 -Ley para disponer la oficialidad de la bandera y el escudo de los setenta y ocho (78) municipios. | website=LexJuris de Puerto Rico | url=https://www.lexjuris.com/LEXLEX/Leyes2006/lexl2006070.htm | language=es | access-date=2021-06-15}}</ref>

===Flag===
The flag of Morovis is divided vertically in two equal parts. The immediate one to the mast is yellow and has the eagle of the coat of arms, in this case with the head facing left, toward the flagpole. The other half is divided in seven vertical stripes of equal width, four red ones alternated with three yellow ones.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020">{{cite web | title=Morovis | website=LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico | date=19 February 2020 | url=http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/MOROVIS.html | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219000440/http://www.lexjuris.com/pueblos/pueblos_files/MOROVIS.html | archive-date=19 February 2020 | url-status=live | language=es | access-date=17 September 2020}}</ref>

===Coat of arms===
. The coat of arms of the Municipality of Morovis is formed by an eagle in the left partition, which symbolizes Saint John the Evangelist and sings in honor of the founder of the town, Don Juan Evangelista Rivera. The five cuatros observable in the right partition symbolize the fact that the town of Morovis is the main center of production of such typical Puerto Rican musical instruments. The shield in the center represents the Order of the Carmelite Nuns, and symbolizes as well the Our Lady of the Carmen, matron of the town. The left half is a field of yellow color (yellow for gold) in which resides a saber (black) eagle, beak and claws in red, its head enclosed with a halo.<ref name="LexJuris (Leyes y Jurisprudencia) de Puerto Rico 2020" />
