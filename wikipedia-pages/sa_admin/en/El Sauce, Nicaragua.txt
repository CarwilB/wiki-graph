'''El Sauce''' ({{IPA|es|el ˈsawse}}) is a town and a [[Municipalities of Nicaragua|municipality]] in the [[León Department]] of [[Nicaragua]].<ref name="manfut">{{cite web |url= http://www.manfut.org/leon/sauce.html |title= Bienvenidos a El Municipio de El Sauce. León, Nicaragüa |accessdate=2007-01-31 |work= manfut.org}}</ref> Located in the foothills of the Segovia Mountains, the municipality has a population of 31,750 and covers an area of 693&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.

El Sauce is bordered to the north by the municipalities of [[Achuapa]] and [[Estelí]] and bordered to the south by the municipality of [[Larreynaga]].

==History==
{{see also|Cristos Negros of Central America and Mexico}}
El Sauce was originally called Guayabal. The town gained much attention when a statue called the Black Christ of Esquipulas was sent there from Guatemala was placed for the first time under a willow tree. From then, the area was renamed "El Sauce".

During the decade of 1900, El Sauce was a railway crossing which included items such as coffee and animals being moved to the port city of [[Corinto, Nicaragua|Corinto]]. The railway closed 40 years ago because it was sold as scrap, an event that had significant impact on the railway closure. El Sauce is still a small town dedicated to agricultural production, bees, and livestock.

The town was the site of a [[Battle of El Sauce|battle]] that pitted the [[United States Marine Corps|American Marines]] and [[National Guard (Nicaragua)|Nicaraguan National Guardsmen]] against [[Augusto César Sandino|Sandinista]] rebels on December 26, 1932, during the [[Sandino Rebellion]]. It was the last major action of the war.

==Population==
In 1971, El Sauce had a population of 12,869 inhabitants. In 1995 the population grew to 24,289 inhabitants, which represents 7.36% of total department. The annual growth rate of the municipality of the last inter-census period (1971–1995) was 2.82%.
