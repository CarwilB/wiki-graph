'''Itacoatiara''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|municipality]] in the central eastern portion of state of [[Amazonas (Brazilian state)|Amazonas]], inland [[northern Brazil]].
 
Its population was 103,598 ([[2022 Brazilian Census|2022 Census]])<ref name="IBGE" /> and its area is 8,600&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>.<ref>[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) - {{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php |title=Archived copy |access-date=2014-08-03 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070109005631/http://www.ibge.gov.br/cidadesat/default.php |archive-date=2007-01-09 }}</ref> 
 
The town is known as "City of painted stone" - which is its name translated from [[Tupian languages|Tupi]]. Itacoatiara has an important port, which is responsible for a considerable amount of cargo transportation in Amazonas.

Its Catedral Prelatícia Nossa Senhora do Rosário, dedicated to [[Our Lady of the Rosary]], is the episcopal see of the pre-diocesan [[Roman Catholic Territorial Prelature of Itacoatiara]].

== History ==
In 1655, an ''aldeia'' (hamlet) named Arroquis, on Albi Island{{where|date=February 2014}} on the [[Madeira River]], was founded by Portuguese [[Jesuit]] missionary [[António Vieira]]. It was thereafter moved four times due to aggression by the indigenous [[Mura people]].

In 1757, the inhabitants of ''aldeia'' Abacaxi (where did the inhabitants of Arroquis go?) moved to the left bank of the Amazon River, where Itacoatiara is now located.

In 1758, Francisco Xavier de Mendonça passed through the region for the second time in order to found the [[captaincy of São José do Rio Negro]].{{relevance inline|date=June 2016}}

In 1759, the ''aldeia'' was elevated to the category of village, and was then called Serpa.

Itacoatiara became an actual town by law 74 of December 10, 1857, but in 1858 it was once more demoted as village, named ''Nossa Senhora do Rosário de Serpa''. Finally, on April 25, 1874, by law 283, Itacoatiara was again elevated to the category of town, with its current name.

==Demography==
===Ethnic composition===

Itacoatiara is marked by the cultural, political and economic traits inherited from the [[Portuguese people|Portuguese]], [[Spanish people|Spanish]] and [[Dutch people|Dutch]] settlers. The city grew that way, but going back a bit in history, one can not forget the importance of [[Amerindians]] in the ethnic contribution. It was the Amerindians who began human occupation in the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazon]], and their descendants, the [[caboclos]], developed in intimate contact with the environment, adapting to the regional peculiarities and opportunities offered by the forest.

In historical formation, Itacoatiara's demography is the result of the miscegenation of the three basic ethnic groups that compose the Brazilian population: [[Native Brazilian]], European and African, thus forming the region's mestizos (caboclos). Later on, with the arrival of the immigrants, especially Japanese,<ref name="Japoneses">{{cite web |publisher=Japão 100 |url=http://www.japao100.com.br/arquivo/quarta-raca/ |title=A quarta raça |date=2007 |access-date=28 January 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150602220436/http://www.japao100.com.br/arquivo/quarta-raca/ |archive-date=2 June 2015 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Arabs,{{citation needed|date=August 2021}} Syrians, Lebanese{{citation needed|date=August 2021}} and [[Jews]] coming mostly from Morocco,<ref name="Judeus">{{cite web |publisher=Revista Morashá |url=http://www.morasha.com.br/conteudo/artigos/artigos_view.asp?a=645&p=2 |title=Judeus marroquinos que vieram para a Amazônia |date=December 2010 |access-date=28 January 2011 |archive-date=17 January 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120117184449/http://www.morasha.com.br/conteudo/artigos/artigos_view.asp?a=645&p=2 |url-status=dead }}</ref> a unique culture was formed that characterizes the population of the city, its values and way of life.

According to the [[IBGE]] 2000 census, the population of Itacoatiara is composed of: [[Pardo]] (58.37% or 41.367 inhabitants), [[White Brazilians|White]] (32.21% or 22.504 inhabitants), [[Afro Brazilians|Black]] (5.7% or 4 110 inhabitants), [[Amerindians]] (3.09% or 2,228 inhabitants) and [[Asian Brazilians|Yellow]] (0.63% or 430 inhabitants).<ref name="Etnias">{{cite web |publisher=IBGE |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P |title=Tabela 2094 — População residente por cor ou raça e religião |date=2000 |access-date=28 January 2010}}</ref><ref name="Cor">{{cite web |publisher=IBGE |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&z=cd&o=7&i=P|title=Tabela 2094 — População residente por cor ou raça e religião |date=2000 |access-date=28 January 2011}}</ref> There are also 50 people who did not declare their ethnicity, representing 0.10% of the total population.<ref name="Etnias" /><ref name="Cor" />

==Geography==
The town is located 176&nbsp;km east by air or 270&nbsp;km downstream by river from [[Manaus]]. It is the third most populous town in the state, on the bank of the [[Amazon River]].
