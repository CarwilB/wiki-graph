'''Dajabón''' ({{IPA|es|daxaˈβon}}) is a [[municipality]] and capital of the [[Dajabón Province|Dajabón]] province in the [[Dominican Republic]], which is located on the northwestern Dominican Republic frontier with Haiti. It is a market town with a population of about 26,000, north of the [[Cordillera Central, Dominican Republic|Cordillera Central]] mountain range.

Dajabón is located on the [[Dajabón River]]. This river is also known as the ''Massacre River'' due to an incident that took place in 1728 in which Spanish settlers killed 30 French Buccaneers.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.haitiobserver.com/blog/tag/massacre-river|title=Massacre River|website=Haiti Observer|access-date=2017-05-08}}</ref> The name also became popular after being the site of the [[Parsley massacre]] in 1937, when thousands of [[Haitians]] were killed by the [[Dominican Army]] under the orders of [[Rafael Trujillo]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2012-03-27 |title=Dajabón y Juana Méndez, Ciudades Gemelas |url=http://www.elmasacre.com/modulos/articulos/imprimir.php?articulo=6152 |access-date=2024-03-31 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120327013539/http://www.elmasacre.com/modulos/articulos/imprimir.php?articulo=6152 |archive-date=2012-03-27 }}</ref> 

==History==
A battle took place in what is now Dajabón between the Spanish and the French in the 17th century. At the time of the battle, the governor of the French colony, Mr. Cussy, was killed in the Battle of the Sabana Real de la Limonada, near modern-day [[Limonade]] in northern Haiti.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.bagn.academiahistoria.org.do/boletines/boletin18/BAGN_1941_No_18-04.pdf |title=Gobernadores de la Isla de Santo Domingo (see page 375 for information on the battle of the Sabana Real de la Limonada in 1690) |access-date=2014-06-27 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140210203204/http://bagn.academiahistoria.org.do/boletines/boletin18/BAGN_1941_No_18-04.pdf |archive-date=2014-02-10 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

The city was initially founded between 1771 and 1776, but was soon after abandoned during the [[Dominican War of Independence|War of Independence]]. It was settled again soon after the [[Dominican Restoration War|War of Restoration]] in 1865.

==Population==
[[File:Dajabon Dominican Republic.jpg|thumb|right|Streets of Dajabón Dominican Republic.]]
According to the Population and Housing Census, the municipality has a total population of 25,685, of which 12,644 were men and 13,041 women.{{cn|date=March 2024}} The urban population of the municipality was 63.57%.{{cn|date=March 2024}} This population data includes the population of the Cañongo municipal district.

The bridge across the river connects Dajabón to the Haitian city of [[Ouanaminthe]]. On Mondays and Fridays, Haitians are permitted to temporarily cross the bridge to sell their goods. Most of the goods are used clothes, shoes, bulk dry goods, and housewares; an area of several acres on the western edge of the city becomes a crowded business place. In addition to the Haitians, Dominicans go to the market to sell food (vegetables grown in their country).
