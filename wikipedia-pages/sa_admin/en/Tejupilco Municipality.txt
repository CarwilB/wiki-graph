'''Tejupilco''' is a [[municipality]] in the [[Mexico State|State of Mexico]], [[Mexico]], located approximately {{convert|100|km}} southwest of the state capital [[Toluca]], along [[Mexican Federal Highway 134|Federal Highway 134]]. Its municipal seat is [[Tejupilco de Hidalgo]].<ref name="turista">{{cite web |url=http://www.turista.com.mx/edomexico/tejupilco-viewarticle-53.html |title=Tejupilco |accessdate=2008-01-28 }}</ref> The municipality has a total area of about {{convert|1,327| km2|abbr=on}}, with a contrasting topography ranging from deep ravines and canyons to high ridges; the highest elevation within the municipality reaches some {{convert|1,117 |m|round=10}} [[above mean sea level|asl]]. The 2005 census recorded a population of 62,547 inhabitants.<ref name=e-local15082a>{{cite web|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15082a.htm |title=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |accessdate=2008-01-28 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070607215525/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/mexico/mpios/15082a.htm |archivedate=June 7, 2007 }}</ref>

Tejupilco was the first municipalities founded in April 1829.<ref name="e-local15082a"/>

Dating from before the [[Spanish Conquest of Mexico|Spanish Conquest]], [[indigenous peoples in Mexico|indigenous groups]] such as [[Otomi people|Otomi]], [[Mazahua people|Mazahua]] and [[Matlatzinca people|Matlatzinca]] have lived in the area now contained by the modern municipality. A number of [[pre-Columbian]] archaeological sites within the municipal boundaries are known, but as yet little investigated. The name "Tejupilco" derives from [[Nahuatl]] and means "in the toes."<ref name="turista"/>

Main economic activities are in the [[agriculture|agricultural]] and local retail sectors. Agriculture is the most significant, with some  {{convert|93,586|ha}} under cultivation. Commerce and retail ranks as the second-most productive economic sector.<ref name="e-local15082a"/>

A market held each Sunday is a main retail venue, where products and crafts typical of the region are sold.<ref name="turista"/>

The typical gastronomy of the region includes a bread known as "niguas", which is made with natural fruit.<ref name="turista"/>

The total municipal area is {{convert|1,327.56|km2|abbr=on}}, and it has a total population of 62,547 people. It borders [[Otzoloapan]], [[Zacazonapan]], [[Temascaltepec]], [[San Simón de Guerrero]], [[Amatepec]], [[Sultepec]], [[Texcaltitlán]] as well as with the states of [[Michoacán]] and [[Guerrero]] to the west.<ref name="e-local15082a"/>
