'''Tacna''' ({{IPA|es|ˈtaɣna}}; [[Aymara language|Aymara]] and [[Quechuan languages|Quechua]]: ''Taqna'') is a [[Provinces of Peru|province]] of the [[department of Tacna]], [[Peru]]. It is located in the country's south, on the border with [[Chile]] and [[Bolivia]] and it's is the largest of the department's four provinces. From 1883 to 1929, the province's territory south of the [[Sama River]] was [[Tacna Province (Chile)|administered by Chile]] following the [[War of the Pacific]].

== Etymology ==
The term ''Tacna'' is derived from two [[Quechua languages|Quechua]] words: ''Taka'', meaning 'to hit', and ''na'', which means 'place'. Thus, the full name means "I hit this place" or "I rule this place".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.munitacna.gob.pe/pagina/sf/tacna/historia|title=Historia|website=Municipalidad Provincial de Tacna}}</ref>

==History==
The area that today constitutes the province was once dependent on the port city of [[Arica|San Marcos de Arica]], which functioned as a ''[[corregimiento]]'' from 1565 to 1787, as a ''[[Partido (region)|partido]]'' of the [[intendancy of Arequipa]] until 1823 and, finally, as a [[Arica Province (Peru)|province of Peru]]. On June 25, 1855, the province of Arica, then suppressed, was reestablished as part of the [[department of Moquegua]], and its northern part was separated to form the province of Tacna.<ref name=Congreso1855>{{Cite news |title=Law creating again the province of Arica in the department of Moquegua |date=1855-06-25 |url=http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1855061.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090730223715/http://www.congreso.gob.pe/ntley/Imagenes/LeyesXIX/1855061.pdf |archive-date=2009-07-30 |work=[[Congress of the Republic of Peru|Congreso de la República]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Fundación de Tacna, Perú |url=https://www.parlamentoandino.org/index.php/actualidad/noticias/1852-fundacion-de-tacna-peru |date=2025-06-25 |website=[[Andean Parliament|Parlamento Andino]]}}</ref> Each province was divided into the following districts:<ref name=Congreso1855/>

Each had their eponymous cities as political capitals. The sub-prefect resided in Arica, while the prefect continued to reside in Tacna.<ref name=Congreso1855/> On June 25, 1875, the [[department of Tacna]] was created by law. It included the provinces of Tacna, [[Arica Province (Peru)|Arica]] and [[Tarata province|Tarata]]. This was the last administrative change prior to the [[War of the Pacific]], which began in 1879.

The first Peruvian territory to be occupied by the [[Chilean Army]] during the war was the port of [[Pisagua, Chile|Pisagua]], whose occupation took place on November 2 of that year. The [[Tarapacá campaign|Chilean campaign]] was successful, and led to the [[Chilean occupation of Peru|military occupation]] of the provinces of [[Department of Tarapacá (Peru)|Iquique and Tarapacá]] following the Peruvian retreat despite a military success at the [[battle of Tarapacá]].

The first Peruvian troops that left [[Tarapacá]] arrived to the city of Arica on December 17. The following year, the [[Chilean Navy]] carried out an amphibious landing at the [[Ilo, Peru|port city of Ilo]] on February 26, and the bombardment of Arica began the following day. The army continued to the south until it reached the city of [[Tacna]] on May 26, after which [[Battle of Tacna|a battle was fought]] at [[Intiorko Hill]], located on the outskirts of the city. The Chilean Army subsequently occupied the city and Bolivia withdrew from the conflict. On June 7, the Chilean advance [[Battle of Arica|reached and conquered Arica]].

Following [[Lima campaign|another campaign]] that reached the capital, the government of [[Miguel Iglesias]] signed the [[Treaty of Ancón]] on October 20, 1883. Under the terms of the agreement, Peru ceded its department of Tarapacá, while the provinces of Tacna and Arica would be subject to Chilean control, after which a plebiscite would be held. This never came to pass.<ref>Egaña, Rafael (1900) ''The Tacna and Arica question. Historical antecedents.--Diplomatic action. Present state of the affair'' (translated from the Spanish edition by Edwin C. Reed) Barcelona Printing Office, Santiago, Chile, {{OCLC|19301902}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article238538689 |title=Chili and Peru. A money offer for Provinces |newspaper=[[The Daily Telegraph (Sydney)|The Daily Telegraph]] |issue=5144 |location=New South Wales, Australia |date=16 December 1895 |accessdate=8 December 2018 |page=5 |via=National Library of Australia}}  , ''...Senor de Pierola, the President of the Republic of Peru, has offered Chili the sum of 10,000,000 piastres (about £790,000) for the provinces of Tacna and Arica. These provinces were ceded by Peru to Chili for ten years (1883-93), at the end of which period a plebiscite was to decide their definite nationality. Owing to the troubles in Peru last year this decision was deferred...''</ref>

===Chilean period===
From 1880 to 1929, the provinces of Arica and Tacna were central to the [[Chilean–Peruvian territorial dispute|46-year dispute]] between both Peru and Chile. Tacna was divided into an area administered by Chile, and another one administered by Peru. Both territories were divided by the [[Sama River]].<ref name=USGPO25>{{Cite book |title=Peru: a Commercial and Industrial Handbook |last=Dunn |first=William Edward |publisher=[[United States Government Publishing Office|U.S. Government Printing Office]] |year=1925 |pages=465–466 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=w-4-R69K5LIC |issue=25}}</ref>

On April 1, 1884, [[Miguel Iglesias]] created the department of Moquegua, incorporating the districts of Locumba and Ilabaya. On October 31 of the same year, the Chilean government established its own [[Tacna Province (Chile)|province of Tacna]], subjecting the territory to a process of [[Chilenization of Tacna, Arica and Tarapacá|forced acculturation]]. Meanwhile, three years after Moquegua's creation, [[Andrés Avelino Cáceres]] declared its law null and void, with the districts returning to their original jurisdiction.

The Supreme Resolution of April 18, 1887, designated the towns of [[Ilabaya District|Ilabaya]] and [[Candarave]] as provincial seats of the sub-prefects of Tacna and Tarata. On January 10, 1890, a Supreme Resultion designated the town of [[Locumba]] as the capital of the province and [[Department of Tacna|department]] of Free Tacna ({{langx|es|Tacna Libre}}), a name that would apply to the non-occupied area for the remainder of the dispute.<ref name=Tizon1904>{{Cite book |title=Texto de Geografía del Perú para colegios |last=Tizón y Bueno |first=Ricardo |publisher=Tipografía Nacional |year=1904 |location=Lima |pages=20-21 |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |title=Plan Estratégico del Distrito de Locumba |publisher=Municipalidad de Locumba |year=2013 |location=Locumba |pages=9 |language=Spanish |url=http://www.munijorgebasadre.gob.pe/pagina/web_inst/archivos/transparencia/trans_1948.pdf |quote=Luego de los resultados de la guerra con Chile y producto de ello la ocupación de la ciudad de Tacna con las nuevas autoridades invasoras, se suscita el acontecimiento mediante el acuerdo de ambos países y mediante Resolución Suprema del 10 de Enero de 1890 se designa al pueblo de Locumba, como capital provisional tanto a nivel provincial como su reconocimiento en lo departamental, llamándosele desde entonces Tacna Libre. Este periodo duró 40 años aproximadamente, y durante el mismo fue recinto de las autoridades políticas, militares y judiciales hasta el 26 de Agosto de 1929, en que Tacna es reincorporada al Perú según plebiscito y aceptando el desmembramiento del Puerto de Arica, pasando este a poder de territorio chileno. |access-date=18 May 2022 |archive-date=4 March 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304094130/http://www.munijorgebasadre.gob.pe/pagina/web_inst/archivos/transparencia/trans_1948.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |title=Tacna, monografia historico-geografica |last=Vargas Hurtado |first=Gerardo |publisher=Imp. Minerva-Sagastigui |year=1929 |pages=52 |language=es |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=QrLUAAAAMAAJ}}</ref> At the time, the town had a population of 300 inhabitants.<ref name=USGPO25/> The same document established the parliamentary representation of one senator and two deputies representing Tacna and Tarata.

On June 3, 1929, the [[Treaty of Lima (1929)|Treaty of Lima]] was signed between [[Emiliano Figueroa]] (then [[President of Chile]]) and [[Pedro José Rada y Gamio]] (then [[Prime Minister of Peru]]). A [[Handover of Tacna|handover ceremony]] took place on August 28 of the same year. While Tacna was reincorporated into Peru, Arica was permanently ceded to Chile.

===Contemporary period===
Following the agreement, a boundary commission worked to formally determine a final border. Its work concluded on July 21, 1930.

On August 29, 1975, a [[Tacnazo|successful military coup]] began in the city with the aim of overthrowing [[Juan Velasco Alvarado]], then president of the [[Revolutionary Government of the Armed Forces of Peru]].

On November 13, 1999, an act to execute the treaty's pending clauses was signed by [[Juan Gabriel Valdés]] ([[Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Chile)|Foreign Minister of Chile]]), [[Alberto Fujimori]] (president of Peru) and [[Fernando de Trazegnies]] ([[Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Peru)|Foreign Minister of Peru]]).

==Politics==
The province of Tacna is administered by a municipal government that also administers [[Tacna District]]. The [[Regional Government of Tacna]] is based in the province's capital, Tacna. The [[Catholic Church in Peru]] administers the province as part of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Tacna and Moquegua]] since 1944.{{efn|name=Diocese|The province was originally part of the [[Archdiocese of Arequipa|diocese of Arequipa]] and, during its Chilean period, it was administered by the [[Diocese of Iquique|Apostolic Vicariate of Tarapacá]]. The original seat of the diocese was [[San Ramón Church]], demolished following its closure in 1910.}}

===List of mayors===
Since 2023, the incumbent mayor is Pascual Milton Güisa Bravo.

=== Boundaries ===
The province is limited to the northwest by [[Jorge Basadre Province]], to the north by [[Tarata Province]], to the east by [[Chile]] and [[Bolivia]], to the south by Chile and to the southwest by the [[Pacific Ocean]].

== Demographics ==
=== Ethnic groups ===
The province is inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|indigenous]] citizens of [[Aymara people|Aymara]] and [[Quechua people|Quechua]] descent. [[Spanish language|Spanish]], however, is the language which the majority of the population (80.88%) learnt to speak in childhood, 16.34% of the residents started speaking using the [[Aymara language]] and 2.55% using [[Quechua language|Quechua]] ([[2007 Peru Census]]).<ref>[http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales?ori=C inei.gob.pe] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130127005551/http://iinei.inei.gob.pe/iinei/RedatamCpv2007.asp?id=ResultadosCensales%3Fori%3DC |date=2013-01-27 }} INEI, Peru, Censos Nacionales 2007</ref>
