'''Port-au-Prince''' ({{langx|ht|Pòtoprens}}) is an [[Arrondissements of Haiti|arrondissement]] in the [[Ouest (department)|Ouest]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Haiti]]. It had 2,109,516 inhabitants at the 2003 Census which was estimated to have risen to 2,759,991 in 2015 in an area of 735.78 [[sq km]] (284.09 [[sq mi]]).<ref>''Institut Haïtien de Statistique et d'Informatique'' (IHSI), 7 August 2003.</ref><ref name="ht2015est">{{Cite web|url=http://www.ihsi.ht/pdf/projection/Estimat_PopTotal_18ans_Menag2015.pdf|title=Population totale, population de 18 ans et plus ménages et densités estimés en 2015|date=March 2015|website=IHSI|language=fr|trans-title=Total Population, Population of 18 and over, Households and Densities Estimated in 2015|access-date=8 May 2017}}</ref> Postal codes in the Port-au-Prince Arrondissement start with the number 61.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.haitiphilately.org/Documents/Downloads/Haiti-An_Update_to_its_Administrative_Organization.pdf |title=Haiti- An update to its Administrative Organization |accessdate=2025-07-05 |work=Haiti Philately Society}}</ref>

==History==

===2010 7.0 earthquake===
[[File:2010 Haiti earthquake damage4.jpg|thumb|Downtown Port-au-Prince after the 12 January 2010 earthquake]]
{{main|2010 Haiti earthquake}}

On 12 January 2010, a magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck in the arrondissement, the largest in Haiti in two centuries.<ref>CNN News Morning, 13 January 2010</ref> The city of [[Port-au-Prince]] suffered much damage, and estimates of upwards of 50,000 deaths, with many facilities destroyed.<ref>CNN, "The Situation Room", 15 January 2010</ref> In [[Pétion-Ville]], the earthquake collapsed a hospital in the city.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2010/01/12/world/AP-CB-Haiti-Earthquake.html|title=Haitian Earthquake Causes Hospital Collapse|date=12 January 2010|work=The New York Times|access-date=12 January 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=http://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/20100113203633/https://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2010/01/12/world/AP-CB-Haiti-Earthquake.html|archive-date=13 January 2010}} </ref> In [[Carrefour, Haiti|Carrefour]], half of the buildings were destroyed in the worst-affected areas.<ref name=NoOther>{{cite web |publisher=ABC News |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2010-01-17/haiti-disaster-like-no-other/1211514 |title='Haiti Disaster Like "No Other"' |first=Lisa |last=Millar |date=17 January 2010 |accessdate=5 July 2025}}</ref> Roughly half the structures were destroyed in [[Gressier]].<ref name=NoOther/>

== Crime ==
By 2020, the level of crime in the Arrondissement was considered so bad that a survey team from the [[World Bank]] was unable to survey the whole Arrondissement beyond the communes of Port-au-Prince, Delmas, Pétion-Ville, and Tabarre. This was due to it being considered too dangerous to survey elsewhere because of the risk of gun violence and kidnapping in the rest of the Arrondissement.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/collecting-data-amid-civil-unrest-first-enterprise-survey-haiti|title=Collecting data amid civil unrest: First enterprise survey in Haiti|website=World Bank |accessdate=5 July 2025}}</ref> By 2024, it was considered that a lot of the territory within the Port-au-Prince Arrondissement was directly under the control of the [[Haitian gang]]s.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://acleddata.com/2024/10/16/viv-ansanm-living-together-fighting-united-the-alliance-reshaping-haitis-gangland/|title=Viv Ansanm: Living together, fighting united – the alliance reshaping Haiti's gangland|first=Sandra|last=Pellegrini|work=ACLED|date=16 October 2024|accessdate=5 July 2025}}</ref> Most of Port-au-Prince was being run by [[Jimmy Chérizier|Jimmy "Barbecue" Chérizier]] and his [[Revolutionary Forces of the G9 Family and Allies]] gang alliance.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-68697406|title=Haiti: Gang leader Jimmy 'Barbecue' Chérizier demands talks role|date=30 March 2024|accessdate=5 July 2025|publisher=BBC News}}</ref>
