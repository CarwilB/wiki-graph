'''Pará de Minas''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the [[States of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]], in the [[Southeast Region of Brazil]]. It is part of the [[Metropolitan belt (Brazil)|metropolitan belt]] of [[Greater Belo Horizonte|Belo Horizonte]] and is situated west of the [[Belo Horizonte|state capital]], approximately {{convert|75|km|mi|abbr=on}} away in the Central region of Minas Gerais. The municipality covers an [[area]] of {{convert|551.247|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}, with {{convert|9.9|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} within the [[urban area]]. Its [[Population (human biology)|population]] was estimated at {{formatnum:102033}} inhabitants in 2024.<ref name="IBGE_ParádeMinas"/>

The municipality's average annual temperature is 21.8&nbsp;°C (71.2&nbsp;°F), and its original vegetation is predominantly [[Atlantic Forest]]. With 95% of the population residing in the urban area, the city had 50 healthcare facilities in 2009. Its [[Human Development Index]] (HDI) is 0.725, classified as high compared to the national average.

The exploration of the area that is now Pará de Minas began in the second half of the 17th century, following the establishment of a rest stop for ''[[bandeirantes]]'' traveling to and from the mines of [[Pitangui]], some of whom settled in the area. The construction of the Our Lady of Mercy Chapel in the 18th century marks the establishment of the settlement, which developed over decades through [[subsistence agriculture]]. It was elevated to district status in 1846 and achieved municipal emancipation in 1859. Throughout the 20th century, the [[textile industry]], [[steel industry]], and [[agribusiness]] gained prominence, establishing Pará de Minas as a key regional hub for [[pig farming]] and [[poultry farming]].

Events such as the city's [[carnival]] (ParáFolia), the Pará de Minas [[Cavalcade]], and the Fest Frango (State Chicken and Pork Fair) are among the main attractions of Pará de Minas, alongside cultural programs at the House of Culture, Cine Café, and the Geraldina Campos de Almeida Municipal Theater. Within the urban area, Bariri Park offers spaces for walking, relaxation, and children's recreation. On the Santa Cruz Ridge, the Christ the Redeemer monument of Pará de Minas, inspired by the [[Christ the Redeemer (statue)|Christ the Redeemer]] in [[Rio de Janeiro]], has become one of the municipality's most iconic landmarks.

==History==
===Origins===
Before the arrival of the first European explorers, most of the territory of present-day Minas Gerais was inhabited by [[Indigenous peoples of Brazil|indigenous peoples]] speaking languages of the [[Macro-Jê languages|Macro-Jê]] linguistic family.<ref name="CRV">{{Cite web |url=http://crv.educacao.mg.gov.br/sistema_crv/banco_objetos_crv/%7B584204C0-DBBD-49A9-A78A-16308219535D%7D_Indio.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PLb8e3w2?url=http://crv.educacao.mg.gov.br/sistema_crv/banco_objetos_crv/%7B584204C0-DBBD-49A9-A78A-16308219535D%7D_Indio.pdf |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=The Indigenous Population of Minas Gerais |author=Descubra Minas |date=16 April 2009 |access-date=5 May 2014}}</ref> The area of present-day Pará de Minas was likely visited around 1555 by [[Bandeirantes|expeditions]] from [[Bahia]], but significant exploration only began in the late 17th century by [[Paulistas|Paulista]] ''bandeirantes'' searching for gold and indigenous people to enslave. Notable expeditions included those led by [[Fernão Dias Pais]] and [[Borba Gato]], who opened paths along the [[Pará River (Minas Gerais)|Pará River]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras">{{Cite web |url=http://www.parademinas.mg.gov.br/st/Politica_Municipal_de_Protecao_do_Patrimonio_Cultural/Dossies_de_tombamento_e_laudos_tecnicos_de_estado_de_conservacao/pdm_2012_dossie_oficio_das_biscoiteiras.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PG6A8wHj?url=http://www.parademinas.mg.gov.br/st/Politica_Municipal_de_Protecao_do_Patrimonio_Cultural/Dossies_de_tombamento_e_laudos_tecnicos_de_estado_de_conservacao/pdm_2012_dossie_oficio_das_biscoiteiras.pdf |archive-date=2 May 2014 |title=Registration Process - Intangible Heritage - Craft of the Biscuit Makers of Pará de Minas |author=Municipality |pages=6–20 |date=29 January 2012 |access-date=5 May 2014}}</ref>

The discovery of gold in [[Pitangui]] led to the opening of a road connecting the mines to São Paulo. Along the Paciência Stream, a rest stop was established for travelers, but the fertile lands prompted some to settle, forming a small community.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> In 1710, Borba Gato received a large portion of the land from Antônio de Albuquerque Coelho de Carvalho, governor of the then Captaincy of São Paulo and Minas de Ouro, where ''sesmarias'' were established. Manuel Batista, nicknamed Pato Fofo, built a chapel dedicated to Our Lady of Mercy, inaugurated on 2 July 1772.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> Due to his nickname, the place became known as Arraial do Patafofo.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico">{{Cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/parademinas.pdf |title=Pará de Minas - History |author=Encyclopedia of Brazilian Municipalities |date=2007 |publisher=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |access-date=4 January 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304003228/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/parademinas.pdf |archive-date=4 March 2016}}</ref>

Initially, the local economy revolved around supplying provisions to expeditions heading to the Pitangui mines, and the settlement's growth was tied to the consumer market for agricultural products from the neighboring city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> Until the early 19th century, the village consisted mainly of farms producing goods, with much of the economy based on [[sugarcane]] derivatives, and small-scale textile industries began to emerge. By 1826, the population was approximately {{formatnum:1646}} residents, when livestock farming was introduced.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> In 1860, an inventory recorded 165 registered properties, with 36.94% engaged in cattle farming and 36.39% in horse breeding. In January 1885, the first major healthcare facility, the Nossa Senhora da Conceição do Pará Hospital, was inaugurated, and in 1893, the first nighttime lighting system, initially using lamps, was implemented.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/>

===Administrative development===
[[File:Rua principal (atual Rua Benedito Valadares) de Pará de Minas MG em 1896.jpg|thumb|left|Main street (now Benedito Valadares Street) of Pará de Minas in 1896]]
[[File:Vista parcial de Pará de Minas MG em 1926.jpg|thumb|left|Partial view of Pará de Minas in 1926]]
Under provincial law no. 312, dated 8 April 1846, the district named Arraial do Patafufo was created, subordinate to the municipality of [[Pitangui]], following the establishment of the [[parish]]. Provincial law no. 386, dated 9 October 1848, established the town, which was abolished twice. The first abolition occurred under provincial law no. 472, dated 31 May 1850, and it was reestablished by provincial law no. 882, dated 8 June 1858, officially established on 20 September 1859 as Vila do Pará and the Parish of Our Lady of Mercy of Pará, as per the aforementioned law.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> Under provincial law no. 1889, dated 15 July 1872, the town was abolished again and annexed to Pitangui as a district, only to be reestablished by provincial law no. 2,081, dated 23 December 1874, and reestablished on 25 March 1875.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/>

The name Pará, meaning a large and fast-flowing river, was changed to Pará de Minas by state law no. 806, dated 22 September 1921, to distinguish it from the state of [[Pará]].<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> Under provincial law no. 2,416, dated 5 November 1877, the town was elevated to city status, and by provincial law no. 3,141, dated 18 October 1883, the districts of Santo Antônio do Rio São João Acima and São Joaquim de Bicas were created. In the following decades, the districts of Mateus Leme, São Gonçalo do Pará, and São José da Varginha (state law no. 2, dated 14 September 1891) and Florestal (state law no. 556, dated 30 August 1911) were established.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> Santo Antônio do Rio São João Acima was renamed Igaratinga by state law no. 843, dated 7 September 1923, and São Joaquim de Bicas was renamed Igarapé by state law no. 1,002, dated 30 July 1931.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/>

By state decree-law no. 148, dated 17 December 1938, Mateus Leme and Igarapé were separated to form the new municipality of [[Mateus Leme]], and by state law no. 1,039, dated 12 December 1953, the district of Carioca was created from lands detached from Igaratinga. State law no. 2,764, dated 30 December 1962, created the districts of Ascensão and Córrego de Barro, while [[Florestal]], [[Igaratinga]], and [[São José da Varginha]] were elevated to municipality status. State law no. 8,285, dated 8 October 1982, established the district of Torneiros.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/> Law no. 4,416, dated 16 September 2004, created the district of Tavares de Minas. Currently, the districts are Ascensão, Carioca, Córrego do Barro, Tavares de Minas, and Torneiros, in addition to the seat district.<ref name="IBGECidades_Histórico">{{Cite web |url=http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/painel/historico.php?codmun=314710&search=minas-gerais%7Cpara-de-minas%7Cinfograficos:-historico&lang=_PT |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140511151328/http://cidades.ibge.gov.br/painel/historico.php?codmun=314710&search=minas-gerais%7Cpara-de-minas%7Cinfograficos:-historico&lang=_PT |archive-date=11 May 2014 |title=History |author=Encyclopedia of Brazilian Municipalities |date=2007 |publisher=Cities@ - IBGE |access-date=11 May 2014}}</ref>

===20th century and recent history===
[[File:Rua principal de Pará de Minas MG e Igreja Nossa Senhora da Piedade ao fundo na década de 1900.jpg|thumb|right|Main street of the city with the Our Lady of Mercy Mother Church in the background, early 20th century]]
The church around which the settlement emerged was demolished in 1953. A second Mother Church was inaugurated on 1 January 1901 but was demolished on 21 April 1971 to make way for the expansion of Padre José Pereira Coelho Square, popularly known as Matriz Square. On 9 April 1972, a new church, the Our Lady of Mercy Mother Church, was inaugurated.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> In 1908, the National Paraense Company, the first major [[textile industry]] in the municipality, was established and operated using [[electricity]]. Early in the 20th century, the Paracatu Railway arrived, and the city's railway station was inaugurated in 1912.<ref name="Estações_Pará">{{Cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rmv_paracatu/para.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PLbBVTkE?url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/rmv_paracatu/para.htm |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=Pará de Minas |author=Brazilian Railway Stations |date=4 March 2013 |access-date=5 May 2014}}</ref> Between the 1910s and 1920s, the Torquato de Almeida School—the only school in the area until 1945—the House of Culture, and a new building for the Nossa Senhora da Conceição do Pará Hospital were established, alongside other urban infrastructure developments, including the first electricity distribution network in 1917 and garbage collection services in 1924.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/>

The [[agribusiness]] sector gained momentum in 1927 with the opening of the first factory for [[butter]], [[ice]], and [[pasteurization]], strengthened by the Inconfidência Plant of Pará de Minas in 1937, later managed by the Minas Gerais Agricultural Company (CAMIG).<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> In 1949, the first Regional Agroindustrial Exhibition of Pará de Minas was held, and in 1951, the Pará de Minas Spinning and Weaving Company was founded, with the population reaching {{formatnum:28432}} inhabitants by 1955. In 1959, the Alterosa Steelworks, Paraense Steel Plant (Usipa), and Pará de Minas Telephone Company were established, pioneering the extractive and steel sectors in the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/>

In 1980, Pará de Minas accounted for approximately 85% of the value of mineral extraction, 61% of industrial production, and 52% of service provision in the west-central region of Minas Gerais.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> The railway line serving the city was deactivated in 1988, overshadowed by the availability of highways.<ref name="Estações_Pará"/> Agriculture's share in the municipal economy declined, becoming closely tied to agribusiness, which, along with the textile industry, accounted for about 70% of municipal revenue by the early 21st century. The introduction of [[poultry farming]] established Pará de Minas as the largest producer in its microregion, alongside improvements in infrastructure and the environment, such as the completion of a sewage treatment plant in 2007.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/>

==Geography==
According to the [[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE), the municipality's area is {{convert|551.247|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}}, with {{convert|9.9387|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} in the [[urban area]] and the remaining {{convert|541.308|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} in the [[rural area]].<ref name="Embrapa_MG">{{Cite web |url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/mg.html |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62tfmRdzK?url=http://www.urbanizacao.cnpm.embrapa.br/conteudo/uf/mg.html |archive-date=2 November 2011 |title=Minas Gerais |author=Embrapa Satellite Monitoring |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> It is located at 19°51'37" south [[latitude]] and 44°36'30" west [[longitude]]<ref name="Embrapa_MG"/> and is {{convert|86|km|mi|abbr=on}} west of the [[Belo Horizonte|state capital]], forming part of the [[Metropolitan belt (Brazil)|metropolitan belt]] of [[Greater Belo Horizonte|Belo Horizonte]] along with 16 other cities.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.itabira.mg.gov.br/portal/?p=6331 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMy6127S?url=http://www.itabira.mg.gov.br/portal/?p=6331 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Itabira seeks inclusion in BH metropolitan belt |author=Itabira Municipality |date=9 February 2014 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> Its neighboring municipalities are [[São José da Varginha]] to the north; [[Onça de Pitangui]] to the northeast; [[Conceição do Pará]] to the east; [[Igaratinga]] to the southwest; [[Mateus Leme]] and [[Itaúna]] to the south; [[Florestal]] to the east; and [[Esmeraldas, Minas Gerais|Esmeraldas]] to the northeast.<ref name="cidadesnet.com">{{Cite web |url=http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/parademinas.htm |title=Pará de Minas - MG |author=Cidades.Net |access-date=6 May 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130909194611/http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/parademinas.htm |archive-date=9 September 2013}}</ref>

According to the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] in effect since 2017, established by the IBGE,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Regional Division of Brazil |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=25 September 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170925170226/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=25 September 2017}}</ref> the municipality belongs to the [[Intermediate Geographic Region of Divinópolis]] and the [[Immediate Geographic Region of Pará de Minas]].<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{Cite web |url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Database by municipalities of the Immediate and Intermediate Geographic Regions of Brazil |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=25 September 2017}}</ref> Previously, under the division into [[Microregions of Brazil|microregions]] and [[Mesoregions of Brazil|mesoregions]], it was part of the Pará de Minas microregion, which was included in the Metropolitan mesoregion of Belo Horizonte.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{Cite web |url=ftp://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=Brazilian Territorial Division 2016 |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=25 September 2017}}</ref>

===Topography, geomorphology, and hydrography===
[[File:Parcial de Pará de Minas, MG.JPG|thumb|left|Partial view of Pará de Minas, showcasing its undulating terrain]]
The terrain of Pará de Minas is predominantly undulating. Approximately 60% of the municipal territory is covered by rolling hills, about 20% consists of mountainous terrain, and the remaining 20% is flat.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> The highest elevation is at Serra da Piteira, reaching {{convert|1196|m|ft|abbr=on}}, while the lowest is at the mouth of the Paciência Stream, at {{convert|772|m|ft|abbr=on}}, with the city center at {{convert|791.93|m|ft|abbr=on}}. The soil is rich in [[agalmatolite]], [[quartzite]], and [[flint]],<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> with a predominance of [[gneiss]] rocks. Along the rivers, the terrain is more sharply rejuvenated, featuring high gneiss escarpments and rocky outcrops.<ref name="CPRM_Pará">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/publique/media/rel_para_minas.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMyNo25q?url=http://www.cprm.gov.br/publique/media/rel_para_minas.pdf |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Geology of the Pará de Minas Sheet |author=Brazil Geology Program |pages=2–8 |date=9 September 2007 |publisher=Mineral Resources Research Company (CPRM) |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

The drainage network displays a distinct rectangular pattern, with deeply incised watercourses, visible in aerial photographs. Alluvial deposits are significantly repositioned and elevated relative to the watercourses. The mountainous terrain is primarily composed of [[quartzite]]s and hydrothermalites, which are resistant to [[weathering]], corresponding to the Andaime and Piteira ridges. On the flattened ridge tops, consolidated [[laterite]] crusts of [[Miocene]]-[[Pliocene]] age are observed.<ref name="CPRM_Pará"/>

===Climate===
The climate of Pará de Minas is classified by the IBGE as [[monsoon-influenced humid subtropical climate]] (type Cwa according to the [[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] system),<ref name="vetmedunivienna">{{Cite web |url=http://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/pics/kottek_et_al_2006.gif |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62twqml5g?url=http://koeppen-geiger.vu-wien.ac.at/pics/kottek_et_al_2006.gif |archive-date=2 November 2011 |title=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification |author=World Map of the Köppen-Geiger climate classification |publisher=Institute for Veterinary Public Health |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> with an average annual temperature of {{convert|21.8|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, featuring dry and mild [[winter]]s and rainy [[summer]]s with high temperatures.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.brasil.gov.br/sobre/geografia/mapas/clima |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62twu0PtQ?url=http://www.brasil.gov.br/sobre/geografia/mapas/clima |archive-date=2 November 2011 |title=Climate |author=Portal Brasil |date=6 January 2010 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/BrasilClimas.jpg |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/62twzLUBt?url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/mapas/GEBIS%20-%20RJ/BrasilClimas.jpg |archive-date=2 November 2011 |title=Brazil - Climates |author=IBGE Library |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> The warmest month, February, has an average temperature of {{convert|24|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, with an average maximum of {{convert|29.8|°C|°F|abbr=on}} and a minimum of {{convert|18.3|°C|°F|abbr=on}}. The coldest month, June, averages {{convert|18.5|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, with maximum and minimum averages of {{convert|26.2|°C|°F|abbr=on}} and {{convert|10.8|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, respectively. [[Autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] are transitional seasons.<ref name="Clima"/>

The average annual precipitation is {{convert|1374.9|mm|in|abbr=on}}, with August being the driest month, receiving only {{convert|10.4|mm|in|abbr=on}}. December, the wettest month, averages {{convert|293.8|mm|in|abbr=on}}.<ref name="Clima"/> In recent years, hot and dry days during winter have become increasingly common, often exceeding {{convert|30|°C|°F|abbr=on}}, particularly between July and September. For example, in August 2013, rainfall in the Pará de Minas region was {{convert|0|mm|in|abbr=on}}.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://graficos_snirh.ana.gov.br/Aplicativos/grafico/graficoChuva.php?tipo=Chuvas&estacao=1944082&ano=2013&mes=8&xtela=1024&ytela=738 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140502224534/http://graficos_snirh.ana.gov.br/Aplicativos/grafico/graficoChuva.php?tipo=Chuvas&estacao=1944082&ano=2013&mes=8&xtela=1024&ytela=738 |archive-date=2 May 2014 |title=Rainfall - Daily Averages – 08/2013 |author=Mineral Resources Research Company (CPRM) |publisher=National Water and Basic Sanitation Agency (ANA) |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> During the dry season and prolonged [[Indian summer]]s in the rainy season, [[wildfires]] in hills and thickets are common, especially in rural areas, contributing to [[deforestation]] and the release of pollutants into the [[atmosphere]], further degrading [[air quality]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.santacruzam.com/parademinas/noticia/3869/3756 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMyarp7J?url=http://www.santacruzam.com/parademinas/noticia/3869/3756 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Firefighters issue alert about wildfire season in Pará de Minas |author=Rádio Santa Cruz |date=31 July 2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.radiostilo.com/noticias/regiao/queimadas-fora-de-epoca-ja-preocupam-os-bombeiros/pag/108 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMydZ3ZM?url=http://www.radiostilo.com/noticias/regiao/queimadas-fora-de-epoca-ja-preocupam-os-bombeiros/pag/108 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Off-season wildfires already concern firefighters |author=Rádio Stilo |date=24 February 2014 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

According to the Mineral Resources Research Company (CPRM), between 1941 and 1963, the highest daily rainfall recorded in Pará de Minas was {{convert|130.4|mm|in|abbr=on}}, observed on 29 February 1960.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://graficos_snirh.ana.gov.br/Aplicativos/grafico/graficoChuva.php?tipo=Chuvas&estacao=1944007&ano=1960&mes=2&xtela=1024&ytela=738 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140502223110/http://graficos_snirh.ana.gov.br/Aplicativos/grafico/graficoChuva.php?tipo=Chuvas&estacao=1944007&ano=1960&mes=2&xtela=1024&ytela=738 |archive-date=2 May 2014 |title=Rainfall - ===Ecology and environment===
[[File:Parque Bariri 2012.jpg|thumb|left|Bariri Park, one of the city's main green spaces]]
The native [[vegetation]] belongs to the [[Atlantic Forest]] domain in transition with the [[Cerrado]]. In 2011, remaining Atlantic Forest reserves covered {{convert|3662|ha|acre|abbr=on}}, or 9.5% of the total municipal area,<ref name="SOSMata">{{Cite web |url=http://www.sosma.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Atlas_municipio_completo2012.pdf |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6OQQVhqSR?url=http://www.sosma.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Atlas_municipio_completo2012.pdf |archive-date=29 March 2014 |title=Atlas of Atlantic Forest Remnants |author=[[SOS Mata Atlântica Foundation]] |page=10 |date=20 August 2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> and in 2009, the Cerrado covered {{convert|6879.13|ha|acre|abbr=on}} (12.52% of the municipal area), reforestation with [[eucalyptus]] plantations occupied {{convert|262.63|ha|acre|abbr=on}} (0.48%), fields covered {{convert|312.89|ha|acre|abbr=on}} (0.57%), watercourses covered {{convert|162.99|ha|acre|abbr=on}} (0.3%), and urbanized areas spanned {{convert|2197.69|ha|acre|abbr=on}} (4.0%).<ref name="FlorestasMG">{{Cite web |url=http://geosisemanet.meioambiente.mg.gov.br/inventario_bi/Interface/InventarioFlorestal.aspx?aplicacao=InventarioFlorestal.xml&objeto=municipio&id_marcador=15833&id=3169406&id_camada_referencia=4&tabela=public.municipio# |title=Minas Gerais Forest Inventory - Municipal Query |author=Minas Gerais Forest Inventory |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> Regarding land use, approximately {{convert|4354|ha|acre|abbr=on}} of Pará de Minas are used for crops, and another {{convert|366|ha|acre|abbr=on}} are used for pastures, primarily for livestock.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=3&codmun=314710 |title=2006 Agricultural Census |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=2006 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

[[Wildfire]]s are a recurring issue throughout the municipality, which occur mainly during dry months when vegetation is more susceptible to fire, affecting air quality already compromised by industrial pollution in the region,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.santacruzam.com/acontece/noticia/12907 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PN23cdGx?url=http://www.santacruzam.com/acontece/noticia/12907 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Alterosa Steelworks Pollution |author=Rádio Santa Cruz |date=5 August 2013 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> and soil quality.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.santacruzam.com/acontece/noticia/12904 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PN26qBYz?url=http://www.santacruzam.com/acontece/noticia/12904 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Fire on Cristo Ridge devastates large vegetation area |author=Rádio Santa Cruz |date=5 August 2013 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> During the rainy season, flooding causes significant damage in lower, densely populated areas, and landslides affect residences. These issues are often due to constructions on hillsides and high-risk areas, as well as litter and sewage dumped into streams and rivers,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.totalfm.com.br/index.php?Conteudo=noticias&submod=para_minas&codigo=15018 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PN2Axx7E?url=http://www.totalfm.com.br/index.php?Conteudo=noticias&submod=para_minas&codigo=15018 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Pará de Minas Civil Defense takes action to minimize rain damage |author=Rádio Total |date=2 February 2014 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/app/noticia/brasil/2011/03/08/interna_brasil,241654/chuva-deixa-estragos-em-divinopolis-e-para-de-minas-gerais.shtml |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PN2NIawy?url=http://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/app/noticia/brasil/2011/03/08/interna_brasil,241654/chuva-deixa-estragos-em-divinopolis-e-para-de-minas-gerais.shtml |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Rain causes damage in Divinópolis and Pará de Minas Gerais |author=Luana Cruz and Juliana Zoet |date=8 March 2011 |publisher=Correio Braziliense |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> with the city being one of the largest contributors to pollution in the [[Pará River (Minas Gerais)|Pará River]] basin, according to the Minas Gerais Institute for Water Management (IGAM).<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.abes-mg.org.br/visualizacao-das-noticias/pt-br/ler/4713/igam-detalha-qualidade-das-aguas-em-minas |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PN2RhvZg?url=http://www.abes-mg.org.br/visualizacao-das-noticias/pt-br/ler/4713/igam-detalha-qualidade-das-aguas-em-minas |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=IGAM details water quality in Minas |author=Brazilian Association of Sanitary and Environmental Engineering (ABES) |date=14 February 2014 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

===Poverty, development, and inequality===
From 2000 to 2010, the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to half the minimum wage decreased by 70.6%. In 2010, 96% of the population lived above the [[poverty line]], 3.3% were at the poverty line, and 0.7% were below it.<ref name="ODM_1">{{Cite web |url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031551/para-de-minas---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505135551/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031551/para-de-minas---mg |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=1 - End hunger and poverty |author=Portal ODM |date=2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[social inequality]], was 0.435, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{Cite web |url=http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA003031551/para-de-minas---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505135727/http://www.portalodm.com.br/relatorios/perfil/BRA003031551/para-de-minas---mg |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=Municipal profile |author=Portal ODM |date=2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> The wealthiest 20% of the population accounted for 49.5% of the city's total income, 8.7 times higher than the 5.7% share of the poorest 20%.<ref name="ODM_1"/> According to the IBGE, in 2010, the only [[Favela|subnormal agglomeration]] in Pará de Minas was Vila Nossa Senhora Aparecida, also known as Morro Santa Cruz, located at the city's entrance along [[BR-262 (Brazil highway)|BR-262]], with 638 residents in 174 permanent private households.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.gazetaparaminense.com.br/colunistas/colunas/1765/156 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMyi4dQb?url=http://www.gazetaparaminense.com.br/colunistas/colunas/1765/156 |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Writer donates books |author=Gazeta Paraminense |date=13 May 2011 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Mapa">{{Cite web |url=http://www.censo2010.ibge.gov.br/agsn/ |title=Subnormal agglomerations - Resident population |author=[[Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics]] (IBGE) |date=21 December 2011 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

The [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) of Pará de Minas is considered high by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (UNDP), with a value of 0.725 (the 1154th highest in Brazil). The city has most indicators at or above the national average, according to the UNDP. The education index is 0.628, the longevity index is 0.848, and the income index is 0.715.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/> The city ranks 45th among the most developed municipalities in Brazil, according to the [[Fundação Getulio Vargas|Getulio Vargas Foundation]] (FGV) in 2012, considering education, health, income, and safety.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://exame.abril.com.br/brasil/noticias/lista-definitiva-das-500-cidades-mais-desenvolvidas-do-pais?page=1 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6QBDsWZ4q?url=http://exame.abril.com.br/brasil/noticias/lista-definitiva-das-500-cidades-mais-desenvolvidas-do-pais?page=1 |archive-date=8 June 2014 |title=Definitive list of the 500 most developed cities in the country |author=Marco Prates |date=8 June 2014 |publisher=Exame.com |access-date=8 June 2014}}</ref>

===Religion===
[[File:Igreja Nossa Senhora da Piedade - Pará de Minas, MG.jpg|thumb|left|upright|Façade of the Our Lady of Mercy Mother Church]]
The majority of Pará de Minas residents identify as Catholic, though the city is home to dozens of different Protestant denominations, as well as practitioners of [[Buddhism]], [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritism]], and other faiths. There are also notable Jewish, Mormon, and Afro-Brazilian religious communities. According to the 2010 IBGE census, the population of Pará de Minas comprises {{formatnum:70526}} [[Catholicism|Catholics]] (83.74%), {{formatnum:9004}} [[Protestantism|evangelicals]] (10.69%), {{formatnum:1759}} [[Irreligion|people with no religion]] (2.09%), 757 [[Kardecist spiritism|Spiritists]] (0.9%), 47 [[Buddhism|Buddhists]] (0.06%), and the remaining 2.52% are divided among other religions.<ref name="Religião">{{Cite web |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=14&i=P&c=2094 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505143305/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=2094&i=P&nome=on&qtu8=137&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=2094&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&poc133=2&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&nivt=0&orc86=3&orp=5&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&poc86=1&opc133=2&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&orc133=4&qtu2=5&qtu15=3&sev=93&opc86=1&sec133=0&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&opn13=0&sec86=0&ascendente=on&sep=38559&orn=1&qtu7=36&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Par%C3%A1+de+Minas&OpcCara=44&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&opn7=0&decm=99 |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=Table 2094 - Resident population by color or race and religion |author=IBGE Automatic Recovery System (SIDRA) |date=2010 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

====Roman Catholic Church====
[[Catholicism]] has been present in Pará de Minas since the first settlers arrived, with the construction of the Our Lady of Mercy Chapel in the early 18th century, ordered by Manuel Batista, also known as Pato Fofo, marking the establishment of the settlement that was formalized as a district with the creation of the [[parish]] on 8 April 1846.<ref name="IBGE_Histórico"/><ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> According to the current division by the [[Catholic Church]], the municipality is part of the [[Roman Catholic Diocese of Divinópolis|Diocese of Divinópolis]], created on 11 July 1958,<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=199 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505151548/http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=199 |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=History of the Diocese |author=[[Roman Catholic Diocese of Divinópolis|Diocese of Divinópolis]] |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> which, as of May 2014, included 53 parishes across 25 cities, six of which were in Pará de Minas: Our Lady of Mercy, Our Lady Help of Christians, Saint Anthony, Saint Francis of Assisi, Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, and Saint Peter.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=508 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505151611/http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=508 |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=Parishes of the Diocese of Divinópolis |author=[[Roman Catholic Diocese of Divinópolis|Diocese of Divinópolis]] |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

The parishes of Pará de Minas are under the [[Deanery]] of Our Lady of Mercy, which also includes the municipalities of [[Florestal]], [[Igaratinga]], and [[São José da Varginha]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=504 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505151642/http://www.diocesedivinopolis.org.br/index.asp?c=padrao&modulo=conteudo&url=504 |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=Deanery of Our Lady of Mercy |author=[[Roman Catholic Diocese of Divinópolis|Diocese of Divinópolis]] |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> The Our Lady of Mercy Chapel was replaced by the Our Lady of Mercy Mother Church in 1901, which was demolished 20 years later and replaced by a new temple in 1972. Since then, the current Our Lady of Mercy Mother Church has been one of the city's main architectural landmarks.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/>

====Protestant Churches====
The city is home to a variety of Protestant or Reformed denominations, including the [[Christian Congregation in Brazil]], Maranatha Christian Church, [[Foursquare Church]], [[Presbyterian Church]], [[Methodist Church]], [[Anglican Episcopal Church]], [[Baptist Church|Baptist churches]], [[Assembleias de Deus|Assemblies of God]], [[Seventh-day Adventist Church]], Blessing Cathedral, [[Universal Church of the Kingdom of God]], [[International Grace of God Church]], [[God Is Love Pentecostal Church]], [[Brazil for Christ Pentecostal Church]], among others.<ref name="Religião"/> Among Protestant groups, 5.61% of the population follows Pentecostal evangelical churches, 1.76% mission-based evangelical churches, and 3.33% non-specific evangelical churches.<ref name="Religião"/>

There are also Christians from other Reformed denominations, such as [[Jehovah's Witnesses]] (representing 1.20% of residents) and members of [[the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints]] (0.02% of the population).<ref name="Religião"/>

===Ethnicity and migration===
The first settlers in what was then Arraial do Patafofo, during the 18th century, were mostly ''[[bandeirantes]]'' from the state of [[São Paulo (state)|São Paulo]].<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> However, the customs and social organization at the time reflected African and Portuguese influences from the [[Colonial Brazil|colonial period]]. For instance, women were typically dedicated to household tasks and, later, small commercial activities, while men handled agricultural work.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> The city saw a significant influx of immigrants during the 20th century, drawn by its development, including Chinese, Japanese, American, German, Portuguese, Spanish, and Argentine communities.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.jcnoticias.com.br/index.php?Conteudo=detalhes&Tipo=dodia&Codigo=24631 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140505154055/http://www.jcnoticias.com.br/index.php?Conteudo=detalhes&Tipo=dodia&Codigo=24631 |archive-date=5 May 2014 |title=JC Notícias tells the story of a successful Chinese family |author=JC Notícias |date=21 June 2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref>

==Politics and administration==
[[File:Prefeitura Municipal de Pará de Minas, MG.jpg|thumb|right|Pará de Minas City Hall]]
Municipal administration is carried out by the [[Executive power|Executive]] and [[Legislative power|Legislative]] branches. The Executive is led by the mayor, supported by a cabinet of secretaries. The current mayor is Inácio Franco of the [[Liberal Party (Brazil, 2006)|Liberal Party]] (PL), elected in the 2024 municipal elections with 60.05% of valid votes and sworn in on 1 January 2025, alongside Luiz Lima as [[vice-mayor]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.eleicoes2016.com.br/dr-walter-pp/ |title=Elias Diniz 55 |author=Elections 2016 |date=2 October 2016 |access-date=18 April 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170418220212/https://www.eleicoes2016.com.br/dr-walter-pp/ |archive-date=18 April 2017}}</ref> The Legislative branch consists of the [[Câmara municipal|municipal chamber]], composed of 17 councilors.<ref name="vereadores">{{Cite web |url=http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-para-de-minas-mg/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PMv4FKxO?url=http://www.eleicoes2012.info/candidatos-vereador-para-de-minas-mg/ |archive-date=6 May 2014 |title=Candidates for Councilor Pará de Minas/MG |author=Elections 2012 |date=7 October 2012 |access-date=6 May 2014}}</ref> The current President of the Chamber of Councilors is Délio Alves. ==Economy==
In the [[Gross Domestic Product]] (GDP) of Pará de Minas, agriculture, linked to agribusiness, and the [[tertiary sector|service sector]] stand out. According to 2021 IBGE data, the municipal GDP was {{BRL|4025297487}}, ranking as the 31st largest GDP in the state.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In 2021, {{BRL|637159835}} were from taxes on products net of subsidies at current prices, and the per capita GDP was {{BRL|42098.58}}.<ref name="IBGE_PIB">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=125&codmun=314710 |title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2011 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> In 2010, 71.91% of the population over 18 was [[Workforce|economically active]], with an unemployment rate of 4.49%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

Wages and other remunerations totaled {{BRL|311687000}}, with an average monthly wage of 2.0 [[minimum wages]]. There were {{formatnum:3047}} local units and {{formatnum:2939}} active companies.<ref name="IBGE_Empresas">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=115&codmun=314710 |title=Statistics of the Central Business Register |author=Cities@ - IBGE |date=2011 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> According to the IBGE, 55.49% of households survived on less than one minimum wage per month per resident ({{formatnum:14138}} households), 35.90% had one to three minimum wages per person ({{formatnum:9141}} households), 4.48% earned three to five wages ({{formatnum:1142}} households), 2.83% had incomes above five minimum wages (720 households), and 1.31% had no income (334 households).<ref name="SIDRA_Rendimento">{{Cite web |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=4&i=P&c=3261 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140507133833/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3261&i=P&opc386=2&nome=on&qtu8=137&digt102=&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=3261&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&qtu11=658&nivt=0&orp=4&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&orc386=3&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&qtu2=5&opn10=0&qtu15=3&sev=96&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&qtu102=14208&opn13=0&poc386=2&sep=26272&orn=1&digt10=&opn11=0&qtu7=36&sec386=9680&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Par%C3%A1+de+Minas&opn102=0&OpcCara=44&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&qtu10=10282&digt11=&opn7=0&decm=99 |archive-date=7 May 2014 |title=Permanent private households by nominal monthly household income per capita classes |author=IBGE Automatic Recovery System (SIDRA) |date=2010 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref>

===Primary sector===
[[File:Avenida Ovídio de Abreu - Pará de Minas, MG.JPG|thumb|left|Ovídio de Abreu Avenue and the Guabi complex (an agribusiness company), with the city in the background.]]
In 2021, the gross value added from agriculture and livestock to the city's GDP was R$128,982,280.<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> In 2010, 8.08% of the municipality's economically active population was employed in this sector.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), in 2012, the municipality had a livestock inventory consisting of 44,496 [[cattle]], 605 [[Bubalus|buffalo]], 14 [[donkey]]s, 134 [[goat]]s, 1,386 [[horse]]s, 117 [[mule]]s, 242 [[sheep]], 123,958 [[pig]]s, and 12,740,806 [[poultry]], including 220,136 [[Chicken|hens]] and 12,520,670 [[rooster]]s, broilers, and chicks.<ref name="Agropecuária" /> In the same year, the city produced 19,189,000 litres of milk from 10,149 [[cow]]s and 2,750,000 dozen eggs from hens.<ref name="Agropecuária">{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=121&codmun=314710 |title=Pecuária 2012 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2012 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> Pará de Minas is recognized as a regional hub for agriculture, [[pig farming]], and [[poultry farming]]. Occasionally, the Francisco Olivé Diniz Exhibition Park hosts local and statewide events to promote agricultural production, with the largest being the Fest Frango (State Chicken and Pig Fair).<ref name="Prefeitura_Agricultura">{{cite web |url=http://www.parademinas.mg.gov.br/agricultura-em-pauta/ |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6POYlTIje?url=http://www.parademinas.mg.gov.br/agricultura-em-pauta/ |archive-date=7 May 2014 |title=Agricultura em pauta |author=Prefeitura | work=Prefeitura Municipal de Pará de Minas |date=18 September 2013 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> Livestock farming has been a cornerstone of the local economy since the 19th century and gained prominence in the 20th century with the establishment of the first agro-industries and cooperatives. Poultry farming was introduced in the 1970s, followed by pig farming in the 1990s, both of which saw significant growth at the start of the 2000s.<ref name="Museu_Economia">{{cite web |url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=215:o-agronegocio-em-para-de-minas&catid=36:textos&Itemid=89 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6POYp1VGY?url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=215:o-agronegocio-em-para-de-minas&catid=36:textos&Itemid=89 |archive-date=7 May 2014 |title=O agronegócio em Pará de Minas |author=Luiz Viana David |date=26 March 2014 |publisher=Museu Histórico de Pará de Minas |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref>

In temporary crop production, the main products are [[sugarcane]] (6,400 tonnes produced across 80 hectares), [[tomato]]es (3,290 tonnes across 47 hectares), and [[cassava]] (2,175 tonnes across 145 hectares), in addition to [[garlic]], [[peanut]]s, [[rice]], [[sweet potato]], [[bean]]s, and [[maize]].<ref name="IBGE_Lavoura">{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=123&codmun=314710 |title=Lavoura Temporária 2012 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2012 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> In permanent crop production, notable outputs include [[banana]]s (360 tonnes across 24 hectares), [[Orange (fruit)|oranges]] (187 tonnes across 45 hectares), and [[latex]] (160 tonnes across 80 hectares), alongside smaller-scale production of [[coffee]] and [[Passion fruit (fruit)|passion fruit]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=122&codmun=314710 |title=Lavoura Permanente 2012 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2012 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> Coffee cultivation was introduced in the 1970s by Frenchman George Collin, who achieved successive harvests, though much of his land was later converted into a rubber plantation. In the 1910s, there was an attempt to establish Pará de Minas as a major latex producer, but the endeavor was largely unsuccessful. Sugarcane, maize, and rice have been cultivated since the 19th century, primarily for subsistence farming. However, [[cotton]] was the first crop grown on a large scale to meet the demands of the textile industry. The decline of textile factories, replaced by agro-industries, and the rise of polyester production from [[petroleum]] led to the end of the cotton cycle in the city.<ref name="Museu_Economia"/>

===Secondary sector===
In 2021, R$1,134,222,022 of the municipal GDP came from the gross value added by industry (secondary sector).<ref name="IBGE_PIB"/> Much of the city's industrial output is tied to agribusiness and agro-industries, a sector present since the early 20th century, initially linked to livestock farming and later bolstered by the introduction of pig and poultry farming.<ref name="Museu_Economia"/> The textile industry was the first to gain prominence in Pará de Minas, emerging in the 1900s and serving as the municipality's primary source of income until agro-industries became dominant. In the 1950s, industries producing [[pig iron]], foundry products, and ceramics were introduced,<ref name="Museu_Economia"/> reaching their peak in the 1980s.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/> Notably, Alterosa Steel Mill, one of Brazil's largest pig iron producers, operates two industrial units in the municipality.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.grupoalterosa.ind.br/alterosa.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6POYqFmHh?url=http://www.grupoalterosa.ind.br/alterosa.php |archive-date=7 May 2014 |title=Institucional |author=Grupo Alterosa |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> The Western Minas Farmers' Cooperative (COGRAN), established in 1980, had 240 members by March 2014, producing an average of about 3 million poultry birds per month in Pará de Minas alone, and 12 million when including neighboring municipalities.<ref name="Museu_Economia"/>

A significant portion of Pará de Minas' secondary sector output originates from its two [[industrial district]]s. The first spans 247,379.00 m<sup>2</sup> and is divided into 70 plots, hosting approximately 45 operational companies. The second, covering 62,657.00 m<sup>2</sup> and subdivided into 12 plots, is located along the [[BR-262 (Brazil highway)|BR-262]] highway and includes a complex belonging to Itambé Laticínios.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.unincor.br/unidade-para-minas |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6POYsZIi8?url=http://www.unincor.br/unidade-para-minas |archive-date=7 May 2014 |title=Pará de Minas |author=Prefeitura |publisher=Universidade Vale do Rio Verde (UNINCOR) |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> In 2012, according to IBGE, 8,517 m<sup>3</sup> of timber was extracted for firewood.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=124&codmun=314710 |title=Extração vegetal e silvicultura - 2012 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2012 |access-date=7 May 2014}}</ref> In 2010, statistics showed that 0.47% of Pará de Minas' workforce was employed in the extractive industry, while 20.43% worked in manufacturing.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

[[File:Rua Benedito Valadares - Pará de Minas, MG.JPG|thumb|right|Commercial establishments and a [[Caixa Econômica Federal|Caixa]] branch on Benedito Valadares Street]]

===Housing and services===
[[File:Pará de Minas adormecida - panoramio.jpg|thumb|left|Partial nighttime view of the city, with electricity supplied by [[CEMIG|Cemig]].]]
In 2010, Pará de Minas had 25,480 households, including 24,214 houses, 48 houses in villages or condominiums, 1,187 apartments, and 31 tenement or slum dwellings. Of the total households, 19,137 were owned, with 17,193 fully paid and 1,944 under acquisition; 4,746 were [[rented]]; 1,542 were loaned, with 563 provided by employers and 979 through other means; and 55 were occupied in other ways.<ref name="IBGE_Domicílios">{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=67&codmun=314710 |title=Censo Demográfico 2010: Características da População e dos Domicílios: Resultados do Universo |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2010 |access-date=8 May 2014}}</ref> Electricity is supplied by [[CEMIG|Cemig]], which serves much of Minas Gerais. In 2003, there were 27,365 consumers, with 184,629,129 [[Watt-hour|kWh]] of energy consumed.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /> In 2010, according to IBGE, 25,459 households (99.91% of the total) had access to the electrical network.<ref name="IBGE_Domicílios"/>

Water supply and sewage collection are managed by Águas de Pará de Minas, a subsidiary of Águas do Brasil. In 2008, there were 24,968 consumer units, with an average of 15,160 m<sup>3</sup> of treated water distributed daily.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cidades.ibge.gov.br/xtras/csv.php?lang=&idtema=20&codmun=314710 |title=Pesquisa Nacional de Saneamento Básico - 2008 |author=Cidades@ - IBGE |date=2008 |access-date=8 May 2014}}</ref> The contract with the Minas Gerais Sanitation Company (Copasa),<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> the former service provider, expired in October 2009. Due to prolonged droughts, the city faced water rationing between 2013 and 2014. The concessionaire had warned of water scarcity issues since the 1990s, and a preliminary project to draw water from the [[Paraopeba River]] at Córrego do Barro was proposed, but the contract renewal was rejected by the municipal administration in December 2012. In 2014, a state of public emergency was declared due to worsening water shortages, prompting Mayor Antônio Júlio de Faria to hire Seremco, a company from [[Curitiba]], to identify the best water extraction point. On 13 May 2014, the Municipal Sanitation Plan was approved. The water shortage led to the cancellation of traditional events, such as the 2014 Chicken and Pig Festival, resulting in an estimated loss of R$2 million in revenue and 100,000 tourists.<ref>{{cite ===Transportation===
[[File:Transito na Avenida Presidente Vargas..jpg|thumb|left|upright|Traffic during peak hours on Presidente Vargas Avenue]]
The arrival of the Paracatu Railway, built in the early 20th century and later incorporated into the [[Estrada de Ferro Oeste de Minas|Oeste de Minas Railway]], marked the first connection between Pará de Minas and other regions of Minas Gerais. The city's [[railway station]] was inaugurated on 22 March 1912 and operated until the early 1980s. In 1987, the railway branch passing through the city was closed due to urban expansion, and the station building was converted into a cinema in 1998.<ref name="Prefeitura_Biscoiteiras"/><ref name="Estações_Pará"/> The decline of rail transport in Brazil resulted from the rise of highways and airports, with rail infrastructure deprioritized in favor of road construction between the 1950s and 1960s.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.brasilescola.com/brasil/industrializacao-regiao-sudeste.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PTneHvxa?url=http://www.brasilescola.com/brasil/industrializacao-regiao-sudeste.htm |archive-date=11 May 2014 |title=Industrialização da Região Sudeste |author=Eduardo de Freitas |publisher=Brasil Escola |access-date=10 May 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://360graus.terra.com.br/ecoturismo/default.asp?did=31234&action=geral |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PTnjwWGF?url=http://360graus.terra.com.br/ecoturismo/default.asp?did=31234&action=geral |archive-date=11 May 2014 |title=Estrada de ferro Leopoldina: você verá paisagens de tirar o fôlego |author=360 Graus |date=2 October 2013 |access-date=10 May 2014}}</ref>

By road, the municipality is served by federal highways [[BR-352 (Brazil highway)|BR-352]], which starts in Pará de Minas and ends in [[Goiânia]], and [[BR-262 (Brazil highway)|BR-262]], which begins in [[Vitória, Espírito Santo|Vitória]], [[Espírito Santo]], passes through cities such as [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Uberaba]], and [[Campo Grande]], and ends at the [[Bolivia]] border in [[Corumbá]], [[Mato Grosso do Sul]], serving as the main link to the state capital. ==Culture==
[[File:Cine Café - Pará de Minas, MG.jpg|thumb|right|View of Cine Café]]
The Municipal Department of Culture and Education oversees cultural activities in Pará de Minas, aiming to plan and implement cultural policies through programs, projects, and activities that promote cultural development. Subordinate to the department are operational bodies, including the Professor Melo Cançado Public Library, the Raimundo Nogueira de Faria Municipal Arts and Crafts School (Sica), the Geraldo Martins Municipal Music School, the Mário Luiz Silva Public Archive, and the Pará de Minas Historical, Documentary, Photographic, and Sound Museum (MUSPAM).<ref name="MUSPAM_Histórico">{{cite web |url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=46&Itemid=53 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PVKLBubo?url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=46&Itemid=53 |archive-date=12 May 2014 |title=Histórico |author=Museu Histórico de Pará de Minas |date=29 March 2014 |access-date=11 May 2014}}</ref><ref name="MUSPAM_Cultura">{{cite web |url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=198:luciano-pereira-assume-a-secretaria-de-cultura-de-para-de-minas&catid=35:noticias&Itemid=88 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PVKMU7W6?url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=198:luciano-pereira-assume-a-secretaria-de-cultura-de-para-de-minas&catid=35:noticias&Itemid=88 |archive-date=12 May 2014 |title=Luciano Pereira assume a Secretaria de Cultura de Pará de Minas |author=Museu Histórico de Pará de Minas |date=3 January 2013 |access-date=11 May 2014}}</ref> The Municipal Department of Sports, Leisure, and Tourism was separated from the culture department on 1 April 2013, taking responsibility for those sectors.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.gazetaparaminense.com.br/noticias/noticia/3640/252 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PXqCqFOC?url=http://www.gazetaparaminense.com.br/noticias/noticia/3640/252 |archive-date=13 May 2014 |title=Finalmente, o esporte e o turismo ganham uma secretaria |author=Gazeta Paraminense |date=26 April 2013 |access-date=12 May 2014}}</ref>

Notable artists from Pará de Minas who have achieved national or international recognition include composer, singer, and actor Benjamin de Oliveira, known as Brazil's first Black clown;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.acordacultura.org.br/herois/heroi/benjaminde-oliveira |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PPYn2NVH?url=http://www.acordacultura.org.br/herois/heroi/benjaminde-oliveira |archive-date=8 May 2014 |title=Benjamin de Oliveira (1870-1954) |author=Maurício Tizumba |date=11 September 2012 |publisher=Heróis de Todo Mundo |access-date=11 May 2014}}</ref> visual artist, illustrator, and [[stained glass]] artist Mário Silésio, who created numerous panels in public buildings in Belo Horizonte;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.itaucultural.org.br/aplicExternas/enciclopedia_IC/Enc_Artistas/artistas_imp.cfm?cd_verbete=2736&imp=N&cd_idioma=28555 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PVKm6bsY?url=http://www.itaucultural.org.br/aplicExternas/enciclopedia_IC/Enc_Artistas/artistas_imp.cfm?cd_verbete=2736&imp=N&cd_idioma=28555 |archive-date=12 May 2014 |title=Silésio, Mário (1913 - 1990) |author=Enciclopédia Itaú Cultural de Artes Visuais |access-date=11 May 2014}}</ref> and actress Cida Mendes, known for playing Concessa in the program ''A Turma do Didi''.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.concessa.com.br/cida/images/clip_tempo.gif |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PVL3sMXU?url=http://www.concessa.com.br/cida/images/clip_tempo.gif |archive-date=12 May 2014 |title=Toque feminino no humor |author=Soraya Belusi |date=7 January 2008 |publisher=O Tempo |access-date=11 May 2014 |format=GIF}}</ref>

The most successful football team in the city is Paraense Esporte Clube, founded on 15 May 1936, which has competed in several editions of the [[Campeonato Mineiro]], in both its main division and youth categories. Football has been a part of Pará de Minas since the early 20th century, when people played with makeshift balls made of socks in the streets. Later, workers responsible for the construction of the Paracatu Railway introduced leather balls for recreation. The Mello Sobrinho Foot-Ball Club, founded in 1923 and dissolved shortly after, was the city's first team, succeeded by the Americano Futebol Clube, which existed from 1924 to 1931 and paved the way for the establishment of other teams, particularly between the 1940s and 1960s. In 1976, the Pará de Minas Sports League was created to organize football championships and events in the city and region, with the Paraminense Amateur Football Championship held annually since 1977.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=category&id=36&layout=blog&Itemid=89&limitstart=2 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PagIYwN5?url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=category&id=36&layout=blog&Itemid=89&limitstart=2 |archive-date=15 May 2014 |title=Futebol em Pará de Minas |author=Luiz Viana David |date=26 March 2014 |access-date=15 May 2014}}</ref>

==Holidays==
In Pará de Minas, there are four municipal [[public holiday|holidays]], established by Law No. 1,327 of 8 February 1967, in addition to eight national holidays and optional holidays. The municipal holidays are: [[Good Friday]], which in 2025 is celebrated on April 18; [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], which is observed in the same year on June 19; the day of [[Our Lady of Sorrows]], celebrated on 15 September; and the [[Immaculate Conception|Immaculate Conception Day]], on 8 December.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ascipam.com.br/feriados.php |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PXpXR3NZ?url=http://www.ascipam.com.br/feriados.php |archive-date=13 May 2014 |title=Relação dos feriados nacionais e municipais de 2014 |author=Associação Empresarial de Pará de Minas (ASCIPAM) |access-date=15 September 2011}}</ref> According to Federal Law No. 9,093 of 12 September 1995, municipalities may have up to four religious municipal holidays, including Good Friday.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tscUhIs?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/tematicas/carnaval.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Carnaval - é ou não feriado? folga automática pode gerar alteração contratual |author=Sérgio Ferreira Pantaleão |publisher=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=15 September 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/61tsfGoVI?url=http://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm |archive-date=23 September 2011 |title=Lei Nº 9.093, de 12 de setembro de 1995 |author=Guia Trabalhista |access-date=15 September 2011}}</ref>

The city's anniversary, celebrated on 20 September, refers to the date of the first establishment of the then Vila do Pará in 1859, as the municipality was abolished in 1872 and re-emancipated in 1874, with a second (definitive) establishment on 25 March 1876.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=68:25-de-marco-ou-20-de-setembro-aniversario-de-para-de-minas&catid=36:textos&Itemid=89 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PXpZzJkF?url=http://www.muspam.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=68:25-de-marco-ou-20-de-setembro-aniversario-de-para-de-minas&catid=36:textos&Itemid=89 |archive-date=13 May 2014 |title=25 de março ou 20 de setembro, aniversário de Pará de Minas? |author=Ana Maria Campos |date=25 March 2009 |publisher=Museu Histórico de Pará de Minas |access-date=13 May 2014}}</ref> There is no municipal holiday to commemorate the city's anniversary, though there has been discussion about designating 20 September as one, which would require the elimination of another existing holiday.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radiostilo.com/noticias/regiao/-o-feriado-do-aniversario-de-para-de-minas-podera-voltar-em-2014/pag/621 |archive-url=https://www.webcitation.org/6PXpb9Gyt?url=http://www.radiostilo.com/noticias/regiao/-o-feriado-do-aniversario-de-para-de-minas-podera-voltar-em-2014/pag/621 |archive-date=13 May 2014 |title=O feriado do aniversário de Pará de Minas poderá voltar em 2014 |author=Rádio Stilo |date=9 September 2013 |access-date=13 May 2014}}</ref>
