'''Nunchía''' is a town and municipality in the [[Departments of Colombia|Department]] of [[Casanare Department|Casanare]], [[Colombia]]. Its economy is primarily based on [[agriculture]] and [[livestock farming]], with the four main rivers [[Tocaría River|Tocaría]], [[Payero River|Payero]], [[Nunchía River|Nunchía]], and [[Pauto River|Pauto]] affecting the agricultural landscape. Extensive [[cattle ranching]] is the dominant economic activity with significant crops including [[rice]], [[oil palm]], [[coffee]], and [[cocoa bean]]s. [[Poultry]], [[pig farming]], and [[fish farming]] are also present but on a smaller scale.

==Demographics==
As of the [[2018 Colombian census]], the municipality of Nunchía had a total population of 8,826, with a modest [[population density]] of 8.01 inhabitants per square kilometer, spread across an area of 1,102 km<sup>2</sup>. The municipality has experienced a gradual population increase, with an [[annual growth rate]] of 0.36% from 2005 to 2018.<ref name="MunicipalityCityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/colombia/casanare/85225__nunch%C3%ADa/|title=Nunchía Municipality in Casanare|website=citypopulation.de|date=6 September 2020|access-date=17 June 2025}}</ref>

The town of Nunchía, which is the municipal capital, had a population of 1,904 in 2018.<ref name="CapitalCityPopulation.de">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/colombia/casanare/nunch%C3%ADa/85225000__nunch%C3%ADa/|title=Nunchía in Nunchía (Casanare)|website=citypopulation.de|date=6 September 2020|access-date=17 June 2025}}</ref> It has a relatively high population density of 2,037 people per square kilometer, with 51.7% of the population being male (984 individuals) and 48.3% female (920 individuals).<ref name="CapitalCityPopulation.de"/> The town is one of the two main urban centers in the municipality, the other being La Yopalosa, which had a population of 474 in 2018.<ref name="MunicipalityCityPopulation.de"/>

In terms of age structure, the population of Nunchía the capital was predominantly composed of [[working-age]] individuals. Of the total population in the urban area, 67.1% (1,200 people) were between 15 and 64 years old, which is a key demographic for economic activities. [[Children]] aged 0-14 years made up 25.2% of the population (451 people), while 7.7% (138 people) were aged 65 years or older.<ref name="CapitalCityPopulation.de"/>

==Economy==
Nunchía's economy is primarily based on [[agriculture]] and [[livestock farming]], with the latter accounting for 60% of land use. The municipality is surrounded by four main rivers: [[Tocaría River|Tocaría]], [[Payero River|Payero]], [[Nunchía River|Nunchía]], and [[Pauto River|Pauto]], which influence its agricultural landscape.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia">{{cite web|url=https://www.nunchia-casanare.gov.co/MiMunicipio/Paginas/Economia.aspx|title=Economía|trans-title=Economy|lang=es|publisher=nunchia-casanare.gov.co|access-date=17 June 2025}}</ref> While extensive [[cattle ranching]] is the dominant economic activity with approximately 65,000 head of cattle, significant crops include [[rice]] (12,524 hectares), [[oil palm]] (753 hectares), [[coffee]] (371 hectares), and [[cocoa bean]]s (39 hectares).<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/> [[Poultry]], [[pig farming]], and [[fish farming]] are also present but on a smaller, empirical scale lacking proper controls and technification.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/>

Rice is the main seasonal crop, with an average of 19,218 hectares cultivated between 2011 and 2014, yielding around 4.8 tons per hectare. However, [[climate change]] is projected to reduce rice productivity in the region.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/> Other seasonal crops like [[corn]] show low production levels and declining prices.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/> For permanent and semi-permanent crops, Nunchía cultivates [[cooking banana|plantains]], [[cassava]], [[pineapple]]s, and [[sugarcane|honey cane]], primarily for self-consumption.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/> Coffee and cocoa beans are cultivated in mountainous areas, with average annual plantings of 350 hectares for coffee and around 40 hectares for cocoa. While these crops have lower yields, their market prices have strengthened since 2013.<ref name="Nunchia-CasanareEconomia"/>
