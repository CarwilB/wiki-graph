'''Ocaña''' is a city and municipality in [[Norte de Santander Department]] of [[Colombia]]. Ocaña is the second largest populated center of this department. It played an important role during the [[Military career of Simón Bolívar|Independence of Colombia]] from the [[Spanish monarchy]].

==History==
The city was founded on 14 December 1570 by Captain [[Francisco Fernández de Contreras]], as part of the third populating project of the east, patronised by the Audiencia y Cabildo de Pamplona. Contreras chose the Hacaritama Indigenous Society in order to have more control over the indigenous population of the area and thus be able to found the city.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ramos |first=Aristides |date=2020-03-16 |title=Ocaña |url=https://www.revistacredencial.com/historia/temas/ocana |access-date=2022-07-30 |website=Revista Credencial |language=es}}</ref> The first name the city received was Santa Ana de Hacarí when it was founded in 1570. During 1575, it was assigned the status of city with its current name, Ocaña. During the time of colonization, Fernandez de Contreras stated that all merchandise coming from Spain and destined for the [[Magdalena River|Madgalena River]] should pass through the city.<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal |last=Sanchez |first=Sonia Verjel |date=2019-01-01 |title=El comercio internacional en Ocaña desde el apogeo al abandono |url=https://www.fesc.edu.co/Revistas/OJS/index.php/mundofesc/article/view/368 |journal=Mundo FESC |language=es |volume=9 |issue=17 |pages=60–68 |issn=2216-0388}}</ref> The city served as a commercial route during the Viceroyalty to connect the city of Pamplona with the Colombian Caribbean Coast. It also contributed to the development of commercial activities during the post-independence period.<ref name=":0" /> Given the geographic location of the settlement, the Audiencia de Santafé, presided over by Andrés Díaz Venero de Leiva, decided that the new town would fall under the jurisdiction of the province of Santa Maria, the governor of which was, at the time, Don Pedro Fernández del Busto. The name Ocaña was given to the new town to honour Fernández del Busto, who was born in Ocaña, Spain. The native peoples in the region were those traditionally called Hacaritamas and those called Cultura Mosquito (Mosquito Culture) or Bajomagdalenense.<!-- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitarero https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motilon https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukpa_people (has to be these, please fix)-->

In 1828, Ocaña was the site of the inconclusive [[Convention of Ocaña]] led by Vice-president [[Francisco de Paula Santander|Santander]] where delegates attempted to reform [[Gran Colombia]]'s written constitution.<ref>{{cite web |title=Ocaña |url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Ocana |website=Britannica |publisher=Encyclopaedia Britannica |access-date=1 November 2020}}</ref> In 1849, during the period of the New Granada, the Congress ordered the creation of the province of Ocaña, in order to create a Provincial Chamber to legislate the creation of schools and land roads, in addition to organizing the public force and fairs. During the second half of the 19th century, Ocaña adopts a {{Ill|Librecambismo|lt=Librecambista|es|Librecambismo}} model, which generates that the area of Ocaña begins to establish commercial routes with Europe and North America.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=2020-12-14 |title=Ocaña más de 450 años de historia |url=https://www.senalmemoria.co/oca%C3%B1a-450-fundacion-historia |access-date=2022-07-30 |website=Señal Memoria |language=es}}</ref> In 1935, the History Center of Ocaña was created, today it is the History Academy of Ocaña, in order to promote culture, in this had been members, historians, poets and influential musicians such as {{Ill|Lucio Pabón|lt=Lucio Pabón|es|Lucio Pabón}}, thirteenth governor of Norte de Santander, the writer Luis Eduardo Paez Courvel influential in the literature of the department and one of the most prominent poets of the city, Jorge Pacheco Quintero.<ref name=":1" />

==Economy==
The main economic activities are agriculture, the livestock raising, commerce, small industries and mining, with silver operation, copper and iron. The city is served by the [[Aguas Claras Airport|Aguas Claras airport]].
