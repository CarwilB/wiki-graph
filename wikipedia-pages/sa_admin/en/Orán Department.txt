'''Orán''' is a [[Departments of Argentina|department]] of the province of [[Salta Province|Salta]] ([[Argentina]]).<ref name=":cp">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/php/argentina-admin.php|title=Argentina: Administrative Division|publisher=citypopulation |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=http://microdata.worldbank.org/index.php/catalog/441/download/13645|title=CENTRO LATINOAMERICANO Y CARIBEÑO DE DEMOGRAFÍA (CELADE)|publisher=World Bank|year=1970}}</ref> It has about 124,000 residents and an area of ​​11,892 km². It is bordered by [[Santa Victoria Department|Santa Victoria]], [[Iruya Department|Iruya]] and [[San Martín Department, Mendoza|San Martin]] and [[Bolivia]], in the north, [[Rivadavia Department, Mendoza|Rivadavia]] to the east, [[Anta Department|Anta]] to the south and the [[Jujuy Province|province of Juruy]] to the south.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Cultura e Historia – Orán |url=https://oran.gob.ar/cultura-e-historia/ |access-date=2025-02-07 |publisher=Department of Orán  |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=SALTA mi querida provincia |url=http://saltamiqueridaprovincia.blogspot.com/2017/06/los-departamentos-de-salta-oran.html |access-date=2026-06-08 |language=es}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=City Population: Check for Humans |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/checkhuman.html?url=%2Fen%2Fargentina%2Fadmin%2Fsalta%2F66126__or%25C3%25A1n%2F |access-date=2026-06-08 |website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref>

==Seismicity==
The seismicity of the Salta area is frequent and of low intensity, with medium to severe earthquakes every 40 years.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.inpres.gov.ar/seismology/historicos.php | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130220160119/http://www.inpres.gov.ar/seismology/historicos.php | archive-date=20 February 2013 | title=Instituto Nacional de Prevención Sísmica }}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |url = http://www.scielo.org.ar/scielo.php?pid=S0004-48222006000400011&script=sci_arttext |title = Fenómenos de licuefacción asociados a terremotos históricos. ==Flora==
The piedmont forests constitute an altitudinal zone with a significant percentage of endemic species, representing approximately 50 % of the total. The biogeographic origin of these forests is closely linked to other seasonal forests in South America, such as the forests of the Brazilian Caatinga and the Guajira Peninsula of Venezuela and Colombia. These connections are so strong that it has been postulated that these forests once existed continuously across South America in the past, during climatic periods with rainfall intensity and distribution different from those of today. In this sense, the forests considered in this project constitute a biogeographic "relict," and this condition increases their conservation value and underscores the need for their long-term preservation.<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Morales |first1=Adriana Marisel |last2=Politi |first2=Natalia |last3=Rivera |first3=Luis Osvaldo |last4=Vivanco |first4=Constanza Guadalupe |last5=Defossé |first5=Guillermo Emilio |date=2020-06-24 |title=Fire and distance from unburned forest influence bird assemblages in Southern Andean Yungas of Northwest Argentina: a case study |journal=Fire Ecology |language=en |volume=16 |issue=1 |pages=15 |doi=10.1186/s42408-020-00074-0 |doi-access=free |bibcode=2020FiEco..16...15M |issn=1933-9747}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Prado |first=D. There are no exhaustive lists of the species diversity of these forests, but preliminary estimates indicate that they are home to at least 278 species of woody plants (trees, shrubs, and lianas). Of the trees alone, 104 species were found, 40 of which (38  %) are exclusive to the area covered by this study. This area represents the so-called "Palo Blanco and Palo Amarillo Forest" and, therefore, constitutes one of the areas with the greatest exclusive species richness in the Yungas of Argentina, making it a priority area for conservation. It is also the altitudinal zone with the highest number of valuable timber species, some of which are vulnerable or close to local extinction, such as the oak (''Amburana cearensis''), the Salta cedar
