'''San Pedro de Macorís''' is a city and [[Municipalities of the Dominican Republic|municipality]] (''[[municipio]]'') in the [[Dominican Republic]]. The capital of [[San Pedro de Macorís Province|its eponymous province]] in the east region of the country, it is among the ten largest cities of the Dominican Republic.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.one.gob.do/index.php?option=com_docman&task=cat_view&gid=113|title=Listado de Codigos de Provincias, Municipio y Distritos Municipales, Actualizada a Junio 20 del 2006|author=Oficina Nacional de Estadistica, Departamento de Cartografia, Division de Limites y Linderos|language=es|access-date=2007-01-24|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070314215735/http://www.one.gob.do/index.php?option=com_docman&task=cat_view&gid=113|archive-date=2007-03-14}}</ref> The city has approximately 217,000 inhabitants when including the whole municipality.<ref>{{Cite web |title=San Pedro de Macorís (Municipality, Dominican Republic) - Population Statistics, Charts, Map and Location |url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/domrep/admin/san_pedro_de_macor%C3%ADs/2301__san_pedro_de_macor%C3%ADs/ |access-date=2024-02-01 |website=www.citypopulation.de}}</ref> As a provincial capital, it houses the [[Universidad Central del Este]]. San Pedro de Macorís is also well known for producing professional [[baseball]] players at an exceptionally high [[per capita]] rate.

==Name==
There are four possible origins of the name San Pedro: the first is that the name was chosen in honor of [[Saint Peter|Saint Peter (San Pedro)]]<ref name=":0" />; the second is that it references San Pedro Beach near the city's port; the third is that it is a tribute to General [[Pedro Santana]], who was president at the time; and finally, it's possible that San Pedro was chosen simply to distinguish the city from [[San Francisco de Macorís]], a city in the [[Cibao|north]].

Macorís comes from the previous name of the [[Iguamo River|Higuamo river]], which appears in ancient documents as ''Macorix'' river. There is debate as to whether the name is of [[Kalinago|Carib]], [[Arawak|Arawakan]], or [[Ciguayos|Ciguayo]] origin.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Díaz Mejía |first=Ramón |date=September 10, 2025 |title=San Pedro de Macorís: 143 años de historia, cultura y aportes al país |url=https://acento.com.do/opinion/san-pedro-de-macoris-143-anos-de-historia-cultura-y-aportes-al-pais-9548403.html |website=Acento}}</ref>

San Pedro de Macorís has been poetically referred to as "Macorís of the Sea" and "the Sultana of the East", and many call it the "Capital of the East".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://dominicanrepublic.gour.net/South.526.0.html|title=San Pedro de Macoris|access-date=2007-10-13|author=gour.net|date=2006-01-12|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080517034718/http://www.dominicanrepublic.gour.net/South.526.0.html|archive-date=2008-05-17}}</ref>
==History==
{{Copy edit|section|date=June 2025}}
{{More citations needed section|date=June 2025}}
The western bank of the Higuamo river saw a visible population increase after 1822 as a result of refugees from [[Santo Domingo]] fleeing [[Haitian occupation of Santo Domingo|invading Haitian forces]]. The [[Conservative Council|Dominican Senate]] declared the region (Macorix) to be a military post in 1846 at the request of the inhabitants. At that time, Macorís was part of the [[El Seibo Province|Seybo province]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Historia: Ayuntamiento Municipal de San Pedro de Macorís |url=https://ayuntamientosanpedro.gob.do/historia/ |website=Ayuntamiento Municipal San Pedro de Macorís}}</ref>
[[File:Calle Sánchez, Centro Histórico.jpg|thumb|left|Calle Sanchez in the historic center of San Pedro de Macoris]]
After 1840, the inhabitants moved from the western side of the Higuamo river to the eastern side, where the city sits today. Over the years the population gradually increased, dedicating itself to the production of basic provisions.
[[File:Edificios en el Centro Histórico de San Pedro.jpg|thumb|right|Historic center of San Pedro de Macoris]]
On October 1, 1856, Macoris' first [[Catholic]] [[Church (building)|church]] was founded by Pedro Carrasco Capeller, a priest from [[San José de los Llanos]]. The church was subsequently destroyed and rebuilt several times. In 1910 the city saw the opening of the San Pedro Apóstol cathedral.

In 1858 the clergyman Elías González suggested changing the name ''Macorix'' to ''Macorís'' and adding the name of the [[patron saint]] San Pedro (Saint Peter) at the beginning, making the full name ''San Pedro de Macorís''. The name change was accepted and marked the beginning of the [[Patronal festival|patronal festivals]], which start every year on Jun 22 and ending on Jun 29.<ref name=":0" />

San Pedro de Macoris experienced a significant wave of migration in the late 19th century from [[Cubans]] who were fleeing their country's [[Cuba#History|War of Independence]]. They brought their extensive sugarcane farming knowledge and contributed to making the sugar industry the most important economic activity in the area. San Pedro de Macorís reached its peak during the first quarter of the 20th century, when its sugar production enjoyed high prices on the international market as a result of the [[World War I|First World War]]. Many [[Europeans]] also settled in the city, making it a very cosmopolitan urban center. 

Macorís had the privilege of being the first Dominican city to receive seaplanes. [[Pan American World Airways|Pan American Airways]] regularly flew its seaplanes into Macorís via the Higuamo River. At that time the city enjoyed more commercial activity than the Santo Domingo. The next economic boom resulted in the recruitment of a large number of [[Afro-Caribbean]] workers from the [[Lesser Antilles]]. These workers and their descendants would soon comprise the majority of the population in the city and are known as the "[[Cocolo]]s of San Pedro de Macorís."
[[File:Primer edificio de 3 niveles del país, Edificio Morey.jpg|thumb|right|Morey building]]
San Pedro de Macorís pioneered many areas, such as the first firefighting corps, the first national baseball championship, the first town to have telephone and telegraph centers, the first racetrack and the first boxing coliseum, among others.{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}} The first sugar factory was founded by Juan Amechazurra, milling for the first time on January 9, 1879. By 1894 there were many factories in the province that reached a high level of progress.{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}} The rapid industrial development placed the young city among the main ones of the Republic. The intellectual culture surged at the same pace with schools and the press; among the first newspapers were "Las Novedades", "Boletín", "La Locomotora" and "El Cable."{{Citation needed|date=January 2026}}

==Culture==
Some distinguished poets from San Pedro de Macorís include {{ill|René del Risco|es}}, [[Pedro Mir]], who held the title of National Poet; Esterbina Matos, Ludín Lugo, Juan Brayan and Mateo Robinson, among others.

Regarding drinks, Macorís produces "Guavaberry," a drink based on the [[Myrciaria floribunda|guavaberry]] fruit, which is consumed during [[Christmas]] and has a sweet taste, similar to a [[wine]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=El Arrayán o Guavaberry |url=https://mipais.jmarcano.com/recursos-naturales/alimentos/guavaberry/}}</ref> It is frequently consumed with [[rum]] as an infusion. Although the fruit is native to [[Hispaniola]], its use as a beverage was introduced and spread by immigrants from the Eastern Caribbean, where colonists had discovered it as a substitute for [[Myrtaceae|myrtleberry]]-infused schnapps.

The people of San Pedro de Macorís also enjoy gathering in the Malecon, an avenue for drinking and enjoying music.

==Sports==
===Baseball===
The city is home to the [[Estrellas Orientales]] in the [[Dominican Winter Baseball League]], who play at the [[Estadio Tetelo Vargas]].

San Pedro de Macorís is well known as the birthplace of a large number of [[professional baseball]] players.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dominicanvacation.com/san-pedro-de-macoris.html |title= San Pedro de Macoris, Dominican Republic |access-date=2007-10-13 |author=Marta Lugo  |year= 2005}}</ref> The city has been referred to as "the Cradle of [[Shortstop]]s." The Dominican capital of Santo Domingo has produced 106 [[Major League Baseball|Major League Baseball (MLB)]] players compared to San Pedro's 99, despite having a population ten times larger than that of San Pedro.

When immigrants were brought in as contract labor for sugar plantations from the Eastern Caribbean, they brought the sport of [[cricket]]. They formed teams and played against each other. The ownership and management of many of the sugar estates were American. The cricket teams were offered money by their players plantation managers to switch sports and they did. Native Dominicans picked up the game watching teams composed of Eastern Caribbeans. Over time, [[Vic Power (baseball)|Vic Power]] established a youth system in San Pedro de Macorís and was one of the largest influences on making the city one of the largest sources of baseball talent in the world.<ref>James, p. 469</ref>
