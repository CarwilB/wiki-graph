'''Trinidad García de la Cadena''' is a [[Municipalities of Zacatecas|municipality]] in the [[Mexico|Mexican]] state of [[Zacatecas]], located approximately {{convert|200|km}} southwest of [[Zacatecas City]], the state capital. It is named after General {{ill|Trinidad García de la Cadena|es}}, governor of Zacatecas from 1869 to 1870 and from 1876 to 1880.<ref>{{cite book |last=Rodríguez Valadez |first=Juan Manuel |title=Zacatecas: Historia de las instituciones jurídicas |url=https://archivos.juridicas.unam.mx/www/bjv/libros/6/2863/2.pdf |publisher=[[Senate of the Republic (Mexico)]] |location=Mexico |date=16 August 2010 |chapter-url=https://archivos.juridicas.unam.mx/www/bjv/libros/6/2863/11.pdf |chapter=Gobernantes de Zacatecas |language=es |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

==Geography==
The municipality of Trinidad García de la Cadena lies at an elevation between {{convert|900|and(-)|2200|m}} in the foothills of the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] in southwestern Zacatecas. It borders the Zacatecan municipalities of [[Teúl de González Ortega Municipality|Teúl de González Ortega]] to the north, and [[Mezquital del Oro]] to the east, and the Jaliscan municipalities of [[San Cristóbal de la Barranca]] to the south and [[Tequila, Jalisco|Tequila]] to the west.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www3.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/app/mexicocifras/datos_geograficos/32/32011.pdf |title=Trinidad García de la Cadena, Zacatecas |work=Prontuario de información geográfica municipal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos |language=es |publisher=INEGI |year=2009 |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref> The municipality covers an area of {{convert|307.1|km2}} and comprises 0.4% of the state's area.<ref name=panorama/>

As of 2009, 17.5% of the land in Trinidad García de la Cadena is used for agriculture. The remainder of the land cover comprises temperate forest (43.2%), tropical forest (22.1), and grassland (16.6%), and urban areas (0.6%). The municipality is situated in the drainage basin of the [[Cuixtla River|Cuixtla]] and [[Juchipila River]]s, both tributaries of the [[Río Grande de Santiago]].<ref name=compendium/>

===Climate===
Trinidad García de la Cadena's climate ranges from temperate to tropical depending on elevation, with dry winters. Average temperatures in the municipality range between {{convert|16|and(-)|24|C}}, and average annual precipitation ranges between {{convert|800|and(-)|1000|mm}}.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
Trinidad García de la Cadena is located in territory that was inhabited by the [[Caxcan]] people prior to Spanish contact.<ref name=encyclopedia>{{cite web |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM32zacatecas/municipios/3201a.html |title=Trinidad García de la Cadena |language=es |website=Enciclopedia de los Municipios y Delegaciones de México |publisher=[[INAFED]] |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref> It was formerly a ''[[hacienda]]'' named La Estanzuela, whose existence dates at least to 1793. After Mexican independence, it became part of the ''partido'' of Tlaltenango. The ''hacienda'' was expropriated by the state in 1869, and after a further legal battle, the sale of its land to the citizens of La Estanzuela was finalized on 27 July 1883.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Núñez de la Peña |first=Francisco J. |date=1983 |title=La llamamos La Estanzuela: una microhistoria |journal=Relaciones Estudios de Historia y Sociedad |language=es |url=https://www.colmich.edu.mx/relaciones25/files/revistas/016/FranciscoJ.NunezDeLaPina.pdf |volume=4 |issue=16 |pages=72–92 |publisher={{ill|El Colegio de Michoacán|es}} |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

When the ''partidos'' of Zacatecas were abolished in 1916, Estanzuela became an independent municipality. On 4 May 1935, it changed its name to García de la Cadena, in honour of the former governor of Zacatecas. It acquired its present name on 15 November 1986.<ref name=division>{{cite book |date=1996 |title=Estado de Zacatecas. División Territorial de 1810 a 1995. |url=http://internet.contenidos.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/integracion/pais/divi_terri/1810-1985/zac/ZACATECAS.pdf |language=es |location=Mexico |publisher=[[INEGI]] |isbn=970-13-1519-7 |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

==Administration==
The municipal government of Trinidad García de la Cadena comprises a president, a councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico''),<ref name=encyclopedia/> and seven trustees (''regidores''), four elected by [[relative majority]] and three by [[proportional representation]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://igualdad.ine.mx/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Lineamientos-de-Paridad-Zacatecas.pdf |title=Lineamientos para el registro de candidaturas a cargos de elección popular de los partidos políticos y coaliciones |language=es |pages=12–13 |date=27 November 2017 |publisher=Instituto Electoral del Estado de Zacatecas |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref> The current president of the municipality is Arcelia Muro Guzmán.<ref>{{cite web |last=Alvarado |first=Silvia |date=8 June 2021 |url=http://portico.mx/2021/06/08/quienes-ganaron-en-los-municipios-de-zacatecas |title=Va por Zacatecas gana más municipios |language=es |website=Pórtico |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2020 Mexican Census, Trinidad García de la Cadena recorded a population of 3362 inhabitants living in 1080 households.<ref name=panorama>{{cite book |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/702825198053.pdf |title=Panorama sociodemográfico de Zacatecas. Censo de Población y Vivienda 2020 |pages=70–71 |language=es |publisher=INEGI |date=2021 |accessdate=2 January 2022}}</ref> The 2010 Census recorded a population of 3013 inhabitants in Trinidad García de la Cadena.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=32&mun=011 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Trinidad García de la Cadena |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[SEDESOL]] |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

There are 35 inhabited localities in the municipality,<ref name=panorama/> of which only the municipal seat, also called Trinidad García de la Cadena, is classified as urban.<ref name=catalog/> It recorded a population of 2721 inhabitants in the 2020 Census.<ref name=panorama/>

According to the 2020 Census, 4% of the population in Trinidad García de la Cadena speaks the [[Huichol language]].<ref name=panorama/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
The main economic activity in Trinidad García de la Cadena is agriculture.<ref name=encyclopedia/> In the 2015 Intercensal Survey, 36% of the municipality's workforce was employed in the primary sector, 22% in the secondary sector, 10% in commerce, and 31% in services.<ref>{{cite book |title=Cuadro 10.5: Población ocupada por municipio y su distribución porcentual según sector de actividad económica, al 15 de marzo de 2015 |work=Anuario estadístico y geográfico de Zacatecas 2017 |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/productos/prod_serv/contenidos/espanol/bvinegi/productos/nueva_estruc/anuarios_2017/702825092047.pdf |language=Spanish |location=Mexico |publisher=INEGI |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref> The main crops grown in Trinidad García de la Cadena are corn and agave.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://coepla.zacatecas.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Trinidad-García-de-la-Cadena.docx |title=Información estadística básica. Municipio: 011 Trinidad García de la Cadena |language=es |publisher=Coordinación Estatal de Planeación |date=17 June 2021 |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref> Crops more recently introduced to the area include [[chia seed]] and [[Persian lime]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Rodríguez Robles |first=Itzel Montserrat |url=https://www.trinidadgarciadelacadena.com.mx/t-garcia-de-la-cadena/agricultura/ |title=Agricultura |language=es |publisher=Municipality of Trinidad García de la Cadena |access-date=2 January 2022}}</ref>

[[Mexican Federal Highway 23|Federal Highway 23]] runs north to south through the municipality, connecting it to [[Jerez de García Salinas|Jerez]] and [[Fresnillo]] in the north, and [[Zapopan]] and [[Guadalajara]] in the south.
