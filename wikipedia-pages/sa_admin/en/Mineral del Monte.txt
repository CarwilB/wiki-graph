'''Mineral del Monte''', commonly called '''Real del Monte''' ({{IPA|es|reˈal del ˈmonte|lang|RealdelMontePronunciation.ogg}}) or '''El Real''', or "Moon an Menydh" in [[Cornish language|Cornish]] is a small [[mining town]], and one of the 84 [[municipalities of Hidalgo]], in the [[Hidalgo (state)|State of Hidalgo]] in east-central [[Mexico]].

It is located at an [[altitude]] of {{convert|2700|m|ft}} on a [[mountain pass]], it is the highest town in Hidalgo. As of 2020, the municipality had a total population of 14,324 — with Mauricio Rodriguez Téllez as head of the municipal council.

== Etymology ==
Mineral Del Monte was originally known as "Magotsi" by native [[Otomi]] [[Indigenous peoples of Mexico|amerindians]]. The name is a compound noun of "Ma" and "Gohtsi", roughly translating to "high pass"; e.g. the site of the [[mountain pass]] between [[Huasteca]] (to the east) and territories of the Otomi state of [[Metztitlán|Metztitlan]] (to the north), and city of [[Tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlán]] (Mexico City) in the west.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2015-09-24 |title=Hidalgo - Mineral del Monte |url=http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM13hidalgo/municipios/13039a.html |access-date=2024-08-31 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924034322/http://www.inafed.gob.mx/work/enciclopedia/EMM13hidalgo/municipios/13039a.html |archive-date=24 September 2015 }}</ref> This [[Historic roads and trails|historic road]] was first seized by the Spanish in colonisation that followed the [[Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire|defeat of the Aztec Empire]] in 1519 and renamed "''Real del Monte''"; from the Spanish "''real''" meaning "''[[Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor|kingly/royal]]''" and "monte" for mountain.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024 |title=Translation of real – Spanish–English dictionary |url=https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/spanish-english/real |website=Cambridge Spanish Dictionary Online}}</ref> The vernacular usage of the original Otomi "Magosti" continued and eventually transmuted to "El Hiloche", this name survives but is now only applied to the forest to the west of the town.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-07-23 |title=Parque Estatal Bosque El Hiloche – Comarca Minera Geoparque Mundial |url=https://geoparquecomarcaminera.mx/?ocean_portfolio=parque-estatal-bosque-el-hiloche |access-date=2024-08-31 |language=es-MX}}</ref>

"Mineral del monte" literally translates to "mineral of the mountain"<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024 |title=Translation of mineral – Spanish–English dictionary |url=https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/spanish-english/mineral |website=Cambridge Spanish Dictionary Online}}</ref>

== History ==
The Pachuca—Real del Monte Mining District has a long and rich heritage. The mines in the district are conservatively estimated to have produced 1.2 billion Troy ounces of silver and 6.2 million ounces of gold. That is 6% of the silver mined throughout the world during the last five centuries. Some of the mines have continued limited production until the present day.

[[Gold]] and [[silver]] were discovered after the [[Spanish conquest of Mexico]] in the 1520s. The [[New Spain|Colonial Spanish]] began [[mining]] in the 16th century in the Pachuca area, but the mines were suffering from flooding by 1725.   In 1741, [[Pedro Romero de Terreros]] and Jose Alejandro Bustamante started a drainage program using an [[adit]].<ref name=Edith>{{cite book|last1=Couturier|first1=edith|title=The Silver King|date=2003|publisher=University of New Mexico Press|location=Albuquerque|isbn=0826328741|pages=47–73}}</ref>

The native [[Indigenous peoples of Mexico|Mexican Amerindians]] were often enslaved to work the mines, despite the Royal mandate for Colonial [[Jesuit Reductions]] and later Franciscan [[Indian Reductions]]. In addition, Africans were imported to replace Indian slaves.  The [[Real del Monte 1766 Strike]] is considered by many to be the first real labor strike in North American history.<ref name=Edith/>{{rp|77–90}}

The town's steep streets, stairways and small squares are lined with low buildings, some dating back to the Spanish colonial [[New Spain|Viceroyalty of New Spain]] period (1500s - 1810). The older houses with high sloping roofs and [[chimney]]s in town show the 19th century Cornish and English influence.

===19th-century Cornish miners===

====Mining====
The  [[Cornish people|Cornish]] role in the development of Mexican [[silver mining]] is significant. The Cornish brought "new" mining technology of the [[Industrial Revolution]] in the 1820s, reviving Mexican silver mining. Especially important was British equipment to drain mine tunnels flooded by [[groundwater]] since the Spanish period. The majority of immigrant miners came from the Cornish Central Mining District of [[Camborne]]-[[Redruth]]-[[Gwennap]] in [[Cornwall]]. The Company of Adventurers in the Mines of Real del Monte, which operated the mines between 1824 and 1848, employed approximately 350 Cornish miners. During this period of full operation, the introduction of high-pressure steam engines and Cornish expertise transformed the district, making the municipality one of the wealthiest in the state of Hidalgo.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Schwartz |first=Sharron |url=https://cornubianpress.com/books/mining-a-shared-heritage/ |title=Mining a Shared Heritage: Mexico's 'Little Cornwall' |year=2011|language=en, es}}</ref>

One of the leading personalities in 19th-century Mexico mining was [[Francis Rule]], from [[Camborne]] in Cornwall. Known as ''El Rey de la Plata'' (Silver King), Rule left a mark on the Pachucan cityscape, with civic generosity to his adopted homeland.  His opinion could be a barometer for the rise and fall of mining shares. He became very wealthy from his numerous mining interests in the district. One was the Santa Gertrudis Mine, which by 1898 was one of the most productive in the State of Hidalgo under Cornish management. <!---?-$-?-U.S./MX/UK-?----In 1903, profits to stockholders had exceeded 1 million dollars.--->

Four extant Cornish mine engine houses, and the 'English' Cemetery (''Panteon Inglés'') containing the graves of hundreds of Cornishmen, bear witness to the Cornish contributions to Mexican silver mining for over a century. The cemetery is undergoing restoration following storm damage in 2016.<ref>{{cite news|title=Fund started to help restore Cornish cemetery in Mexico|work=West Briton|date=24 March 2016|page=14}}</ref>

====Sports====
It was the Cornish who first introduced [[soccer]]/football to [[Mexico]] (Pachuca). The first game of what would go on to be Mexico's national pastime (''futbol''), was first played in Mexico by Cornish miners at Pachuca in 1900, a fact that is celebrated each year. The first soccer club in the country, the [[C.F. Pachuca|Pachuca Athletic Club]], was also founded in that year.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026 |title=Mexico's “Little Cornwall”, Cornish Miners and Football in Mexico|url=https://www.cousinjacksworld.com/cornish-miners-and-football-in-mexico|access-date=2026-02-20 |language=en|last=Sharron|first=Schwartz}}</ref>  The first team consisted of Charles Dawe, John Dawe, James Bennetts, John Bennetts, William Blamey, Richard Sobey, William Bragg, William Thomas, Percy Bunt, Lionel Bunt, Albert Pengelly and William Pengelly, a decidedly 'Cornish' team. The Pachuca club encouraged the formation of teams in Mexico City and [[Orizaba]], the first championship was played in 1902 and 'El Pachuca' won the 1904-05 tournament.  Also [[rugby union]], [[cricket]], [[tennis]], [[polo]], [[chess]] among other sports, were introduced here.

[[Methodism]] was introduced to Roman Catholic Mexico by the [[Cornish people|Cornish]] upon their arrival, and most of the descendants of the Cornish in Real del Monte and [[Pachuca]] are of [[Methodist]] faith. They brought Methodist Christianity to other major Mexican cities as they relocated. Mexican remittances helped to build the Wesleyan Chapel in Redruth, Cornwall, in the 1820s.

==Geology==
The Pachuca-Real del Monte Mining District is at the southern end of a metalliferous mineral zone that extends northwest to encompass [[Mineral del Chico]] to the Zimapan lead-mining district to the northwest.  The [[ore deposit]]s occur in the [[volcanic]] [[Tertiary period|Tertiary]] Pachuca Group, principally [[andesite]]-[[rhyolite]] flow series with intervening [[tuff]] beds.  The deposits are [[epithermal]] [[sulfide minerals]] within steeply dipping [[normal fault]] [[vein (geology)|veins]].<ref name="Segerstrom">{{cite book|last1=Segerstrom|first1=Kenneth|title=Geology of South-Central Hidalgo and Northeastern Mexico, Mexico|date=1962|publisher=US Government Printing Office|location=Washington|pages=87–162|edition=USGS Bulletin 1104-C}}</ref>  [[Acanthite]] and [[argentite]] are the principal silver ore minerals.  Between 1973 through 1981, the Compañia de Real del Monte y Pachuca, S.A., produced 24,762,667.2 [[Troy ounces]] of silver and 133,950.33 Troy ounces of gold.<ref name="Fries">{{cite book|last1=Fries|first1=C.|title=Pachuca-Real del Monte Mining District, Hidalgo, in The Geology of North America|date=1991|publisher=The Geological Society of America|pages=323–326|edition=Vol P-3, Economic Geology, Mexico}}</ref>

The richest vein of silver was the Veta Vizcaina, or the Vizcaina Vein, which extended 5&nbsp;km to the east of Real del Monte.  Productive mines along this vein included the San Francisco, Santa Agueda (1767), Santa Teresa (1734), La Joya (1734), San Cayetano (1751), Dolores (1734), and La Palma (1734).  By 1750, [[Pedro Romero de Terreros]] had control of this lucrative vein.  Production continued until the mid-1770s.<ref name=Edith/>{{rp|158–159}}

Real del Monte was named a "''[[Pueblos Mágicos (Mexico)|Pueblo Mágico]]''" by the federal government, for its unique historical qualities, aesthetics, and traditions.<ref>[http://www.pueblosmexico.com.mx/pueblo_mexico_ficha.php?id_rubrique=307 Pueblos Magicos] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121013162405/http://www.pueblosmexico.com.mx/pueblo_mexico_ficha.php?id_rubrique=307 |date=2012-10-13 }} . accessed 1.24.2014.</ref><ref>Real del Monte was named a ''Pueblo Mágico'' in 2004</ref>  There are significant examples of [[Spanish Colonial architecture]], in individual buildings and via the overall [[cityscape]], that are preserved in the town.

===Little Cornwall===
The twin silver mining settlements of [[Pachuca]] and Real del Monte (Mineral del Monte) in the State of Hidalgo have been marketed as 'Mexico's Little Cornwall' by the Mexican Embassy in [[London]] since 2007. This represents the first attempt by the Spanish-speaking part of the [[Cornish diaspora]] to establish formal links with Cornwall. The [[Camborne]] Town Council voted on 19 July 2007 to twin with Pachuca and on 16 August 2007 a public meeting confirmed the earlier decision of [[Redruth]] Town Council to twin with Real del Monte. The formal twinning ceremony took place at Mineral del Monte in July 2008 during the visit of the [[Cornish Mexican Cultural Society]].

[[File:PastesTejadaRealMonte.JPG|thumb|right|150px|A shop selling [[Paste (pasty)|Pastes]], testament to its Cornish past]] 
;''Paste''
Real del Monte is a home of the [[Paste (pasty)|paste]] in Mexico, with 30 ''paste'' producers in the town. A little larger than cocktail pasties common to the UK, the Mexican-made versions are widely varied including: meat and potato, black bean, shredded chicken, and sausage, all heavily laced with chillies plus a range of sweet pasties including: pineapple, apple, strawberry, and blackberry. A number have developed into national chains with shops in towns and cities across Mexico. The town of Real del Monte is the site of a museum of Cornish pasties, opened in 2011.<ref>i (newspaper) October 19, 2015; Cornwall's pride wrapped up in pastry; Adam Lusher (pp. 26-27)</ref><ref name="Museum">{{Cite news|author=Millward, David|date=14 November 2011|title=World's first Cornish pasty museum opens in Mexico|newspaper=The Daily Telegraph|location=UK|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/uknews/8887155/Worlds-first-Cornish-pasty-museum-opens-in-Mexico.html}}</ref>

;International Pasty Festival
In October 2009 the town of Real del Monte (twinned with Redruth, Cornwall) held the first [[International Pasty Festival]] (Festival Internacional del Paste). Organised by the Municipality of Real del Monte, the paste producers and the Cornish Mexican Cultural Society Chapter Mexico, the Festival was a great success drawing an estimated 8,000 visitors to the town for the three-day event.  A coach of visitors travelled from Cuernavaca, some four hours away, and another from Mexico City.  The Festival was opened by the President Municipal of Real del Monte, Ing. Omar Mariano Skewes. During the opening speeches it was stated that "Cornish people rebuilt our shattered mining industry giving us work and now again, when we have lost that industry, the Cornish have given us pastes and a new source of income."
  
As is usual on festival days in this town, the programme commenced with a visit to the Cornish Cemetery and a guided tour by Bridget Galsworthy of the British Society, followed by wreath-laying at the Miner's Monument. The main street was transformed with a long line of tented stands where 'pastes' of all descriptions were produced and a large stage next to the Miners' Memorial provided dance and other entertainments throughout the Festival.

==Notable people ==
At the end of the 19th century, the engineer [[Andrés Aldasoro]] worked in the Las Dos Estrellas Mine. Two of his sons, [[Juan Pablo Aldasoro]] and [[Eduardo Aldasoro Suárez]], were born in Mineral del Monte. As adults, they became pioneers in [[aviation]], and both became members of the [[Early Birds of Aviation]]. [[Alfred C. Crowle]] was the [[Cornish people|Cornish]] born [[miner]] who emigrated to [[Mexico]] and in 1935 became manager to the national [[Mexican football team]].

John Edgar Benjamin Vial <ref>{{Cite web |last=Schwartz |first=Sharron |date=2024-08-11 |title=Johnnie Vial of Pachuca |url=https://www.cousinjacksworld.com/johnnie-vial/ |access-date=2026-03-31 |website=Cousin Jacks World |language=en-GB}}</ref> was a Cornish-Mexican who fought in the [[British Imperial Forces]] during [[World War I]]. He died in the [[Battle of Somme]]. For his service he received the [[British War Medal]] and the [[Victory Medal (United Kingdom)|Victory Medal]].<ref name="Zamora">{{cite web|url=http://www.oem.com.mx/elsoldezamora/notas/n3588664.htm |title=La visita del Principe Carlos de Inglaterra |publisher=El Sol de Zamora |accessdate=November 4, 2014}}</ref> There is a monument in his honor at the ''Panteon Inglés''.

== Demographics ==
{{Main|https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_del_Monte#Demograf%C3%ADa|l1 = Demografía}}
According to the 2020 census by [[National Institute of Statistics and Geography|INEGI]], the town had a population of 11,149 which accounted for 77.83% of the municipality's inhabitants. There were 5255 men and 5894 women: a ratio of 89.16 men per 100 women. There were 2499 privately owned homes.

In terms of language and ethnicity; only 38 spoke an indigenous language (0.34%). 1.17% (130 people) were [[Afro-Mexicans|afro-Mexican]]. Catholicism was by far the most common religion with 9579 declarations (85.91%), but 813 were Protestant (7.29%; possibly [[Cornish people|Cornish]] [[Methodism|Methodist]]) and 15 declared other religions. 730 declared no religion.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Descarga masiva |url=https://www.inegi.org.mx/app/descarga/ficha.html?tit=325911&ag=0&f=csv |access-date=2024-09-01 |website=www.inegi.org.mx |language=es}}</ref>

All mines in the municipality extracted silver ore which was then dressed using the patio process. The mines were originally worked by indigenous [[Otomi]]ni later by the Spanish from the 16th to 19th centuries. From 1824many of the mines became British interests operated by the [[:es:Compañía Real del Monte y Pachuca|Real del Monte Company]] until 1849 after a loss of $5,079,283;<ref>{{Cite book |last=Randall |first=Robert W. |title=Real del Monte: a British mining venture in Mexico |date=1972 |publisher=Univ. of Texas Pr |isbn=978-0-292-77000-3 |series=Latin American monographs |location=Austin, Tex.}}</ref> these were soon [[renationalised]] an in turn became worked by American companies in the 20th century. The last mine to operate was Mina San José La Rica which closed in 2005.<ref>{{Cite web |title=REAL DEL MONTE'S MINES |url=https://www.rutadelaplata.org/en/real-del-monte-s-mines.html |access-date=2024-09-17 |website=www.rutadelaplata.org}}</ref><ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Schwartz |first=Sharron |url=https://cornubianpress.com/books/cornish-in-latin-america/ |title=The Cornish in Latin America: 'Cousin Jack' and the New World |publisher=Cornubian Press |year=2016 |isbn=978-1-873489-50-5 |pages= |language=en|location=Wicklow, Ireland}}</ref>
