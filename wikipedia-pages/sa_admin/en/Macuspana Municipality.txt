'''Macuspana''' is a [[Municipalities of Tabasco|municipality]] in the [[List of states of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Tabasco]]. Its seat is the city of [[Macuspana]]. The municipality covers an area of {{cvt|2429|km2}} and has a total population of 158,601 as of the 2020 census. It is located in the [[Chilapa River]] basin in the [[Usumacinta]] region.

== Etymology ==
Several theories have been proposed for the origin of the word Macuspana. It might have been derived from the [[Nahuatl]] "Macui-chpana" meaning "place of the five sweeps" or "Macu-pane soque" meaning "place where the priest goes". The name of the city could have come from the combination of the names of Marcos and Juana, the alleged founders of the city.<ref name="Hist">{{cite web|url=https://tabasco.gob.mx/macuspana|title=Macuspana|work=State of Tabasco|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref>

==History==
The region was earlier known as "El Tortuguero" with traces of [[Mayan civilization]] from the sixth to ninth centuries CE. [[Macuspana]] was elevated to city status in 1930, and the municipality was established on 4 May 1946.<ref name="Hist"/>

==Geography==
Macuspana is one of the 17 [[Municipalities of Tabasco|municipalities]] in the [[List of states of Mexico|Mexican state]] of [[Tabasco]] in Mexico.<ref name="Hist"/><ref>{{cite web|title=Tabasco|url=http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/tabasco/ |work=Enciclopedia de los Municipios de México |publisher=[[Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal]] |access-date=4 January 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070302103940/http://www.e-local.gob.mx/work/templates/enciclo/tabasco/ |archive-date=2 March 2007 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.zonaturistica.com/en/destinations/tabasco/macuspana|title=Macuspana|work=Zonaturistica|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref> It is located in the [[Usumacinta]] region, about {{cvt|45|km}} from [[Villahermosa]], the capital of Tabasco. It shares borders with the municipality of [[Jonuta]] in the east, [[Centro, Tabasco|Centro]], [[Centla]], and Jonuta in the north, [[Tacotalpa]] in the south, and Tacotalpa, [[Jalapa, Tabasco|Jalapa]] and Centro in the west.<ref name="Hist"/> It is spread over an area of {{cvt|2429|km2}},<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/mexico/admin/tabasco/27012__macuspana/|title=Macuspana|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref> and has an average elevation of {{cvt|10|m}}.<ref name="Hist"/> It is located in the [[Chilapa River]] basin, and is watered by the Tepetitan, Puxcatán, Tulijá, and Maluco rivers.<ref name="Hist"/> 

Macusapana has a hot and humid climate with an average annual temperature of {{cvt|23.6|C}}. The months of April and May are the hottest, accompanied by rainfall.<ref name="Hist"/>

The municipality has its seat at the city of Macuspana, and includes 15 villages, 129 rancherías, 20 ejidos, and 12 rural colonies apart from the city.<ref name="Hist"/>

==Demographics==
The municipality had a population of 142,954 as of the 2005 census.<ref>{{cite report|url=http://www.beta.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/proyectos/ccpv/2005/tabulados/Cont2005_Tab_Poblacion.pdf|year=2005|title=Census Results for Tabasco, Mexico|work=Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía (INEGI)|access-date=1 April 2010}}</ref> It increased to 158,601 in the 2020 census. The population consisted of 77,531 males and 81,070 females. About 28% of the population was below the age of fourteen. The municipality had a literacy rate of 94.5%. [[Roman Catholics]] (75%) formed the majority of the population. About 96.3% of the population were born locally.<ref name="CP"/><ref name="Econ">{{cite web|url=https://www.economia.gob.mx/datamexico/en/profile/geo/macuspana|title=Macuspana|work=Economia|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref>
