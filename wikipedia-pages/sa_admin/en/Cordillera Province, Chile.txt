'''Cordillera Province''' ({{langx|es|Provincia de Cordillera}}) is one of six [[Provinces of Chile|provinces]] of the [[Santiago Metropolitan Region]] in central [[Chile]]. It spans an area of {{cvt|5528.3|km2}}. Its capital is [[Puente Alto]]. It had a population of 614,587 inhabitants as per the [[2024 Chilean census]].

==Etymology==
"Cordillera" is derived from [[Spanish language|Spanish]] and means a chain or system of parallel mountain ranges. It comes from the Old Spanish word "cordilla" meaning "little rope" or "string", which itself is derived from the [[Latin]] word "chorda".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/cordillera|title=Cordillera|work=[[Merriam Webster]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

==History==
Chile was re-organized into twelve regions excluding the Santiago Metropolitan Area by Law No. 575 enacted on 10 July 1974.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?i=6210|lang=es|title=Decreto Ley 575 del 14 de julio de 1974|trans-title=Decree Law 575 of 14 July 1974|work=[[Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile]]|access-date=1 May 2026}}</ref> The [[Santiago Metropolitan Region]] was officially created as per Law No.3260, enacted on 3 March 1980, and was made up of six provinces including Cordillera.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://nuevo.leychile.cl/servicios/Consulta/Exportar?radioExportar=Normas&exportar_formato=pdf&nombrearchivo=DL-3260_01-ABR-1980&exportar_con_notas_bcn=False&exportar_con_notas_originales=False&exportar_con_notas_al_pie=False&hddResultadoExportar=7083.1980-04-01.0.0%23|lang=es|title=Establece regimen de gobierno y administracion de la region metropolitana de Santiago|trans-title=Establishes the government and administration system of the Santiago metropolitan region|work=[[Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile]]|access-date=1 May 2026}}</ref>

==Geography==
Cordillera Province is one of the six provinces of the [[Santiago Metropolitan Region]] in Chile.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Santiago-region-Chile|title=Santiago Region|work=[[Britannica]]|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref><ref name="Admin">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/chile/mun/admin/|title=Administrative division of Chile|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 May 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://en.fao.xm.gov.cn/2025-05/06/c_879566.htm|title=Santiago Metropolitan Region|work=[[Government of Xiamen]]|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref> It covers an area of {{cvt|5528.3|km2}}.<ref name="INE"/> Most of the land area of the province belongs to the [[Maipo River]] basin.<ref name="MN">{{cite web|url=https://simbio.mma.gob.cl/DPA/DetailsProvincia/13|lang=es|title=Provincia details, Cordillera Provincia|trans-title=Details of the Province, Cordillera Province|work=[[Ministry of the Interior (Chile)]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref> Only about {{cvt|16000|ha}} of the province is covered by forests, which forms three percent of the total land area.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.globalforestwatch.org/dashboards/country/CHL/14/2/?gfwfires=true&map=eyJjYW5Cb3VuZCI6dHJ1ZX0%3D|title=Cordillera Province|work=Global Forest Watch|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

The region has a warm [[Mediterranean climate]] ([[Koppen classification]]: Csb), with the capital recording an average annual temperature of {{cvt|16.24|C}}, and receiving approximately {{cvt|3.3|cm}} of rainfall annually.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://weatherandclimate.com/chile/region-metropolitana-de-santiago/puente-alto|title=Puente Alto|work=Weather and Climate|access-date=1 June 2025}}</ref> However, surface temperatures in the summer reach {{cvt|35|C}} in the province.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.pas.va/en/publications/scripta-varia/sv156pas/orrego.html|title=Climate Crisis in Santiago: The New Face of Territorial Inequality|work=Pontifical academy of sciences|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

==Administration==
As a province, Cordillera is a second-level [[administrative division of Chile]], governed by a provincial governor.<ref name="Admin"/><ref name="Gobernadores">{{cite web|url=http://www.subdere.gov.cl/1510/w3-article-67516.html |access-date=18 March 2011 |title=Gobierno de Chile: Gobernadores |work=[[Government of Chile]] |language=es |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110707010623/http://www.subdere.gov.cl/1510/w3-article-67516.html |archive-date=7 July 2011 |df=dmy }}</ref> It is further subdivided into three [[Communes of Chile|communes]] (''comunas'')&ndash;[[Pirque]], [[Puente Alto]] and [[San José de Maipo]].<ref name="Admin"/><ref name="MN"/> The city of Puente Alto serves as the capital of the province.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://statoids.com/ycl.html|title=Province of Chile|work=Statoids|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://postal-codes.cybo.com/chile/puente-alto/|title=Puente Alto|work=Cybo|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

==Demographics==
According to the [[2024 Chilean census]], the province had a population of 614,587 inhabitants. The population consisted of 317,586 females (51.7%) and 297,001 males (48.3%). About 18.3% of the population was below the age of 15 years, 69.7% belonged to the age group of 15–64 years, and 11.9% was aged 65 years or older. The province had an urban population of 592,218 inhabitants (96.4%) and a rural population of 22,369 inhabitants (3.6%). Most of the residents were born in Chile, accounting for 583,367 inhabitants (94.9%). Indigenous people formed 54,581 inhabitants (8.9%) of the population, while 559,949 inhabitants (91.1%) identified themselves as non-indigenous. [[Roman Catholics]] formed the largest religious group with 251,003 adherents (50.3%), followed by [[Evangelicals]] or [[Protestants]] with 70,001 adherents (14.0%), and 155,389 inhabitants (31.1%) indicating no religious affiliation.<ref name="CP">{{cite web|url=https://www.citypopulation.de/en/chile/mun/admin/132__cordillera/|title=Cordillera Province|work=City Population.de|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite report|url=https://storage.googleapis.com/bktdescargascenso2024/viviendas_censo2024.zip|title=Chilean Census 2024, Results|work=[[National Statistics Institute (Chile)]]|access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>
