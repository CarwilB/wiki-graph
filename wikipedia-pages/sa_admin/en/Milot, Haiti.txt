'''Milot''' ({{IPA|fr|milo}}; {{langx|ht|Milo}}) is a [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] in the [[Nord (Haitian department)|Nord]] [[Departments of Haiti|department]] of [[Haiti]], 12 miles south of [[Cap-Haïtien]]. It is the site of [[Sans-Souci Palace]], one of Haiti's most revered landmarks.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Geller |first=Pamela L |author-link=Pamela L. Geller|last2=Marcelin |first2=Louis Herns |date=2020 |title=In the shadow of the Citadel: Haitian national patrimony and vernacular concerns |url=http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1469605319883483 |journal=Journal of Social Archaeology |language=en |volume=20 |issue=1 |pages=49–73 |doi=10.1177/1469605319883483 |issn=1469-6053}}</ref> The [[Citadelle Laferrière]], Haiti's best-known landmark, is five miles (8&nbsp;km) by road to the south. The town also hosts a hospital, Hôpital Sacré Coeur, run by the [[Sovereign Military Order of Malta]] and the Crudem Foundation.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Home |url=https://crudem.org/ |access-date=2024-02-11 |website=The CRUDEM Foundation, Inc |language=en}}</ref>

== Communal sections ==
The commune consists of three [[communal section]]s, namely:
* [[Perches-de-Bonnet]], rural
* [[Bonnet à l'Evêque]], urban (town of Milot) and rural
* [[Genipailler]], urban (Carrefour des Pères neighborhood) and rural

== History ==
The earliest documentation of inhabitants in Milot is 1786, when there was a small plantation in the area.<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal |last=Monroe |first=J. Cameron |date=2020-10-01 |title=Sovereignty after Slavery: Universal Liberty and the Practice of Authority in Postrevolutionary Haiti |url=https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/710104 |journal=Current Anthropology |language=en |volume=61 |issue=S22 |pages=S232–S247 |doi=10.1086/710104 |issn=0011-3204|url-access=subscription }}</ref> Milot's growth was strongly shaped by the Haitian revolutionary [[Henri Christophe]]. From 1802 to 1804, Milot was the base of his military operations. It grew further after the [[Haitian Declaration of Independence]] marked the end of the revolution, becoming a small town by 1806. At this time, it may have consisted of 160 houses with 500 residents.<ref name=":0" />
