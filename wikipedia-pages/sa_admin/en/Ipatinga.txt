'''Ipatinga''' is a [[Municipalities of Brazil|Brazilian municipality]] located in the interior of the [[Federative units of Brazil|state]] of [[Minas Gerais]], in the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]] of Brazil. Situated in the [[Vale do Rio Doce (mesoregion)|Vale do Rio Doce]], it is part of the [[Vale do Aço metropolitan area|Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]], approximately {{formatnum:210|km}} km east of the [[Belo Horizonte|state capital]]. The municipality covers an area of just over {{formatnum:160|km²}} km², with about {{formatnum:40|km²}} km² in [[urban area]], and its population was estimated at 235,311 inhabitants in 2025, making it the eleventh most populous municipality in Minas Gerais. The municipal seat is located near the confluence of the [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]] and the [[Doce River]].<ref name="IBGE_Ipatinga"/>

Exploration of the region where Ipatinga now stands began in the 19th century with the arrival of ''[[bandeirantes]]''. However, significant settlement only occurred between the 1910s and 1920s with the establishment of the [[Vitória-Minas Railway|EFVM]]. In 1953, the area was designated a district under [[Coronel Fabriciano]], and during the same decade, it was selected as the site for the industrial hub of [[Usiminas]], leading to rapid population growth as people migrated from various parts of Brazil. At the request of the company, the first neighborhoods of Ipatinga were constructed to house its workers, culminating in the municipality's emancipation in 1964.

Alongside the original "Workers' Village," the growth of the non-industrial population spurred the development of new neighborhoods unrelated to Usiminas during the second half of the 20th century, although industry remains the primary source of municipal revenue. The sustained industrial activity in the region contributed to the formation of the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region, one of the main urban hubs in the state's interior. Ipatinga plays a pivotal role as an employer for surrounding cities and generates over 60% of the metropolitan region's [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]].

Cultural traditions such as handicrafts and ''congado'' from rural communities are present in the municipality, alongside recreational attractions such as [[Ipanema Park]], [[Shopping Vale do Aço (Mall)|Shopping Vale do Aço]], and Usipa. Much of Ipatinga's entertainment stems from Usiminas' community investments, notably the Usiminas Cultural Center, which hosts cultural events of regional or even national significance.

== Etymology ==
The origin of the [[Toponymy|name]] "Ipatinga" varies by source. One version suggests it is a term from the [[Tupi language]] meaning "clear water landing."<ref name="IBGE_História"/> However, according to [[Eduardo de Almeida Navarro|Eduardo Navarro]] in his ''[[Dictionary of Old Tupi]]'' (2013), the name translates to "clear lagoon" (''upaba'', lagoon, and ''tinga'', clear).<ref>NAVARRO, E. A. Dicionário de Tupi Antigoː a Língua Indígena Clássica do Brasil. São Paulo. Global. 2013. p. 569.</ref> Another account claims that the name was created by engineer Pedro Nolasco, responsible for the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]] project, combining "Ipa" (from [[Ipanema, Minas Gerais|Ipanema]]) and "tinga" (from [[Caratinga]]).<ref name="SócioC_8e9"/>

== History ==
=== Colonization of the region ===
Exploration of the region, initially known as the ''Sertões do Rio Doce'', began in the late 16th century with expeditions seeking precious metals. However, exploration was banned in the early 17th century to prevent [[gold]] smuggling from the nearby [[Diamantina, Minas Gerais|Diamantina]] region. Settlement was permitted in 1755,<ref name="Circuito_HistóriaFabriciano">{{cite web|url=http://www.circuitomataatlantica.tur.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=159 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140209120652/http://www.circuitomataatlantica.tur.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=159 |archive-date=9 February 2014 |title=Coronel Fabriciano |author=Circuito Turístico Mata Atlântica de Minas Gerais |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> and during the 19th century, under Emperor [[Pedro I of Brazil]], the ''[[bandeirantes]]'' visited the area, noting the presence of [[Indigenous peoples in Brazil|indigenous peoples]].<ref name="IBGE_História"/> Nevertheless, significant colonization in the Ipatinga area did not occur until the 20th century.<ref name="UCAM_21e22"/> By around 1920, small settlements had formed in the current neighborhoods of Barra Alegre,<ref name="AmoIpat_BarraAlegre">{{cite web|url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Barra%20Alegre&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615192730/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Barra%20Alegre&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=Barra Alegre Neighborhood |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Ipaneminha,<ref name="AmoIpat_Ipaneminha">{{cite web|url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Ipaneminha&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615192804/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Ipaneminha&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=Ipaneminha Neighborhood |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Taúbas,<ref name="AmoIpat_Taúbas">{{cite web|url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Ta%FAbas&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615192853/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Ta%FAbas&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=Taúbas Neighborhood |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> and Bom Jardim, resulting from land appropriations<ref name="AmoIpat_BomJardim">{{cite web|url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Bom%20Jardim&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150603163247/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/bairros/noticias.asp?video=Bom%20Jardim&cidade=Ipatinga |archive-date=3 June 2015 |title=Bom Jardim Neighborhood |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> or, in the case of Ipaneminha, as stopping points along a road used by [[Tropeiro|''tropeiros'']] traveling to [[Ouro Preto]] and Diamantina.<ref name="AmoIpat_Ipaneminha"/>
[[File:Estação Pedra Mole após restauro, Ipatinga MG2.jpg|thumb|left|Pedra Mole Station, the first railway station in Ipatinga and the Vale do Aço, after restoration.]]

The construction of the [[Vitória-Minas Railway|EFVM]] through eastern Minas Gerais stimulated population growth along the banks of the [[Doce River|Doce]] and [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba]] rivers, as it would in the Vale do Aço region. On 1 August 1922, the Pedra Mole Station was inaugurated near the current Cariru neighborhood,<ref name="UCAM_21e22"/> and in the same year, the Nossa Senhora Station was built, leading to the emergence of the Córrego de Nossa Senhora or Horto de Nossa Senhora settlement in the present-day Horto neighborhood.<ref name="UFMG_48">{{Harvtxt|Dias|2011|p=48}}</ref> At this location, the first pharmacy in the current Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region was established by pharmacist (and future politician) Raimundo Alves de Carvalho.<ref name="AmoIpat_RaimundoA">{{cite web |url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=547 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141118223604/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=547 |archive-date=18 November 2014 |title=Notable Figures of Vale do Aço - Raimundo Alves de Carvalho |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref>

The Pedra Mole Station was deactivated a few years after its opening due to a change in the EFVM's route, and a new terminal was built in the current Center of Ipatinga.<ref name="Cronologia">{{cite web|url=http://www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/9674/Historia-da-Cidade |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150428181140/http://www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/9674/Historia-da-Cidade |archive-date=28 April 2015 |title=City History |author=Prefeitura |date=17 April 2013 |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> In 1924, the [[Calado station|Calado Station]] was established in [[Coronel Fabriciano]], where the Belgo-Mineira Steel Company set up operations in the 1930s. Large areas were acquired to centralize the company's timber extraction in the region to fuel its furnaces in [[João Monlevade]].<ref name="UCAM_21e22">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|pp=21–22}}</ref> However, only Coronel Fabriciano experienced significant population and urban growth between the 1930s and 1940s, intensified by the establishment of [[Aperam South America|Acesita]] in 1944, leading to its emancipation from [[Antônio Dias]] in 1948.<ref name="Circuito_História"/> Under Law No. 1,039, dated 12 December 1953, the Ipatinga settlement was elevated to a district of Fabriciano.<ref name="IBGE_História">{{cite web|url=http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/ipatinga.pdf |title=Ipatinga - History |author=Enciclopédia dos Municípios Brasileiros |date=2007 |publisher=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |access-date=15 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140828214323/http://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtbs/minasgerais/ipatinga.pdf |archive-date=28 August 2014}}</ref>

The presence of charcoal kilns operated by Belgo-Mineira and Acesita boosted local commercial activity, further supported by the construction of the Salto Grande Hydroelectric Plant in [[Braúnas]]. Machinery for this project was unloaded in the settlement, stored, and gradually transported to the dam. Nevertheless, the Ipatinga district received little attention from the municipal seat until the late 1950s.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Genovez |first1=Patrícia Falco |last2=Valadares |first2=Vagner Bravos |date=October 2013 |doi=10.5212/Rev.Hist.Reg.v.18i2.0005 |title=The territorial formation of Coronel Fabriciano (seat) and Ipatinga (district) between the 1920s and 1960s: Who are the Established and the Outsiders? |pages=381–382 |journal=Revista de História Regional |publisher=Universidade Estadual de Ponta Grossa (UEPG) |url=http://www.revistas2.uepg.br/index.php/rhr/article/viewFile/5382/3740 |access-date=30 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170709000847/http://www.revistas2.uepg.br/index.php/rhr/article/viewFile/5382/3740 |archive-date=9 July 2017 |url-status=dead |issn=1414-0055|doi-access=free }}</ref> Until then, its primary economic activities remained tied to agriculture and livestock.<ref name="Circuito_História">{{cite web|url=http://www.circuitomataatlantica.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=160 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615192943/http://www.circuitomataatlantica.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=160 |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=Ipatinga |author=Circuito Turístico Mata Atlântica de Minas Gerais |access-date=15 June 2016 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Economic and industrial expansion ===
In 1956, a Japanese commission visited the then-district of Ipatinga, selecting it as the site for the [[Usiminas]] steel plant. Factors influencing this decision included suitable [[topography]], proximity to raw material sources and consumer markets, availability of water resources, abundant electricity, local railway infrastructure, and proximity to other steelmaking centers. News of the steel plant's construction attracted many new residents even before its establishment, necessitating urban planning for the city.<ref name="IPOG_5">{{Harvtxt|Giusti|2013|p=5}}</ref><ref name="UCAM_24a25e30">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|pp=24–25; 30}}</ref>

With the establishment of Usiminas in Ipatinga, entire neighborhoods were built to house its workers, with Horto being the first.<ref name="UFMG_79">{{Harvtxt|Dias|2011|p=79}}</ref> At that time, the settlement had about 60 houses and 300 inhabitants.<ref name="UFMG_48"/> The development of the first urban plan for what was then called the Workers' Village,<ref name="UFMG_75e76">{{Harvtxt|Dias|2011|pp=75–76}}</ref> designed by architect Raphael Hardy Filho in 1958, enabled the establishment of the municipality's initial neighborhoods.<ref name="AmoIpat_RaphaelHardy">{{cite web |url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=825 |title=Notable Figures of Vale do Aço - Raphael Hardy Filho |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160611185537/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/personagens/noticias.asp?codigo=825 |archive-date=11 June 2016}}</ref> The residential complexes of the former Workers' Village are located between the Piracicaba River and the steel plant<ref name="IPOG_7">{{Harvtxt|Giusti|2013|p=7}}</ref> and were distributed according to the company's hierarchy, distinguishing between engineers, technicians, workers, and supervisors.<ref>{{Harvtxt|Giusti|2013|p=3}}</ref> Usiminas began operations on 26 October 1962, after then-President [[João Goulart]] ignited the [[blast furnace]] for the first time, enabling the initial run of [[pig iron]].<ref name="UCAM_246e247">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|pp=246–247}}</ref>

However, state investments, which accounted for 55% of Usiminas' state capital—5% from national entrepreneurs and 40% from Japanese investors—<ref name="Integradias">{{cite web|url=http://www.integradias.com.br/2013/10/reveja-sadacruzeiro-x-funvictaubat/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141016151846/http://www.integradias.com.br/2013/10/reveja-sadacruzeiro-x-funvictaubat/ |archive-date=16 October 2014 |title=History – The Ipatinga Massacre of 1963 turns 50 |author=Rádio Integração |date=7 October 2013 |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> were largely confined to the company's surroundings and gave little consideration to Coronel Fabriciano as a whole, which had exempted Usiminas from taxes.<ref name="Rotary">{{cite web|url=http://www.bdtd.ufes.br/tedesimplificado/tde_busca/arquivo.php?codArquivo=611 |title=Rotary Club: activities in Canaã, Minas Gerais |author=Rotary International |page=10; 26 |publisher=Universidade Federal do Espírito Santo (UFES) |access-date=15 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141013122702/http://www.bdtd.ufes.br/tedesimplificado/tde_busca/arquivo.php?codArquivo=611 |archive-date=13 October 2014}}</ref> Moreover, the infrastructure provided by the company was insufficient to meet the needs of workers from the region or those arriving from other parts of Brazil seeking employment, as well as those not employed in the industry. In Ipatinga, housing was scarce, and violence rates were high.<ref name="PDDI1_42a49">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=42–49}}</ref><ref name="IFCH_63a69">{{Harvtxt|Farias|2010|pp=63–69}}</ref>

=== Urban conflicts and emancipation ===
[[File:Monumento 7 de Outubro, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|upright|"7 October" Monument in the Bom Retiro neighborhood, commemorating the victims of the [[Ipatinga massacre|Ipatinga Massacre]].]]
Protests by Usiminas workers against poor housing and working conditions, compounded by humiliations such as being searched before entering and leaving the company, intensified military oppression under orders from Minas Gerais Governor [[José de Magalhães Pinto]].<ref name="Movaço_65e66">{{Harvtxt|Fernandes|2013|pp=65–66}}</ref> On 7 October 1963, approximately six thousand striking workers at the company's entrance<ref name="Movaço_111">{{Harvtxt|Fernandes|2013|p=111}}</ref> were fired upon by 19 soldiers positioned on a truck. The incident, known as the [[Ipatinga massacre|Ipatinga Massacre]], officially resulted in eight deaths and 79 injuries, though these figures have been contested.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ebc.com.br/noticias/brasil/2013/10/comissao-da-verdade-realiza-audiencia-para-relembrar-massacre-de-ipatinga |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160118204444/http://www.ebc.com.br/noticias/brasil/2013/10/comissao-da-verdade-realiza-audiencia-para-relembrar-massacre-de-ipatinga |archive-date=18 January 2016 |title=Truth Commission holds hearing to commemorate the Ipatinga Massacre |author=Luciano Nascimento |date=6 October 2013 |publisher=Agência Brasil |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref>

In the months following the massacre, salary increases were granted, the security team was replaced, and the soldiers involved in the attacks and massacre were convicted. However, the [[1964 Brazilian coup d'état|1964 coup]] ousted then-President and unionist [[João Goulart]], initiating the [[Brazilian military dictatorship|military regime]]. This led to the arrest of local unionists and labor movement leaders, and the involved police officers were acquitted.<ref name="Movaço_159">{{Harvtxt|Fernandes|2013|p=159}}</ref> The construction of residential complexes also intensified during the 1960s.<ref name="UCAM_34e35">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|pp=34–35}}</ref>

Prior to the massacre, there was already dissatisfaction among Ipatinga's population with Coronel Fabriciano's administration due to the distance to the municipal seat and a sense of isolation, leading to the formation of a pro-emancipation commission in 1962.<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoII">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=313 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193029/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=313 |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=The political-administrative emancipation of Ipatinga - Chapter II |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref><ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoV">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=328 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193110/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=328 |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=The political-administrative emancipation of Ipatinga - Chapter V |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Like the district of Timóteo, Ipatinga's emancipation was approved by the Coronel Fabriciano City Council and enacted by the [[Legislative Assembly of Minas Gerais]] through state law No. 2,764, dated 30 December 1962,<ref name="IBGE_História"/><ref name="G1_53anos"/> but was vetoed by Governor Magalhães Pinto under the influence of a political alliance with then-Fabriciano mayor Cyro Cotta Poggiali.<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoV"/><ref name="UCAM_43">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|p=43}}</ref><ref name="AmoIpat_Jogada">{{cite web |url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=56 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150827231304/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=56 |archive-date=27 August 2015 |title=Municipal emancipation involved a political maneuver |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Fabriciano's administration was reluctant to lose revenue from Acesita (in Timóteo) and Usiminas,<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoII"/> and the governor claimed he aimed to maintain the political, administrative, economic, and financial unity of this industrial hub.<ref name="SócioC_8e9">{{Harvtxt|Câmara Municipal|1990|pp=8–9}}</ref>

In 1962, the district had about 20,000 inhabitants.<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoVI">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=329 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193145/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=329 |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=The political-administrative emancipation of Ipatinga - Chapter VI |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> However, influenced by the 1963 massacre and a rift between Fabriciano's mayor and Magalhães Pinto,<ref name="AmoIpat_Jogada"/> a new commission secured Ipatinga's emancipation approval by the State Interior Secretariat on 28 April 1964. The same process also emancipated the district of Timóteo, as well as [[João Monlevade]] and [[Bela Vista de Minas]], from Coronel Fabriciano.<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoÚltimo"/> The independence of Ipatinga and Timóteo was announced on a stage set up in [[Centro (Coronel Fabriciano)|Fabriciano's Centro]] around noon on the same day, formalized with publication in the Official Gazette the following day, 29 April.<ref name="G1_53anos">{{cite web|url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/g1-vales-faz-reportagem-especial-em-comemoracao-aos-53-anos-de-ipatinga-e-timoteo.ghtml |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170516154225/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/g1-vales-faz-reportagem-especial-em-comemoracao-aos-53-anos-de-ipatinga-e-timoteo.ghtml |archive-date=16 May 2017 |title=G1 Vales produces special report to celebrate 53 years of Ipatinga and Timóteo |author=InterTV dos Vales |date=28 April 2017 |publisher=G1 |access-date=16 May 2017}}</ref> José Orozimbo da Silva was appointed as interim mayor, later replaced by Délio Baêta Costa,<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoÚltimo">{{cite web|url=http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=332 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193240/http://euamoipatinga.com.br/causos/noticias.asp?codigo=332 |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=The political-administrative emancipation of Ipatinga - Final chapter |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> but Fernando Santos Coura was the first elected mayor, assuming office on 4 December 1965.<ref name="Cartilha_P206">Cartilha do Cidadão do Vale do Aço, 2000, pag. 206.</ref>

=== Urban consolidation ===
[[File:Monumento Tomie Ohtake, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|[[Tomie Ohtake]] Monument (2004). Commissioned by Usiminas to mark its 41st anniversary, it is located near the site of the Ipatinga Massacre, though its designer does not assign a specific meaning to the work.<ref name="IFCH_200a202">{{Harvtxt|Farias|2010|pp=200–202}}</ref>]]
By 1967, the Workers' Village included the neighborhoods Amaro Lanari, Bom Retiro, Cariru, Castelo, Horto, Imbaúbas, and Vila Ipanema.<ref name="UCAM_34e35"/> Among other essential infrastructure, the São Francisco Xavier School (1962) and the Márcio Cunha Hospital (1967) were established.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fsfx.com.br/historia/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160615193313/http://www.fsfx.com.br/historia/ |archive-date=15 June 2016 |title=History |author=Fundação São Francisco Xavier (FSFX) |work=Fundação São Francisco Xavier |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Influenced by the 1963 massacre, Usiminas adopted human resources and social assistance policies from the 1960s onward, as employment at the steel plant guaranteed access to housing, infrastructure, and healthcare.<ref name="UCAM_34e35"/> To regulate the purchase and sale of homes and land and preserve the original city plan, Raphael Hardy Filho developed a housing plan in 1965, stipulating that any construction in the residential complexes required approval from the company's Housing and Urbanism Department.<ref name="UCAM_34e35"/> Between the 1970s and 1980s, cemeteries, movie theaters, cultural and theatrical foundations, a bus terminal, and the [[Estádio Municipal João Lamego Netto|Ipatingão Stadium]] were built.<ref name="Cronologia"/>

Parallel to the original Workers' Village, the growth of the non-industrial population led to the emergence of new neighborhoods unrelated to the company, particularly in the city's periphery, during the second half of the 20th century.<ref name="PDDI1_98a100">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=98–100}}</ref><ref name="PDDI1_80a85">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=80–85}}</ref> Unplanned growth, combined with natural conditions, contributed to natural disasters during the [[1979 Brazil floods|floods of 1979]].<ref name="PDDI2_482">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|p=482}}</ref> In February of that year, floods, bridge collapses, and landslides isolated the city after days of heavy rain across the Doce River basin.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=66671 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707015155/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=66671 |archive-date=7 July 2016 |title=34 years and over ten thousand editions... |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=21 September 2012 |access-date=6 July 2016 |url-status=dead}}</ref> In Ipatinga alone, the rains left about 10,000 people homeless and 42 dead, many due to a landslide in the so-called Iapi gully, located between the current Cidade Nobre and Esperança neighborhoods.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aquafluxus.com.br/historico-de-enchentes-no-brasil-parte-2/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707015326/http://www.aquafluxus.com.br/historico-de-enchentes-no-brasil-parte-2/ |archive-date=7 July 2016 |title=History of floods in Brazil - part 2 |author=Luiza Ribeiro |date=27 November 2014 |publisher=AquaFluxus |access-date=6 July 2016}}</ref>
[[File:MPJ3451 IPN aguia branca 5480 viale OF-1721 (30141736196).jpg|thumb|left|Air-conditioned Viação Águia Branca bus, part of Ipatinga's former public transportation service, photographed on Pedro Linhares Gomes Avenue around 2003.]]

The Avenida 28 de Abril (formerly Rua do Comércio or Rua do Buraco) in Ipatinga's Center was also flooded during the 1979 rains.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=65284 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231194244/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=65284 |archive-date=31 December 2012 |title=The curse of years ending in 9 |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=2 December 2008 |access-date=6 July 2016 |url-status=dead}}</ref> This avenue is another example of disorganized development,<ref name="Avenida_SecEducação">{{cite web |url=https://ensfundamental1.files.wordpress.com/2010/06/histc3b3rico-da-avenida-vinte-e-oito-de-abril1.doc |title=History of Avenida Vinte e Oito de Abril |author=Ipatinga Municipal Education Department |date=9 June 2010 |access-date=15 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140922083127/https://ensfundamental1.files.wordpress.com/2010/06/histc3b3rico-da-avenida-vinte-e-oito-de-abril1.doc |archive-date=22 September 2014}}</ref> with a significant presence of [[slum]]s, contributing to high levels of violence and prostitution, in addition to flooding issues. In the early 1990s, booming commercial activity spurred the restructuring of this area, with part of its population relocated to public housing.<ref name="Avenida_SecEducação"/><ref name="AmoIpat_Avenida">{{cite web |url=http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/pracas/noticias.asp?codigo=9 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141202173021/http://www.euamoipatinga.com.br/pracas/noticias.asp?codigo=9 |archive-date=2 December 2014 |title=Avenida 28 de Abril |author=Revista Ipatinga Cidade Jardim |publisher=Eu Amo Ipatinga |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref> Despite housing projects, land regularization, and lot acquisitions, the presence of [[favela]]s in Ipatinga remained significant between the 2000s and 2010s, placing the city among those with the highest percentages of residents living in such conditions in Minas Gerais.<ref name="JVA_Pobreza">{{cite web|url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94563 |title=Fabriciano: highest number of slum dwellers |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=22 December 2011 |access-date=6 July 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121215045615/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94563 |archive-date=15 December 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="PDDI3_600a605">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=600–605}}</ref>

The privatization of Usiminas in the early 1990s shifted public administration focus from the company to the city as a whole. The Municipal Master Plan drafted in 1991 emphasized goals to "highlight the city's history and spaces, propose ways to integrate it into daily life, enable the creation of collective references, and foster recognition and appropriation of the city by its population."<ref name="UCAM_66a68">{{Harvtxt|Sampaio|2008|pp=66–68}}</ref> After its privatization, Usiminas increased investments in arts and cultural facilities to maintain credibility following past events,<ref name="IFCH_199">{{Harvtxt|Farias|2010|p=199}}</ref> such as the establishment of the Zélia Olguin Theater in 1994 and the Usiminas Cultural Center in 1998.<ref name="Cronologia"/> Employment at Usiminas also guaranteed free dental care for entire families, resulting in Ipatinga recording an index of 0.3 decayed, missing, or filled teeth per inhabitant in 2001, compared to Brazil's average of 4.9 and [[Finland]]'s 1.2 (the lowest globally, according to the [[United Nations|UN]]).<ref name="Exame_HistóriaComum">{{cite web|url=http://exame.abril.com.br/revista-exame/edicoes/754/noticias/uma-historia-em-comum-m0044462 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160828000505/http://exame.abril.com.br/revista-exame/edicoes/754/noticias/uma-historia-em-comum-m0044462 |archive-date=28 August 2016 |title=A shared history |author=José Maria Furtado |date=27 November 2001 |publisher=Exame |access-date=30 August 2016}}</ref> Its employees held 10% of the company's capital with profit-sharing rights.<ref name="Exame_Usiminas">{{cite web|url=http://exame.abril.com.br/revista-exame/edicoes/647a/noticias/usiminas-m0052190 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160828000359/http://exame.abril.com.br/revista-exame/edicoes/647a/noticias/usiminas-m0052190 |archive-date=28 August 2016 |title=Usiminas |author=Maria Tereza Gomes |date=21 October 1997 |publisher=Exame |access-date=30 August 2016}}</ref> In 2002, investments from the municipality, federal and state governments, and Usiminas made Ipatinga one of the first cities of its size in [[Latin America]] to treat 100% of its [[wastewater]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.saneamentobasico.com.br/portal/index.php/arquivo/ipatinga-e-a-primeira-cidade-da-america-latina-a-ter-todo-esgoto-coletado-e-tratado/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160512214015/http://www.saneamentobasico.com.br/portal/index.php/arquivo/ipatinga-e-a-primeira-cidade-da-america-latina-a-ter-todo-esgoto-coletado-e-tratado/ |archive-date=12 May 2016 |title=Ipatinga is the first Latin American city to have all sewage collected and treated |author=Portal Saneamento Básico |date=30 April 2002 |access-date=15 June 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil/noticia/2003-04-05/cidade-mineira-tem-100-de-esgoto-sanitario-tratado |title=Minas Gerais city has 100% of sanitary sewage treated |author=Agência Brasil |date=5 April 2003 |access-date=15 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160603172002/http://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil/noticia/2003-04-05/cidade-mineira-tem-100-de-esgoto-sanitario-tratado |archive-date=3 June == Geography ==
The municipality's area is {{formatnum:164.884|km²}} km², representing 0.0282% of Minas Gerais' territory, 0.0179% of the [[Southeast Region, Brazil|Southeast Region]], and 0.0019% of Brazil's total territory.<ref name="CNM_Dados">{{cite web|url=http://www.cnm.org.br/dado_geral/mumain.asp?iIdMun=100131358 |title=General data Ipatinga - MG |author=Confederação Nacional dos Municípios (CNM) |access-date=30 March 2010}}</ref> Of this total, {{formatnum:39.78|km²}} km² are urban.<ref name="IBGE_Ipatinga"/> According to the [[Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions|regional division]] effective since 2017, established by the IBGE,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |title=Regional Division of Brazil |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=5 September 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170905163107/http://www.ibge.gov.br/home/geociencias/geografia/default_div_int.shtm |archive-date=5 September 2017}}</ref> the municipality belongs to the [[List of Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions of Minas Gerais|Intermediate and Immediate Geographic Regions]] of Ipatinga.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2017">{{cite web|url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/divisao_regional/divisao_regional_do_brasil/divisao_regional_do_brasil_em_regioes_geograficas_2017/tabelas/regioes_geograficas_composicao_por_municipios_2017_20180911.xls |title=Database of municipalities in the Immediate and Intermediate Geographic Regions of Brazil |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2017 |access-date=10 February 2018}}</ref> Previously, under the division into microregions and mesoregions, it was part of the Ipatinga microregion, included in the Vale do Rio Doce mesoregion.<ref name="IBGE_DTB_2016">{{cite web|url=https://geoftp.ibge.gov.br/organizacao_do_territorio/estrutura_territorial/divisao_territorial/2016/DTB_2016_v2.zip |title=Brazilian Territorial Division 2016 |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2016 |access-date=5 September 2017}}</ref>

=== Topography and hydrography ===
[[File:Foz do Rio Piracicaba no Rio Doce vista do mirante da Estação Pedra Mole, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Confluence of the [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]] (right) and [[Doce River]] viewed from the Pedra Mole Station lookout.]]
Ipatinga is situated in the interplateau depression of the Vale do Rio Doce, with its [[topography]] shaped by [[fluvial erosion]] acting on [[Granite gneiss|granite-gneiss]] rocks from the [[Precambrian]] period.<ref name="CBH-Doce_Caracterização">{{cite web|url=http://www.riodoce.cbh.gov.br/bacia_caracterizacao.asp |title=Characterization of the Doce River Basin |author=Comitê da Bacia Hidrográfica do Rio Doce |access-date=6 July 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141006105252/http://www.riodoce.cbh.gov.br/bacia_caracterizacao.asp |archive-date=6 October 2014}}</ref> The geological complex includes [[gneiss]]ic, [[Igneous rock|magmatic]], and [[Metamorphic rock|metamorphic]] rocks, such as [[Biotite|biotite-gneiss]], [[Granite|granitic]] rocks, and granite-gneiss.<ref name="PDDI1_63a65">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=63–65}}</ref> The terrain is diverse, with 55% of Ipatinga's territory being flat, 30% undulating, and the remaining 15% rugged.<ref name="cidadesnet.com">{{cite web|url=http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/ipatinga.htm |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130119074908/http://cidadesnet.com/municipios/ipatinga.htm |archive-date=19 January 2013 |title=Ipatinga - MG |author=Cidades.Net |access-date=31 March 2010 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The highest elevations are found in the northwest, in the [[Serra dos Cocais]] range, reaching up to {{formatnum:1163}} meters. Conversely, the lowest altitudes are along the riverbanks, with the minimum elevation of 235 meters at the [[River mouth|mouth]] of the [[Piracicaba River (Minas Gerais)|Piracicaba River]] in the [[Doce River]].<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/><ref name="PDDI1_111a114">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=111–114}}</ref> The Piracicaba River's location and the flat terrain nearby facilitated the establishment of the Vitória-Minas Railway and Usiminas, and later the urban perimeter of Ipatinga, which was forced to expand toward higher elevations. Consequently, significant occupation, particularly by lower-income communities, has occurred in areas with steep slopes.<ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/>

The municipality lies within the [[Doce River Basin]] and is part of the Piracicaba River sub-basin. The mouth of the Piracicaba River in the Doce River is near the Cariru neighborhood, on the border with Timóteo.<ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/> Underground, beneath the Piracicaba River's mouth, lies an alluvial aquifer, the source of water for most of the Vale do Aço's supply.<ref name="PDDI1_111a114"/><ref name="JVA_Seca">{{cite web|url=http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=81202 |title=Lack of rain reduces reserves |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=27 February 2010 |access-date=6 July 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120118151458/http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=81202 |archive-date=18 January 2012 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The main sub-basin within the municipal territory is that of the Ipanema Stream, covering about {{formatnum:150|km²}} km²,<ref name="PDDI2_457a458">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=457–458}}</ref> with its main course traversing the city before reaching its mouth in the Doce River after a {{formatnum:28.5|km}} km journey.<ref name="Diário_Pesquisa">{{cite web|url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=49502 === Neighboring municipalities and metropolitan region ===
{{Main|Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region}}

[[File:Vista aérea de Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Aerial view of Ipatinga]]
Ipatinga borders [[Mesquita, Minas Gerais|Mesquita]] and [[Santana do Paraíso]] to the north, [[Caratinga]] to the east, [[Timóteo (municipality)|Timóteo]] to the south, and [[Coronel Fabriciano]] to the west.<ref name="cidadesnet.com" /> The region's rapid growth has blurred political boundaries between municipalities, forming the [[Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region]] (RMVA), which includes Ipatinga, Coronel Fabriciano, Santana do Paraíso, and Timóteo, along with 24 other municipalities in the [[Metropolitan belt (Brazil)|metropolitan belt]]. As the seat of Usiminas and other metal-mechanical companies, Ipatinga is a key employer for surrounding cities<ref name="ABEP_15">{{Harvtxt|ref=Dinâmica|ABEP|2012|p=15}}</ref> and, in 2011, generated 68.9% of the metropolitan region's [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]].<ref name="PDDI1_61">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=61}}</ref> The region gained international recognition due to major companies such as Cenibra (in [[Belo Oriente]]), [[Aperam South America]] (in Timóteo), and Usiminas (in Ipatinga), all with significant export volumes. Despite its recent settlement, the region is one of the state's main urban hubs.<ref name="ABEP_15"/>

[[File:Pôr do sol no Parque Ipanema, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|A sunset viewed from [[Ipanema Park]] on a September afternoon]]

[[Autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]] are transitional seasons.<ref name="Climatologia_IpatingaMG"/><ref name="Weatherspark">{{cite web|url=https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30691/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Ipatinga-Brasil-durante-o-ano |title=Average weather conditions in Ipatinga |author=Weather Spark |access-date=30 August 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180830214131/https://pt.weatherspark.com/y/30691/Clima-caracter%C3%ADstico-em-Ipatinga-Brasil-durante-o-ano |archive-date=30 August 2018}}</ref> The transition between dry and wet periods is marked by [[thunderstorm]]s and high [[Diurnal temperature variation|thermal amplitude]], particularly from late [[winter]] to spring.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0029508-primavera-pode-trazer-temporais |title=Spring may bring storms |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=13 September 2010 |access-date=7 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200907122213/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0029508-primavera-pode-trazer-temporais |archive-date=7 September 2020}}</ref>

With over 1,800 hours of [[sunshine]] annually, Ipatinga's average yearly [[relative humidity]] is 84%.<ref name="NCB-INMET"/> However, low humidity levels can occur during the dry season or prolonged [[Indian summer]]s (dry spells). During these periods, dry air combined with [[air pollution]] increases the concentration of atmospheric pollutants, contributing to poorer air quality.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Araújo |first=Viviane Macedo Reis |year=2011 |title=Air Quality Monitoring in the Vale do Aço Region, MG: Municipalities of Coronel Fabriciano and Ipatinga |journal=Centro Universitário Católica do Leste de Minas Gerais (Unileste) |pages=103, 121 |url=https://www.unilestemg.br/portal/mestrado/dissertacoes/dissertacao_030_viviane.pdf |access-date=2018-04-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180424224709/https://www.unilestemg.br/portal/mestrado/dissertacoes/dissertacao_030_viviane.pdf |archive-date=2018-04-24 |url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Fog]] forms when high humidity combines with low temperatures.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://ultimosegundo.ig.com.br/brasil/nevoeiro-em-ipatinga-mg/n1237680533570.html |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150707181444/http://ultimosegundo.ig.com.br/brasil/nevoeiro-em-ipatinga-mg/n1237680533570.html |archive-date=2015-07-07 |title=Nevoeiro em Ipatinga - MG |author=Climatempo |date=2008-05-06 |publisher=IG |access-date=2015-07-08}}</ref> The prevailing [[wind]] comes from the east, with the windiest period occurring between 3 August and 6 December, when the average wind speed is 10.6 kilometers per hour, peaking slightly in September and October. During the calmer season from March to June, average wind speeds range between 8 and 9 kilometers per hour.<ref name="Weatherspark"/>

=== Ecology and environment ===
[[File:Ribeirão Ipanema no B. Iguaçu, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Ipanema Creek in the Iguaçu neighborhood]]
The native vegetation of Ipatinga belongs to the [[Atlantic Forest]] biome, with only a few fragmented areas remaining amidst [[reforestation]] zones, pastures, and urban areas.<ref name="PDDI1_63a65"/><ref name="PDDI1_118a120">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=118–120}}</ref> Monoculture reforestation with [[eucalyptus]] is significant, covering a larger area than the original biome, primarily to supply raw material for the Cenibra cellulose factory in Belo Oriente. In 2009, eucalyptus plantations covered 3,055.74 hectares (18.36% of Ipatinga's territory), while watercourses accounted for 51.55 hectares (0.31%) and urbanized areas spanned 3,299.01 hectares (19.82%).<ref name="FlorestasMG">{{cite web |url=http://geosisemanet.meioambiente.mg.gov.br/inventario_bi/Interface/InventarioFlorestal.aspx?aplicacao=InventarioFlorestal.xml&objeto=municipio&id_marcador=15833&id=3169406&id_camada_referencia=4&tabela=public.municipio# |title=Minas Gerais Forest Inventory - Municipal Query |author=Minas Gerais Forest Inventory |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref> By 2014, eucalyptus plantations occupied 32.75&nbsp;km² (19.5% of the municipality's area), while native Atlantic Forest covered 6&nbsp;km² (3.65% of the total area).<ref name="PDDI1_118a120"/>

Ipatinga is home to one [[Environmental protection area (Brazil)|environmental protection area]] (APA) and two [[Private natural heritage reserve (Brazil)|Private Natural Heritage Reserves]] (RPPNs), which, together with neighboring preservation areas such as the Serra dos Cocais in Coronel Fabriciano and the Santana do Paraíso APA, form a [[wildlife corridor]] connecting to the [[Rio Doce State Park]] (PERD),<ref name="PDDI1_120a124">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=120–124}}</ref> the largest remaining Atlantic Forest fragment in Minas Gerais and one of the state's most significant lake systems.<ref name="PDDI1_63a65"/> However, some local preservation areas are used for pastures or eucalyptus cultivation.<ref name="PDDI2_358a360">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=358–360}}</ref> The Ipanema APA, established in 1997, contains Atlantic Forest remnants that serve as a [[riparian forest]] for the headwaters of the Ipanema Creek and other smaller watercourses.<ref name="Diário_Queimadas">{{cite web |url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/97880-6/cidades/incendio-devasta-area-de-protecao-ambiental |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706212355/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/97880-6/cidades/incendio-devasta-area-de-protecao-ambiental |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Incêndio devasta área de proteção ambiental |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2015-10-13 |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref> The Fazenda Córrego da Bucaina and Sítio do Zaca RPPNs are privately owned but registered at the state level as conservation areas.<ref name="PDDI2_361a363">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=361–363}}</ref>
[[File:Interior do Viveiro Municipal de Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Interior of the Ipatinga Municipal Nursery]]

The municipality manages five municipal parks: Ipanema, Samambaias, Parque das Montanhas, Parque Ecológico das Águas, and Parque Campo da União. The [[Ipanema Park]], with approximately 1 million m², is the largest urban green area in Minas Gerais. The Samambaia Municipal Park, located in the Bom Jardim neighborhood and established in 2000, features a lake and an Atlantic Forest remnant. Also created in 2000, the Campo da União Park lies between the Planalto II and Parque das Águas (Veneza) neighborhoods. The remaining parks were designated by the 2014 Municipal Master Plan.<ref name="PDDI2_361a363"/> A [[Plant nursery|municipal nursery]] is also maintained by the city, where fruit, ornamental, arboreal, and medicinal seedlings are cultivated for public spaces or purchased by residents.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=84183 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231170134/http://www.jornalvaledoaco.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=84183 |archive-date=2012-12-31 |title=Ipês floridos enfeitam Ipatinga |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=2010-07-08 |access-date=2010-09-25 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The Usipa Biodiversity Center (CEBUS), managed under Usiminas' oversight, includes a small [[zoo]], a seedling nursery, and a conservation area, offering visits, ecological activities, and programs for schools and the community.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=64031 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706212614/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=64031 |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Compromisso com a sustentabilidade |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2012-06-07 |access-date=2016-07-06 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Ipatinga is considered one of Brazil's most tree-covered cities, with over 100 m² of green space per inhabitant. In 2014, the city reported approximately 100,000 trees planted in public areas, including native species such as [[mahogany]], [[pine]], [[Arecaceae|palms]], [[Spondias mombin|yellow mombins]], [[Mangifera|mangoes]], [[Psidium guajava|guavas]], and [[Handroanthus chrysotrichus|golden trumpet trees]]. That year, there were 450,000 m² of grassy areas in squares and parks, 40,000 m² of gardens, and about 100 public squares and green spaces, adorned with roughly 20 native species, including purple heart, [[Chlorophytum comosum|spider plant]], [[Coleus|spurflower]], [[Erica (plant)|heath]], ''[[ixora]]'', [[Portulaca grandiflora|rose moss]], [[Vinca|periwinkle]], and daisy.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=9101 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706212655/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=9101 |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=A cidade das árvores fica mais bonita na primavera |author=Jornal Diário Popular | work=Jornal Diário Popular MG |date=2014-09-19 |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref> Local [[fauna]] includes birds such as the [[rufous-bellied thrush]], ''[[thraupis]]'', [[Rufous-collared sparrow|Andean sparrow]], ''[[Columbina (bird)|columbina]]'', [[tanager]], and [[great kiskadee]], while wildlife such as [[Leopardus tigrinus|oncillas]], [[armadillos]], [[pacas]], [[foxes]], and wild rats inhabit the remaining forest areas.<ref name="Portal_Fauna">{{cite web |url=http://perfil.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/perf_vis.asp?cd=51 |title=Fauna |author=Portal Cidadão |access-date=2010-04-10 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070731083004/http://perfil.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/perf_vis.asp?cd=51 |archive-date=2007-07-31}}</ref>

==== Environmental Issues ====
[[File:Céu esfumaçado em Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Smoky, polluted sky after a prolonged dry period in 2024, viewed from Cidade Nobre]]
Key environmental challenges in Ipatinga include flooding, which causes significant damage in low-lying, densely populated areas during the rainy season,<ref name="PDDI1_115a116">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=115–116}}</ref> and landslides on hills and slopes.<ref name="PDDI1_239">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|p=239}}</ref> Drainage issues in some areas hinder proper rainwater runoff to watercourses.<ref name="PDDI1_115a116"/> However, landslide-prone areas are relatively small compared to the urban expanse, as the city's terrain is less rugged, and large portions of the municipal territory are managed by Usiminas. A Municipal Risk Plan identifies areas susceptible to damage.<ref name="PDDI1_239"/>

Forest fires destroy native vegetation, degrade soil quality, and worsen air quality, compounded by [[air pollution]] from the Vale do Aço's industrial plants.<ref name="Diário_Queimadas"/><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=30649 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120730030248/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=30649 |archive-date=2012-07-30 |title=Pesquisa sobre a poluição industrial no Vale do Aço |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2009-09-27 |access-date=2016-07-06 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Air quality monitoring panels installed in some neighborhoods provide pollution level updates.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=50584 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706212911/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=50584 |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Cinco meses de monitoramento |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2010-11-20 |access-date=2016-07-06 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Water pollution is mitigated by [[wastewater treatment plants]] operated by Copasa and Usiminas, serving over 99% of the population.<ref name="PDDI1_200e201">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=200–201}}</ref> However, [[visual pollution]] is prevalent in urban areas. Posters on [[Orelhão|payphones]], poles, and signs<ref>{{cite web |url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/94095-6/cidades/cidade-cada-vez-mais-poluida |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706213133/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/94095-6/cidades/cidade-cada-vez-mais-poluida |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Cidade cada vez mais poluída |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2015-06-04 |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref> exacerbate the problem, as does [[Pichação|graffiti]] on the walls and gates of homes and businesses.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/90269-6/cidades/comunidade-do-iguacu-reclama-de-pichacoes |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706213358/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/90269-6/cidades/comunidade-do-iguacu-reclama-de-pichacoes |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Comunidade do Iguaçu reclama de pichações |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2015-02-11 |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref>

=== Indicators and inequality ===
[[File:Aglomerado Vila da Paz com prédios ao fundo, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Vila da Paz, one of Ipatinga's [[slums]], contrasting with buildings in the Iguaçu and Cidade Nobre neighborhoods in the background]]
Ipatinga's [[Human Development Index]] (HDI-M) is considered high by the [[United Nations Development Programme]] (PNUD), with a value of 0.771, ranking 220th in Brazil and 16th in Minas Gerais. Most of the city's indicators are close to the national average, according to the PNUD. The education index is 0.705, longevity is 0.864, and income is 0.752.<ref name="PNUD_IDH_2010"/>

From 2000 to 2010, the proportion of people with a per capita household income of up to half a minimum wage decreased by 61.6%. In 2010, 93.5% of the population lived above the [[Poverty threshold|poverty line]], 4.7% were at the poverty line, and 1.7% were below it.<ref name="ODM_1">{{cite web |url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031357/ipatinga---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150429150734/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/1-acabar-com-a-fome-e-a-miseria/BRA003031357/ipatinga---mg |archive-date=2015-04-29 |title=1 - End hunger and poverty |author=Portal ODM |access-date=2015-04-29}}</ref> The [[Gini coefficient]], which measures [[economic inequality]], was 0.524, where 1.00 is the worst and 0.00 is the best.<ref name="ODM_Perfil">{{cite web |url=http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031357/ipatinga---mg |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150429150634/http://www.relatoriosdinamicos.com.br/portalodm/perfil/BRA003031357/ipatinga---mg |archive-date=2015-04-29 |title=Municipal profile |author=Portal ODM |date=2012 |access-date=2015-04-29}}</ref> The wealthiest 20% of the population accounted for 57.0% of the city's total income, 14.1 times higher than the 4.0% share of the poorest 20%.<ref name="ODM_1"/> In 2010, according to the Gorceix Foundation, there was a housing deficit of 10,495 units, while 6,331 properties were vacant.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=56608 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120724133856/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=56608 |archive-date=2012-07-24 |title=Déficit de 10.495 moradias |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2011-08-03 |access-date=2016-07-07 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 2022, according to the IBGE census, 11.73% of the population lived in slums and urban communities,<ref name="Diário_Favelas"/> the tenth-highest proportion among Minas Gerais municipalities.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=2024-11-08 |title=Infographic: See how many people live in slums in your city |url=https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2024/11/08/infografico-veja-quantas-pessoas-moram-em-favelas-na-sua-cidade.ghtml |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102133125/https://g1.globo.com/economia/censo/noticia/2024/11/08/infografico-veja-quantas-pessoas-moram-em-favelas-na-sua-cidade.ghtml |archive-date=2025-01-02 |access-date=2025-01-02 |website=G1}}</ref> The institute identified 19 slums and urban communities in Ipatinga, housing 26,718 people.<ref name="Diário_Favelas">{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0119931-coronel-fabriciano-e-ipatinga-estao-entre-as-10-cidades-mineiras-com-mais-favelas-revela-censo |title=Coronel Fabriciano and Ipatinga among the top 10 Minas Gerais cities with the most slums, census reveals |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2024-11-09 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102133140/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0119931-coronel-fabriciano-e-ipatinga-estao-entre-as-10-cidades-mineiras-com-mais-favelas-revela-censo |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> Despite planned housing developments for industrial workers during Usiminas' establishment, slums emerged due to uncontrolled land occupation by migrants drawn to local economic growth.<ref name="PDDI1_98a100"/><ref name="PDDI1_80a85"/> Housing projects, land regularization, and lot acquisition are initiatives that aim to improve the management of policies related to these issues.<ref name="PDDI3_600a605"/>

== Politics and administration ==
[[File:Palestra na Câmara Municipal de Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Lecture on [[urban mobility]] with Ricardo Mendanha, director of the Ruaviva institute, held at the Municipal Chamber in 2019]]
Municipal administration in Ipatinga is carried out by the [[Executive Branch|Executive]] and [[Legislative Branch|Legislative]] powers. The Executive is led by the mayor, supported by a cabinet of secretaries.<ref name="lei-orgânica"/> The first executive representative was José Orozimbo da Silva, appointed as [[intendant]] by Governor [[José de Magalhães Pinto]] after the city's political emancipation,<ref name="AmoIpat_EmancipaçãoÚltimo"/> but Fernando Santos Coura was the first elected mayor, assuming office on 4 December 1965.<ref name="Cartilha_P206"/> In the 2024 municipal elections, Gustavo Morais Nunes was re-elected mayor by the [[Liberal Party (Brazil, 2006)|Liberal Party]] (PL) with 44.65% of valid votes for his second consecutive term, alongside José Pedro de Freitas (Pedrão da AAPI, [[Cidadania|Citizenship]]) as [[vice-mayor]].<ref name="Folha_Eleição2024">{{cite web |url=https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/eleicoes/candidatos/2024/mg/prefeito/gustavo-nunes-130002302990.shtml |title=Gustavo Nunes |author=Folha de S.Paulo |date=2024 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102133323/https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/eleicoes/candidatos/2024/mg/prefeito/gustavo-nunes-130002302990.shtml |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref><ref name="Diário_Eleição2024">{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0119136-gustavo-nunes-e-reeleito-em-ipatinga |title=Gustavo Nunes é reeleito em Ipatinga |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2024-10-06 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102133445/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0119136-gustavo-nunes-e-reeleito-em-ipatinga |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref>

The Legislative Branch consists of the municipal chamber,<ref name="lei-orgânica"/> composed of nineteen [[councilors]].<ref name="atual">{{cite web |author= |date=2016-10-02 |title=Candidates / Elected Councilors of Ipatinga |url=https://www.eleicoes2016.com.br/candidatos-ipatinga-mg/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170103142054/https://www.eleicoes2016.com.br/candidatos-ipatinga-mg/ |archive-date=2017-01-03 |access-date=2017-01-03 |website=Eleições 2016}}</ref> The chamber is responsible for drafting and voting on fundamental laws for administration and the Executive, particularly the participatory budget (budget guidelines law).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm |author=DJI |title=Federal Constitution - CF - 1988 / Art. 29 |access-date=2015-04-29 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140708010104/http://www.dji.com.br/constituicao_federal/cf029a031.htm |archive-date=2014-07-08 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Complementing the legislative process and secretarial work, active municipal councils include those for children's and adolescents' rights (created in 1995), guardianship (2008), elderly rights (2000), disability rights (2007), and women's policies (2005).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2011&node=1695&ordem=7.4&munic=313130 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20170104135657/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2011&node=1695&ordem=7.4&munic=313130 |archive-date=2017-01-04 |title=Municipal councils |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2011 |access-date=2017-01-04}}</ref>

Ipatinga operates under its [[organic law]], enacted on 1 May 1990,<ref name="lei-orgânica">{{cite web |url=http://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/uploads/files/contaspublicas/oficio_gp_06-2013_-_desnecessidade_registro_ata_de_posse_de_vereadores.pdf |title=Desnecessidade de registro de Ata de Posse de Vereadores |author=Câmara Municipal |date=2013-02-27 |access-date=2017-01-04 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170104235652/http://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/uploads/files/contaspublicas/oficio_gp_06-2013_-_desnecessidade_registro_ata_de_posse_de_vereadores.pdf |archive-date=2017-01-04}}</ref> and hosts a ''[[comarca]]'' of special jurisdiction at the Doutora Valéria Vieira Alves Forum, serving the municipalities of [[Ipaba]] and [[Santana do Paraíso]].<ref name="TJMG_Comarcas">{{cite web |url=http://www8.tjmg.jus.br/servicos/gj/guia/docs/comarcas.pdf |title=List of Courts |author=Tribunal de Justiça do Estado de Minas Gerais (TJMG) |date=2013-07-21 |access-date=2015-04-29 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131015014325/http://www8.tjmg.jus.br/servicos/gj/guia/docs/comarcas.pdf |archive-date=2013-10-15 |url-status=dead}}</ref> As of September 2024, the city had 180,396 voters, according to the [[Superior Electoral Court]] (TSE).<ref>{{cite web |author=Tribunal Superior Eleitoral (TSE) |date=2024-09-13 |title=MG - Municipality - Voter Evolution |url=https://sig.tse.jus.br/ords/dwapr/r/seai/sig-eleicao-eleitorado/evolu%C3%A7%C3%A3o-do-eleitorado?clear=5&session=100563007127300 |access-date=2025-02-27}}</ref>

== Economy ==
[[File:Trecho da Avenida 28 de Abril, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Shops on Avenida 28 de Abril]]
The [[gross domestic product]] (GDP) of Ipatinga is driven primarily by the [[Secondary sector of the economy|industrial sector]]. According to 2021 IBGE data, the municipality's GDP at current prices was R$17,609,176.97 thousand.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> Of this, R$1,968,496.61 thousand came from net product taxes at current prices, and the per capita GDP was R$65,869.82.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/38/46996 |title=Gross Domestic Product of Municipalities - 2021 |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2021 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102133743/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/38/46996 |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> In 2010, 65.23% of the population over 18 was [[Workforce|economically active]], with an unemployment rate of 8.77%.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/> Approximately 10% of the population commuted to other municipalities for work, facilitated by the proximity and accessibility of the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region.<ref name="PDDI2_304a305">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=304–305}}</ref>

In 2022, [[Salary|salaries]] and other remunerations totaled R$2,619,194 thousand, with an average monthly salary of 2.3 [[minimum wages]]. The municipality had 12,602 local units and 11,895 active companies.<ref name="IBGE_Empresas">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/19/143491 |title=Central Business Register |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2022 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102134129/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/19/143491 |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> According to the IBGE in 2010, 49.92% of households earned less than one minimum wage per person monthly (36,384 households), 36.82% earned between one and three minimum wages per person (26,840 households), 6.37% earned between three and five minimum wages (4,646 households), 4.09% earned above five minimum wages (2,980 households), and 2.80% had no income (2,040 households).<ref name="SIDRA_Rendimento">{{cite web |url=http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/listabl.asp?z=cd&o=4&i=P&c=3261 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160706153114/http://www.sidra.ibge.gov.br/bda/tabela/protabl.asp?c=3261&i=P&opc386=2&nome=on&qtu8=137&digt102=&qtu14=3&notarodape=on&tab=3261&opn8=0&opn14=0&unit=0&pov=3&OpcTipoNivt=1&opn1=0&qtu11=658&qtu23=294&nivt=0&orp=4&qtu3=27&qtu13=47&opv=2&orc386=3&pop=1&opn2=0&opn15=0&orv=2&qtu2=5&opn10=0&qtu15=3&sev=96&opp=f1&opn3=0&qtu6=5565&qtu102=14208&opn13=0&poc386=2&sep=26272&orn=1&digt10=&opn11=0&qtu7=36&opn23=0&sec386=9680&pon=1&qtu9=558&opn6=3&digt6=Ipatinga&opn102=0&OpcCara=104&proc=1&qtu1=1&opn9=0&cabec=on&qtu10=10282&digt11=&opn7=0&decm=99 |archive-date=2016-07-06 |title=Permanent private households, by nominal monthly household income per capita classes |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2010 |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref>
[[File:Vista parcial do Shopping Vale do Aço, Ipatinga MG2.JPG|thumb|right|View of the [[Shopping Vale do Aço (Mall)|Vale do Aço Shopping Mall]], one of the largest shopping centers in inland Minas Gerais]]

In 2012, the [[Sebrae|Brazilian Service of Support for Micro and Small Enterprises]] (SEBRAE) ranked Ipatinga the seventh-best city in Minas Gerais to start a business,<ref name="Exame_Negócio"/> but a decline in national and international steel demand led to layoffs and cuts in investments and wages by local industries in the early 2010s.<ref name="G1_Crise"/><ref name="Diário_Desempregados"/> By the early 2020s, the situation had stabilized. From January to November 2024, there was a surplus of 636 jobs in commerce and services, though industry played a smaller role in job creation.<ref name="Diário_Fraca"/>

=== Industry and services ===
[[File:Fábrica da Usiminas em Ipatinga MG vista do B. Cidade Nova.JPG|thumb|left|Usiminas factory viewed from the Cidade Nova neighborhood]]
In 2021, industry was the leading economic sector, contributing R$9,195,871.95 thousand to the municipal GDP's gross added value.<ref name="IBGE_PIB_2021"/> Much of this is driven by [[Usiminas]], which exports a significant volume of [[steel]], [[stainless steel]], and [[metalworking]] products.<ref name="ABEP_4">{{Harvtxt|ref=Dinâmica|ABEP|2012|p=4}}</ref> The industrial complex attracts supplier, complementary, and service companies to Ipatinga and surrounding municipalities.<ref name="PDDI1_61"/> An [[industrial district]], established in 1998, spans approximately 35,113,336 m².<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.codemig.com.br/distritos-industriais |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160703130124/http://www.codemig.com.br/distritos-industriais |archive-date=2016-07-03 |title=Map of industrial districts |author=Companhia de Desenvolvimento Econômico de Minas Gerais (Codemig) |access-date=2016-07-06}}</ref>

Other industrial activities include the manufacture of clothing and accessories, the extraction and processing of non-metallic minerals, the production of furniture, food and beverages, and the fabrication of metallurgical products.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> The Cenibra cellulose plant in Belo Oriente is supported by eucalyptus extraction.<ref name="PDDI1_42a49"/> In 2023, the IBGE reported 2,265 cubic meters of eucalyptus logs extracted, with 84.55% used for paper and cellulose production.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/16/12705 |title=Vegetable Extraction and Forestry |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102135234/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/16/12705 |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> In 2010, 0.39% of workers were in extractive industries and 21.42% in manufacturing.<ref name="PNUD_Atlas"/>

== Housing and services ==
[[File:ETE Ribeirão Ipanema, Ipatinga MG2.JPG|thumb|right|[[Wastewater treatment plant]] operated by Copasa, located near the mouth of the Ipanema stream]]
In 2022, there were 85,709 occupied, permanent private households in Ipatinga. Of these, 60,240 were houses (70.28%), 24,792 were apartments (28.93%), 195 were townhouses or condominiums (0.23%), 411 were [[tenement]]s (0.48%), and 71 were dilapidated or unfinished structures (0.08%).<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Of the total occupied households, 53,341 were owned (62.24%), with 49,075 fully paid and 4,266 under acquisition; 26,748 were [[Renting|rented]] (31.21%); 5,445 were borrowed or provided (6.35%); and 162 were occupied in other forms (0.19%).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3131307/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |title=Tabela 9930 - Domicílios particulares permanentes ocupados, por número de cômodos, condição de ocupação do domicílio e tipo do domicílio |author=Sistema IBGE de Recuperação Automática (SIDRA) |date=2022 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250102140414/https://sidra.ibge.gov.br/geratabela?format=xlsx&name=tabela9930.xlsx&terr=N&rank=-&query=t/9930/n6/3131307/v/all/p/all/c65/95810/c63/1055,1058,4343,73126,73553,73554,95826/c125/2932/d/v1000381%202/l/v,p%2Bc65%2Bc63,t%2Bc125 |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> The Municipal Master Plan sets out the geographical and urban parameters for the construction of residences, buildings and commercial establishments. Among various aspects, the master plan regulates the occupation and [[verticalization]] of the city. Certain neighborhoods, such as Cariru, Castelo, and Das Águas, have specific legislation prohibiting the construction of high-rise buildings.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/88230-6/cidades/novos-parametros-para-construcoes |title=Novos parâmetros para construções |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2014-12-04 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713185345/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/88230-6/cidades/novos-parametros-para-construcoes |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2014/05/apos-anos-de-debate-plano-diretor-e-aprovado-em-ipatinga.html |title=Após anos de debate Plano Diretor é aprovado em Ipatinga |author=Patrícia Belo |date=2014-05-10 |publisher=G1 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713185614/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2014/05/apos-anos-de-debate-plano-diretor-e-aprovado-em-ipatinga.html |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref>

Water supply and sewage collection services are provided by the Minas Gerais Sanitation Company (Copasa).<ref name="PDDI1_198a202"/> In 2022, according to IBGE, 90.09% of households relied on the general water network as their primary water supply, and 99.93% had exclusive-use bathrooms. Regarding [[sewage treatment]], 98.54% of households were served by the general or stormwater sewer system.<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> The water used to supply the Vale do Aço region originates from an alluvial aquifer located underground, extracted and treated at the Copasa [[water treatment plant]] in the Amaro Lanari neighborhood of Coronel Fabriciano.<ref name="JVA_Seca"/> Sewage treatment is facilitated by four [[wastewater treatment plants]] distributed throughout the municipality.<ref name="PDDI1_198a202">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv1|PDDI|2014|pp=198–202}}</ref>

Electricity supply is managed by the [[CEMIG|Energy Company of Minas Gerais]] (Cemig), which serves much of the state of Minas Gerais.<ref name="cidadesnet.com"/> According to IBGE, 99.9% of households had access to the electrical network in 2010.<ref name="PDDI3_580">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|p=580}}</ref> Regarding waste disposal, the 2022 census indicated that 99.82% of households were served by [[waste collection]] services.<ref name="IBGE_Censo2022"/> Until 1988, the city's waste was disposed of in an open-air [[landfill]], which was later treated and covered.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.saneamentobasico.com.br/portal/index.php/arquivo/antigo-lixao-de-ipatinga-vira-aterro-sanitario/ |title=Antigo lixão de Ipatinga vira aterro sanitário |author=Portal Saneamento Básico |date=2000-06-19 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713190014/http://www.saneamentobasico.com.br/portal/index.php/arquivo/antigo-lixao-de-ipatinga-vira-aterro-sanitario/ |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref> In 2003, the Vale do Aço Waste Center (CRVA) was inaugurated in Santana do Paraíso, handling waste from Ipatinga and other municipalities in the Vale do Aço region.<ref name="PDDI1_198a202"/> In addition, [[waste picker]]s maintain an informal system for collecting [[Recycling|recyclable materials]].<ref name="PDDI2_431a433">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=431–433}}</ref>

== Transportation ==
=== Railway ===
[[File:Vista da Estação Intendente Câmara, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Intendente Câmara Station]]
Ipatinga is served by the [[Vitória-Minas Railway]] (EFVM), managed by [[Vale S.A.|Vale]], which is a key route for transporting Usiminas production to the [[Port of Tubarão]] in Espírito Santo.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,usiminas-fecha-contrato-de-logistica-de-r900-mi-com-vale,461840 |title=Usiminas fecha contrato de logística de R$900 mi com Vale |author=Alberto Alegeri |date=2009-11-05 |publisher=Estadão |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713190657/http://www.estadao.com.br/noticias/geral,usiminas-fecha-contrato-de-logistica-de-r900-mi-com-vale,461840 |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref> The Intendente Câmara Station, located in the Ferroviários neighborhood, facilitates passenger transport with daily stops for trains traveling between the metropolitan areas of [[Greater Vitória|Vitória]] and [[Greater Belo Horizonte|Belo Horizonte]]. The EFVM is the cheapest and safest regular public transport option for several cities with stations.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/intendente.htm |title=Intendente Câmara |author=Estações Ferroviárias do Brasil |date=2014-01-16 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121208123118/http://www.estacoesferroviarias.com.br/efvm/intendente.htm |archive-date=2012-12-08}}</ref>

Railway transport has been integral to Ipatinga since its early days, with the construction of the Pedra Mole Station near the Cariru neighborhood in 1922. A change in the EFVM route led to the construction of a new terminal in the city center in 1930 to replace the original,<ref name="Cronologia"/> but another alteration in the railway layout resulted in its deactivation in 1951 and the establishment of the current Intendente Câmara Station in 1960. The Pedra Mole Station was designated a cultural heritage site in 1996,<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/> but remained abandoned until 2019, when its ruins were opened for visitation.<ref name="Diário_PedraMole"/> The former Ipatinga Station in the city center was also designated a cultural heritage site in 1991 and converted into a museum in 1992.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/>

=== Air and road ===
[[File:Vista do Terminal Rodoviário de Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|View of the Ipatinga Bus Terminal]]
Air transport in the municipality is facilitated by the [[Vale do Aço Regional Airport|Ipatinga Airport]] {{Airport codes|IPN|SBIP}}, known as the Vale do Aço Regional Airport (formerly Usiminas Airport), located in the neighboring municipality of [[Santana do Paraíso]]. Built by Usiminas, it is approximately 3&nbsp;km from Ipatinga's city center and serves the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region with daily flights to the Belo Horizonte Metropolitan Area, with potential for various connections, though it previously offered other destinations.<ref name="Unileste_Regional">{{cite web |url=http://issuu.com/danielalima53/docs/tcc_pronto |title=Aeroporto Regional do Vale do Aço |author=Daniele Lima |date=2015 |pages=16–22 |publisher=Centro Universitário Católica do Leste de Minas Gerais (Unileste) |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-date=13 March 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160313013433/http://issuu.com/danielalima53/docs/tcc_pronto |url-status=dead }}</ref> The airport covers an area of about 700,000 m², with an asphalt runway measuring 2,004 m in length and 45 m in width,<ref name="JVA_Revitalizado">{{cite web |url=http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94558 |title=Usiminas entrega aeroporto revitalizado |author=Jornal Vale do Aço |date=2011-12-23 |access-date=2017-09-14 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120905131656/http://www.jvaonline.com.br/novo_site/ler_noticia.php?id=94558 |archive-date=2012-09-05 |url-status=dead}}</ref> and an [[Airport apron|apron]] measuring 72.5 m x 285 m.<ref name="Unileste_Regional"/>

Ipatinga has one of the largest [[Bus station|bus terminals]] in the region, located in the city center, with regular daily departures to major cities in Minas Gerais and beyond.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rodoviariadeipatinga.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=107&Itemid=111 |title=Horários e Tarifas |author=Terminal Rodoviário de Ipatinga |access-date=2010-04-12 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231233431/http://www.rodoviariadeipatinga.com.br/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=107&Itemid=111 |archive-date=2012-12-31}}</ref> The city is intersected by the [[Rodovia Fernão Dias|BR-381]], the main access route to the state capital and [[Espírito Santo]]; it is also the starting point of the [[BR-458 (Brazil highway)|BR-458]], connecting the Vale do Aço to the [[BR-116 (Brazil highway)|BR-116]], and the [[List of state highways in Minas Gerais|MG-232]], linking to Santana do Paraíso and the [[MG-010 (Minas Gerais highway)|MG-010]].<ref name="Google">[http://maps.google.com.br/ Google Maps]. Accessed on 13 July 2016.</ref> The BR-381 runs parallel to the EFVM, and this axis has guided the urban occupation and expansion process since the second half of the 20th century.<ref name="PDDI1_42a49"/><ref name="PDDI1_80a85"/> Although the urban section of the BR-381 has dual carriageways, the lack of duplication along most of its length causes access to the city to be hindered by heavy traffic.<ref name="PDDI1_63a65"/>

=== Urban ===
[[File:Avenida Pedro Linhares Gomes e Usiminas ao fundo, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|left|Pedro Linhares Gomes Avenue, urban section of [[Rodovia Fernão Dias|BR-381]], in the Iguaçu neighborhood]]
In 2023, the municipal vehicle fleet totaled 164,028, comprising 92,367 cars, 39,766 motorcycles, 11,028 pickup trucks, 5,259 vans, 3,496 trailers, 3,343 trucks, 2,542 mopeds, 1,706 utility vehicles, 1,073 semi-trailers, 991 mopeds, 846 buses, 833 truck-tractors, 665 minibuses, 53 wheeled tractors, 41 tricycles, five sidecars, two platform chassis, and 12 other vehicle types.<ref name="IBGE_Frota">{{cite web |url=https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/22/28120 |title=Frota |author=IBGE Cidades |date=2023 |access-date=2025-01-02 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250102140606/https://cidades.ibge.gov.br/brasil/mg/ipatinga/pesquisa/22/28120 |archive-date=2025-01-02}}</ref> The growing number of vehicles in Ipatinga contributes to increasingly slow traffic, particularly in the city center.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.otempo.com.br/cidades/problemas-de-tr%C3%A2nsito-t%C3%ADpicos-de-capital-chegam-ao-interior-1.961327 |title=Problemas de trânsito típicos de capital chegam ao interior |author=Joana Suarez |date=2015-12-15 |publisher=O Tempo |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713191146/http://www.otempo.com.br/cidades/problemas-de-tr%C3%A2nsito-t%C3%ADpicos-de-capital-chegam-ao-interior-1.961327 |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref>

Near commercial hubs, parking availability is often limited, impacting local businesses. Consequently, measures such as paid parking systems have been implemented.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=51642 |title=Vagas de estacionamento no Horto motivam novas reclamações |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2011-01-12 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160512214257/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=51642 |archive-date=2016-05-12 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/101881-6/cidades/ipatinga-tera-novo-modelo-de-estacionamento-rotativo |title=Ipatinga terá novo modelo de estacionamento rotativo |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2016-03-11 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160512214514/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/101881-6/cidades/ipatinga-tera-novo-modelo-de-estacionamento-rotativo |archive-date=2016-05-12}}</ref> From January to November 2023, Ipatinga recorded 4,020 traffic accidents, the eighth highest in Minas Gerais, with 83.34% classified as minor and 15.73% as serious.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://plox.com.br/noticia/06/12/2023/ipatinga-registra-mais-de-4-mil-acidentes-em-2023-a-8a-cidade-de-mg |title=Ipatinga registra mais de 4 mil acidentes em 2023; a 8ª cidade no ranking de MG |author=Plox |date=2023-12-06 |access-date=2024-04-01 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240401201054/https://plox.com.br/noticia/06/12/2023/ipatinga-registra-mais-de-4-mil-acidentes-em-2023-a-8a-cidade-de-mg |archive-date=2024-04-01}}</ref> The Pedro Linhares Gomes Avenue, the urban section of BR-381, recorded the highest number of accidents in the Vale do Aço from 2014 to 2024, with 4,000 incidents, followed by Avenida José Selim de Sales with 2,101.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0116656-av-pedro-linhares-gomes-e-a-via-com-maior-numero-de-acidentes-no-vale-do-aco-na-ultima-decada |title=Av. Pedro Linhares Gomes é a via com maior número de acidentes no Vale do Aço na última década |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2024-06-25 |access-date=2024-07-12 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240712220948/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0116656-av-pedro-linhares-gomes-e-a-via-com-maior-numero-de-acidentes-no-vale-do-aco-na-ultima-decada |archive-date=2024-07-12}}</ref>

Public transportation is managed by Saritur, operating 55 urban and rural bus lines with 108 vehicles, as of July 2015.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://blogs.uai.com.br/faixaexclusiva/donos_do_transporte_mantem_participacao_em_cidades_medias_e_grandes_de_minas/ |title=Donos do transporte mantém participação em cidades médias e grandes de Minas |author=Bruno Rodrigues |work=Faixa Exclusiva |date=2015-07-09 |publisher=Uai |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713191238/http://blogs.uai.com.br/faixaexclusiva/donos_do_transporte_mantem_participacao_em_cidades_medias_e_grandes_de_minas/ |archive-date=2016-07-13}}</ref> Viação Univale provides intermunicipal lines connecting Ipatinga to other municipalities in the Vale do Aço Metropolitan Region and some surrounding areas.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=51371 |title=Passagem intermunicipal está 5,86% mais cara |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2010-12-29 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713191436/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=51371 |archive-date=2016-07-13 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The city also has an extensive [[bike lane]] network, approximately 40 kilometers long, according to 2015 municipal data.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/pol%C3%ADtica-e-economia/obras-melhorias-ciclovia-avenida-ipatinga |title=Obras de melhorias em ciclovia em avenida de Ipatinga |author=Plox |date=2015-04-14 |access-date=2016-07-13 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160713191503/http://www.plox.com.br/caderno/pol%C3%ADtica-e-economia/obras-melhorias-ciclovia-avenida-ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-13 |url-status=dead}}</ref> However, sidewalk infrastructure is deficient in many neighborhoods, with issues such as irregularities, obstacles, and discontinuous surfaces.<ref name="PDDI2_327a328">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv2|PDDI|2014|pp=327–328}}</ref>

== Culture ==
[[File:Vista da Igreja São Vicente de Paulo, Ipatinga MG.jpg|thumb|right|The São Vicente de Paulo Church in Ipaneminha, an example of a [[heritage site|listed]] municipal [[cultural heritage]] asset.]]
When [[Usiminas]] was established, the only leisure options in Ipatinga were small [[bar (establishment)|bars]]. Heavy work at the plant, long lines at restaurants, cultural differences, and distance from families strained relations among residents, many of whom came from diverse regions of Brazil.<ref name="IFCH_63a69"/> These factors contributed to the unrest that culminated in the [[Ipatinga massacre|Ipatinga Massacre]] in 1963, prompting the adoption of social assistance policies to mitigate workplace conflicts, including increased investment in leisure facilities.<ref name="UCAM_34e35"/>

For the 2024 ICMS Cultural Tax calculation, Ipatinga's cultural policy score was 3.60 on a scale of 0 to 4, with a total score of 16.84 based on factors such as conservation, expenditure, and the number of listed or registered heritage assets.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |title=ICMS Critério do Patrimônio Cultural |author=Instituto Estadual do Patrimônio Histórico e Artístico de Minas Gerais (IEPHA) |page=16 |access-date=2023-09-06 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230906154209/http://www.iepha.mg.gov.br/images/ICMS_2023/Pontuacao_DEFINITIVA_exercicio_2024-v1.1_-_com_logos.pdf |archive-date=2023-09-06}}</ref> In addition to its work in public administration, Usiminas supports arts and cultural facilities, including those funded through the [[Rouanet Law|Federal Cultural Incentive Law]].<ref name="IFCH_199"/><ref name="Plox_CircuitoUsiminas">{{cite web |url=http://www.plox.com.br/cultura/circuito-usiminas-de-cultura-movimenta-ipatinga-e-santana-do-paraiso |title=Circuito Usiminas de Cultura movimenta Ipatinga e Santana do Paraíso |author=Plox |date=2016-03-22 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160703193809/http://www.plox.com.br/cultura/circuito-usiminas-de-cultura-movimenta-ipatinga-e-santana-do-paraiso |archive-date=2016-07-03 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Ipatinga has a municipal culture council and a heritage preservation council,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=638&ordem=6.2&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Conselho municipal de cultura e de preservação do patrimônio |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707212612/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=638&ordem=6.2&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref> with provisions for [[heritage listing]] of tangible and intangible assets. As of 2020, listed tangible assets included: the São Vicente de Paulo Church (Ipaneminha Church), the Nossa Senhora do Rosário Dance Club (headquarters of the Ipaneminha Congado), the Fazendinha in the Ferroviários neighborhood, the ruins of the former Pedra Mole Station, the Old Ipatinga Railway Station (Zeza Souto Memory Station), the Iron Bridge over the Ipanema River between Centro and Veneza, the Railway Workers' Houses in Veneza, the Nossa Senhora da Esperança Church (Horto Church), the Zélia Olguin Theater, the [[Ficus elastica|rubber fig]] tree in Cariru, the former Grande Hotel Ipatinga building in Castelo, the Olguin Academy, the [[Ipanema Park]], and the Pouso de Água Limpa Station complex. The Nossa Senhora do Rosário Congado (Ipaneminha Congado) was the only listed intangible asset.<ref name="Cartilha_5">{{Harvtxt|Arquitetura OG|2020|p=5}}</ref>

=== Cultural events and spaces ===
[[File:Entrada do Centro Cultural Usiminas, Ipatinga MG2.JPG|thumb|left|Entrance to the Usiminas Cultural Center, located next to the Vale do Aço Shopping Mall.]]
Ipatinga boasts a rich and diverse [[folklore]]. According to 2012 IBGE data, the city is home to artistic groups specialising in theatre, dance, choirs, folklore, and music.<ref name="IBGE_Grupos">{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3762&ordem=8.3&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Grupos artísticos |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707212826/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3762&ordem=8.3&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref> A prominent cultural event is the Ipaneminha Congado, a traditional [[fandango]] group that performs marches in honor of [[Our Lady of the Rosary]] during festive occasions. This tradition, derived from African roots, was established in 1925 by descendants of muleteers.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio">{{cite web |url=http://www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/10422/Cartilha-do-Patrimonio-Cultural |title=Cartilha do Patrimônio Cultural |author=Prefeitura |date=2013-01-02 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151017070338/http://www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/Materia_especifica/10422/Cartilha-do-Patrimonio-Cultural |archive-date=2015-10-17}}</ref> Other popular cultural events include the Folia de Reis, the Feast of the Divine, the Feast of the Rosary, [[Bumba Meu Boi]], [[Festa Junina|June festivals]], and the Banana Festival.<ref name="PDDI3_663–665">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=663–665}}</ref><ref name="IBGE_Festas">{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3737&ordem=8.5&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Principais festas populares |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2005 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707212907/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3737&ordem=8.5&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref> In [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], certain parishes create carpets in local neighbourhoods.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/94046-6/cidades/corpus-christi-e-um-dia-santo-e-nao-um-feriado |title="Corpus Christi é um dia santo e não um feriado" |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2015-06-03 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707213137/http://diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/94046-6/cidades/corpus-christi-e-um-dia-santo-e-nao-um-feriado |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref>

[[Handicraft]]s are an important part of Ipatinga's cultural identity. According to the IBGE, the most common crafts are [[embroidery]] and the production of leather and metal artefacts.<ref name="IBGE_Artesanato">{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=679&ordem=6.4&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Principais atividades artesanais |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2012 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707213228/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=679&ordem=6.4&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref> In rural areas, [[ceramics]] and mosaic crafts complement agrotourism activities. Rural tourism also involves sampling the local cuisine, which includes homemade jams, liqueurs, honey, cheeses, breads, desserts, and meals prepared on [[wood-burning stove]]s. These products can be enjoyed at farms, properties, and rural establishments open to visitors.<ref name="EstadodeMinas_Turismo">{{cite web |url=https://turismo.uai.com.br/destino-descoberta/muito-alem-do-vale-do-aco-descubra-o-ipatinga-rural/ |title=Muito além do Vale do Aço: Descubra o Ipatinga Rural |author=Iolanda Loiola |date=2023-11-03 |publisher=Uai Turismo |access-date=2024-04-01 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240401201118/https://turismo.uai.com.br/destino-descoberta/muito-alem-do-vale-do-aco-descubra-o-ipatinga-rural/ |archive-date=2024-04-01}}</ref>

Cultural spaces dedicated to preserving and promoting popular traditions include municipal libraries, theaters, stadiums, sports complexes, cinemas, clubs, and recreational associations, as reported by IBGE in 2005 and 2012.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3737&ordem=8.2&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Equipamentos culturais |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2005 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707213336/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2005&node=3737&ordem=8.2&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=657&ordem=6.3&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |title=Equipamentos culturais e meios de comunicação |author=Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE) |date=2005 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707213914/http://munic.ibge.gov.br/ver_tema.php?periodo=2012&node=657&ordem=6.3&munic=313130&uf=31&nome=Ipatinga |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref> The old central railway station, opened in 1930 and deactivated in 1951, was repurposed as a museum in 1992. Named the Zeza Souto Memory Station, it hosts cultural activities and historical documents related to the city.<ref name="Prefeitura_Patrimônio"/> Under the Federal Cultural Incentive Law, Usiminas, in partnership with the municipality, organizes diverse programs, including workshops, cultural performances, and cinema screenings, held in schools and public squares for the general population.<ref name="Plox_CircuitoUsiminas"/> The municipality supports initiatives such as the Tenente Oswaldo Machado Music School and the Sete de Outubro Theater School, offering classes for various age groups.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo">{{cite web |url=http://www.ipatinga-mg.com.br/turismo.php |title=O Turismo em Ipatinga |author=Prefeitura |access-date=2010-03-29 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231155303/http://www.ipatinga-mg.com.br/turismo.php |archive-date=2012-12-31 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
[[File:Lago da Usipa, Ipatinga MG.JPG|thumb|right|Lake at Usipa]]

Through the Usiminas Institute, Usiminas promotes projects and maintains spaces dedicated to the arts, such as the Usiminas Cultural Center, which serves the region with cultural workshops, a diverse theater program, an art gallery, and a library.<ref name="PDDI3_654a655">{{Harvtxt|ref=PDDIv3|PDDI|2014|pp=654–655}}</ref> Located next to the [[Shopping Vale do Aço (Mall)|Vale do Aço Shopping Mall]], its theater is among Brazil's most modern and received the ''Herity'' certification in 2015, recognized by [[UNESCO]] for its conservation, quality, and relevance.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.plox.com.br/acontece/centro-cultural-usiminas-recebe-certificacao-internacional |title=Centro Cultural Usiminas recebe certificação internacional |author=Plox |date=2015-12-08 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707214015/http://www.plox.com.br/acontece/centro-cultural-usiminas-recebe-certificacao-internacional |archive-date=2016-07-07 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The Zélia Olguin Theater in the Cariru neighborhood is also managed by Instituto Usiminas.<ref name="PDDI3_654a655"/> The former Grande Hotel Ipatinga building in the Castelo neighborhood was repurposed by Usiminas as the Usiminas Memory Center, inaugurated in 2021.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/economia/2021/10/26/internas_economia,1317346/centro-de-pesquisa-da-usiminas-completa-50-anos-com-desenvolvimento-do-aco.shtml |title=Centro de Pesquisa da Usiminas completa 50 anos, com desenvolvimento do aço |author=Mariana Costa |date=2021-10-26 |publisher=Jornal Estado de Minas |access-date=2023-09-06 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230906155540/https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/economia/2021/10/26/internas_economia,1317346/centro-de-pesquisa-da-usiminas-completa-50-anos-com-desenvolvimento-do-aco.shtml |archive-date=2023-09-06}}</ref>

In addition to hosting the Usiminas Cultural Center, the Vale do Aço Shopping Mall is the region's largest leisure and entertainment facility, with its cinemas being the area's main venues.<ref name="PDDI3_689a691e693"/> Ipatinga's cultural spaces frequently host regionally or nationally significant events, such as the Theater and Dance Popularization Campaign<ref>{{cite web |url=http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2013/02/ipatinga-recebe-10-campanha-de-popularizacao-do-teatro-e-da-danca.html |title=Ipatinga recebe a 10ª Campanha de Popularização do Teatro e da Dança |author=InterTV dos Vales |date=2013-02-21 |publisher=G1 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141012202410/http://g1.globo.com/mg/vales-mg/noticia/2013/02/ipatinga-recebe-10-campanha-de-popularizacao-do-teatro-e-da-danca.html |archive-date=2014-10-12}}</ref> and the Ipatinga Live Jazz Festival.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=6766 |title=Ipatinga Live Jazz começa na sexta com João Bosco |author=Jornal Diário Popular |date=2013-11-06 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304192935/http://www.diariopopularmg.com.br/vis_noticia.aspx?id=6766 |archive-date=2016-03-04}}</ref> At Usipa, events such as the Xerimbabo Project (focused on environmental awareness) and the Expo Usipa (a local business exhibition) occur regularly.<ref name="PDDI3_689a691e693"/> According to the Minas Gerais State Culture Department, the city's vibrant events, theater groups, and diverse activities make it one of the state's main cultural hubs.<ref name="Diário_Polo">{{cite web |url=http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=58420 |title=Cultura é um bom negócio |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2011-10-16 |access-date=2016-07-06 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231223227/http://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticias.aspx?cd=58420 |archive-date=2012-12-31 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Landmarks and attractions ===
[[File:Cachoeira e piscina natural no Parque das Cachoeiras, Ipatinga MG.jpg|thumb|left|Waterfall and natural pool at the Parque das Cachoeiras]]
Ipatinga is part of the Atlantic Forest Tourist Circuit of Minas Gerais, established in 2010 by the Minas Gerais State Tourism Department to promote tourism in member cities.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |title=Circuitos Turísticos de Minas Gerais |author=Secretaria de Turismo de Minas Gerais |date=2013-03-18 |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731133231/http://www.turismo.mg.gov.br/images/stories/circuitos/lista-circuitos.pdf |archive-date=2013-07-31}}</ref> The main natural attractions include trails, forests, lagoons, and waterfalls in rural areas, some equipped with visitor infrastructure. Particularly in the environmental preservation areas and headwaters of the Ipanema stream sub-basin, there are farms, lodges, and guesthouses fostering [[rural tourism]] and [[ecotourism]].<ref name="EstadodeMinas_Turismo"/><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.aetri.com.br/index.php/aetri |title=AETRI |author=Associação dos Empreendedores do Turismo Rural de Ipatinga (AETRI) |access-date=2016-07-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707214045/http://www.aetri.com.br/index.php/aetri |archive-date=2016-07-07}}</ref>

In rural areas, the privately managed Parque das Cachoeiras stands out, covering approximately 5 million m² with camping areas, chalets, and a restaurant.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/> At Fazenda do Zaca, native [[Atlantic Forest]] preservation is complemented by trails and gardens open to visitors.<ref name="EstadodeMinas_Turismo"/> Recognized as a [[Private natural heritage reserve (Brazil)|private natural heritage reserve]] (RPPN), it hosts environmental awareness campaigns for schools and the public.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0024864-fazenda-do-zaca-investe-na-educacao-e-conscientizacao |title=Fazenda do Zaca investe na educação e conscientização |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2009-06-06 |access-date=2024-04-01 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240401201222/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0024864-fazenda-do-zaca-investe-na-educacao-e-conscientizacao |archive-date=2024-04-01}}</ref> At Sítio Recanto Vovô Teixeira, built in the 1930s, artisanal [[molasses]] and [[brown sugar]] are produced using a preserved [[Engenho|sugar mill]]. The property also produces [[cornmeal]] with a [[water mill]] from its founding era. Empório da Serra is notable for its cheese production and wood-stove meals, available during visits.<ref name="EstadodeMinas_Turismo"/>
[[File:Vista parcial do Parque Ipanema, Ipatinga MG2.JPG|thumb|right|Partial view of the Ipanema Park]]

The urban area also features leisure facilities that attract visitors from other cities.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/> The [[Ipanema Park]], one of Brazil's largest urban green spaces, was one of the last projects by landscape architect [[Roberto Burle Marx]], covering over 1 million m² with 12,000 planted trees. The park includes leisure amenities such as the Science Park, where visitors can observe or interact with physical, biological, chemical, or astronomical phenomena, as well as a playground, sports courts, soccer fields, walking trails, bike lanes, and an amphitheater.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/> Its surroundings include the Municipal Nursery, the Emerson Fittipaldi Kart Track, the Sete de Outubro Sports and Cultural Center, and the João Lamego Netto Municipal Stadium (''Ipatingão'').<ref name="Cartilha_60a63">{{Harvtxt|Arquitetura OG|2020|pp=60–63}}</ref> The Caminho das Águas Railway, spanning 2.6&nbsp;km, connects Ipanema Park to the Pouso de Água Limpa Station, a small tourist train station.<ref name="Cartilha_65a67">{{Harvtxt|Arquitetura OG|2020|pp=65–67}}</ref>

Usipa, in addition to its Biodiversity Center with a mini zoo garden and botanical garden, offers ecological trails, a water park, sports areas, snack bars, and children's leisure spaces.<ref name="Prefeitura_Turismo"/> The Vale do Aço Shopping Mall, as previously noted, is the largest shopping center in eastern Minas Gerais and among the largest in the state's interior, serving as the primary metropolitan leisure and entertainment hub.<ref name="PDDI3_689a691e693"/> In the Cariru neighborhood, the restored ruins of the Pedra Mole Station have been open to visitors since 2019, featuring a trail with informational signs, the station's remnants, and a lookout point for viewing the confluence of the Piracicaba and Doce rivers.<ref name="Diário_PedraMole">{{cite web |url=https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0074077-estaaao-de-pedra-mole-a-restaurada |title=Estação de Pedra Mole é restaurada |author=Jornal Diário do Aço |date=2019-12-10 |access-date=2020-01-26 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20200126192241/https://www.diariodoaco.com.br/noticia/0074077-estaaao-de-pedra-mole-a-restaurada |archive-date=2020-01-26}}</ref>

=== Holidays and symbols ===
In Ipatinga, there are three municipal holidays and eight national holidays, in addition to optional holidays. The municipal holidays are the city’s anniversary, celebrated on 29 April; [[Feast of Corpus Christi|Corpus Christi]], which in 2025 is observed on 19 June; and the [[Assumption of Mary|Assumption of Our Lady]], on 15 August.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/abrir_arquivo.aspx/CALENDARIO_DE_FERIADOS_MUNICIPAIS_-_2016.?cdLocal=2&arquivo=%7BD21EE521-82DE-AC44-4C84-46E76BC2ADED%7D.pdf |archive-url=https://archive.today/20160707195348/http://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:KJsEHG-Ei-UJ:www.ipatinga.mg.gov.br/abrir_arquivo.aspx/CALENDARIO_DE_FERIADOS_MUNICIPAIS_-_2016.%3FcdLocal%3D2%26arquivo%3D%257BD21EE521-82DE-AC44-4C84-46E76BC2ADED%257D.pdf+&cd=1&hl=pt-BR&ct=clnk&gl=br |archive-date=2016-07-07 |title=Feriados federais e municipais 2016 no município de Ipatinga |author=Prefeitura |access-date=2016-07-07}}</ref>

The municipality also has official symbols, namely the [[flag]] and the [[coat of arms]]. The flag consists of the municipal coat of arms centered on a rectangle with blue, red, and green colors, which are the city’s official colors. The coat of arms was established by Law No. 88 of 6 September 1967<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/normas-juridicas/93?slug=institui-o-brasao-do-municipio-de-ipatinga |title=Law No. 88 of 06/09/1967 |author=Câmara Municipal |date=1967-09-06 |access-date=2025-02-27 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250227191655/https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/normas-juridicas/93?slug=institui-o-brasao-do-municipio-de-ipatinga |archive-date=2025-02-27}}</ref> and amended by Law No. 2,541 of 4 June 2009.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/normas-juridicas/8413?slug=dispoe-sobre-a-forma-e-a-apresentacao-dos-simbolos-do-municipio-de-ipatinga-e-da-outras-providencias |title=Law No. 2,541 of 04/06/2009 |author=Câmara Municipal |date=2009-06-04 |access-date=2025-02-27 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250227191839/https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/normas-juridicas/8413?slug=dispoe-sobre-a-forma-e-a-apresentacao-dos-simbolos-do-municipio-de-ipatinga-e-da-outras-providencias |archive-date=2025-02-27}}</ref> It features a crown symbolizing strength and authority, with a star representing Barra Alegre, the municipality’s only district. Below, a steel ladle in the shape of a shield symbolizes the steel industry, surrounded by two branches representing the laurels of victory. At the center of the emblem is a globe with two stylized human figures, symbolizing the unity of peoples and technology, and a ribbon bearing the motto "Trust, Work, and Progress."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/brasao |title=Coat of arms |author=Câmara Municipal |date=2024-04-23 |access-date=2025-02-27 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250227191905/https://www.camaraipatinga.mg.gov.br/brasao |archive-date=2025-02-27}}</ref>
