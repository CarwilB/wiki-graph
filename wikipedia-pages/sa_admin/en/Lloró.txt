'''Lloró''' is a municipality and town in the [[Chocó Department]], [[Colombia]]. It claims the second world record for highest average annual precipitation with {{convert|12717|mm|0|abbr=on}},<ref>http://www.banrepcultural.org/blaavirtual/faunayflora/pacific1/cap10.htm {{Dead link|date=February 2022}}</ref><ref name="Lacerda2004">{{cite book|author=Luiz Drude de Lacerda|title=Environmental Geochemistry in Tropical and Subtropical Environments|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=3th0bNos0OcC&pg=PA177|date=9 February 2004|publisher=Springer|isbn=978-3-540-42540-3|pages=177–}}</ref> after [[López de Micay]], which holds an also disputed record with {{convert|12892.4|mm|0|abbr=on}}.<ref>{{Cite web

If accurate, that would make it the wettest place in the world.<ref>[http://www.ncdc.noaa.gov/oa/climate/globalextremes.html#highpre NCDC: Global Measured Extremes of Temperature and Precipitation<!-- Bot generated title -->]</ref> Although “lloró” is the 3rd person singular [[preterite]] tense of the Spanish verb “llorar” (to cry) which may colloquially be compared to rain, this similarity is merely incidental; the town is named for Gioró, a pre-Columbian indigenous chief.

== Climate ==
Lloró has a very wet [[tropical rainforest climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Af]]). The town of Lloró itself has only 8000&nbsp;mm of rain but the farm located to the east of the city has 12,892.4&nbsp;mm.
