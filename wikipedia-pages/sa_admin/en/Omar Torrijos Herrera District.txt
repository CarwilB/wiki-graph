'''Omar Torrijos Herrera''' is a [[Districts of Panama|district]] in the [[Panama]]nian [[Provinces of Panama|province]] of [[Colón Province|Colón]], located approximately {{convert|115|km}} west of [[Panama City]]. It is named after [[Omar Torrijos Herrera]], ''de facto'' leader of Panama from 1968 to 1981.

==Geography==
Omar Torrijos Herrera is located in the western part of Colón Province on the Atlantic side of the Panamanian cordillera. It borders the [[Donoso District]] of Colón Province to the west and north, and [[La Pintada District]] of [[Coclé Province]] to the east and south. The municipality covers an area of {{convert|198.6|km2}}.<ref name=decree/>

The terrain is mountainous with steep slopes, and covered by rainforest.<ref name=study>{{cite report |last1=Quinn |first1=Courtney |last2=Pouliot |first2=Jeanne |title=Long-term Monitoring Impact Assessment of Mining Activity in Panama |url=https://www.mcgill.ca/pfss/files/pfss/long-term_monitoring_impact_assessment_of_mining_activity_in_panama_0.pdf |year=2014 |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> The [[Cocle del Norte River]] forms the district's eastern border with La Pintada. The district lies within the Donoso Multiple-Use [[Protected areas of Panama|protected area]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.protectedplanet.net/107292 |title=Donoso |publisher=[[UNEP-WCMC]] |work=[[World Database on Protected Areas]] |date=November 2020 |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> Deforestation and pollution caused by mining are serious environmental concerns.<ref>{{cite news |last=Arcia |first=José |translator-last=Castagnino |translator-first=Romina |date=24 December 2018 |url=https://news.mongabay.com/2018/12/copper-mine-destroying-forests-in-panamas-mesoamerican-biological-corridor/ |title=Copper mine destroying forests in Panama’s Mesoamerican Biological Corridor |publisher=[[Mongabay]] |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

==History==
The town of [[Coclesito]] was officially founded by Omar Torrijos on 8 August 1970. In 1971 he brought several heads of [[water buffalo]] from Trinidad and Tobago to set up an agricultural cooperative in Coclesito. The cooperative lasted until 1990, when the buffalo were divided between the households who had operated it. Nowadays mining has become the major industry in the area, and domestic tourism is also being developed.<ref name=history>{{cite news |date=11 December 2019 |title=Aspectos historicos |url=https://www.gacetaoficial.gob.pa/pdfTemp/28918_A/GacetaNo_28918a_20191211.pdf |language=es |newspaper=Official Digital Gazette of Panama |pages=102–104 |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref><ref name=stories>{{cite news |last=Burón-Barahona |first=Luis |date=8 October 2017 |url=https://www.prensa.com/impresa/panorama/Historias-Coclesito_0_4866263391.html |title=Historias de Coclesito |language=es |newspaper=[[La Prensa (Panama City)|La Prensa]] |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

On 20 February 2018, the [[National Assembly (Panama)|National Assembly]] decreed the establishment of Omar Torrijos Herrera District from the former ''corregimiento'' of [[San José del General]] in Donoso District.<ref name=decree/>

==Administration==
Omar Torrijos Herrera District is divided into three ''[[Corregimientos of Panama|corregimientos]]'': San José del General, San Juan de Turbe, and Nueva Esperanza.<ref name=decree/>
Omar Torrijos Herrera elected its first mayor, Eulalio Yángüez, in 2019.<ref>{{cite news |last=Jiménez |first=Thaylin |date=8 May 2019 |url=https://www.metrolibre.com/nacionales/134762-la-mayoria-de-alcaldes-electos-son-nuevos-11-lograron-la-reeleccion.html |title=La mayoría de alcaldes electos son nuevos; 11 lograron la reelección |language=es |newspaper=Metro Libre |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Census of Panama, the former ''corregimiento'' of San José del General (contiguous with the current Omar Torrijos Herrera District) recorded a population of 2248 inhabitants living in 380 households.<ref name=census/> The [[National Institute of Statistics and Census of Panama]] projected the population of the former ''corregimiento'' to reach 2789 by the year 2020.<ref name=projection/> Coclesito is the largest community in the district and has a population of roughly 1200.<ref name=study/>

Near the mining concessions are several communities inhabited by the indigenous [[Ngäbe]] people,<ref name=study/> who began moving into the area in the late 1980s and early 1990s.<ref>{{cite report |title=Supporting Communication to the Special Rapporteur on the rights of indigenous peoples |url=https://justiceprojectdotorg1.files.wordpress.com/2012/03/supporting-communication-jcap-mw-dsg-final-english-1.pdf |year=2012 |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

==Culture==
Omar Torrijos built a house in Coclesito in 1978. It was converted into a museum after his death, preserving his belongings and the original furnishings.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://coclesito.es.tl/Omar-Torrijos-y-La-Casa-Museo.htm |title=Omar Torrijos y La Casa Museo |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

Since 2014, Coclesito has celebrated an annual Water Buffalo Festival on 8 August, the anniversary of the founding of the town.<ref name=stories/>

==Economy and infrastructure==
In recent times, the economy of Omar Torrijos Herrera has been dominated by mining. The short-lived Molejón gold mine, approximately {{convert|10|km}} west of Coclesito, was the first modern [[Mineral industry of Panama|mine in Panama]].<ref>{{cite news |date=18 November 2009 |url=https://ca.proactiveinvestors.com/companies/news/71533/petaquilla-minerals-to-begin-commercial-production-at-panamas-first-modern-gold-mine-3037.html |title=Petaquilla Minerals to Begin Commercial Production at Panama’s First Modern Gold Mine |publisher=ProactiveInvestors |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> It was operated from 2010 to 2013 by Petaquilla Minerals.<ref>{{cite news |last=Bourdillon |first=Rémy |date=11 January 2018 |url=https://www.nationalobserver.com/2018/01/11/news/canadian-company-leaves-dust-and-debt-after-abandoning-its-gold-mine-panama |title=Canadian company leaves dust and debt after abandoning its gold mine in Panama |publisher=[[National Observer (Canada)|National Observer]] |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> Adjacent to it is the copper-producing [[Cobre mine, Panama|Cobre Panamá mine]], which was the largest single private sector investment in Panama's history<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/cobre-panama-copper-mine/ |title=Cobre Panama Copper Mine |publisher=NS Energy |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> and began operations in 2019.<ref>{{cite news |last=Jamasmie |first=Cecilia |date=25 June 2020 |url=https://www.mining.com/panama-miners-push-for-restart-of-first-quantums-copper-operation/ |title=Panama miners push for restart of First Quantum copper operation |publisher=mining.com |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref>

The only road connection between the district and the rest of Panama is the gravel road built in 2006 that runs from the mines through Coclesito south to [[La Pintada, Panama|La Pintada]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Camus |first=Yann |url=https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947121/000117625613000599/exhibit99-1.htm |title=NI 43-101 Palmilla Deposit Resource Update, Rio Belencillo Zone 1 Concession, Colon Province, Panama |publisher=[[SGS S.A.|SGS Canada]] |date=9 October 2013 |access-date=14 November 2020}}</ref> There was formerly an airfield at Coclesito, now closed,<ref name=stories/> which was the destination of the airplane carrying Omar Torrijos that [[1981 Panamanian Air Force Twin Otter crash|crashed nearby in 1981]].
