'''Tacaná''' is a town and [[Municipalities of Guatemala|municipality]] in the [[Guatemala]]n [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[San Marcos Department|San Marcos]].

==History==

{{Main|Tejutla, San Marcos}}

[[File:Pedrocortesylarraz.jpg|170px|thumb|left|Bishop [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]] portrait; he arrived to Tejutla in 1770.]]

In 1690, [[Tejutla, San Marcos|Tejutla]] had a large area and included the modern municipalities of [[Comitancillo]], [[Ixchiguán]], [[Concepción Tutuapa]], [[Sipacapa]], [[Sibinal]], [[Tajumulco]], Tacaná and part of what is now [[San Miguel Ixtahuacán]]. According to the historical writings from ''Recordación Florida'' of [[Francisco Antonio de Fuentes y Guzmán]], Tejutla belonged to [[Quetzaltenango Department]] and it was a "prosperous land with rich weathers and comfortable forest with enough water".{{sfn|Fuentes y Guzmán|1932|p=}}

Tejutla was an important commercial and religious center; in August 1767, Joseph Domingo Hidalgo described Santiago Tejutla as "El Curato" -i.e., the focal center of commerce of all the towns that were around it− in the ''Gaceta de Guatemala'', the official newspaper of the times.  Then, in the last quarter of the 18th century, bishop Dr. [[Pedro Cortés y Larraz]], who arrived from [[Cuilco]] in 1770 as part of the inspection he was doing of the Guatemalan dioceses, called Tejutla "Santiago en la Cima del Monte" (English: Santiago at the top of the hill" and reported that there were "sixty four families who lived very well" in the area.{{sfn|Cortés y Larraz|1770|p=}}

The Central American United Provinces constitution from 11 October 1821, showed Tejutla under modern San Marcos jurisdiction for the very first time.

In 1870 Tejutla reached "Villa" category and, due to its development, its authorities requested to the [[Congress of Guatemala|House of Representatives of Guatemala]] to be named a Department capital. The department was going to have the municipalities mentioned above, along with the modern municipalities of [[Cuilco]], [[Santa Bárbara, Huehuetenango|Santa Bárbara]] and  San Gaspar, Huehuetenango, from the modern [[Huehuetenango Department]]. Besides, in those days, [[Motocintla]], [[Cacahuatán]] and [[Tapachula]]—which would go definitively to [[Mexico]] in 1892 due to the [[Herrera-Mariscal treaty]]<ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5nFVAAAAMAAJ|title=Memoria sobre la cuestión de límites entre Guatemala y México|author=Comisión Guatemalteca de Límites con México|publisher=Tipografía Nacional|location=Guatemala|year=1900|pages=325|language=es}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal|year=1896|title=Nuestras fronteras|journal=[[La Ilustración Guatemalteca]]|url=https://archive.org/stream/ilustracionguate00guat#page/470/mode/2up|location=Guatemala|volume=I|issue=13|publisher=Síguere, Guirola y Cía|language=es}}</ref>— were under the jurisdiction of the [[Mercedarian]] convent located in Tejutla.  Furthermore, Tejutla even had House representatives of its own in those days.

But power shifted when the conservatives led by Field Marshal [[Vicente Cerna]] were defeated by the liberal forces of generals [[Miguel Garcia Granados]] and [[Justo Rufino Barrios]] −who was a [[San Lorenzo, San Marcos|San Lorenzo]] native;  once the liberals were in power, the expelled the regular clergy from Guatemala and abolished mandatory tithing for the [[secular clergy]], leaving Tejutla without their main administrative and leadership support, the curato.  In fact, Barrios government confiscated monasteries, large extensions of farm land, sugar mills and Indian doctrines from [[regular clergy|regular orders]] and then distributed it to his liberal friend and comrades, who became large landowners in the area.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Castellanos Cambranes|first=Julio|journal=500 años de lucha por la tierra. Estudios sobre propiedad rual y reforma agraria en Guatemala|title=5. Tendencias del desarrollo agrario en el siglo XIX y surgimiento de la propiedad capitalista de la tierra en Guatemala|volume=1|location=Guatemala|publisher=FLACSO|year=1992|url=http://www.flacsoandes.edu.ec/libros/digital/53099.pdf|language=es}}</ref>
