'''Cité Soleil''' ({{IPA|fr|site sɔlɛj}}; {{langx|ht|Site Solèy}}; English: {{italic correction|''Sun City''}}) is an extremely impoverished and densely populated [[List of communes of Haiti|commune]] located in the [[Port-au-Prince]] metropolitan area in [[Haiti]]. Cité Soleil originally developed as a [[shanty town]] and grew to an estimated 200,000 to 400,000 residents, the majority of whom live in [[extreme poverty]].<ref>{{cite web
|url=http://www.icrc.org/Web/Eng/siteeng0.nsf/html/photos-haiti-280606
|title=Cité-Soleil: Grinding poverty, relentless violence
|publisher=International Committee of the Red Cross
|access-date=2007-08-16
|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070930183538/http://www.icrc.org/Web/Eng/siteeng0.nsf/html/photos-haiti-280606 |archive-date = 2007-09-30}}
</ref> The area is generally regarded as one of the poorest and most dangerous areas of the [[Western Hemisphere]] and it is one of the biggest [[slum]]s in the [[Northern Hemisphere]]. The area has virtually no sewers and has a poorly maintained open canal system that serves as its sewage system, few formal businesses but many local commercial activities and enterprises, sporadic but largely unpaid-for electricity, a few hospitals, and two government schools, ''Lycée Nationale de Cité Soleil'', and École Nationale de Cité Soleil. 

For several years until 2007, the area was ruled by a number of gangs, each controlling their own sectors.<ref name="post">{{cite news |date=2 February 2006 |title=Glimmers of Hope in Cité Soleil |url=http://blog.washingtonpost.com/haitisstruggle/2006/02/glimmers_of_hope_in_cite_solei_1.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060418143546/http://blog.washingtonpost.com/haitisstruggle/2006/02/glimmers_of_hope_in_cite_solei_1.html |archive-date=18 April 2006 |access-date=2006-12-30 |work=WashingtonPost.com}}</ref> Government control was reestablished after a series of operations in early 2007 by the [[United Nations Stabilization Mission in Haiti]] (MINUSTAH)<ref name="redcross">{{cite web
|url=http://www.walterdorn.org/pub/53
|title= INTELLIGENCE-LED PEACEKEEPING: The United Nations Stabilization Mission in Haiti (MINUSTAH), 2006-07
|publisher=Intelligence and National Security
|access-date=2010-09-10
}}
</ref> with the participation of the local population. After the [[2010 Haiti earthquake|devastating 2010 earthquake]], it took nearly two weeks for relief aid to arrive in Cité-Soleil.<ref>{{cite news |date=January 25, 2010 |title=Quake aid finally reaches Haiti's Cité-Soleil slum |url=http://www.euronews.net/2010/01/25/quake-aid-finally-reaches-haitis-cite-soleil-slum/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100127232230/http://www.euronews.net/2010/01/25/quake-aid-finally-reaches-haitis-cite-soleil-slum/ |archive-date=27 January 2010 |access-date=25 January 2010 |publisher=euronews}}</ref>

==Overview==
The neighborhood is located at the western end of the runway of [[Toussaint Louverture International Airport]] and adjoins the grounds of the former [[Haitian American Sugar Company|Hasco Haitian American Sugar]] complex. [[File:Womens Group Cite Soleil Haiti.jpg|thumb|Sign for a Cité Soleil women's group, 2002. The sign translates, "Organization of Activist Women of Cité Soleil".]]
Most of the residents of Cité-Soleil are children or young adults. The mortality rate is high from diseases such as [[AIDS]], or from violence.<ref name="redcross"/>
At times Cité Soleil has been filled with armed gangs. Politically affiliated gangs or militias, often with quasi-official powers, have been a regular element of [[Politics of Haiti|Haitian politics]] throughout [[History of Haiti|the country's history]].

According to a 1996 report, half of the houses of Cité Soleil are made of cement with a metal roof, half are made completely of scavenged material. An estimated 60 to 70 percent of houses have no access to a latrine, particularly in the marshy Brooklyn area which includes Cité Carton.<ref>{{cite web |date=May 1996 |title=Activity Report No. 21: A Plan for CDS to Establish a Water and Sanitation District in Cité Soleil, Haiti and Monitoring Visit Report |url=http://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PNABY701.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120313051903/http://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PNABY701.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-date=March 13, 2012 |access-date=11 July 2018 |publisher=USAID Mission to Haiti}}</ref>

== History ==
It began with the construction in 1958 of homes for 52 families. In the summer of 1966, a mysterious fire in the slum of La Saline displaced many of its residents. 1,197 homes were built there and it was named Cité Simone, after Haiti's First Lady [[Simone Ovide Duvalier]]. In 1972, a major fire near the central market of Port-au-Prince displaced yet more people who ended up in the Boston section of Cité Simone. In 1983, the census recorded 82,191 people in Cité Simone.<ref>{{cite web |date=25 May 1984 |title=Project Paper: Urban Health and Community Development – Phase II |url=http://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PDAAT248.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110824044442/http://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PDAAT248.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-date=August 24, 2011 |access-date=11 July 2018 |publisher=USAID/Haiti}}</ref>

Originally designed to house sugar workers, Cité Simone later housed manual laborers for a local Export Processing Zone (EPZ). [[Neoliberal]] reforms beginning in the early 1970s made this place a magnet for [[squatter]]s from around the countryside looking for work in the newly constructed factories. This movement accelerated in the early 1980s with the destruction of the [[Creole pig]]s by American order in response to an [[African swine fever|African swine flu]] outbreak, followed by the rise of Finance Minister [[Leslie Delatour]] who took this post following the ouster of [[Jean-Claude Duvalier]] in 1986. Delatour openly advocated the depopulation of much of the Haitian countryside and that these people work instead in cities, living in places such as the newly named Cité Soleil, though not for Hasco that Delatour shut down in 1987.  This industrial sector was however damaged following the [[1991 Haitian coup d'état|1991 ''coup d'état'']] that deposed President [[Jean-Bertrand Aristide]], causing a boycott of Haitian products that closed the EPZ.<ref>{{cite news |date=2010-01-25 |title=Quake aid finally reaches Cité Soleil |url=http://www.euronews.net/2010/01/25/quake-aid-finally-reaches-haitis-cite-soleil-slum/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100127232230/http://www.euronews.net/2010/01/25/quake-aid-finally-reaches-haitis-cite-soleil-slum/ |archive-date=2010-01-27 |access-date=25 January 2010 |publisher=euronews}}</ref> Cité Soleil continued to be plagued by extreme poverty and persistent unemployment, with high rates of illiteracy.<ref name="redcross" />

==Current status==
The [[United Nations Stabilization Mission in Haiti|United Nations Stabilization Mission]] (MINUSTAH) has been in Haiti since 2004. It now numbers 8,000 troops but continues to struggle for control over the armed gangs. In October 2006, a group of heavily armed Haitian police were able to enter Cité Soleil for the first time in three years and were able to remain one hour as armored UN troops patrolled the area. Since this is where the armed gangs take their kidnap victims, the Haitian police's ability to penetrate the area even for such a short time was seen as a sign of progress.<ref name="police">{{cite web

==2010 Haiti earthquake==
{{Main|2010 Haiti earthquake}}
[[File:CiteSoleilEarthquake8.JPG|thumb|A survivor camp in Cité Soleil in January 2010]]

On January 12, 2010, at 21:53 UTC (4:53 PM local time) Haiti was struck by a [[moment magnitude scale|magnitude]]-7.0 [[earthquake]], the country's most severe earthquake in over 200 years.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eqinthenews/2010/us2010rja6/ |title=Magnitude 7.0 – Haiti Region |access-date=January 12, 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100115110510/http://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eqinthenews/2010/us2010rja6/ |archive-date=January 15, 2010 }}</ref> The epicenter of the quake was just outside the Haitian capital, [[Port-au-Prince]], in [[Léogâne]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/worldnews/centralamericaandthecaribbean/haiti/6977308/Major-earthquake-off-Haiti-causes-hospital-to-collapse.html |title=Major earthquake off Haiti causes hospital to collapse – Telegraph |publisher=telegraph.co.uk |access-date=2010-01-20 | location=London | date=2010-01-12}}</ref> As the biggest slum of Port-au-Prince, Cité Soleil fared relatively well, as most of its cinder block and corrugated steel shacks survived. [[Médecins Sans Frontières]] reopened its Choscal Hospital (which operated between 2005 and 2007 during the gang war) in the heart of the slum within 24 hours.<ref name="marlowe">{{cite web |url=http://www.irishtimes.com/newspaper/world/2010/0120/1224262713628.html |title=Just this once, the stricken capital's biggest slum was comparatively lucky|publisher=The Thomas Reuters Foundation|date=January 20, 2010|last=Marlowe|first=Chris |access-date=2010-01-20 }}</ref> However, the area remained in desperate need of help, according to World Emergency Relief.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.alertnet.org/thenews/fromthefield/221038/126383383770.htm |title=Haiti: Much needed supplies for Cité Soleil |publisher=The Irish Times|date=January 18, 2010 |last=Hobbes |first=Lara |access-date=2010-01-20}}</ref>

Gang members who escaped from Haiti's damaged prison returned to the area to continue to commit crimes.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/idUSTRE60G0CO20100117|title=Gangs return to Haiti slum after quake prison break|last=Brown|first= Tom |date=January 17, 2010|publisher=Reuters|access-date=22 January 2010}}</ref> The crime rate rose and police urged citizens to take matters into their own hands.<ref name="katz">{{cite web |url=http://www.streetgangs.com/news/011910_haitiangang |title=Gang members in Haitian slum profit from disaster |publisher=streetgangs.com|date=January 19, 2010|last=Katz |first=Jonathan |access-date=2010-01-20 }}</ref> As of January 23, 2010, Cité Soleil had largely remained neglected by earthquake relief workers and was doing what it could to survive and help on its own.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/americas/8476293.stm|title=Haiti's Cité Soleil still deprived of post-quake help |last=Davis|first=Nick|date=2010-01-23|publisher=BBC News, Haiti|access-date=23 January 2010}}</ref> [[Samaritan's Purse]] worked in Cité Soleil by building the largest cholera treatment center during [[2010 Haiti cholera outbreak|the outbreak]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.samaritanspurse.ca/rss/disaster-relief/haiti/helping-earthquake-victims-in-haiti.aspx |title=Helping rebuild in Haiti |access-date=2014-04-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140408220545/http://www.samaritanspurse.ca/rss/disaster-relief/haiti/helping-earthquake-victims-in-haiti.aspx |archive-date=2014-04-08 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
