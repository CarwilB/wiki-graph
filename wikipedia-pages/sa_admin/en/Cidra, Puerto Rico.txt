'''Cidra''' ({{IPA|es|ˈsiðɾa}}) is a [[Cidra barrio-pueblo|town]] and [[Municipalities of Puerto Rico|municipality]] of [[Puerto Rico]] located in the central region of the island, north of [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]]; south of [[Comerío, Puerto Rico|Comerío]] and [[Aguas Buenas, Puerto Rico|Aguas Buenas]]; east of [[Aibonito, Puerto Rico|Aibonito]] and [[Barranquitas, Puerto Rico|Barranquitas]]; and west of [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]]. Cidra is spread over 12 barrios and [[Cidra barrio-pueblo|Cidra Pueblo]] (the downtown area and the administrative center of the city). It is part of the [[San Juan-Caguas-Guaynabo Metropolitan Statistical Area]].

Cidra is known as ''"El Pueblo de la Eterna Primavera"'' ("[[Spring (season)|Town of the Eternal Spring]]") and ''"El Pueblo de la Paloma Sabanera"'' ("[[Plain Pigeon|Town of the Plain Pigeon]]").

== History ==
The region of what is now Cidra belonged to the [[Taíno people|Taíno]] region of Cubuy, which covered a portion of the center of Puerto Rico.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.taino-tribe.org/jatiboni-s.html|title=Gobierno Tribal del Pueblo Jatibonicu Taíno de Puerto Rico|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140122012139/http://www.taino-tribe.org/jatiboni-s.html|archive-date=January 22, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> The region was led by cacique [[Caguax]].

There are not many records that show that the region was populated after the [[Spanish colonization of the Americas|Spanish colonization]]. However, it is believed that around 1795, a [[Catalan people|Catalan]] named Frujols built a [[Hermitage (religious retreat)|hermitage]] or shrine around which a small village settled. The settlement became a ward of [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]], until 1807 when the residents gave the power to Victoriano de Rivera to ask the Governor for permission to found an independent town. In 1809, a landowner known as Bibiana Vázquez donated the land needed for the foundation, and Governor [[Salvador Meléndez Bruna]] approved the petition. Victoriano de Rivera was appointed to lead the town, but he was quickly replaced by Wenceslao Vázquez due to illness.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09022001&page=2|title=Cidra: Fundación e historia|publisher=Enciclopedia de Puerto Rico|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140703102426/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/esp/article.cfm?ref=09022001&page=2|archive-date=July 3, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

At the time of its foundation, Cidra had 26 houses and 11 huts. It is believed that its name is derived from the [[citron]] fruit which was widely cultivated in the region.<ref name="uprm">{{cite web|url=http://www.uprm.edu/agricultura/sea/mapa/Portal%20de%20Cidra.pdf|title=Municipio de Cidra|publisher=[[University of Puerto Rico at Mayagüez]]|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101214071028/http://www.uprm.edu/agricultura/sea/mapa/Portal%20de%20Cidra.pdf|archive-date=December 14, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://sondeaquipr.net/Pueblos/Cidra/cidra_origen.htm|title=Cidra: Origen e historia|publisher=Sn de aquí|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140407094101/http://sondeaquipr.net/Pueblos/Cidra/cidra_origen.htm|archive-date=April 7, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> During the years following its foundation, a church and city hall were built. By 1822, there were already two public schools in town. Two years later, a road communicating the town with [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]] was opened. In 1868, the population of Cidra was more than 5,000 residents, which included European immigrants and slaves.

Puerto Rico was ceded by [[Spain]] in the aftermath of the [[Spanish–American War]] under the terms of the [[Treaty of Paris of 1898]] and became a territory of the United States. In 1899, the United States conducted its first [[census]] of Puerto Rico finding that the population of Cidra was 7,552.<ref name="OfficeSanger1900">{{cite book|first1=Joseph|last1=Prentiss Sanger|first2=Henry|last2=Gannett|first3=Walter|last3=Francis Willcox|title=Informe sobre el censo de Puerto Rico, 1899, United States. War Dept. Porto Rico Census Office|url=https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog|year=1900|publisher=Imprenta del gobierno|page=[https://archive.org/details/informesobreelc00joangoog/page/n254 163]| language=es}}</ref>

In 1902, the [[Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico]] approved a law for the consolidation of certain municipalities. As a result, Cidra was again incorporated to the town of [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]]. However, in 1905 a new law revoked the previous one, turning Cidra into an independent municipality again.<ref name="psh">{{cite web|url=http://www.parroquiacidra.com/historia_de_cidra.htm|title=Historia de Cidra|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140702181617/http://www.parroquiacidra.com/historia_de_cidra.htm|archive-date=July 2, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Hurricane Maria===
[[File:Map of landslides in Puerto Rico - Hurricane Maria 2017.jpg|thumb|Much of Cidra suffered landslides|left]]
[[File:Downed power line in Cidra, Puerto Rico after Hurricane Maria.jpg|thumb|Downed power line in Cidra after Hurricane Maria|left]]
A category 5, and high-end category 4 [[Hurricane Maria]] when it hit Puerto Rico, on September 20, 2017 triggered numerous landslides in Cidra. A week before [[Hurricane Irma]], another category 5 had passed over Puerto Rico causing some damage. With Hurricane María, some areas of Cidra experienced more than 25 landslides per kilometer.<ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/ |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="USGS_Maria_Landslides map">{{cite web |title=Preliminary Locations of Landslide Impacts from Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico |url=https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |website=USGS Landslide Hazards Program |publisher=USGS |access-date=2019-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190303143147/https://landslides.usgs.gov/research/featured/2017/maria-pr/images/PR_Maria_LS_density_map.pdf |archive-date=2019-03-03 |url-status=live }}</ref> Citizens, with machetes in hand, worked to clear the roadways of debri after the hurricane hit, especially in [[Rabanal, Cidra, Puerto Rico|Rabanal]], [[Arenas, Cidra, Puerto Rico|Arena]] and [[Ceiba, Cidra, Puerto Rico|Ceiba]], barrios of Cidra located at high altitudes. 1500 homes were left without a roof and many would never receive funds for repairs. One local (analog) radio antenna survived the catastrophe allowing for some communication within the town to take place. Before the hurricane there had been plans to eliminate the radio tower, but when 92.7% of Puerto Rico's towers were destroyed by the hurricane, plans to eliminate the analog radio tower were scrapped. [[Puerto Rico Highway 172]], the main highway into Cidra from the [[San Juan, Puerto Rico|San Juan]] area had to remain closed for repairs for five months. It was not until six months after the hurricane struck that power was restored to Cidra.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2018/los-vecinos-de-cidra-la-primera-linea-de-respuesta-ante-la-emergencia/|title=Los vecinos de Cidra, la primera línea de respuesta ante la emergencia – El Nuevo Día|website=Los vecinos de Cidra, la primera línea de respuesta ante la emergencia – El Nuevo Día|access-date=2019-12-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191212051509/https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2018/los-vecinos-de-cidra-la-primera-linea-de-respuesta-ante-la-emergencia/|archive-date=2019-12-12|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news | title=María, un nombre que no vamos a olvidar. Los vientos de María dejan en silencio a Cidra | trans-title=Maria, a name we will never forget. Marías' winds silence Cidra| newspaper=El Nuevo Día| date=2019-06-13 | url=https://huracanmaria.elnuevodia.com/2017/municipio/cidra/| language=es | access-date=2021-09-19}}</ref>

==Geography==
Cidra is located at the top of a hill in the eastern part of the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Cordillera Central]]. It is bordered by the municipalities of [[Aguas Buenas, Puerto Rico|Aguas Buenas]], [[Caguas, Puerto Rico|Caguas]], [[Cayey, Puerto Rico|Cayey]], [[Aibonito, Puerto Rico|Aibonito]], and [[Comerío, Puerto Rico|Comerío]]. Cidra is a small municipality, covering only 36.1 square miles (94&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup>).<ref name="linktopr">{{cite web|url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/cidra.html|title=Cidra... El Pueblo de la Eterna Primavera|publisher=SalonHogar|access-date=March 30, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140720140656/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/Link%20P.R/www.linktopr.com/cidra.html|archive-date=July 20, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="PR_Ency">{{cite web|url=https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/cidra-municipality/|title=Cidra Municipality|publisher=Fundación Puertorriqueña de las Humanidades (FPH)|website=enciclopediapr.org|access-date=2019-03-20|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190827202502/https://enciclopediapr.org/en/encyclopedia/cidra-municipality/|archive-date=2019-08-27|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Water features===
[[File:Lago de Cidra, Cidra, Puerto Rico.jpg|thumb|Lago de Cidra]]
Cidra's hydrographic system consists mainly of {{lang|es|Lago de Cidra}} reservoir, located in the municipality. The reservoir, which was built in 1946, provides water to several areas in the island.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/diversos_temas/lagos_y_embalses_de_puerto_rico.htm|title=Lagos y Embalses de Puerto Rico|publisher=Proyecto Salón Hogar|via=[[Puerto Rico Department of Natural Resources]]|access-date=April 8, 2014|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140719230115/http://www.proyectosalonhogar.com/diversos_temas/lagos_y_embalses_de_puerto_rico.htm|archive-date=July 19, 2014}}</ref> There are also several rivers and creeks that cross the town like [[Río Arroyata|Arroyata]], [[Río Bayamón|Bayamón]], and [[Rio de la Plata (Puerto Rico)|Río de la Plata]].

===Fauna===
The town is known for being the nesting place of the [[Plain Pigeon|paloma sabanera]] (a subspecies of the plains pigeon). The bird, which is at risk of [[extinction]], is currently found mostly in the municipality of Cidra and other nearby municipalities.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fws.gov/caribbean/PDF/Paloma_Sabanera.pdf|title=Paloma Sabanera|publisher=[[U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service]]|access-date=April 8, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140712121054/http://www.fws.gov/caribbean/PDF/Paloma_Sabanera.pdf|archive-date=July 12, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> In addition, there is various species of fish residing in the Lago de Cidra, as well as [[Green iguana|iguanas]] which have been introduced into the surrounding the area and are now considered an [[invasive species]].

==Places of interest==
Although Cidra is not known as a tourist main stop, it has some attractions. The town center features historic places like the {{lang|es|Parroquia Nuestra Señora del Carmen}} church<ref>{{cite book | first= José A. |last=Mari Mut |title=Los Pueblos de Puerto Rico y Las Iglesias de Sus Plazas [The Pueblos of Puerto Rico, and the Churches of its Plazas]| date=2013-08-28 | url=http://edicionesdigitales.info/PueblosPR/pueblospr.pdf | language=es}}</ref> and the Iberia Theater, both built during the first half of the 20th century.

On the outskirts of the town, there is Lake Cidra and Perico's Waterfalls. Frog's Rock and Hamacas Bridge are also landmarks visited by tourists.<ref name="uprm" />

The renovation of {{lang|es|el Parque del Niño}} (children's park), which includes water features and a skatepark was completed in 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/reanudaraoperacioneselparquedelninoencidra-1369366/|title=Reanudará operaciones el Parque del Niño en Cidra|date=October 10, 2019|website=Primera Hora|access-date=October 11, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191102221257/https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/nota/reanudaraoperacioneselparquedelninoencidra-1369366/|archive-date=November 2, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

To stimulate local tourism, the [[Puerto Rico Tourism Company]] launched the ''Voy Turistiendo'' ("I'm Touring") campaign, with a passport book and website. The Cidra page lists {{lang|es|Destilería Club Caribe}}, {{lang|es|Mirador del Lago de Cidra}}, and {{lang|es|Parque del Niño}}, as places of interest.<ref>{{cite book | title= Pasaporte: Voy Turisteando |publisher=Compañia de Turismo de Puerto Rico | year=2021| language=es}}</ref>

==Economy==
The economy of Cidra has relied mostly on [[agriculture]], particularly [[coffee]], [[tobacco]], and minor fruits. [[Cattle ranch]]ing is also a source of economy in the town. In recent years, pharmaceutical and clothing industries have become participants of the economy of Cidra.<ref name="linktopr" /><ref>{{cite web|url=http://edicionesdigitales.info/PueblosPR/PueblosPR/Cidra.html|title=Cidra: Pueblo de la Eterna Primavera|access-date=March 31, 2014}}</ref>

==Culture==
===Festivals and events===
Cidra celebrates its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|patron saint]] festival in July. The {{lang|es|Fiestas Patronales de Nuestra Sra. del Carmen}} is a religious and cultural celebration that generally features parades, games, artisans, amusement rides, regional food, and live entertainment.<ref name="PR_Ency" />

A number of cultural events take place during the year, most prominently:<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.enciclopediapr.org/ing/article.cfm?ref=09042802&page=4|title=Trujillo Alto: Events|work=Encyclopedia Puerto Rico|access-date=2014-04-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140407070052/http://www.enciclopediapr.org/ing/article.cfm?ref=09042802&page=4|archive-date=2014-04-07|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Religion===
There are a number of churches and chapels from several denominations in Cidra. The main [[Catholic Church|Catholic]] parish, Parroquia Nuestra Señora del Carmen, was established in 1813.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.parroquiacidra.com/Historia.htm|title=Parroquia Nuestra Señora del Carmen: Nuestra Historia|access-date=April 5, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140417004618/http://www.parroquiacidra.com/Historia.htm|archive-date=April 17, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> The original structure was destroyed by earthquakes in the 1860s, but completely rebuilt by 1952.<ref name="uprm" /> Cidra's patron saint is the [[Our Lady of Mount Carmel|Virgin Mary]]. Its [[Fiestas patronales in Puerto Rico|festivities]] are held each July.

Also in front of the town square is the [[First Baptist Church in America|First Baptist]] Church, which was established by missionary Edgar Humphrey in 1903. The original wood structure was destroyed in 1928 by [[1928 Okeechobee hurricane|San Felipe Hurricane]], but rebuilt in concrete, wood, and zinc.<ref name="uprm" />

===Sports===
Cidra doesn't have a professional sports team, however, they have a successful amateur team, Bravos de Cidra, that participate in the [[Federación de Béisbol Aficionado de Puerto Rico|Puerto Rican Amateur Baseball Federation]].<ref name="lbsdapr">{{cite web|url=http://www.lbsdapr.org/franquicias/central|title=Liga Beisbol Superior: Franquicias|access-date=April 5, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140407064407/http://www.lbsdapr.org/franquicias/central|archive-date=April 7, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="Isla_News_PR">{{cite news |title=El Béisbol Recuerda A Héctor Ferrer |url=http://www.islanewspr.com/2018/11/06/el-beisbol-recuerda-a-hector-ferrer/ |access-date=1 February 2019 |work=Isla News PR |date=November 6, 2018 |language=es |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190202100821/http://www.islanewspr.com/2018/11/06/el-beisbol-recuerda-a-hector-ferrer/ |archive-date=2 February 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The Bravos team has won nine championships, and as of 2014, are en route to win their third consecutive one.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.elnuevodia.com/losbravosdecidrabuscanterceracorona-1710389.html|title=Los Bravos de Cidra buscan tercera corona|newspaper=[[El Nuevo Día]]|first=Sara|last=del Valle|date=February 12, 2014|access-date=April 5, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140408112704/http://www.elnuevodia.com/losbravosdecidrabuscanterceracorona-1710389.html|archive-date=April 8, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> The main baseball facility is the Jesús María Freire Stadium, where the Bravos play.

Cidra is also the birthplace of several important figures in the Puerto Rican sports scene. Some of them are former [[Major League Baseball|MLB]] players [[Luis Rivera (infielder)|Luis Rivera]] and [[Luis López (infielder)|Luis López]], as well as former coach of the [[Puerto Rico men's national basketball team]] [[Flor Meléndez]]. Boxer [[José Pedraza (boxer)|José Pedraza]] was also born in Cidra.

The population of Cidra increased steadily during the 20th Century. Since 1899, the population has risen more than 200%. According to the 2010 census, the municipality had 43,480 inhabitants.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://soph.md.rcm.upr.edu/demo/images/CDCES/Poblacion/Pob-1899-2010-total.pdf|title=Población total por municipios, Puerto Rico: 1899-2010|publisher=University of Puerto Rico|access-date=March 31, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140629121111/http://soph.md.rcm.upr.edu/demo/images/CDCES/Poblacion/Pob-1899-2010-total.pdf|archive-date=June 29, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The 2020 census shows a decline in population to 39,970.<ref>{{Cite report|date=2004-04-28|title=Census of Population and Housing, 2000 [United States]: Summary File 4, Puerto Rico|doi=10.3886/icpsr13563.v1|access-date=2021-08-29|website=ICPSR Data Holdings| url=http://www.icpsr.umich.edu/ICPSR/studies/13563/version/1|url-access=subscription}}</ref>

According to the 2010 Census, 77% of the population identifies themselves as [[White Puerto Ricans|White]], and 8.3% as [[Afro–Puerto Ricans|African-American]]. Also, 48.9% of the population identified themselves as males, and 51.1% as females. Finally, 25% of the population is under 18 years old. The next biggest percentage of population (20.7%) is between 35 and 49 years old.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|title=2010 Census Interactive Population Search: Cidra, Puerto Rico|work=US Census 2010|access-date=2017-12-09|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130308164225/http://www.census.gov/2010census/popmap/ipmtext.php?fl=72|archive-date=2013-03-08|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Government==
{{main|Mayoralty in Puerto Rico}}
All municipalities in Puerto Rico are administered by a mayor, elected every four years. The current mayor of Cidra is [[Delvis Pagán Clavijo]], of the [[New Progressive Party of Puerto Rico|New Progressive Party]] (PNP). He was first elected at the [[Puerto Rican general election, 2024|2024 general elections]].

The city belongs to the [[Puerto Rico Senatorial district VI]], which is represented by two Senators. === Flag and coat of arms ===
[[File:Bandera de Cidra, Puerto Rico.svg|thumb|200px|right|Flag of Cidra]]
The flag of Cidra features one brown vertical stripe on the left side, with a width of about one-third the length of the flag. It also features two equal-sized horizontal stripes to the right side of the brown one. The above stripe is green, while the lower one is yellow, or golden. The golden color symbolizes the richness of the fruits and its hydrography, as well as the spiritual wealth of the region. The green symbolizes the green landscape that can be seen in Cidra throughout the year, which is also the reason why the town is called the "Town of the Eternal Spring". The brown symbolizes the [[Our Lady of Mount Carmel|Virgin]], patron saint of the town, and also the [[Plain Pigeon|Paloma sabanera]] that is widely seen in the area.<ref name="psh" />

Some versions of the flag also feature the coat of arms on the left hand side, over the intersection of the three stripes. The coat of arms was approved in the 1970s by the Municipal Assembly. It features a red band crossing the shield diagonally, from left to right. Over the red band lies a golden [[citrus]], which is believed to be one of the reasons for the town's name, and a [[cornucopia]] of fruits, which alludes to Cidra's role as one of the major producers of fruits of the island. The upper field is either white or silver, and features two symbols: a black [[Scapular#Devotional scapular|scapular]] representing the Virgin, and a black bishop hat referencing [[Nepomuceno|San Juan Nepomuceno]], bishop and martyr. The lower field is mostly blue and features a brown pigeon flying over a mountain range, representing the location of the town in the [[Cordillera Central (Puerto Rico)|Central Mountain Range]], and a set of silver water waves. The whole seal is capped by a three-tower crown, which is usually seen in municipal seals in the island.<ref name="linktopr" /><ref name="LexJuris" />

===Nicknames===
[[File:Patagioenas inornata wetmorei.jpg|thumb|150px|right|Paloma sabanera]]
Cidra is known mostly by two names. It is known as the ''"Town of the Eternal Spring"'' for its cool temperatures. It is also known as the ''"Town of the Plain Pigeon"'' because of the abundance of that bird in the town.<ref name="linktopr" />

==Transportation==
The main road to Cidra is [[Puerto Rico Highway 172|Road 172]], which branches out from the [[Puerto Rico Highway 52]]. Its distance from the capital is approximately 40 minutes.<ref name="linktopr" /> Other roads that lead to the town are #787 and #173.
