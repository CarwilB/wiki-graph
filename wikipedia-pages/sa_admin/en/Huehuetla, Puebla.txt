'''Huehuetla''' ([[Nahuatl]]: "old place"<ref name=encyclopedia/>) is a rural [[Municipalities of Puebla|municipality]] in [[Puebla]], [[Mexico]].<ref name=snim/>

==Geography==
Huehuetla is located in northern Puebla on its border with [[Veracruz]]. The southern exclave of San Juan Ocelonacaxtla is separated from the rest of the municipality by [[Caxhuacan]] and [[Ixtepec, Puebla|Ixtepec]]. The main part of the municipality also borders [[Olintla]] and [[Hueytlalpan]] in the west and the Veracruzian municipalities of [[Coxquihui]] and [[Zozocolco de Hidalgo]] in the east. San Juan Ocelonacaxtla additionally borders the Pueblan municipalities of [[Tuzamapan de Galeana]], [[Jonotla]], [[Zoquiapan]] and [[Atlequizayan]].<ref name=infocard/> Huehuetla covers an area of {{convert|48|km2}}.<ref name=snim/>

Situated in the [[Sierra Norte de Puebla]], Huehuetla's topography is quite rugged, with hills topping {{convert|800|m}} above sea level descending to river valleys less than {{convert|300|m}} above sea level. The municipality is located in the [[Tecolutla River]] basin. The main river in the municipality is the Tehuancate, which flows through it from west to east. The Tehuancate drains into the Zempoala River, which also forms the southeastern boundary of the Ocelonacaxtla exclave.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Over half of the land in the municipality is used for agriculture, although the soils in the municipality are [[leptosol]]s and poorly suited for agriculture.<ref name=compendium>{{cite web |url=http://www.inegi.org.mx/geo/contenidos/topografia/compendio.aspx |title=Compendio de Información Geográfica Municipal 2010: Huehuetla, Puebla |publisher=[[INEGI]] |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref>

Huehuetla is located in a climatic transition zone between the temperate Sierra Norte and the hot [[Gulf of Mexico]] coast. Rain occurs throughout the year.<ref name=encyclopedia/> Average temperatures range between 18 and 24 degrees Celsius and average annual precipitation is between 2900 and 3600 millimetres.<ref name=compendium/>

==History==
In [[Pre-Hispanic]] times, Huehuetla was inhabited by [[Nahua peoples|Nahua]] and [[Totonac people|Totonac]] peoples. Spanish settlement of the area began in 1550: property titles were first given to settlers in 1574, in which the area was named San Salvador Huehuetla. Previously part of the district of [[Zacatlán]], Huehuetla was established as a free municipality in 1895.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Administration==
The municipal government comprises one councillor ([[Spanish language|Spanish]]: ''síndico'') and eight trustees (''regidores''), six of whom are elected by [[relative majority]] and two by [[proportional representation]], headed by a president. The current president of the municipality is Ruben Vázquez Cabrera. San Juan Ocelonacaxtla elects an auxiliary board (''junta auxiliar'') whose functions are subject to the municipal authorities.<ref name=encyclopedia/>

==Demographics==
In the 2010 Mexican Census, the municipality of Huehuetla recorded a population of 15,689 inhabitants living in 3361 households.<ref name=catalog>{{cite web |url=http://www.microrregiones.gob.mx/catloc/LocdeMun.aspx?tipo=clave&campo=loc&ent=21&mun=072 |title=Resumen municipal: Municipio de Huehuetla |language=es |website=Catálogo de Localidades |publisher=[[SEDESOL]] |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref> It recorded a population of 18,803 inhabitants in the 2015 Intercensal Survey.<ref name=survey/>

There are 14 localities in the municipality,<ref name=snim/> of which only the municipal seat, also called Huehuetla, is classified as urban. It recorded a population of 1764 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=catalog/> The Ozelonacaxtla exclave, located {{convert|12|km}} south of the municipal seat,<ref name=encyclopedia/> recorded a population of 1326 inhabitants in the 2010 Census.<ref name=catalog/>

==Culture==
Huehuetla is located in the cultural region of [[Totonacapan]].<ref name=hernandez>{{cite thesis 
|last=Hernández Aguilar |first=Luis Manuel |date=2010 |title="Si con el nombre de indios nos humillaron y explotaron, con el nombre de indios nos liberaremos": El proceso de configuración de un Sujeto Político Indígena, estudio de caso de la Organización Independiente Totonaca (OIT) |language=es |type=MSSc thesis |publisher=[[FLACSO]] |url=http://www.flacso.edu.mx/biblioiberoamericana/TEXT/MCS_XVII_promocion_2008-2010/Hernandez_LM.pdf |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref> In the 2015 Intercensal Survey, 97.68% of people in the municipality identified themselves as [[Indigenous peoples of Mexico|indigenous]].<ref name=survey/> In the 2010 Census, 12,904 people or 82% of the population in Huehuetla reported speaking an indigenous language, of which 12,187 spoke [[Totonacan languages|Totonac]].<ref name=snim/> In the 2015 Intercensal Survey, 29.52% of the people reported speaking an indigenous language did not speak Spanish.<ref name=survey/> [[Sociolinguistics|Sociolinguistic]] fieldwork done in Ozelonacaxtla indicates that Totonac language vitality in that community remains high.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.artsrn.ualberta.ca/totonaco2/?page_id=2230 |title=Other communities |website=Upper Necaxa Totonac Project |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref> The Independent Totonac Organization (''Organización Independiente Totonaca'', OIT) is an indigenous social movement founded in 1989 that has played an influential role in promoting the revitalization of Totonac culture and advancing the interests of the Totonac people in the area.<ref name=hernandez/><ref>{{cite web |url=http://docfilm.com/site/totonac-cultural-revitalization/ |title=Totonac Cultural Revitalization |publisher=Ethnoscope |date=29 April 1995 |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref>

==Economy==
The main economic activity in Huehuetla is agriculture:<ref name=encyclopedia/> main crops include coffee and corn.<ref name=hernandez/> The [[gross domestic product]] of the municipality was unofficially estimated at 263.9 million [[Mexican peso]]s in 2010.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=González-Estrada |first1=Adrián |last2=Gallegos-Cedillo |first2=Gilberto |date=2014 |title=The gross domestic product of municipalities of Mexico: II. Estates M-Z |url=http://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/remexca/v5n8/v5n8a6.pdf |journal=Revista Mexicana de Ciencias Agrícolas |volume=5 |issue=8 |pages=1405–1421 |access-date=16 November 2017}}</ref>
