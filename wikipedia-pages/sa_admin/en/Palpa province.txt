'''Palpa''' is the smallest of five [[Provinces of Peru|provinces]] of the [[Ica Region]] of [[Peru]] and the only [[landlocked]] province of the region. The capital of the province is the city of [[Palpa, Peru|Palpa]].

==Boundaries==
*'''North''': [[Huancavelica Region]]
*'''East''': [[Ayacucho Region]]
*'''South''': [[Nazca Province|province of Nazca]]
*'''West''': [[Ica Province|province of Ica]]

==Political division==
The province of Palpa is divided into five [[district]]s ({{langx|es|distritos}}, singular: {{lang|es|distrito}}), each of which is headed by a mayor (''alcalde''):

* [[Palpa District, Peru|Palpa]]

==History==
The province of Palpa has been involved with several pre-Columbian societies, from the [[Archaic stage|Archaic Period]] to the [[Late Intermediate Period]]. As a result, there are traces from these civilizations, which include the [[Nasca Lines]] and various archeological sites near [[Palpa, Peru]], including [[Pinchango Alto]]. Other sites include: [[Pinchango Bajo]], [[Mollake Chico]], PAP-294, PAP-365, PAP-64A and PAP-64B, [[Juaranga]], [[Los Molinos, Palpa|Los Molinos]], [[Estaqueria]], [[La Muña]], El Arenal, Chillo, [[Parasmarca]], PAP-379, [[Pernil Alto]], and [[Huayuri]].<ref name=ScienceDirect>[https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305440312000854 "A comprehensive numerical chronology for the pre-Columbian cultures of the Palpa valleys, south coast of Peru"]</ref> There has been work done here in [[3D modeling]] of the Nasca Lines.<ref name=3DModel>[https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/bitstream/handle/20.500.11850/147649/eth-26689-01.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y "A 3D Model of the Nasca Lines at Palpa"].</ref>

Artifacts from the following civilizations have been found in the chronology of the Palpa Valleys: [[Paracas culture|Paracas]], [[Nazca culture|Nasca]], [[Wari culture|Wari]], and the [[Inca people|Inka]]. Like many other Andean civilizations, there is overlap between them. Other civilizations include the [[Loro people|Loro]] and [[Chakimpampa]], but little is known as there is a gap in the [[Middle Horizon Period]].<ref>{{cite journal |title=A comprehensive numerical chronology for the pre-Columbian cultures of the Palpa valleys, south coast of Peru |journal=[[Journal of Archaeological Science]] |volume=39 |issue=7 |date=July 2012 |pages=2294–2303 |first1=Ingmar |last1=Unkel |first2=Markus |last2=Reindel |first3=Hermann |last3=Gorbahn |first4=Johny |last4=Isla Cuadrado |first5=Bernd |last5=Kromer |first6=Volker |last6=Sossna |doi=10.1016/j.jas.2012.02.021}}</ref> Each period, and with them the civilizations, had different styles of ceramics, which aided in the organizing of the timeline of the area.

==Archeological sites==
===Pinchango Alto, Palpa, Peru===
One such famous archeological site is Pinchango Alto. It is the largest Late Intermediate Period site in the Palpa region, and is the upper part of a larger site, denoted by Alto, while the lower half is denoted by Bajo.<ref>\"Co-Registration of Large Volume Laser Scanner Point Clouds: The Pinchango Alto (Peru) Data Set"</ref> Located roughly three kilometers north of the town of Palpa, it sits on the mountain Cerro Pinchango, between the Grande and Palpa rivers. This site is famous for its place in the experimentation of recreating the site with 3D modeling, as well as autonomous recording, both of which made examining the hard to reach site easier than before.<ref>[http://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstream/handle/123456789/29171/Eisenbeiss_253687.pdf cite "Photogrammetric Documentation of an Archeological Site (Palpa, Peru) Using an Autonomous Model Helicopter"]</ref><ref>{{cite journal | doi=10.1016/j.jas.2006.12.008 | title=Combining photogrammetry and laser scanning for the recording and modelling of the Late Intermediate Period site of Pinchango Alto, Palpa, Peru | year=2007 | last1=Lambers | first1=Karsten | last2=Eisenbeiss | first2=Henri | last3=Sauerbier | first3=Martin | last4=Kupferschmidt | first4=Denise | last5=Gaisecker | first5=Thomas | last6=Sotoodeh | first6=Soheil | last7=Hanusch | first7=Thomas | journal=Journal of Archaeological Science | volume=34 | issue=10 | pages=1702–1712 | url=https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/a70504f7-c0c3-4898-be9d-451437fd693e/download }}</ref> Given its isolated location, it's one of the best preserved sites in the region. These methods allowed for proper examination without disturbing the site and area around it. Surveys in the region have shown that settlements first started in the [[Formative Period]], and increased over time, but that there was also a partial abandonment during the Middle Period. The population grew again, however, during the Nasca period.{{citation needed|date=July 2021}}

This site is specifically located on a plateau overlooking the Rio Grande, and is marked by old, deep pits. The site was likely an economic based one, with connections to mining. Many artifacts here have been dated to the Later Intermediate Period.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Pinchango Bajo, Palpa, Peru===
This site is theorized to be the lower part of Pinchango Alto, given its location right below the other site. However, this site is not well preserved by comparison, as many structures have been destroyed by modern roads and waste dumps. Artifacts found in the area date back to the Late Intermediate Period as well.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Mollake Chico, Palpa, Peru===
Located west of Pinchango Alto, excavations of the site revealed an Early Paracas tomb, as well as funerary items from the Nasca Period. There were also signs of settlement at this site.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Geoglyph sites===
[[File:Geoglifo La Familia - panoramio.jpg|thumb|right|"The Family" set of Palpa geoglyphs]]
[[Geoglyphs]] refer to the type of structure that the [[Nazca Lines]] are, shapes drawn into the Earth. Sites PAP-294, PAP-365, PAP-64A and PAP-64B, and PAP-379 are all geoglyph sites in the Palpa region.<ref name=ScienceDirect/> 
* PAP-294 Coordinates: UTM 481.604 E, 8.394.291 N
* PAP-365 Coordinates: UTM 481.504 E, 8.391.593 N
* PAP-64A and PAP-64B Coordinates]]: UTM 476.925, 8.392.634 N. PAP-64A is to the West, and PAP-64B is to the East.
* PAP-379 Coordinates: UTM 479.247 E, 8.389.797 N{{citation needed|date=July 2021}}

===Juaranga, Palpa===
Located about three kilometers southwest of Palpa, this site is close to the Palpa River. This site holds primarily Paracas Period remains.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Los Molinos, Palpa===
This site is located roughly 4 kilometers west of modern Palpa, near where the Rio Grande and Palpa River meet. Adobe brick structural remains have been found here, and the site has been dated at being built primarily during the Early Nasca Period, though had inhabitants during the Middle Horizon as well. In addition, the presence of funerary contexts and other evidence shows that the place was used as a cemetery at some point.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Estaqueria===
This settlement is roughly 4 kilometers southwest of Palpa, and covers several hilltops with its terraces. Ceramic sherds of the Initial Nasca Period have been found here, as well as samples from wooden posts.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

===Pernil Alto, Palpa===
'''Pernil Alto''' is another famous site in the region, located in the middle of the Rio Grande valley. This site is one of the few sites known to have been occupied during the Archaic Period, and that has been more thoroughly excavated. After a time of abandonment in the Late Archaic Period, it was re-inhabited during the [[Initial Period]]. This introduced adobe structures to the site.<ref name=ScienceDirect/>

==Nazca Lines and 3D modeling==
Given the nature of the Nazca Lines, studying them had been hampered for a long time, as accurate documentation was hard to get ahold of. Being major landmarks, outside in the weather, many factors made it difficult to get records of them- weather, people moving around them, and general lack of preservation to the structures. As a result, [[M. Sauerbier]] and [[K. Landers]] strove to attain records that wouldn't be changed by rainfall, as the region had been relatively dry, and as such didn't wash away the lines. With climate change becoming more of an issue, however, this could change at any time. Utilizing aerial imagery, they produced a 3D model of the northern part of the Nazca Lines, near the modern Palpa.<ref name=3DModel/>
