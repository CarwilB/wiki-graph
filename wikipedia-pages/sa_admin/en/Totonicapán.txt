'''Totonicapán''' is a city in [[Guatemala]]. It serves as the capital of the [[Departments of Guatemala|department]] of [[Totonicapán (department)|Totonicapán]] and as the administrative seat for the surrounding municipality of Totonicapán.

== History ==
In 1820, the city experienced [[Totonicapán Uprising of 1820|an uprising]] of indigenous people led by {{ill|Atanasio Tzul|es}}. The rebellion lasted 24 days, and was successfully suppressed by the Spanish.

In 1838 Totonicapán was declared an independent republic, in which the adjoining departments of [[Sololá Department|Sololá]] and [[Quetzaltenango Department|Quezaltenango]] were included. This state existed for two years, and was then again merged in the republic of Guatemala.<ref name="EB1911">{{EB1911|inline=y|wstitle=Totonicapam|volume=27|page=92}}</ref>

Totonicapán suffered greatly in the [[1902 Guatemala earthquake|earthquake of April 18, 1902]].<ref name="EB1911"/>

Historically, Totonicapán was known for its [[hot spring]]s.<ref name=Baily>{{cite book|last=Baily|first=John|title=Central America; Describing Each of the States of Guatemala, Honduras, Salvador, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica|year=1850|publisher=Trelawney Saunders|location=London|page=85|url=http://www.wdl.org/en/item/7306/view/1/85/}}</ref>
