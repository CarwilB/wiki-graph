'''Inírida'''  ({{IPA|es|iˈniɾiða}}), formerly '''Puerto Inírida''', is the capital city, and a municipality, of the [[Departments of Colombia|department]] of [[Guainía Department|Guainía]] in [[Colombia]]. It was established in 1963 on the site of the small village of Obando, at the time in the municipality of San Felipe.  The municipality was renamed in 1974.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20071009132153/http://ssiglims.igac.gov.co/ssigl/datosLimites/94-DC-1593-1974a-In%EDrida.txt "Decreto Número 1593 de 1974 (Agosto 5)" Instituto Geográfico Agustín Codazzi (IGAC), Colombia] (Colombian Presidential Decree #1593 of 5 August 1974)</ref>  The municipal population of 31,514 (2018 census) is mostly indigenous and accounts for about a third of the department's population.<ref name="auto"/>

==Geography==
Located in the ''[[llanos]]'' at the confluence of the [[Inírida River]] and [[Guaviare River]], most of the territory of the municipality of Puerto Inírida is river terrace and bottomland, although there are some hills. It is {{convert|30|km|mi|0|disp=or}} from the [[Venezuela]]n border.

===Climate===
Inírida has a [[tropical rainforest climate]] ([[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]]: ''Af''). It has heavy to very heavy rainfall year-round.

==Economy==
The main economic activities in the municipality, aside from government, are agriculture, fishing<ref>Castro, Dario M. (1988) ''Algunos aspectos de la pesca ornamental en Puerto Iñírida'', Museo del Mar, Universidad de Bogotá  Jorge Tadeo Lozano, Bogotá, D.E.</ref> and cattle raising.<ref>Mejía Gutiérrez, Mario and Mejía Fernández, Fernando (1980) ''Puerto Inírida, Comisaría del Guainía : economía básica'' Corporación Araracuara, Departamento Administrativo de Intendencias y Comisarías, Bogotá, D.E.</ref> Agriculture includes subsistence farming,<ref>Triana, Gloria (1985) ''Los Puinaves del Inírida:: formas de subsistencia y mecanismos de adaptación'' Universidad Nacional de Colombia, Bogotá, D.E.</ref> the production of fiber from the [[Petiole (botany)|petioles]] of the young chiquichiqui palm (''Bactris speciosa''), used for rope, and the [[rattan]] known as "Yaré" used for furniture and baskets.

Tourists visit for several activities including fishing, canoeing, sightseeing and hiking. Hotels and hostels have appeared as national and international visitors continue to come. Handicrafts have now become a popular industry, and are supported by government institutions.

==Demographics==
The society of Inírida is composed of 53% of indigenous people: [[Baniwa|Kurripacos]], {{ill|Puinaves|es|Pueblo puinave}} and {{ill|Piapocos|es|Pueblo wenaiwika}}. 30% are colonizers from the other parts of Colombia and 17% are other indigenous ethnic groups.<ref>[http://www.dimar.mil.co/VBeContent/VerImp.asp%3FID%3D5055%26IDCompany%3D71+princesa+inirida&hl=en&ct=clnk&cd=11&gl=us DIMAR.MIL.CO]{{Dead link|date=January 2020 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

==Transportation==

===Airports===
The local airport is [[César Gaviria Trujillo Airport]] {{Airport codes|PDA|SKPD}}, named in honor of [[César Gaviria Trujillo|a former Colombian president]]. As of 2007, its runway is serviced by two airlines, Satena,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.satena.com/|title=Satena Colombia &#124; Vuela Con Satena &#124; Mejor Cobertura Nacional|first=SATENA|last=S.A|website=www.satena.com|accessdate=19 July 2023}}</ref> with three flights per week to and from [[Bogotá]] and two flights a week to and from [[Villavicencio]], and Air Colombia twice a week from Villavicencio, through Barrancominas, Guainía and then arriving at Inírida. This airline operates a Douglas DC-3 which makes the trip without stopping. However, the [[San Fernando de Atabapo]] airport in Venezuela is just 30&nbsp;km away. There is also a cargo airline that flies twice a week from Bogotà only.  The airport was formerly known as ''Aeropuerto Obando''.

It is also possible to get to Inírida by river, but the trip takes hours or days and costs almost as much as a flight.

===Public transportation===
With the arrival of more and more people, a public transportation mechanism has been established. Around 500 auto rickshaws provide an efficient and inexpensive means of transportation. The standard fare (which covers almost all the destinations within the town) is around US $.50. There are also mini-vans available for large groups; the fare remains US $.50 per person.
