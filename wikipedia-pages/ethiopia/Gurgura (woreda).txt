'''Gurgura''' is a [[woreda]] in the [[Subdivisions of Ethiopia|chartered city]] of [[Dire Dawa]] in [[Ethiopia]]. Gurgura is named after the [[Gurgura]] Madaxweyne [[Dir (clan)|Dir]] clan family of the [[Somalis]].

== Historic provinces and awrajjas ==
See also: [[Provinces of Ethiopia]] and [[Awrajja]] 
[[File:Awraja_of_Ethiopia.png|thumb|Historic: Former provinces and awrajjas (includes the Eritrea territory)]]
Prior to the [[1995 Constitution of Ethiopia|1995 Constitution]], Ethiopia was divided into [[Provinces of Ethiopia|provinces]], and those were further subdivided into [[awrajja]]s, then into woredas and then sub-woredas. Previously, Gurgura woreda was part of the Gurgura na Gara Gurgurcha Awwraja, then during the Mengistu regime, Issa na Gurgura [[Awrajja]] (which was Dire Dawa + zone shinile ) and during the TPLF rule the zone shinile saw further subdivision  subdivisions into Gurgura woreda (Dire Dawa) and sitti zone of the somali region. After the 1995 Constitution, the terms "province" and "awrajja" were dropped in favor of the terms "region" and "zone".<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Wubneh |first=Mulatu |date=2017 |title=Ethnic Identity Politics and the Restructuring of Administrative Units in Ethiopia |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/26586251 |journal=International Journal of Ethiopian Studies |volume=11 |issue=1 & 2 |pages=105–138 |issn=1543-4133}}</ref>

The woreda of Gurgura was in existence as early as Menelik's era but saw military force during the Haile Salesai rule in the 1960s, when its administrative center was at [[Kersa (Hararge), Ethiopia|Kersa]].<ref name="NAI-web">[http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/g/ORTGIA.pdf "Local History in Ethiopia"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110528224956/http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/g/ORTGIA.pdf |date=2011-05-28 }} The Nordic Africa Institute website (accessed 17 August 2009)</ref><ref>{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=LAG_bETsbGEC&q=gurgura%2520people%2520%2520%2520The%2520Coming%2520African%2520Hour%253A%2520Dialectics%2520of%2520Opportunities%2520and%2520Constraints&pg=PA210 |title=The Coming African Hour: Dialectics of Opportunities and Constraints|last=Sindjoun|first=Luc|date=2010-01-01|publisher=African Books Collective |isbn=9780798302302 |pages=210|language=en}}</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 116,250, of whom 58,004 are men and 58,246 are women; 14,250 or 12.26% of its population are urban dwellers, which is less than the average for entire chartered city of 74.4. With an estimated area of 1,195.52 square kilometers, Gurgura has an estimated population density of 97.2 people per square kilometer, which is less than the average for the administrative region of 328.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070813054932/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2007-08-13 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 87,013 in 15,827 households, of whom 45,098 were men and 41,915 were women; 8,337 or 9.58% of its population were urban dwellers. The three largest ethnic groups reported in Gurgura were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (31.48%), the Somali (56.53%), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (1.24%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.75% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] is spoken as a first language by 82.29%, 15.77% [[Somali language|Somali]] and 1.39% speak [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.55% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 98.34% of the population reporting that as their faith, while 1.48% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]]. Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 7.98% of the population were considered literate. Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 90% of the urban houses and 31% of all houses had access to safe drinking water at the time of the census; 37% of the urban and about 7% of the total had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck15%5Ck15.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Dire Dawa Provisional Administration'', Vol. 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718050207/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck15%5Ck15.pdf |date=2011-07-18 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.13, 2.15, 2.18, 3.7, 6.3, 6.11, 6.13 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>

Censuses record that Oromo are the greatest percentage of the Gurgura woreda, based on the number who speak the Oromo language. But the [[Gurgura]] clan share both Somali and Oromo identities, speaking the Oromo language and tracing their genealogy to the [[Dir (clan)|Dir]], a Somali clan family.<ref>{{cite journal |author=Asnake Kefale <!-- Kefale is his patronym, not his last name. -->|date=2014 |title=Ethnic decentralization and the challenges of inclusive governance in multiethnic cities: The case of Dire Dawa, Ethiopia |journal=Regional & Federal Studies |volume=24 |issue=5 |pages=589–605 |doi=10.1080/13597566.2014.971772 |s2cid=154137709 }}</ref> Gurgura are mentioned in the ''[[Futuh al-Habasha]] : Conquest of Abyssinia'' as source dating back as far as the 16th century as Somalis who fought alongside [[Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi|imam Ahmed]].<ref>{{Cite book |url= https://books.google.com/books?id=YgIwAQAAIAAJ&q=gorgorah |title=The conquest of Abyssinia: 16th century |last=ʻArabfaqīh |first=Shihāb al-Dīn Aḥmad ibn ʻAbd al-Qādir|date=2003-01-01|publisher=Tsehai Publishers & Distributors|isbn=9780972317269|pages=120, 123 and 401|language=en}}</ref> So most inhabitants are of the Gurgura clan.
