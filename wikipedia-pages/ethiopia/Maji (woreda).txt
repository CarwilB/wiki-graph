{{short description|District in South West Ethiopia People's Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Maji''' (also known as Dizi) is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[South West Ethiopia Peoples' Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[West Omo Zone|West Omo zone]], Maji is bordered on the south by the [[Kibish River]] which separates it from [[South Sudan]], on the west by [[Surma (woreda)|Surma]], on the northwest by [[Bero (woreda)|Bero]], on the north by [[Meinit Shasha]], and on the east by the [[Omo River (Ethiopia)|Omo River]] which separates it from the [[Debub Omo Zone]]. Towns in Maji include [[Tum, Ethiopia|Tum]] and [[Maji, Ethiopia|Maji]]. The western part of Maji was separated to create Bero woreda and some southern kebeles were added to [[Nyangatom (woreda)|Nyangatom]] woreda.

Rivers in this woreda include the [[Netube River|Netube]] and the [[Mui River|Mui]]. High points include [[Mount Tiyaki]] and [[Mount Siski]]. A major portion of Maji is included in the [[Omo National Park]]. Maji suffers from a lack of roads and means of transport; remote locations are accessible only by air.<ref>Joachim Ahrens, {{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20101009024049/http://ocha-eth.org/Archive/DownloadableReports/KEFA_JAN.pdf "Kefa - the Cradel of Coffee"]}} UNDP-EUE Report, January 1997 (accessed 19 February 2009)</ref>

In May 2009, a Malaysian investor with over 3.7 billion [[Ethiopian Birr|Birr]] in capital was granted a lease to over 31,000 hectares of land to develop [[palm oil]] [[tree plantation]] on. The Zonal authorities also granted him an additional 10,000 hectares to cultivate rubber trees on.<ref>[http://www.ena.gov.et/EnglishNews/2009/May/09May09/86999.htm "Malaysian investor launches 3.7 b birr palm oil tree plantation"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090614065424/http://www.ena.gov.et/EnglishNews/2009/May/09May09/86999.htm |date=2009-06-14 }}, [[Ethiopian News Agency]] 9 May 2009 (accessed 30 May 2009)</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the CSA, this woreda has a total population of 31,088, of whom 15,072 are men and 16,016 women; 4,838 or 15.56% of its population are urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 62.23% of the population reporting that belief, 18.44% practiced traditional beliefs, 16.66% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], and 1.16% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113220306/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2012-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, and 3.4.</ref>

In the 1994 national census Dizi had a population of 22,346, of whom 10,738 were men and 11,608 women; 4,316 or 19.31% of its population were urban dwellers. The three largest ethnic groups reported in this woreda were the [[Dizi people|Dizi]] (84.89%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (9.41%), and the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (3.07%); all other ethnic groups made up 2.63% of the population. [[Dizin language|Dizin]] was spoken as a first language by 83.42% of the inhabitants, and 15.3% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 1.28% spoke all other primary languages reported.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 24.74% of the population were considered literate; 15.67% of children aged 7–12 were in primary school; 7.85% of the children aged 13–14 were in junior secondary school, and 5.41% of the inhabitants aged 15–18 were in senior secondary school.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119202355/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 3.5, 3.7 (accessed 17 April 2009)</ref> Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], about 57% of the urban and 14% of the total had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119193032/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 6.3, 6.13 (accessed 17 April 2009)</ref>
