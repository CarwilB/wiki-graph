'''Banja Shekudad''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Amhara Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. It is named after a significant mountain located in the woreda, [[Mount Banja]], where [[Fasil of Damot|Fasil]] crushed a revolt of the [[Agaw]] in the late 18th century.<ref>[[James Bruce]], ''Travels to Discover the Source of the Nile'', selected and edited with an introduction by C.F. Beckingham (Edinburgh: University Press, 1964), p. 128.</ref> Part of the [[Agew Awi Zone]], Banja Shekudad is bordered on the south by [[Ankasha Guagusa]], on the west by [[Guangua]], on the north by [[Faggeta Lekoma]], on the east by the [[Mirab Gojjam Zone]], and on the southeast by [[Guagusa Shekudad]]. Towns in Banja Shekudad include [[Injibara]] and [[Kessa, Ethiopia|Kessa]]. Bodies of water in this woreda include the [[Zengena Lake|Zengena crater lake]]. Banja Shekudad was part of former [[Banja (woreda)|Banja]] woredas.

==Demographics==
Based on the 2007 national census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 111,975, of whom 55,611 are men and 56,364 women; 22,473 or 20.07% are urban inhabitants. The majority of the inhabitants practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 99.45% reporting that as their religion.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=266&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Amhara Region] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114004005/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=266&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=November 14, 2010 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4.</ref>
