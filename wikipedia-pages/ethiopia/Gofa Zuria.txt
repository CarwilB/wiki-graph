'''Gofa Zuria''' ([[Amharic language|Amharic]] "Greater Gofa Area") is a [[List of zones of Ethiopia|zone]] in the [[South Ethiopia Regional State]] of [[Ethiopia]]. It was previously one of the 17 Zones in the [[Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region]] (SNNPR) of [[Ethiopia]]. Within the SNNPR, Gofa Zone was bordered to the south by [[Kamba (woreda)|Kamba]] and [[Daramalo]] woredas, to the southwest by the [[Debub Omo Zone|Debub (South) Omo Zone]], to the west by the [[Basketo special woreda]], to the northwest by [[Dawro Zone]], to the north by the [[Dawro Zone]], and to the east by [[Kucha (woreda)|Kucha]]. The administrative center of Gofa Zone is [[Sawla]]; other towns included [[Bulki]]. Gofa Zone is administratively subdivided into the aforementioned towns of Bulki and Sawla and the seven [[woreda]]s of [[Demba Gofa]], [[Gada (woreda)|Gada]], [[Geze Gofa]], [[Melokoza]], [[Oyda (woreda)|Oyda]], [[Uba Debretsehay]], and [[Zala (woreda)|Zala]].

Gofa Zone is part of a region known for hilly and undulating midland and upper lowland terrain. It is prone to floods and landslides.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ethiopia landslides: Frantic digging for survivors in Gofa |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cyr728y2gz7o |access-date=2024-07-24 |website=www.bbc.com |language=en-GB}}</ref> Due to terrain and weather patterns, less than one in five households is food secure. Food crops include [[maize]], [[enset]], sweet potatoes, [[taro]], [[teff]], and yams; income sources include butter and selling firewood.<ref>[http://www.fews.net/livelihood/et/Baseline.pdf "Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region, Ethiopia Livelihood Profiles: January 2006"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110723130716/http://www.fews.net/livelihood/et/Baseline.pdf |date=July 23, 2011 }}, USAID/FEWSNET, p. 27 (accessed 11 January 2011)</ref> According to a 2004 report, Gofa Zuria had 75 kilometers of all-weather roads for an average road density of 44 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers.<ref>[http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls "Detailed statistics on roads"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151415/http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls |date=July 20, 2011 }}, SNNPR Bureau of Finance and Economic Development website (accessed 15 September 2009)</ref>
