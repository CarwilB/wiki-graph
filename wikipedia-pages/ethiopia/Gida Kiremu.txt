{{short description|District in Oromia Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Gida Ayana and Kiremu''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in the [[Oromia Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[East Welega Zone]], Gida Ayana and Kiremu is bordered on the south by [[Guto Gida]], on the west by [[Limmu (woreda)|Limmu]], on the northwest by [[Ibantu]], on the east by [[Horo Gudru Welega Zone]], and on the north by the [[Blue Nile]] river. The administrative center of the woreda is '''Gida Ayana'''; other towns include [[Gutin]] and [[Kiremu]].

This district is characterized by undulating hills north of Dicho Ridge and by plains south of it; it was once covered by extensive forests, but as of 2005 only a few fragments remain. Rivers within the woreda include the Werabessa, [[Wajja River|Wajja]], Chinia and Werabu. A survey of the land in this woreda shows that 65.7% is arable or cultivable (61% was under annual crops), 22.8% pasture, 8.7% forest, and the remaining 2.8% is considered unusable. [[Sesame]] and [[khat]] are two important cash crops.<ref name=Oromia-web>[http://oromiagov.org/Socio%20Economic%20Profile/East%20Wellega/East%20Wellega.pdf ''Socio-economic profile of the East Wellega Zone''] Government of Oromia Region (last accessed 1 August 2006).</ref> A third is [[Coffee]], but less than 20&nbsp;km<sup>2</sup> is planted with this crop.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20160815124929/http://www.oromiacoffeeunion.org/production.htm "Coffee Production"]}} Oromia Coffee Cooperative Union website</ref>

Industry in the district includes 13 [[grain mill]]s; [[granite]] has been identified as a potential commercial resource. There were 22 Farmers Associations with 19,168 members and 14 Farmers Service Cooperatives with 9,982 members. Gida Kiremu has 143 kilometers of all-weather road, for an average of road density of 60.3 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers. About 27% of the total population has access to [[drinking water]].<ref name=Oromia-web/>

== History ==
It is said to have been found in 1807 by a man named Ayane Goro. In February 2001, fighting between former drought victims from the Amhara region, who had been resettled in the East Wellega zone of the Oromia region, and Oromos in the Gida district resulted in the death of one Oromo policeman and several Amharas. Press reports indicated that there were hundreds of deaths throughout the year; however, the total number remained unknown at year's end.<ref>[https://2001-2009.state.gov/g/drl/rls/hrrpt/2001/af/8372.htm "Ethiopia: Country Reports on Human Rights Practices"], Bureau of Democracy, Human Rights, and Labor, US State Department (accessed 9 July 2009)</ref>  Other reports stated  that 12,000 [[Amhara people|Amhara]] settlers had been forced to flee due to the violence and that 500 houses were burned and 3,000 head of cattle stolen.<ref>[http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/g/ORTGIA.pdf "Local History in Ethiopia"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110528224956/http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/g/ORTGIA.pdf |date=2011-05-28 }} (pdf) The Nordic Africa Institute website (accessed 12 February 2008)</ref>

Despite these reports, Gida was selected two years later by the [[Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (Ethiopia)|Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development]] as an area for voluntary resettlement for farmers from overpopulated areas. Along with Guto Wayu and [[Jimma Arjo]], Gida became the home for a total of 8435 heads of households and 31,781 total family members in that year.<ref>[http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202003.pdf "Resettlement 2003"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080229100156/http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202003.pdf |date=2008-02-29 }}, Disaster Prevention and Preparedness Agency (DPPA) (accessed 26 November 2006)</ref> Gida was selected again in 2004 and along with Amuru Jarte, [[Bila Seyo]], Ibantu, Jimma Arjo, Limmu and [[Nunu Kumba]] became the new home of another 22,462 heads of households and 112,310 total family members.<ref>[http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202004.pdf "Resettlement 2004"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080227155134/http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202004.pdf |date=February 27, 2008 }}, DPPA (accessed 26 November 2006)</ref>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this district of 158,635, of whom 79,878 were men and 78,757 were women; 27,115 or 17.09% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 59.33% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 23.4% of the population said they were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], and 16.17% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Moslem]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111113202722/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=November 13, 2011 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this district has an estimated total population of 144,866, of whom 72,673 are men and 72,193 are women; 21,777 or 15.03% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 13.9%. With an estimated area of 2,370.94 square kilometers, Gida has an estimated population density of 61.1 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 81.4.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=November 23, 2006 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref> By area, it is the largest woreda in Misraq Welega Zone.
 
The 1994 national census reported a total population for this district of 101,766, of whom 50,805 were men and 50,961 women; 12,176 or 11.96% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The three largest ethnic groups reported in Gida Ayana were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (67.65%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (29.01%), and the [[Tigrayans|Tigray]] (3.25%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.09% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromo]] was spoken as a first language by 67.43%, 29.31% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]], and 3.19% spoke [[Tigrinya language|Tigrinya]]; the remaining 0.07% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 77.59% of the population reporting they observed this faith, while 15.45% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Moslem]], and 6.53% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091115040247/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf |date=November 15, 2009 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.13, 2.16, 2.20 (accessed 6 April 2009)</ref>
