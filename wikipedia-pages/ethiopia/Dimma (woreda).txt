'''Dimma''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Gambela Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Anuak Zone]], Dimma is bordered on the southeast by the [[Southern Nations, Nationalities and Peoples Region]] (SNNPR), on the southwest by the [[Akobo River]] which separates it from [[South Sudan]], on the north by [[Gog (woreda)|Gog]], and on the northeast by the [[Mezhenger Zone]]. The major town in Dimma is [[Dimma, Ethiopia|Dimma]].

Originally part of Gog woreda, at some point between the 1994 national census and the 2001 ''Sample Agricultural Enumeration'', Dimma was split from Gog. At first Dimma, along with Gog and [[Jor (Ethiopian District)|Jor]] woredas, were part of the [[Administrative Zone 2 (Gambela)|Administrative Zone 2]]; however between 2001 and 2007 the Zone was reorganized and Gog became part of the Anuak Zone.<ref>According to Dereje Feyissa this reorganization, which happened in 2003, was done to align territories inside the Gambela Region with the presence of local ethnic groups. (Dereje, "The Experience of the Gambela Regional State", in ''Ethnic Federalism: The Ethiopian Experience in Comparative Perspective'' [Oxford: James Currey, 2006], p. 223)</ref> The western part of [[Sheko (Ethiopian District)|Sheko]] from SNNPR was also added to Dimma.

On 30 January 2004, a group of [[Anuak people|Anuak]] rebels attacked a group of outsiders working the gold fields in Dimma. Reportedly 196 people were killed and 25 wounded, most of whom were traditional miners mainly from the SNNPR.<ref>[http://www.mfa.gov.et/Press_Section/publication.php?Page_Number=415 "The Current Situation in Gambella"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720154044/http://www.mfa.gov.et/Press_Section/publication.php?Page_Number=415 |date=2011-07-20 }}, Press Release from the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, Ministry of Federal Affairs</ref> This was considered the largest number of civilian casualties in single incident since the December 2003 killings in [[Gambela (city)|Gambela]].<ref>Medhane Tadesse, [http://www.issafrica.org/uploads/CPRDPAPERGAMBELLA.PDF "Gambella: The impact of local conflict on regional security"], p. 19</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 8,001, of whom 4,568 are men and 3,433 women; with an area of 7,762.43 square kilometers, Dimma has a population density of 1.03, which is less than the Zone average of 4.83 persons per square kilometer. Reportedly 2,103 or 26.28% are urban inhabitants. A total of 2,435 households were counted in this woreda, which results in an average of 3.3 persons to a household, and 2,203 housing units. The majority of the inhabitants said they were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], with 35.55% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 33.73% of the population practised [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], 17.52% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], and 7.61% practiced traditional religions.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=270&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Gambela Region] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114005224/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=270&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=November 14, 2010 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, and 3.4.</ref>
