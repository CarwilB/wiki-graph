'''Melokoza''' is one of the [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]]s in the [[Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Gamo Gofa Zone]], Melokoza is bordered on the south by [[Basketo special woreda]], on the southwest by the [[Debub Omo Zone|Debub (South) Omo Zone]], on the northwest by the [[Konta special woreda]], on the north by the [[Dawro Zone]], and on the east by [[Demba Gofa]] and [[Geze Gofa]]; the [[Omo River (Ethiopia)|Omo River]] defines its northwestern boundary separating the woreda from Konta and the Dawro Zone. The major town in Melokoza is [[Leha]].

== Overview ==
Food crops in Melokoza include [[enset]], sweet potatoes and yams, maize and [[horse bean]]s, while coffee and the cardamom-like spice ''aframomum'' are significant cash crops.<ref>[http://www.fews.net/livelihood/et/Baseline.pdf "Southern Nations and Region of people in Ethiopia Livelihood Profiles: January 2006"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110723130716/http://www.fews.net/livelihood/et/Baseline.pdf |date=2011-07-23 }}, USAID/FEWSNET, p. 27 (accessed 11 January 2011)</ref> According to a 2004 report, this woreda had no reported roads or tracks.<ref>[http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls "Detailed statistics on roads"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151415/http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls |date=2011-07-20 }}, SNNPR Bureau of Finance and Economic Development website (accessed 15 September 2009)</ref>

A September 2007 landslide in Melokoza was reported to have killed three people, displaced 42 households, and damaged 15 hectares of crops.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20100929114853/http://ocha-eth.org/Reports/downloadable/FocusonEthiopiaOctober2007.pdf "Focus on Ethiopia, October 2007"]}}, UN-OCHA Archive (accessed 24 February 2009)</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the CSA, this woreda has a total population of 120,398, of whom 59,877 are men and 60,521 women; 3,277 or 2.72% of its population are urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], with 56.22% of the population reporting that belief, 32.87% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 6% practiced traditional beliefs.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113220306/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2012-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, and 3.4.</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 74,992 of whom 37,349 were males and 37,573 were females; 1,351 or 1.8% of its population were urban dwellers. The five largest ethnic groups reported in Melokoza were the [[Goffa people|Goffa]] (49.75%), the [[Mello people|Melo]] (24.74%), the [[Basketo people|Basketo]] (21.9%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (1.99%), and the [[Dime people|Dime]] (0.75%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.87% of the population. [[Gofa language|Goffa]] is spoken as a first language by 40.49%, 30.94% [[Basketo language|Basketo]], 26.33% [[Melo language|Melo]], and 0.85% speak [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 1.39% spoke all other primary languages reported.<ref name=census>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> However, Ralph Siebert's local research in 1995 led him to believe that this woreda was predominantly inhabited by the Goffa people, although in the same report he notes that "Laha is one of the main places in the Melo area, and the variety [of language] spoken there is similar to Gofa".<ref>Ralph Siebert, [https://www.sil.org/silesr/2002/SILESR2002-058.pdf "Recent Developments Regarding Education Policy and Languages in the North Omo Administrative Region"] SIL Electronic Survey Reports SILESR 2002-058, p.6</ref> Concerning religious beliefs, the 1994 census reported that 37.47% of the population said they practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], 29.04% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], and 28.03% observed traditional religions.<ref name=census/>
