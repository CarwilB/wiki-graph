'''Tanqua Millash''' ({{Langx|ti|ጣንቋ ምላሽ}}) is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in the [[Tigray Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Located in the [[Maekelay Zone|Central Tigray]] zone, Tanqua Millash was created from parts of the [[Abergele_(Ethiopian_District)|Tanqua Abergelle]] and [[Dogu’a Tembien]] in 2020. [[Agbe]] is the capital of this district.

== Geography ==
The literal translation of Tanqua Millash is "beyond (south of) [[Tanqua]] River". Agriculture forms the mainstay of Tanqua Millash’s economic life. As elsewhere in most districts of Tembien, social services were hardly developed until the 1990s. After that, schools and health centers of the calling have emerged. The backwardness was partly attributed to the region's relative geographic remoteness from the center.<ref name=":0" /> 

== History ==
Tanqua Millash was a constituent of the old [[Tembien Province|Tembien]] [[awrajja]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Igziabher |first=Taddesse Gabre |title=Power Struggle in Tigray During Zamana Masafint |publisher=Haile Sellassie I University |year=1972 |location=Addis Ababa}}</ref>

In 2020, the earlier district Abergele became inoperative and its territory belongs to the following new districts:
*Abergele (new, smaller, district)
*Tanqua Millash district (part of it)
Tanqua Millash comprises the municipalities of [[Simret]], [[Menachek]] and [[Mizan (Dogu'a Tembien) |Mizan]] which were transferred from [[Dogu'a Tembien]].

== Agriculture ==
===Cropping===
A sample enumeration performed by the CSA in 2001 interviewed 15,123 farmers in this district, who held an average of 1.63 hectares of land. Of the 24,690 hectares of private land surveyed, 87% was in cultivation, 0.2% pasture, 11% fallow, and 1.7% was devoted to other uses. For the land under cultivation in this district, 82% was planted in cereals, 2.3% in pulses, 3% in oilseeds, and 0.02% in vegetables. Four hectares were planted in fruit trees and 18 in [[gesho]]. Land tenure in this district is distributed amongst 85% owning their land, and 15% renting; the percentage reported as holding their land under other forms of tenure is missing.<ref name="auto">[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Agricultural_Sample_Enumeration_2001/Agricultural_Sample_Enumeration_2001(Land_Use)/survey0/data/docs%5Cpdf%5Creport%5CTigray%20land%20use.pdf "Central Statistical Authority of Ethiopia. ===Livestock===
In 2001, 89% of the farmers both raised crops and livestock, while 10% only grew crops and 0.5% only raised livestock.<ref name="auto" />
[[File:Abergele_cattle.jpg|thumb|right|Abergele cattle, a landrace that is very popular in Tanqua Millash]]
Mainly used for draught, among the different cattle landraces in Tigray,<ref name="merha2007">{{Cite journal |last=Merha Zerabruk |last2=Vangen |first2=O |last3=Mitiku Haile |date=2007 |title=The status of cattle genetic resources in North Ethiopia: On-farm characterization of six major cattle breeds |journal=Animal Genetic Resources Information |volume=40 |pages=15–32 |doi=10.1017/S1014233900002169}}</ref><ref name="ch28">{{Cite book |title=Cattle breeds, milk production, and transhumance in Dogu'a Tembien. In: Geotrekking in Ethiopia's tropical mountains, Chapter 28 |date=2019 |publisher=SpringerNature |location=Cham |doi=10.1007/978-3-030-04955-3_28}}</ref> Jointly with Abergele, Tanqua Millash holds its own landrace, the small and resistant [[Abergele cattle]].<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Merha Zerabruk |last2=Vangen |first2=O |date=2005 |title=The Abergelle and Irob cattle breeds of North Ethiopia: description and on-farm characterization |journal=Animal Genetic Resources Information Bulletin |volume=36 |pages=7–20 |doi=10.1017/S101423390000184X}}</ref>
Other landraces include the [[Arado cattle]], the widely dominant variety of adjacent districts. In addition, long-horned [[Raya cattle|Raya]] oxen are purchased from [[Southern Tigray]] as draught animals.
