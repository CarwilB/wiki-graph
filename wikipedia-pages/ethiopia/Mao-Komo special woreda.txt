'''Mao-Komo''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Benishangul-Gumuz Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. Because it is not part of any [[Zones of Ethiopia|zones]] in Benishangul-Gumuz, it is considered a Special woreda, an administrative subdivision which is similar to an [[autonomous area]]. The southernmost woreda in the Region, Mao-Komo is bordered on the west by [[Sudan]] and [[South Sudan]], on the north by the [[Asosa Zone]], and on the east and south by the [[Oromia Region]]. Towns in this woreda include [[Tongo, Ethiopia|Tongo]], which has a weekly market. The Tongo refugee camp, housing 12,483 displaced people from Sudan and South Sudan, is also located in Mao-Komo.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://data.unhcr.org/Sudanese_Refugees_in_Ethiopia/settlement.php?id%3D29%26country%3D65%26region%3D23 |title=Displaced Sudanese |accessdate=2012-05-23 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20120130003516/http://data.unhcr.org/Sudanese_Refugees_in_Ethiopia/settlement.php?id=29&country=65&region=23 |archivedate=2012-01-30 }}, The UN Refugee Agency website</ref>

The region and its inhabitants were described by the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] explorer [[Juan Maria Schuver]], who travelled to the area in 1880-1883.<ref>Gerd Baumann, Douglas H. Johnson and [[Wendy James]] (editors), ''Juan Maria Schuver's Travels in North East Africa 1880-1883'' (London: Hakluyt Society, 1996). {{ISBN|090418045X}}</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 50,061, of whom 25,055 are men and 25,006 women. 3,392 or 6.78% of population are urban inhabitants. A total of 9,844 households were counted in this woreda, which results in an average of 5.08 persons to a household, and 9,503 housing units. The five largest ethnic groups reported in the Mao-Komo special woreda were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (35.1%), the [[Mao people]] (26.42%), the [[Berta people|Berta]] (18.8%), the [[Kwama people|Kwama or Komo]] (14.46%), and the [[Fadashi people|Fadashi]] (4.12%); all other ethnic groups made up 1.1% of the population. Main languages are [[Oromo language|Oromo]] (47.51%), [[Berta language|Berta]] (17.47%), [[Mao languages|Mao]] (15.61%), [[Kwama language|Kwama]] (13.7%), and [[Fadashi language|Fadashi]] (4.91%). The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 94.56% of the population reporting that they held that belief, while 2.64% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], and 2.13% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=268&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Benishangul-Gumuz Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114004725/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=268&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2010-11-14 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4.</ref>

Based on figures from the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] (CSA) in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 18,668, of whom 9,110 are men and 9,558 are women. With an estimated area of 1,792.66 square kilometers, Mao-Komo has a population density of 10.4 people per square kilometer which is less than the Zone average of 19.95.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2006-11-23 }}, Table B.3</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 14,071 in 3,086 households, of whom 6,899 were men and 7,172 were women; no urban dwellers were recorded. The six largest ethnic groups reported in Mao-Komo were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (52%), the [[Fadashi people|Fadashi]] (21.4%), the [[Berta people|Berta]] (12%), the [[Mao people]] (8%), the [[Kwama people|Kwama or Komo]] (5%) and the [[Gumuz people|Gumuz]] (0.7%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.9% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] is spoken as a first language by 58%, 29% speak [[Fadashi language|Fadashi]], 6% speak [[Mao languages|Mao]] one of the [[North Omotic languages|northern group]] of [[Omotic languages]], 5% [[Kwama language|Kwama]], 1% [[Berta language|Berta]], and 0.7% speak [[Gumuz language|Gumuz]]; the remaining 0.3% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 95.6% of the population reporting the followed that religion, while 4% observed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>''1994 Population and Housing Census, Vol. 1'', Tables 2.1, 2.7, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19</ref> The Oromo, who arrived to the area in the 18th and 19th century, tend to live in the highlands, whereas the Nilo-Saharan communities (Kwama, Gwama, Mao, Ganza) usually occupy the lowland savannas.

Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 0.89% of the population were considered literate; a negligible number of children aged 7-12 were in primary school as well as children aged 13-14 in junior secondary school, while none of the inhabitants aged 15-18 were in senior secondary school. Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 6.6% of all houses had access to safe drinking water, and 2.7% had toilet facilities at the time of the 1994 census.<ref>''1994 Population and Housing Census, Vol. 1'', Tables 3.5, 3.7, 6.11, 6.13</ref>
