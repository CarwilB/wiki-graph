'''Rabaso''' ({{langx|so|Rabaso}}),<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://nai.uu.se/library/resources/thematicresources/local_history_of_ethiopia/d/ORTDA.pdf |title=Archived copy |access-date=2019-11-23 |archive-date=2018-01-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180101030616/http://nai.uu.se/library/resources/thematicresources/local_history_of_ethiopia/d/ORTDA.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> is a town in the  [[Misraq_Gashamo|Daror]]  of Ethiopia.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.somaliaonline.com/community/topic/ethiopia-daroor-district-the-newest-district-of-dhagaxbuur-province-somali-region-of-ethiopia/|title=Ethiopia: rabaso district the newest district of Dhagaxbuur province Somali region of Ethiopia – Somalia Online|website=www.somaliaonline.com|accessdate=30 May 2017}}{{Dead link|date=April 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> Part of the [[Jarar Zone|Jarar zone]], Rabaso is bordered on the south by [[Aware (woreda)|Aware]], on the west by [[Gashamo|Daror]], on the north by the [[Burco-Duuray]], on the northeast by [[Somaliland]], and on the east by [[Shabelle]]. Nearby town include [[Celiyo]], and [[Duduma Cad]].

==History==
According to the 2007 census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA) the town had a population of 4,042.<ref name="towns">{{cite web|title=Population and Housing Census 2007 – Somali Statistical|url=https://www.statsethiopia.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Somali_Statistical.pdf|pages=9<!--endif p.totales-->|language=en|publication-date=|access-date=2022-04-12|archive-date=2022-09-27|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220927075615/https://www.statsethiopia.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Somali_Statistical.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2005 Daroor had 40,000 inhabitants, according to the Ethiopian [[Central Statistical Agency]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.csa.gov.et/images/documents/pdf_files/nationalstatisticsabstract/2005/2005%20population.pdf|title=2005 National statistics, section-B population, table b.4 (PDF)|website=csa.gov.et|accessdate=30 May 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160204031922/http://www.csa.gov.et/images/documents/pdf_files/nationalstatisticsabstract/2005/2005%20population.pdf|archive-date=4 February 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 1997 of 9,397 inhabitants 99,47% of these were [[Somali (people)|Somali]], and 50 residents were from other ethnic groups.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.csa.gov.et/newcsaweb/images/documents/surveys/population%20and%20housing%20census%201994/survey0/data/docs/report/statistical_report/k05/k05.pdf|title=the 1994 population and housing census of Ethiopia: results for Somali region, volume I: part I. statistical report on population size characteristics, 1998 (PDF), S. 73|website=csa.gov.et|accessdate=30 May 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923212114/http://www.csa.gov.et/newcsaweb/images/documents/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs/report/Statistical_Report/K05/K05.pdf|archive-date=23 September 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 1988 a refugee camp was set up for Somalis. The refugee camp housed predominantly members of the Eidagale - Garhajis [[Isaaq]] clans from nearby [[Somaliland]]. The camp was poorly equipped. The camp's population fell from around 32.000 on 12.000 in September 1994. After renewed fighting in November, the population rose to 49.000.<ref>Guido ambroso: [http://www.unhcr.org/3d5d0f3a4.pdf pastoral society and transnational refugees: population movements in Somaliland and Eastern Ethiopia 1988-2000. new issues in refugee research, working paper No. 65], UNHCR - evaluation and policy analysis unit, 2002 (PDF; 492&nbsp;survey)</ref> At the end of 2001 / the beginning of 2002 the camp was closed after most refugees voluntarily went home.<ref>[http://www.unhcr.org/cgi-bin/texis/vtx/search?page=search&docid=3c3332a94 return of 2,400 Somali refugees leads to closing of third camp in Ethiopia], February 2, 2002.</ref>

The economy of the place was strongly affected when the [[Saudi Arabia]]n 1998 import of cattle from northeast Africa stopped.<ref>Joachim D. Ahrens, field officer, UNDP emergencies unit for Ethiopia: [http://www.africa.upenn.edu/eue_web/livestock.htm cessation of livestock export severely affects the pastoralist economy of Somali region (mission: 31 March to April 7, 1998]</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia, 100% of the population said they were [[Muslim]]. The Center is inhabited by the [[Eidagale]] [[Isaaq]] clan.

The 1997 national census reported that 21.79% of its population were urban dwellers. The largest ethnic group reported was the [[Somalis|Somali]].

==Education==
Rabaso has two schools. One is Primary and Intermediate school and the other is Secondary School.
