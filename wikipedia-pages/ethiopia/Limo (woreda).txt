'''Leemo''' (also spelled '''Lemo''') is one of the [[woreda]]s in the [[Central Ethiopia Regional State]] of [[Ethiopia]]. The relationship of the name of this woreda to that of the [[Limmu (Ethiopia)|medieval kingdom]] in the [[Gibe region]] is unclear. A part of the [[Hadiya Zone]], Limo is bordered on the south by the [[Kembata Tembaro Zone]], on the southwest by [[Duna (woreda)|Duna]] and [[Soro (woreda)|Soro]], on the west by [[Gomibora]], on the northwest by [[Misha (woreda)|Misha]], on the northeast by [[Ana Lemo]], and on the southeast by [[Shashogo]]. Towns in Lemo include [[Belesa]] and [[Lisana]]. The town of [[Hosaena]] is surrounded by Limo. Parts of Limo woreda were separated to create Ana Lemo, Hosaena, [[Mirab Azernet Berbere]] and [[Misraq Azernet Berbere]] woredas.

Limo has 67 kilometers of all-weather roads and 56 kilometers of dry-weather roads, for an average road density of 123 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers.<ref>[http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls "Detailed statistics on roads"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151415/http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls |date=2011-07-20 }}, SNNPR Bureau of Finance and Economic Development website (accessed 15 September 2009)</ref>

During the [[Ethiopian general election, 2000|2000 general elections]], when voters at the polling station in Nabor [[kebele]] protested [[Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front]] cadres, kebele officials and even [[National Election Board of Ethiopia]] officials coercing voters to vote for the ruling party over the [[Hadiya National Democratic Organization]], "the officials and cadres became nervous and the police were ordered to shoot into the crowd of protesting voters. People panicked and ran away, but left on the ground were two dead women and several injured."<ref>[[International Law and Policy Institute|Kjetil Tronvoll]], [https://www.jstor.org/stable/3557346 "Voting, Violence and Violations: Peasant Voices on the Flawed Elections in Hadiya, Southern Ethiopia", ''Journal of Modern African Studies''], 39 (2001), pp. 709f. Tronvoll adds that he was in the vicinity when this happened.</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the CSA, this woreda has a total population of 118,594, of whom 58,666 are men and 59,928 women; 2,049 or 1.73% of its population are urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], with 74.07% of the population reporting that belief, 12.37% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], 7.2% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 6.14% were [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113220306/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2012-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, and 3.4.</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 287,430 of whom 143,587 were males and 143,843 were females; 8,743 or 3.04% of its population were urban dwellers. The five largest ethnic groups reported in Konteb were the [[Hadiya people|Hadiya]] (62.13%), the [[Silte people|Silte]] (30.3%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (3.05%), the [[Kambaata people|Kambaata]] (2.67%), and the [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet Gurage]] (0.45%); all other ethnic groups made up 3.4% of the population. [[Hadiya language|Hadiya]] is spoken as a first language by 57.81%, 31.35% [[Silte language|Silte]], 6.63% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]] and 3.36% spoke [[Kambaata language|Kambaata]]; the remaining 0.85% spoke all other primary languages reported. 58.52% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], 22.09% embraced [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], 18.36% were [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 0.45% [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 30.97% of the population were considered literate, which is less than the Zone average of 33.01%.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119202355/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Table 3.7 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 68.48% of the urban houses and 21.33% of all houses had access to safe drinking water at the time of the census; 48.52% of the urban and 5.71% of all houses had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 4] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119193032/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 6.11, 6.13 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>
