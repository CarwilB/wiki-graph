'''Meskanena Mareko''' ("Meskane and Mareko") was one of the 77 [[woreda]]s in the [[Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Gurage Zone]], Meskanena Mareko was bordered on the south by [[Silte (woreda)|Silte]], on the southwest by [[Gumer]], on the west by [[Ezhana Wolene]], on the northwest by [[Kokir Gedebano Gutazer]], on the north by [[Sodo (woreda)|Sodo]], and on the east by the [[Oromia Region]]. Towns in Meskanena Mareko included [[Butajira]] and [[Inseno]]. Meskanena Mareko was divided for [[Mareko (woreda)|Mareko]] and [[Meskane]] woredas and Butajira town.

Near Butajira are the Tufta Swamps at {{coord|8|8|N|38|24|E}}, located in the Western highlands near the headwaters of the [[Meki River]].<ref>Robert Mepham, R. H. Hughes, and J. S. Hughes, [https://books.google.com/books?id=VLjafeXa3gMC&pg=PP1 ''A directory of African wetlands''], (Cambridge: IUCN, UNEP and WCMC, 1992), p. 169</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 331,832, of whom 166,875 are men and 164,957 are women; 53,343 or 16.08% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 6.3%. With an estimated area of 872.5 square kilometers, Meskanena Mareko has an estimated [[population density]] of 380.3 people per square kilometer, which is greater than the Zone average of 278.3.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080731152750/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2008-07-31 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 227,135 of whom 113,095 were males and 114,040 were females; 29,440 or 12.96% of its population were urban dwellers. The five largest ethnic groups reported in Meskanena Mareko were the Meskan sub-group<ref>cf. Dirk Bustorf 2005: “Mäsqan ethnography”. In: ''Encyclopaedia Aethiopica''. vol. 3: He-N. Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz. pp. 847-48</ref> of the [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet]] [[Gurage people|Gurage]] (35.62%), the [[Silte people|Silte]] (22.36%), the Mareqo or [[Libido people|Libido]] (15.7%), the [[Soddo language|Soddo Gurage]] (12.06%) and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (3.65%); all other ethnic groups made up 10.61% of the population. [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet Gurage]] is spoken as a [[first language]] by 32.25%, 21.8% [[Silte language|Silte]], 14.7% speak [[Libido language|Libido]], 11.35% speak [[Soddo language|Soddo Gurage]], and 9.88% speak [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 10.02% spoke all other primary languages reported. 62.32% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], 32.70% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 1.77% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.14, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 22.8% of the population were considered literate, which is more than the Zone average of 20.62%; 7.75% of children aged 7–12 were in primary school, 1.8% of the children aged 13–14 were in junior secondary school, and 1.82% of the inhabitants aged 15–18 were in senior secondary school.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119202355/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 3.5, 3.7 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 74.95% of the urban houses and 19.98% of all houses had access to safe drinking water at the time of the census; 52.98% of the urban and 8.49% of all houses had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 4] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119193032/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 6.11, 6.13 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>
