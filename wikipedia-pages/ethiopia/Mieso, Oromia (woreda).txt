'''Mieso''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Oromia Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[West Hararghe Zone]], Mieso is bordered on the south by [[Guba Koricha]], on the west by the [[Afar Region]], on the north by the [[Somali Region]], on the east by [[Doba (woreda)|Doba]] and on the southeast by [[Chiro (woreda)|Chiro]]. The administrative center for this woreda is [[Mieso]]; other towns in Mieso include [[Asabot|Asebot]], [[Kora, Ethiopia|Kora]], Bordode and Gololcha.

== Overview ==
The altitude of Mieso ranges from 1107 to 3106 meters above sea level; the highest point is [[Mount Asabot]] (1523 meters). Rivers include the Beke. A survey of the land in Mieso shows that 11.5% is arable or cultivable (10.7% of the total area was under annual crops), 23.7% cultivable if water were available, 8.9% pasture, 28.7% forest or brushland, and the remaining 27.3% is considered hilly, built-up or otherwise unusable. [[Sesame]] and [[haricot bean]]s are important cash crops.<ref name=IPMS>[http://www.ipms-ethiopia.org/content/files/Documents/PLS-DPD/Meiso.pdf "Mieso Pilot Learning Site Diagnosis and Program Design"] IPMS Information Resources Portal - Ethiopia (July 2005), pp. 6-20 (accessed 10 March 2009)</ref> Local points of interest in Mieso woreda include the [[Monastery of Asabot Selassie]], located at the top of Mount Asabot, 20 kilometers northeast of the town of Asabot.<ref>Philip Briggs, ''Ethiopia: The Bradt Travel Guide'', 3rd edition (Chalfont St Peters: Bradt, 2002), p. 351</ref> This woreda is served by the [[Ethio-Djibouti Railways]], with stops at Arba Bordode, Asabot, Kora and Mieso.

In 8 of the 37 [[kebele]]s, the predominant agricultural practice is pastoralism; some pastoralists are sedentary and other migrate with their herds in search of forage and water. Cattle and goats are the most common livestock, and the vegetation is primarily [[acacia]] with grass cover beneath. Mieso reportedly has become, since 2003, one of the major markets of goats supplying the slaughterhouses in [[Mojo, Ethiopia|Mojo]] and [[Metehara]] which export [[mutton]]. In 2005 there were 5 goat meatpacking companies based in this woreda, which included [[ELFORA|ELFORA Agro-Industries]].<ref name=IPMS/>

There are seven non–agricultural cooperatives organized in this woreda. Three of these are multipurpose, another three specialize in [[Mining in Ethiopia|mining]], while the last one is dedicated to saving and credit. There are also 13 farmers' cooperatives: 9 multipurpose, 2 credit and saving, and 2 dairy cooperatives run by women which are at the beginning stage of organization. There is also one self-initiated cooperative in Oda Roba kebele, for the purpose of crop harvesting. There are no [[micro-finance]] institutions in Mieso.<ref name=IPMS/>

== History ==
It was in this woreda that Emperor [[Iyasu V of Ethiopia|Iyasu V]], hurrying back to the capital city of [[Addis Ababa]] to deal with a palace coup, was defeated by the plotters in October, 1916.<ref>{{cite book |last= Henze|first= Paul B. |title= Layers of Time, A History of Ethiopia|year= 2000|publisher= Palgrave|location= New York|isbn=0-312-22719-1|page = 195}}</ref> In the 1950s, the woreda was one of the about fifteen most important cotton production areas
in Ethiopia outside of Eritrea.<ref>[http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/m/ORTMI.pdf "Local History in Ethiopia"] (pdf) The Nordic Africa Institute website (accessed 11 December 2007)</ref>

Numerous conflicts between the local [[Oromo people|Oromos]] and [[Somali people|Somalis]] in Mieso followed the October 2004 referendum to establish the disputed boundary between the Oromia and [[Somali Region]]s; over 2,500 people displaced from their homes in December 2004 had sought refuge in the woreda. Conflicts had erupted at Mieso by mid-February.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20101006032021/http://ocha-eth.org/Reports/downloadable/ReliefBulletin21February2005.pdf "Relief Bulletin: 21 February 2005"]}}, UN-OCHA-Ethiopia (accessed 26 February 2009)</ref> [[NGO]]s working in the area reported these conflicts continued in the town of Mieso as late as 14 July 2005.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20170118051231/http://www.ocha-eth.org/Reports/downloadable/FocusonEthiopiaJuly2005.pdf "Regional Overview: Oromiya Region", ''Focus on Ethiopia'']}} (July 2005), p. 6 (accessed 11 February 2009)</ref>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 130,709, of whom 66,891 were men and 63,818 were women; 25,388 or 19.42% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants (97.06%) said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], while 2.66% of the population practised [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111113202722/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2011-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 137,126, of whom 66,553 are men and 70,573 are women; 36,165 or 26.37% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 9.6%. With an estimated area of 2,573.44 square kilometers, Mieso has an estimated population density of 53.3 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 101.8.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080731152750/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2008-07-31 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 91,572, of whom 47,061 were men and 44,511 women; 18,073 or 19.74% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The four largest ethnic groups reported in Mieso were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (88.09%), the [[Somali people|Somali]] (5.77%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (3.46%), and the [[Argobba people|Argobba]] (0.66%); all other ethnic groups made up 2.02% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 88.37%, 5.61% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]], and 5.29% spoke [[Somali language|Somali]]; the remaining 0.73% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Moslem]], with 95.23% of the population reporting they practiced that belief, while 4.55% of the population said they professed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091115040247/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf |date=2009-11-15 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.13, 2.16, 2.20 (accessed 6 April 2009)</ref>

This woreda is primarily inhabited by the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] from the Ittu clan and the [[Hawiye]] from the [[Somali people|Somalis]], with it being disputed by both regional states, where the Hawiye inhabit the Bordode and Kora areas of the woreda as well as the full Mieso woreda in the [[Somali Region]].
