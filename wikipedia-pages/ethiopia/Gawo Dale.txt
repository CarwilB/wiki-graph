{{Short description|Former district in Oromia Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Gawo Dale''' was one of the [[woreda]]s in the [[Oromia Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Mirab Welega Zone]], Gawo Dale was bordered on the south by [[Hawa Welele]], on the west by [[Jimma Gidami]], on the northwest by [[Begi, Oromia (woreda)|Begi]], on the north by [[Jarso (Welega)|Jarso]], on the northeast by [[Ayra Guliso]], on the east by [[Dale Lalo]], and on the southeast by the [[Illubabor Zone]]. The administrative center of this woreda was [[Kake, Ethiopia|Kake]]; other towns in Gawo Dale included [[Chanka, Ethiopia|Chanka]]. It was separated for [[Dale Wabera]] and [[Gawo Kebe]] woredas.

== Overview ==
Elevations in this woreda range from 1150 to 3330 meters above sea level; the highest point is Guri Maryam. Rivers include the Qeto. Natural vegetation covers 444.34 square kilometers of Gawo Dale and 172.18 square kilometers are covered by [[Forestry in Ethiopia|forest]]. It has been estimated that 281.15 square kilometers are arable. Important crops produced include [[maize|corn]], [[sorghum]], [[finger millet]], [[sesame]], pepper, and [[navy bean]]s. Land is tilled using indigenous plough drawn by oxen.<ref>Asfaw Keno Werka, [http://etd.aau.edu.et/dspace/bitstream/123456789/738/1/ASFAW%20KENO%20WERKA.pdf "Resettlement Dynamics in Ethiopia: with Special Reference to Qeto Resettlement Area, West Wellega Zone, Oromia Region"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151936/http://etd.aau.edu.et/dspace/bitstream/123456789/738/1/ASFAW%20KENO%20WERKA.pdf |date=2011-07-20 }}, Master's Thesis, Graduate School of the University of Addis Ababa (February 2006)</ref> [[Coffee]] is an important cash crop of this woreda; over 50 square kilometers are planted with this crop.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20160815124929/http://www.oromiacoffeeunion.org/production.htm "Coffee Production"]}} Oromia Coffee Cooperative Union website</ref>

There are 65 primary schools in this woreda and 2 secondary education schools. [[Health in Ethiopia|Health services]] are provided by one health center, 11 clinics, and 10 health posts; most of these facilities are located in urban areas.<ref>Span Consultants Ltd, ''et al.'' [http://www-wds.worldbank.org/external/default/main?pagePK=64193027&piPK=64187937&theSitePK=523679&menuPK=64187510&searchMenuPK=57313&theSitePK=523679&entityID=000333038_20090324021236&searchMenuPK=57313&theSitePK=523679 "Mekenajo - Dembidolo Road Upgrading Project, Revised Final EIA Report"], World Bank website (March 2009), pp. 30f</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 155,984, of whom 78,699 are men and 77,285 are women; 13,422 or 8.60% of its population are urban dwellers, which is less than the Zone average of 10.9%. With an estimated area of 1,949.85 square kilometers, Gawo Dale has an estimated population density of 80 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 91.7.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=November 23, 2006 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 111,251 in 20,345 households, of whom 55,276 were men and 55,975 were women; 7,495 or 6.74% of its population were urban dwellers. The two largest ethnic groups reported in Gawo Dale were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (93.1%), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (6.26%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.67% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 91.91%, and 7.41% [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.41% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants observed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 46.09% reporting that as their religion, while 28.77% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], and 24.49% [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091115040247/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf |date=November 15, 2009 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.16, 2.20, Annex II.2 (accessed 9 April 2009)</ref>
