'''Damot Gale''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|district]] in [[South Ethiopia Regional State]], [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Wolayita Zone]]. Damot Gale is bordered on the southwest by [[Sodo Zuria]], on the northwest by [[Boloso Sore]] and [[Damot Pulasa]], on the north by the [[Hadiya Zone]], on the east by [[Diguna Fango]], and on the southeast by [[Damot Weyde]]. The administrative center of Damot Gale is [[Boditi]]. Damot Pulasa woreda was separated from Damot Gale.

Damot Gale has 29 kilometers of asphalt roads, 1 kilometer of all-weather road and 57 kilometers of dry-weather roads, for an average road density of 209 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers.<ref>[http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls " Detailed statistics on roads"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151415/http://www.snnprbofed.gov.et/Reports/Roads.xls |date=July 20, 2011 }}, SNNPR Bureau of Finance and Economic Development website (accessed 3 September 2009)</ref>

== History ==
A megalithic site with four steles has been found within Damot Gale.<ref>[http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/d/ORTDAK.pdf "Local History in Ethiopia"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081217170140/http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/d/ORTDAK.pdf |date=2008-12-17 }} The Nordic Africa Institute website (accessed 3 May 2009)</ref>

In March 1996, Damot Gale suffered a [[hail]] [[storm]] that destroyed not only an estimated 625 hectares of crop land but the roofs of 266 houses. The number of households affected was 1,956, whom the local Committee for Disaster Prevention and Preparation provided with tents and plastic sheets, and to whom the [[NGO]] [[Redd Barna]] handed out crop seeds.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20050122174547/http://www.ocha-eth.org/Archive/DownloadableReports/Wolay96apr.pdf "Field Trip Report to Welayita and North Omo (2 - 6 April)"]}}, UNDP-EUE Report (accessed 19 February 2009)</ref>

With the assistance of a €76,200 loan from the [[Ethiopian-Greek relations|Greek]] government, {{As of|2006|lc=on}} construction is underway to build the Damte earthen [[dam]], a pilot project which will provide a reliable source of [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|drinkable water]] for the inhabitants of this woreda. The Ethiopian government hopes to use this project as an example in other areas of the country that faces a water shortage.<ref>[http://waltainfo.com/EnNews/2006/Sep/02Sep06/17529.htm "Greece finances earth dam construction in SNNPS"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070311002510/http://waltainfo.com/EnNews/2006/Sep/02Sep06/17529.htm |date=2007-03-11 }} ([[Walta Information Center|WIC]])</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2019 population projection conducted by the CSA,<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.statsethiopia.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Projected-Population-of-Ethiopia-20112019.pdf |title=Projected population of Ethiopia |access-date=2021-07-28 |archive-date=2021-07-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210728101857/https://www.statsethiopia.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Projected-Population-of-Ethiopia-20112019.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> this woreda has a total population of 467,245, of whom 233,500 are men and 233,745 women; 24,133 or 15.97% of its population are urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], with 49.15% of the population reporting that belief, 39.71% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], 11.11% were [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]], and 1.38% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113220306/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=273&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=November 13, 2012 }}, Tables 2.1, and 3.4.</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 217,336 of whom 107,201 were men and 109,239 were women; 13,400 or 6.17% of its population were urban dwellers. The four largest ethnic groups reported in Damot Gale were the [[Welayta people|Welayta]] (94.68%), the [[Hadiya people|Hadiya]] (2.78%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (0.78%), and the Mareqo or [[Libido people|Libido]] (0.77%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.99% of the population. [[Welayta language|Welayta]] is spoken as a first language by 96.39%, 1.62% [[Hadiya language|Hadiya]], 0.77% [[Amharic language|Amharic]], and 0.61% speak [[Libido language|Libido]]; the remaining 0.61% spoke all other primary languages reported. Concerning religious beliefs, the 1994 census reported that 42.33% of the population said they were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], 34.1% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 19.94% were [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Roman Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=November 19, 2008 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>
