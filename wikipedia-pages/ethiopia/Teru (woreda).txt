'''Teru''' is one of the [[Districts of Ethiopia]], or ''woredas'', in the [[Afar Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[Administrative Zone 4 (Afar)|Administrative Zone 4]], Teru is bordered on the south by [[Aura (Ethiopian District)|Aura]], on the southwest by [[Gulina]], on the west by [[Yalo (Ethiopian District)|Yalo]], and on the north and east by the [[Administrative Zone 2 (Afar)|Administrative Zone 2]]. The major settlement in this woreda is [[Alelo]].

== Overview ==
There are two rivers in this woreda, the Awra and Megale, but {{as of|2004|lc=on}}, they have "changed their course and ... [their] water disappears in deep cracks in the ground". [[Deforestation in Ethiopia|Deforestation]] is a problem in Teru. There are two roads in the woreda, but both are in poor condition.<ref name=APDA>Afar Pastoralist Development Association, [http://www.utviklingsfondet.no/filestore/Afar_Dev_Conf_Document.pdf "Document of Afar Development Conference Aysaita, December 15-30, 2004"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110728175401/http://www.utviklingsfondet.no/filestore/Afar_Dev_Conf_Document.pdf |date=2011-07-28 }} (accessed 13 January 2009)</ref> Debeha hot springs in the woreda is said to have high potential for geothermal power generation.<ref>[http://www-wds.worldbank.org/external/default/WDSContentServer/WDSP/IB/2007/04/02/000020439_20070402115053/Rendered/PDF/E15460v40CD0RE1al0V0010212710710pdf.pdf "Ethiopia - Road Sector Development Support Program Project: environmental impact assessment (Vol. 4 of 4): Final report for Yalo - Dalol"] Ethiopian Roads Authority, June 2002, p. 63 (accessed 14 May 2009)</ref> The tallest point in Teru is the volcano [[Mount Dabbahu]] (1440 meters), which erupted in 2005. 'Teru' is also the title of a ballad composed by saxophonist [[Wayne Shorter]].

[[Education in Ethiopia|Education]] in Teru consists of 4 schools, where the first four grades are taught; there are a total of 304 students of whom 22 are female. There are a total of 10 teachers, 3 of whom teach in [[Afar language|Afar]]. Of the 4 schools, 3 were constructed by Ethiopian Social Rehabilitation and Development Fund and one by the community.<ref name=APDA/>

On 29 September 2005, a 5.5 magnitude earthquake struck Boyna and Dabbahu kebeles in Teru. Because this earthquake caused hot springs in the area to erupt with enormous clouds of steam, locals at first believed this was a volcanic eruption. The fault that moved was measured as being 35 kilometers long. No one was killed, but by 21 October 11,000 people were reported as having fled the affected areas and taking refuge in three camps.<ref>[http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/Earthquake_2005_10_09.htm Statement on the current earthquake in central Afar Region] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070929021113/http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/Earthquake_2005_10_09.htm |date=2007-09-29 }}, [http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/APDA/apda20051021.htm Update to the Teeru Earthquake] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070929021055/http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/APDA/apda20051021.htm |date=2007-09-29 }}, Afar Pastoralist Development Association</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 67,753, of whom 39,727 are men and 28,026 women; with an area of 5,792.85 square kilometers, Teru has a population density of 11.70. While 1,838 or 2.71% are urban inhabitants, a further 37,608 or 55.51% are pastoralists. A total of 9,983 households were counted in this woreda, which results in an average of 6.8 persons to a household, and 10,511 housing units. 99.9% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=267&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Afar Region], Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4.</ref>
