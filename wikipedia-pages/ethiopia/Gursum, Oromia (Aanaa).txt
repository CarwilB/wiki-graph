'''Gursum''' ({{langx|om|Aanaa Gursum}}) is one of the [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]]s in the [[Oromia Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[East Hararghe Zone]], Gursum is bordered on the south by [[Babille, Somali (woreda)|Babille]], on the west by the [[Harari Region]], on the north by [[Jarso (Hararge)|Jarso]], and on the east by the [[Somali Region|Gursum]] district in the [[Somali Region|Somali]] region.<ref>The Disaster Prevention and Preparedness Agency [https://web.archive.org/web/20070927204328/http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/administrative/Atlas_Oromiya.pdf map for Somali] (copyright 2006) shows a woreda of the Somali Region named Chenaksen east of Gursum, inside the Jigjiga Region.</ref> The administrative center of the woreda is [[Funyan Bira]].

== Overview ==
The altitude of this District ranges from 1200 to 2950 meters above sea level; [[Kondudo]] and [[Mount Medero|Medero]] are amongst the highest points. Perennial rivers include the Hariro, Goro Obole, Bombas, Ejerti, and Agemsa. A survey of the land in this woreda (reported in 1996) shows that 15.7% is arable or cultivable, 8.9% pasture, 13.2% [[Forestry in Ethiopia|forest]], 22.4% built-up, and the remaining 39.8% is considered degraded or otherwise unusable.<ref name=Oromia>[http://oromiagov.org/Socio%20Economic%20Profile/East%20Hararghe/East%20Hararghe.pdf ''Socio-economic profile of the East Hararghe Zone''] Government of Oromia Region (last accessed 1 August 2006)</ref> [[Peanut|Groundnut]]s are an important cash crop for this woreda.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20040922184619/http://www.ocha-eth.org/Archive/DownloadableReports/harar0599.pdf Mission Report - East and West Hararghe (Mission: 20 to 28 April 1999)]}} UNDP Emergencies Unit for Ethiopia report, dated June 1996 (accessed 16 January 2009)</ref> [[Coffee]] is another important cash crop; between 20 and 50 square kilometers are planted with it.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20160815124929/http://www.oromiacoffeeunion.org/production.htm "Coffee Production"]}} Oromia Coffee Cooperative Union website</ref>

Industry in the District includes 10 [[grain mill]]s and one brick factory employing 44 people, as well as 314 registered business including wholesalers, retailers and service providers; although [[feldspar]], [[quartz]] and [[garnet]] deposits are known to exist, only [[dolomite (rock)|dolomite]] and [[granite]] are quarried. There were 36 Farmers Associations with 25,727 members and one Farmers Service Cooperatives with 1336 members. Gursum has 75 kilometers of gravel and 30 kilometers of road, for an average road density of 119.7 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers. About 4.2% of the rural and 21.2% of the urban population have access to [[water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|drinking water]].<ref name=Oromia/>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this District of 151,931, of whom 77,112 were men and 74,819 were women; 12,048 or 7.93% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 97.35% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 2.34% of the population practised [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111113202722/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2011-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this District has an estimated total population of 211,593, of whom 105,509 are men and 106,084 are women; 15,478 or 7.31% of its population are urban dwellers, which is about the same as the Zone average of 6.9%. With an estimated area of 876.57 square kilometers, Gursum has an estimated population density of 241.4 people per square kilometer, which is greater than the Zone average of 102.6.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070813054932/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2007-08-13 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this District of 151,405, of whom 76,2467 were men and 75,159 women; 8,657 or 5.72% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The three largest ethnic groups reported in Gursum were (70.32%) (from the Akisho/Gurre clan), (29.8%) (from the [[Arap]], [[Jidwaaq]] and [[Karanle]] clans), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (2.71%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.47% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 70.8%, 29.87% spoke [[Somali language|Somali]], and 3.01% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.32% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 96.8% of the population having reported they practiced that belief, while 2.92% of the population said they professed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119200333/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs/report/Statistical_Report/k04/k04_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.13, 2.16, 2.20 (accessed 6 April 2009)</ref>
