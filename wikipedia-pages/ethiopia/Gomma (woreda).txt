{{short description|District in Oromia Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Gomma''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Oromia Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. It is named after the former [[Kingdom of Gomma]], whose territory was roughly the same as the modern woreda. Part of the [[Jimma Zone]], Gomma is bordered on the south by [[Seka Chekorsa (woreda)|Seka Chekorsa]], on the southwest by [[Gera (woreda)|Gera]], on the northwest by [[Setema]], on the north by the [[Didessa River]] which separates it from the [[Illubabor Zone]], on the northeast by [[Limmu Kosa]], and on the east by [[Mana (woreda)|Mana]]. Towns include [[Beshasha]], [[Choche]], [[Ghembe]], and [[Limmu Shaye]]. [[Guma (woreda)|Guma]] woreda was separated from Gomma.

== Overview ==
The altitude of this woreda ranges from {{convert|1,380|to|1,680|meters}} above sea level; however, some points along the southern and western boundaries have altitudes ranging from {{convert|2,229|to|2,870|meters}}. A survey of the land in this woreda shows that 60.7% is arable or cultivable (52.7% was under annual crops), 8.1% pasture, 4.6% forest, and the remaining 20.1% is considered swampy, mountainous or otherwise unusable. Land in cultivation included the two state coffee farms. Fruits, [[avocado]]es and spices are important cash crops.<ref name=IPMS>[http://www.ipms-ethiopia.org/content/files/Documents/PLS-DPD/goma.pdf "Goma Pilot Learning Site Diagnosis and Program Design"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110726185425/http://www.ipms-ethiopia.org/content/files/Documents/PLS-DPD/goma.pdf |date=2011-07-26 }} IPMS Information Resources Portal - Ethiopia (25 May 2007), pp. 7-33 (accessed 10 March 2009)</ref>

[[Coffee]] is also an important cash crop in Gomma; over {{convert|50|km2|sp=us}} are planted with this crop.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20160815124929/http://www.oromiacoffeeunion.org/production.htm "Coffee Production"]}} Oromia Coffee Cooperative Union website</ref> Coffee is grown in this woreda [[Shade-grown coffee|under shade trees]]; while the dominant species are ''[[Albizia gummifera]]'', which can shade as many as 150 coffee trees, and ''[[Millettia ferruginea]]'', other species are also used as shade trees.<ref name=IPMS/>

Industry in the woreda includes 118 [[grain mill]]s, 35 coffee hulling and 33 coffee [[pulper|pulping mills]], one [[sawmill]], an office and furniture factory, and one [[edible oil]] mill. There were 45 Farmers Associations with 45,226 members and 21 Farmers Service Cooperatives with 43,088 members.<ref name=Oromia>[http://oromiagov.org/Socio%20Economic%20Profile/Djimma%20Zone/Djimma%20Zone.pdf ''Socio-economic profile of the DJimma (sic) Zone''] Government of Oromia Region (last accessed 1 August 2006).</ref> One [[micro-finance]] institution operates in this woreda: the [[Oromiyaa Credit and Saving SC]] (OCS), established in 1997; its branch in Gomma was one of the first the OCS opened. While mismanagement forced the OCS to close that branch within the year, it was reopened in 2001 and serves customers in Agaro and 17 of the 39 [[kebele]]s.<ref name=IPMS/> Gomma has {{convert|55|km|sp=us}} of dry-weather and {{cvt|89|km}} all-weather road, for an average of road density of {{cvt|117|km}} per {{convert|1,000|km2|sp=us}}. About 41% of the urban and 15.9% of the rural population has access to [[water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|drinking water]].<ref name=Oromia/>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 213,023, of whom 108,637 were men and 104,386 were women; 12,769 or 5.99% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Moslem]], with 83.88% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 14.68% of the population said they practised [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], and 1.34% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111113202722/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2011-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 350,882, of whom 172,888 are men and 177,994 are women; 71,018 or 20.24% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 12.3%. With an estimated area of {{convert|1,230.16|km2|sp=us}}, Gomma has an estimated population density of {{convert|285.2|PD/km2|sp=us}}, which is greater than the Zone average of {{cvt|150.6|PD/km2}}.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2006-11-23 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 243,376, of whom 123,354 were men and 120,022 women; 39,663 or 16.3% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The five largest ethnic groups reported in Gomma were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (79.11%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (7.28%), the [[Kullo people|Kullo]] (4.2%), the [[Silt'e people|Silt'e]] (2.6%), and the [[Kafficho people|Kafficho]] (2.04%); all other ethnic groups made up 4.77% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 78.78%, 14.22% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]], 2.43% [[Kullo language|Kullo]], 1.14% [[Silt'e language|Silt'e]], and 1.12% spoke [[Kafa language|Kafa]]; the remaining 2.31% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 80.15% of the population having reported they practiced that belief, while 19.03% of the population said they professed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091115040247/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf |date=2009-11-15 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.13, 2.16, 2.20 (accessed 6 April 2009)</ref>
