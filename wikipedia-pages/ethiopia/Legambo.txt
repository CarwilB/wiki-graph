'''Legambo''' ([[Amharic]]: ለጋምቦ) is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Amhara Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. This woreda is named for one of the "Houses" or subgroups of the [[Bete Amhara]], who were located there.<ref>[https://archive.org/stream/journalsofrevmes00isen/journalsofrevmes00isen_djvu.txt ''Journals of the Rev. Messrs. Isenberg and Krapf, Missionaries of the Church Missionary Society, Detailing their proceedings in the kingdom of Shoa, and journeys in other parts of Abyssinia, in the years 1839, 1840, 1841 and 1842''], (London, 1843), pp. 324f</ref> Part of the [[Debub Wollo Zone]], Legambo is bordered on the south by [[Legahida]] and [[Kelala (woreda)|Kelala]], on the southwest by [[Wegde]], on the west by [[Debre Sina (woreda)|Debre Sina]], on the northwest by [[Sayint]], on the north by [[Tenta (woreda)|Tenta]], on the northeast by [[Dessie Zuria]], and on the southeast by [[Were Ilu (woreda)|Were Ilu]]. Towns in Legambo include [[Aqesta]] and [[Embacheber]].

Elevations in this woreda range from 1500 to 3700 meters; the highest point in this woreda, as well as the Debub Wollo Zone, is [[Amba Ferit|Mount Amba Ferit]], which lies on the border with Sayint.<ref>Svein Ege, [https://web.archive.org/web/20110718142857/http://www.svt.ntnu.no/ethiopia/c16xSouthW%E4loMapFull.pdf "South Shäwa 1:100,000. Topographic and administrative map of South Wälo Zone, Amhara Region, Ethiopia."]</ref>

==Demographics==
Based on the 2007 national census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 165,026, an increase of 3.93% over the 1994 census, of whom 81,268 are men and 83,758 women; 7,327 or 4.44% are urban inhabitants. With an area of 1,017.35 square kilometers, Legambo has a population density of 162.21, which is greater than the Zone average of 147.58 persons per square kilometer. A total of 39,078 households were counted in this woreda, resulting in an average of 4.22 persons to a household, and 37,384 housing units. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 93.34% reporting that as their religion, while 6.5% of the population said they practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=266&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Amhara Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114004005/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=266&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2010-11-14 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4.</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 158,785 in 38,182 households, of whom 78,087 were men and 80,698 were women; 4,286 or 2.7% of its population were urban dwellers. The largest ethnic group reported in Legambo was the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (99.9%). [[Amharic language|Amharic]] was spoken as a first language by 99.92%. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 92.99% of the population having reported they practiced that belief, while 6.82% of the population said they professed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck03%5Ck03_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Amhara Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101115052151/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck03%5Ck03_partI.pdf |date=2010-11-15 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.7, 2.10, 2.13, 2.17, Annex II.2 (accessed 9 April 2009)</ref>
