{{short description|District in Afar Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Mille''' is a  [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] of [[Awsi Rasu|Awsi Rasu (Administrative Zone 1)]] within [[Afar Region]], [[Ethiopia]].
It is named for the [[Mille River]], a tributary of the [[Awash River]].
Mille is bordered on the south by the [[Administrative Zone 3 (Afar)|Administrative Zone 3]], on the southwest by [[Administrative Zone 5 (Afar)|Administrative Zone 5]], on the west by the [[Amhara Region]], on the northwest by [[Chifra (Ethiopian District)|Chifra]], on the northeast by [[Dubti (Ethiopian District)|Dubti]], and on the southeast by the [[Somali Region]]. 
Towns in Mille include [[Mille, Ethiopia|Mille]] and [[Eli Wuha]].

The highest point in this district is [[Mount Gabillema]], at 1,459 meters, a dormant volcano in the southeastern part. 
Roads in this woreda include the feeder road between [[Chifra, Ethiopia|Chifra]] and Mille, which is 105 kilometers in length; it was constructed in two segments between February 1999 and February 2001 by [[SUR Construction]].<ref>[http://www.sur.com.et/MajProjExp.html "SUR Construction PLC - Major Projects"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100226180507/http://www.sur.com.et/MajProjExp.html |date=2010-02-26 }}, SUR Construction webpage (accessed 6 May 2010</ref> 
Important local landmarks include the [[Yangudi Rassa National Park]], which covers the southeast corner of Mille, but not Mount Gabillema; and the archeological sites at [[Hadar, Ethiopia|Hadar]] and [[Dikika]] where specimens of ''[[Australopithecus afarensis]]'' have been recovered.

==History==
In 2004, grazing conditions in Mille woreda had become degraded due to the encroachment by [[invasive species|invasive]] thick and thorny ''[[Prosopis juliflora]]'' bushes, leading to significant tensions between [[Issa (clan)|Issa]] and [[Afar people|Afar]] herders, due to the resulting scarcity of pasturage.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20060112183216/http://www.ocha-eth.org/Reports/downloadable/ACFSituationAssessmentinZn4&Dubti%20ReportDec,04.pdf "Assessment Report: Impact of poor Karma rains in Zone 1 & 4, Afar National Regional State"]}}, Action contra le Faim report, 8–12 December 2004, p. 2 (accessed 18 January 2010)</ref>

On 4 February 2007, the Afar Regional Cabinet approved the division of this woreda, creating a new woreda out of the western part, with its administrative center to be at Hadar.<ref>[http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/APDA/apda20070204.pdf "Three new woredas decided in Afar Region"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140729214201/http://www.afarfriends.org/Dok%20t%20websida/APDA/apda20070204.pdf |date=2014-07-29 }}</ref> The Ethiopian [[paleoanthropologist]] [[Yohannes Haile-Selassie]] led a number of digs in this woreda between 2004 and 2007.

== Demographics ==
Based on the 2007 Census conducted by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] of Ethiopia (CSA), this woreda has a total population of 90,673, of whom 49,705 are men and 40,968 women; with an area of 5,345.71 square kilometers, Mille has a population density of 16.96. While 14,208 or 15.67% are urban inhabitants, a further 66,212 or 73.02% are pastoralists. A total of 14,515 households were counted in this woreda, which results in an average of 6.2 persons to a household, and 15,642 housing units. 98.72% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], and 1.22% were [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity|Orthodox Christians]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=267&format=raw&Itemid=521 Census 2007 Tables: Afar Region] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121113215726/http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=267&format=raw&Itemid=521 |date=2012-11-13 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4.</ref>
