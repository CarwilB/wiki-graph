'''Rayitu''' is one of the [[Districts of Ethiopia|District]]s in the [[Oromia Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. Located in East Bale zone or Eastern part of the [[Bale Zone]], Rayitu is bordered on the south by the [[Somali Region]], on the west by [[Ginir (woreda)|Ginir]], and on the north and east by [[Seweyna]]; the Gestro River (or [[Weyib River]]) defines the southwest boundary.<ref>The Disaster Prevention and Preparedness Agency [https://web.archive.org/web/20070927204328/http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/administrative/Atlas_Oromiya.pdf map for Oromia] (copyright 2006) shows a woreda of the Oromia Region named Dawe Serara southeast of Rayitu, inside the Somali Region.</ref> Towns in Raytu include Dhedecha Bela Town.

== Overview ==
The geography of this woreda consists of mountainous terrain along the northern and southern edges, while the remainder is dominated by a flat plain. High points include [[Mount Elwak]]. Perennial rivers include the [[Shabelle River|Shabelle]], [[Weyib River|Weyib]], and Dinikte. A survey of the land in this woreda shows that 17% is arable (15% was under annual crops), 39% pasture, 37% [[Forestry in Ethiopia|forest]] or heavy vegetation, and the remaining 7% is considered swampy, mountainous or otherwise unusable. [[Teff]], [[maize|corn]] and [[sorghum]] are important local crops.<ref name=Oromia>[http://oromiagov.org/Socio%20Economic%20Profile/Bale/Bale%20Zone.pdf ''Socio-economic profile of the Bale Zone''] Government of Oromia Region (last accessed 1 August 2006)</ref> However raising livestock is a more important source of food and income, which include in order of importance [[shoat]]s, camels and cattle. Rayitu is a chronically food insecure woreda, and food aid is needed an average of five to six months in an average year.<ref>[http://www.pcdp.org.et/Docs/baseline/oromia-final.pdf "Pastoral and Agro Pastoral Livelihood baseline profile - Oromiya (7 Weredas)"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110720151752/http://www.pcdp.org.et/Docs/baseline/oromia-final.pdf |date=2011-07-20 }}, Pastoral Community Development Project (2004), p. 66</ref>

Industry in the woreda consists of a few small-scale establishments, as well as some retailers and service providers. There were 12 Farmers Associations with 6,273 members and 3 Farmers Service Cooperatives. Rayitu has 52 kilometers of dry-weather road, for an average road density of 0.28 kilometers per 1000 square kilometers. About 7% of the total population has access to [[water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|drinking water]].<ref name=Oromia/>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 33,169, of whom 16,876 were men and 16,293 were women; 3,204 or 9.66% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 98.47% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 1.2% of the population practised [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1], Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 43,222, of whom 20,788 were males and 22,434 were females; 2,409 or 5.57% of its population are urban dwellers, which is less than the Zone average of 13.5%. With an estimated area of 7,055.22 square kilometers, Raytu has an estimated population density of 6.1 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 27.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics], Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 31,056, of whom 16,182 were men and 14,874 women; 1,343 or 4.32% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The two largest ethnic groups reported in Raytu were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (91.25%), and the [[Somali people|Somali]] (8.47%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.28% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 89.34%, and 10.35% spoke [[Somali language|Somali]]; the remaining 0.31% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], with 99.56% of the population reporting they practiced that belief.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119200333/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs/report/Statistical_Report/k04/k04_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.7, 2.12, 2.15, 2.17 (accessed 6 April 2009).</ref>
