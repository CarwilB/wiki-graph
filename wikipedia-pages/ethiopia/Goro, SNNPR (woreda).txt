'''Goro''' was one of the 77 [[woreda]]s in the [[Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. This woreda was named after one of the sub-groups of the [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet]] [[Gurage people|Gurage]], the Goro. Part of the [[Gurage Zone]], Goro was bordered on the south by the [[Wabe River]] which separates it from [[Cheha]], on the west and north by the [[Oromia Region]], on the farther east by [[Kokir Gedebano Gutazer]], and on the southeast by [[Ezhana Wolene]]. The major town in Goro was [[Welkite]]. Goro was divided for [[Abeshge]], [[Kebena]] and [[Welkite]] woredas.

Local landmarks include the [[Acho Falls]] on the Wabe river which is 60 meters in height, and Walga Falls on the Walga river which is 30 meters in height.<ref>[http://www.guragezone.gov.et/Tourisms/Tourisms.htm "Tourisms"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101129020411/http://www.guragezone.gov.et/Tourisms/Tourisms.htm |date=2010-11-29 }}, Gurage Zone Government website (accessed 11 August 2009)</ref>

Goro was selected by the [[Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (Ethiopia)|Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development]] in 2004 as one of several woredas for voluntary resettlement for farmers from overpopulated areas, becoming the new home for a total of 3000 heads of households and 12,000 total family members.<ref>[http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202004.pdf "Resettlement 2004"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080227155134/http://www.dppc.gov.et/downloadable/map/Thematic%20maps/resettlement/Resettlement%202004.pdf |date=2008-02-27 }}, Disaster Prevention and Preparedness Agency (accessed 26 November 2006)</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 169,000, of whom 84,033 are men and 84,967 are women; 27,775 or 16.43% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 6.3%. With an estimated area of 925.01 square kilometers, Goro has an estimated [[population density]] of 182.7 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 278.3.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=2006-11-23 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref> In the 1990s, a survey revealed an isolated enclave of 1,000 speakers of [[Gumuz language|Gumuz]] around Welkite, a language otherwise found only in western Ethiopia.<ref>[http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/vw/ORTWEL.pdf "Local History in Ethiopia"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927023056/http://130.238.24.99/library/resources/dossiers/local_history_of_ethiopia/vw/ORTWEL.pdf |date=2007-09-27 }} The Nordic Africa Institute website (accessed 4 September 2007)</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 115,583 of whom 58,193 were men and 57,390 were women; 15,329 or 13.26% of its population were urban dwellers. The five largest ethnic groups reported in Goro were the Sebat Bet Gurage (38.56%), the [[Kebena people|Kebena]] (22.67%), the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (14.48%), the [[Silte people|Silte]] (10.02%) and the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (7.49%); all other ethnic groups made up 6.18% of the population. [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet Gurage]] is spoken as a [[first language]] by 31.82%, 24.19% [[Amharic language|Amharic]], 22.67% speak [[Kebena language|Kebena]], 10.77% [[Silte language|Silte]], and 6.08% speak [[Oromo language|Afan Oromo]]; the remaining 4.47% spoke all other primary languages reported. 62.53% of the population said they were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], 31.71% practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], 4.79% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], and 0.92% [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 30.3% of the population were considered literate, which is more than the Zone average of 20.62%; 21.04% of children aged 7–12 were in primary school, 6.2% of the children aged 13–14 were in junior secondary school, and 5.56% of the inhabitants aged 15–18 were in senior secondary school.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119202355/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 3.5, 3.7 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 93% of the urban houses and 36.69% of all houses had access to safe drinking water at the time of the census; 30.36% of the urban and 6.39% of all houses had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 4] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119193032/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 6.11, 6.13 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>
