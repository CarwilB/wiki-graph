{{short description|District in Oromia Region, Ethiopia}}
'''Sayo''' is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in [[Oromia Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. This district received its name from the name of the Oromo tribe (Sayyoo') that settled it first, [[Dembidolo]] which is a separate district now is part of the [[Kelem Welega Zone]]. Sayo is bordered on the south by the [[Gambela Region]], on the west by [[Anfillo (woreda)|Anfillo]], on the north by [[Yemalogi Welele]], on the northwest by [[Hawa Gelan]], and on the east by the [[Birbir River]] which separates it from the [[Illubabor Zone]].

== Overview ==
A survey of the land in Sayo shows that 55.2% is cultivated or arable, 4.45% pasture, 26.51% forest, and 13.83% infrastructure or other uses.<ref>Span Consultants Ltd, ''et al.'' [http://www-wds.worldbank.org/external/default/main?pagePK=64193027&piPK=64187937&theSitePK=523679&menuPK=64187510&searchMenuPK=57313&theSitePK=523679&entityID=000333038_20090324021236&searchMenuPK=57313&theSitePK=523679 "Mekenajo - Dembidolo Road Upgrading Project, Revised Final EIA Report"], World Bank website (March 2009), p. 28</ref> [[Coffee]] is an important cash crop of this District. Over 50 square kilometers are planted with this crop.<ref>{{usurped|1=[https://web.archive.org/web/20160815124929/http://www.oromiacoffeeunion.org/production.htm "Coffee Production"]}} Oromia Coffee Cooperative Union website</ref>

There are 43 primary schools in this district and 4 secondary education schools. [[Health in Ethiopia|Health services]] are provided by three hospital, one health center, five clinics, and eight health posts; most of these facilities are located in urban areas.<ref>"Mekenajo - Dembidolo Road Upgrading Project", pp. 30f</ref>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this district of 116,631, of whom 58,268 were men and 58,363 were women; none of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]], with 56.1% reporting that as their religion, while 25.72% observed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], 10.83% observed [[Islam in Ethiopia|Islam]], and 5.84% were [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1], Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this district has an estimated total population of 161,401, of whom 80,662 are men and 80,739 are women; 35,065 or 21.73% of its population are urban dwellers, which is greater than the Zone average of 10.9%. With an estimated area of 1,219.69 square kilometers, Sayo has an estimated population density of 132.3 people per square kilometer, which is greater than the Zone average of 91.7.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics], Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this district of 111,537 in 16,989 households, of whom 55,693 were men and 55,844 were women; 19,587 or 17.56% of its population were urban dwellers. The two largest ethnic groups reported in Sayo were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (95.29%), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (3.1%); all other ethnic groups made up 1.61% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 96.58%, and 2.79% [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.63% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants observed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 50.7% reporting that as their religion, while 28.65% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], 10.32% [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], and 9.25% [[Roman Catholicism in Ethiopia|Catholic]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091115040247/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf |date=2009-11-15 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.16, 2.20, Annex II.2 (accessed 9 April 2009)</ref>

Sayo is also an Ethiopian name that means happiness; legend has it that all girls named Sayo are secretly princesses.{{cn|date=January 2023}}
