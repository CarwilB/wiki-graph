'''Welene''' ("Welene") was one of the 7 [[List of zones of Ethiopia|Zone]]s in the [[Central Ethiopia Region]] of [[Ethiopia]]. This woreda was named after two of the sub-groups of the [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet]] [[Gurage people|Gurage]], the silte and the Wolene-Worriro. Part of the [[Gurage Zone]], Silte Wolene was bordered on the south by [[Muhor]], on the west by [[Sodo]], on the north by [[Goro, SNNPR (woreda)|Goro]], on the northeast by [[Oromia Region|Oromo]], and on the east by [[Meskanena Mareko]]. The major town in Site Welene was [[Agenda, Ethiopia|Agenda]]. It was divided for [[Welene]] and [[Silte Zone|Silte]] zones.

== Overview ==
Bodies of water in this woreda include [[Lake Bozebar]], which is 10 hectares in area. Local landmarks include Trer Falls which is 70 meters in height and is located 2 kilometers from Agenda, Fokra Falls which is 30 meters in height and 10 kilometers from Agenda, and Muhur Eyesus monastery which according to tradition was founded by Abba Zena Markos during the reign of Emperor [[Yekuno Amlak of Ethiopia|Yekuno Amlak]].<ref>[http://www.guragezone.gov.et/Tourisms/Tourisms.htm "Tourisms"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101129020411/http://www.guragezone.gov.et/Tourisms/Tourisms.htm |date=November 29, 2010 }}, Gurage Zone Government website (accessed 11 August 2009)</ref>

The completion of a [[drinking water|potable water]] improvement project in this woreda was announced 19 December 2006. Costing over 230,000 [[Ethiopian Birr|Birr]], contributed jointly by the Ethiopian and [[China|Chinese]] governments, the project is expected to benefit the inhabitants of three rural [[kebele]]s.<ref>[http://www.waltainfo.com/EnNews/2006/Dec/19Dec06/22040.htm "Over 230,000 birr potable water project goes operational in woreda"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071007234137/http://www.waltainfo.com/EnNews/2006/Dec/19Dec06/22040.htm |date=2007-10-07 }} [[Walta Information Center]] (accessed 5 February 2009)</ref>

== Demographics ==
Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 232,220, of whom 121,703 are men and 110,517 are women; 4,569 or 1.97% of its population are urban dwellers, which is less than the zone average of 6.3%. With an estimated area of 815.63 square kilometers, Ezhana Wolene has an estimated population density of 284.7 people per square kilometer, which is greater than the Zone average of 278.3.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061123121716/http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm |date=November 23, 2006 }}, Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 164,126 of whom 78,126 were men and 86,000 were women; 2,522 or 1.54% of its population were urban dwellers. The four largest ethnic groups reported in Ezhana Wolene were the Sebat Bet Gurage (97.21%), the [[Silte people|Silte]] (1.18%), the [[Kebena people|Kebena]] (0.92%), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (0.48%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.21% of the population. [[Sebat Bet Gurage language|Sebat Bet Gurage]] is spoken as a first language by 97.27% of the population, 1.16% [[Silte language|Silte]], 0.94% speak [[Kebena language|Kebena]], and 0.49% speak [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.14% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants observed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 70.85% of the population reporting they practiced that belief, while 27.93% were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]], and 0.66% were [[P'ent'ay|Protestants]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples' Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119232405/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partI.pdf |date=November 19, 2008 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.12, 2.15, 2.19 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Education in Ethiopia|education]], 24.76% of the population were considered literate, which is more than the Zone average of 20.62%; 19.06% of children aged 7–12 were in primary school, 3.19% of the children aged 13–14 were in junior secondary school, and 2.81% of the inhabitants aged 15–18 were in senior secondary school.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 2] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119202355/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partII.pdf |date=November 19, 2008 }}, Tables 3.5, 3.7 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref> Concerning [[Water supply and sanitation in Ethiopia|sanitary conditions]], 12.96% of the urban houses and 8.08% of all houses had access to safe drinking water at the time of the census; 15.23% of the urban and 1.9% of all houses had toilet facilities.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia'', Vol. 1, part 4] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119193032/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck07%5Ck07_partIV.pdf |date=November 19, 2008 }}, Tables 6.11, 6.13 (accessed 30 December 2008)</ref>
