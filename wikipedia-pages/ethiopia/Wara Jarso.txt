'''Wara Jarso''' ({{langx|om|Warra Jaarsoo}}) is a [[Districts of Ethiopia|woreda]] in the [[Oromia Region]], [[Ethiopia]]. Part of the [[North Shewa Zone (Oromia)|North Shewa Zone]], Wara Jarso is bordered on the south by [[Kuyu]], on the west by the [[Muger River]] which separates it from the [[West Shewa 
Zone]], on the north by the [[Abay River]] which separates it from the [[Amhara Region]], on the northeast by the [[Jamma River]] which separates it from [[Dera, Oromia (woreda)|Dera]], and on the east by [[Hidabu Abote]]. Towns in Wara Jarso include [[Filiklik]], [[Gohatsion]] and [[Tullu Milki]].

Wara Jarso is connected to [[Dejen (woreda)|Dejen]] in Amhara by the Abay Bridge, which also carries the [[Addis Ababa]]-[[Bahir Dar]] highway; before the bridge was erected, the Abay was crossed at the Shefartak ford (at 10° 5' N 38° 17' E).  A new bridge, the Hidasie Bridge, was dedicated 10 September 2008 at the presence of senior government officials and other guests. Funded by the [[Ethiopia-Japan relations|Japanese government]] at a cost of 319.3 [[Ethiopian Birr|Birr]], the Hidasie Bridge is 303 meters long and part of the new Addis Ababa-Dejen road.<ref>[http://www.ena.gov.et/EnglishNews/2008/Dec/31Dec08/76897.htm "'Hidasie Bridge' opens for traffic: ERA"]{{Dead link|date=July 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}, [[Ethiopian News Agency]] (accessed 29 April 2009)</ref>

== Demographics ==
The 2007 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 141,426, of whom 70,787 were men and 70,639 were women; 10,531 or 7.45% of its population were urban dwellers. The majority of the inhabitants said they practiced [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 93.55% of the population reporting they observed this belief, while 4.65% of the population were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], and 1.46% of the population were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Muslim]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/index.php?option=com_rubberdoc&view=doc&id=272&format=raw&Itemid=521 ''2007 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1], Tables 2.1, 2.5, 3.4 (accessed 13 January 2012)</ref>

Based on figures published by the [[Central Statistical Agency (Ethiopia)|Central Statistical Agency]] in 2005, this woreda has an estimated total population of 159,653, of whom 80,075 are men and 79,578 are women; 11,934 or 7.47% of its population are urban dwellers, which is less than the Zone average of 9.5%. With an estimated area of 1,172.97 square kilometers, Wara Jarso has an estimated population density of 136.1 people per square kilometer, which is less than the Zone average of 143.<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/text_files/2005_national_statistics.htm CSA 2005 National Statistics], Tables B.3 and B.4</ref>

The 1994 national census reported a total population for this woreda of 114,212, of whom 57,254 were men and 56,958 women; 6,691 or 5.86% of its population were urban dwellers at the time. The two largest ethnic groups reported in Wara Jarso were the [[Oromo people|Oromo]] (78.11%), and the [[Amhara people|Amhara]] (21.68%); all other ethnic groups made up 0.21% of the population. [[Oromo language|Oromiffa]] was spoken as a first language by 76.26%, and 23.68% spoke [[Amharic language|Amharic]]; the remaining 0.06% spoke all other primary languages reported. The majority of the inhabitants professed [[Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity]], with 96.74% of the population reporting they practiced that belief, while 1.96% said they were [[P'ent'ay|Protestant]], and 1.13% of the population were [[Islam in Ethiopia|Moslem]].<ref>[http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs%5Creport%5CStatistical_Report%5Ck04%5Ck04_partI.pdf ''1994 Population and Housing Census of Ethiopia: Results for Oromia Region'', Vol. 1, part 1] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119200333/http://www.csa.gov.et/surveys/Population%20and%20Housing%20Census%201994/survey0/data/docs/report/Statistical_Report/k04/k04_partI.pdf |date=2008-11-19 }}, Tables 2.1, 2.13, 2.16, 2.20 (accessed 6 April 2009)</ref>
